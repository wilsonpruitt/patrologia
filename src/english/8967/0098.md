## PSALM CXLI.

VERS. 1.-- *The understanding of David.* [n: (CASS.)] This psalm is the fifth of those which are entitled by the name of a prayer, where no other things are said than those which have been said in other psalms, but the same. [n: (AUG.)] But because by a repetition of this kind provision is made for the common infirmity, they are not repeated to no purpose, that things repeated may the better be committed to memory; whence it is written in the Proverbs: *A desirable treasure rests in the mouth of the wise man [n: (Prov. XII)]*, [var: Vulg. Prov. 21:20 *Thesaurus desiderabilis, et oleum in habitaculo justi*, "a desirable treasure, and oil in the dwelling of the just"] because the wise man ponders what he has heard; he ruminates upon it by going over it again; but the foolish man swallows it down, [1064D] that is, he does not ponder it, he gives it over to forgetfulness; and these are those clean animals in the law which chew the cud; those are the unclean. David, fleeing from Saul, lay hid in a cave, of which nevertheless there is no question here, but of the thing signified. For David signifies Christ. The cave, the flesh, or the world, or the sepulchre. David therefore, that is, Christ, with his members in the world, or Christ in the flesh, or in the sepulchre.

VERS. 2.-- *With my voice I have cried to the Lord.* First the prayer against evils is set down, secondly he treats of the end of evils, there: *I have cried to thee, O Lord.* He admonishes us therefore to pray. And in the person of the whole Christ, as of one crying to God out of a cave, he says: *I have made supplication.* [n: (AUG.)] That which he says, *I have cried, he expounds, I have made supplication.* As if he should say: My cry [1065A] is a supplication, not a murmur, not a blasphemy.

VERS. 3.-- *In his sight.* [n: (AUG.)] The Lord is about to repay, pray in secret; whence in the Gospel: *But thou when thou shalt pray, enter into thy chamber, and having shut the door, pray to thy Father who sees in secret,* etc. [n: (Matth. VI.)]

VERS. 4.-- *And thou hast known.* [n: (ID.)] This he adds, lest those hearing, *my spirit hath failed,* should suppose him cast down. As if he were to say: If they suppose me cast down, because my spirit has failed, thou seest me standing, whose Spirit gives me life.

*Paths.* Of justice, which they do not see.

*In the way.* Many are the ways, because many are the precepts: one is the way, because all are brought back to one, that is, to charity.

[1065B]AUG.) *In the way,* according to their desire; beside the way, according to the truth. For Christ is the way, who does not suffer them to set a snare *in the way,* so that we should have nowhere to go; yet he suffers them to set it beside the way, lest we turn aside from the way.

VERS. 5.-- *I looked to the right hand.* [n: (AUG.)] Which the wicked did not see; and this is, *and there was none that knew.* [n: (CASS.)] For when thou sufferest all things, who knows what thou art regarding? [n: (AUG.)] He who attends to the right hand, sees; he who attends to the left, is blinded. Or, the apostles were not standing at the right hand, because they were scattered by a carnal fear, but I stood fast.

*Flight hath perished.* As much of the body as of the mind. *And there is none that seeks.* [n: (CAS.)] Because there is no consolation, but he was left alone to the passion. *Flight hath perished.* [1065C] [n: (AUG.)] He says that he is shut in on every side. *Flight hath perished,* because he does not flee in mind, though sometimes in body. He who is shut in does not flee in body, because he cannot. More strongly [cj: *Fortius*; read *Fortis*, "the strong man"] does he not flee in mind, who does not will it. *Flight hath perished* for the man shut in or for the strong man; flight indeed, whence it is said in the Gospel: *He sees the wolf coming and flees [n: (Joan. X)]*. Where the Lord reproves the hireling. But there is another flight, of the body, which the Lord permits, saying: *If they shall have persecuted you in one city, flee into another [n: (Matth. X)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 10:23 *Cum autem persequentur vos in civitate ista, fugite in aliam*, "when they shall persecute you in this city"]

*And there is none that seeks the soul.* The soul is sought either by persecutors or by those who love it. So flight perishes in two ways, both for the man taken captive and for the strong man.

VERS. 6.-- *I have cried.* [n: (CAS.)] The second part, where he treats of the end of evils. *My hope.* Because I choose thee out of all. *Portion.* Humbly and in common with others he says that a portion [1065D] is his in the land of the living, whereas the whole is his. This appears from the fact that he said to the thief: *This day thou shalt be with me in paradise:* which is the land of the living everlastingly.

VERS. 7.-- *From them that persecute me.* For the devil through his vessels, that is, through the children of unbelief, works persecution of the body, that within there may come about the ruin of the heart.

VERS. 8.-- *Bring forth out of custody,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Speaking in the person of the Church, he sets forth from what he is to be delivered. Otherwise, *out of the prison.* Because in corruption (which is the punishment of the body) he is straitened: after which straits in the house of God the whole life shall be confession, that is, the praise of God; now the prison hinders him from confessing. This prison or cave may be called the world, where all things are vain: whence he asks [1066A] that the soul be led forth, which suffers many things there, etc., *as far as* but its corruption, and temptation, is the prison.
