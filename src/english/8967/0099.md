## PSALM CXLII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A psalm of David.* ⟦s2⟧ This psalm is the seventh of the penitential psalms, which are therefore seven, that by the same number by which we have sinned we may wash away our sins. [n: (CAS.)] For as we sin in the week of this world, so by this number of psalms the remedy of penance is set against our sins. ⟦s3⟧ In which psalms the form is such, that it is begun from tears, and the end is in joy, lest any despair of pardon. [n: (AUG.)] As Absalom did to David, so Judas to Christ, false brethren to his body; or unlawful motions and action.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *O Lord, hear,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] First [1066B] he takes his exordium; secondly he narrates his own evils, and what he has done against them, there, *because he has persecuted;* thirdly the supplication is set down, confirmed by reasons, there, *hear speedily;* fourthly the joyful conclusion is set down, there, *thy spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ He admonishes to penance, and to prayer, that we should pray. ⟦s3⟧ In the person of the whole Christ he says: *O Lord,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ *In thy truth.* [n: (CAS.)] This is not against the humility which was asking to be heard: but it is the truth and justice of God, that he should not dispute with the one asking, that he should not judge him who condemns himself; whence it is added: *and enter not,* he did not ask for the justice of judgment. [n: (AUG., CAS.)] *In thy truth, in thy justice.* ⟦s5⟧ Or he calls justice and truth that which spares the penitent, and absolves those who condemn themselves, and he takes *truth and justice* for the same thing. ⟦s6⟧ And the sense is, *in thy truth, and thy justice,* that is, according to the justice and [1066C] truth which thou grantest to the penitent and to those who accuse themselves.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And enter not into judgment.* [n: (AUG.)] They enter into judgment with the Lord who establish their own justice, which this humble man does not do; whence the prophet: *Why will you contend with me in judgment? ⟦s2⟧ You have all forsaken me, says the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ With thy servant.* ⟦s4⟧ It is unworthy to contend with a servant, not to say with a friend: and if thou sayest 'friend', and I 'servant', a fugitive I return, I seek peace, *I am not worthy to be called thy son.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Because he has persecuted.* [n: (CAS.)] The second part, where there is the narration of the evils which he suffers, and what he has done against them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *I have been mindful.* [n: (AUG.)] This the members say, etc., *as far as* but thou hast begun not to be. ⟦s2⟧ *I have [1066D] meditated.* ⟦s3⟧ Because assuredly all things are good, wherein he perceives that whatsoever of good is in him, is from God. ⟦s4⟧ *In the works of the hands.* ⟦s5⟧ As if he should say: I have sought the craftsman in the work, and in all created things the creator: thy creature has been made a spectacle for me.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I have been mindful of the days.* [n: (CAS.)] This man, calling to mind that he made heaven and earth, and that he did good to the ancients, forgets the miseries of the world. ⟦s2⟧ And out of those things which God has made he consoles himself.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Speedily.* [n: (CAS.)] The third part, where the supplication confirmed by reasons is set down. ⟦s2⟧ *The spirit hath failed.* [n: (AUG.)] Let thy Spirit fill me, because mine has failed: already I am poor in spirit. ⟦s3⟧ For in whom his own spirit lives, he is proud in his own spirit, and is lifted up [1067A] against God. ⟦s4⟧ Whence elsewhere: *Thou shalt take away their spirits, and they shall fail [n: (Psal. CIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ *Into the pit.* ⟦s6⟧ He goes down into the deep who loses even confession, as one who sets before himself licence to sin without hope of pardon, the reins of iniquity let loose. ⟦s7⟧ Such a man does not hear God saying: *Turn to me, and I will turn to you [n: (Zach. I)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *In the morning.* [n: (CAS.)] After the night of sins. [n: (AUG.)] It is morning, when sins are loosed. ⟦s2⟧ *Make the way known to me.* ⟦s3⟧ This God has done, etc., *as far as* we shall come to the pure light, to the Word in the beginning with God. ⟦s4⟧ *The soul.* (CAS.) ⟦s5⟧ As a vessel I have brought it to the fountain: fill it therefore.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Thy good spirit.* [n: (CAS.)] The fourth part, where the joyful conclusion is. ⟦s2⟧ *For thy name's sake, [1067B] O Lord, thou wilt quicken me in thy equity.* ⟦s3⟧ The grace of God is here commended: because he says that he is quickened not out of merits, but out of the name, which is Jesus; yet along with this the grace of equity shows mercy, because although God does all things freely, it belongs nevertheless to his justice to justify the suppliants whom he sees the demons assail.
