## PSALM CXLIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A psalm of David to Goliath.* [var: Vulg. Ps. 143:1 *Psalmus David. Adversus Goliath*] [n: (CAS.)] David, the whole Christ, etc., *as far as* and so his head is cut off with his own sword.--David slew Goliath, Christ the devil, etc., *as far as* whence spiritually the following psalm is entitled *praise.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Blessed.* [n: (CAS.)] A triumphal psalm, etc., *as far as* praying to be delivered through him, there, *O God, a canticle.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1067C] *Who teaches my hands.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, who through the Holy Spirit, etc., *as far as* that which follows. ⟦s2⟧ *My mercy.* [n: (CAS.)] Because it is tedious to explain the causes of many things, he touches them briefly in single words, etc., *as far as* because with contempt and under wonder it is added, *O Lord, what is man, that,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] *My mercy.* ⟦s3⟧ Because thou grantest me to be merciful, etc., *as far as* forgive therefore, and I forgive; give, and I give.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *That thou makest account of him?* [n: (AUG.)] Great is that which God esteems, and holds of such worth that for it he gave his Son, for whom he even becomes bread, who is the bread of angels: for God does not so esteem man as man does, who buys a horse more dearly than a man.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *He is become like to vanity: his days [1067D] as a shadow.* [n: (ID.)] When man was created, he was made like [sic: *simlis*] to truth [sic: *veriteti*]; like to vanity by sinning. ⟦s2⟧ All transitory things are vain, in comparison with the truth that ever abides. [n: (CAS.)] That is vain which, falling away from its own substance, vanishes: as smoke, as a shadow. ⟦s3⟧ So man himself also wastes away, whence he adds, *His days,* that is, his notorious life, that is, his transitory life, so passes away as a shadow.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *O Lord, bow down.* [n: (AUG.)] The humble David, in the person of the Church, full of grace, presuming upon God, fighting here calls upon the help of God. ⟦s2⟧ As if: we are in a wrestling, but let us not fail. ⟦s3⟧ *O Lord, bow down.* [n: (CAS.)] *O Lord.* ⟦s4⟧ He discloses the same thing, etc., *as far as* and the form of God took upon it the form of a servant.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1068A]VERS. 7.-- *Send forth the hand.* [n: (CAS.)] He turns his words to the Father: *Send forth the hand,* that is, Christ; *from on high* where he is with the Father, that he may put on flesh. [n: (AUG.)] Or he treats of the second coming in the person of the Church. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: So do now. ⟦s3⟧ *Send forth thy hand from on high,* that is, Christ from heaven unto judgment. ⟦s4⟧ For the Lord shall come in the voice of an angel, and in the trumpet of God he shall descend from heaven. ⟦s5⟧ Then in the person of the Church he adds: *Take me out,* namely, by that hand. ⟦s6⟧ *And deliver,* etc., as in the letter.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *O God, I will sing to thee a new canticle.* [n: (CAS.)] The second part, where, as though he whom he was entreating had come as victor and deliverer, he promises to sing to him in the New Testament and in the Old: and he prays to be delivered by him from those who think beatitude to lie in earthly things. [1068B] *A canticle.* [n: (AUG.)] By this other name he signifies the law, which he signified by the five stones.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *With the sword.* [n: (AUG.)] Those which above he called waters, the same he here calls a sword; whence here also he adds what he added there: *And rescue me from the hand of strange children. ⟦s2⟧ Malignant.* ⟦s3⟧ Of which: *Their tongue is a sharp sword [n: (Psal. LVI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ There is also a kindly sword, of which the Lord says: *I came not to send peace, but the sword [n: (Matth. X)]*. ⟦s5⟧ This is the sword twice driven [cj: *actus*; read *acutus*, "sharpened"] [n: (Apoc. XIX)], mighty with either edge, namely of the Old and of the New Testament, by which all rottenness is cut away from the members of Christ.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Whose sons.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he sets forth who the strangers are, and what their right hand of iniquity is, and in what manner they have spoken vanity. [n: (AUG.)] He enumerates their felicity: [1068C] this is their right hand, this is the vanity which they speak, while they call these things beatitude. [n: (CASS.)] *Whose sons.* ⟦s3⟧ Or, mystically these things can be understood from that place, etc., *as far as* but they have sprouted with new errors.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Daughters.* [n: (CASS.)] Which have not the strength of constancy.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Decked out.* ⟦s2⟧ Not beautiful by nature, but decked out by men. ⟦s3⟧ These daughters are the thoughts or the preachings of those who have not the true Church, but a likeness of it.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Storehouses.* ⟦s2⟧ Hearts made for divers things: whence they breathe out the worst belchings, which stink.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From this into that.* ⟦s2⟧ Unstable, because they have not the root [1068D] of truth.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Their sheep fruitful.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the letter, because he wishes to show that they cleave to perishable things. ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: Their flock is fruitful, while they themselves are barren in faith; whence mystically their disciples, these are the fruitful sheep, because they themselves beget many others in error. ⟦s4⟧ *In the goings out.* ⟦s5⟧ Not in the goings in, because all the good which they seem to have goes out from them, who are soon to perish. ⟦s6⟧ *Oxen.* ⟦s7⟧ Not I labour [cj: *laboro*; read *labore*, "with labour"] lean, but fat with idleness, like those who abound in riches, him [cj: *eum*; read *cum*, "whereas"] they themselves are fasting from truth. ⟦s8⟧ Or mystically, oxen, the masters, abound in earthly things. ⟦s9⟧ *The fence.* ⟦s10⟧ Which are of stones alone in gardens but or in vineyards; nor does it itself fall down for them, therefore they are prosperous.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CAS.)] *Neither passage, nor cry,* turbulent, [1069A] interrupts their quiet, etc., *as far as* which leads to death.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Blessed.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not blame them on this account, because they abound in these things, but because they hold them for a right hand, that is, for beatitude: and this is the vanity which they have spoken. ⟦s3⟧ In these Abraham also, and Isaac, and Jacob abounded. ⟦s4⟧ Happiness is a left hand, that is, temporal, mortal.
