## PSALM CXLV.

VERS. 1.-- *Alleluia.* [n: (CAS.)] This voice, as it were saving musics [cj: *musicas*; read *musica*, "a saving music"], like a heavenly harmony, which delights not men only but angels also, setting forth the praise of God. And it is fitly prefixed to this psalm, [1071D] where the just man exhorts his soul to the praise of God: because in adversities the soul is sometimes troubled, whether it will or no. Whence above: *Why art thou sad, O my soul, and why dost thou disquiet me?* But that he may take away her disquiet, he suggests joy out of hope, if not out of the thing itself: *Hope in God,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] This soul, lifted up by hope, praises God here. [n: (CAS.)] First, he counsels himself, as it were all men, to praise, lest prayer be made among vain things; and as though acquiescing in the counsel, he affirms that he will praise there where he shall be able to praise fully. Secondly, he admonishes others not to hope in man, there: *Trust not.* Thirdly, he teaches that all hope is to be placed in the Almighty, there: *Blessed,* etc.

VERS. 2.-- *Praise the Lord, O my soul.* But [1072A] it is asked, by whom, and to whom, is this counsel given? If it be said that the flesh counsels the soul, this does not seem to be true, since counsel is wont to be given by the better and the worthier to the inferior: but the flesh is inferior to the soul. For the soul, although it be blamed because it is a sinner, is nevertheless called better by nature than the body that is praised in its own kind, because a spiritual nature excels the body, which it moves, rules, endows with sense, and apprehends: and being itself unbounded, it is near to the substance of God. So gold, blamed because it is foul, surpasses lead in purity, praise [cj: *laudatio*; read *laudato*, "that is praised"]: so too a bad man surpasses the best of horses. Counsel therefore is not given to the soul by the flesh, however greatly it be subdued and made peaceable: which nevertheless is dead because of sin. Nor yet does the mortal flesh counsel the immortal soul, because it is always beneath the soul; but the higher part of the soul, [1072B] that is, the mind, which thinks upon the divine wisdom, cleaving to it and sighing after it, sees certain of its own parts troubled by the motions of the world, and hurried away to outward things by the desire of earthly goods: whence it calls it back to things inward and higher. Exhorting itself therefore according to its sensuality, it says: *Praise the Lord, O my soul.* For what pleases thee in this world? Divided among so many loves of the things of the ages, gather thyself to thy Author: here is peace and security for thee. Then the soul answers the mind, as one weighed down here and not able to leave off, as is meet. *I will praise in life.* Here in death I shall praise but thinly, because the body *is a load upon the soul, and the earthly habitation presseth down the sense that thinketh upon many things [n: (Sap. IX)]*; here hope feeds us, life is in the world to come.

*In my life.* [n: (CAS.)] When I shall have cleaved to him perfectly, from [1072C] whom to be parted, as now befalls, is death: but hope consoles, in which, living after a sort, it praises.

*As long as.* Not that at some time he shall not be, because he shall be for ever, and therefore truly long.

VERS. 3.-- *Trust not,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] The second part. The counsel being given and confirmed, where he shall be able to praise fully, he admonishes them, as though set far off, and in the meantime while evils last (by which the soul is troubled), not to hope in man, but in God. [n: (AUG.)] Or, as one set in mind at the end of the world: he teaches them not to love things soon to end in place of things eternal; as if by my example also, *trust not,* etc. *In the children of men.* [n: (ID.)] In one son of man is salvation, and in him do ye hope. Not because he is a son of man, but because he is the Son of God. Not for that which he took from thee, but for that which he kept in himself.

[1072D]VERS. 4.-- *His spirit shall go forth,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] First he cleanses hearts by forbidding what is hurtful, that afterward he may aim at what is saving by admonishing to wholesome things.

VERS. 5.-- *Blessed is he whose God is the God of Jacob.* [n: (CAS.)] The third part, where he teaches that the whole hope is to be placed in the Almighty, whom he describes from his works, that the Gentile world, so often confuted, may be still. [n: (AUG.)] Hurtful things being removed, he teaches what belongs to salvation, namely, in whom hope is to be placed. *His helper is his hope.* [n: (CAS.)] Of that Jacob himself he was so the helper, etc., *as far as* that is, of this true Jacob, as of that one.

VERS. 6.-- *Who made heaven and earth.* [n: (ID.)] Because the worshippers of demons also have hope in their god, he determines in whom hope ought to be, that is, in Christ, and this [1073A] he shows even to the end of the psalm, describing him through all things. *All things that are in them.* Lest thou think any other to be the creator, or that there exists a creator even of the smallest things.

VERS. 7.-- *Who keepeth truth for ever.* [n: (CAS.)] Christ, who is the truth; every man is a liar. *He executeth judgment.* These two pertain to justice. Christ the judge avenges those who suffer for his name, and gives food to them that love him. *For them that suffer wrong.* [n: (AUG.)] Not every trouble is a wrong. What thou sufferest by right is not a wrong, as is that which the robber and other wicked men suffer. It is one thing therefore to suffer wrong, another to suffer punishment. For a man suffers punishment by right, but wrong is contrary to right.

VERS. 8.-- *The Lord looseth them that are fettered.* [n: (CAS.)] This [1073B] belongs to the abundance of his sweetness.

*The Lord lifteth up them that are cast down.* Whom the devil casts down, the Lord lifts up.

*He loveth the just.* [n: (CAS.)] Whom he sets in eternal blessedness.

VERS. 9.-- *The fatherless and the widow he will receive.* [sic: *suseipiet*] [n: (AUG.)] The whole Church of the Gentiles is come as a stranger to the fathers, a daughter not by flesh but by imitation.

*The fatherless* are all who are in pilgrimage, whose father is absent; and every faithful soul is a *widow* while the bridegroom is absent: whence they desire the more both the father and the bridegroom, and God protects them, not seen but desired.

VERS. 10.-- *The Lord shall reign for ever.* [n: (CAS.)] This is of his magnificence.

[1073C]He shall reign in us when our hearts shall be firm in him, which is not yet.
