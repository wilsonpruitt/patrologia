## PSALM CXLVI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Alleluia.* [n: (AUG.)] Here likewise he speaks of the praise of God, where the prophet admonishes men to praise him that gathers Israel.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Praise ye the Lord.* [n: (CAS.)] First he shows whom he gathers, and in what manner. ⟦s2⟧ Secondly, after these things, he rebukes them as still doubting and wavering, showing whence they may be able to praise God, namely if they shall have been humble, there, *Sing praise.--Praise ye the Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] This is said to all the Gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ And as though we should ask in what manner we shall praise, he brought forward the cause of praise, *for a psalm is good.* [n: (CAS.)] In the praise of God the act itself is the reward which shall be in time to come; therefore is he to be praised, because [1073D] he builds up, and thus, because *the dispersions* [sic: *disper iones*] *of Israel,* that is, of the faithful, out of the whole world at the end of the age he *shall gather* into his kingdom. ⟦s4⟧ And here is praise from his wonderful works. [n: (AUG.)] This is the whole reward of them that praise, etc., *as far as* let us for the present be exercised in this praise of good works.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let it be pleasant to our God,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is not enough to sing psalms unto this end, that praise may please God, but there is need that we please him also by cleanness of life.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The Lord buildeth up Jerusalem.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, he has taken *Jerusalem* for the Church universal, etc., *as far as* whom the Father sent to redeem our captivity by the price of his blood.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *And he bindeth up.* ⟦s2⟧ From the likeness of a physician, etc., *as far as* and the Lord binds up their bruises. [n: (HIER.)] [1074A] Even as that Samaritan who was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho [n: (Luc. X)], when he saw the wounded man lying in the way, bound up his wounds. ⟦s3⟧ But concerning those who have not bruised their heart it is said by Isaias: *There is no plaster to lay on, nor oil, nor bandages [n: (Isa. I, secundum LXX)]*. ⟦s4⟧ The bandages of God are the sacraments of time, by which for the present we have consolation; and these shall then be taken away when health shall be perfect.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Who numbereth the multitude of the stars, and calleth them all by their names.* [n: (CAS.)] Not strange names, as the astrologers who name Jupiter and Venus. ⟦s2⟧ Or, figuratively, of the saints, because to each he gives his proper gift; or, all are in his familiarity, from the likeness of a lord who knows the names of his own household servants.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1074B]VERS. 5.-- *Of his wisdom,* etc., [n: (AUG.)] The measure itself, and the number and the weight, etc., *as far as* see what follows, *but humbling sinners.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *But humbling sinners.* [n: (AUG.)] For they who find fault with things intelligible, therefore savour of earthly things, thinking bodily concerning the incorporeal God. ⟦s2⟧ These things he does to those who wish to mock the law before they know it. ⟦s3⟧ Because therefore it is a great thing to advance to things intelligible and spiritual, let us be meek, let us knock and do that which follows.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Sing praise.* ⟦s2⟧ So shall a man come to understanding, so shall the image be reformed unto God. ⟦s3⟧ *Sing praise.* [n: (CAS.)] Secondly, out of the things aforesaid he rebukes and urges them as still wavering, showing whence they may be able to understand, if they be humble and not proud. ⟦s4⟧ As if to say: And that ye may be able [1074C] to understand, *sing praise,* etc.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Who covereth the heaven.* [n: (CAS.)] He calls to mind the works through which his power is shown. ⟦s2⟧ For unto this end he willed certain things in the Scriptures to be obscure, that the servants of God might have somewhat which by interpreting they should pour upon the ears and hearts of men, who receive from the clouds of God the fatness of spiritual gladness.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To the service of men.* ⟦s2⟧ To the preachers, who serve men. ⟦s3+4⟧ Whence the Apostle says: *But we ourselves your servants through Jesus Christ [n: (II Cor. IV)]*. [var: Vulg. 2 Cor. 4:5 *nos autem servos vestros per Jesum*, "your servants through Jesus"] These sow spiritual things and receive from *the high ones of the world hay and grass,* that is, temporal things.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *And to the young of the ravens.* [n: (AUG.)] For all things that are given to the Church by the rich are hay.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1074D] [n: (ID.)] The Jews, who called themselves alone just and all the Gentiles sinners. ⟦s2⟧ And truly our fathers were *ravens,* but we do not so remain: yet rightly are we called, and are, the young of ravens. ⟦s3⟧ The raven calls not upon God, but we being let go do call. [n: (CAS.)] The young of ravens are said to feed upon dew, and by the benefit of their age they know not their fathers' food, that is, the stench of carcasses: so the innocent children of the pagans, who call upon God.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *In the legs of a man.* ⟦s2⟧ In the composed bearing [cj: *In composito*; read *Incomposito*, "in the uncomposed bearing"] of the presumptuous and bold. ⟦s3⟧ For by a bold man he denotes a presumptuous one. ⟦s4⟧ In the legs is noted an uncomposed gait, *in the legs of a man.* ⟦s5⟧ Or, according to another reading, *in the tabernacles of a man,* that is, in the conventicles of heretics, *shall it be well pleasing to him.* ⟦s6⟧ Note that he says not [1075A] in the tabernacles of God, but *of a man,* thereby designating the boldness of heretics.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Upon them that fear him,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] He joined hope to fear, because fear avails not without hope of pardon: for Judas, fearing but despairing, hanged himself with a halter. ⟦s2⟧ He therefore that fears God, let him hope also in him. ⟦s3⟧ And thou shalt appease him if thou hope in his mercy, and for the rest so beware of sinning that for things past thou entreat that they may be forgiven thee.
