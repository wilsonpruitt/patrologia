## PSALM CXLVII.

VERS. 12.-- *Alleluia.* This psalm works the love of the Jerusalem to come. It is said to be of Aggeus and Zacharias, who in the captivity prophesied the restoring of Jerusalem, which, as Jeremias foretold, was accomplished after seventy years, [1075B] that is, after the rolling round of the whole of time, which is carried on in seven days.

*Praise, O Jerusalem.* [n: (CASS.)] This whole psalm proclaims the power of God. First, it tells the good things of the Jerusalem to come; secondly, it tells what good things are given here to the saints, there: *Who sendeth forth.* [n: (AUG.)] The prophet, being as yet set in captivity, etc., *as far as* we who have heard him thus pouring it forth.

VERS. 13.-- *He hath strengthened the bolts of thy gates.* [n: (AUG.)] Note that the door is shut, the prudent virgins being admitted and the foolish shut out, so that now none may go out or come in. And fitly after the admonition to praise he subjoins concerning the shut gates: for with the gate of Jerusalem shut she praises securely, because none has gone out for whom she should grieve; none comes in whom she should fear, whence scandals should be.

VERS. 14.-- *With the fat of corn.* [n: (ID.)] With the bread that came down from heaven: [1075C] in the world he is shut up in flesh, known by faith alone; then shall he be seen as he is.

VERS. 15.-- *Who sendeth forth his speech.* The second part subjoins what gifts are given here to the elect, through which the truth of that which is to come may be known.

*To the earth.* [n: (AUG.)] That he may be known on earth. For unless to send [cj: *mittere*; read *mitteret*, "he were to send"] his word to the earth, by which we are weighed down and hindered from our returning, we should not be lifted up to the fat of corn that satisfies. For in the earth, where we labour weary, faint, sluggish, cold, that is, in the desert, he forsakes us not, but manna: *who sendeth forth his speech,* etc.

*Swiftly runneth.* [n: (AUG., CASS.)] Or even unto swiftness, that is, from God, so that it is swiftness itself, than which nothing could be said more choicely or more expressly.

He says not that the word is swift, that it be swift with a swiftness [1075D] of which it partakes more or less: but than swiftness itself, whereby all other things are called swift, nothing is swifter. And again, that which is swift, as a horse is swift to a man, is not so everywhere: but swiftness itself is everywhere, it is not in a part. To the Word of God it belongs not to be in a part, but to be everywhere through himself. This is that wisdom *which reacheth from end to end mightily,* yet not by motion of place, but by immobility. And of the spirit of wisdom also it is said, *driven, moveable, sure, undefiled;* this swiftness effects, that it be both always everywhere, and nowhere held shut in. But who is sufficient to think upon these things? Yet there is hope of laying hold of the Word, because he has predestinated us.

VERS. 16.-- *Who giveth snow,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] To the earth. For charity growing cold, etc., *as far as* and while he calls these [1076A] to penance, *he scattereth mist like ashes.*

[n: (ID.)] *He scattereth mist like ashes,* etc. Ashes pertain to penance, etc., *as far as* and who has predestinated and has called to penance. According to the letter: God scatters snow like wool, mist like ashes, he makes crystal like morsels of white bread. For crystal is a certain kind of thing after the manner of glass, but it is white: and it is said to be snow hardened by ice through many years, so that it cannot be dissolved.

*His crystal,* etc. That is, the obstinate, who are not dissolved easily as snow is.

VERS. 17.-- *He sendeth as morsels,* etc. Otherwise, *as crumbs of bread,* that they may feed others, that they may preach the truth which they assail. All together, one bread; singly, [1076B] morsels; whence: *Being many, we are one bread in Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 10:17 *unus panis, unum corpus multi sumus*, "one bread, one body, we being many"] Paul was crystal, hard and shining: hard, because he persecuted the Church; shining, because he was skilled in the law. But God dissolved him, and he was made bread, who taught the truth. But unless he dissolve,

*Before the face of him, of God, who shall stand?* Otherwise, shall subsist. [var: Vulg. Ps. 147:6 *ante faciem frigoris ejus quis sustinebit*, "before the face of his cold who shall stand?"] [n: (AUG.)] It is cold when he forsakes, calls not, pours not in grace: and who else shall dissolve it? None. Whence the Apostle: *Who shall deliver me from the body of this death? [n: (Rom. VII.)]* As if he said: I grow cold, none dissolves me, unless the grace of God *send forth his word.*

VERS. 18.-- *He shall send out his word, and shall melt it.* [n: (CASS.)] For when the word of God comes, neither cold, nor darkness, nor hardness can despair. For they are melted healthfully, and pour out streams of spiritual benefit [1076C] to others whereof they may drink, according to that saying: *All you that thirst, come to the waters [n: (Isa. LV)]*.

[sic: VERS. 16.]-- *Jacob . . . Israel.* [n: (AUG.)] Or the same thing is signified by Israel, etc., *as far as* because he was not sent but *to the sheep that were lost of Israel [n: (Matth. XV)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 15:24 *domus Israel*, "of the house of Israel"]

*His judgments.* [n: (ID.)] Judgment begins at the house of God, etc., *as far as* subjoining three other psalms of praise, that in this work the graces of the Trinity may shine forth.

## PSALM CXLVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Praise ye the Lord from the heavens.* [n: (CASS.)] By the mention of a few he exhorts the whole creation to praise God. There are certain things which [1076D] have a spirit of praising God in that affection wherewith God pleases them: certain have no understanding, but because they themselves are good in their own order, and are referred to the beauty of the whole, they praise not of themselves, but by them God is praised through those that consider them: and so after a sort they praise, because from them the praise of God is born in us, whereto the prophet here admonishes us.

VERS. 5.-- *For he spoke, and they were made.* [n: (CASS.)] This against those who say that the world is coeternal with God; there follows: *He commanded.* By his word, and they were created. First he said, *made,* afterward *created.* For make we also can, but not create. He therefore alone creates, which is proper to the Godhead. Praise from his works

[1077A]VERS. 6.-- *He hath established.* From his power he is almighty, whose works are not changed.

*Unto the age, and unto the age of the age.* [var: Vulg. Ps. 148:6 *Statuit ea in aeternum, et in saeculum saeculi*, "he hath established them for ever, and for ages of ages"] [n: (CAS.)] And though the heaven be new, yet they are said to be well established by God, which are made new through Christ and remain bettered. And here is praise from his power.

VERS. 7.-- *Praise ye the Lady [cj: *Dominam*; read *Dominum*, "the Lord"].* The second part, where, the heavenly things being enumerated, he descends to earthly things. Turning therefore to them, as exhorting us that by the consideration of them we may praise God, which things considered lay open the praise of God, he says: O ye that are of the earth. *All ye deeps.* [sic: *ubyssi*] [n: (AUG.)] The depth of the waters, etc., *as far as* these therefore, that is, he exhorts us to praise by the consideration of them.

VERS. 8.-- *Which do his word.* This therefore [1077B] because it seemed to certain men that God ruled the higher things, but despised these lower ones, and that they were ruled by chance; but these also God does, and in these least things he is praised, and alleluia is sung.

VERS. 13.-- *Confession.* [n: (CAS.)] Above he spoke first of heaven, afterward of earth; here of all together. And he takes by heaven all higher things, and by earth all lower things. [n: (AUG.)] *His confession above heaven and earth.* All things confess him in themselves or in others; the beauty of all things is the voice of them that confess, in those who consider them, that is, let those who consider praise him, both from earthly and from heavenly things, than which there is nothing better.

VERS. 14.-- *He hath exalted the horn of his people.* [n: (ID.)] Behold what Aggeus and Zacharias prophesied. Now the horn of his people is lowly [1077C] in tribulations, but when the Lord shall come, it shall be exalted.
