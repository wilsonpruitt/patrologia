## PSALM CXLIX.

VERS. 1.-- *Alleluia.* [n: (CAS.)] After all the creatures which in the foregoing psalm he admonished to praise God, for that they were created by him, here more properly and more especially he invites Israel to a new canticle for the newness wrought in it, which is the more wonderful working of God: this is the sanctification of Israel, whereby of old it was made new.

*Sing ye to the Lord,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] First in many ways he admonishes them to praise the King who is kind and bountiful to them. Secondly he treats of the joys of the saints, there, *They shall rejoice.* [n: (AUG.)] He that would sing this canticle, let him love things eternal, [1077D] that he may be made new. For the old man sings an old canticle of earthly things. The new man sings a new one of spiritual things; a canticle, namely, of peace, a canticle of charity and of gladness. For whosoever follows the old animosity, and not the new charity, sings not a new canticle.

*His praise in the Church.* [n: (CAS.)] Here he determines where and by whom this canticle ought to be sung.

VERS. 2.-- *In their king.* [n: (ID.)] That is, in Christ, who is king and priest. [n: (AUG.)] As king he fought for us from the cross, overcoming the devil: as priest, he offered himself for us, finding nothing in men that he might offer for them.

VERS. 3.-- *In choir.* [n: (ID.)] Because there is no dispersion, [1078A] but a most dear unity; then shall the rejoicing be of the timbrel and the psaltery, not the act of the thing itself.

VERS. 4.-- *For the Lord is well pleased.* [n: (ID.)] In the first framing of things we read that all things pleased God, etc., *as far as* or when he shall make them equal to the angels.

VERS. 5.-- *The saints shall rejoice.* [n: (ID.)] The second part, where he treats of the joys of the saints and of their power. *In glory.* It is the frequent praise of good deeds, wherein the just rejoice in their beds, that is, in their conscience, therefore because they have so good a Lord, who gives grace to sinners and eternal life upon their merits. The fool rejoices without, in the tales of men; to the saints this is the manner of joy, that good things be referred to him who gave them.

VERS. 6.-- *Exaltations.* [n: (ID.)] The strength and power of God is preached in the glory of the saints. In their beds, [1078B] in the divers mansions, is the glory of the saints. Whereof the Lord says: *In my Father's house there are many mansions. [n: (Joan. XIV.)]*

*In the throat.* They who in heart and in tongue cease not to praise him from whom they have things eternal.

*Swords.* Receiving the power of the word of God, they shall judge with the Lord, sitting upon twelve seats; and then shall truly be wrought *vengeance and rebuke among the peoples,* then shall the kings of the earth be bound: whence it may not be free to go out. And the nobles shall bear iron fetters, as heavy to carry, and such as cannot be broken: and so shall be wrought the judgment written, that is, approved by the angels. Whereof the Apostle: *Know you not that we shall judge angels? [n: (I Cor. VI.)]* And lest thou think that the apostles alone judge, he adds, *this glory,* that is, the power of judgment, is to all [1078C] his saints, that is, to the perfect, who for Christ left all earthly things.

[n: (AUG.)] *The exaltations of God in the throat.* For so do they rejoice in their beds and in their throats, that is, in heart and tongue, that they ascribe not to themselves that they are good, but to God, from whom they received what they are, and hope for that which they are not yet.

VERS. 8.-- *To bind.* With these bonds of the commandments and precepts of God are bound the kings of the Gentiles, the nobles of the world, by the weak and contemptible of this world, whom the Lord chose that he might confound all the strong things; and he filled these same with the Holy Ghost, and gave them two-edged swords, that they might turn the hearts of nobles to the fear of Christ, and that, worldly nobility [1078D] being despised, they might choose poverty and nobility in Christ. For he willed to profit the emperor by a fisherman, not the fisherman by an emperor.

[n: (AUG.)] By these he binds kings and nobles in iron fetters, that they should not go forward to unlawful things.

## PSALM CL.

VERS. 1.-- *Praise ye the Lord.* [n: (CAS.)] Of the praise of God in his saints, who being glorified praise him without end. The city of God is admonished, that is, the unity of all the saints, to sing with mouth and with mind. In this is all psalmody brought to its summit, which is sung with nuptial sweetness.

*Praise ye him in his firmament.* [n: (AUG.)] There is power in [1079A] the firmament, etc., *as far as* who, honouring one another, give praise to God.

[n: (CAS.)] To whom the prophet in the beginning showed the form of their conversation, now, being received into that Jerusalem, he says that in them God is to be praised, who gave all things. *In the firmament,* because by his own death he overcame the devil, and led believers to heaven. In his powers or mighty acts, because all things are subject under his feet.

VERS. 2.-- *According to the multitude of his greatness.* How is this, since of his greatness there is no end? but this is said to the spiritual and to the saints. And since there is no end of his greatness, he is praised according to it. God, since he is praised without end. But in what manner he ought to be praised, he tells by mystical similitudes even to the end of the psalm: let *the trumpet* sound loud to the King, let *the psaltery* sing [1079B] to God: *the harp* with the rest to the Bridegroom. For the trumpet is terrible, and is either taken up in war, or made ready at the coming of kings; the psaltery sounds from above, the harp from below: all these things, being compared to human acts, are there said figuratively to sound forth the praises of God.

VERS. 4.-- *On strings and organ.* [n: (CAS.)] *Strings* he puts for every musical instrument which sounds by the stretching of strings. *The organ* is as it were a tower framed of divers pipes, sounding by the blast of bellows; and that thou mightest take all these [1080A] things of men, he comes to the mouth of man: and expounding what it is to praise *on strings and organ* he subjoins.

*Praise ye him on cymbals,* etc. *Cymbals* well sounding are our lips, which being tuned to the heart sound well in the praises of God, wherein is the likeness of cymbals, and they render a harmony, and therefore are they rightly among musical instruments. [n: (CAS.)] Cymbals well tinkling are very small bowls of mingled metals, which render a shrill sound. But because he would have all things understood spiritually, he concludes with a fitting end:

VERS. 6.-- *Let every spirit,* angelic and human, *praise the Lord.* Not flesh and blood, not the habit of the world: but that which is more sublime in the nature of things, [1080B] which savours of heavenly things, which lays hold of things eternal. A brief and perfect knowledge, that all things should praise God spiritually; in these is the perfection of all music. For there is breath in the trumpet, striking in the harp and in the cymbals, voice in the choir. By which musical instruments is signified the spiritual harmony, which is perceived not by the ears, but by a pure mind.

[n: (AUG.)] There are three kinds, etc., *as far as* it is the voice of life eternal: *Let every spirit praise the Lord.*
