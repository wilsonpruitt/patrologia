## CHAPTER II.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *It happened.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the holy Church belongs to Christ, whose bride and body she is. ⟦s3⟧ Of whom it is said: *The Lord is my strength and my praise [n: (Psal. CXVII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *The Lord who is strong and mighty. ⟦s5⟧ The Lord mighty in battle [n: (Psal. XXIII)]*. ⟦s6⟧ He himself is the kinsman of Elimelech, who, born of Bethlehem and of the stock of David, has testimony from the law and the prophets. ⟦s7⟧ He brought words of salvation and of peace to his own household, at whose nativity the angels sang, *Glory to God in the highest, and on [0534D] earth,* etc. [n: (Luc. II)]. ⟦s8⟧ To whom, as he came into Jerusalem, the crowd that met him cried out, saying: *Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord [n: (Matth. XXI)]*.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And she asked.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the holy Church perseveres in the field of divine reading, after the reapers and preachers, gathering the mysteries of the Scriptures, that she may recall within the bosom of her mind the testimonies and examples of the virtues. ⟦s3⟧ Or that field is the discipline of heavenly study; the harvest, spiritual understanding; the reapers, the preachers; the ears left behind, the sentences of the Scriptures, which, being much hidden by mystery, remain as it were fuller for the exercise of one who meditates. ⟦s4⟧ The Gentile people therefore ardently desires the discipline of the Church, that it may be admitted to the meditation of the divine law and to the fellowship of the saints, [0535A] and be refreshed by the documents and the examples of the saints.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Go not.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he said: Withdraw not from the state of faith, follow not the errors of heretics or of schismatics: but rather join thyself to holy souls, that thou mayest reap the holy Scriptures by meditating and by fulfilling them in work; and that thou too mayest drink the draught of divine wisdom out of the books of the two Testaments (whence the servants, that is, the saints, drink).

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And she, falling on her face.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church of the Gentiles gives thanks to the Saviour, who deigned to look upon her. ⟦s3⟧ To whom he answered: that it pleased him that, her husband being dead — that is, the devil — she left her idolatrous parents and the land of her birth, that is, fleshly desires, and joined herself to the people of the saints, who beforetime [0535B] was unknown to her, while she followed the covetousness of the old man.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *May I find,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Note the humility of the Gentile Church, who knows herself unequal to so great a grace, and does not dare to set herself level with the primitive Church; whence she says: *For the whelps also eat of the crumbs,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ And elsewhere: *Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldst enter under my roof, but only say the word and my servant shall be healed [n: (Matth. VIII)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *When the hour.* ⟦s2⟧ The hour of eating was when it was said to the apostles: *Go, teach ye all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost [n: (Matth. XVI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ For it had been said by the Lord: *Go ye not into the way of the Gentiles [n: (Matth. X)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0535C] *Dip thy morsel.* ⟦s2⟧ The law, that is, which, long meditated on and abiding in the former people, was corrupted by the conditions or the traditions of the Pharisees and lost its native savour. ⟦s3⟧ In this the Church dips her morsel, that is, she says that the mystery of Christ's incarnation is in the Old Testament, and therefore believes it the more firmly, because she knows it was prefigured long before.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Of your own.* ⟦s2⟧ Note the humility of the father of the household, who not only allows the ears of the testimonies that are left to be gathered, but even bestows handfuls of knowledge with unasked generosity.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Cast forth of your own hands,* etc. [var: Vulg. *de vestris quoque manipulis proicite*, "cast forth of your handfuls also"] Because he does not despise the weakness of the Gentile people, nor cast up to it its former errors, but by the shrewdness of his zeal leads it on to edification.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0535D]VERS. 17.-- *About an ephi.* ⟦s2⟧ When in her reading she learns the catholic faith through the confession of the holy Trinity. ⟦s3⟧ In the measure of the ephi, which holds three pecks, is signified the one substance of the Godhead and the propriety of the three persons.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Carrying which.* ⟦s2⟧ Because she shows her faith to her mother the Church. ⟦s3⟧ Or the Church shows to the synagogue the grace which she received by the gift of her bridegroom, that she might provoke her to the faith.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He gave her.* ⟦s2⟧ My heart hath uttered a good word, that is, out of the fulness of the heart the food of preaching.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Blessed be he.* ⟦s2⟧ The synagogue, at the preaching of the Church of the Gentiles, comes to know the goodness and the power of the Lord, and the strength of her own protector: and at length, the truth compelling her, [0536A] she blesses the name of the Lord, because he kept for the dead the grace which he showed to the living.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *A kinsman.* ⟦s2⟧ Our mother the Church knows her kinsman through the free benefits bestowed upon the people of the Gentiles, and at the hearing of his name she remembers the benefits of old; whence: *I remembered the days of old, I meditated on all thy works [n: (Psal. CXLII)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *He charged me.* ⟦s2⟧ Because Christ charges all who come to him that they be joined rather with his faithful to the harvest of the spiritual crop than with strangers; to which persuasion the mother-in-law consents, saying: *It is better, my daughter;* this, as good and useful, the Synagogue cannot deny.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Until the wheat.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, she cleaves to the teachers in the meditation of the Scriptures so long, until [0536B] she has laid up the knowledge of the Old and the New Testament in the storeroom of her heart: whence she might have food enough for her soul.

## CHAPTER III.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The threshing-floor.* ⟦s2⟧ The synagogue of the Jews, where the barley of the law was stored: this Christ winnows, when, preaching the Gospel on the floor, he threshes out the counsels and the wills of each man toward himself; whence: *But Jesus did not trust himself unto them, for that he knew all men, and because he needed not that any should give testimony of man [n: (Joan. II)]*.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Let no man see thee.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the faith of the Church of the Gentiles did not appear before Christ had fulfilled the mystery of his incarnation, when, living bodily among men, he used food and drink, and at last, supping [0536C] with his disciples, he shared with them the sacraments of his body and his blood. ⟦s3⟧ But when the mystery of the dispensation was fulfilled, the Church of Christ from the Gentiles came to the faith with confidence.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Thou shalt uncover,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ What he says is: Acknowledge Christ as having suffered for thee, and come with a devout mind; shake off the covering of the letter of the Old Testament, in which the sacrament of Christ's incarnation is hidden; and when thou hast known that salvation was promised thee therein, flee humbly to his help, that thou mayest abide there at all times.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *The heap.* ⟦s2⟧ The testimonies of the Scriptures; whence: *The Son of man goeth, as it is written of him [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Or, beside the throngs of faithful souls [0536D] which by his death he called back from hell. ⟦s4⟧ To this bed the Church hastened, and, the mantle being uncovered, etc.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 12.-- *But there is another nearer than I.* John the Baptist, who was thought to be the Christ, but did not usurp the name of the bridegroom, and kept it for Christ, saying: *He that hath the bride is the bridegroom,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ He is nearer, because he was born first in the world. ⟦s4⟧ Or this kinsman is the decalogue of the law, which seemed nearer to the Synagogue than the Gospel, because it was given especially to that people and is prior in time.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *She arose therefore before.* ⟦s2⟧ Because, before the Jews, steeped in the teaching of the law, could come to know the rights of their own nature, the Church of the Gentiles, shaking off the sleep of her own sloth, rose again in the dawn of a faith just being born, and hastened to the grace of Christ.
