## PROLOGUE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1167B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The book of Wisdom is nowhere to be found among the Hebrews. ⟦s2⟧ Whence its very style savours rather of Greek eloquence. ⟦s3⟧ The Jews affirm this book to be Philo's. ⟦s4⟧ It is accordingly named of Wisdom, because in it the coming of Christ, who is the wisdom of the Father, and his passion, are plainly set forth.

## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Love justice,* etc. [n: (RAB. in lib. Sap., tom. III.)] Jerome asserts that this book is not by Solomon, etc., *as far as Love justice,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *The earth.* ⟦s3⟧ The flesh, that is: you restrain it with discernment, lest it grow proud against the spirit. ⟦s4⟧ As if to say: *Seek ye first the kingdom of God [n: (Matth. VI)]*, etc. ⟦s5⟧ *In simplicity.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, subject yourselves to his will, and keep his commandments; [1167C] as if he should say: You who do not understand, do not find fault, because the secrets of God cannot be comprehended by human reason, because his judgments are incomprehensible, and his ways unsearchable [n: (Rom. XI)].

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *For into a malicious soul,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, into the sensual man, according to that saying: *The sensual man perceiveth not [n: (I Cor. II)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore he did not say "spirit," that is, a spiritual soul. [n: (RAB.)] In vain do philosophers, and heretics, and false Christians flatter themselves. ⟦s4⟧ For only the clean of heart can receive the wisdom of God.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Kind.* ⟦s2⟧ He inveighs against those who say that thoughts both good and evil are sent in by God; whereas God will have all men to be saved, and to come to [1167D] the knowledge of the truth. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself tempts no man in order to deceive, but in order to prove, whence: *God tempted Abraham [n: (Gen. XXII)]*; and, *Prove me and try me [n: (Psal. XXV)]*, etc. ⟦s4⟧ But every man is tempted by his own concupiscence, and by the just judgment of God he is permitted to fall who has refused to obey the spirit of truth.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And that which containeth all things.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the Spirit, who governs and rules all things. ⟦s3⟧ Or, he who reacheth from end to end, *hath knowledge of the voice,* that is, he affords men the understanding of the Scriptures. ⟦s4⟧ Or, *that which containeth all things,* that is, man, who has something in agreement with every creature. ⟦s5⟧ *Hath knowledge of the voice,* that is, of divers tongues, by the Holy Spirit, who gave the apostles to speak the wonderful works of God in divers tongues. ⟦s6⟧ Or *that which containeth all things,* that is, the Holy Spirit, [1168B] who fills all things with his essence, *hath knowledge of the voice,* that of the Word of God, that is, he is of the same knowledge with the Word. ⟦s7⟧ Or, *this which containeth all things,* that is, the Church, in which *to another is given the word of knowledge,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Who in the beginning *was borne over the waters,* and with his majesty fills and contains every creature.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For this cause.* [n: (RAB.)] As the elect receive the gift of knowledge and of piety through the Spirit: so the reprobate, puffed up with their own pride, shamelessly put forth their error; since they cannot hide it from the Spirit of God, nor do they escape a just punishment: because as the eyes of the Lord are upon the just, and his ears unto their prayers, so is the countenance of the Lord upon them that do evil things.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Keep yourselves therefore.* [n: (RAB.)] Murmurings and detractions are pernicious, whence: *For whisperers [1168C] and detractors are hateful to God [n: (Rom. I)]* [var: Vulg. Rom. 1:29–30 has these as items in a list of vices, *susurrones, detractores, Deo odibiles*, not as a proposition]; whence: *He that detracteth from any thing bindeth himself for the time to come [n: (Prov. XIII)]*. ⟦s2⟧ *The mouth that lieth.* [n: (ID.)] There is a lie of lighter fault, etc., *as far as* and rewards the good itself.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Be not zealous for death.* [n: (ID.)] He has enumerated the things that are hurtful to man, etc., *as far as* but rather out of vice.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And covenants.* ⟦s2⟧ We have struck a league with death, and with hell we have made a pact.

## CHAPTER II.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *For they have said.* [n: (RAB.)] The voice of the lost, etc., *as far as* and we shall be brought to nothing.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For smoke.* ⟦s2⟧ Truly we shall be nothing, [1168D] because *smoke and breath is in our nostrils.* [var: Vulg. Sap. 2:2 *fumus flatus est*, "breath is smoke," with no *and*] that is, in our body, that is, our body is compared to smoke and to breath, which rise together and fail together. ⟦s3⟧ *And speech a spark.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, the soul is comparable to a spark, which straightway becomes nothing, *speech,* I say, that is, the soul. ⟦s5⟧ *To move our heart.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, it quickens the body. ⟦s7⟧ And that it is so to be expounded he proves by what he adds. ⟦s8⟧ *Because ashes being quenched,* [var: Vulg. Sap. 2:3 *qua extincta, cinis erit corpus nostrum*, "which being quenched, our body shall be ashes"] and afterwards, *the spirit shall be poured abroad*

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Let us oppress.* [n: (RAB.)] These things pertain generally to those, etc., *as far as* nor do they themselves obey the head, but blaspheme and find fault.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Let us see.* [n: (RAB.)] The like is said in the Gospel, where the chief priests and the Pharisees took counsel how they might deliver Jesus to death: and when he was set upon the cross they mocked him, saying: *He saved others, himself [1169A] however,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *If he be the king of Israel, let him now come down from the cross [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*, etc.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *By a most shameful death.* ⟦s2⟧ By the cross, that is, which until the passion of Christ was the punishment of the guilty, and is now the trophy of victory.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *For God.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, God is not the author of evils; for he created man unto immortality, who, consenting to the devil's suggestion, was made subject to the sentence of death.

## CHAPTER III.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *But the souls of the just.* [n: (RAB.)] In the former chapter the sentence of the wicked was set forth, etc., *as far as* whence: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints [n: (Psal. CXV)]*.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1169B]VERS. 7.-- *Sparks.* ⟦s2⟧ A spark does two things, namely, it swiftly changes a reed-bed, and it avenges.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And the Lord shall reign.* ⟦s2⟧ With whom his members also shall reign. ⟦s3⟧ Whence in Daniel: *The kingdom and the power, and the greatness of the kingdom, which is above heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the Most High [n: (Dan. VII)]* [var: Vulg. Dan. 7:27 *quae est subter omne caelum, detur*, "which is under the whole heaven, let it be given"].

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Wisdom.* [n: (RAB.)] Heretics may be noted in this sentence, etc., *as far as* but he shows that the working of the reprobate is worthy of everlasting malediction.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *For happy.* [n: (ID.)] In the *barren woman* and in the *eunuch* the order of virgins may be understood, etc., *as far as* in the will of the mind, not in the weakness of the body.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *In the day of acknowledgment.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, of judgment, when the elect, acknowledged by their judge, shall be [1169C] invited to possess the kingdom; but the wicked shall be comforted by no hope of restoration, but, to be tormented for ever, shall be driven out from the sight of the sovereign king.

## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *O how beautiful is,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Having found fault with heretics and schismatics, he praises catholics, etc., *as far as* and therefore he says: *With glory,* etc.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A reward surpassing.* ⟦s2⟧ A reward given for a passing contest, which is greater than those contests and persecutions.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *But the multiplied brood of the wicked.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks by turns of catholics and of heretics, that he may show the praiseworthy wisdom of the one and the blameworthy craft of the other. ⟦s3⟧ *Plantings.* ⟦s4⟧ Since μόσχος in Greek is called vitulus in Latin: certain men [1169D] did not understand that μοσκεύματα are plantings, and rendered them *vitulamina*: which error has taken possession of so many manuscripts that it is scarcely to be found written otherwise.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *The just man,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] When he had spoken of the destruction of the wicked, etc., *as far as* they from life unto everlasting death.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Pleasing God.* [n: (RAB.)] God does not take him away for this cause, etc., *as far as* whence Peter, *God knoweth how to deliver the godly out of temptation, but to reserve the unjust unto the day of judgment to be tormented [n: (I Petr. I)]* [var: Vulg. 2 Petr. 2:9 *Novit Dominus*, "the Lord knoweth"].

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Bewitching.* [n: (ID.)] By this sentence there is noted, etc., *as far as* for God separated them from the fellowship of the wicked, that *he might hide them in the hiding of his face from the disturbance of men [n: (Psal. XXX)]*.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1170A]VERS. 19.-- *He shall burst them puffed up and speechless,* because they shall be inexcusable: *because when they had known God, they have not glorified him as God, or given thanks, but became vain in their depraved thoughts [n: (Rom. I)]* [var: Vulg. Rom. 1:21 *in cogitationibus suis*, without *pravis*, "depraved"].
