## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1-7.-- *Then,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *We wearied ourselves,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The philosophers wearied themselves, etc., *as far as* whence they say, grieving, *What hath pride profited us?*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9-12.-- *As a shadow.* [n: (RAB.)] By *a shadow,* the forgetfulness of the mind, etc., *as far as* which go before the setting of a corruptible life. [n: (ID.)] By *a bird,* excellence, etc., *as far as* groaning, they shall be relieved by no voice of consolation.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Such things they said in hell, they that.* [1170B] He introduces yet four other kinds, by which he shows the foolish hope of the wicked, whereby they reckon present things only by measure, or that they escape unpunished.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *It is down, which is taken away by the wind.* ⟦s2⟧ Which, springing from the withered flower of the grasses, expresses the frailty of the flesh, which shall quickly be taken away by the wind of sickness, or by the trouble of the body, *because all flesh is grass. ⟦s3⟧ And as a thin froth.* ⟦s4⟧ Froth, brought forth out of the waters, swims upon the water. ⟦s5⟧ This is the appetite for human favour and for common boasting, which is dissolved by the storm of temptation or by the sifting of judgment, or is scattered abroad, that is, it is proved how vain it was.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *But the just shall live for evermore.* [n: (RAB.)] After the punishments of sinners, etc., *as far as* of God they shall enjoy, in whom they have placed their whole hope.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1170C]VERS. 18.-- *He shall take armour.* [n: (RAB.)] The Creator of all things, etc., *as far as* since they are not able to resist.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *And to a certain place.* ⟦s2⟧ Wheresoever it is necessary: for that cannot be confused which has been ordered by the divine wisdom.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *And the rivers,* that is, the commotions of persecutions, for very often the scourge of gathering breaks the hearts of the depraved which preaching does not soften.

## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Wisdom is better,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He admonishes the rulers of the Church, etc., *as far as* he will judge not only works, but knowledges and wills.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *For to the little one mercy is granted.* ⟦s2⟧ To those, that is, who have sinned through ignorance, or frailty, or necessity, and by humble penance have [1170D] blotted it out. ⟦s3⟧ *But the mighty mightily,* etc., that is, they who, by despising the commandments of God, have committed grievous sins, and have not repented. ⟦s4⟧ For as they were the mightier in impiety, so shall they endure the stronger torments of hell. [n: (ANAST.)] For what cause, etc., *as far as but to whom much, of him also much shall be required.*

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For he will not except any man's person.* ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *God is not a respecter of persons, he judgeth every man according to his own ways [n: (Act. X)]* [var: Vulg. Act. 10:34 ends at *non est personarum acceptor Deus*; the following clause stands nowhere in Acts, and is nearest Jer. 17:10].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *To you therefore, O kings,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ His discourse to the mighty being ended, and their hardness rebuked with hard words; he turns his exhortation to the rulers of the Church, and extols wisdom with wonderful praises.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Love them,* etc., *and you shall have discipline.* ⟦s2⟧ He that loves wisdom keeps discipline in his manners. ⟦s3⟧ For he does not love wisdom who does not keep discipline. ⟦s4⟧ Whence: *If you love me, keep my commandments [n: (Joan. XIV)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *She preventeth them that covet her.* ⟦s2⟧ Offering herself freely. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself is *the way, the truth, and the life [n: (Joan. XIV)]*: through him one goes, unto him one strives, to him one attains.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Her sitting by,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Ever ready, that is, to help, whence: *Behold, I stand at the door and knock, if any man shall open to me, I will go in to him and will sup with him, and he with me [n: (Apoc. III)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc. 3:20 *si quis audierit vocem meam, et aperuerit mihi januam, intrabo ad illum*, "if any man shall hear my voice and open to me the door, I will come in to him"]. ⟦s3⟧ In the present life wisdom must be studied, and life led according to the rule of her discipline.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1171B]VERS. 22.-- *If then you be delighted.* [n: (RAB.)] He admonishes kings, etc., *as far as* he shall obtain the crown of the heavenly kingdom.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Neither will I go with pining envy.* ⟦s2⟧ Envy does not have fellowship with wisdom, because through the envy of the devil death entered into the world, and through wisdom the human race is delivered from it.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Receive therefore.* [n: (RAB.)] Wisdom, about to show, etc., *as far as* they are said to be the more perfect and both healing and life-giving.

## CHAPTER VII.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For none of the kings.* [n: (RAB.)] He shows that every man, etc., *as far as* as he is perfect God, so also is Jesus Christ perfect man.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Neither did I compare,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Whence: Having found [1171C] one precious pearl, he sells all things and buys it. ⟦s3⟧ For he who truly finds heavenly wisdom, that is, the word of God, despises all the profits of the present life, philosophy, eloquence, and strives to be at leisure for the divine contemplation.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *There came,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Solomon, having proved this, etc., *as far as* whence also the sun is so named, because it alone among the stars shines in the day.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The guide of wisdom itself.* [var: Vulg. Sap. 7:15 *ipse sapientiae dux est*, "he himself is the guide of wisdom"] [n: (RAB.)] God the Father of the incarnate Son, to whom he gave all power in heaven and on earth.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *For he.* [n: (RAB.)] Of Solomon the Scripture says, God gave, etc., *as far as* for he enlightens every man that cometh into this world, *whom he that followeth walketh not in darkness* [1171D] [n: (Joan. I)]

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22-24.-- *For in her is the spirit of understanding, holy,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] As Isaias shows, all the gifts of the Holy Spirit exist in the humanity of the Saviour, in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge hidden, and the fulness of the Godhead, to whom the spirit is not given by measure. [n: (ID.)] The Spirit who fills all things is said to be at once movable and stable. ⟦s2⟧ For *man* runs about everywhere, everywhere comes to meet one, and suddenly, where he is not thought to be, is found. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore the almighty Spirit, that he may be marked as everywhere present, is said to be at once stable and movable. [n: (ID.)] Whence: *The brightness of glory and the figure of his substance [n: (Heb. I)]*, ruling all things by his word, etc., *as far as* in time the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.

## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1172A]

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *She reacheth from end.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, from the beginning of the world unto the coming of Christ she mightily asserts wondrous works and sincere testimonies through the Old Testament, and from the incarnation of the Word unto the end of the world she sets forth the sweetness of the Gospel. ⟦s3⟧ *From end* therefore *unto end she reacheth mightily,* because she works perfectly everywhere: for "end" signifies perfection.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *She glorifieth the fellowship,* that is, she represents *the nobility of him,* namely of the Father, who is coeternal and co-omnipotent with her, or it may be read partly of the temporal nativity, partly of the eternal. ⟦s2⟧ She has *the fellowship of God.* ⟦s3⟧ That is, the humanity in which God dwells, who is the wisdom of the Father. ⟦s4⟧ Or *she glorifieth the nobility of him,* that is, of God the Father, that is, she is of the same essence [1172B] with the Father, begotten of him from eternity, born in time of a mother.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *For she is the teacher of discipline.* ⟦s2⟧ Of his fulness we all have received: through whom grace and truth was made, that is, revealed. ⟦s3⟧ *No man hath ever seen God, but the Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him [n: (Joan. I)]* [var: Vulg. Joan. 1:18 *unigenitus Filius*, "the only-begotten Son"].

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And her labours.* ⟦s2⟧ For there is nothing slothful in her works. ⟦s3⟧ Whatsoever things are true, whatsoever modest, whatsoever just, she does, and commits to her own to be done. ⟦s4⟧ *For sobriety and wisdom.* [var: Vulg. Sap. 8:7 *sobrietatem enim et prudentiam docet*, "for she teacheth sobriety and prudence"] [n: (RAB.)] He who is temperate, etc., *as far as* and he gave her to Jacob his servant.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Admirable.* ⟦s2⟧ The Virgin's offspring was to all men [1172C] one to be wondered at, because he came forth unto the resurrection and the ruin of many, who stands for a sign of the peoples, over whom kings shall shut their mouth, whom the nations shall beseech.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Entering into the house.* [n: (RAB.)] After preaching, after the labour of work, by which the saints afford comfort to their hearers, returning to themselves they are at leisure for divine contemplation, where they find the rest of the sweetness above. [n: (ID. ex Greg. )] Bodily delights, etc., *as far as* whence David: *Taste and see that he is sweet [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*, etc. [n: (ORIG.)] He who opens the mouth of the dumb, etc., *as far as* but justice, in that he is the distributor and the judge of merits.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And I was a child,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Solomon in his boyhood had asked wisdom of God, and that he might rightly [1172D] judge the people, and discern between good and evil: he was made therefore most wise, and seems to have obtained a good soul, who had a wise and understanding heart. ⟦s2⟧ But how does that agree with him which is said:

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *I came to a body undefiled,* etc., since he loved a woman of a strange nation, and built idols? ⟦s2⟧ It seems therefore to agree rather with the people of the saints, which has been regenerated in baptism, and, steeped in the faith of the Saviour, has received the boyhood of its good disposition and of a simple innocence, and with all its endeavours has striven to come to the summit of the virtues: whence there follows: *And when I was yet more good,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The Father judgeth no man, but hath given all judgment to the Son. ⟦s4⟧ They that imitate him are made his members.
