## CHAPTER IX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1173A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *But thou hast chosen,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] David, because a man, etc., *as far as* the power and the wisdom of God, and the image of the invisible God.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *For what man.* ⟦s2⟧ He wonders at the excellence of God. ⟦s3⟧ As if: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! [n: (Rom. XI.)] And, Who hath known the mind of the Lord, or who hath been his counsellor? [n: (Isa. XL.)]*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *But the sense.* ⟦s2⟧ No man knows the things that are done in the heart of a man but the spirit of a man, and no man knows the things that are of God but the Spirit of God. ⟦s3⟧ But the Spirit searcheth all things, even the deep things of God, who enlightens the hearts of the saints.

## CHAPTER X.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1173B]VERS. 1.-- *She kept him who was first formed.* [n: (RAB.)] The first man, created by the goodness of God, set in paradise, preferred before all living things: he was cast down through the transgression of the commandment into the miseries of this world; and that he was restored through the wisdom of God, that is, through Christ, is a great token of the divine mercy.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *The unjust man in his wrath.* ⟦s2⟧ As Cain, who slew his brother unjustly, the vengeance for which crime was discharged in the flood, and the generation of Cain blotted out: but the wisdom of God preserved Noe, of the stock of Seth, with his sons in the ark, for the restoring of the world.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *She also, in the consent of pride,* etc. [var: Vulg. Sap. 10:5 *in consensu nequitiae*, "in the consent of wickedness"] [n: (RAB.)] The devil through pride, etc., *as far as* the humility of Christ has united.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *She delivered the just man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Hence Peter says: [1173C] The city *of the Sodomites and of the Gomorrhites, reducing it into ashes, he condemned with overthrow; setting them for an example of those that should act impiously, and delivered just Lot, oppressed by the injuries of the wicked, and by their riotous manner of life [n: (II Petr. II)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ Lot's wife, looking back, was turned into a statue of salt, that we who through the grace of God have been called unto life should not look back upon the old man, remaining in the middle of the way. ⟦s4⟧ Turned into a statue of salt she salts others, whence: *Remember Lot's wife [n: (Luc. XVII)]*, that is, that we should not be negligent like fools, but should beware prudently.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For wisdom,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *But she,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Jacob, fleeing from Esau his brother, came to Laban his uncle, and on the way saw in a dream a ladder set up, and the angels of God ascending and [1173D] descending, and the Lord himself leaning upon the ladder and saying to him: *I am the God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac; the land wherein thou sleepest I will give to thee and to thy seed [n: (Gen. XXVIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And coming into Mesopotamia he took Lia and Rachel, for whom he long fed the sheep of Laban. ⟦s5⟧ And Laban wished fraudulently to cheat him of his wages. ⟦s6⟧ But by the wisdom of God Laban was stripped, and Jacob enriched, and returned with much wealth. ⟦s7⟧ But mystically these things pertain to the martyrs, whom the mercy of Christ delivered from their persecutors, strengthening them with the grace of the Holy Spirit and with the hope of the reward to come; and at last, their enemies overcome, it led them through to the kingdom of heaven, to the praise and glory of his own name.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *She forsook not the just man that was sold,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] [1174A] Mystically the Wisdom of God rescued the martyrs out of persecutions, and exalted them in the heavenly kingdom. ⟦s2⟧ Whence they shall come with Christ as judges of their enemies.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *She delivered the just people.* [n: (RAB.)] Mystically. ⟦s2⟧ God delivers his elect from their persecutors, etc., *as far as* to the rewards of the kingdom, where they perpetually sing praises to their deliverer.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Because wisdom,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Without the wisdom of God neither can the human mind worthily think anything, nor the mouth utter it: from him therefore both wisdom and eloquence must be sought.

## CHAPTER XI.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *She hath spoken the works,* etc. [var: Vulg. Sap. 11:1 *Direxit opera eorum*, "she directed their works"] [n: (RAB.)] The Christian people follows prophecy [1174B] through the desert of this world, that it may come to the fatherland of the heavenly kingdom; whence Peter: *We have the prophetic word, whereunto you do well to attend, as to a light shining in a dark place [n: (II Petr. I)]* [var: Vulg. 2 Petr. 1:19 *firmiorem propheticum sermonem*, "the more sure word of prophecy," and no *loco*], etc.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Out of the highest rock,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ And the rock was Christ, who, struck by the rod of the cross, which was in their hands, that is, in the writings of the prophets, poured forth living water.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For by what things they suffered punishments,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The benefits being recalled, etc., *as far as* whether therefore they suffer, or not, it is glory to them.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *For when they were tried.* [n: (RAB.)] God chastised the sinning Israelites with worthy plagues, and comforted them when they repented, the Egyptians and the Chanaanites, as unworthy of mercy, he destroyed. ⟦s2⟧ *Torments,* etc. [1174C] From Pharao, that is, wheresoever they were. ⟦s3⟧ Or, not so much were these punished whom the Israelites destroyed with a recent slaughter through the several cities; but all the Chanaanites also who heard of the victory of the people of God; whence Rahab says: *Your terror is fallen upon us [n: (Jos. II)]*, etc. ⟦s4⟧ But mystically, the Jews, who despised the incarnation of the Son of God and his preaching, wandering over the whole earth and as fugitives, are afflicted with divers slaughters. ⟦s5⟧ But the Gentiles, converted to the faith and receiving a father's correction, do not depart from the mercy of God: so that they who had been at the head are at the tail, and the contrary.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *A double,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The remembrance of past evils is not lessened, and the tempest of present ones is increased. ⟦s3⟧ Whence neither have they hope of a remedy, because, feigning penance, they are not zealous for amendment; [1174D] whence: *When he slew them, they sought him, and they loved him [n: (Psal. LXXVII)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 77:34 *quaerebant eum et revertebantur, et diluculo veniebant ad eum*, "they sought him and returned, and came to him early in the morning"], etc.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Not in like manner as the just,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because they do not sin in like manner. ⟦s3⟧ For the just man falls seven times, and rises again: but the wicked, when he has come into the depth of evils, contemneth.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *For that some, going astray,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] It was fitting, etc., *as far as* for their whole intention is to persecute the human race, and to torment it in all ways.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *But all things in measure,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Because all things according to truth, etc., *as far as* when the hidden things of darkness shall be enlightened, and the counsels of hearts shall be revealed.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1175A]VERS. 23.-- *The weight of a balance,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Isaias says the like: *Behold the nations are as a drop of a bucket, and are reckoned as the weight of a balance [n: (Isa. XL)]*; for he would have it understood that the mind, and the reason, and the sense of the Lord, through whom all things were made and without whom nothing was made, is he of whom it is said: *By the word of the Lord the heavens were established [n: (Psal. XXXII)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ *All the nations which have not known their creator,* in comparison of God *are as a drop of a bucket, and as the weight of a balance* which is lightly turned down, *and as a drop of the morning dew [n: (Sap. XI)]*, which is dried up at the first sight of the sun. ⟦s4⟧ Or, as the drop of a bucket, if it flows away it is despised or neglected by him that carries it: so the multitude of the nations, compared with the heavenly splendour, with the mysteries above, and with the multitude of angels, is reckoned as nothing.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1175B]VERS. 25.-- *For thou lovest,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ A good workman both loves and rules his own work: and if a man sometimes sins, he bears with him in much patience, and through penance turns him to better things. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *He maketh his sun to rise upon the good and the bad, and raineth upon the just and the unjust [n: (Matth. V)]*; but the impenitent are condemned by a just judgment: *For the Lord is just, and hath loved justice [n: (Psal. X)]*.
