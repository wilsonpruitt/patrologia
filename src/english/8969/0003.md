## CHAPTER XII.

*O how good and sweet,* etc. Through those whom thou fillest, or through others whom thou shalt will; whence: *He shall convince the world of sin, and of justice, and of judgment [n: (Joan. XVI)]*.

VERS. 3.-- *For those ancient inhabitants.* [n: (RAB.)] He speaks historically, etc., *as far as* the virtues, with [1175C] the Holy Spirit helping, shall bring forth useful fruits.

VERS. 12.-- *For who shall say,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He shows by manifest reason that the Creator is not to be contradicted, if he disposes his own creature according to his own will: because all things are placed in his will, and there is none who can resist him. *He is just in all his ways [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 144:17 *Justus Dominus in omnibus viis suis*, "the Lord is just in all his ways"]; whence there follows, *Since therefore thou art just [n: (Rom. IX)]*, etc.

VERS. 16.-- *For thy power,* etc. Because the judgment of God, whether in the correction of the elect or in the damnation of the wicked, is the perfection of the highest equity: and therefore through his goodness, by long deferring, he suspends judgment, that he may have the more to whom he may render a benefit. For when a creature raises itself against the Creator it is punished with a just vengeance, that it may know itself the lower. [1175D] These things may be referred to the passion of Christ, whom the Father delivered unto death for us, and made him, who had not known sin, to be sin for us: who, unless he had undergone an undeserved death, would not have freed us from the death we deserved. The Father therefore, being just, in punishing the just one disposes all things justly. Almighty God judges all things with the highest tranquillity: yet human affections and members are ascribed to him by a figurative manner of speech, that the divine utterances may be conformed to our capacity. For there are read in the Scriptures: the eyes of the Lord, ears, hands, mouth, feet, anger, fury, and the like, whereas a simple nature never admits variety, nor true unity division. But one and the same works manifold and diverse things: Whence: *Thou shalt change them, and [1176A] they shall be changed: but thou art the selfsame, and thy years [n: (Psal. CI)]*, etc. And elsewhere: *I am God, and I change not [n: (Mal. III)]* [var: Vulg. Mal. 3:6 *Ego enim Dominus, et non mutor*, "for I am the Lord, and I change not"]. And elsewhere: *With whom there is no change [n: (Jac. I)]*, etc. And thou hast taught thy people: through the wondrous works of God the faithful are instructed, that they be not entangled with the wicked; but that they may study to keep themselves undefiled by confessing, and by hoping in the Lord, and may not neglect the time of penance.

VERS. 15.-- *Since therefore,* etc. Whence: *Many are the scourges of the sinner, but mercy shall encompass him that hopeth in the Lord [n: (Psal. XXXI)]*. There is a great distance between the judgment of the elect and that of the reprobate. Those the Lord chastises, that he may amend them; these pay the penalties of pride and of perfidy, that they may know that they cannot fight back against the ruler of all. Happy the judge who always looks upon the mercy and the goodness [1176B] of his own judge. *For judgment without mercy to him that hath not done mercy. [n: (Jac. II)]*.

VERS. 23.-- *Senselessly,* etc., that they who would not acknowledge and honour the Creator, being worthily delivered up to a reprobate sense, should do those things which are not fitting, dishonouring their own bodies in themselves: *Because they changed the truth of God into a lie, and served the creature rather than the Creator [n: (Rom. I)]*.

VERS. 26.-- *But they that by mockeries,* etc. That is, they who, being scourged by God, were not corrected, have experienced a condemnation fully deserved: to this end God chastises, that he may amend. But he who, ungrateful and impatient, murmurs, is led from temporal torment to eternal.

VERS. 27.-- *For in these things,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The Gentile world, [1176C] while it served idols, etc., *as far as* he inflicts eternal punishment upon the uncorrected.

## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Knowledge.* Fear, that is, and reverence. Whence: Behold the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom, and to depart from evil is understanding. For he departs from evil who departs from the worship of idols: he fears God who keeps his commandments: because *The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom. A good understanding to all that do it [n: (Psal. CX)]*. *From these* works, that is, of man, they were able to know the good workman, who properly is, because he is unchangeable and always the same, always abiding in himself, and rules all things. Most vain therefore is the vanity of men, who venerate creatures instead of the Creator. *Nor [1176D] by the works.* Some worshipped fire, which they called Vulcan; others the ether or the air, which they called Jupiter and Juno; others the excess of water, that is, Neptune; others the circle of the stars, men who distributed the positions and the course of the stars among the various figures of animals; others the sun, which they called Phoebus; others the moon, that is, Diana. [n: (RAB.)] They sin in two ways, etc., *as far as* in which the covetousness of man is condemned.

VERS. 6.-- *But yet,* etc. Hence Paul says: *For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all the impiety of those who detain the truth of God in injustice; because that which is known of God is manifest in them [n: (Rom. I)]* [var: Vulg. Rom. 1:18 *super omnem impietatem, et injustitiam hominum eorum*, "against all the impiety and injustice of those men"], etc. The knowledge of God is perceived from the fabric of the world. For that the invisible God might be known from invisible things [cj: *invisibilibus*; read *visibilibus*, "from visible things"], [1177A] a work was made by him which should manifest the workman, that he should be understood to be the God of all who made this thing, which others cannot make. But *when they had known him, they glorified him not as God, but became vain in their thoughts* [n: (RAB.)]

VERS. 10.-- *Unhappy,* etc. They, that is, who honour gods that they may drink in gold with their eyes, and the sheen of polished marble or of ivory, or garments notable with gems and with colours: and the more adorned the temples are, and the fairer the images, the more of majesty they have, or are said to have. Religion therefore is, or is believed to be, that which human covetousness admires.

VERS. 11.-- *Or if any artificer.* [n: (RAB.)] Wisdom insults over these, etc., *as far as Whose God is their belly, and their glory is in their confusion [n: (Philip. III)]*.
