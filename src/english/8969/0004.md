## CHAPTER XIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1177B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Again, another purposing to sail,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ These things also are said against idolatry. ⟦s3⟧ For it is the greatest madness that a man brought forth to the image of God, having from him a reasonable mind, by which he devised how he might cross the waves, building a ship according to the skill of his art, should not honour him who gave him this knowledge, and should call upon an idol which he has formed by a like art and with a greater vanity. ⟦s4⟧ For since he who has made is greater than that which is made, to adore one's own handiwork is to be mad. ⟦s5⟧ From God the course of sailing must be sought, from whom is the equipping of the ship. ⟦s6⟧ But heretics, who feign the vain images of their errors, perish in the waves of the world for this cause, that they have forsaken the ship of the Church, [1177C] and God the pilot.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *For thou hast given.* ⟦s2⟧ The power of God led the Israelites through the Red Sea dry-shod, and divided the waters of the Jordan, that it might strike terror into the nations that were to be destroyed, for the faithful also, amid the waves and the persecutions of the world, it prepares a path of faith and of constancy, that they may pass over unhurt, nor is there need of human solace where God shows the wondrous effects of his power.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *But that they might not be void,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Creator gave knowledge to his creature, whereby it might take counsel for itself in the present, that it might serve his will. ⟦s3⟧ Whence he commanded Noe to make an ark, in which he himself and his offspring should be saved, and the rest of the living creatures reserved for the restoring of the world, when the human race for its iniquity [1177D] should be blotted out by the flood; whence Peter: *Wherein a few, that is, eight souls, were saved by water: which also we now, of like form, baptism hath saved [n: (Petr. III)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Petr. 3:21 *Quod et vos nunc*, "which also you now"], etc. ⟦s4⟧ The ark, built of planed timbers, signifies the Church, which is made of the gathering of the faithful, by the craftsmanship of the preachers. ⟦s5⟧ A few, that is, eight souls, were saved by water while the world perished: because in comparison with the reprobate the number of the elect is small. ⟦s6⟧ For they are few who find the way of life. ⟦s7⟧ The water of the flood slew those placed outside, because, though a heretic have the sacrament of baptism, those very waters drown him to hell which lifted the ark to heaven. ⟦s8⟧ The number eight also signifies the Church, in that in the sacrament of the Lord's resurrection it receives the laver [1178A] of baptism. ⟦s9⟧ That, as he himself rose from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we also, washed with the water of regeneration, may walk in newness of life. ⟦s10⟧ Hence Jeremias: *Every man is become a fool by his own knowledge, the maker is confounded in the graven thing: because his founder is a liar, and there is no spirit in them. ⟦s11⟧ For their works are vain, and worthy of laughter, and in the time of visitation they shall perish [n: (Jerem. X)]* [var: Vulg. Jer. 10:14 *confusus est artifex omnis in sculptili, quoniam falsum est quod conflavit*, "every artificer is confounded in the graven thing, for what he hath cast is false"].

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And he that made it.* ⟦s2⟧ The image and the worship of false gods. ⟦s3⟧ For the devil shall be punished on this account, that he usurps to himself divine honour; and man too, who honours the creature instead of the Creator. ⟦s4⟧ And wood often signifies man. ⟦s5⟧ Blessed therefore is the man who speaks and does the truth. ⟦s6⟧ But the heretic, who worships his own fictions, is accursed, and given over to perdition, and so is his work, that is, his doctrine.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1178B]VERS. 11.-- *For this cause.* ⟦s2⟧ There is no pardon to be given to idols, or to those that dwell in them, that is, to demons, because they draw back from him men made to the image of God, and overthrow them in the snare of error. ⟦s3⟧ Heretics also, who seduce men, shall not escape a strict judgment.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The beginning.* ⟦s2⟧ For it is the worst kind of fornication, by which the soul departs from God and commits fornication with idols, or forsakes the truth of the divine teaching and follows the falsehood of heretical depravity, or of opinion. ⟦s3⟧ Hence Paul calls covetousness the service of idols. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it is plain that every evil concupiscence is called fornication, by which the soul, having left the higher law, is corrupted by the pleasure of lower things, being laid low or prostituted for the base wage, as it were, of natures.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [1178C]VERS. 13.-- *For not,* etc. [var: Vulg. Sap 14:13 *neque enim*] *May the Lord destroy all deceitful lips, and all that speak lies [n: (Psal. XI)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 11:4 *et linguam magniloquam*, "and the tongue that speaketh proud things"; the second clause here is Ps. 5:7, *perdes omnes qui loquuntur mendacium*]. ⟦s3⟧ Hence Jeremias: *Let the gods that have not made heaven and earth perish from the earth [n: (Jer. XI)]*, etc.; [n: (LACT. t. I)]. ⟦s4⟧ Because of the folly of men, etc., *as far as* they bestowed divine honours upon their parents, and commanded them to be bestowed.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *The incommunicable name.* ⟦s2⟧ The incommunicable name, that is, of almighty God; who alone is incomprehensible, they ascribed to senseless and frail matter, or to an image.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *For either they sacrifice their own sons.* ⟦s2⟧ As in the rites of Saturn, to whom, because of his hatred of Jupiter, without regard of piety [1178D] they immolated their own little ones, and to Bellona the priests themselves sacrificed with their own blood. ⟦s3⟧ Whence Quintilian: That, says he, if God compels it, he is angry: and if he is angry, he does not accept your sacrifices. ⟦s4⟧ *Or dark sacrifices.* ⟦s5⟧ As they did in the rites of Isis of Egypt, and of Ceres of Eleusis. ⟦s6⟧ For as there Osiris is sought with the wailing of his mother, so here Proserpine, carried off, is sought for the incestuous marriage of her uncle Pluto. ⟦s7⟧ And because Ceres is said to have sought her in Sicily with torches kindled from Aetna, therefore her rites were celebrated with the tossing of burning brands. ⟦s8⟧ In like manner they rave in the rites of father Liber, and of the great mother, where after the example of the gods (who, being sated in the festivity, spent the whole night in debaucheries) they play. ⟦s9⟧ Because by lust and by uncleanness they thought to please their own gods.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1179A]VERS. 25.-- *All things mingled together.* ⟦s2⟧ For all things were confused: there was no reason there, no true religion. ⟦s3⟧ The more wicked a man was, the more he thought he pleased his own god.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *For of the unspeakable.* ⟦s2⟧ There is no greater madness than to forsake the living God and to serve the dead. ⟦s3⟧ For when the mind turns itself away from its creator, it is entangled in all crimes. ⟦s4⟧ For *the beginning* of *all sin is pride [n: (Eccli. X)]*. ⟦s5⟧ *The end.* ⟦s6⟧ The consummation and the fulness. ⟦s7⟧ For as to those who worship an idol instead of God, all things in them are reputed unto sin.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *For either they are mad while they rejoice.* ⟦s2⟧ Because, while they swear, they defile the name of God, who says: *Thou shalt not take the name of thy God in vain, and by the name of strange gods you shall not swear [n: (Exod. XX)]* [var: Vulg. Exod. 20:7 *nomen Domini Dei tui*, "the name of the Lord thy God"; the second clause is Exod. 23:13, *per nomen externorum deorum non jurabitis*]; but when they think that they mock [1179B] God, they cast themselves headlong into everlasting death. ⟦s3⟧ But heretics, worshipping the images of their own errors, become the cause of their own perdition, whose doctrine also is false, their life unjust, their joy insane, their death unspeakable: because, when they swear unjustly by an idol, and do not hope in their own God, therefore they shall receive the vengeance of their own deserts. ⟦s4⟧ Whence there follows: *For the power is not of them that swear.* [var: Vulg. Sap. 14:31 *Non enim juratorum virtus*, "for it is not the power of the things sworn by"]
