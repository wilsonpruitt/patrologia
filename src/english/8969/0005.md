## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- *But thou,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] For those condemned by a just judgment, repenting too late, acknowledge what before they were unwilling to believe; whence: *The Lord shall be known when he executeth judgments, the sinner hath been caught in the works of his own hands [n: (Psal. IX)]*; whence John: *He that hath hope in him sanctifieth, even as he also is holy: [1179C] and every one that abideth in him sinneth not [n: (I Joan. III)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Joan. 3:3 *sanctificat se*, "sanctifieth himself"].

VERS. 3.-- *For to know thee,* etc. It is the receiving of incorruption, and in the world to come of contemplation; whence: *For this is life everlasting, that they may know thee the true God [n: (Joan. XVII)]* [var: Vulg. Joan. 17:3 *ut cognoscant te, solum Deum verum*, "that they may know thee, the only true God"], etc.

VERS. 4.-- *For not into error.* He speaks in the person of the faithful, who, fortified by the grace of God, did not mingle themselves with the iniquity or the idolatry of the Gentiles: because that labour is without the fruit of justice, and deserves everlasting death.

VERS. 6.-- *Lovers of evil things,* etc. *Let them that make them become like unto them, and all such as trust in them [n: (Psal. CXXXIV)]*. The inventors also of false doctrines, and their worshippers, are worthy of death. For God shall destroy the man that shall have done these things, that is, the master and [1179D] his disciple. Above he reproved idols of wood, of stone, or of brass, now he condemns also those of clay. For it was justly requited to them; that they who were made of earth, because they were unwilling to follow the better part of themselves, that is, the soul, created to the image of God for the marking of the living God; should, by imitating the baser part of themselves, that is, the earthly body, adore a god of mud, and, without the fruit of good work, should be rendered senseless and useless to the insensible earth out of which they were taken. But mystically, heretics and schismatics, who detain the truth of God in injustice, and worship a lie for the truth in the images of their own teaching, although they cloak it with eloquence or with a feigned virtue, yet the things they feign are frail and of clay.

[1180A]VERS. 10.-- *Their heart is ashes,* [var: Vulg. Sap. 15:10 *Cinis est enim cor ejus*, "for his heart is ashes," in the singular] and their thought is foul, and their life viler than mire; because they know not their maker, and become vain in their own thoughts. But they despise the understanding of the holy fathers, and feign new errors, like vain games, for human favour and earthly gain; erring, that is, and sending others into error, that they may be inexcusable: when God shall judge the hidden things of hearts, and shall render to every one according to his works.

VERS. 13.-- *For he knoweth,* etc. Heretics have no excuse; because they sin knowingly, while they will not follow the sound faith and right doctrine: following rather the earthly and frail understanding than the spiritual.

VERS. 17.-- *He is better.* Because man is better [1180B] than idols by the reckoning of creation; because he lives and understands, but they never do. But because he follows the vanity of error rather than the knowledge of truth, *when he is in honour he understandeth not: and is compared to senseless beasts [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 48:21 *cum in honore esset, non intellexit. Comparatus est jumentis insipientibus*, in the past tense].

VERS. 18.-- *Senseless.* As if: neither had they discernment. For in truth living things are better than dead, things that feel than things that feel not, rational than irrational. But it was fitting that they who were ignorant of the Creator should not know the distinction among creatures. For he who neglects to have the praise and blessing of God in himself shows that he himself errs in the discernment of animals.

## CHAPTER XVI.

[1180C]VERS. 2.-- *Instead of which,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The people of Israel in the desert lusted after flesh, etc., *as far as* therefore they were justly delivered over to them to be tormented.

VERS. 6.-- *But not for ever.* [n: (RAB.)] For not the brazen serpent, etc., *as far as* because, dead through his humanity, he is as it were brazen through his Divinity.

VERS. 9.-- *For them,* etc. The elect are proved through affliction, and are warned to beware of sins and to do good things. The reprobate are scattered, because they are not corrected. Therefore little creatures kill idolaters. But serpents cannot destroy the Israelites, the worshippers of God; because the Lord knoweth how to deliver the godly out of temptation: but to reserve the unjust unto the day of judgment to be tormented.

VERS. 12.-- *But thy word, O Lord.* That is, [1180D] the Son, through whom are all things, whose power is everywhere, who has done all things whatsoever he willed; he heals bodies, he saves souls from spiritual beasts.

VERS. 14.-- *But man killeth.* [n: (CHRYS.)] Man through malice, etc., *as far as* he is free from every crime.

VERS. 16.-- *For denying to know,* etc. [var: Vulg. Sap. 16:16 *Negantes enim te nosse impii*, "for the wicked, denying to know thee"] [n: (RAB.)] The Egyptians were afflicted with divers plagues, etc., *as far as* because all things consent to the will of the Creator.

VERS. 18.-- *For at one time.* [n: (RAB.)] As is read in Genesis: The Lord rained upon Sodom and Gomorrha fire and brimstone, that he might destroy the impious: and in Exodus, hail together with fire. In the book of Numbers: He sent fiery serpents against [1181A] the murmurers, that the impious might know that every creature is ready to avenge the injury of the Creator.

VERS. 20.-- *Instead of which,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In the desert manna was given to the children of Israel, etc., *as far as Many are the scourges of the sinner, but mercy shall encompass him that hopeth in the Lord [n: (Psal. XXXI)]*.

VERS. 24.-- *For the creature,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Let sinners be confounded here: who, whereas the other creatures serve their maker, themselves fight against him. Whence they are scourged by the concord of all creatures: and if they shall not have repented, they shall be tormented perpetually; but for the good, true good things are prepared.

VERS. 25.-- *For this cause,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] God rained manna from heaven, etc., *as far as* as a transgressor of the law he shall perish for ever.

[1181B]VERS. 28.-- *That it might be known to all,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] That food fed them historically, etc., *as far as* but at the rising of the sun, that is, of Christ, let us say: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.*

## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 1.-- *For great.* [n: (RAB.)] Mystically. All the persecutors of the Church of Christ suffer the darkness of their own crimes, nor can they ever be secure, whom the rulers of these darknesses harass, even unto the exterior darkness, where there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth; whence: *Every one that doth evil hateth the light [n: (Joan. III)]*, etc. Hence Paul: *You were heretofore darkness, but now light in the Lord [n: (Ephes. V)]*, etc.

VERS. 4.-- *For neither.* For horror always [1181C] accompanies darkness: and the less a man discerns the things that are about him, the more he fears, trembling at adversities on every side. So the Egyptians for three days suffered the greatest darkness, which neither sun nor stars were able to lighten, and wherever [sic: *unicunque*] they lay they trembled with excessive terror; but as to what he says. That *sad apparitions* terrified them, it could be that demons obtruded certain phantasms, who would terrify them for the increase of their punishments. The persecutors also of the faith and of the truth [sic: *vertitatis*] the darkness of sins encompasses, because they know not the sun of justice, nor do preachers enlighten them with the brightness of doctrine. These also *a sound coming down,* that is, a heavenly threatening, disturbs, and *sad apparitions,* that is, demons, who shall one day torture them, terrify; nor yet do they perform penance fruitfully: but despairing they treasure up [1181D] to themselves wrath in the day of judgment.

VERS. 6.-- *Full of fear.* Because on every side they dreaded punishments. For the heavier the pains are, the greater the horrors that are feared.

*And of the magic art,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] When the magicians in Egypt, etc., *as far as* and the old serpent with his angels, a savage enemy, patiently.

VERS. 9.-- *And the air,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He marks the excess of fear which they suffer when punishments are imminent and conscience accuses them, but since wickedness is always fearful, it gives a token of its own dread, and is condemned to punishment.

VERS. 11.-- *A betraying,* etc. Fear, that is, betrays the weakness of the proud man, who with a vain hope of helps [1182A] exalts himself in his thinking, when he hopes that he can do what he cannot, or could not, accomplish: and while his strength is the less, the force of the things which he suffers from without makes him think the power to be greater, this happens frequently in the persecutors of the faithful, who reckon themselves powerful when they torment the saints, overcome by their power, saddened by the straitness of their mind, they acknowledge that they cannot do what they desired, and that the fortitude of God is greater, who thus conquers in his own soldiers.

VERS. 13.-- *But they,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The Egyptians, that is, encompassed with the horror of the darkness, etc., *as far as* they dreaded it as dangerous.

VERS. 19.-- *For the whole world.* As if: the impious were in darkness, but wherever the children of Israel dwelt, there was light.

[1182B]VERS. 20.-- *An image of darkness.* Spiritual darkness is heavier than bodily: because it leads men to the exterior darkness, where there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, which the Egyptian darkness prefigures. For as bodies there are useful for no good work: so here also souls together with bodies are condemned to an everlasting prison; where there is neither reason, nor place, nor time for working.
