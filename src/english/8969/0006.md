## CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *But to thy holy ones [sic: Santis].* [n: (RAB.)] The Israelites in Egypt, etc., *as far as* he protects them in prosperity and in adversity.

VERS. 4.-- *Worthy indeed,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Deservedly did the Egyptians suffer the punishment of darkness, etc., *as far as* the reason of which mystery was prefigured in the ancient fathers, and [1182C] was fulfilled in the coming of Christ.

VERS. 9.-- *For they sacrificed secretly.* [n: (RAB.)] The people of Israel in Egypt celebrated the Pasch at the command of the Lord, etc., *as far as* but in the hiding of the heart they always sacrifice to the Lord, whence: *A sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit [n: (Psal. L)]*. And elsewhere: *In me, O God, are thy vows [n: (Psal. LV)]*, etc. [n: (ID.)] He records that God smote the firstborn of Egypt, where the Egyptians did not dissemble as before, but openly compelled the people of God to go out, saying: *We shall all die [n: (Exod. XI)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *Thy almighty word.* The Son, of whom it is said: *In the beginning was the Word [n: (Joan. I)]*, etc., he is here named the arm and the right hand: because of the effective power of the divine strength, he then through an angel's [1182D] ministry executed his judgments upon the firstborn of the Egyptians; and he afterwards sanctified to himself the firstborn of the Hebrews. Now also through the water of baptism he saves the multitude of believers, and slays the multitude of spiritual enemies.

VERS. 19.-- *Visions.* [n: (RAB.)] This belongs to the divine goodness, which chastises sinners that they may abstain and do penance. But if they abuse the patience of God, they shall be punished for ever without excuse.

VERS. 20.-- *But then it touched the just also.* [n: (RAB.)] He records, etc., *as far as* through whom we may be able to escape destruction.

VERS. 24.-- *For in the robe reaching to the feet.* [n: (RAB.)] The Hebrews assign fine linen to the earth, etc., *as far as* in the pontifical figure, [1183A] it foreknew Christ who was to come, but because it had seen him clothed with flesh, it believed him to be not God but mere man; whence: *If they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory [n: (I Cor. II)]*.

## CHAPTER XIX.

*But upon the wicked,* etc. Upon the enemies of the people of God: vengeance came even unto the end, because they corrected neither their life nor their manners, for with feigned arguments they counterfeited penance, and therefore did not deserve indulgence. Whence Pharao, when, afflicted with plagues, he promised that he would let the people of God go, taking breath a little, returned to his domestic cruelty, until he was drowned in the waves of the Red Sea. For the persecutors of the Christian name, if they shall have persisted in their malice, [1183B] shall feel in punishment how much the justice of God avails.

VERS. 8.-- *Seeing thy wonderful works.* So it behoves us, rejoicing, to give thanks to our deliverer, saying: *Bless the Lord, O my soul [n: (Psal. CIII)]*. Whence the Apostle: *Pray without ceasing, in all things give thanks [n: (I Thess. V)]*.

VERS. 10.-- *The earth brought forth.* [var: Vulg. Sap. 19:10 *eduxit terra muscas*, "the earth brought out flies"] Note that for the enemies of God the earth brought forth flies, and the river frogs; but for the worshippers of God the earth gave fruit, and the sea [1184A] the nourishment of flesh. For it is fitting that the divine providence should provide nourishment for its faithful through its own works. But that it should inflict a fully deserved torment upon its enemies through the creature that is subject to it.

*For they suffered justly.* [n: (RAB.)] They who the ancient servants of God, etc., *as far as* he does not acknowledge the way of truth, and does not find the door which is Christ.

VERS. 17.-- *In themselves the elements.* As in an organ different sounds agree by tempering to the concord of a melody, so the discordant elements, the law of nature being preserved, are bent to the will of the Creator: for he founded a creature not contrary to himself, but agreeable in all things to his own will. *Whence it may be reckoned,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] For men and beasts, etc., *as far as* that it might appear that the elements of the world served not only their own condition, [1184B] but the will of the Creator.

VERS. 20.-- *For in all things thou hast magnified.* The Lord is merciful and compassionate, and is always present to his faithful, whom he always protects and rules: whom sometimes he purges in martyrdom. Whence: *The furnace trieth the potter's vessels, and the trial of tribulation just men [n: (Eccl. XXVII)]*. For many are the tribulations of the just, and out of them all the Lord hath delivered them.
