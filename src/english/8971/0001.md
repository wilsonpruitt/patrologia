## Prologue on the Commendation of Holy Scripture (cont.)


And an example of these four senses can be set forth in this word Jerusalem, which according to the literal sense signifies a certain city, which was once the metropolis in the kingdom of Judaea, which was first founded by Melchisedech, afterwards spread abroad and fortified by Solomon. But according to the moral sense it signifies the faithful soul, according to which sense it is said in the fifty-second chapter of Isaiah: « Arise, sit, Jerusalem, » etc. But according to the allegorical sense it signifies the Church militant, according to which it is said in the twenty-first chapter of the Apocalypse: « I saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven, prepared by God, as a bride adorned for her husband. » But according to the [0029A] anagogical sense it signifies the Church triumphant, according to what is said in the fourth chapter of Galatians: « That Jerusalem which is above is free, which is our mother, » And just as the example has been set forth in one word, so it could be set forth in one sentence: and as in one, so also in others. Of this book, then, containing several senses under one letter, it is said in the second chapter of Ezekiel: « Behold a hand sent to me, in which was a book rolled up, and he spread it out before me, which was written within and without. » And in Apocalypse « five: I saw in the right hand of him that sat upon the throne a book written within and without. » The outer writing is the literal sense, which is more evident, because it is signified immediately by the words: but the inner writing is the mystical or spiritual sense, which is more hidden, because it is designated by the things signified by the words, as was said before. And so the third is plain.

Concerning the fourth, in which saving efficacy is marked, when it is said, *of life,* it must be considered that, as was said [0029B] at the beginning, no other writing, except this one, leads immediately to the blessed life, which alone is life simply, because it knows no death. Therefore the Saviour, in the fifth chapter of John, speaking of the efficacy of this Holy Scripture, said to the Jews learned in the law: « Search [0030A] the Scriptures, in which you think you have eternal life. » Concerning which it must be known that this word, *you think,* which imports an opinion of what is false, or at least a doubt about what is true, is not referred to Holy Scripture itself, which contains nothing false, as is plain from what was said above; nor is there any doubt either that it leads to the blessed life, since it is a certain instrument for arriving at eternal felicity, as is plain from the words of blessed Augustine brought forward above: but it is referred to the men learned in the law who expounded Holy Scripture badly and lived badly, for which things they were deservedly to be deprived of the attainment of eternal beatitude, which is the end of this science, which those without doubt attain who understand it soundly, and live piously according to its sound understanding, according to what is said in the twenty-fourth chapter of Ecclesiasticus: « They that explain me shall have eternal life. » Now Holy Scripture is explained in its true exposition, and in holy working; because this Scripture, as being more eminent than all [0030B] others, contains the perfections of practical and speculative science; and so through the knowledge of the true and the working of the good it leads to the blessed life, which may he grant us who lives and reigns without end.
