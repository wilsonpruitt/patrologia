## Prologue on the Intention of the Author (cont.)

The fifth rule is about times; which occurs in four ways. In one way through synecdoche, when [0032C] a part of the time is put for the whole, as it is said in the Gospel that Christ lay three days in the sepulchre: and yet the first day and the third were not whole. In another way on account of the fractions of time, which are sometimes counted in Scripture, and sometimes omitted: And according to this, Holy Scripture, speaking of some number of years in several places, sometimes in one place puts more years, counting the aforesaid fractions; but in another place puts fewer, omitting the said fractions. In the third way it occurs in this, that the reckoning of the years begins in one place from an earlier term, in another from a later, as in Gen. XV it was said to Abraham: « Thy seed shall be a sojourner four hundred years. » And in Exod. XII it is said of this sojourning that the dwelling of the children of Israel in the land of Egypt was of four hundred and thirty years, because the reckoning of this greater number begins from the time when it was said to Abraham, Gen XII: « Go forth out of thy country, » etc. But the reckoning of the lesser number begins from the birth of Isaac, which was thirty years after Abraham's going out from Haran, as will be seen more fully below at Exod. [0032D] XII. In the fourth way it occurs, in that Holy Scripture speaks of the future by way of the past, as in Isa. IX: « A child is born to us, » etc., and this is to denote the certainty of the prophecy, whose outcome regarding the future is as certain as if it had already passed. And this is on account of the certainty of the divine foreknowledge, from which the revelation is made to the prophet himself. It must be noted, however, that such a manner of speaking has no place except for the prophecy of predestination, which is when some contingent future thing is revealed to the prophet in the way in which it is in the foreknowledge of God, which infallibly beholds future things as present and past: but in the prophecy of threatening it has no place, which is when some punishment to be inflicted on a people, or on some person, is revealed to the prophet, not according as it is in God's foreknowledge, but according to the order of secondary causes, as for instance according to the demerits of men, as is that prophecy of Jon. III: « Yet forty days and Nineveh shall be overthrown; » [0033A] for the sins of that city deserved this overthrow. Nevertheless, because such a cause is changeable, therefore the effect sometimes does not follow: as in the case in hand, because the Ninevites did penance, and so the Lord did not inflict the threatened punishment.

The sixth rule is about recapitulation and anticipation. For in Holy Scripture deeds and events are not always written in the same order in which they happen: and therefore when later things are placed first, it is called anticipation: but when the converse happens, it is called recapitulation, as in Gen. X it is said of the sons of Noe: « By these were divided the islands of the nations in their regions, every one according to his tongue, » etc. And below in the same chapter: « These are the sons of Cham in their kindreds and tongues and generations, » etc. And afterwards in chapter XI it is said: « And the earth was of one lip, and of the same speech. » From which it is plain that what is said beforehand of the division of tongues is said by anticipation. Likewise in Gen. II, after Moses in chapter I had described the creation of heaven and earth, and the distinction [0033B] and adornment of the parts of the world, it is said: [0034A] « These are the generations of heaven and earth in the day in which they were created. » From which it is plain that this is said by recapitulation.

The seventh rule is about the devil and his body. For according to what blessed Gregory says in hom. 10, the devil is surely the head of all the wicked, and the members of this head are all the wicked. And therefore, on account of the connexion of the head with the members, Scripture, speaking of the one, passes in the same context to speaking of the other. As Isa. chap. XIV, speaking of the king of Babylon, who was a member of the devil, passes to speaking of the prince of the demons, when there is added in the same place: « How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, who didst rise in the morning? » And Ezech. XXVIII, speaking of the prince of Tyre, passes to speaking of the devil, when there is added: « Thou the seal of resemblance, full of wisdom, perfect in beauty, wast in the pleasures of the paradise of God. » These rules or keys for expounding Holy Scripture having therefore been set down beforehand, with confidence in the divine help let us descend to the letter [0034B] to be expounded.
