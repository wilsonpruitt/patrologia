## §0 · Two printings; the English follows the twin (= the archive printing)

The same situation as 8971 (see `src/english/8971/cruces-0000.md` §0). The Gallica and archive
copies are different settings: the PROLOGUS SECUNDUS subtitle is italic on Gallica and in small
capitals on the archive. Our twin matches the archive printing at every disagreement below. **The
English follows the twin.** Patches are filed only where the twin departs from both printings.

## §1 · Conventions (shared with 8971)

- Book-title preamble *PROLOGUS SECUNDUS. De intentione auctoris et modo procedendi.* → "SECOND
  PROLOGUE. On the intention of the author and the manner of proceeding." Head → "Prologue on the
  Intention of the Author".
- *Rabbi Salomon* → **Rabbi Solomon** (brief §8; not glossed as Rashi in the text).
- *doctores catholici / Hebraici* → "catholic doctors / the Hebrew ones"; *expositores catholici et
  Hebraei* → "catholic and Hebrew expositors".
- *puncta* → "points" (the correctors' punctuation marks); *versus* → "verses".
- *regulae … claves* → "rules … keys". *Prima … Septima regula* → "The first … The seventh rule":
  **the ordinal chain is preserved one for one**, 1 to 7, each opening its own paragraph as Migne
  sets it. In chunk 0000 *Prima est* has no *regula*, and the English follows ("The first is").
- The three causes of obscurity keep Lyra's *partim … partim … partim* as "partly … partly …
  partly": (1) *scriptorum vitio* (scribes); (2) *imperitia aliquorum correctorum*, which covers
  both points wrongly placed AND verses begun or ended wrongly (one *partim*, two *qui* clauses joined
  by *et*); (3) *ex modo translationis nostrae*. A fourth obscuring cause opens the next paragraph as
  a separate head (*Sciendum etiam … propter modum exponendi communiter traditum*), and its structure
  is kept.
- The fifth rule's four modes: *Uno modo … Alio modo … Tertio modo … Quarto modo* → "In one way … In
  another way … In the third way … In the fourth way". Preserved.
- *secundum quid* → "in a certain respect" (scholastic technical sense, against *simpliciter* =
  "simply").
- *prophetia praedestinationis / comminationis* → "prophecy of predestination / of threatening".
- Pattern 17: *super vos Medos* (plural) → "against you"; *Semen tuum*, *Egredere de terra tua*,
  *ubera tua*, *cecidisti*, *Tu signaculum* (singular) → thy / thou.

## §2 · Cruces

- **0031C `[var: Vulg. *filiae*, "daughters"]`: Cant 1:4.** Migne prints ***filia** Jerusalem*, singular;
  the Clementine has *filiae Jerusalem*, plural. The addressee changes number. Two witnesses:
  Gallica f16 (*filia-Jerusalem*, native crop) and archive p. 21 (*filia Jerusalem*).
- **0032B `[var: Vulg. *eos*, "against them"]`: Is 13:17.** Migne prints *suscitabo super **vos**
  Medos*; the Clementine has *super eos*. The person changes, from threat reported to threat
  addressed. Two witnesses: Gallica f16 and archive p. 21.
- **0029D *in libro: de Hebraicis, quaestionibus*: press variant.** The archive printing and the twin
  set a colon after *libro* and a comma inside the title. The Gallica printing is clean (*in libro de
  Hebraicis quaestionibus*). The English follows the twin's marks literally: "in the book: on Hebrew,
  questions". No patch.
- **0029B *sacra Scriptura, qui dicitur*.** Gallica shows a stop, while the archive and the twin have
  a comma. It is a worn comma on Gallica. The English follows the twin.
- **0031D unopened » after *sicut tabernacula Cedar*.** *At quod subditur (quasi cum exemplo) sicut
  tabernacula Cedar, » ad malos refertur*: Migne's own, on both printings. Mirrored exactly, and no
  « is supplied (Pattern 5).
- **0031D *historicus et mysticus: quia*.** Gallica shows a single spaced dot, which is a worn colon
  (Migne spaces colons, not stops). The archive and the twin have a colon. The English follows the twin.
- **0032A *secundi Regum XII Praedicta*: no stop in the archive printing or the twin.** Gallica prints
  *XII.* The English follows the twin and supplies nothing (Pattern 8): "as is had in II Kings XII The
  aforesaid authority". It reads as a run-on because one printing sets it that way.
- **0032A *litteralis simpliciter secunda tamen*: MARK LOST BY OUR TWIN.** Gallica prints a comma
  after *simpliciter*; the archive prints a point (1-bit copy, so it may be a comma). The twin has
  nothing. The English carries the mark as a comma. Patch filed.
- **0032C *Gen XII* vs Gallica *Gen. XII*.** Press variant; the archive agrees with the twin. English
  "Gen XII". No patch.
- **0032D *non habet locum, nisi prophetiae praedestinationis*: press variant, WORD-LEVEL.** Gallica
  prints ***nisi in** prophetiae*; the archive printing and the twin omit *in*. Both construe, with
  a dative ("has no place except for the prophecy") or a locative. The English follows the twin. No
  marker, no patch.
- **0034A *beatus Gregorius hom. 10*.** The plate prints *10,* (Gallica f17 at 8×). The sentence
  *certe iniquorum omnium diabolus caput est, et hujus capitis membra sunt omnes iniqui* is Gregory,
  *Hom. in Ev.* 16. It is rendered as printed and not corrected.
- **0031A Isidore *primo libro de summo bono, capite vigesimo*.** Printed thus. The seven rules of
  Tyconius stand in Isidore, *Sent.* I.19. Rendered as printed.
- **0029C, 0032A Augustine *contra Vincentium Donatistam*.** Printed thus (= Ep. 93). Rendered as printed.
- **0032C Exod. XII paraphrase** (*mansio filiorum Israel in terra Aegypti fuit quadringentorum
  triginta annorum*) is Lyra's own wording of Ex 12:40, not quoted with guillemets. Listed.
- **Column anchors interleave with 8971** (0029B–0030B carries both works: 8971's tail at the top of
  both columns of f15, 8972 below the rule). The twin's anchors match the plate's marginal letters on
  f15. False positive #1; nothing to report.
- No foot-of-page apparatus on f15, f16 or f17 (my columns). Checked zero, both layers.
