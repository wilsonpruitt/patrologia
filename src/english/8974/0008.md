## *Copy of the reply to the aforesaid letter.* (cont.)

But perhaps thou wouldst say that all the aforesaid things are also had in the mystical sense, for through the allegorical sense are had those things which are to be believed, and through the tropological those which are to be done. To which it must be said that nothing necessary to faith or morals is contained under the spiritual sense which Scripture does not somewhere manifestly hand down through the literal [0054A] sense. Hence Augustine to Vincent the Donatist: « But who would not most imprudently strive to bring forward for himself something set in allegory, unless he also has manifest testimonies, by whose light the obscure things may be illumined? » But further, concerning the mystical sense, thou seemest to speak thus: For this is the true life, which the Word of life preached to Jewish blindness, when he said: « Search the Scriptures, in which ye think ye have eternal life [n: (Joan. V)]; » by these words of thine I perceive [thee] to reckon that by the searching of the Scriptures, commanded by Christ to the Jews, he is understood to command them to draw out, or extract, from the sacred letters the spiritual or mystical sense: which, saving thy peace be it said, contains no truth. For I dare to say to thee, brother: Search all the holy Gospels of God, and thou wilt never find that Christ alleged any authority of sacred Scripture, whether in instructing the disciples, or to the Jews contradicting, or even to the devil tempting, except as understood under the literal [0054B] sense. Hence master Nicholas too, rightly considering this, in his whole *Postill* on the Old Testament, wherever there occurs any text alleged in the New by Christ and by the disciples, always expounds it according to the literal sense, insomuch that even that saying of the Psalmist: « The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner [n: (Psal. CXVII)], » he expounds as to be understood according to the literal sense of Christ, because Christ alleged that text of himself to the Jews. Likewise all the authorities of the Old Testament which the Apostle alleges in Hebrews I to prove the divinity of Christ, and his other mysteries, he tries to reduce to the literal sense, however much at first sight they have another appearance; which is necessary for this, that they be effective for concluding what is intended; and so in any other places whatever of the whole *Postill*. From which it is concluded against thee: for Christ commanded those very senses to be searched, concerning which he himself daily taught, preached, or disputed: which are only [0054C] literal, as has been said; and therefore that word of Christ: « Search the Scriptures, » which thou allegest for thyself, is expounded, according to the aforesaid master, of the mysteries of Christ, which are found in the sacred Scriptures understood according to the literal sense. For he says on the aforesaid word, namely: « Search the Scriptures: » Thus the Scripture of the Old Testament designates the time of his coming; Gen. penult.: « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Juda, » etc.; and Dan. IX, where the coming of the Holy of holies is determined under a certain number of weeks. It determines also the place of the nativity, Mich. V: « And thou Bethlehem? » It determines also the manner of being born, Isa. VII: « Behold a virgin shall conceive? » And the manner of his conversation, Zach. IX: « Behold thy king cometh to thee? » And the manner of dying, Isa. LIV: « As a sheep he shall be led to the slaughter, » etc. And briefly, all the mysteries of Christ are found expressed in the Old Testament, and fulfilled in Christ. Thus he. And it is certain [0054D] that all the aforesaid authorities, alleged by the Postillator, signify the aforesaid mysteries according to the literal sense. And so it is plain that when it is said: « Search the Scriptures, » it is to be referred to the literal sense, which is what was intended.

But perhaps thou wilt say that the literal sense does not need searching, since it is taken from the things signified by the word, so that it offers itself at once to the understanding, with little searching, or investigation of reason: whence it seems superfluous that Christ preached to Jewish blindness: « Search the Scriptures; » since they had been sufficiently taught about this sense. Not so, my brother, not so: for the literal sense of sacred Scripture, although it is taken from the things signified by the word, as thou sayest, yet many have erred in finding or acquiring it, both in the Old and in the New Testament. The reason for which is: For just as it is read through the prophet concerning the ways of the Lord, which are his precepts, or counsels: « The ways of the Lord are right, and the just shall walk in them; but the transgressors [0055A] have fallen [var: Vulg. *corruent*, "shall fall"] in them [n: (Ose. XIV)]; » so it happens in the literal sense; namely, that although it is right, that is, plainer; and the just, that is, those having the light of faith, shall walk in them, namely, in the things intended and signified through the literal sense, by adhering to the faith: yet the transgressors, namely those lacking light of this kind, namely of faith, shall fall in them. In the Old Testament the Jews contemporary with Christ erred in many ways concerning this literal sense: as do the moderns too, following the error of their forebears, as is plain in many places of the Gospel. Hence in the precepts of the decalogue, which are the principal ones among the precepts of the law, they erred so far that by the precepts on the observance of the sabbath they understood it to be forbidden to the letter that anyone should cure his neighbour on the sabbath, which is inhuman; on which point Christ confuted them, showing that it is lawful to do good on the sabbaths, saying: « Which of you that hath one sheep, and if it fall on the sabbaths [n: (Matth. XII)], » etc. Likewise in homicide they did not understand [0055B] anything to be forbidden except the killing of the human body: to whom the Lord opened the sense, asserting that every unjust motion toward harming a brother ought to be reckoned in the kind of homicide. Augustine declares this same thing against Faustus concerning all those things of which it is said in the Gospel: « Ye have heard that it was said to them of old. » They erred also concerning the precept of the honouring of parents, making void the commandment of God for their tradition, as Matthew XV and Mark VII testify; by which reason he shows that it was said of them through Isaiah: « This people honoureth me with the lips, » etc. Upon which, and upon other errors to which they adhered, failing in the literal sense of sacred Scripture, he concludes of them in the same place: « They are blind, and leaders of the blind. » Hence the Jewish blindness which Christ reproves consists in a false understanding of the literal sense. This is plain also through the aforesaid authorities of the Old Testament which the Postillator alleged on that word: « Search the Scriptures; » which authorities indeed according to the true [0055C] literal sense signify the mysteries of Christ, as has been said above: which literal sense the Jews perverting in manifold ways, do not fear to deny the truth of faith expressed through them; whence it is rightly said to them: « Search the Scriptures; » that is, attend diligently to the true literal sense contained in them. Likewise it must be noted that because among the aforesaid errors the more dangerous was the error concerning the knowledge of Christ, in which they held themselves most pertinaciously; for Luke testifies that when once in the synagogue he expounded to them that saying of Isaiah LXI: « The Spirit of the Lord is upon me; wherefore he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor, » etc., as said of himself, they finally cast him out of the city, to throw him down headlong; rightly therefore did Christ say to such: « Search the Scriptures; » because if they had had the true literal sense of such Scriptures, they would have found true testimony of Christ, and therefore he adds: « They are they which bear testimony of me. » But testimony [0055D] is not had as certain from sacred Scripture except from the literal sense, as Augustine testifies in many places.

The same is plain in the New Testament. For if Arius had had the true literal sense of this saying: « I and the Father are one, » understanding this unity, moreover, in the divine nature; and likewise had had the true understanding of this saying: « The Father is greater than I, » understanding the greaterness in respect of the assumed nature, he would not have fallen into his condemned heresy. But because he understood the aforesaid otherwise to the letter than the catholics illumined by the Holy Spirit understood them, he fell into the snare. And so it is plain that the aforesaid error consists about the true literal understanding of the Gospel: and so it is to be understood of the others. From which what was intended is plain.

Further thou sayest that the first of my decision does not satisfy thee, nor the second, nor the third, nor the fourth, nor the fifth. Concerning which, if thou hadst shown the reason for thy [0056A] unsatisfiedness, perhaps thou wouldst be satisfied. But because thou hast omitted to assign a reason, I give thee no answer; except that one must beware of insatiability of heart, which is reproved in the sacred Scriptures.

Concerning the sixth, because there thou hast declared thy mind, thou must deservedly be answered. Thou sayest first that between the literal and the mystical sense comparison is not possible. Which I reckon very unreasonably said: both because thou thyself comparest the aforesaid senses with one another: for thou hast asserted that the mystical sense is more worthy, and consequently they are comparable by thy own account; and because no one could reasonably deny that the creator is more worthy than the creature, and the distance between the creator and the creature exceeds any distance whatever between created things, as is manifest; and also because these two senses of which we treat are species contained under the genus of the exposition of sacred Scripture, as is plain from all the doctors who speak of this, such as Bede, Augustine and others. For nothing [0056B] prohibits that, just as we shall say that the human species is more worthy than the brutish or irrational, so one sense of expounding sacred Scripture is more worthy than another.
