## Plate divergences from our twin (English renders the plate; patches filed)

- **0017C `poena` → plate `poeta`.** Our twin: *poena etiam pro illo aevo non infelix* ("a punishment,
  too, not unhappy for that age", which does not construe). Gallica f9 prints *poeta* with a worn
  **t**; the archive copy prints *poeta* cleanly. Two witnesses agree. English: "a poet, too, not
  unsuccessful for that age." Walafrid was a known poet, which fits.
- **0016D `petulantiz` → plate `petulantia`.** Gallica f8's final sort is worn into a z-like shape
  (it may be what the digitizer transcribed); the archive copy prints *petulantia.* cleanly. The
  sentence needs a nominative subject for *decebat*, and *petulantia* is one. Rendered "petulance".
  No `[sic:]`: the two witnesses split, the worn copy being Gallica's (same class as the 8956 worn
  sorts in CLAUDE.md).
- **0018D `clucidans` → plate `elucidans`.** Sixtus's quotation, *aperiens et clucidans litterae
  sensum*. Gallica f9 reads **c** (a worn **e** with its crossbar gone); the archive copy prints
  *elucidans* cleanly. Rendered "elucidating". No `[sic:]`, same reasoning as above.
- **0019A `nonnulis` → plate `nonnullis`.** Both witnesses print *nonnullis*. Rendered "by some".
- **0020A `in suis consiliis. Navarrus.` → plate `in suis consiliis Navarrus.`** Both witnesses print
  NO full stop after *consiliis*. With our twin's stop, *Navarrus.* stands alone as a one-word
  sentence. The English follows the plate: "as Navarrus testifies in his counsels." (Martín de
  Azpilcueta, "Doctor Navarrus", *Consilia*.)
- **0019A the two distichs are LINEATED on the plate** (set in smaller type, indented, on lines of
  their own), in both witnesses. Our twin runs the first line of each distich onto the prose line
  (*distichon: Cui veteris…* / *epitaphium Hierophanta…*). The English keeps the twin's line
  structure so that the parallel columns stay aligned. The lineation is filed as a pending patch
  and not rendered separately. Pattern 8a says the verse should be lineated, and once the patch
  lands, splitting those two lines in the English is a one-line edit.
  - After *epitaphium* Gallica shows a mid-dot, and the archive shows nothing. Our twin prints no
    mark there. The mark is uncertain (it may be a speck), so none was supplied: "And the epitaph is
    closed with this distich Hence the hierophant…".

## Readings kept as printed

- **0014B Greek `ἔσθλα καὶ ὕργα`.** The plate was read at 6× native. *ἔσθλα* has a smooth breathing and
  an acute on the epsilon, as in our twin. *ὕργα* has a rough breathing and an acute on the upsilon,
  also as in our twin. *καὶ* is set with the ϰ kappa variant. The accent on its iota is too worn to
  tell grave from acute, so our twin's grave stands. The phrase is not good Greek. It seems to echo
  Homer's Egyptian drugs, *ἐσθλὰ … λυγρά* (Od. 4.230), "good and baneful", and the Latin gloss
  *commoda et incommoda* that follows supports that reading. *ὕργα* is no Greek word. It is carried
  verbatim, and the text itself glosses it. No marker, because Greek is not rendered.
- **0017C Greek `κατ' ἕξοχὴν`.** The plate has an acute on the epsilon and a grave on the eta. The
  breathing is too small and worn to call smooth or rough with confidence, even at 8×. The correct
  form is *ἐξοχήν*, with a smooth breathing. Our twin's rough breathing is carried unchanged. No
  patch is proposed on a mark this doubtful.
- **0018B `Utraque vera glossa`.** Both witnesses print *vera*, which is probably meant for *vero*
  ("but each gloss"). *vera* is a real word and fits its slot, so it was rendered literally: "Each
  true gloss". No `[cj:]`: the English asserts nothing false.
- **0017D `qui ipso Strabo posterior est aetate`.** Both witnesses print *ipso Strabo*, not
  *Strabone*. A case slip with no English exponent (Pattern 9 territory). Rendered "later in age than
  Strabus himself".
- **0013B `aliorum piorum consensui praeferre audet et propterea submittunt se`.** There is no
  punctuation between *audet* and *et*, and the verb changes from singular to plural. The plate was
  confirmed on f7. Rendered as printed, with no mark supplied.
- **0020B `a D. Francisco Luca Burgensi`.** The corrector of the Roman Bible is Franciscus Lucas
  *Brugensis* (of Bruges). The plate prints *Burgensi*, which reads as "of Burgos" in a preface
  that has just used *Burgensis* for Paul of Burgos. Rendered "Francis Lucas Burgensis" as printed.
- **0015B `(inquit in verbo *Glossa ordinaria* ),`.** The space before the closing parenthesis
  comes from the transform. The plate sets `ordinaria),`. It was kept as it is in our twin, since
  whitespace is not a mark.
- **0014B stray mark after `solebant`.** Gallica f7 shows a small raised tick before the comma. It
  is a speck and not a semicolon. Our twin's comma stands.
- **Name forms.** *WALAFRIDUS STRABUS* (in small caps on the plate) became "WALAFRID STRABUS".
  *Strabus* is kept rather than the usual "Strabo", because the passage explains the surname as
  "squint-eyed" (*ab oculorum vitio*). *Strabone monacho Anglo* became "Strabo, the English monk".
  *Lypomannus* was kept as printed. *Mathiae Tornigi* (0016D) and *Matthaeus Dornick, seu Tornigus*
  (0020A) became "Mathias Tornigus" and "Matthew Dornick, or Tornigus", following the plate's two
  spellings.
- **0013A–C `examinantes et exanimantes`.** A pun ("examining and deadening"). Rendered "sifting and
  stifling" to keep the play. *exanimo* means to deprive of breath.
- **0016A `non possumus nobis persuadere, ordinem … fuisse … observatum, sed … neglectum`.** The
  *sed* clause stands grammatically under *persuadere*, so the printed sentence can be read as "we
  cannot persuade ourselves that it was neglected". The sense plainly intended is "we are persuaded
  that it was neglected". The English keeps the Latin's shape ("…had been observed by the workmen,
  but, the press hurrying…, for the most part neglected") and adds no negation or verb of its own.

- ⚑ **FOR WILSON — verse lineation @0019A, not resolved at merge.** Both witnesses set each distich
  (*Cui veteris … / Cognomen …*; *Hierophanta … / Et Brabantiaco …*) on lines of its own. Our TEI
  already encodes them as `<l>` lines, but the chunker's `<l>` convention (`scripts/lib/chunk-core.mjs`:
  `</l>` → line break, `<l>` → nothing) breaks only AFTER each verse line, so the first line of each
  distich runs on from the preceding prose — the same rendering shipped works carry (e.g. 11057
  @0370A). Matching the plate needs a chunker change (a break before the first `<l>` of a run), which
  would also re-flow verse in shipped works on their next rebuild. Not invented here. Pattern 8a says
  lineate where Migne lineates; this is the one place the machinery falls short of it. English mirrors
  the Latin line structure.
