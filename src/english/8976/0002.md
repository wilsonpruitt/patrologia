## CHAPTER II.

[0430A]

VERS. 1.-- *In the same place.* Namely in the upper room, into which they had gone up. He who desires the Spirit treads upon the dwelling-place of the flesh, transcending it by the contemplation of the mind.

VERS. 2.-- *From heaven.* The Spirit is given from heaven, that God may be loved; on earth he is given, that the neighbour may be loved: for twice after the resurrection was he given, for the commending of the two precepts of charity. *A sound.* It may be called the speaking of tongues sounding from the mouth of the apostles. *Spirit.* Before the passion the Spirit was given to the apostles for the grace of teaching and of healings; after the resurrection Jesus breathed the spirit, and said: *Receive the Holy Spirit; whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them [n: (Joan. XX)]*, etc. Which gift he had given to Peter alone [0430B] beforehand: but, as is believed, then by anticipation, now with the rest by completion it is bestowed on Peter also: and the power of forgiving was given to those whom he was sending to the office of preaching.

*Mighty.* By comparison with what went before, the grace of the apostles is called mighty, or the Spirit came in the likeness of a mighty wind. *The whole house.* The Spirit is poured into them sitting in one house, that the unity of the Church may be commended, the third law beginning in the upper room. At the giving of the law the people stood far off for fear; here, at the coming of the Spirit, they had been gathered into one out of love. *Sitting.* That there might be fulfilled what had been said to them: *Sit you here in the city.* Sitting is a token of humility and of stability: by which a seat is prepared for the Holy Spirit, who rests upon the humble.

[0430C] [n: (GREG.)] The Spirit appeared in fire, but through himself, etc., *as far as* and hearts were taught by the voice or by the sound.

VERS. 3.-- *Parted.* The dispersion of tongues was made at the tower, but what pride dispersed, humility gathers together again: in pride is dispersion, in humility concord is made. Behold, the sentence of the Lord is fulfilled which says: *I will not leave you orphans, but I will send the Paraclete Spirit [n: (Joan. XIV)]*, who, being their advocate, spoke for them in all tongues. [var: Vulg. Jo. 14:18 *Non relinquam vos orphanos: veniam ad vos*, "I will come to you"] He brought tongues, who came for the Word, for the tongue has kinship with the word, so that they cannot be separated from one another. So the Word of the Father, that is the Son, and the Holy Spirit are inseparable, nay rather of one nature. *As of fire.* The same Spirit came in fire: because sinners [0430D] *the Spirit purges, enlightens, kindles, burns.* For fire has four natures, *it burns, purges, warms, enlightens.* In like manner the Spirit burns up sins, purges hearts, shakes off torpor, illumines ignorances. Fire is incorporeal and invisible in its own nature; but when some body has been assumed it is seen, appearing of diverse colour, on account of the materials in which it burns. So the Spirit will not be able to be seen, save through the creatures in which he works.

VERS. 4.-- *And they began.* Behold the sign of fulness: a full vessel bursts out. Fire cannot be hidden in the bosom. *With divers tongues.* Because they spoke with all tongues, or, speaking in their own, that is the Hebrew tongue, they were so understood by all as if they spoke in the proper tongues of each, [0431A] according to that of the Prophet: In other tongues and with other lips will I speak to this people, etc.

VERS. 5.-- *Out of every nation,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] As if it were said: Wheresoever born. But the captivity, which was made under Antiochus not many times before, caused the Jews to be born among all nations.

VERS. 6.-- *Confounded.* That confusion is set over against the confusion of tongues, from which Babel obtained its name. So the humility of the apostles answers to human pride. *Every man.* All the tongues are said to be seventy-two: whence also the disciples of the second order were chosen under this number, that the number of the preachers might accord with the number of the tongues.

VERS. 8.-- *We have heard.* In this it was signified that one Church would speak in the tongues of all nations.

[0431B] [n: (BEDA.)] It is asked whether those who were speaking, etc., *as far as* it seemed rather to be a miracle of the hearers than of the speakers.

VERS. 9.-- *Cappadocia,* etc. These five provinces which are set after Judaea do indeed speak Greek: but from the custom of their country there is a diversity in sound. Whence wonderful was the grace in the apostles, which not only taught them the diversity, but also made in their speech the distance of properties in each of the tongues according to the number of the provinces.

VERS. 10.-- *Strangers of Rome.* In the Greek, sojourning Romans, that is, Jews who led a pilgrim life at Rome.

VERS. 14.-- *Jewish men.* That is, there are two causes of prudence, namely that they were Jews, and that they dwelt in [0431C] Jerusalem, which sobriety becomes.

VERS. 17.-- *I will pour out.* By this word he shows the largeness of the gift, because the Spirit was to be given not, as of old, to prophets and priests only, but to all persons everywhere in either sex. *Of my Spirit.* He looks to the diverse gifts while he says this, but not to a diminution of the substance. *Upon all flesh.* What all flesh is he consequently expounds there: *And the sons shall prophesy,* etc.

[n: (RAB.)] Upon all flesh, of male and female, or of the circumcised and the heathen, according to that: *There is not male and female, circumcision and uncircumcision, Barbarian and Scythian, bond and free [n: (Gal. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Gal. 3:28 reads *Non est Judaeus, neque Graecus: non est servus, neque liber: non est masculus, neque femina*; the clauses on circumcision and on the Barbarian and the Scythian belong to Col. 3:11, not to Galatians]

*And they shall prophesy.* The law and the prophets until John: from him the kingdom is preached. But it is one thing to prophesy of [0431D] Christ, another to foretell and to teach things to come.

VERS. 19.-- *And I will give.* Mystically, wonders in heaven are labours in the Church. Signs, upon the earth. Vengeances, upon the earthly. Blood, in martyrdom. Fire, of the Gospel or of the Holy Spirit. Vapour of smoke, the compunction of the soul or of the heart. *Wonders in heaven.* For indeed at the birth of Christ a new star appeared; at his ascending the cross, the sun was darkened and heaven was overspread with darkness.

*Blood.* Not only of the wound, but also of the sweat, when in prayer his sweat became as drops of blood, which is set among the signs of the Deity, because it is not found in human nature. Therefore it signifies [0432A] that the whole world is to be washed by the blood of Christ, that as the tabernacle had been consecrated by the blood of victims, so the faithful people might be dedicated by the blood of the same: and that not only at Jerusalem should there be a place of prayer, but that in every place of his dominion the elect should lift up their hands by prayers to the Lord. *Vapour of smoke.* A vapour, namely, of compunction and of weepings. Because as smoke from fire, so compunction from the ardour of the Holy Spirit.

*The sun shall be turned,* etc. This is believed to have been done partly in the passion of Christ, partly to be about to come before the day of judgment. For then the sun was darkened, but the moon turned into blood could not appear to men: because then (as being in the Pasch) standing as the fifteenth, it had by day been hidden from human sight by the interposing of the earth.

[0432B]VERS. 23.-- *By the determinate counsel. [n: (RAB.)]* In this alone were the Jews blameworthy, that they did it not with an obedient mind.

*And foreknowledge of God,* etc. [n: (ID.)] In the Greek it is thus: *Receiving him thus delivered up by foreknowledge.* For he was delivered by the governor into their power, that they might choose either him or the robber, but life being granted to the robber, they slew Jesus by the hands of the soldiers.

VERS. 24.-- *He hath raised up.* Not by the resurrection usual with the rest and common, which is deferred to the end, but by that which was celebrated on the third day, that the assertion of this resurrection might be a testimony of the Deity.

[n: (RAB.)] He raised up, according to the humanity, which the Jews most greatly hated.

*The sorrows being loosed.* The sense of this sentence seems to be, [0432C] that when the Lord descended to the lower regions the sorrows of the punishments were loosed, because they could not touch him, but according to the Greek, in which it is written: *Loosing through him the sorrows of death, according as it was impossible that he should be held by it,* it is plain that through his descent the saints were freed from the places of the infernal regions; who, although they were in the bosom of Abraham, that is in the consolation of rest, yet were not wholly loosed from the sorrow of death or of hell, because they had not deserved to enter the joys of heaven, until that of the Prophet should come to pass: *In the blood of the testament thou hast led forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water [n: (Zach. V)]*. [var: Vulg. Zach. 9:11 *in sanguine testamenti tui emisisti vinctos tuos*, "thou hast sent forth"] For they were bound before they were led forth.

VERS. 25.-- *I foresaw the Lord.* The voice of Christ exulting after the resurrection at the right hand. The left is the present life; the right, the eternal. *That I be not moved.* [0432D] After the resurrection all things will be immovable. *And the tongue hath exulted.* Because rest after labour is given: for he laboured in the hope of eternal life. *Thou wilt not leave.* Let me not again be moved by the corruption of a repeated cross and death. *Thou hast made known to me.* I know that I shall always live. *Thou shalt fill me* with full gladness, that is, I shall not die again. *Because he is at the right hand.* The cause why he is not moved is that the Lord helps at the right hand, the left not prevailing. For if he be not at the right hand, the devil occupies that part, as is said of Judas: *And let the devil stand at his right hand [n: (Psal. CVIII)]*.

VERS. 26.-- *And hath exulted.* In the passion he said: *My soul is sorrowful even unto death [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*: and he began to fear and to be heavy: that he might show himself true man [0433A] in soul and body, and truly passible. But afterwards the soul and the tongue exulted, because by his passion the human race was to be saved.

VERS. 27.-- *In hell.* It is agreed that the soul was not left in hell, which, when those had been taken away for whose sake he had descended, straightway returned to the things above, nor was the flesh corrupted, but glorified by a swift resurrection.

VERS. 28.-- *Thou hast made known to me.* Not only are these things understood of him who, the fulness of divine power and wisdom being once received, destroyed death by himself, rising again to life; but also of the just who, the way of truth having been found through him, will return to life and will be gladdened, seeing his face: because that sight is above all things and is perfect beatitude.

VERS. 33.-- *The promise of the Holy Spirit being received.* [0433B] Christ received it as true man, and as God pours out the Spirit, as Joel says: *I will pour out,* etc. Likewise the apostles receive from him the promise of the Spirit, who in the Gospel is said to be about to be sent by the Father. *He hath poured forth this,* etc. He who before said that Jesus was raised up by God, now raising the faith of his hearers higher, declares him to be the Lord, whom he affirms to have poured forth the Spirit: for this is the sign. And beautifully has he used the word of pouring out: which he recorded above that the prophet Joel had set down, that from this also it may be established that he himself is the Lord Jesus, who before the assumption of flesh was wont to speak in the prophets, who gives signs and wonders in heaven and on earth, who saves all that call upon him. And the rest which the prophet describes, he fulfils as true God.

[0433C]VERS. 34.-- *Sit thou at the right hand. [n: (RAB.)]* Manifestly Peter, from this psalm, the kingdom of Christ not earthly, but heavenly, etc., *as far as* in the other, he is Lord of the same.

VERS. 36.-- *Him Lord.* Setting down the name of majesty and of power, he shows him to be one to whom every creature ought to be subjected. Christ is the name of royal or of pontifical dignity. In the law kings and pontiffs were anointed, in a figure of him. *And Christ.* He is truly Christ, anointed with all the fulness of the Holy Spirit, who of the same Spirit gave powerfully to whom he would. *Jesus whom,* etc. Jesus is Lord and Christ, one person existing in two natures, because born eternally of the Father in the Divinity, made temporally in the humanity, when by the working of the Holy [0433D] Spirit he was incarnate in the womb of the virgin.

VERS. 37.-- *Pricked.* The prophecy of Joel is fulfilled, because after the fire of the Holy Spirit follows the vapour of compunction. Smoke is wont to draw out tears, they begin to weep who had mocked, they beat the breast, they give prayer to God as a sacrifice, that they may be able to taste that blood, to be saved by him, which they had before called down upon themselves and upon their sons.
