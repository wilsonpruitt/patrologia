## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The priests and the magistrates,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ These seemed to be the teachers and the judges of the people. ⟦s3⟧ *The Sadducees.* ⟦s4⟧ These denied the resurrection: and therefore they grieved at the teaching of the apostles, who announced the resurrection.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0435D]VERS. 2.-- *Being grieved,* that the multitude flowed together to hear the apostles.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The resurrection.* ⟦s2⟧ This is most of all against the Sadducees: but both grieved that the apostles affirmed Jesus, whom they had slain as a man, to have been glorified by God the Father.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And there was made,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If in the five thousand men whom the Lord fed in the desert the people of the law freed by Christ is understood, here also the five thousand instructed by the apostles can signify the people of the Gentiles: which was to follow the mysteries of the same law spiritually. ⟦s3⟧ And well indeed at evening are they endowed with a heavenly gift, because when the fulness of time came, God sent his Son upon the earth, etc.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0436A]VERS. 11.-- *This is the stone. [n: (RAB.)]* Here, answering the things asked of him, he preaches concerning Christ, and most of all concerning David, in whom, as in their own patriarch, they believed before all others.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Rejected. [n: (AUG.)]* Of the rejected stone no history is had.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Without letters,* etc. ⟦s2+3⟧ These things were said not because they were wholly ignorant of letters, but because they had not the skill of the grammatical art. *Idiots.* ⟦s4⟧ They were called idiots, because they had not exercised their own proper wit through teaching: ἰδίωμα in Greek, in Latin, property.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] Unlettered men are sent to preach, lest the faith of believers should be thought to come about not by the power of God, but by eloquence: according to that: *Not in wisdom of speech, lest the cross of Christ should be made void [n: (I Cor. I)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0436B]VERS. 19.-- *But Peter,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] You who are judges, do your office, not by uttering a crooked speech, but by judging justly. ⟦s2⟧ Behold, full grain is found in the ear: which before was bending as it were in the blade.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *For we cannot.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, we will not, or it has been turned into nature (as with the confirmed angels) not to sin.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *They made renowned.* ⟦s2⟧ In the Greek: All made renowned in that which was done: and "in that which had befallen" is not had.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Deeds or words are made renowned when, by the spreading of report, they are brought to the knowledge of very many. ⟦s2⟧ But God is glorified in the deeds of men, which is proper to Christian piety and humility, himself saying: [0436C] *That they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven [n: (Matth. V)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *For he was of years,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Whence appeared a fuller glory or grace of the power, and therefore he speaks not of the age of the man, but of the time of the infirmity.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *With one accord,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Without sound. ⟦s3⟧ Or by the perfection of unanimity, according to that: If two or three shall come together into one in my name, of every thing which they shall ask, it shall be done for them.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *The Gentiles have raged.* ⟦s2⟧ Roaring is the voice of a lion. ⟦s3+4⟧ Whence Jeremias: *My inheritance is become to me as a lion in the wood: it hath given forth its voice over me [n: (Jer. XII)]*. [var: Vulg. Jer. 12:8 *dedit contra me vocem*, "gave her voice against me"] *They have devised vain things.* ⟦s5⟧ Of Christ to come, or that he might be held fast in death.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *They came together.* ⟦s2⟧ Herod and Pilate came together [0436D] into friendship.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Thou hast anointed.* ⟦s2⟧ They expound the name of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ Of Christ, from chrism, that is from unction, according to that: *God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *With all confidence.* ⟦s2⟧ This they ask of God, that with all confidence they may be able to speak the word of God.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *By the name.* ⟦s2⟧ In the Greek: *By the name of thy child Jesus,* which agrees better with the desires of the apostles, who were busied about this, that the man crucified by the Jews, the miracles growing bright, might through the name of him who was taken up into heaven become known as himself the Son of God.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *The place was moved.* ⟦s2⟧ Great is the power of the prayer [0437A] of the faithful, but this was done for the rousing up of minds.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *There was one heart.* ⟦s2⟧ They who had left the world did not, glorying in the nobility of their race, prefer themselves to others, but, as brought forth from the bowels of one and the same mother the Church, rejoiced in the same love of brotherhood. ⟦s3⟧ *Neither did any one of them.* ⟦s4⟧ The virtues which the just possess they judge to be not their own, but of those to whose profit they dispense them. ⟦s5⟧ *Common.* ⟦s6⟧ In Greek κοῖνα· whence coenobites, that is, those living in common. ⟦s7⟧ Coenobia are their dwellings.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *The apostles gave.* ⟦s2⟧ He distinguishes the order of the teachers and of the hearers. ⟦s3⟧ For the multitude of believers, temporal things being despised, were joined to one another by the bond of charity. ⟦s4⟧ But the apostles, shining with power, [0437B] set forth the mysteries of Christ.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *Joseph. [n: (RAB.)]* Increased, Barnabas is interpreted *son of consolation,* being driven by the increase and the advance of spiritual good.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Which is, being interpreted.* ⟦s2⟧ Wherever Scripture sets down the names of things or of persons with an interpretation, it signifies that a more sacred sense is in them. ⟦s3⟧ Deservedly is he called son of consolation, who, present things being despised, consoles himself with the hope of things to come. ⟦s4⟧ For the Holy Spirit also is therefore called Paraclete, because to those having tribulation in the world he bestows, by the infusion of himself, paraclesis, that is, consolation, as Peter also was called Barjona, that is son of the dove, on account of the grace of the Spirit.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Son of consolation. [n: (RAB.)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Either on account of the alms [0437C] with which he consoled the poor, or for the consolation of the reward which he deserved to receive from God, or because he was held worthy to console the apostles in Judas' stead.
