## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *He tempted. [n: (RAB.)]* He sets down the preterite, *he tempted*, because the temptation was complete. ⟦s2⟧ Temptation is carried on in three ways, by persuasion, by delight, by consent: all of which was fulfilled in Ananias. ⟦s3⟧ *And to defraud. [n: (ID.)]* In this it seems there was a lie, because he had vowed the whole. ⟦s4⟧ But it is better not to vow than after a vow not to pay to the Holy Spirit.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *But to God.* ⟦s2⟧ Above he had said: *to the Holy Spirit:* it is plain therefore that the Holy Spirit is God.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He fell. [n: (RAB.)]* The beginnings of laws are always commended by [0437D] punishments. ⟦s2⟧ The two sons of Aaron, offering fire, are consumed: Oza, upholding the ark, died. ⟦s3⟧ He fell, that the rest of the transgressors might be terrified by his example.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And there came fear,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] An anticipation: since by the death of the man alone, before the wife's end, few had heard of it, although it could be said of the few that they feared.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Of hours,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] A short time of penance is given to the wife, whose fault is more venial than the husband's, inasmuch as she consented to her head.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Tell me,* etc. [n: (ID.)] By questioning, he declares the wife's fault out of her own confession, the fault which the husband had shown to be his own in the deed.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0438A]VERS. 9.-- *Why is it that,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Was it said by way of rebuke? or it is thus to be distinguished: what? as if: What is this: then he himself answers himself and says: Is it indeed agreed among you to tempt the Spirit of the Lord?

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To tempt,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The tempting of the Spirit lay in this, that they made trial how great his knowledge is.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They buried. [n: (ID.)]* From this many take an example, that wives should be buried beside their husbands. ⟦s2⟧ Others think it unworthy that examples be taken from transgressors, for whom their partnership had made their desert equal alike in the crime and in the death and in the burial.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And there came.* ⟦s2⟧ First Ananias by [cj: *a*; read *ac*, "and"] Saphira died quickly in the sight of the Church, that the apostolic authority, how great it was, might be shown: and that it might be shown how great a sin it is to withdraw [0438B] again from the Church what has been offered, and that the rest might be chastened by this example.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Of the apostles.* ⟦s2⟧ It is said in the plural, of the apostles, because it was not by the hand of Peter alone; for although for his honour the rest are passed over in silence, yet their deeds are not passed over in silence.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Of the rest,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By the example of Ananias and his wife: for the punishment of the two who had fraudulently joined themselves to them gave an example to the rest.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *It was increased,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] So great was the multitude of them that believed, that, the strength of faith growing, the shadow alone sufficed. ⟦s2⟧ Or: So many sick were then brought by the believers, that they could not all touch him.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Heresy. [n: (RAB.)]* Heresy is interpreted *a division*, because the heretic, despising the sayings of others, [0438C] chooses what he thinks is to be followed.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the Sadducees they were filled,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Sadducees are interpreted *the just*, named from Sadoch the priest, either for their race or for their manners; but justifying themselves they claimed for themselves what they were not: denying the resurrection of bodies, they said that souls perish with the flesh: but they believed that there was neither an angel nor any of spirits, and they rejected all the Scriptures except the books of Moses: and therefore they consented most of all with the princes in the persecution of the apostles, who by power and by signs bore witness to the resurrection of Christ.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Of this life. [n: (RAB.)]* Christian, or eternal. ⟦s2⟧ But this is not added for this reason, that the sect of the Christians was famous, for which they had been delivered to prison.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0438D]VERS. 23.-- *The prison indeed,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Mad are the Jews who at the goading of envy say that Jesus was carried off from the tomb by the apostles stealing him, by whose theft they may say that the apostles were carried off out of the closed prison.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *The magistrates.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the Greek, *magistratus* is to be read in the singular number, as also in what follows where it is said: Then the magistrate went away with the ministers. ⟦s3⟧ *They doubted.* ⟦s4⟧ Not even so do they believe: for inward malice hardens a faithless heart even against manifest signs.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *By commanding* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the Greek: *Did we not by commanding command you?* ⟦s3⟧ For to speak thus better befits one who questions. ⟦s4⟧ *You have filled Jerusalem.* ⟦s5⟧ The chief of cities, in which we also dwell. ⟦s6⟧ In this they praise the apostles unwittingly, who, terrified by threats, [0439A] cease not to preach. ⟦s7⟧ *Upon us the blood. [n: (RAB.)]* It is plain that it was said by antiphrasis: *His blood be upon us.*

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The rash questioning of the high priest is refuted by a prudent answer.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] They question, for there is wont to be doubt in words, when in the heart a fixed opinion is held.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *The God of the fathers. [n: (RAB.)]* Not new, nor lately found out by us: but he says this, that their disobedience may be inexcusable.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *The Holy Spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ The Holy Spirit has strengthened us, that even though you resist we may firmly preach the great works of God, speaking in us and forming the words which you hear.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To all. [n: (RAB.)]* Not to us alone, but to all [0439B] that obey, to you also, if you will receive. ⟦s2⟧ In this he calls them to penance.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Gamaliel.* ⟦s2⟧ He was a disciple of the apostles, as is thought, remaining among the Jews by the counsel of the apostles, that he might destroy their worst counsels and mitigate their fury.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *Theodas. [n: (BEDA.)]* Theodas persuaded many, when they had taken their goods out of the city, to beset the banks of the Jordan, and though he was a magician, he called himself a prophet, and promised that, the river being divided, he would afford a passage. ⟦s2⟧ A troop of horsemen coming upon him by order of Fadus the procurator, many being slain or taken, carried his head back to Jerusalem.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *Judas the Galilean,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Of him Josephus says that he compelled the people not to lose their liberty by paying tribute [0439C] to the Romans: bringing forward out of the law that God alone is to be served, and that those who gave tithes ought not to pay tribute. ⟦s3⟧ Which heresy grew so far that the Pharisees and a great part of the people asked Christ whether it were lawful to give tribute to Caesar.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *Having beaten them,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Behold, the fortitude of faith is made plain, which could be restrained neither by words nor by scourges. ⟦s2⟧ Before they were forbidden by speech, now they are beaten with scourges.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *That they were worthy.* ⟦s2⟧ They thought not that they laboured, but that they received a very great gift: because *Blessed are they that suffer persecution for God [n: (Matth. VI)]*. [var: Vulg. Matth. 5:10 *propter justitiam*, "for justice' sake"]

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *And every day,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Often such summaries are interposed, briefly gathering many things, so that, as it were, an end may be set to the discourse that has gone before, and a foundation [0439D] to that which follows.

## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Of the Greeks. [n: (RAB.)]* Either of the gentile strangers dwelling in the city: or, etc., *as far as* they were burdened by the daily ministry.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They were despised. [n: (ID.)]* From the want they were the more burdened.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *It is not right that we,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The apostles show that bishops and teachers of the Church ought not to be entangled in bodily affairs.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Consider ye,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] From this the apostles have now decreed, etc., *as far as* not without the mystery of the septenary number.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0440A]VERS. 4.-- *But we,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Concerning themselves they do not ask, but judge.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And they chose,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] This order is to be kept in ordaining: let the people choose, let the bishop ordain.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Stephen.* ⟦s2⟧ He is set first, and he alone is praised, that in one all may be praised, or that the honour of martyrdom may be paid to the first.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *And praying.* ⟦s2⟧ The common dispensation required that ministers should be chosen, who, when they were found worthy, the providence of the saving counsel growing by degrees, it pleased that these very men should be ordained ministers of the sacred altar and of the Lord's blood, as of the refreshment and of the common table. ⟦s3⟧ Which is proved from this, that it is said: *Praying they laid hands upon them:* this is proper [0440B] to those who are promoted to the office of the most holy altar. ⟦s4⟧ By this example prayers are made at ordinations, although the prayer of the apostles is not written.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And the word,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] It seems as it were a recapitulation, because he had said: *Every day they ceased not teaching,* etc., and he returns to that very thing, saying: *And the word of the Lord increased.*

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Often in this book, and in the Gospel also, such summaries are interposed, etc., *as far as* because before, when the people increased, zeal had been stirred up.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Stephen,* in Greek; in Latin, *a crown:* who forestalled by the presage of his name that which he was to receive in fact: because after the cross of Christ he was the first crowned with martyrdom on high, who was stoned [0440C] in lowliness. ⟦s2⟧ In Hebrew Stephen is interpreted *our rule*, who, suffering first, was made to the martyrs that followed a pattern of dying for Christ.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *This man.* ⟦s2⟧ Out of contempt they do not call him by his proper name, but call him a man, as it were carnal and lowest.
