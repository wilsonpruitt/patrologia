## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 2.-- *Men, brethren, and fathers.* Because Stephen was charged with having blasphemed against Moses and God, at the beginning of his discourse he meets their calumny, saying that the God who spoke to the fathers and to the prophets is the God of majesty and of glory. Moses also he praises: but he proves them rebellious and always disobedient. But he used art of speaking toward the rebellious, beginning [0440D] as though he feared them: thus, *Men, brethren, and fathers:* a gentle and clement speech wins over the hearer, so that it may commend the pleader. He began smoothly, that he might be heard long, and he who refused to speak against God and the law preaches the true God at the beginning of his oration. The law also he so expounded, that he was the preacher of that whereof he was accused of being the destroyer: in the further course of his discourse also, refuting their errors, he taught fully the vigour of his mind, and that he was free from the fear of the enemy.

*The God of glory,* etc. Stephen, mindful of that precept: *Love your enemies [n: (Luc. VI)]*, etc., announced salvation to his persecutors.

*In Mesopotamia.* Mesopotamia is set at the head of Syria toward the north.

[0441A] [n: (RAB.)] Mesopotamia and Charra are in Chaldaea, and from Mesopotamia he passed over into Charra.

VERS. 3.-- *And he said,* etc. In Genesis it seems to be contained that, after the death of his father, who died in Charra where the son dwelt with him, the Lord spoke to Abraham. But before he dwelt in the city, when he was in Mesopotamia, it was said to him:

*Go out from thy land,* etc. By the land and the kindred of Abraham he calls the country and the race of the Chaldeans, whence he had already gone out, living now in Mesopotamia; but he was still held by the hope and the desire of returning, and therefore he hears: *Go out from the land and from thy kindred;* not that he should draw out his body (which he had already done), but that he should pluck out the love of his heart.

[n: (RAB.)] Stephen passed over the third, that is, *and [0441B] from the house of thy father:* which was done because of the exceeding skill or unskilfulness of the people.

VERS. 4.-- *Then he went out,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Abraham went out from Ur of the Chaldees. Ur, etc., *as far as* his father being dead it was said to him: *Go out from thy land,* etc.

*His father,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He returns to the origin of the race of Israel, and to the beginning of the divine friendship, that he may pass over nothing in which he might seem to blaspheme.

*You dwell.* He said "you," and not "we," because the apostles had already in a manner left the land, and Stephen, who was already as it were set in death

VERS. 6.-- *That his seed shall be,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] It is not so to be understood, as though it were to be ill treated, etc., *as far as* whether in the land of Chanaan or in Egypt.

*Four hundred years. [n: (RAB.)]* It is thus to be joined: [0441C] *A sojourner,* etc., *as far as* it was completed at the distribution of the land of promise.

VERS. 7.-- *I will judge. [n: (ID.)]* Judgment sometimes signifies discernment, as there: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause [n: (Psal. XLII)]*. Sometimes punishment, as: *Judge them, O God [n: (Psal. V)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *The covenant of circumcision. [n: (ID.)]* A covenant or a sign made for this end, that the people of Israel, etc., *as far as* it is betokened that the boasting of glory in spiritual circumcision is to be shunned.

*On the eighth day. [n: (RAB.)]* By eight days the mystery of our circumcision is shown, we who through the octave of the New Testament (which is contained in the resurrection of the Lord and in the eight beatitudes) are circumcised from vices on the eighth day.

[0441D]VERS. 9.-- *They sold him into Egypt.* Narrating briefly he passes to the end, for he was sold not first to the Egyptians, but to Ismaelite traders and to Madianites, and they sold him into Egypt to Putiphar the priest.

VERS. 14.-- *In souls.* This is said according to the edition of the Seventy interpreters. In the Hebrew only seventy are found.

[n: (RAB.)] If Jacob and Joseph with his two sons are not numbered, etc., *as far as* than for the Hebrew verity to make his own discourse suspect.

VERS. 15.-- *And he went down.* According to the situation of the places there is a descent from the land of promise into Egypt, [0442A] and because servitude had succeeded to liberty, Jacob is said to have gone down with his offspring into Egypt.

VERS. 16.-- *They were carried over. [n: (RAB.)]* By synecdoche they are said to be carried over because of Joseph, to whom Jacob, etc., *as far as, Carry my bones hence with you [n: (Gen. L)]*. [var: Vulg. Exod. 13:19 *efferte*, "carry out"]

[n: (BEDA.)] Of Joseph alone the Scripture testifies that his bones, etc., *as far as* the twelve patriarchs are not buried in Arbe, but in Sichem.

*And they were posili, [n: (RAB.)]* Genesis teaches that Abraham, from Ephron the son of Seor the Ethite, etc., *as far as* they would serve neither Moses nor the Lord.

*In the sepulchre,* etc. This of Jacob alone, whose body his sons, taking it out of Egypt, buried in the double cave, where Abraham was buried with Sara, and Isaac with Rebecca: and there Lia was laid: whence that city [0442B] is called Hebron, that is, a marriage, because the fathers were buried there with their wives. But he confounds two histories, and involves the one with the other. For Abraham did not buy from the sons of Emor the sons of Sichem, but Jacob bought Sichem from them; therefore it is thus to be joined: They were carried over into Sichem from the sons of Emor the son of Sichem: as if it were said, into that Sichem which Jacob bought from the sons of Emor the son of Sichem. In Genesis it is read that Sichem was the son of Emor, whence some think that there were two Sichems, of whom the one is said by Luke to be the father of Emor, the other by Genesis the son. Some join the beginning and the end of the saying, saying, from the sons of Sichem, and afterwards bring in, of Emor the son, that this place may agree with Genesis.

VERS. 18.-- *Another king.* That king is the devil, who loves the effeminate, hates the male.

[0442C]VERS. 19.-- *Our kindred,* etc. He says "our fathers," and, "our kindred," lest he should seem to blaspheme the fathers.

VERS. 20.-- *Acceptable to God,* etc. The blessed Stephen shows that Moses did not blaspheme.

VERS. 22.-- *Moses. [n: (RAB.)]* Moses is as much as to say *of the water,* because he was taken out of the water, or *daubing,* because he was found in a basket daubed with bitumen. Moses is a type of the Saviour, who, the devil being struck down, set his own race free.

VERS. 24.-- *He avenged him,* etc. He shows that he who was doing the wrong was not of his own race.

VERS. 26.-- *He appeared to them. [n: (RAB.)]* So Christ after the ascension appeared through the apostles, to the contending [0442D] Jews and unbelievers who resisted the apostles.

VERS. 27.-- *Who hath appointed thee,* etc. As if Moses should answer: No one, but: God alone. There is no power but from God. Hence a certain man had received such an answer from Christ: *O man, who hath appointed me a judge or a divider over you [n: (Luc. XII)] Who hath appointed thee,* etc. In his contention he refutes them, that even then they were contrary to the law and to Moses.

VERS. 28.-- *Wilt thou,* etc. Some think that he who was defended uttered the matter out of anger.

VERS. 29.-- *He fled.* Moses was in Madian, that is, Christ into the nations.

VERS. 30.-- *In a flame of fire,* etc. The fire in the bush is the Holy Spirit, the sins of the people are as thorns, because the ardour of the Holy Spirit did not consume the sins of that people [0443A] which set the thick thorns of wickedness against the benefits of God.

[n: (RAB.)] Therefore the form of fire (which is not imitable) was shown to them, lest any, etc., *as far as* how ready a thing it is for a man to take off his shoe.

VERS. 32.-- *I am. I am,* is the proper word of God, according to that: *I am who am,* etc.: *He who is hath sent me to you.* Serpents are not to be feared, where God speaks present.

VERS. 33.-- *Loose the shoe.* This admonishes us, that standing in the Church (which is holy ground), we should renounce dead works.

VERS 34.-- *Seeing,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] God is wholly sight, wholly hearing, he sees all things, he hears all things, but he is said to see and to hear when he has mercy.

[0443B] *I have seen the affliction. [n: (ID.)]* I who, although I permit someone to be tempted, yet it is not to be thought that I am ignorant of it.

VERS. 35.-- *Whom they denied.* A synecdoche. Many are said to have denied, when one had spoken, or perhaps because many consented to the one denying.

*A prince.* Great praise, that he is called prince and redeemer and leader forth of the people.

VERS. 37.-- *This is Moses.* He turns back upon them the fault which they themselves, disobedient and unbelieving, were casting upon Moses.

VERS. 38.-- *The words of life. [n: (RAB.)]* That is, the two tables with the Decalogue, and the other commandments, of which it is said: *He that shall do them shall live in them.*

VERS. 40.-- *To Aaron. [n: (ID.)]* As to a prince. They speak as ignorant of the Godhead. For the true God [0443C] was not made, but made all things, nor is he a creature, but the Creator of all.

*Make us gods. [n: (ID.)]* They say gods, because of the two pillars, etc., *as far as* the pillar of fire went before the people by night.

VERS. 42.-- *The host of heaven.* The host of heaven is sometimes said to be the army of angels, but in this place he names the stars the host of heaven, since he presently adds, *the star of your God. In the book,* etc. Although the prophets are many, yet it is said to be one book, and among the Hebrews there is one book.

*Did you offer victims. [n: (RAB.)]* Although by the necessity of serving God they made offerings, yet in mind, etc., *as far as* they always returned in heart into Egypt.

It is asked: how did they not offer to God, when it is read [0443D] that they offered certain things to God, not of will, but from the fear of punishments? But God receives not the things which are offered, but the will of those who offer.

VERS. 43.-- *Figures,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The star of your God is Lucifer, to whom they assigned figures. The same gods were adored in figures and in stars.

[n: (ID.)] Some so say "the star of your God," that the word pertains to God, because they received not the Lord himself, but his star.

*And I will carry you away.* For these sacrileges you shall be led captive beyond Babylon. The first martyr is not to be thought to have erred, who, for that which is written in the prophet, They were led beyond Damascus, said, beyond Babylon: for he set down the meaning rather than the word [0444A], because they were led beyond Damascus into Babylon, or beyond Babylon.

VERS. 44.-- *The tabernacle.* Because they said that he acted against the holy place, he shows that he did not speak blasphemy against the holy place

*According to the form. [n: (RAB.)]* Which he had shown him in Sina, because, since it was made also according to a form, the tabernacle itself is shown to have been a form.

[n: (ID.)] Historically it is said with Jesus, because Moses was dead.

VERS. 46.-- *That he might find a tabernacle.* That is, the true temple, of which that was the figure, which should not be changed in place, that is, the Church which he foresaw would be firm and unchangeable, of which the Apostle says: *The firm foundation of the Lord standeth [n: (II Tim. II, 9)]*. [var: Vulg. 2 Tim. 2:19 *fundamentum Dei*, "the foundation of God"] And elsewhere: [0444B] *No man can lay another foundation but that which is laid, which is Christ Jesus [n: (I Cor. III)]*.

VERS. 48.-- *But not the Most High. [n: (RAB.)]* From the saying of Solomon he convicts the Jews, etc., *as far as* that he does not dwell in the temple of the Jews alone.

*As by the prophet,* etc. Of the first tabernacle, which was a shadow and not the truth, he spoke before, and afterward set down the testimony of one speaking of the tabernacle which was truer. *If I shall enter into the tabernacle of the Lord,* etc., at the last sentence he set God. That *in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may stand.* [var: Vulg. Psal. 131:3 *tabernaculum domus meae*, "the tabernacle of my house"]

VERS. 49.-- *Heaven to me.* Thus he shows that he encompasses all things; whence elsewhere he asserts that he measures heaven with a span, and encloses the earth with his fist.

*And the earth.* By the example of the earth he designates every creature [0444C] of the Creator.
