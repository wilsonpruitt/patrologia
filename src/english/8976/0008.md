## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- *In that day. [n: (RAB.)]* On which Stephen was slain, not because it was made then only, but because from that day the great persecution began, one of the princes of the Church having been slain.

*The Church.* A collection or a congregation, or a calling. *They were dispersed.* This is what the Lord commanded: *When [0445C] they shall persecute you in this city, flee into another [n: (Matth. X)]*. For by his nod it was carried on: that by the occasion of tribulation there might be made a seed-plot of the Gospel.

VERS. 2.-- *And devout men took care of Stephen,* that is, men fearing God, either Christians, who were afterwards dispersed, or Jews fearing God though not yet Christians.

VERS. 3.-- *Saul. [n: (RAB.)]* From Saul the persecutor, who was king of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, of which Paul also was; for indeed according to the custom of the ancients descendants draw their names from their forebears.

*He laid waste. [n: (ID.)]* As a wolf the flock, according to the prophecy of Jacob: *Benjamin a ravening wolf, in the morning shall eat the prey, at evening shall divide the spoil [n: (Gen. XLIX)]*.

*Dragging.* He dragged, he did not kill, because perhaps the Lord was guarding his hands, lest they should be polluted with the blood of the innocent.

*Dispersed. [n: (RAB.)]* Although dispersed by fear, yet they were steadfast in the preaching of the Gospel.

VERS. 5.-- *Philip.* He was of the number of those that were scattered, who first preached Christ to Samaria. A deacon second from Stephen. He is related as second after him, as it were his heir and of the same degree.

VERS. 6.-- *They gave heed. [n: (RAB.)]* They believed, hearing the words and seeing the signs. Two causes of faith: the words of preaching, and the signs of the miracles. From the present chapter and from the history of the Samaritan woman it is proved that the minds of this people were ready to believe, says Bede.

[0446A]VERS. 11.-- *By magic arts. [n: (RAB.)]* Magicians philosophize about particular things, about the nature of the world, the course of the stars, and their motions. *Seducing.* Leading apart and away from the truth, or turning them into excess, or into stupor and wonder, by his tricks. *He had bereft of mind.* To bereave of mind is to make the rational mind absent.

VERS. 12.-- *Philip. [n: (RAB.)]* Some say that this Philip was an apostle, and one of the twelve, but it does not seem true according to the order of this book.

*Of Jesus Christ. [n: (ID.)]* Of whom they were ignorant, or (as has been said before) in the name of Jesus he preached the Trinity, and he who perfectly believes one person only of the Trinity cannot lack the belief of the other two.

[0446B]VERS. 13.-- *Then Simon. [n: (ID.)]* Either he believed the Lord, by the words of Philip and by the power, etc., *as far as* having fraudulently entered the Church, they steal baptism.

VERS. 14.-- *They sent.* Peter joins John to himself, because virginity is acceptable to the Church, and pleases.

VERS. 16.-- *In the name.* It is not to be read thus, *in the name of Jesus only,* as though the other persons were excluded, but thus: *they were only baptized,* etc., that is, not yet confirmed. *Of Jesus.* Yet before, they *credeban* in the Father and in the Holy Spirit, as did the other Jews. Or, the full faith of one person confirms the faith of the Trinity.

VERS. 17.-- *They laid on hands. [n: (BEDA.)]* Philip, who preached the Gospel in Samaria, one of the seven, etc., *as far as* when he delivers the Spirit the Paraclete to the baptized.

VERS. 19.-- *Power. [n: (RAB.)]* Not of baptizing [0446C] only in water and of preaching, or of working miracles, which Philip had, but of laying hands upon whomsoever, etc.

VERS. 20.-- *Thy money.* Not ours, who do not covet, but *thine,* which thou comparest with the Spirit, that thou mayest sell it for him, and buy it with the Spirit. Therefore he wished to buy the Spirit, that selling him to others he might be able to receive more.

*Money. [n: (RAB.)]* From this an example is taken, that the alms of the lost be not received.

The apostles, not willing the money, say, *be it with thee,* that is, keep thine own, and this, unto perdition, that, the money being unjustly gathered, thou mayest pay an eternal penalty.

VERS. 22.-- *Do penance.* For the covetousness, and the depraved sense by which thou hast thought that a traffic of the Spirit could [0446D] be made.

[n: (RAB.)] That all may see what thou art. Not that Peter is ignorant that he will not do penance, but because it is just to preach to all, he does his own office, that the man may be inexcusable. Before all men also he is rebuked, lest they be any more deceived by him.

*Pray to God.* To whom the heart lies open, of whom the Prophet says: *How good is God to Israel [n: (Psal. LXXII)]*; whom no man can deceive, because *the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the deceitful [n: (Sap. I)]*.

VERS. 24.-- *Pray ye.* By reason of despair he does not himself dare to pray, but asks the apostles to pray for him, nor does he dare to pray for himself, who does not believe that he will be heard. To the apostles also he speaks feignedly. [0447A] *Which you have said.* Not, which I have believed, but as an unbeliever he imputes to them the things which are to come.

VERS. 26.-- *And an angel.* After the return of the apostles, the history is woven concerning Philip. *To the way,* etc. For the way goes down, and by the way one goes down, from the nature of the place.

VERS. 27.-- *An Ethiopian.* By nature and by desert. For an Ethiopian is dark. Whence: *Ethiopia shall prevent her hands to God [n: (Psal. LXVII)]*.

*A man of power,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The queen of the Ethiopians sending her treasurer before her, etc., *as far as, Hearken, O daughter, and see, and incline thy ear [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*, etc.

*Of queen Candace.* It was the custom of that region to be ruled by women, and that they should be called Candaces.

VERS. 28.-- *He was returning.* He was beginning to return, reading [0447B] Isaias. Wherever in the New Testament the beginning of salvation is related, the testimony is taken from Isaias, who is interpreted *the salvation of the Lord.*

VERS. 30.-- *Thinkest thou that thou understandest,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] They know each other's tongues, either through the learning of the eunuch or through the spirit of Philip.

VERS. 31.-- *How,* etc. Here he reproves the boldness of those who think that they understand Holy Scripture without a teacher.

VERS. 33.-- *In humility. [n: (BEDA.)]* For indeed the judge of all found not the truth of judgment, but without any fault of his own, by the sedition of the Jews and by the voice of Pilate, he was condemned.

*Humility. [n: (RAB.)]* By the passion in which *he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death [n: (Philip. II)]*.

[0447C] *He shall be taken away.* The second man from heaven, heavenly, separated by holiness from earthly things, or, he shall pass from life into death.

VERS. 35.-- *He opened. [n: (RAB.)]* He opened the treasure of knowledge. Or he opened his mouth, that the Spirit might speak in him, according to that: *Open wide thy mouth, and I will fill it [n: (Psal. LXXX)]*.

Mystically, Philip opened the mouth of the Church, that from this he whom before they knew not might be preached to the Gentiles.

*He preached the Gospel.* He solves the question of him that asked: *Of whom did the prophet speak?* But he shows that it was prophesied of Jesus.

VERS. 36.-- *To a certain water.* For to this the preaching had led, that he should need water, the things which he had heard on the way concerning baptism.

[0447D]VERS. 37.-- *If thou believest. [n: (RAB.)]* He was not ignorant of his faith, but wished an open confession, that to those who are to baptize and to those who are to be baptized an example might be given of questioning and of confessing.

VERS. 39.-- *The Spirit of the Lord. [n: (RAB.)]* Or the Spirit rushed upon him mightily, as also upon the apostles, etc., *as far as* upon whom the Spirit had descended?

VERS. 40.-- *He was found.* By men, he who in his rapture was not found, because he walked in the wilderness, or with exceeding swiftness.

*Caesarea. [n: (BEDA.)]* He means the Caesarea of Palestine, where below he is described to have had a house, which is shown even to this day, and also the chamber of his four daughters, virgins that prophesied.
