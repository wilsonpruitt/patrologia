## CHAPTER X.

VERS. 1.-- *A man, however,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] By the merit of his perfection and by his manly work this centurion is called a man, according to [0450B] that: *There was a man in the land of Hus, whose name was Job.*

*Cornelius. [n: (BEDA.)]* Not by virtues did he attain to faith, but by faith he attained to virtues. [n: (GREG.)] Cornelius, whose alms before baptism, an angel bearing witness, etc., *as far as* but in faith he is made solid through works.

VERS. 2.-- *Religious,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] All these things by the good of nature, etc., *as far as* without manly seed, that is, he may be believed to have conceived by the teaching of a master.

VERS. 3.-- *He saw. [n: (RAB.)]* Not in the night, not in sleep, because the Gentiles, being of a gross understanding, etc., *as far as* as was shown before in the example of Peter and John.

*An angel of God. [n: (ID.)]* Not of Satan. In this the apostles are excused who preach to the Gentiles and baptize them: for how should that be grudged by a man which was made manifest [0450C] by an angel? *Entering in,* to him perhaps, calling him, and occasion is given to Peter of excusing himself to the Jews who dispute against him and say: *Why hast thou gone in to men having the foreskin [n: (Act. XI)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. Act. 11:3 *Quare introisti ad viros praeputium habentes*, "Wherefore hast thou gone in"] *O Cornelius.* A familiarity is shown, by which the name of a gentile man was inserted into the acquaintance of the angels.

VERS. 5.-- *And now send men,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Here begins the recompense, that God may have mercy on thee who art merciful, because: *Blessed are the merciful, for mercy,* etc. [n: (Matth. V)]. [var: Vulg. Matth. 5:7 *quoniam ipsi misericordiam consequentur*, "for they shall obtain mercy"; Migne's quotation lacks *ipsi*]

*Joppa. [n: (ID.)]* It is a city of the land of promise, nearest to the Tyrrhene sea, and situated upon its eastern shore, into which Jonas going down entered a ship, that he might flee.

VERS. 6.-- *He lodgeth,* etc. Peter lodges in [0450D] the house of Simon, that is, of one obeying, who is a tanner, because a teacher of the Church gladly dwells there where there are obedient men, who chastise the body and bring it into subjection, having crossed over the waves of the world by liberty of mind. For by the constancy of faith they despise the fleeting glory of the world and its terrors.

VERS. 7.-- *And when he was departed. [n: (RAB.)]* He does not say, when Cornelius had awaked, because to him the angel appeared neither through a dream, nor in ecstasy, but manifestly.

*A soldier. [n: (ID.)]* He does not say the household servants fearing the Lord, because at the beginning he had said that he was religious, fearing the Lord with all his house.

VERS. 9.-- *As they drew near,* etc. Another reading: *As they appeared,* namely those who were sent by Cornelius, [0451A] that is, drawing near in such wise that they could be seen from the house of Simon the tanner.

[n: (RAB.)] For *into the upper parts,* another reading has *into the upper room,* and rightly: because the gentile people is as it were the upper house: but the Synagogue is beneath, because sunk in the world.

In saying *into the upper parts,* he signified that the Church, the coveting of earthly things being forsaken, would have her conversation in the heavens. At the sixth hour Peter, amid his prayers, hungers, and that for the salvation of the world, which Jesus came in the sixth age of the world to seek and to save: which he showed when at that same hour he sat over the well of Samaria.

VERS. 10.-- *When he was hungry. [n: (RAB.)]* By compassion he hungers for the salvation of the Gentiles, which is now shown in the vision, which was therefore seen at the sixth hour, because it was wrought in the sixth age [0451B] of the world.

VERS. 11.-- *Heaven opened. [n: (BEDA.)]* The opening of heaven is the revelation of the heavenly mysteries shortly to come to the Gentiles. And in this he designates that the heavens are opened to the Gentiles, and that an entrance is to be laid wide.

*A certain vessel. [n: (ID.)]* That vessel designates the Church, etc., *as far as* through whom the Church, steeped in the heavenly gift, is exalted.

*A linen cloth.* For the Church, for the whiteness of holiness, and for the mortification of the flesh. *From heaven to earth. [n: (RAB.)]* Thence we are admonished that where our head is and our root (whose branches we are), thither the anchor of our hope may penetrate.

VERS. 12.-- *All fourfooted beasts.* All the Gentiles who shall believe and who are to be taken up into the unity of the body of Christ, that all may be made his members.

[0451C]VERS. 13.-- *Kill.* Cleanse, mortify, and slay, that thou mayest make them victims to God, that being plucked out of oldness they may pass over into newness.

VERS. 14.-- *I have eaten,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] He that eats food set outside transfers it into his own body. He commanded therefore that the Gentiles, set outside through unbelief, be grafted into the fellowship of the Church (which Peter signifies).

[n: (ID.)] Peter thought the Gentiles unclean, etc., *as far as* but may bind fast in the love of God alone.

VERS. 21.-- *Going down. [n: (BEDA.)]* He is bidden to come down from the roof, and to go to preach, etc., *as far as, But the conversation of the saints is in the heavens [n: (Philipp. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Philipp. 3:20 *Nostra autem conversatio in caelis est*, "But our conversation is in heaven"]

*Behold, I am he,* etc. He shows that he knows of their coming, giving also an example of humility, dismissing a circuit [0451D] of words.

*What is the cause,* etc. The Spirit said that the soldiers were at hand, and was silent for what cause they had come, because for the keeping of humility the spirit of prophecy sometimes touches the mind in part, and in part touches it not.

VERS. 22.-- *He received an answer.* To his alms and his prayers the Lord answered by an angel. *Into the house.* By this they persuade him, because the angel commanded that he should enter into his house, although he was a gentile.

VERS. 23.-- *Bringing them in,* etc. Here begins the communion of the apostles with the Gentiles, because he had gone down to them who had heard from the angel the command that they should enter into the house of a gentile, therefore he received the Gentiles into hospitality.

VERS. 24.-- *His kinsmen being called together,* etc. Twofold [0452A] is the cause of the calling together; nearness of kindred, and friendship.

VERS. 27.-- *And talking with him,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The holiness of his action earned the communion of equality, just as in the fault of Ananias and Sapphira the zeal of his vengeance laid open that of his power.

VERS. 30, 31.-- *And behold a man,* etc. He does not say an angel, so far was he from disdain, and he says: *Cornelius, thy prayer hath been heard, and thine alms have been had in memory before God.* [var: Vulg. Act. 10:31 *eleemosynae tuae commemoratae sunt in conspectu Dei*, "thy alms are remembered in the sight of God"]

*In the sight of God. [n: (RAB.)]* In the Greek, *In the sight of God we are present.* This is rightly said by him who was ready to obey God, believing him to be everywhere present.

VERS. 34.-- *He is not a respecter of persons,* etc. [0452B] [n: (BEDA.)] It is plain that God is not a respecter of persons, etc., *as far as* but that they should receive all that believe.

VERS. 37.-- *You know,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] It is to be read thus: You know Jesus as John preached him: Jesus of Nazareth, how he anointed him, etc. Or: this John preached, that he anointed him, not indeed after an earthly manner, but by the spirit and the power of signs.

VERS. 38.-- *Who went about,* etc. In the Greek is added, *through forty days,* not that they ate and drank with him daily, for it would be contrary to John, who interposes eight days in which he was not seen by them, that he might be made manifest the third time, and for as many days he fasted before his death, being in need of food, whereas he ate after the resurrection being now not in need of food. He fasted before his death, that we might abstain from the desires [0452C] of this world. He ate, teaching by this that he is with us until the consummation of the world. Fasting is in the contest, because he who is in the struggle abstains from all things. Food is in the hope of peace, which shall be perfect, when this mortal shall have put on immortality. But we, while we take the way of the Lord, fast from the vanity of the world, and are refreshed by the promise to come.

[n: (RAB.)] Through forty days, not continually, but at certain turns taken within that quadragenary number of days.

VERS. 41.-- *And we drank. [n: (BEDA.)]* Here Peter sets forth what is passed over in silence in the Gospel, that after the resurrection he drank with Christ, unless perhaps we believe it to be indicated there, where he says: *Until I drink it with you in the kingdom of my Father [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*. [var: Vulg. Matth. 26:29 *cum illud bibam vobiscum novum in regno Patris mei*, "when I shall drink it new with you"]

[0452D]VERS. 45.-- *The Spirit.* The Spirit is the house of God, inasmuch as he is given to those who through him love God; but with himself he is God, even if he be given to none, coeternal with the Father and the Son. Nor, because they give, and he himself is given, is he less than they. For he is so given as the gift of God, that he gives himself as God: *For the Spirit breatheth where he will [n: (Joan. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Joan. 3:8 *Spiritus ubi vult spirat*, without *enim*]

VERS. 47.-- *Can any man forbid water,* etc. As if, God first gave what is greater, and inverted the order, can he forbid what is less You have come for this, that you may be witnesses, therefore judge.

VERS. 48.-- *In the name of Christ.* Whereas the rule of the Church is that baptism be delivered in the name of the Trinity, Luke nevertheless testifies that it was given in the name of Jesus Christ. Ambrose, resolving this, says that through the unity of the name [0453A] the mystery is made full, because he who says Christ designates also the Father, from whom the Son is, and the Holy Spirit: he also who says the Father shows alike both the Son and the Spirit: and he who names the Spirit calls also the Father, from whom the Spirit proceeds, and the Son, whose Spirit he also is. Whence, that authority may be joined to reason, one is baptized also in the Spirit, according to that: *But you shall be baptized in the Holy Spirit [n: (Act. XI)]*, and the Apostle: *In one Spirit we have been baptized [n: (I Cor. XII)]*. [var: Vulg. Act. 11:16 *vos autem baptizabimini Spiritu Sancto*, without *in*] [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 12:13 *Etenim in uno Spiritu omnes nos in unum corpus baptizati sumus*]
