## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And there were in the Church,* etc. The same men were prophets and teachers. Or, some of them were prophets, others teachers.

*Niger. [n: (RAB.)]* From the colour, if it is Latin. Or, it is interpreted *ascending,* if Hebrew.

VERS. 4.-- *Sent by the Holy Spirit. [n: (RAB.)]* Dismissed by men, they are straightway sent by the Holy Spirit to Seleucia. He was the guide of their journey who separated them [0454D] from the rest.

*They went to Seleucia. [n: (ID.)]* In certain copies is found: *They went to Seleucia, which is Salamina;* but they are two names of one city.

VERS. 7.-- *To Sergius Paulus.* They are two names of one man, Sergius and Paulus.

VERS. 8.-- *Elymas. [n: (RAB.)]* Elymas, according to some, is interpreted *the measure of my God,* or *Magician,* and therefore he resisted.

*To turn away. [n: (ID.)]* Him who had begun to be converted he wished to turn from the faith to transgression, according to his own name.

VERS. 9.-- *Saul. [n: (ID.)]* Saul is interpreted *the temptation of one looking back,* or *security,* or *wonderful.*

[0455A] *Who is also Paul. [n: (BEDA.)]* From Sergius Paulus the proconsul (whom he subdued to the faith) he obtained by lot the name of Paul.

Fitly does the apostle of the Gentiles obtain his name from a gentile.

[n: (RAB.)] From Sergius Paulus the apostle was called Paul, etc., *as far as, I am the least of the apostles [n: (I Cor. XV)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 15:9 *Ego enim sum minimus apostolorum*; Migne's quotation lacks *enim*]

VERS. 11.-- *And now,* etc. *Thou shalt be blind,* etc. It behoved Paul to work miracles for the perfecting of his apostleship, whom the Lord had chosen while long kicking against the goads.

VERS. 14.-- *Antioch of Pisidia. [n: (RAB.)]* For distinction from the other Antioch, as is believed; but by many it is thought to be the same.

*They sat down. [n: (ID.)]* As about to hear the law, not to preach. Behold an example of humility.

[0455B]VERS. 19.-- *The land of Chanaan,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] That is, of promise, which was therefore (as it seems to most) given to Israel, etc., *as far as* and therefore he deserved to be accursed.

VERS. 20.-- *He gave,* etc. The time of the judges passes, for the proving of the patience of God.

VERS. 26.-- *To you. [n: (RAB.)]* To the Jews. *I came not but to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel [n: (Matth. XV)]*. [var: Vulg. Matth. 15:24 *Non sum missus nisi ad oves quae perierunt domus Israel*, "I was not sent but to the sheep"] Or, to you who are dispersed, because (as follows) the inhabitants of Jerusalem rejected it.

VERS. 27.-- *Judging. [n: (ID.)]* That Jesus was guilty of death. Or, judging the voices of the prophets, that is, not believing: for he who detracteth from his brother detracteth from the law, and judgeth the law, how much more they who detract from Christ?

*The holy things of David faithful. [n: (BEDA.)]* Isaias, foretasting the mysteries of the New Testament, says: *I will appoint for you [0455C] an everlasting testament, the holy things of David faithful.* In the Hebrew: *And I will make with you an everlasting covenant, the faithful mercies of David.* [var: Vulg. Is. 55:3 *et feriam vobiscum pactum sempiternum, misericordias David fideles*; the first quotation, which Migne ascribes to Isaias, stands in neither Is. 55:3 nor Act. 13:34 (*dabo vobis sancta David fidelia*)]

VERS. 35.-- *Thou wilt not give,* etc. [n: (ID.)] After the resurrection he dies not, death shall no more have dominion over him, and therefore he says holy things faithful, that is, incorrupt and about to remain eternally.

VERS. 41.-- *Behold, ye despisers.* In the Greek: Hear, ye despisers, and look, and be destroyed.

*Wonder. [n: (RAB.)]* Join it thus: *Wonder and be scattered* by the Assyrians and Chaldeans, last of all by the Romans, because I work a work of both vengeances. *You will not believe,* etc. Or, on account of the greatness of both vengeances, they are said not to believe until they be fulfilled. [var: Vulg. Act. 13:41 *disperdimini*, "be destroyed"; Migne prints *dispergimini*, "be scattered", and glosses it of the scatterings by Assyrians, Chaldeans and Romans]

[0455D]VERS. 43.-- *And of the worshippers. [n: (ID.)]* It is well read *worshipping God,* as it is had in the Greek. And it signifies those who were by nature gentiles, but by religion Jews, called in Greek proselytes.

VERS. 46.-- *First. [n: (ID.)]* Jesus came first to heal the daughter of the ruler, but the woman suffering an issue of blood snatched the healing before her. *Of eternal life.* Not because they knew that to be eternal life which they rejected, but the apostles spoke out of their own understanding and their own faith.

VERS. 47.-- *For so,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The prophecy is to us an unmoveable precept: first it is spoken, then the testimony of the Prophet is brought in, to the person of the Father speaking to the Son.

*I have set thee.* He does not say, *I have set you,* because what is [0456A] said to Christ pertains to the apostles, who are his members. *I have set thee for a light to the Gentiles. [n: (BEDA.)]* This was said specially to Christ, and yet the apostles judge that the same was said to themselves, because they are his members: as, on account of the union of the same body, it was said, *Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?* etc. [var: Vulg. Act. 13:47 *Posui te in lucem gentium*, "I have set thee to be the light of the Gentiles"]

VERS. 48.-- *Foreordained.* This is the order, that after present labours and the mortification of the flesh, life should follow.

VERS. 50.-- *Religious women. [n: (RAB.)]* Either of the Jews who kept the religion of the law, or of the gentiles beginning to preserve religion.

VERS. 51.-- *Shaking off the dust,* etc. According to the precept of the Gospel, the dust is shaken off for a testimony that they made their labour void.

[0456B] [n: (RAB.)] Deservedly is the dust shaken off, because in the judgment they shall be so cast out by Christ, as *the dust which the wind driveth from the face of the earth [n: (Psal. I)]*. But if they received the apostles, they would loose their feet, that is their senses, from the dust, that is from vices.

## CHAPTER XIV.

VERS. 6.-- *They fled. [n: (RAB.)]* The flight of the apostles was not more out of fear than out of love of the word of God. *And all,* etc. That which follows in the Greek: *And God made peace,* some Latin copies have not.

VERS. 10.-- *They lifted up. [n: (RAB.)]* In this wantonness is noted. Or, the meaning was higher than their own barbarous tongue, ignorant of the one God.

VERS. 12.-- *Crowns. [n: (ID.)]* That he might adorn the front of the temple according to [0456C] the rite of the gentiles, or might set them upon them (as about to sacrifice to gods). It was the custom of the gentiles: that those sacrificing to the honour of their gods should take crowns, the victims being crowned withal.

VERS. 16.-- *Filling with food,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He speaks of carnal things, because gladness is from meats and from wine which maketh glad the heart of man.

VERS. 18.-- *They drew him. [n: (ID.)]* To be stoned like a corpse, of this he himself to the Corinthians: *Once I was stoned [n: (II Cor. XI)]*, etc.

VERS. 21.-- *That they should continue in the faith. [n: (ID.)]* Against many tribulations: because the multitude of tribulations demands perseverance of faith. *Because through many tribulations.* Labour is set first, thereafter the entrance of the kingdom is proclaimed, because the hope of the reward is the solace of [0456D] labour.
