## CHAPTER XV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *By sedition.* ⟦s2⟧ By Paul and Barnabas, against those who said these things.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They determined. [n: (RAB.)]* Namely those who had come down from Judaea, etc., *as far as* he is proved to have come to the faith.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *And the apostles came together. [n: (ID.)]* Here is given an example of holding a council, for discerning those things which are necessary to the faith.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *By my mouth. [n: (ID.)]* Not of another of the apostles, as if he should say: I who first preached to the Gentiles will first shake off from them the yoke of the law. ⟦s2⟧ For to the eunuch none of the apostles preached: but Philip the deacon, and he was filled with a gratuitous gift.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0457A]VERS. 9.-- *Purifying by faith. [n: (CHRYS.)]* By faith alone, he says, they attained those things, and not by works or by circumcision. ⟦s2⟧ For they say these things not only as rendering an account for the Gentiles, but as desiring also to lead those others away from the law: yet this as yet they do not say.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And all the multitude held their peace.* ⟦s2⟧ The order of speaking, or of the speakers, is to be noted, because while each one speaks they keep silence, Peter first.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And after that,* etc. [n: (CHRYS.)] This man was bishop of the Church of Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* where, namely, he also sets an end to works.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *I will return and rebuild,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ I will return to the Gentiles, whom he had before said to go into their own ways, and for the strengthening of which sense James brought in a testimony. [0457B] This testimony plainly agrees with the words of Simon.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] The shadow of the law, which, corrupted by the traditions of the Pharisees, was raised up again through grace when the Lord revealed it, that is, when the Lord appeared in the flesh, that not only the Jews, but the Gentiles also might seek his name.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Upon whom hath been invoked. [n: (ID.)]* According to that: *The priests shall invoke my name upon the children of Israel [n: (Num. VI)]*; and elsewhere: *Moses and Aaron his priests [n: (Psal. XCVIII)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. Num. 6:27 *Invocabuntque nomen meum super filios Israel*, "And they shall invoke my name upon the children of Israel"; the Clementine has no *sacerdotes*] [var: Vulg. Psal. 98:6 *Moyses et Aaron in sacerdotibus ejus*, "Moses and Aaron among his priests"; Migne's quotation lacks *in*]

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It hath been invoked.* ⟦s2⟧ For the future, after the manner of the prophets.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *I judge. [n: (RAB.)]* It was rightly to be judged, since it is confirmed by the testimony of three, of two apostles and of the prophets.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *And from things strangled.* ⟦s2⟧ A thing strangled is that which has died of itself, [0457C] of which Ezechiel says: *Every thing dead of itself, and taken by a beast, of birds and of cattle, let not the priests eat [n: (Ezech. IV)]*. ⟦s3⟧ It is fitting that they keep this precept who are anointed with spiritual oil, that they eat not that whose blood has not been poured out, which the epistle of the apostles decreed to be kept. [var: Vulg. Ezech. 44:31 *Omne morticinum, et captum a bestia, de avibus et de pecoribus, non comedent sacerdotes*, "the priests shall not eat"]

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And from blood. [n: (RAB.)]* By the shedding of human blood, etc., *as far as* but that they be chaste and abstain.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Then it pleased. [n: (ID.)]* Peter speaking, Barnabas and Paul relating, James the bishop of Jerusalem judging, the sentence deservedly pleased. ⟦s2⟧ *It pleased them to send to Antioch.* ⟦s3⟧ Legates sent by Christ can send their own legates.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *The apostles and the ancients.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the apostles the [0457D] common title is set first, lest any occasion of dissension be left.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To the brethren of the Gentiles. [n: (RAB.)]* The first benefit of the style is that the apostles call gentiles brethren, that is, sons of one God. ⟦s2⟧ *Greeting.* ⟦s3⟧ An ellipsis, because every word is too narrow for the breadth of charity, and therefore no word is set down, so that they may be understood to wish them all good things together.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Subverting souls.* ⟦s2⟧ In the Greek: *Subverting your souls, saying: It behoveth you to be circumcised, and to keep the law to whom we gave no commandment.*

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *It hath seemed good.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, it pleased. ⟦s3⟧ *To the Holy Spirit.* ⟦s4⟧ Who, being arbiter of his own power, breatheth where he will, and speaketh what he will.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0458A] *To the Holy Spirit. [n: (RAB.)]* And to us, with a human understanding indeed, but a sound one.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And to us.* ⟦s2⟧ Yet not by our own will alone, but by the prompting of the same Spirit.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Than these necessary things. [n: (RAB.)]* These are necessary things without which there is no salvation, but circumcision is not necessary: but the things which you would not have done to you, do not to another.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *That you abstain,* etc. [n: (TERT.)] Therefore then, since either kind, etc., *as far as* whose cleanness pertains more to God than that of the entrails.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *And it seemed good [n: (RAB.)]* It is the matter of the discourse to follow: *But Judas alone went to Jerusalem.*

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *A dissension.* ⟦s2⟧ They did not offend in this, [0458B] because not every commotion is a sin, but the unreasonable one, and that which is not justly made. ⟦s3⟧ *Mark being taken.* ⟦s4⟧ He was joined to Barnabas by the bond of affinity, whence they are also read to have been cousins. ⟦s5⟧ Barnabas, dismissed by Paul, sought again his native island, and none the less carried out the work of the Gospel enjoined upon him.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *Silas being chosen. [n: (RAB.)]* For this the Lord made him remain, according to that: *And it seemed good to Silas to remain there,* that he might work together with Paul as Mark with Barnabas. ⟦s2⟧ For in this there was a divine counsel.

## CHAPTER XVI.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The son of a widow. [n: (RAB.)]* Or, of a Jewess. ⟦s2⟧ But that she was by race a Jewess appears from this, that he says he was sprung *of a gentile father.* [var: Vulg. Act. 16:1 *filius mulieris Judaeae fidelis*, "the son of a Jewess that believed"; Migne's lemma reads *Viduae filius*, the reading the gloss then offers as an alternative]

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0458C]VERS. 3.-- *And he circumcised him. [n: (ID.)]* He circumcised Timothy because of the scandal of the Jews, lest he should seem to condemn the law of Moses, but not to impose it upon the Gentiles as though not necessary. ⟦s2⟧ Titus he did not circumcise, lest the Gentiles should take from him occasion of being circumcised. ⟦s3⟧ And this is what he says: *But because of false brethren unawares brought in,* etc. [var: Vulg. Gal. 2:4 *sed propter subintroductos falsos fratres*; Migne prints *fratres falsos*]

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *To keep the decrees, which were. [n: (ID.)]* They did not preach what they themselves had done, but that which the apostles had decreed for the will of God. ⟦s2⟧ And it is an example of rightly teaching even what thou hast not done. ⟦s3⟧ In a figure of this deed the priests were clothed with linen breeches, as though about to sow a work, but not as though about to do it.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And the churches indeed,* etc. [n: (ID.)] This has been [0458D] more often expounded in the earlier chapters, when we said that by a sentence of this kind the earlier narrative is closed, and the beginning of the following is made.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *They attempted to go. [n: (ID.)]* The apostles are permitted to will, but not to go, etc., *as far as* Luke in Bithynia, etc.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *A man of Macedonia.* ⟦s2⟧ The angel of that nation likened to a man of Macedonia, by the property of the tongue, or by a special form, or by the judgment of the word.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Help us. [n: (RAB.)]* This was said in the person of a man, for angels do not need human help. ⟦s2⟧ Or the angel says this on account of those over whom he presides, that they may be baptized and be saved.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Help us.* ⟦s2⟧ He shows that the Macedonians, or the angels, are inspired by [0459A] God, or that certain teachers had begun to be taught.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Samothracia.* ⟦s2⟧ Thracia, Neapolis and Philippi are cities.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Certain days, conferring.* ⟦s2⟧ In certain books is found: Conferring the word of the Lord on the sabbath day.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *A seller of purple.* ⟦s2⟧ A queen clothed in royal purple, or a weaver of purple, or one selling purple, signifies the Church which sells purple while she pours out blood for Christ, that life may be purchased. ⟦s3⟧ Purple rightly designates blood by its colour and by its nature, because it is made from the blood of shellfish. ⟦s4⟧ Lydda compelled the apostle, with his companions, to enter her house: because the Church received with a devout heart the preaching rejected by the Jews.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0459B]VERS. 15.-- *She constrained us. [n: (RAB.)]* Here is given an example, or two examples are given: one of constraining guests, the other of entering in to the newly converted.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Following Paul. [n: (ID.)]* Although she had met them, the woman cries out not to their face, but behind them, keeping modesty.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *They cast them into prison. [n: (ID.)]* By this the power of the deliverer is the greater. ⟦s2⟧ To the same purpose looks also that which follows: *He put into the inner prison, and made their feet fast in the stocks.* [var: Vulg. Act. 16:24 *misit eos in interiorem carcerem*, "he put them into the inner prison"; Migne's quotation lacks *eos*]

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *And at midnight,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Prayer is to be made at midnight, and in dangers the diligence of prayer is to be kept.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *A great earthquake was made,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The devotion of them that pray and the power of prayer is expressed, [0459C] because while they sing hymns in the prison their praise moves the earth: it shakes the foundations of the prison, opens the doors, looses the chains of the bound.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *He fell down,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] On account of power and knowledge: power, because by the earthquake the doors were opened: knowledge, because Paul knew why he wished to kill himself: therefore before all he fell down at the feet of Paul and Silas.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *He washed their stripes. [n: (BED.)]* A beautiful variety of things. ⟦s2⟧ Those whose stripes of wounding he washed, through them he lost the stripes of his crime. ⟦s3⟧ *Immediately. [n: (RAB.)]* The swiftness of obedience is betokened. ⟦s4⟧ The grace of the Holy Spirit knows no slow endeavours.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *And when he had brought them, he set a table before them,* etc. [n: (ID.)] An excellent example of both: [0459D] the entering of the house, and the setting of the table.
