## CHAPTER XVII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And of the worshippers and of the Gentiles.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, both of those who had exchanged the Gentile rite for Judaism, and of those who had remained Gentiles, many were flowing together to Christ.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] The first cause of the malice is that these are of the common crowd; the second, that they were evil even beyond the crowd.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *They dragged Jason. [n: (BED.)]* in the Greek: *Jason and certain other brethren.* ⟦s2⟧ Whence it is to be understood that Jason was a brother and faithful.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Another king. [n: (RAB.)]* Besides Caesar. ⟦s2⟧ This was against the decree of Caesar, whence in the Gospel: [0460A] *Every one that maketh himself a king, speaketh against Caesar [n: (Joan. XVIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ But the apostles spoke against it, that Christ was king of the Jews, that is, of them that confess, whence the Jews also accused Christ himself.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *By Paul.* ⟦s2⟧ Who was the leader of the word: and therefore afterward he alone was sent away, that he might go from Macedonia to the sea.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *They departed. [n: (RAB.)]* From Athens those brethren who had conducted Paul departed. ⟦s2⟧ Some think it was said of Silas and Timothy, that they departed from Macedonia to Paul.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The Epicureans. [n: (ID.)]* They are so called from their author, who is said to be the champion of pleasure. ⟦s2⟧ Stoics in Greek, in Latin elements; who contended that sins were equal, that is, that every sin is uniform.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0460B] *What meaneth this word-sower.* ⟦s2⟧ A word-sower is he who sows the word; or a word-sower, that is, a *sermologus*; because the word of God is seed; whence: If we have sown unto you, etc.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] We read that Paul the apostle was called a sower of words, which was said by them that mocked, but is not to be rejected by them that believe; for he was indeed a sower of words, but a reaper of morals. ⟦s2⟧ And we too, though little ones, and not to be compared with him, sow the word of God in God's field (which is your heart), looking for an abundant crop, or fruit, from your morals.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But others. [n: (RAB.)]* From the Jews they had heard that the name of demons was abominable, and therefore they compare Paul's teaching to this abomination; or it is the Jews [0460C] who say this, that is, the Sadducees.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *To the Areopagus. [n: (RAB.)]* It is the name of the court at Athens, called from the idol of Mars, which among them is called Arios.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22, 23.-- *Men of Athens,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *What you worship in ignorance, that I preach.* ⟦s3⟧ He answers to that which they had said or had put forward: *Thou bringest in certain new things,* as if he said: The new things which I bring in you have among your old ones. ⟦s4⟧ And this he says for persuasion, lest they refute his words as new.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To an unknown God. [n: (RAB.)]* That is, to the true God, whom no one knows as he is, etc., *as far as* that which is passed over in silence, or it was simply written: *To an unknown God.*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *God, who made the world.* ⟦s2⟧ Note the order of this disputation, because first he teaches that one God is the author [0460D] of the world, of all things, in whom we live, and are moved, and are, whose offspring also we are: so that not only for light and life, but also for the kinship of race, he may be shown to be one who ought to be loved.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Of one all the race of men.* ⟦s2⟧ In the Greek: *of one blood,* because blood designates the propagation of the flesh, and by the flesh man is understood.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *For in him we live. [n: (AUG.)]* Because he works that in us, which is that *we live, are moved, and are,* etc., *as far as* because what is not of him, of whom it is said: *Of him, and by him, and in him are all things [n: (Rom. II)]*?

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As also certain of your own poets.* ⟦s2⟧ To those who did not receive the prophets he speaks the [0461A] testimony, not of Moses, not of Isaias, or of any of the prophets, but of their own authors, setting down a verse of Aratus, and out of their own false things, which they could not contradict, he preserves and confirms his own truth. ⟦s3⟧ *For his,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ This testimony was taken from the poet Aratus, not from the divine page, lest he should seem to bring in new things.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Being therefore,* etc., *we ought not. [n: (RAB.)]* Man is in the middle, having God as higher, gold from silver, with the rest of this kind as lower, which ought not to be compared to him.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *God despising. [n: (ID.)]* That is, pardoning them as venial, and then despising, that is, judging them unworthy of his notice.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *Giving faith to all. [n: (ID.)]* By the example of his own resurrection: or, he has appointed to judge, and afterward it is to be joined thus, giving faith to all that [0461B] they shall rise again.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Dionysius. [n: (BED.)]* This is that Dionysius who, being afterward ordained bishop of the Corinthians, ruled the Church well, for whose profit also he left many volumes, taking his surname from the place over which he presided. ⟦s2⟧ The Areopagus is the court of Athens, drawing its name from Mars, who in Greek is called Arios, *pagus*, a village.

## CHAPTER XVIII.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Because Claudius had commanded all to depart. [n: (BED.)]* Josephus relates that this was done in the ninth year of Claudius. ⟦s2⟧ Suetonius says that Claudius expelled the Jews from Rome.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Of the tentmaking craft. [n: (ID.)]* As exiles and pilgrims build themselves tents which they may use on the way, [0461C] etc., *as far as* and by word and deed protects from the winds of snares.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Your blood. [n: (CHRYS.)]* This he does, that he may terrify not by word alone, and he discourses the more vehemently, inasmuch as many had been persuaded.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Of Titus Justus, one that worshipped God.* ⟦s2⟧ Titus Justus was either the man's own name, or he was so surnamed by the merit of his justice. ⟦s3⟧ This Titus was not the disciple of Paul to whom he himself wrote the Epistle, and whom he ordained bishop of Crete; with whom Barnabas also, going up to Jerusalem, conferred the Gospel with the apostles, inquiring in their council whether he preached rightly that the Gentiles were saved through the baptism of faith without circumcision, not because he himself doubted of this, but that he might confirm the minds of the doubting by the authority of the apostles.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0461D]VERS. 15.-- *But if they be questions of a word.* ⟦s2⟧ Now indeed declared and narrated, that is, the Gospel: *and of names* (Jesus) and (God) or (God the Father). ⟦s3⟧ In the Greek: *All the Greeks apprehending Sosthenes,* etc.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Who had shorn himself. [n: (RAB.)]* Who shore their own head is uncertain, yet they were Paul or Aquila; whether from fear of the Jews, or from eagerness of converting them? ⟦s2⟧ Yet this is said ambiguously, but according to Jerome it is to be understood of Paul.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Caesarea. [n: (ID.)]* Caesarea is the metropolis of Cappadocia. ⟦s2⟧ For he had not yet come into Syria of Phoenicia.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *He helped much.* ⟦s2⟧ Another translation: [0462A] *He profited much them that believed,* by grace, this is what the Apostle writes: *I have planted, Apollo watered [n: (I Cor. III)]*, etc.
