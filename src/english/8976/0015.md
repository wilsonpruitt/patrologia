## CHAPTER XIX.

VERS. 4.-- *John baptized. [n: (BED.)]* John did not remit sins by his baptism, but taught penance. For as circumcision among the patriarchs was a seal of the faith which they had: so also the penitent people received this washing as a seal of their devotion: which prefigured the baptism of Christ, in which remission is made.

[n: (RAB.)] It is not baptism, which is not given in the name of the Trinity. Those baptized by Judas are not rebaptized, because it is in the faith of the Trinity; but those baptized by John, because [0462B] John baptizes in water only, but Christ in the Holy Spirit. When therefore a minister good or bad baptizes, if the faith of the Trinity is entire, the Lord baptizes; but those baptized by John in water alone were baptized by Paul.

[n: (BED.)] The question is: ought he who through ignorance has perchance been baptized by one not baptized, but nevertheless of right faith, to be rebaptized? Which I think has been expounded in this chapter. For what difference does it make, whether then, before the beginning of Christ's baptism, or now, someone were baptized by anyone without the reception of the same? since those whom John baptized in the faith and name of Christ who was to come, he foretold must be baptized again, saying: *I baptize in water, he shall baptize in the Holy Spirit.*

VERS. 7.-- *And they were all. [n: (RAB.)]* The judgments [0462C] of God are a great deep. Behold, Asia, unworthy of the visitation of the apostles, is now consecrated by the apostolic number and exalted by the gift of prophecy. And it is to be noted that the Holy Spirit showed the signs of his coming here upon twelve disciples, and above upon a hundred and twenty (which number is completed by the duodenary drawn ten times), that in Jerusalem, this in Ephesus, which is a city of the Greeks. In which it is shown that the spirit fills only those (whether of the Jews or of the Gentiles) who are in catholic unity.

VERS. 9.-- *Daily disputing in the school. [n: (BED.)]* From the fifth hour until the ninth and the tenth: as is handed down by certain men: because he gave five hours to work, another five to teaching, two to food and prayer, which however is confirmed by no authority. *In [0462D] the school of Tyrannus. [n: (RAB.)]* The name of Tyrannus is put for a dignity, or it is his own name, and is interpreted *containing* them, or *confirming.*

VERS. 10.-- *For two years.* At this time Paul wrote at Ephesus the first Epistle to the Corinthians.

VERS. 12.-- *Half-girdles [n: (RAB.)]* Garments hanging from one side, or girdles, or night-garments, or a kind of napkin, which the Hebrews use upon the head.

VERS. 13.-- *Exorcists.* Solomon devised, and taught his nation, modes of exorcism, that is, of adjuration, by which the unclean spirits, being cast out, dared not return to men. And this is done through reprobates unto their own condemnation and unto the profit of those who see and hear: because although they despise those [0463A] who work signs, yet they honour God, at whose invocation it is done.

VERS. 14.-- *Seven sons,* etc. [n: (BED.)] Since Satan transfigures himself into an angel of light, etc., *as far as* he shows concerning what guardians of the Church it is enjoined: *Catch us the little foxes that lay waste the vineyards [n: (Cant. II)]*.

VERS. 19.-- *And many of them who had followed curious things. [n: (BED.)]* The industry of the magic art: whose followers deservedly burn their books, though valued at a great price, because they see that the demons (whom they had served) call upon Jesus and honour his apostles.

VERS. 21.-- *And when these things were ended,* etc. [n: (CHRYS.)] Having dwelt long enough in the city, etc., *as far as* thus everywhere he weaves his works together.

[0463B] *To see Rome. [n: (RAB.)]* Perhaps he had not seen it before, or because he counted light the tribulation which he was to suffer there; he says only *to see,* and not to suffer many things.

VERS. 22.-- *Into Macedonia. [n: (RAB.)]* He wished to pass from Asia, in which Ephesus is, into Europe, where Macedonia and Achaia are; and so to go again into Asia, that is, to Jerusalem with the alms, of which he wrote to the Corinthians.

VERS. 28.-- *Great is Diana of the Ephesians. [n: (BED.)]* Diana, whom Demetrius said would perish, they on the contrary marvel that she is great.

VERS. 32.-- *And some cried one thing, some another.* That other thing which they cried was, either that they should be haled into the theatre, or that another was the great God, or [0463C] that it was another idol, or they even cast reproaches upon Paul.

VERS. 34.-- *There was made one voice of all, as it were for two hours crying out: Great is Diana of the Ephesians.* Deservedly do the Jews drag the scribe Alexander out of that crowd which wholly adored Diana.

VERS. 37.-- *Neither guilty of sacrilege, nor blaspheming your goddess. [n: (RAB.)]* Sacrilegious men are so called from profane diligence. For *sacrum* signifies both the clean and the profane.

VERS. 40.-- *And when he had said these things.* He speaks this as a Jew, not wishing the Jews to be slain by the worshippers of idols; or he spoke as a Christian.

## CHAPTER XX.

[0463D]VERS. 2.-- *To Greece.* Greece is a province of Achaia; to this Paul came after Macedonia: because he had so purposed, as was said above; and he passes from Macedonia and Achaia to go to Jerusalem.

VERS. 4.-- *Sosipater.* [var: Vulg. Act. 20:4 *Sopater*; the form here glossed stands at Rom. 16:21] In the Hebrew Sosipater is interpreted to be the same as *telling the dispersion,* or *saving the dispersed.*

VERS. 7.-- *And on the first day of the week.* The name of the day is spoken, that it may be a preparation of the virtue that is to be done and to be spoken.

[n: (BED.)] The upper room, the height of the gifts of grace. The night, the obscurity of the Scriptures, etc., *as far as* he subjoined: *And these are the two Testaments.*

VERS. 9.-- *Eutichus,* etc. [n: (ID.)] In Hebrew he is interpreted *out of his mind,* in Greek *fortunate,* etc., *as far as* that the memory [0464A] also of the master now about to depart might be the more closely fixed in the mind.

VERS. 10.-- *When Paul had gone down, he fell upon him. [n: (ID.)]* He went down and fell upon him, he embraced him, etc., *as far as* and, the sun of justice breathing upon him, he is brought back alive. *For his soul is in him. [n: (RAB.)]* That is, it did not wholly depart, or it returned. This he said because of the unbelieving, as: *The girl is not dead, but sleepeth:* for so despair is taken away.

VERS. 16.-- *Pentecost.* It is agreed that the time of the Pasch and of Pentecost was celebrated in the times of the apostles.

VERS. 31.-- *Keeping in memory. [n: (RAB.)]* What he had said to them, he wishes that to be kept in memory, for the memory of a divine precept is no small keeping.

[0464B]VERS. 35.-- *It is a more blessed thing. [n: (ID.)]* This is not read in the Gospel, for not all things are written, but Paul perhaps heard this from the apostles, or received it from the Holy Spirit, or it may be said that, if not the words, Jesus indicated this sense, saying: *Give to every one that asketh;* and elsewhere: *Do not receive gold,* etc. [var: Vulg. Matth. 10:9 *Nolite possidere aurum*, "do not possess gold"] [n: (BED.)] He does not prefer rich almsgivers to those who, leaving all things, followed the Lord, but he chiefly glorifies those who, renouncing in themselves all that they possess, nonetheless labour, working with their hands, that they may have whence to give to him that suffers need. You therefore, bishops, be solicitous concerning the nourishing of orphans, etc.
