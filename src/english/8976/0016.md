## CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 1.-- *And when it came to pass,* etc. *Cos, Rhodes, and thence [0464C] Patara. [n: (RAB.)]* Cos and Patara are islands.

VERS. 8.-- *Of Philip the evangelist.* For the ready office of preaching he deserved to be called an evangelist.

VERS. 9.-- *And he had daughters. [n: (RAB.)]* Elsewhere the daughters of Philip the apostle are read to have been prophetesses, but the truth of this place is not to be contradicted, unless perhaps each of the two be understood to have had daughters that were prophetesses.

VERS. 11.-- *Thus saith the Holy Spirit. [n: (BED.)]* He imitates the ancient prophets, who were wont to say: *Thus saith the Lord God,* because the Holy Spirit, equally as the Father and the Son, is Lord and God, nor can the operation of those be separated whose nature and will is one.

VERS. 21.-- *Saying that they ought not. [n: (BED.)]* As if they should say: In the doctrine of Christ thou condemnest as sacrilegious things, [0464D] etc., *as far as* but the Jews were not forbidden.

*Neither according to.* The Jews believing in Christ were not forbidden at that time to walk according to the law, the temple and their religion still standing, although they were to have salvation in the sacraments of the New Testament, but they that believed of the Gentiles were forbidden to turn to the law, who nevertheless were bidden diligently to observe the commandments of the law pertaining to morals and to the cleanness of the body, such as: *Thou shalt not covet,* etc. A fourfold synod was held at Jerusalem: the first was concerning the election of another apostle in place of Judas; the second concerning the election of the seven deacons; the third concerning circumcision not being imposed upon those who believed of the Gentiles; the fourth this one, concerning the Jews not being forbidden at that time [0465A] (where necessity required) to be initiated in the legal ceremonies, because of the scandal of those who thought that the apostles condemned the law of Moses as idolaters condemn their doctrines.

VERS. 24.-- *That the things which they have heard of thee,* etc. For thou dost not reject the legal observances in the manner that has been said, but if he celebrated for this reason, that by a feigned action he might conceal his own opinion, James would not say this.

VERS. 27.-- *And when the seven days,* etc. They were not yet completed, but their course was still being run, etc., *as far as* lest the Romans should come and take away the place and the nation.

VERS. 35.-- *And when he was come to the stairs.* It signifies stairs not of the descent from the temple, but of the ascent into the castle.

[0465B]VERS. 37.-- *Knowest thou Greek? [n: (RAB.)]* It is a question or an affirmation, because (as is thought) the tribune was speaking in the Greek tongue, or, lest the Jews should know.

VERS. 39.-- *From Tarsus,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Whither, having been taken captive, he migrated with his parents.

## CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 3.-- *Born in Tarsus. [n: (BED.)]* Paul was born in a town of Galilee named Giscala, etc., *as far as* so Christ, born in Bethlehem, was called a Nazarene.

*A zealot of the law.* In the Greek: *A zealot of the law of the God that is,* according to that: *I bear witness to them, that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge [n: (Rom. X)]*.

[0465C]VERS. 7.-- *Saule, Saule. [n: (RAB.)]* Certain copies have: *Saul, Saul,* that it may be shown that Saulus was named from the name of Saul.

VERS. 9.-- *They heard. [n: (ID.)]* They understood, but only the sound of the voice of Paul, or of him who was speaking to Paul.

VERS. 10.-- *And there. [n: (ID.)]* An example of humility, of not disputing against teachers, nor of presuming concerning ourselves, as though we were taught by God.

VERS. 13.-- *Brother Saul, look up. [n: (ID.)]* There were two things in the eyes of Paul, blinding and enlightening: and therefore he deservedly believed.

VERS. 14.-- *He hath preordained thee. [n: (ID.)]* A fitting order: first, he is preordained in the foreknowledge of God; then he knows the truth, sees the Just One, hears from his own [0465D] mouth; afterward he becomes a witness of those things which he knew, saw, heard.

VERS. 17.-- *That I was in a trance of mind.* This he relates not for boasting, but because of necessity, that he may be seen fit to evangelize the Gentiles.

VERS. 20.-- *I consented.* By confessing that he consented, he notes that he did not kill, unless he that strikes be taken for him that kills, or for one taking care to kill.

VERS. 28.-- *I with a great sum.* Another edition more plainly intimates what the tribune said. The tribune said: dost thou so easily say that thou art a Roman citizen? for I with how great a price possessed this citizenship.

*Citizenship.* That is, the social intercourse among citizens, or the administration of the commonwealth. For it is not that the [0466A] tribune could not be a citizen of the Roman city, but he had bought a partnership of the city, that he might be a sharer. Paul was the more a Roman citizen, in that he had this not by purchasing, but by being born.

## CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 3.-- *He shall strike thee. [n: (RAB.)]* He does not say *let him strike,* because it is a prophecy, not a curse, etc., *as far as* but the Apostle spoke by divine inspiration.

VERS. 5.-- *I knew not, brethren.* That is, I did not know that it was Ananias; or he subtly says that he does not know the prince, because the priesthood of the Jews has been reproved.

VERS. 9.-- *What if a spirit? [n: (RAB.)]* Here it appears, according to Jerome, that the Sadducees do not confess a spirit [0466B] and angels, and therefore the Pharisees name a spirit and an angel, because they knew this to be contrary to them.

VERS. 10.-- *Lest Paul should be pulled in pieces by them. [n: (ID.)]* By the Sadducees wishing to seize him, or by both parties contending together.

VERS. 12.-- *They bound themselves by a vow, saying.* In Greek, they anathematized: how great the authority of this word is, they know who by the priestly anathema are made strangers from the fellowship of Christ and of the Church.

VERS. 23.-- *Prepare two hundred soldiers,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He commands this to be done, lest the Jews should take him away, and the tribune himself be accused of a crime before the governor, as though he were to receive money from the Jews, and the price of Paul's death.

## CHAPTER XXIV.

[0466C]

VERS. 14.-- *To my Father and God. [n: (RAB.)]* Whose son I am shown to be, not *a servant,* as is had in other codices.

VERS. 21.-- *Except for this one thing. [n: (ID.)]* This he speaks by antiphrasis, for he knew this to please them: as if he should say, I have committed nothing, save that I preached what they themselves also believe.

VERS. 23.-- *To minister of his own. [n: (ID.)]* To his disciples and companions; otherwise, he commanded that no one be forbidden to minister to him of his own things or means.

VERS. 25.-- *Trembling,* etc., *and hoping. [n: (ID.)]* Join thus: trembling and hoping, for these two things he dismissed him to a free custody, from the fear of God and the hope of money.

[0466D]VERS. 27.-- *He left Paul bound. [n: (ID.)]* Whether Felix left Paul while living or while dying is uncertain; but nevertheless it seems better that while living he did the Jews a favour.

## CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 1.-- *Festus therefore, when he was come into the province,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Understand the province of Palestine, as other governors of Palestine, always succeeding one another, were sent by the Romans.

VERS. 18.-- *Of whom,* etc. [n: (ID.)] For these causes, or things, or words: concerning which I was suspecting, that is, was expecting from the accusers, or the evils concerning which I was suspecting; otherwise, concerning whom, that is, the accusers, [0467A] I was suspecting evil: because they brought forward no cause worthy of death.

VERS. 19.-- *Their own superstition. [n: (RAB.)]* Of both, that is, he called the law of Paul and of the Jews a superstition.

VERS. 23.-- *With much ambition. [n: (ID.)]* That is, with a great crowd going about them, or with much desire of hearing Paul. For *ambitio* there is put in the Greek *phantasia*, that is, the manifold apparatus and pomp of a royal office: by which, going about her, she was thronged on every side.

## CHAPTER XXVI.

VERS. 8.-- *What incredible thing? [n: (RAB.)]* Or thus: King, what sayest thou? is it judged incredible among you? etc., [0467B] by way of questioning, as if he should say: It is not incredible. For indeed he raised up the dead before, for an example of that which is to come, or he raises them up.

VERS. 10.-- *When they were killed he brought the sentence. [n: (ID.)]* [var: Vulg. Act. 26:10 *detuli sententiam*, "I brought the sentence," in the first person] Another edition: And upon them that were to be killed he brought the sentence, that is, in what manner they should be killed I myself gave the sentence. *I brought the sentence.* I judged them to be killed: he does not say that I killed, unless perhaps what follows pertains to the killing: *Punishing them,* etc., *and being yet more mad,* etc.

VERS. 14.-- *Against the goad. [n: (ID.)]* Hitherto thou hast resisted without a goad, but now if thou resistest, thou shalt kick against the goad, which is hard enough. The goad is correction itself.

*Thou art mad, Paul. [n: (BED.)]* He thinks it madness that a bound man, having received a place of defending, etc., *as far as* and [0467C] the Prophet: *He hath not regarded vanities and false madnesses [n: (Psal. XXXIX)]*

VERS. 24.-- *Much learning doth turn thee. [n: (RAB.)]* The philosophers of the Gentiles by much reading used to change their sects. *To madness,* understand, of mutability; or he calls madness the resurrection of the dead, which he announces.
