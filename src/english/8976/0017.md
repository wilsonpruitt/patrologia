## CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *And when it was determined that he should sail,* etc. In the Greek: When it was determined that we should sail into Italy, they delivered Paul and certain others in bonds to a centurion.

VERS. 2.-- *Adrumetine. [n: (RAB.)]* That is, sailing to Rome, according to some, but it is better derived from the name of the African city which is called Adrumetis.

[0467D]VERS. 5.-- *We came to Lystra. [n: (BED.)]* For Lystra, in the Greek Smyrna is written. But Jerome puts Myrrha.

VERS. 9.-- *The fast. [n: (RAB.)]* A great hunger on the voyage, making the sailors weaker. Others say that it was then the forty days' fast before the birth of the Lord, which is not probable. It is better to be thought the seventh fast, in which Godolias was slain. Or the day of the tenth fast in January, when the captives in Babylon learned that the temple had been destroyed.

VERS. 12.-- *A haven,* etc. [n: (ID.)] That is called a haven of Crete which is in Phoenice over against Crete: but they were seeking the haven of Crete, because they were sailing to Crete.

[0468A] *Toward Africus,* etc. Africus stands between the southern and the western quarter, inclining more to the western; Corus between the northern and the western, more bent toward the west; and the province of Phoenice had Crete to the west.

VERS. 13.-- *Asson. [n: (RAB.)]* It is the name of a place so called, because thence the ships were loosed as they went forward into the sea.

VERS. 14.-- *Typhonic.* Another translation: a tempestuous wind.

*Euroaquilo. [n: (RAB.)]* That is, Boreas, a wind between Aquilo and the east; this wind was contrary to the ship that was to sail into Phoenice.

VERS. 17.-- *Syrtis. [n: (ID.)]* A narrowness or tribulation is called Syrtis.

[0468B]VERS. 21.-- *It behoved indeed,* etc. [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: You did not hear God, in that Paul above was exhorting them; or, it behoved, me being heard, that is, that you should hear me.

VERS. 26.-- *And we must come unto a certain island. [n: (ID.)]* As if he should say: The prophecy of the truth concerning the island is for a sign, that, when they shall have come to it, they may be certain of their salvation.

VERS. 27.-- *In Adria.* It is a region to which they were sailing; or, the name of a certain strait in the Tyrrhene sea, or rather it seems to be the name of that land from which the Adriatic gulf is called. Otherwise, Adria is taken in the plural for rocks, and therefore the sailors thought that a region appeared. In the book of Hebrew names, [0468C] Adria is said to be containing evils, or a place of evils.

VERS. 34.-- *To take food. [n: (BED.)]* No one escapes the tempests of the world, save he who is fed with the bread of life, and who in the night of tribulations leans upon wisdom, fortitude, temperance and justice, and, by the help of the Lord flashing forth, attains the haven of salvation, so that, being disencumbered of the world, he may seek the flame of love by which the inward parts are warmed.

VERS. 37.-- *We were all souls. [n: (RAB.)]* He does not say men, because perhaps there were women also; or, because of the mingling of Christians, soldiers and sailors, it is so said.

VERS. 41.-- *Bithalassum. [n: (BED.)]* [var: Vulg. Act. 27:41 *dithalassum*, "having two seas"] The Greeks call the sea Thalassa, etc., *as far as* and the sea is called by the Greeks Thelasson.

## CHAPTER XXVIII.

[0468D]

VERS. 2.-- *Pyra. [n: (RAB.)]* Some say that pyra is the name of a wood.

VERS. 8.-- *Dysentery* or *dysin. [n: (BED.)]* It is an affliction of the intestines. *He healed him.* Why does he heal by prayer a sick unbeliever, who so deals with Timothy and Trophimus, sick believers, that he cures the one and leaves the other? unless because that man was to be healed outwardly by a miracle, who inwardly did not live: but these did not need this, who within lived healthfully.

VERS. 13.-- *Puteoli.* Puteoli is a place beyond Rome where Virgil made medicinal baths, each inscribed with its proper titles (as is said) against [0469A] which sickness, namely, they availed. Whence the men of Salerno, led by envy, coming upon them with a strong hand, destroyed the titles and mutilated the buildings.

VERS. 15.-- *The brethren had heard. [n: (RAB.)]* Whence Christians dwelling at Rome? it is plain that Paul was not the first to preach to the Romans.

[0470A]VERS. 30.-- *Conductu.* That is, in the free custody through which he was conducted, or in his own assembly in which he disputed against the Jews.

VERS. 31.-- *Without prohibition. [n: (RAB.)]* In the twenty-fifth year after the passion of the Lord, etc., *as far as* Peter and Paul were crowned with martyrdom.
