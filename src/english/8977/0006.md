## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And I saw.* ⟦s2⟧ After the Lamb had fulfilled the things that had been foretold of him, and had established his Church in the faith by the testimony [0721C] of the prophets and of the angels, he opened their understanding that they might understand the Scriptures, and outwardly through preachers he declares that it is fitting to suffer for this faith, and what helps or what crowns he holds out to his own, because, mention having been made of the restoration, if he were to keep silence about the tribulations, they might now fail, if tribulation should rise up unlooked for. ⟦s3⟧ *That he had opened.* ⟦s4⟧ That book was sealed, because Christ opened all things either in himself or in his own. ⟦s5⟧ For he himself opened their understanding, that they might understand these seals in the Scriptures. ⟦s6⟧ In the first seal opened, whence the white horse went forth, is understood the whitening of the Church through baptism. ⟦s7⟧ In the second, the red horse, is intimated the open persecution of those who shed blood, whence tribulations rise up against the baptized. ⟦s8⟧ In the third, the black, the hidden persecution of heretics. ⟦s9⟧ In the fourth, the pale, [0721D] the open and hidden persecution of false charges. ⟦s10⟧ *Come and see.* ⟦s11⟧ Pass over from the mystery, and see the truth or the justice fulfilled.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Behold a white horse.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church whitened in baptism, or the greatest preachers, who bear God everywhere. ⟦s3⟧ *A bow.* ⟦s4⟧ Christ has the Scripture, by which he enlightens his own and slays his enemies.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *And when he had opened the second seal.* ⟦s2⟧ The devil, seeing the Church established to fill up the place whence he himself had fallen, strives to overthrow her, first openly. ⟦s3⟧ *A red horse.* ⟦s4⟧ Contrary to the former, namely open and bloodthirsty persecutors.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 4.-- *That he should take peace from the earth, and that an informer,* etc. [var: Vulg. *ut invicem se interficiant,* "that they should kill one another"] That is, it was granted by God that he should take away [0722A] all quiet of mind from those who are given over to earthly things. ⟦s3⟧ *And a great sword was given to him.* ⟦s4⟧ A great power of killing, because he dared to rage not only against the lesser, but even against the greater, as against Peter.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And behold a black horse.* ⟦s2⟧ The devil, seeing that he had not prevailed by open persecutions. he sends in heretics, who contend with false reasonings that they may the more easily deceive.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A balance.* ⟦s2⟧ Because they say that they have a true discernment in the Scriptures.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *And I heard as it were.* ⟦s2⟧ Because here, in the battle of the heretics, where the danger is greater, help is held forth. ⟦s3⟧ *Two pounds.* ⟦s4⟧ As if he should say: O faithful ones, do not fear those who before were able to hurt you; and you devils, do not hurt these, because you will not prevail, *for two pounds of wheat,* [0722B] etc. ⟦s5⟧ *Of wheat.* ⟦s6⟧ By wheat are understood the more perfect, who, ground down by tribulation, are baked by fire, a sweet food for God. ⟦s7⟧ The barley are the lesser in the same kind. ⟦s8⟧ The wine, those bruised indeed in the press, but not so thoroughly boiled down, sharp only in themselves, and inebriating others unto good working: the oil are those not inebriating others, but first anointing them with prayers. ⟦s9+10⟧ *And do not hurt the living thing and the oil.* [var: Vulg. *et vinum, et oleum ne laeseris,* "and the wine and the oil hurt thou not"] That is, those who are refreshed with wine, that is, with my blood, and anointed with oil, that is, with the Holy Spirit. ⟦s11⟧ Wine likewise is bought for one penny.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Behold a pale horse.* ⟦s2⟧ The devil, seeing that he could prevail neither by open tribulations nor by open heresies, sends before him false brethren: who under the habit of religion obtain the nature of the red horse and of the black, by perverting the faith. ⟦s3⟧ *His name was death.* ⟦s4⟧ Because through him [0722C] death came in, or, to the letter, because he killed some in the flesh, some in the soul. ⟦s5⟧ *And hell.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, those insatiable for earthly things imitate him, and are laid up in hell. ⟦s7⟧ *Over four.* ⟦s8⟧ That is, over all the wicked abiding everywhere, or over the Jews and gentiles, heretics and false Christians.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *And when.* ⟦s2⟧ The tribulations having been shown which in the present threaten the faithful, lest they fail, he in some measure assures them concerning the crown that is near and consoles those who suffer. ⟦s3⟧ *I saw under the altar.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, those humbling themselves to their own head, or, *under,* in hiding, because no one in this life perceives how they are there.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The altar.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ, upon whom all the saints offer themselves, and whom they have as a defence. ⟦s3⟧ Or the souls themselves are the altar from which a smoke goes up delightful to God, [0722D] and because they were smeared with blood, they are now existing beneath, that is, in a lesser dignity than they shall be, because they have not yet received any but one stole. ⟦s4⟧ *Of them that were slain for the word.* ⟦s5⟧ Either by open martyrdom, or by certain anguishes

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *How long.* ⟦s2⟧ The saints desire a greater joy and the fellowship of the saints, and they consent to the justice of God in the damnation of the impious.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Thou judgest and dost not avenge the blood.* [var: Vulg. *non judicas, et non vindicas sanguinem nostrum,* "thou dost not judge, and dost not avenge our blood"] By making a distinction between the good and the evil.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Their fellow servants.* ⟦s2⟧ They are called servants by reason of the Lord himself, out of love they ought to await their brethren.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The sun became black.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, those in [0723A] whom he shines shall be accounted guilty and sinners, keeping their brightness to themselves, or because certain shall fall away into darkness. ⟦s3⟧ Or Christ, the true sun, and now bright, shall in the time of Antichrist be dark, that is, flashing with no miracles. ⟦s4⟧ *As sackcloth.* ⟦s5⟧ A light placed in a sack does not shine without, the faithful will not pour out the light of preaching in that day of great tribulation.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Its green figs.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, empty fruits, for God permits none to fall who serves him with true love.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Heaven.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the Church shall withdraw from the wicked, in faith and in work. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *Go out of Babylon, my people, and touch not the unclean thing.* [var: Vulg. has no such verse; the nearest are Apc 18:4, *Exite de illa populus meus,* and the closing clause of 2 Cor 6:17]

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The kings of the earth.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the diverse honours of the world, which, being unwilling to lose, they destroy the faith. [0723B] *The rich.* ⟦s3⟧ Fulfilling whatever evil they wish. ⟦s4⟧ *The mighty.* ⟦s5⟧ In overthrowing others. ⟦s6⟧ A servant of sin, free of justice. ⟦s7⟧ These, despairing of the saints, seek the helps of demons, that those in whom they trusted may deliver them.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And they say to the mountains.* ⟦s2⟧ Let them seek the suffrages of the angels, who are the more eminent mountains, and rocks by firmness: that by their prayers they may obtain the mercy of the judge.
