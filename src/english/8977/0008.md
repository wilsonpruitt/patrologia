## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And when he had opened.* ⟦s2⟧ In this life, in the third vision, there are seven angels sounding with trumpets unto the destruction of the enemies, after the likeness of those who by sounding with trumpets destroyed the walls of Jericho. ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: When I had understood the salvation of the just, on the other side I understood the damnation of the impious through the office of preaching enjoined upon the good.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Seven angels.* ⟦s2⟧ All of them, or those imbued with the sevenfold Spirit. ⟦s3⟧ *And there were given.* ⟦s4⟧ Because the damnation of the impious does not displease [0725B] God.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3-- *And another angel.* ⟦s2⟧ The angel stood before the altar ready to sacrifice, after the likeness of a priest, or Christ is the altar who offered himself, and upon whom the Church offers prayers to God. ⟦s3⟧ *He stood before the altar,* that is, before his very self, because he has no mediator through whom he may please. ⟦s4⟧ *Having.* ⟦s5⟧ Being about to say that from the preaching of the just the damnation of the impious would come to pass, he sets before it as it were an excuse, lest the blame for the damnation of the impious be imputed to them, because as regards themselves their preaching is good, and enjoined by a good teacher. ⟦s6⟧ *A censer.* ⟦s7⟧ That is, the apostles, who are vessels of fire, that is, of the Holy Spirit, and out of whom go forth prayers which are lifted up before God. ⟦s8⟧ *Of the prayers.* ⟦s9⟧ He said *of the prayers,* and not *the prayers,* because not all prayers [0725C] does he present to the Father, but he hears those which pertain to salvation. ⟦s10⟧ *Of the prayers,* because they sometimes ask what ought not to be asked, as Paul did. ⟦s11⟧ *Upon the golden altar.* ⟦s12⟧ That is, upon him who is above himself according to his humanity, according to which he is the altar of the Trinity.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Smoke.* ⟦s2⟧ Compunction begotten by the zeal of prayer, kindled inwardly by the fire of the vision of God.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *They prepared.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, they first fulfilled in work the things they were about to speak with the mouth. ⟦s3⟧ Or, *they prepared,* that is, they foresaw what was fitting for each several person.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the first angel.* ⟦s2⟧ By the order of the narration, or because this blindness of the Jews befell first, while the apostles were preaching.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0725D]VERS. 7.-- *And there was made hail.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the preaching, sweet to others, seemed to them thick and heavy. ⟦s3⟧ *Upon the earth.* ⟦s4⟧ The Jews are as it were earth tilled by the law and the prophets.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the trees.* ⟦s2⟧ By the trees are understood the greater Jews; who lived in the law without complaint.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And the second angel,* according to the order of the narration, and according to what was fulfilled. ⟦s2⟧ For the Jews being blinded, the apostles were turned to the gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ *Into the sea.* ⟦s4⟧ The gentiles are the sea, that is, flowing into many vices; and although the devil was in them before, yet he is truly said to be sent, because, when certain withdraw, he strives to hold back the rest.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *The third part died.* ⟦s2⟧ Two parts, that is, [0726A] all the good, believed: the third, that is, all the reprobate, were given up to a reprobate sense.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Which had souls,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, of those gentiles who were reckoned worthier than the rest for certain good works.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And the third angel,* by the order of the narration, and because this befell after those two. ⟦s2⟧ For after the devil was not able to blind all the Jews and gentiles, he sent heretics in among them, and overthrew certain of them. ⟦s3⟧ *Fountains.* ⟦s4⟧ Those are called fountains in which the sum and as it were the origin of the whole faith is comprehended, as is the Gospel. ⟦s5⟧ The rivers, which are drawn from thence, are as their expositions, in which there are two senses of the faithful, the historical and the allegorical. ⟦s6⟧ The third is that of the heretics, when they confirm their heresies by depraved expositions [0726B] of the Scriptures.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And the fourth angel.* ⟦s2⟧ He is called the fourth by the order of the narration, and by the order of the thing. ⟦s3⟧ For when the devil had blinded certain of the Jews and of the gentiles, at length he went forth to assail the sons of the Church themselves, and snatched away certain of them, as well the greater as the lesser. ⟦s4⟧ These four damnations he related as past, inasmuch as they are seen daily in the churches. ⟦s5⟧ But three he causes to be foretold as to come, that he may teach that in the last times still heavier ones threaten than those that are past, which also may be turned to the consolation of those now present.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Of one eagle.* ⟦s2⟧ The eagle is all preachers, who behold with a far-reaching mind, and going about the Church, fortify it by foretelling things to come: here [0726C] all are one, because they tend to the same end.
