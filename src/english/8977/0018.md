## CHAPTER XXI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And I saw.* ⟦s2⟧ In this seventh vision he treats of the renewing of the elements and the glorification of the saints, describing the merits through which the saints are thus glorified. ⟦s3⟧ *A new heaven and a new earth.* ⟦s4⟧ The change [0746A] of the air and of the earth is not doubtful, but that it comes to pass through fire. ⟦s5⟧ But concerning water there is doubt, for it is believed to have purgation in itself.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *As a bride adorned.* ⟦s2⟧ As a bridegroom sends gifts beforehand to his bride before he leads her home, so does Christ send to the Church faith and virtues and good works before he receives her into glory.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *A great voice from the throne.* ⟦s2⟧ When he had said that he had seen the renewing of the elements and the glorification of the saints, this was unknown, and, lest it should seem past believing, he adds the authority of the holy Fathers, who foretold this. ⟦s3⟧ *Behold the tabernacle.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, it is plain that the humanity of Christ shall be in that same glory in which it warred, and the men who imitated him and used those things well.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0746B] *And they his people.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, offending in nothing, which cannot be while they are in mortal flesh.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And death.* ⟦s2⟧ Truly there shall be no tears there, because there shall be no cause of tears.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And he said.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Write whatsoever has been shown thee concerning this renewing. ⟦s3⟧ This was done for the profit of those now present, for whom I have been bidden to write these things. ⟦s4⟧ *Write: for these,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Bidding him write, and announce to others, he subjoins an exhortation, that all may labour to belong to this renewing.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *And he said to me.* ⟦s2⟧ When the authority of the saints concerning this renewing has been set down, he sets down the authority of God himself. ⟦s3⟧ *It is done.* ⟦s4⟧ Lest anyone should ask: What shall come to pass after that renewing, it is said to him that nothing remains to be done.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0746C] *Alpha and Omega.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: This renewing I shall be able to make, because I created all things, and in me all things shall be consummated. ⟦s3⟧ *To him that thirsteth.* ⟦s4⟧ I will give, not to him that thirsteth for an hour, but to him that perseveres.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *He shall possess these things.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, myself, who am the beginning of all blessedness.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *To the fearful.* ⟦s2⟧ That is: to those who for fear of punishments flee from receiving the faith, or forsake it when received, there shall be eternal damnation.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Come and I will show thee.* ⟦s2⟧ When the glory which the saints are to have has been described, he tells by what merit, or by what help, they are to receive it.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Into a great mountain.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ is the *mountain*, because he is the defence of his own, he is great, because he fills the whole world, *high,* because unconquerable. [0746D] *He showed me the city.* ⟦s3⟧ And upon that mountain he showed the foundation of the Church.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *The light.* ⟦s2⟧ The heavenly Jerusalem herself, shining after her own measure, is like to Christ, who was firm in purpose, and shining in virtues. ⟦s3⟧ *Of jasper.* ⟦s4⟧ This stone is hard, and green: so the Church shines with faith, and is made firm therein.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The wall.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, Christ the defender, who is great in Sion, high with the Father. ⟦s3⟧ Or understand the defenders of others, who are great in overcoming others. ⟦s4⟧ Others, that they be not overcome, that is, the apostles or the prophets, or the angels, messengers of the truth. ⟦s5⟧ *Twelve gates.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, the patriarchs, by whose example others are brought in to the faith. ⟦s7⟧ Or the gates, the apostles, [0747A] who bring others into the Church. ⟦s8⟧ The angels, that is, the subject folk who, being perfectly taught, have been put in the place of the angels. ⟦s9⟧ The number of the gates, twelve, which are comprehended through the four parts of the world under the number three, belongs to the mystery of the twelve apostles, through whom the faith of the Trinity is spread abroad through the fourfold world. ⟦s10⟧ *Names written,* etc. ⟦s11⟧ That is, the memory of the holy Fathers of the old law is in the apostles, because what those foretold as to come, these teach as fulfilled. ⟦s12⟧ Or all the nations are set in the faith of the apostles and in the love of them. ⟦s13⟧ Whence: *You are our epistle, written in our hearts [n: (II Cor. III)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *From the east three gates.* ⟦s2⟧ The east, the Jews: from whom the sun of justice arose, and in them three gates, because in them first was the faith of the Trinity, announced [0747B] by the apostles and by the prophets. ⟦s3⟧ The north, the cold nations: which believed after the Jews. ⟦s4⟧ From the south, that is, from the brightness of faith, which is from the coming of Christ. ⟦s5⟧ From the west, that is, from the last age of the world, which through Elias and Enoch shall receive the faith of the Trinity.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The wall.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the faith of Christ, wherewith he fortified that city, has twelve patriarchs, by whom it is founded, because those first held this faith, and upon them lean all who come to the faith. ⟦s3⟧ *And in them twelve.* ⟦s4⟧ Which faith these hold, those understood as to come, and foretold.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And he that spoke with me.* ⟦s2⟧ I saw that city so fortified and adorned, and I saw there that not all were equal among themselves: but each one perfect [0747C] after his own measure. ⟦s3⟧ *A golden reed of measure.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, the divine Scripture, framed by the wisdom of God, which touches and enlightens the hearts of the faithful. ⟦s5⟧ *That he might measure the city and the gates thereof.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, that according to the measure he might give to the lesser also an understanding of those same Scriptures.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is laid out foursquare.* ⟦s2⟧ Four sides, faith, hope, charity, working, which are equal, because as much as a man believes, so much he hopes: as much as he hopes, so much he loves: as much as he loves, so much he works. ⟦s3⟧ Foursquare, therefore, because sturdy in faith, long-suffering in hope, ample in charity, effectual in work. ⟦s4⟧ Or the four sides are the four principal virtues, of which one ought not to exceed another. ⟦s5⟧ In a man, then, there is prudence in seeking the good and shunning the evil, which temperance follows, [0747D] that a man may draw himself back from pleasures: after these is fortitude, that he may work what he understands: thereafter justice, that he may so temper his acts that he seem not to himself too just or too wise. ⟦s6⟧ *By furlongs.* ⟦s7⟧ A stadium is a place and a course and a measure, and eight make a mile, and it marks those who press toward the prize.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Twelve thousand.* ⟦s2⟧ These are perfect in the faith of the Trinity out of the four parts of the world.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *The measure of a man.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the letter, holy John shows that the angel appeared to him in the likeness of a man, that he might plainly denote that he who was signified was a true angel, that is, the Son of God, and was a true man.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And it was.* ⟦s2⟧ He had said that in this city there are [0748A] walls and gates and foundations, and these among themselves, some greater, some lesser, here he sets out what manner of thing the wall is, and what manner of thing the city itself and the foundations and the gates. ⟦s3⟧ *The building of the wall thereof was of jasper stone.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, if anyone be equipped for this, that he be a defender of others, he has this from the faith which he firmly holds and preaches, which the green jasper signifies, that he may be a defender of others. ⟦s5⟧ *Gold.* ⟦s6⟧ Gold denotes wisdom; but glass, the purity of faith, whereby a man promises with his mouth what he believes with his heart.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Jasper.* ⟦s2⟧ This stone is green, and therefore it signifies the unfading greenness of faith. ⟦s3⟧ Or the *jasper* is the faith of the saints, not withering, but ever persevering in the greenness of work.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sapphire.* ⟦s2⟧ It is like to a clear sky, which, when struck by the rays of the sun, sends forth a burning brightness; but it signifies [0748B] the height of the hope of the saints, whose conversation is in heaven, who also, renewed by the true sun, more ardently seek eternal things, and teach others to seek them, saying: *Our conversation is in heaven [n: (Phil. III)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *Chalcedony, [n: (BED.)]* This stone has the colour of a paling lamp: it shines, etc., *as far as* they draw sinners and the frail to themselves and join them to themselves.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Emerald. [n: (ID.)]* The fourth, of exceeding greenness above all herbs and leaves, so much so that even, etc., *as far as* faith is called the fourth because of the four Gospels.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Sardonyx. [n: (ID.)]* The fifth, of three colours, black at the bottom, white, etc., *as far as* because it is carried on by the five senses, it is called the fifth.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sardius.* ⟦s2⟧ The sixth, of a bloody colour, of one colour, signifies the glory of martyrdom. ⟦s3⟧ The sixth, because Christ was crucified [0748C] on the sixth day of the week. ⟦s4⟧ Or the sardius, having the appearance of red earth, signifies the saints who, although lofty in the understanding of the mind, yet remember their own frailty, considering that they are sons of Adam, who is called red earth, who was made on the sixth day, whence also the sardius is brought in as sixth.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Beryl. [n: (BED.)]* The eighth is the working of preachers. ⟦s2⟧ The beryl shines, etc., *as far as* returning by the care of earthly administration to the pallor of the active life.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Topaz.* ⟦s2⟧ This stone is set ninth, because it is rarer and more precious, and has two colours, from gold and from an ethereal brightness, shining most of all when it is touched with the splendour of the sun, surpassing the brightnesses of all gems, singularly drawing those who look upon it to look at it, which, if thou polish it more, thou darkenest it: if thou leave it to nature, [0748D] it is clearer: and nothing is dearer to kings among riches. ⟦s3⟧ It signifies the contemplative life, which holy kings prefer to all the riches of works and gems of virtues, and toward which most of all they direct their gaze, and so much the more, the more often they are enlightened by divine grace. ⟦s4⟧ From inward charity comes the golden colour, from the sweetness of contemplation the ethereal, which from the rubbing of the world always grows dark, for scarcely can a man be touched with sorrows and at the same time behold with a tranquil mind the joys of heaven. ⟦s5⟧ Or if thou polish, that is, if thou honour them with the dignities of the world, and adorn them, thou darkenest them: because they have less leisure for contemplation, or become less in merits. ⟦s6⟧ And as in the eighth is the active life, so in the ninth is the contemplative, which is that of the angels, of whom there are nine orders. [0749A] Certain men say that it has the colours of all stones, whence also it has its name, because pan, that is, all. ⟦s7⟧ And it is found in an island of Egypt which is called Topazon, it signifies the saints, shining with all the colours of the virtues.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Chrysoprasus.* ⟦s2⟧ It is born in India, of a green and golden mixture. ⟦s3⟧ A certain kind is also purple with golden drops, it signifies those who by charity merit the greenness of the eternal country, and show forth another, the purple, of martyrdom, and because they follow the example of the Lord, they are in India, that is, near the rising of the sun, and look to reign with Christ. ⟦s4⟧ They are in the number ten, the decalogue is fulfilled through love, which is not ended. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore in the chrysoprasus is signified the work of martyrdom and its reward. ⟦s6⟧ Chryson is gold: prasos is called leek.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Hyacinth.* ⟦s2⟧ This stone is the eleventh, with the air [0749B] it changes, in clear weather transparent, in cloudy dark, it signifies most discerning teachers, tempering themselves to all qualities, characters, ages, understandings.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Amethyst.* ⟦s2⟧ The twelfth stone is purple, of a mingled colour of violet and rose, pouring forth from itself certain little flames. ⟦s3⟧ The purple colour signifies the raiment of the heavenly kingdom, the primacy of the angelic fellowship to which the saints shall come. ⟦s4⟧ The violet, the lowly modesty of the saints with the odour of good working. ⟦s5⟧ The rose, the precious death of the martyrs, who send forth the flames of charity not only among themselves, but even toward their enemies and persecutors. ⟦s6⟧ The amethyst therefore signifies the ever-abiding memory of the heavenly kingdom in the hearts of the humble, and that excellent virtue, that they pray even for their enemies, [0749C] and care for their salvation, which is the virtue of virtues. ⟦s7⟧ The variety of colours, the manifoldness of virtues.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And the twelve gates.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: I said that there are twelve gates, and since those twelve gates are so, that is, the prophets bringing men in to the faith of the Trinity out of the four parts of the world. ⟦s3⟧ *Through each,* that is, through the instruction of each one; *are pearls,* that is, those who imitate them, who, from whatsoever part of the world they be called to the faith of the Trinity, are splendid in virtues. ⟦s4⟧ *The several gates,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ As if he should say: In order that there might be gates, there had to be pearls. ⟦s6+7⟧ *The streets of the city gold.* [var: Vulg. Apc 21:21 *platea*, singular, "the street"] That is, wisdom, or men full of brightness, clean from criminal stain. ⟦s8⟧ *Glass thoroughly clear.* ⟦s9⟧ That is, hiding no filth within themselves. ⟦s10⟧ Or because the wicked can, in their works, as in a mirror, [0749D] recognize their own wickedness.
