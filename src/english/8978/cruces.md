## §1 · ⛔ MIGNE'S OWN SEQUENCE — VERS. 23 BEFORE VERS. 21. NOT REORDERED.

The lemmata run **… 19 · 20 · 23 · 21 · 25**. That is his ordering on the plate and it is
reproduced exactly: nothing reordered, nothing renumbered, no `[ed:]` (no text is lost).

⭐ **But the "21" is almost certainly a misprint for "24", and the words prove it.** The lemma
printed under VERS. 21 is *In exsultatione* — and **Jude 21 contains no such phrase**
(*vosmetipsos in dilectione Dei servate, exspectantes misericordiam …*). *in exsultatione*
is **Jude 24**: *constituere ante conspectum gloriae suae immaculatos **in exsultatione***.
Two further things point the same way:

1. The gloss's own verb is **`constituendos`** — *dicit constituendos in exsultatione* — which
   is Jude 24's *constituere*, not anything in Jude 21.
2. It opens by looking back at the previous gloss: *Quos superius monebat **in timore** Deo
   servire* — i.e. at VERS. 23's *In timore*. So the gloss itself asserts it follows 23.

Read as 24, Migne's sequence is **19 · 20 · 23 · 24 · 25 — continuous and in order.** The
defect is one digit in a verse address, not a displaced gloss.

**Nothing was changed in the text.** Verse addresses pass through verbatim by the settled
convention, and a renumbering would be exactly the silent repair Pattern 7 forbids — the more
so because the reader's own Latin column would then disagree with our English. It is recorded
here instead.

## §3 · 0707A · *Consolatis* — the work's most interesting word, and the gloss decides it

Migne prints `VERS. 1.-- *Consolatis et vocatis.*` The brief flagged the Vulgate as reading
*dilectis*. That is half the story, and the half that matters is the other one: **Jude 1 has
BOTH** — *his qui sunt in Deo Patre **dilectis**, et Christo Jesu **conservatis**, et
**vocatis***. The word standing immediately before *et vocatis* is **`conservatis`**, and
*Consolatis* is one letter-run away from it.

⭐ **The gloss settles it internally, and this is the evidence, not a hunch.** The single
sentence under this lemma is *Vocat, **conservat**, diligit filios* — three verbs answering
the verse's three participles, in the verse's own order reversed:

| gloss verb | Jude 1 participle |
|---|---|
| *Vocat* | *vocatis* |
| ***conservat*** | ***conservatis*** |
| *diligit* | *dilectis* |

**The glossator was reading *conservatis*.** There is no verb answering *consolatis* anywhere
on the page.

**What was done, and what was declined:**
- **Rendered what Migne prints** — "*To them that are consoled and called.*" *consolatis* is
  a real form (*consolor*), so it is translatable and Pattern 7 governs; there is nothing for
  a `[sic:]` to wrap (Pattern 12 marks only carried, untranslatable type).
- **`[var: Vulg. *conservatis et vocatis*, "to them that are preserved and called"]`** stands
  beside it, per the brief and Pattern 14.
- **`[cj: …]` was considered and DECLINED.** Pattern 18's shape fits — a real word whose
  faithful English misleads — but its purpose is to put the conjecture where a reader will
  meet it, and the `[var:]` already does that, naming *conservatis* in the same breath. Two
  markers on four words would be apparatus for its own sake. ⚑ If a later reader thinks the
  conjecture should carry Migne's own voice rather than the Vulgate's, this is the site.
- The gloss's *conservat* is rendered "**preserves**" so that the `[var:]`'s "preserved" and
  the gloss's verb are the same English word and the reader can see the join.

## §4 · MIGNE'S PRINTING, LEFT STANDING

### 0707D · *coarctabit Vel vinculum* — a missing full stop, carried

*Vel sententia divina quae eos in perpetuum coarctabit Vel vinculum est cupiditas infimarum
rerum…* — **no stop between the two *Vel* clauses, and the second *Vel* is capitalized.** The
English prints it the same way: "…will confine them for ever Or the chain is the desire…".
Pattern 8: never supply a mark the plate lacks. It reads as an error because it is one, and
it is his. No `[ed:]` — nothing is missing, only unpunctuated.

### 0705D–0706D · the ARGUMENTUM's column jump

The argument opens at `[0705D]` and takes `[0706D]` mid-sentence (*de sub* | *jugo*). Checked
against runbook false-positive 1 (column-band arithmetic) and **not reported**: no text is
broken mid-word, and the anchor falls between preposition and noun exactly as a column break
does. The English carries the anchor in the same place.

### 0709A · *qui reprobi … dedicant* — a relative with no antecedent

*in electorum vero progenie, Enoch, septimus memoratur, **qui** reprobi in hac vita, quae ante
est, seipsos aedificando dedicant.* The plural *qui* has no plural antecedent: the two Enochs
are singular. Rendered with *reprobi* in apposition — "who, the reprobate, in this life, which
is before, dedicate themselves by building themselves up" — which is the one construction that
uses every printed word. **The alternative is *qui* read as causal (*quia*), "for the reprobate
…"; it construes as well and is NOT excluded.** Nothing in the antithesis (*reprobi … electi
vero*) turns on which is right. No marker: the words are legible and Latin, and no marker in
this corpus means "a relative pronoun with nothing to attach to".

### 0709A · *ab aliis invident* — *invideo* with an ablative

*Avari enim (quibus nihil sufficit) etiam **ab aliis** invident.* *Invideo* governs the dative;
*ab* + ablative will not construe here on any reading that keeps the sentence's sense (the
covetous begrudge other men their good). Rendered "**are envious even of others**". A literal
"envy from others" is not English and asserts nothing. Logged rather than marked, for the
Ruth 0534A reason: nothing is broken type, nothing is missing, and inventing a marker for one
site is worse than saying so here.

### 0709A · *quo congregatur Ecclesia spiritualis efficitur* — two verbs, no conjunction

Printed without *et* and without a comma between the clauses. Rendered as printed: "(by whom
the Church is gathered together, is made spiritual)". The obvious supply is *et*; it is **not**
supplied, and no conjunction is invented — the 8968 *effoetandi* precedent, where the first
draft's invented "and" was caught by both blind readers.

### 0709B–0710A · *petite* — an imperative inside a *ut* clause

*Ut de vestris viribus non praesumatis, sed … non per vos salvari putantes, sed adventum
Spiritus sancti in vos **petite***. The construction breaks off from subjunctive to imperative
mid-sentence. Carried as printed: "…not thinking to be saved through yourselves, but **ask ye**
for the coming of the Holy Spirit". Migne's anacoluthon, not smoothed into a fourth subjunctive.

### 0710B · *imputare* — infinitive for imperative

*Deo, non autem vobis **imputare***. Rendered as the imperative it functions as ("impute it to
God, and not to yourselves"); the infinitive-for-imperative is ordinary medieval usage
(Pattern 9), not a defect, and no verb is supplied that the sentence lacks.

### 0708C–0708D · *Sidera.* twice — NOT dittography

Two consecutive glosses are keyed to the same lemma *Sidera* (13). Considered under Pattern 11
and **rejected**: the two glosses are different (apostasy/fallen angels; then instability and
shipwreck), so this is two glosses on one lemma, the Glossa's normal habit, not a doubled run
of type. No `[d:]`.

## §6 · RENDERING DECISIONS WORTH A SECOND EYE

- **`*usque ad*` → `*as far as*`.** Migne's abridgment formula (*etc., usque ad …* = quote the
  head of Beda's sentence, skip, resume at these words). Englished, in its own italic span, per
  the Glossa convention that the lemma is Englished; four instances, all identical. Leaving it
  in Latin was considered — it is an editorial formula, not the author's prose — and rejected:
  "as far as" is a clean equivalent, and the Latin stands opposite in the parallel column.
  **This is the first Glossa book in the corpus to carry the formula in English; if a later
  book should differ, this is the site that set it.**
- **`Judas apostolus` → "Jude the apostle."** Latin has one name for two men; English has two.
  Corpus precedent decides it: the shipped English already reads "James and John, and Jude,
  wrote their Epistles" for exactly this figure, and keeps "Judas" for Iscariot and Maccabeus.
- **`animales` → "animal", not "sensual" or "natural."** The gloss's whole antithesis is
  *animalis* ⟷ *spiritualis* (*Spiritum sanctum … non habent, ideo animales*), and "animal"
  keeps the *anima* root visible. "Sensual" (Douay) and "natural" (AV) would be conforming the
  gloss's own keyword to an English Bible — the 7a″ class.
- **`coagulum charitatis` → "the rennet of charity."** *Coagulum* is the curdling agent, and the
  verb it answers is *defluunt*, "they flow away": without the rennet the mass never sets. "Curd"
  (the result, not the agent) and "bond" (a paraphrase) were both rejected.
- **`pseudo` → "the false ones."** *propter pseudo qui vobis se ingerunt* — *pseudo* used
  substantivally, masculine plural by its relative. Attested medieval usage, Pattern 9, so no
  carry-through and no marker. "False apostles" was declined as importing *apostoli*, which the
  gloss does print four lines later (*pseudoapostoli*) and does not print here.
- **`Sodomorum` → "the Sodomites"** (genitive of *Sodomi*, the men), not "of Sodom" — Migne's
  own *incendium Sodomorum* against the lemma's place-name *Sodoma*.
- **`ut revocat`** (0707C) — *ut* with the indicative, where a purpose clause wants the
  subjunctive. Rendered "**as** he calls back", which is what the indicative allows. Rendering
  it "that he may call back" would silently mend the mood.

### ✅ 0708B–C · VERS. 12 — CONFIRMED, REPAIRED. **The dominant class again, and the plate itself supplies the answer.**

**Printed:** *Nubes. **Comparat se** praedicatoribus pluentibus justitiam, coruscantibus per
miracula, **sed haeretici sunt nubes** obscurantes solem…*

**Was:** "*Clouds.* **He** compares himself to preachers raining justice…"
**Now:** "*Clouds.* **The heretic** compares himself to preachers raining justice…"

*Comparat* prints no subject, and the running "he" of this work is **Jude** in twelve other
places (*informat, dissertet, Dederat, dat, ostendit* ×2, *significat, monebat, dicit,
inquit, ostendens, noluit*) — including the sentence immediately before it. So the default
English reading was that **Jude** likens himself to justice-raining preachers, which his own
*sed haeretici sunt nubes* contradicts three words later.

⭐⭐ **What settled it is the corpus-is-its-own-control move, inside a single paragraph:**
the exactly parallel gloss five lines down **prints its subject outright** — *Impii et
pseudoapostoli **comparant se** arboribus bonum fructum ferentibus, **sed sunt arbores
autumnales*** — and our English had rendered that one correctly ("The ungodly and the false
apostles compare themselves…"). Same construction, same *sed*-contrast, one with the subject
printed and one without. **The author's own repetition is the witness.**

### ✅ 0707C · VERS. 5 lemma — CONFIRMED, REPAIRED. A complementizer read as a causal.

*Quoniam Jesus.* → **was** "*For Jesus.*", **now** "*That Jesus.*"

In Jude 5 *quoniam* is the content-clause complementizer after *scientes semel omnia* —
"knowing all things once, **that** Jesus, having saved the people out of the land of
Egypt…". "For Jesus." asserted a causal connective the verse does not carry, and as an
English string it reads as a prepositional phrase rather than a clause opener — a lemma that
looked like a caption and meant nothing.

### ✅ 0709A · VERS. 19 — CONFIRMED, REPAIRED. A supplied comma that adjudicated.

Migne prints *(quo congregatur Ecclesia spiritualis efficitur)* with **no mark** between
*Ecclesia* and *spiritualis efficitur*. Our English had supplied a comma. Removed.

⚑ The comma did more than tidy: it **forced** *Ecclesia* as subject of both verbs and
stranded *spiritualis* as predicate of *efficitur*, closing off the reading *quo congregatur
Ecclesia spiritualis, efficitur* — a choice made silently by punctuation. ⭐ **The
inconsistency was the tell**: the same chunk carries 0707D's unpunctuated juncture
(*coarctabit **Vel** vinculum*) raw and deliberately, so one class was being treated two ways
in one work.

### ⛔ 0708B · VERS. 11 — DECLINED, both readings recorded

*Quia per omnia haeretici monstrati sunt **a verbo veritatis** cecidisse, ostendit eos
diversis malis subjectos.* Two constructions genuinely construe: **(A)** ours, *cecidisse a
verbo veritatis*, "fallen **from** the word of truth"; **(B)** *monstrati sunt a verbo
veritatis*, "have been shown **by** the word of truth to have fallen" — the *ab*-phrase sits
in the agent position immediately after the passive.

**Left as (A)**, on the ground the reader did not weigh: under (B), *cecidisse* is left with
**no complement at all** — fallen from what? — while (A) completes it, and *cadere/excidere
a fide* is the standing idiom. Word order favours (B), sense favours (A). Named here so the
choice is on the record rather than invisible.

### ⬜ 0708C · `[var:]` #3 — the marker STANDS, with the counter-evidence recorded beside it

The reader audited all three `[var:]` markers in both directions and found the first two
**sound** (*Consolatis* for *conservatis*; *poenam* for *iudicium*, sense-changing, English
following Migne in each). On the third — *Arbores **autem*** against the Vulgate's *arbores
**autumnales*** — it raised a real objection: **Migne's own text prints *autumnales* one
clause later** (*sed sunt arbores autumnales: tarde enim ferunt bonum fructum sicut arbor in
autumno plantata*), and the whole exposition depends on it. So the plate may not carry a
variant reading of Jude 12 at all, but an abbreviated or mis-set lemma head (*aut͞m* for
*autumn.*) which the exposition immediately corrects.

**The marker stands.** Pattern 14 records what the plate prints against what the Vulgate
prints, and both halves of that are true here; **it never speculates about why they differ**,
so a compositor's hand is not grounds for removing it. But the reader is right that a
`[var:]` implies a textual divergence where the evidence on the same line points elsewhere,
so the counter-evidence is recorded here where a later eye will find it. ⚑ The English
rendering itself — "But trees", the shorter form **not** silently completed — is correct and
is exactly the trap the brief named.
