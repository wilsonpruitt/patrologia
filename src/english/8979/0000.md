## THE ARGUMENT.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0693D]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He makes plain the nature of the Word, and that God himself is charity, and he discourses of the whisperers against the brethren, that they can neither know God nor be made godly, so far that he proves them to be murderers, in that hatred is the occasion of killing.

## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *That which was from the beginning.* ⟦s2⟧ In the nature, or the true being, of the Godhead, *which we have heard,* through the law and the prophets, *which we have seen,* coming as man in a manner perceptible to the senses, *which we have looked upon,* discerning the Godhead in the man, *and hands have handled, of the Word of life:* not agreeing by chance with him who was seen in the flesh, but with much handling bringing forward the Scriptures as [0694D] testimony concerning the Word himself. ⟦s3⟧ What a man has seen he can announce to others, what he has at some time perfectly beheld he cannot unfold in words.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *We have seen* and are witnesses. ⟦s2⟧ We have seen, and by announcing to unbelievers we have been made martyrs. ⟦s3⟧ *We declare unto you.* ⟦s4⟧ Thus we have heard through them, but we have not seen, yet we are not less blessed than they, because it is written: *Blessed are they that have not seen and have believed [n: (Luc. XXIV)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And this is the declaration.* ⟦s2⟧ Why was the Word made flesh? what new thing did he bring to the world? ⟦s3⟧ Why did he come to suffer? ⟦s4⟧ It was not in vain, see what he willed to teach, *that God is light.* ⟦s5⟧ By this sentence he shows the excellence of the divine purity, which we are bidden to imitate. ⟦s6⟧ Hence the Manichaeans are refuted, who say that the nature of God was conquered in war by the prince of [0695A] darkness and corrupted. ⟦s7⟧ *We declare,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ We have applied ourselves: unto light, light has shone upon us. ⟦s9⟧ We were darkness, now through the light we are light, and we enlighten others, while we announce that sins are forgiven and darkness driven out.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *If we shall say.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus far the commendation of the epistle; here he shows in what manner charity is to be held. ⟦s3⟧ *And in darkness,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Those who persist in sins, and who darken others, are not counted among his members; whence: *What concord hath Christ with Belial? what fellowship hath light with darkness? [n: (II Cor. VI.)]*

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *But if in the light.* ⟦s2⟧ We are clean, if we walk in the light, but yet we ought not to think that we can, so long as we live, be wholly cleansed from sins. ⟦s3⟧ *As he also is,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ God is said to be in the light, [0695B] because the highest goodness does not find where it may advance, man walks in the light, because by works of the virtues he advances to better things.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *If we shall say,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Those also have not charity who, being proud of their merits, say that they are clean. ⟦s3⟧ *We have.* ⟦s4⟧ He does not say, *we had:* lest perchance it should seem to have been said of things past.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *That he may forgive us sins.* ⟦s2⟧ Those things in us which are graver, he calls sins, but the lesser, he calls iniquity.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *We make him a liar.* ⟦s2⟧ God alone is truthful of himself: man is truthful from God, of himself a liar. ⟦s3⟧ It is impossible that any saint should at some time not fall into the least sins. ⟦s4⟧ Nor do they cease to be just, because with God's help they rise again the more quickly.
