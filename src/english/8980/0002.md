## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 3.-- *Whose let it not be.* The order of the words is this: whose adorning let it not be the plaiting of the hair, or gold encircling, or let it not be the adorning of the raiment of clothing, that is, let there not be festive garments wherewith they are clothed. But let your adorning be the inward man of the heart, known to God alone, in the incorruptibility of the spirit: of a spirit, I say, quiet from the assault of vices and modest, that it be not proud, which spirit, though it lies not open to men, is nevertheless rich in the sight of God.

[0685A]VERS. 7.-- *Ye husbands.* As if he should say: as I have commanded wives, that by their holy conversation they may gain their husbands, and may serve them, in like manner I command you, O husbands, that by your conversation the women may be gained, and keep ye them, you, I say, dwelling with them, rendering the debt. And this according to conscience, not in the passions of desire, but as we understand God to will, namely that you may beget sons unto the worship of the one God, you, I say, giving honour to the woman's vessel, as to the weaker, and providing for them in clothing and in the other things needful for them: and if it sometimes please them to cease from intercourse, bestow honour, because they are the weaker. Bestow it also as upon co-heirs of the grace given in the present by God, and of the life to be given in the future, or of the life to be given for grace.

[0685B]VERS. 9.-- *Not rendering.* Not only cease ye to render evil for evil, but also for a curse give a blessing.

*Because unto this. [n: (BEDA.)]* Because he had forbidden to render evil for evil, etc., *as far as* that we also shall receive a crown for their salvation (for which we were praying). *That a blessing.* The judge saying: *Come, ye blessed of my Father, to possess the kingdom [n: (Matth. XXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Matt. 25:34 *possidete paratum vobis regnum a constitutione mundi*, "possess you the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world"] Or the blessing is understood by which the saints in the life to come shall bless God. What therefore each one desires to come to pass in the future, let him busy himself to meditate and to do in the present, let him bless his Maker and his brother, and render himself worthy of a brother's blessing.

VERS. 13.-- *And who is he. [n: (BEDA.)]* Therefore you are bound [cj: *debitis*; read *debetis*, "you ought"] to turn aside from evil and to do good, because no one can [0685C] draw you back from the good, nor from the crown, if you shall have willed to persist, nay rather they profit you while they wish to harm.

VERS. 14.-- *But the fear.* In the future you shall be blessed, but in the present *fear ye not their fear,* that is, those things in them which can seem to be feared, such as royal power, and the like.

VERS. 15.-- *But the Lord.* The holiness of Christ; behold with the inmost affection of the heart of how incomprehensible a glory it is, and so sanctify Christ himself in you, that he depart not from the memory, not from the love. He who does not consider this holiness fails before the snares of the enemy. *To every one that asketh.* To him that is willing to receive reasonable moneys on loan, let us not refuse. Whence Paul: *Walk in wisdom for the sake of them that are without, knowing how it behoveth to answer every one.* [var: Vulg. Col. 4:5–6 *In sapientia ambulate ad eos, qui foris sunt … ut sciatis quomodo oporteat vos unicuique respondere*, "Walk in wisdom towards them that are without … that you may know how you ought to answer every man"] For they that are without, [0685D] that is, they that are not yet faithful, wish to know something of the hope which is in you. He therefore who presides over the ecclesiastical teaching, let him teach those that are patient, let him stop the mouths of those that resist.

VERS. 16.-- *But with modesty.* [var: Vulg. 1 Pet. 3:16 *Sed cum modestia*, the ablative where Migne prints a genitive] In the very knowledge of doctrine he admonishes that the manner of teaching be observed, that humility may be shown both in living and in speaking.

VERS. 17.-- *It is better. [n: (BEDA.)]* So ought you to bear with your slanderers and to confound them, etc., *as far as* he is removed and is prepared for eternal vengeance.

VERS. 18.-- *He might offer to God. [n: (ID.)]* Because even then, if any had been willing to believe at the preaching of the Lord (which they set forth through the life of the faithful), he rejoiced to offer them to God the Father. But if any disparaged the [0686A] good as though they were evildoers, at the imminence of the flood they were confounded.

VERS. 19.-- *In prison. [n: (ID.)]* They who have their sense darkened with shadows are rightly said to be shut up in prison even in this life, and in this inward prison of the mind they are weighed down by unjust works, until, loosed from the flesh, they are cast into the exterior darkness of eternal damnation. The just also have a prison here, but one of tribulations, whereas the reprobate have one of vices.

VERS. 20.-- *When they waited for the patience of God.* When the patience of God invited them to repentance, sparing them for the hundred years in which Noe was building the ark, by which was shown what was to come to pass in the world. They therefore did not use the patience of God unto repentance, but awaited it as though it were going to endure [0686B] for ever. *Noe.* Noe is interpreted rest, and signifies Christ who gives to his faithful the rest of souls.

*When the ark was a building. [n: (BEDA.)]* As the ark was built of planed timbers, so the Church of the gathering of faithful souls: and as, when the world was perishing, few were saved by water: so, in comparison with those that perish, small is the number of the elect, because narrow is the way which leads to life, and few there are that find it.

VERS. 21.-- *Whereunto you also of the like form.* Baptism makes men safe, baptism, I say, *of the like form,* that is, likened in all things to that ark, because whatsoever is there done carnally, here is done spiritually.

*Not the putting away of the flesh,* etc. I do not say that that baptism [0686C] saves where there is only *a putting away of the filth of the flesh,* that is, where the flesh only is washed outwardly (which the heretics had), but where there is *the examination of a pure conscience,* [var: Vulg. 1 Pet. 3:21 *conscientiae bonae interrogatio*, "the examination of a good conscience"] that is, where a good conscience of him that is to be baptized is asked and required by the baptizer: because such a baptism saves, another kills; the examination has been made tending unto God, that through a good conscience he may be made one with God.

VERS. 22.-- *Swallowing up death. [n: (BEDA.)]* What we swallow down, we bring it about that, taken into our bodies, it nowhere appears: so the Lord utterly consumed death, that it might avail nothing against him, and, the appearance of a true body remaining, the stain of the ancient frailty was absent. Which is promised to us also, whence he adds, *that we also might be made heirs of eternal life. The angels being made subject to him.* We do not doubt that the angels were always subject [0686D] to the Son of God, but he here makes mention of the subjection for this reason, that he might show the assumed humanity so exalted in the resurrection that it is preferred before every power of angelic dignity. Whence, *thou hast subjected all things under his feet [n: (Psal. VIII)]*.
