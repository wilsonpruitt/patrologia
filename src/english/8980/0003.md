## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- *Because he that hath suffered. [n: (BEDA.)]*. He who by the fear of the heavenly judgments extinguishes carnal concupiscences in his mind is already like to Christ crucified, being as it were dead to sins. He lives for the service of God alone.

VERS. 4.-- *Blaspheming. [n: (ID.)]* And if they blaspheme you as separated from their perfidy, yet, seeing in your [0687A] conversation the works of justice and piety, they marvel, and in a manner venerate the faith.

VERS. 6.-- *For this cause was the gospel [sic: *evangelixatum*] preached also to the dead. [n: (BEDA.)]* So great is God's care that we be mortified in the flesh, etc., *as far as* that their own reason may condemn the evils in the ungodly.

VERS. 7.-- *Of all things.* Because he had said that the living and the dead were to be judged in the judgment, lest any should flatter himself concerning the remoteness of the judgment to come, he advisedly admonishes, because although the coming of the last crisis is uncertain, yet it is certain to all that we cannot long subsist in this life.

*Watch in prayers. [n: (BEDA.)]* Let not the mind think anything beyond that alone which it prays for. When we stand to pray, let every carnal thought depart, [0687B] let the sincere intention of the heart pray to God not with the sound of the voice, but with the sense of the mind.

VERS. 8.-- *Having a continual charity. [n: (ID.)]* He who through charity admonishes his neighbour, rebukes him, etc., *as far as* although in public it is not always, able to be shown.

VERS. 11.-- *If any man speak.* If any man has the knowledge of speaking, let him impute it not to himself, but to God: let him fear lest he teach his brother beside the will of God, or the authority of the holy Scriptures, or beside what is useful, and what ought to be said, let him not keep silent.

*If any man minister. [n: (BEDA.)]* When you shall have done all things well and according to his will, attribute it not to your own merits, but to his grace, that others, seeing your good works, may glorify your Father who is in [0687C] heaven.

VERS. 12.-- *Dearly beloved, be ye not as strangers.* If you suffer the heat of tribulations which are brought against reason for your trial and glory, do not for that reason think yourselves to be exiles from the members of Christ, and do not wonder at the evils brought upon you, that through them God may tempt and prove you, because it is an ancient and frequent thing that the elect of God, for eternal salvation, suffer adversities in the present.

VERS 14.-- *You shall be blessed,* etc. He who suffers for the name of Christ is blessed; for you ought not to believe that you suffer without reward, as those do who suffer for their own crimes, and are punished against their will.

*His Spirit resteth upon you.* Because they shall rest upon you that suffer, both in the present in part unto working well, and in the future perfectly unto rewarding.

[0687D]VERS. 17.-- *For the time,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Therefore must we suffer for the name of Christ, etc., *as far as* but the reprobate now *lead their days in their own good things, and in a moment they shall go down to hell [n: (Job. XXI)]*. [var: Vulg. Job 21:13 *Ducunt in bonis dies suos, et in puncto ad inferna descendunt*, "They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment they go down to hell"]

VERS. 18.-- *And if the just man,* etc. [n: (ID.)] This is taken from the Proverbs according to the old edition. In ours, which is according to the Hebrew verity, it stands thus: *If the just man receive in the earth, how much more the wicked and the sinner?* that is, if so great is the frailty of the moral life that not even the just (who are to be crowned in heaven) pass through it without tribulations, by reason of the innumerable stain of a corrupted nature: how much more do they who are without part in the heavenly glory look upon the certain destruction of their perpetual damnation!

[0688A] [n: (AUG.)] You ask the cause: why can the just man scarcely be saved in the judgment of God, and why? what but because it is certain that the justice of God is so great, that sometimes those things which in men seem to our judgment to be just, are found unjust in the judgment of God, according to that saying: *Man seeth in the face, but God beholdeth the heart [n: (I Reg. XVI)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Kings 16:7 *homo enim videt ea quae parent, Dominus autem intuetur cor*, "man seeth the things that appear, but the Lord beholdeth the heart"]

[n: (BEDA.)] It is not laborious to God to free the just man; but that it may be shown that the whole human nature was deservedly condemned, God himself does not will to free easily from so great an evil: wherefore both sins are easy and justice laborious, save to them that love: but the charity which makes men to love is from God.

VERS. 19.-- *Let them commend their souls,* etc. And because judgment begins at the house of the Lord, and through many tribulations one must enter into the kingdom of God, therefore [0688B] this remains for them that are to suffer: that when they suffer according to what God wills, that is, for justice' sake, they commend their souls to God, presuming nothing of themselves, that both here he may cleanse them according to what he wills, and in the future beatify them, because he is faithful in rendering good things to those whom he created. They suffer, I say, *in well-doing,* not for their sins.

## CHAPTER V

VERS. 1.-- *The ancients.* Whereas above he had distinctly admonished the free and servants, husbands and the women themselves, after a common exhortation had been interposed, he thus addresses the old and the young.

VERS. 2.-- *Not by constraint.* Do not preach the Gospel that you may be able to live of the Gospel, *but voluntarily according to God,* that is, only with a view to the reward above.

VERS. 5.-- *In like manner, ye young men. [n: (BEDA.)]* After [0688C] the ancients, how they should preside, etc., *as far as* he immediately subjoins, admonishing generally:

*And all of you insinuate humility one to another,* the old indeed by teaching, the young by following after.

VERS. 6.-- *Be ye humbled. [n: (BEDA.)]* What the grace is which God confers on the humble, etc., *as far as* there follows the fitting reward of exaltation.

VERS. 8.-- *As a roaring lion,* etc. As the roaring of a lion hinders the ears, that they may not take in any other sound, so the devil, by terrifying the minds of the faithful and by suggesting unlawful things, turns them away from the way of truth, that they may not hear the voice of Christ.

VERS. 9.-- *Knowing. [n: (BEDA.)]* Have so much the greater confidence, etc., *as far as* let it shame you that you alone before all others are not able to suffer.

[0688D]VERS. 12.-- *I have written.* To you, he says, *I have written* not as commanding, but beseeching and testifying by the authority of the Scriptures that this is the true grace which I preach in writing, because there is salvation in no other, in whom it behoves you to be saved. Or, beseeching you that this grace in which you stand, and with which you have been imbued, you may make to be your true grace, and may hold it as profiting you. He who spurns grace does not diminish grace, but renders it not his own, that is, not useful to himself.

VERS. 13.-- *In Babylon. [n: (BED.)]* He calls Rome Babylon because of the confusion of manifold idolatry, etc., *as far as* it cannot be free from the pressure of the devil.
