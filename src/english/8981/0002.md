## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 2.-- *For you are yet carnal.* He reproves those who complained that they had not long since heard spiritual things, when they were as yet unworthy to hear them.

*His ministers. [n: (AUG.)]* Man ministers outwardly [0523A] as a servant, etc., *as far as* because God works both to will and to accomplish.

VERS. 8.-- *Are one.* It might seem that, if they give nothing of themselves, God nevertheless gives more through this man than through that.

VERS. 9.-- *Helpers.* In tilling the Lord's field; not corrupters, as certain men say. Which is plain from this, that you are the husbandry. *Husbandry.* Because he tills you unto fruit. *Building,* because he who tills dwells in you. And so the field and the building are the same, which is not so in visible things. We cultivate God by adoring, not by adorning. But he so cultivates us that he renders us better.

VERS. 11.-- *For other foundation no man can lay. [n: (AUG., lib. de Fide et operibus, c. 15.)]* The faith of Christian grace, that [0523B] is, that faith which works through love, etc., *as far as* of which it is written: *The furnace proveth the potter's vessels, and the temptation of tribulation proveth men [n: (Eccli. XXVII)]* [var: Vulg. Eccli. XXVII, 6 *et homines justos tentatio tribulationis*, with *justos* and with no second verb].

*Christ.* That is, the faith of Christ, which works through love, by which Christ dwells in hearts. There is no other foundation. And this suffers no one to perish.

VERS. 12.-- *But if any man.* In gold, silver, precious stones excellent doctrine is signified, in the three others empty doctrine is marked out, which, if for the present it deceives, will appear in the fire: because it will burn while the good abides, and there will be for it, that is, for the good doctrine, a reward, life eternal. *Wood, hay, stubble.* These are not to be taken of evil works, as though faith without works saved, which it does not.

[0523C] [n: (AUG., in psal. XXX.)] Let no one who has wicked works promise himself the kingdom of God, etc., *as far as* the least and lightest sins, which the fire easily consumes.

VERS. 13.-- *For the day.* Here it is the day of man, acting at his own nod, but in the judgment or in the death of each it is the day of the Lord, judging according to merits, nay, even here judgment begins from the house of God, that is, punishment begins from those who are to be saved, and will be consummated in the reprobate. For the elect are punished for this, that they may not be punished there. In which day there is the fire of tribulation by which the love of temporal things is burned, while they are grieved over what is lost, whence also it is said: *Such shall have tribulation of the flesh.* But the fire of the last judgment will endure so long, until those who are to be saved have been purged. *The fire shall try.* [0523D] We read that there will be two fires. One eternal, by which the reprobate will be punished eternally, which will follow the judgment. The other which will go before, by which the face of this world will be burned away, which will amend those who have built upon it wood, hay, stubble. But those who have built gold, silver, precious stones are safe from either fire.

VERS. 14.-- *A reward.* Not only after the end, but even in this life he has the rest of his soul, which despises all things.

VERS. 16.-- *Know you not,* etc. This he set down beforehand that he might prick those who, living basely, violate their own bodies; and most of all that man who has his father's wife. Here he treats of the builders upon the foundation.

VERS. 17.-- *But if any man.* That man is saved by fire, [0524A] but if any man shall have wished the temple which you are, he will destroy him. [cj: *voluerit*; read *violaverit*, "shall have violated" (I Cor. III, 17)] And this you know not.

*The temple. [n: (AUG.)]* They in whom God dwells by faith are the temple of God, etc., in what manner the Angels of God also are now a temple.

[n: (AUG.)] Such a congregation is the building of the temple of God; such a congregation is made not by carnal generation, but by spiritual regeneration. And so God dwells in individuals as in his temples, and in all gathered together as in his temple. God therefore is everywhere present, and everywhere wholly present, yet not dwelling everywhere, but in his temple, to which he is by grace kind and propitious. But as dwelling he is received by some more amply, by others less. And his dwelling in sensual men and in little ones, not [0524B] in age of flesh, but of mind, is for this reason, that in them he works secretly that they may be his temple, and this he perfects in those who advance and who persevere in advancing.

[n: (ID.)] But someone might say: Before God made heaven and earth, before, etc., *as far as* but most blessed are they, for whom to have God is the same as to know him.

*For the wisdom of this world,* etc. [n: (AMBR.)] Wisdom is put improperly for cunning.

VERS. 21.-- *Let no man,* etc. In baptizers or teachers. *Let no man.* Because they themselves are yours; because all things are given to serve you, and there is no glorying save in things more excellent. *In men.* Because they are not givers of graces, whether they be good or bad, but only ministers.

[0524C]VERS. 22.-- *For all things.* Let them not claim single teachers or baptizers for themselves, since they have the use of all. The world is ours, if we set its course in the will of God. The present life is ours, if it is led modestly and with the glory of God. Death is ours, if in hope of what is to come we willingly die for Christ. Things present are ours, if we so use them that we give no offence. Things to come, if believing them we rather desire them.

*Or life,* etc. Both the life and the death of teachers ought to be for the edification of their subjects. Or by life understand holy men and angels: who study the advancement of the faithful, by death, the devil and his members, who, while they persecute, serve the profit of the servants of God.

VERS. 23.-- *And you are Christ's.* That as these things [0524D] are subject to us, so we may be made subject to Christ. *And Christ is God's.* His proper Son, doing his will, that we also may do his. Why then, to the injury of Christ, do you hope for anything from men?
