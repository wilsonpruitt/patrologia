## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0526D]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *It is absolutely heard.* ⟦s2⟧ He begins to treat of fornication, and rebukes that fornicator who was keeping his father's wife, and those who tolerated him. ⟦s3⟧ For they too were partakers with him, in that they suffered a man guilty of so huge a sin to come together with them uncorrected. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore he showed that not only was that man guilty of death, but that those also who clung to him were not free from the crime.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *You are puffed up.* ⟦s2⟧ With the wind of pride, which drives out mercy; because if one of the members suffers, the rest suffer with it.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *In the name of the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ In this manner I have judged, that you, gathered together into one without any dissension [0527A] (with whom my authority and the power of Christ will cooperate), should deliver such a one to Satan.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *To deliver,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Apostle is said to have had this power, that when he could not remove someone from his folly, he would deliver him to the devil to be vexed; until, professing penitence, he should desist from evil.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That the spirit may be saved.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus the Apostle delivers to Satan not cruelly, but lovingly. ⟦s3⟧ Thus Moses judged but few among the worshippers of idols, terrifying for the present, and establishing discipline for the time to come. ⟦s4⟧ So also Elias and other good men: because thus both fear is struck into others, and their own sin is lessened. ⟦s5⟧ Whence Christ did not reprehend this example of Elias in his disciples; but because it was from hatred and not from love of correction that they desired the vengeance of fire upon those who had [0527B] not afforded them lodging; thus also Peter is read to have taken vengeance upon Ananias and his wife, or he delivers him, that the Holy Spirit, of whom the defiled are stripped, may be saved to them, not lost in the day of judgment.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *The whole lump.* ⟦s2⟧ They were partakers, in that they suffered the guilty man. ⟦s3⟧ But if anyone has not the power to cast out one whom he knows to be guilty, or is not able to prove it, he is free from blame. ⟦s4⟧ And it is not the judge's part to condemn without an accuser, as neither did Christ cast out Judas.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Paste.* ⟦s2⟧ It is flour kneaded together with water without leaven, that is, unleavened bread. ⟦s3⟧ He wills therefore that these be some one thing through love, pure from the corruption of sin, having put on the new man, as they were made in baptism. ⟦s4⟧ Zyma in Greek, fermentum in Latin.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Pasch is an immolation, not a passing over, as to certain men [0527C] it seems, for first the Pasch and so the passing over: because first is the example of the Saviour, and so salvation.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] This renewal of our life is a certain passing over from death to life, etc., *as far as* since Christ our pasch is immolated.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Of sincerity.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the new life and of truth, without any deceit, that sincerity may make life clean, and may exclude every deceit of truth.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Otherwise,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Such men could not be gained for Christ, if they were to avoid conversation with them and their table; whence also the Lord ate with publicans and sinners.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *But now.* ⟦s2⟧ Then I wrote to you, and you did not take it well, but now I write determinately. ⟦s3⟧ *Is named.* ⟦s4⟧ The Apostle wished that naming to be understood: [0527D] which is pronounced against anyone with a sentence, and with judicial order, and with integrity. ⟦s5⟧ For if any naming whatever suffices, many innocent men must be condemned, because crimes are often falsely named against someone. ⟦s6⟧ By saying *is named,* he sufficiently shows that the wicked are to be taken away from the fellowship of the Church not rashly or in any manner whatever, but by judgment, or, if they cannot be taken away by judgment, that they should rather be borne with, lest anyone, by perversely avoiding the wicked and departing from the Church, should send those whom he seems to flee to hell.

## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Doth any man dare.* ⟦s2⟧ On occasion of such a judgment he begins to treat of judgments, in which in many ways [0528A] the Corinthians sinned, as, that despising the faithful they went to unbelieving judges, or appointed foolish judges, and went to law, and defrauded, and did other things of this kind.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *That.* ⟦s2⟧ Because by the example of their faith, perfidy of this kind will be condemned.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *If therefore you have secular judgments.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the Apostle had said that they could judge of these least matters, he determines who are to be appointed for settling business of this kind, namely, the contemptible who are in the Church. ⟦s3⟧ For the greater ought to attend to spiritual things.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. de opere Monac. c. 20.)] The wise therefore, the faithful who were established in those places, etc., *as far as* or to be cut short by intervening.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *To your shame.* ⟦s2⟧ Let them examine earthly causes [0528B] who have received the wisdom of outward things. ⟦s3⟧ But those who are endowed with spiritual gifts ought not to be entangled in earthly business; that while they are not compelled to dispose of lower goods, they may be able to serve the higher goods. ⟦s4⟧ Yet care must be taken that those who shine with spiritual gifts do by no means utterly desert the affairs of their weak neighbours, but either commit them to be handled by others for whom it is fitting, or manage them themselves.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *To your brethren.* ⟦s2⟧ Whom you ought to help, and so you lose the kingdom, and worse, because you do it knowingly.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Know you not.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that he so often repeats, *You know not;* as if: By following me, you ought to know this; but by following the false apostles, you are become fools.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *But you are sanctified.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that *but,* a repeated conjunction, gives no small weight to the sentence.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0528C]VERS. 12.-- *All things are lawful to me.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he said that it was rather to be borne than that causes should be ill conducted, lest anyone should think that his own goods are in no way to be reclaimed, he subjoins: It is lawful for me to reclaim all my own, but yet I will not reclaim them, because not all things are expedient for my course, but hinder it. ⟦s3⟧ Again he shows the same by another reason. ⟦s4⟧ As if: They are lawful, but I will not reclaim them, because, since I am free, I do not wish to be brought under a judge's own power [cj: *suo potestate*; read *sub potestate* — the plate prints *sub* in the parallel clause on the same line]: or, I do not wish to be under the dominion of any thing, so as to offend a brother for its sake.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Meat for the belly.* ⟦s2⟧ For these reasons they are not to be reclaimed, nevertheless meat is due to the belly. ⟦s3⟧ By these understand the necessaries of nature, and therefore it is lawful for them to be reclaimed. ⟦s4⟧ But there must not be much labour for these, because they will be destroyed, and therefore it is lawful either that judgment be made concerning them, or that it be passed over.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Your bodies.* ⟦s2⟧ It cannot be that a body is not holy, [0528D] which the sanctified Spirit uses. ⟦s3⟧ *They are the members,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ As all the members which are ruled by one and the same soul are one body, so all who are quickened by the same Holy Spirit are made one body in Christ. ⟦s5⟧ *Of Christ.* ⟦s6⟧ Because he took a body, and is the head of bodies.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *One body.* ⟦s2⟧ Natural philosophers hand down that they are so united that, if their blood were mingled, it would be wholly joined together.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *But he that is joined,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not by identity of substance, but by participation, since he who here draws near by faith so cleaves to him that he is a partaker of his beatitude, which will then be full, when his desire will be satisfied in unchangeable goods, [0529A] namely in the Trinity itself, his Lord, whose image he is.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Fly fornication.* ⟦s2⟧ With other vices a conflict may be awaited, but flee this one, that you come not near it, because it cannot otherwise be better conquered. ⟦s3⟧ *For every sin.* ⟦s4⟧ No sin so dishonours and defiles the body as that one. ⟦s5⟧ *But he that committeth fornication.* ⟦s6⟧ It is not said on account of the greatness of fornication, that it alone is in the body, and the others outside the body. ⟦s7⟧ For many are as great as fornication, or greater. ⟦s8⟧ But on account of the quality, because it defiles the body more basely than other sins.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *The temple.* ⟦s2⟧ From this it is plain that the Holy Spirit is God; for if he were not God, he would assuredly not have ourselves for a temple. ⟦s3⟧ But not only the Holy Spirit, but the Father also and the Son have us for a [0529B] temple. ⟦s4⟧ The temple therefore of God, that is, of the whole Trinity, is the holy Church.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *And bear God.* ⟦s2⟧ To bear God is to show the image of God in deeds well done. ⟦s3⟧ *In your body.* ⟦s4⟧ If thou sparest not thyself for thine own sake, spare at least for God's sake, who has made thee a house for himself. ⟦s5⟧ And if thou overthrowest it, thou sinnest against God, which is a grave kind of sin; but it is another kind to sin against man.
