## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 1.-- *Concerning the things whereof.* On the occasion of the fornication which he forbids, he goes on to treat of marriage, whereon they had made inquiry. And he handles it in three ways, according to counsel, according to indulgence, according to precept.

VERS. 6.-- *But this,* etc.--Someone will say: If [0529C] the Apostle granted this by way of pardon, then marriage is a sin; for to whom is pardon granted save to sin? Plainly, what the Apostle granted to weakness by way of pardon has regard to the marital act of the wedded, where the evil of incontinence is. The evil of incontinence is that a husband knows his wife even beyond the necessity of procreating children, but there too is the good of marriage. For not because the evil of incontinence is there, is the wedlock which is apart from the intention of generation therefore not good; nay rather, not on account of that evil is this good culpable, but that evil is made venial on account of the nuptial good. This good of marriage the Apostle does not reprehend, but the evil of incontinence. That intercourse which is necessary for the cause of generation is blameless, and it alone is nuptial. But he who [0529D] goes on beyond necessity obeys not reason, but lust.

VERS. 7.-- *Let every one,* etc. No one ought to be constrained, lest, being forbidden what is lawful, he take up what is unlawful, but let him himself choose what he will follow, yet in this it is better to be the more inclined.

VERS. 9.-- *To burn,* is to be driven or overcome by desires. He does not therefore say *better* as though it were good to burn and better to marry, but he has followed the custom of speech.

VERS. 11.-- *But if she depart.* It is one thing to depart, another to abstain by common consent. For the first is evil, the second good.

[n: (AMBR.)]. Therefore he does not add concerning the husband as concerning the wife, because it is lawful for a man to marry another, because the inferior does not altogether [0530A] use this law, because he is the mightier, which is said to have been inserted by falsifiers.

*And the husband the wife.* A husband ought not to put away his wife, because he would make her commit adultery. But if she is an adulteress he does not do this, but puts away an adulteress. The cause which Christ excepted is here passed over in silence, because it is most well known, namely, that of fornication.

VERS. 12.-- *But to the rest.* Where both are believers, the Apostle gave this precept of the Lord. But to the rest, where not both are believers (which happened at the beginning of the Church, when the Gospel began to be preached), I say, etc. *If any brother,* etc. Idolatry and any harmful superstition whatever is fornication, and the Lord did indeed permit a wife to be put away for the cause of fornication, but he did not command it, and so he gave the Apostle place for admonishing, that [0530B] he who will may not put away the unbelieving wife, whereby perhaps she may be made a believer.

VERS. 14.-- *Is sanctified.* Because it often happens that the one is converted to the faith through the other. Or, the unbelieving husband is clean as regards the believing wife, and the believing wife suffers no uncleanness from her union with him. *Otherwise.* If you put away, being unwilling to dwell together, and join yourselves to others, you are adulterers, and your children bastards, and therefore unclean; but now, holy, because born of lawful marriages, and born under reverence for the Creator.

VERS. 15.-- *For not.* That is not so great a marriage which is without devotion to God: and therefore it is no sin for him who puts away for God's sake, if he has joined himself to another. But if both have believed, the marriage is confirmed [0530C] through the knowledge of God. *In peace.* There is no need to quarrel with the one who departs, because he departs out of hatred of God.

VERS. 16.-- *For how.* He exhorted above by the things that had come to pass, now, because the same things can happen, he exhorts to the same. But these things are done not by the command of a law, but by free charity.

VERS. 17.-- *The Lord hath distributed.* To each God has given when he shall be saved, that is, he knows when he will believe, and he bears with him until he believe, so do thou also wait.

VERS. 18.-- *Is any man circumcised.* Nowhere does the Apostle take away a custom which, when kept, does not hinder salvation, admonishing that the hope of salvation be not placed in such things, since they are kept only for the offence of the weak. Whence he says that they are nothing, adding: *Circumcision is nothing.*

VERS. 20.-- *Let every one abide in the same.* This has regard to those conditions [0530D] or usages of life which are in nothing hurtful to faith and to good morals. For as a married man, so also a robber is called to the faith of Christ. But the one in his marriage, not from his marriage. The other, however, not in his robbery, but from his robbery. For it is not necessary that married persons cease to be married for the faith of Christ, as it is necessary that robbers cease to be robbers.

VERS. 21.-- *A servant,* etc. Above he set down one example concerning a manner of living, here another concerning the condition of an office. *Use it rather.* Because the more anyone is despised in this world for God's sake, so much the more shall he be exalted in the world to come.

VERS. 22.-- *A freedman.* Because he has been rescued from the sins which make men servants. Therefore let neither the servant be despised for his condition, nor let the free man who has been rescued be lifted up and set himself before the servant.

[0531A]VERS. 23.-- *You are bought.* Render the recompense, that you may be the servants of Christ, not of men. These are the servants of men, who subject themselves to human superstitions. *The servants of men.* Which happens to you if you say: *I am of Paul, I of Apollo,* etc.

VERS. 25.-- *Concerning virgins.* By occasion of that fornicator, he teaches concerning all fornication that is to be shunned.

VERS. 26.-- By *necessity* he means the want of the world which the married often suffer. To the weak he holds out a weak reason. For he could have rendered a worthier reason for virginity, by commending its gift more amply. For virginity passes beyond the condition of human nature, whereby men are made like to the angels. Yet the victory of virgins is greater than that of the angels. For the angels live without flesh, [0531B] but virgins triumph in the flesh.

VERS. 29.-- *This therefore I say.* Here he admonishes to temperance, as if he were to say: When you are joined together, that it may be done without sin; this counsel I give you, that they who have wives *be as though they had none.* This he does who, having a wife, renders the debt and does not exact it; who takes a wife on account of his own weakness, lamenting rather that he could not be without a wife than rejoicing that he has taken one, and most of all because they keep continence by equal consent. Those marriages surely are to be judged more blessed which, whether children have been begotten or offspring despised, have been able to keep continence by equal consent.

VERS. 34.-- *She thinketh,* etc. For she, not fearing concerning damnation and secure concerning salvation, thinks only [0531C] on the things that are the Lord's. *That she may be holy,* etc. This the married woman also does, but the unmarried more amply, who, free from the worldly necessities by which the married woman is bound, is more intently at leisure for the heavenly precepts.

VERS. 35.-- *Not that I may cast a snare.* But tending to this I say what is honourable, that a man be chaste in body and in mind, not that marriage is base, but that this is more honourable and has the facility of praying to God.

VERS. 36.-- *But if any man,* etc. As he determined concerning virgins, so he determines concerning their guardians.

VERS. 38.-- *Doeth better.* Because with God he lays up merit for her, and frees her from the solicitude of the world. And therefore he rightly says: For better is that which is lawful and expedient, than that which is lawful and not expedient.

VERS. 39.-- *A woman is bound.* He had said that a woman who departs [0531D] from her husband for the cause of fornication remains unmarried, or is reconciled to her husband, and he had not said whether she should so remain the whole time of her own life, or for the time of her husband; this therefore he determines. And so a virgin may always marry whom she will, but a woman, that is, one married, even though put away for the cause of fornication and separated from her husband, is bound to the law while her husband lives, etc. *But if he sleep.* He does not say, the first, or the second, or the how-manieth, for we are not to define what the Apostle does not define: whence neither ought I to condemn any marriages, nor ought their multiplicity to bring shame upon them. But the Lord does not condemn the woman of seven husbands, nor does he say that she cannot be in the resurrection. But only: *neither shall they marry, nor be married.* Whence neither, against the sense of human modesty, [0532A] do I dare to say that they may marry as often as they will, nor from my heart to condemn any marriages whatsoever. What is said of a woman of one husband, this holds of every widow.

VERS. 40.-- *More blessed,* etc. He shows well enough that a faithful woman who marries again even after her husband's death is blessed, but that she who does not marry is more blessed. *But I think.* The Apostle does not doubt, but rebukes the scornful, as the Lord also, saying: *If you knew me, perhaps you would know my Father also:* Or, *Thinkest thou, shall he find faith on the earth?* By a word of doubting he convicts unbelief.
