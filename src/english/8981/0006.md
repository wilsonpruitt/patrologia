## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Concerning these things.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: You know as well as I that it is lawful to eat them; because we all have knowledge. ⟦s3⟧ This he says of those who, having knowledge, ate with [0532B] offence. ⟦s4⟧ These he first reproves, and says that they do not know sufficiently. ⟦s5⟧ But others in ignorance ate under veneration of the idol.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Knowledge puffeth up.* ⟦s2⟧ Of itself knowledge is unprofitable, with charity it is profitable. ⟦s3⟧ Of itself it puffs up into pride, like the demons, who by a Greek name are so called from knowledge. ⟦s4⟧ For the repressing of the elation of knowledge a sting was given to Paul. ⟦s5⟧ It is better to know our own weakness than the natures of things; he who adds this knowledge adds the sorrow of pilgrimage out of longing for the fatherland.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *But if any man love God.* ⟦s2⟧ He loves God who for charity's sake tempers his knowledge, that it may profit the brother for whom Christ died.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Concerning meats.* ⟦s2⟧ Having found fault with their knowledge, [0532C] he at length discloses what that true knowledge is. ⟦s3⟧ *An idol is nothing in the world.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, among the creatures of the world. ⟦s5⟧ For God formed the matter, but the folly of men gave the form. ⟦s6⟧ For whatsoever things are in creatures were made through the Word. ⟦s7⟧ But the form of a man in an idol was not made through the Word, as neither was sin made through the Word. ⟦s8⟧ But it is nothing, and men become nothing when they sin. ⟦s9⟧ *But one.* ⟦s10⟧ This he says, lest God be thought to be in the idol.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For indeed there are many gods.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, many to the Gentiles: whom our God terrifies, that is, the demons, who, wishing to be seen as gods, exact for themselves the things that are owed to the true God, altars, sacrifices, and the rest. ⟦s3⟧ But ours makes men gods.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *God.* ⟦s2⟧ In three ways is God said. ⟦s3⟧ Substantively God is said, the Trinity. ⟦s4⟧ By adoption, the saints [0532D] who by the grace of adoption are gods. ⟦s5⟧ By appellation, the demons and idols. ⟦s6⟧ *Of whom.* ⟦s7⟧ All things are from the Father, but created through the Son. ⟦s8⟧ Nor are some things through the Son and others through the Father, but the same: otherwise now not all things are through the Father, nor all things through the Son; but if all things are through the Father, then all things also through the Son; therefore the same things were made through the Father which were made also through the Son; the Son therefore is equal to the Father, and the operation of both is inseparable. ⟦s9⟧ *And one.* ⟦s10⟧ Of Christ, in respect of his humanity, he adds it, but of the Holy Spirit it was not needful.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Is defiled.* ⟦s2⟧ Namely, through those who have the knowledge of one God, but not with charity, through whom the weak were led into this error, whose food is not defiled, but their conscience.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0533A]VERS. 12.-- *Against Christ.* ⟦s2⟧ To sin against Christ is to deny Christ.

## CHAPTER IX.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Am I not free.* ⟦s2⟧ He begins to set himself forth as an example, that as he himself abstains from what is lawful for the scandal of the brethren, so also they. ⟦s3⟧ As if he were to say: Abstain from this lawful thing, because I have the liberty of receiving a stipend, and yet I do not receive it. ⟦s4⟧ And although it be so introduced, yet he intends to prove himself an apostle, because on occasion of the false apostles the Corinthians thought less of him.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Have we not power,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] For the Lord permitted, he did not command, etc., *as far as* it is therefore a power, not a command of the Lord, to receive maintenance.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0533B] *A woman, a sister.* ⟦s2⟧ The Lord had women in his company, lest they should seem strangers to salvation, who also ministered to him; and so also the apostles.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Of working.* ⟦s2⟧ The word of working is more honourable than that of receiving.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Who serveth as a soldier.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest, because he abstains, he should seem to have reproved his fellow apostles, he adds: *Who serveth as a soldier.*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (CHRYS.)] Who planteth a vineyard, and eateth not of the fruit thereof?* ⟦s2⟧ By the former he shows perils; by this, labour and much misery and care, and he adds likewise a third example in these words.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who feedeth,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He shows the much diligence owed also by a master toward those under him. ⟦s2⟧ Which appears from these things: for soldiers, and husbandmen, and shepherds [0533C] were the apostles, not of wars, nor of the earth, nor of brute animals, but of souls endowed with reason [cj: *rationem*; read *ratione*, "endowed with reason" — *praeditus* governs the ablative], and of that army which is armed against the demons. ⟦s3⟧ *And of the milk.* ⟦s4⟧ The milk of the flock is said to be whatever is given by the people to those set over them.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Do I speak these things according to man,* etc., [n: (ID.)] That is: Do I confirm my case by human examples only? or does not the law also say this?

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Doth God take care for oxen.* ⟦s2⟧ They are indeed a care; but not so, that he should establish a law concerning them.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *That we give no offence.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, lest it be said of us that we preach the Gospel rather for the cause of gain and of temporal profit than for the cause of the salvation of souls and of the eternal reward, and the Gospel be held as a thing for sale.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0533D]VERS. 13.-- *Know you not.* ⟦s2⟧ He returns again to what he had begun, showing that it is lawful for him, and yet that he does not do it. ⟦s3⟧ Natural reason also has this, that a man live from that whereon he labours.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 15.-- *But I.* The Apostle abstains from maintenance, lest he be a pattern to the rapacious false apostles.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is good,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For whoever is unwilling to use what is owed to him spends the more upon the Church. ⟦s3⟧ The Apostle therefore had the greatest glory with God, in not exacting a stipend from the weak, or in procuring his daily food from his own labours. ⟦s4⟧ *Glory.* ⟦s5⟧ Which I have with God, having compassion on the weak.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *For if,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For a reward: or, if [0534A] I shall so preach the gospel, *it is no glory to me;* because then it would be for necessity, since *woe,* that is, want, would be, as it is to the false apostles. ⟦s3⟧ *Necessity,* of sustaining this life. ⟦s4⟧ *Woe.* ⟦s5⟧ Because I have not whence to live.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *For if I do this willingly.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the reward is from the will. ⟦s3⟧ Let us see how, namely, if I so act out of love that I rather suffer want than abuse the power. ⟦s4⟧ *But if against my will.* ⟦s5⟧ So that what I am compelled to by necessity will profit others, but not me. ⟦s6⟧ *A dispensation is committed to me.* ⟦s7⟧ Here such a dispensation is understood, as that a servant dispenses another's revenue, whereof he himself takes nothing beyond his victuals. ⟦s8⟧ Elsewhere he is called a dispenser who, as a son, ministers the Gospel to his co-heirs. ⟦s9⟧ We ought not therefore to preach the Gospel that we may eat, but therefore to eat that we may preach the Gospel; so that food be not the good [0534B] which is sought, but the necessary thing which is added.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *From all.* ⟦s2⟧ From the businesses of men, taking nothing from them; *of all,* understand men, *I made myself the servant,* bearing with all, as a good steward bears with the weak, and most of all you, O Corinthians, through my patience. ⟦s3⟧ But I did this not by the craft of cunning, but by the affection of one who has compassion, *that I might gain the more to Christ.* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 9:19 *ut plures lucrifacerem*, without *Christo*]

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *As a Jew. [n: (HIER.)]* Not truly a Jew, as neither truly a Gentile, to whom in meats, etc., *as far as* how he would wish to be served, if he were sick.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *To them that are under the law,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ To the Samaritans, who receive the books of Moses, which the Jews do not use in common with them. ⟦s3⟧ But they are of Persian origin, whom, when Israel had been taken away, the king of the Assyrians or of the Persians settled to inhabit [0534C] the places of Samaria.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *All things to all men.* ⟦s2⟧ He yielded to all, yet he did not go beyond religion.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Know you not,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He shows how great is the profit of our law, in which the palm is promised not to one, but to all; and he who comes first waits that he may be crowned with the later. ⟦s3⟧ *The prize.* ⟦s4⟧ It is the reward of the race.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *For every one,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ From things not to be praised many similitudes are drawn, as in the Gospel concerning the unjust judge, who would not hear the widow, and concerning the slothful man who lent loaves not out of friendship, but out of weariness; so here athletic and sportive things are not commended.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Not as.* ⟦s2⟧ He runs at uncertainty who [0534D] does such things, so that from some things he may hope, from others despair.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *I chastise my body.* ⟦s2⟧ The Apostle has terrified us by his own fear. ⟦s3⟧ For what shall the lamb do, where the ram trembles? ⟦s4⟧ *Lest perhaps,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Which could quickly happen; then must others the more beware, lest by giving offence they be reprobate.
