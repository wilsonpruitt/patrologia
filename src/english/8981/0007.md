## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *For I would not.* ⟦s2⟧ He who wishes to lay hold must so act; for baptism and the sacraments do not suffice, as some think; even as neither did the benefits of God avail the Jews, since they afterwards sinned. ⟦s3⟧ *That our fathers.* ⟦s4⟧ By the example of the Jews, who by their own negligence gave offence, he makes them anxious. ⟦s5⟧ *Under the cloud.* ⟦s6⟧ Because all things befell them in a figure of us.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0535A]VERS. 2.-- *Baptized in Moses.* ⟦s2⟧ Under the leadership of Moses, purged through those signs that were seen. ⟦s3⟧ Or, they received the sign of baptism, which availed the same for believers. ⟦s4⟧ *They were baptized,* because they were freed from death and through it cleansed from the ignorance of God which pressed upon all the nations, and made ready for receiving the law. ⟦s5⟧ Or they are said to be baptized because they bore the form of our sacrament. ⟦s6⟧ Moses signifies Christ, the cloud the Holy Spirit, the sea baptism.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *The same meat.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, in faith: diverse in time, diverse in appearance. ⟦s3⟧ *Spiritual.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, signifying something spiritual. ⟦s5⟧ When he says *spiritual,* he shows that it is to be understood spiritually in Christ, and this he intimates of them all, and expounds one: *The rock was Christ.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The rock following them.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, satisfying [0535B] their will, because wheresoever they went, overflowing waters followed; so Christ accompanies his own in the desert of this world. ⟦s3⟧ Or, *following,* that is, signifying the truth that was to follow after. ⟦s4⟧ *And the rock.* ⟦s5⟧ A thing which is signified is wont to be named by the name of the thing which it signifies. ⟦s6⟧ *The rock.* ⟦s7⟧ That is, Christ, was following, because where human support failed, he was at hand.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Neither become idolaters,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He touches those who feasted in the idol's temple, thinking themselves free from crime. ⟦s3⟧ *The people sat down.* ⟦s4⟧ By the commemoration of those things he marks what things are in the Corinthians, showing how gravely they were punished in the Jews. ⟦s5⟧ *To play.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, to adore, which is like the play of boys. ⟦s7⟧ For childhood is prone to play. ⟦s8⟧ And what is so like to a boyish game [0535C] as to adore idols?

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Neither let us tempt Christ.* ⟦s2⟧ Note Christ as God, whom the Jews tempted, and therefore they perished by serpents, until the brazen serpent was raised up, and they who looked upon it were healed of the bites.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *In a figure.* ⟦s2⟧ Behold, he shows why those signs are no longer observed, since the thing is made manifest for our correction.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *They are written.* ⟦s2⟧ Not for us only. ⟦s3⟧ For by these examples we are stirred up to a more corrected life, that we may merit either a reward, if we have been obedient, or a heavier punishment if disobedient. ⟦s4⟧ *The ends of the world.* ⟦s5⟧ Because we are in the last age, and by so many examples of those before us we ought the more to be corrected. ⟦s6⟧ Or the ends of the world have come upon us, worldliness [0535D] is ended in us; and therefore it is the more shameful if we sin.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Let him take heed lest he fall.* ⟦s2⟧ As those who, presuming upon knowledge, ate things offered to idols with scandal to the brethren, and glorying in the false ones, judged the apostle, whereas they themselves were guilty.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Let no temptation take hold of you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He exhorts that human temptation may take hold of them, not another. ⟦s3⟧ For human temptation is this, that in necessity or in pressure a man should not distrust God, by seeking human help. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore to suffer for Christ is a human temptation, whereby profit is made with God.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That you may be able to bear it.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is done through humility. ⟦s3⟧ For he keeps the humble in every temptation, so that they do not [0536A] burst in the furnace who have not the wind of pride.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Wherefore,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because the sacraments alone do not save, and because he who falls is punished, and because the help of God is not wanting, flee from the worship of idols. ⟦s3⟧ Or, lest the wise eat things offered to idols with offence to the weak, to whom they would seem idolaters. ⟦s4⟧ Or, lest the weak themselves be idolaters, and that you may flee I speak something lofty, as to the prudent, and therefore judge diligently. ⟦s5⟧ Or. now I speak to the weak, as above to the prudent.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The chalice of blessing.* ⟦s2⟧ Therefore must the worship of idols be fled, because as he who eats a thing offered to an idol is one with the demon, so through the body of Christ he is one with Christ.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0536B] *The bread which we break. [n: (BEDA.)]* Christ, when he is eaten, life is eaten. ⟦s2⟧ But who would dare, etc., *as far as* he remained whole with the Father, that he might feed the angels.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *For one,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ One bread by the union of faith, of hope and of charity. ⟦s3⟧ It is a body through the ministering of charity: because we are one, and ought to be of one mind. ⟦s4⟧ And this he says, that one faith may have one understanding and one work.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Behold.* ⟦s2⟧ It is a similitude for the understanding of what was said above. ⟦s3⟧ He therefore added, *according to the flesh,* because there is an Israel according to the spirit, which no longer follows the old shadows, but that truth which follows after them (which was signified by those foregoing shadows); and it offers to God in the body [0536C] of Christ the sacrifice of praise, from which the God of gods, the Lord, has spoken, and has called the earth from the rising of the sun unto the going down thereof.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *What then?* ⟦s2⟧ Because I have said, flee from the worship of idols, I seem to say that *a thing offered to idols is something* great, but I do not say this, but rather I say that the things which the Gentiles offer, they offer to devils: because in the idol the devil is worshipped, who is worse than the idol.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *To be made partakers of devils. [n: (AUG., lib. II de Doctrin. Christ., c. 20, 21, etc.)]* To this kind belong also consultations and pacts, etc., *as far as* they are called mathematicians, who attempt to foretell the outcomes of actions.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0536D]VERS. 22.-- *Do we provoke the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ They seem to provoke and to envy the Lord, whose kingdom they have diminished, who eat with scandal to the brethren.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *All things are lawful for me.* ⟦s2⟧ By the power of free will and by the teaching of the natural law. ⟦s3⟧ Not that all things are lawful, even unlawful things, but behold, let us suppose that all things are lawful.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Whatsoever is sold in the shambles.* ⟦s2⟧ Because they are lawful, and yet they are not always to be used, he determines how it may be lawful to eat or not to eat.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *For his sake.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he who serves idols will glory in this and be confirmed in his error, and a bad example is given to the brethren.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *For why.* ⟦s2⟧ As if: What need is there [0537A] that I should be thought to eat for the sake of veneration? ⟦s3⟧ For I am judged not to differ from an idolater.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *Whether therefore you eat.* ⟦s2⟧ Not only therefore let thy voice sound the praises of God, but let thy works also agree with thy voice. ⟦s3⟧ For when thou hast sung with the voice, thou wilt sometimes be silent in life. ⟦s4⟧ But sing so that thou never be silent. ⟦s5⟧ For if thou criest out with the mouth and thinkest fraud, thou hast been silent from the praise of God, and, what is more grievous, thou hast gone on into blasphemy. ⟦s6⟧ For when God is praised because of thy good work, thou praisest God: and when God is blasphemed because of thy evil work, thou blasphemest God. ⟦s7⟧ If therefore what thou eatest and drinkest thou takest for the refreshment of the body and the repairing of the members, giving thanks to him who has bestowed on thee these mortal and frail solaces of supports, thy food and thy drink praise [0537B] God. ⟦s8⟧ But if thou exceedest the measure owed to nature by the immoderation of voracity, and gorgest thyself with wine-drinking, however many praises of God thy tongue may sound, thy life blasphemes.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *To the church of God.* ⟦s2⟧ As regards the weak, for whom offences are made, while by their example they cleave to those things which are hostile to God.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *I please all men.* ⟦s2⟧ He who pleases men for the truth's sake, now not he himself, but the truth pleases them. ⟦s3⟧ If a man pleases for his own sake, it is pride; this is what he said elsewhere: *If I pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ [n: (Gal. I)]*. ⟦s4⟧ But did he please his persecutors? ⟦s5⟧ He pleased every kind of men, in that he was gathering the Church of Christ, whether those already placed within, or those to be brought into [0537C] it.
