## CHAPTER XIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For he who speaks with a tongue,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Now this happened when one speaking with a tongue did not understand himself, as if a man knew the utterance of some language and not its signification

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *But now.* ⟦s2⟧ I have said that he who [0544A] prophesies is greater than he who speaks with a tongue. ⟦s3⟧ But in my own person you can see that a tongue profits nothing, whereas prophecy profits. ⟦s4⟧ *But now.* ⟦s5⟧ If I come now to you, when you are believers, *speaking with tongues,* what shall I profit you? as if he should say, nothing, what then did a tongue profit at that time, when you were unbelievers? as if he should say, nothing. ⟦s6⟧ *Or in revelation.* ⟦s7⟧ That I may expound a revelation. ⟦s8⟧ Revelation is when certain mysteries are shown through figures, as in the Apocalypse. ⟦s9⟧ *Or in knowledge.* ⟦s10⟧ That I may expound those things which pertain to knowing. ⟦s11⟧ These are the things which give light to faith, as concerning the nature of the Godhead. ⟦s12⟧ *Or in prophecy.* ⟦s13⟧ That I may expound the Scriptures which treat of things to come.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Or in doctrine.* ⟦s2⟧ That I may expound the Scriptures which form men's manners.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Yet the things which are without life,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By myself I have shown [0544B] that a tongue avails not without an interpreter. ⟦s3⟧ By myself, I say, I have shown it, I who am rational, yet by things without life I can show the same.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For if the trumpet give an uncertain sound.* ⟦s2⟧ Again by another likeness. ⟦s3⟧ *For if the trumpet give an uncertain sound,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Among the Jews there was a use of the trumpet at feasts and in wars, with diverse soundings.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10-- *And without a voice.* ⟦s2⟧ There are many tongues, but they have their own significations of sounds, that they may be understood.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *If then I know not.* ⟦s2⟧ And because others without an interpreter would not understand, I shall be a barbarian.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *So you also.* ⟦s2⟧ Or, under one distinction, thus. ⟦s3⟧ And for this cause, that you be not barbarians, do you also, as I do, seek that you may abound.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *My spirit prays.* ⟦s2⟧ The spirit is the name of that power of the soul which is lower than the mind, where the [0544C] likenesses of bodily things are impressed. ⟦s3⟧ The mind is the other and higher power of the soul, as above. ⟦s4⟧ Pharaoh's spirit was informed, that he might see the images the mind of Joseph was enlightened, that he might understand. ⟦s5⟧ *Without fruit.* ⟦s6⟧ So it is if a man speaks in an unknown tongue, or brings forth the signs of certain things without understanding, as Latin men are wont to sing in Greek, and to be delighted with the sound of the words, not knowing however what they say.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *I will pray with the spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, I approve of so speaking, that the signs of things be formed in the spirit, and the understanding of them shine out in the mind. ⟦s3⟧ Or, *I will pray* with mouth and with mind.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Amen.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that this word *amen,* is neither Greek nor Latin, but Hebrew, and is interpreted *true.* ⟦s3⟧ And though it could have been said, *true,* yet he did not say it, but *amen.* ⟦s4⟧ Neither the Greek nor the Latin [0544D] translator has dared to do this, lest, laid bare, it should grow cheap, but that it might keep its honour by the veil of a secret.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *I give thanks to God.* ⟦s2⟧ He sets himself forth as an example, that they ought not to speak with tongues save unto edification, as he himself who knows them all.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS 20.-- *But in malice be children.* ⟦s2⟧ Be removed from malice, as little ones, giving your zeal to the things that profit, which is the perfection of understanding.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *In the law.* ⟦s2⟧ The reason why one ought not to seek after tongues is that they are for a sign to unbelievers (which these men now are not), and not unto edification, which is now needful for believers. ⟦s3⟧ *In other tongues.* ⟦s4⟧ Not of the sabbath, and the new moons, and circumcision and the like, but the New Testament. ⟦s5⟧ Or, not in revelation, [0545A] of which they are unworthy, but in parables the Gospel is spoken to them. ⟦s6⟧ *And neither so will they hear.* ⟦s7⟧ This he foretold of those whom he foreknew would not believe, to whom, because of their sins, the truth neither lies open nor is pleasing.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Therefore tongues are for a sign.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the words of God in an unknown and foreign tongue are obscure, lest they be seen by the faithless, so that, when they are heard, it may be a sign that it was done because of faithlessness, lest the hearers should understand: this assuredly happens to unbelievers, whose senses are veiled, but to believers prophecy is fitting, and not an unknown tongue.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *If therefore there come together.* ⟦s2⟧ Another reason is given here why they ought not to speak with tongues, as if he should say, because *tongues are for a sign,* not unto edification.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *What then.* ⟦s2⟧ Now he begins to determine [0545B] in what manner tongues are to be used; as if he should say: Since these things come of the speaking of tongues; therefore what is to be done about them? as if he should say: Use tongues unto edification, as the rest of things are to be done. ⟦s3⟧ *Every one,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore let no man excuse himself. ⟦s5⟧ *Has a psalm.* ⟦s6⟧ The praise of God, by a canticle. ⟦s7⟧ *Doctrine.* ⟦s8⟧ A spiritual understanding, by prudence.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Or at the most three.* ⟦s2⟧ Not more than three, lest he who speaks with tongues take up the day, and there be no place for the prophets who discourse upon the Scriptures.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *But let him speak to himself.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks to himself who is pricked in heart by the things he says. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore unto the honour of God, whom he praises or entreats.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Let two prophets speak.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the discourse of so many men suffices, and in the mouth of two or three stands every [0545C] word.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *But if to another.* ⟦s2⟧ It is given to the lesser which is not given to the greater.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *For God is not the God of dissension.* ⟦s2⟧ But he would be a God of dissension if he compelled those whom he fills to speak all at once.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As in all the churches,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Let women keep silence, as I teach in all the churches.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *It is a shame.* ⟦s2⟧ Because this is against ecclesiastical discipline.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *If any seem to be a prophet.* ⟦s2⟧ Which assuredly he is not, if he does not acknowledge that it is so: for he who truly is one acknowledges it so, and he who is ignorant of these things shall be ignored, that is, shall be disapproved. ⟦s3⟧ The little ones who glory in the cross, even though they be ignorant of the things that are discussed most subtly, to glory [0545D] nevertheless attain, because not one of the little ones perishes for whom Christ died. ⟦s4⟧ *Let him acknowledge.* ⟦s5⟧ When he says this, he intimates that the words of the epistles are hard, since he bids them be acknowledged by a prophet or a spiritual man.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *But if any man know not.* ⟦s2⟧ But if any man is ignorant of these things, and will not believe that they are the commandments of God, he shall be ignored, as in the Gospel: *I know you not,* that is, I have not known you to be mine, or among the predestined.
