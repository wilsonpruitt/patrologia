## CHAPTER XV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *By which also you are saved.* ⟦s2⟧ If you hold fast that Gospel, that is, the resurrection of the dead, on the ground on which I confirmed it to you, that is, by the resurrection of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ *Unless you have believed in vain.* ⟦s4⟧ Which is, if [0546A] you do not hold the resurrection of the dead. ⟦s5⟧ That faith is empty which is not received under the hope of resurrection.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *He rose again the third day.* ⟦s2⟧ And this I said according to the Scriptures. ⟦s3+4⟧ For Osee says: *After two days he will quicken us, and on the third day we shall rise again in his sight [n: (Ose. VI)]*. [var: Vulg. Ose. VI, 3 *Vivificabit nos post duos dies ; in die tertia suscitabit nos, et vivemus in conspectu ejus*, "he will raise us up, and we shall live"] If he who was dead and buried rose again, doubt not that the dead and the buried shall rise again.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He was seen by Cephas.* ⟦s2⟧ Before the other men to whom he is read to have appeared in the Gospel, otherwise it would be contrary to that which is read, that he appeared first to the women.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8, 9.-- *As one born out of due time.* ⟦s2⟧ He is called one born out of due time because he was born outside the lawful season, that is, before he ought, or because, born again later and more tardily, he received the apostolate when Christ had already been taken up. ⟦s3⟧ Or, like one born out of due time, because I am the least, whether in time and calling, not in dignity, [0546B] in labour, or in preaching. ⟦s4⟧ This he said truly, for humility's sake, and he proves it, saying: *I am not worthy.* ⟦s5⟧ Why then an Apostle? ⟦s6⟧ *But by the grace of God* At the first, grace alone, since no merits went before save evil ones. ⟦s7⟧ But afterwards, through grace, merits begin.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *More abundantly.* ⟦s2⟧ This was the more fulfilled, because he lived by the work of his hands, and did no less for the Gospel. ⟦s3⟧ And lest the will without the grace of God should be thought able to do anything, he adds: *Not I, but the grace,* etc

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And so you believed.* ⟦s2⟧ He reproves them: because, though this faith was manifest among all the churches, they nevertheless had fallen away from it.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *But if Christ.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus far he has shown that Christ rose again. ⟦s3⟧ Now by his resurrection he proves the resurrection of the dead. ⟦s4⟧ The [0546C] false apostles were denying that Christ had truly suffered, or been buried, or risen again, or come in the flesh.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *But we are found.* ⟦s2⟧ These things and others are brought in for this cause, that the Corinthians may blush who follow the error of the false apostles, an error on which these absurdities follow, which he himself also condemns. ⟦s3+4⟧ Therefore, lest they remain in this error, he says: *I have found,* etc. [cj: *Inveni*; read *Invenimur*, "we are found"] *Against God.* ⟦s5⟧ Not by a lesser, but perhaps by a greater wickedness is falsehood praised in God than truth is blamed.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *And if Christ be not,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If Christ has not risen again; Christ is held in death. ⟦s3⟧ And if in death, then also in sin, which is the cause of death And if he is held in sin, he could not remit your sins. ⟦s4⟧ And so you are still in your sins, which you believed had been forgiven you, and so your faith is vain.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0546D]VERS. 21.-- *For by a man indeed.* ⟦s2⟧ Behold the first-fruits, man and man, a man unto life, a man unto death, but that one is nothing other than man, this one is God and man.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *And as.* ⟦s2⟧ By *as,* a likeness and a cause are noted, because as from the corruption of Adam unto death, so from the Spirit of Christ unto life.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Every one.* ⟦s2⟧ He sets forth the orders, as of a thing certain: and the times, when it was done, and when it shall be, that the dead may rise again. ⟦s3⟧ *Then they who are.* ⟦s4⟧ Another reading: *Then these who are Christ's, at his coming,* namely, shall rise again. ⟦s5⟧ And so those who believe in Christ, and are less worthy than he, shall rise later in time, and lesser in dignity. ⟦s6⟧ And who they are that are Christ's, he sets forth by adding: *Who have believed at his coming,* etc.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0547A]VERS. 24.-- *Then the end.* ⟦s2⟧ This he sets down for the commendation of the resurrection, which being fulfilled there will be the end of the world and the consummation of all things. ⟦s3⟧ *When he shall have brought to nought.* ⟦s4⟧ While the world lasts, angels are set over angels, demons over demons, men over men: unto the profit of the living, or unto their deception. ⟦s5⟧ But when men have been gathered in, among angels and men every prelacy will cease, there will not be that dissension between those who preside and those who are subject. ⟦s6⟧ But then it will be known to all that none of these things did any earthly or heavenly beings possess of themselves, but from him from whom are all things.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *But he must reign.* ⟦s2⟧ The sense is: His kingdom must meanwhile be made manifest, until all his enemies confess that he reigns: by saying *until* he excludes a greater manifestation, not a longer [0547B] continuance of the kingdom. ⟦s3⟧ Whence elsewhere: *Our eyes are unto the Lord our God, until he have mercy,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Not that they are then turned away, but that they seek nothing further.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *But the last.* ⟦s2⟧ Among other things it is plain that Christ has also the power of raising up, when he adds: *But last of all the enemy death shall be destroyed.* ⟦s3⟧ Now Christ reigns, and then he shall reign: but last of all, that is, after all things, death shall be destroyed, because men shall no more be dissolved, as the heretics judge. ⟦s4⟧ *Last of all.* ⟦s5⟧ Because there will not be anything which it may destroy; after this mortal shall have put on immortality. ⟦s6⟧ *The enemy.* ⟦s7⟧ To whom we are enemies: or it to us. ⟦s8⟧ Again a commendation of the resurrection.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Except him who has subjected all things to him.* ⟦s2⟧ Who, as he is from none, so by no reason can he [0547C] be subject to any: for he himself is the beginning of all things.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That God may be all.* ⟦s2⟧ Because, when every creature shall say that Christ is its head, and that the head of Christ is God, not only in all, that is, in the confession of all, will there be one God, but every creature also will confess that he is the one from whom are all things.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *That God may be.* ⟦s2⟧ He himself is the end which above, so often concluding, he has set down: afterwards he explains piece by piece what the consummation to come will be. ⟦s3⟧ *All things.* ⟦s4⟧ Which can be desired. ⟦s5⟧ *In all,* in his own members: because the reward of virtue will be he himself who gave the virtue, because God will be that whence they are satisfied.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *For the dead.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, for the blotting out of sins. ⟦s3⟧ Or, for making themselves dead after the likeness of the death of Christ, what shall they do, seeing they are not to have life? [0547D] *If at all,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ So that not even Christ has risen? to what end are they baptized, since sins are not remitted, if Christ has not risen again?

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *On account of your glory.* ⟦s2⟧ Or: *By your glory.* ⟦s3⟧ Behold, the Apostle swears, that we may know that to swear what is true is not a sin, but not for that reason is one to swear in doubtful matters; and it is safer not to swear than, by the habit of swearing, to forswear oneself. ⟦s4⟧ But to swear what is false is a most grievous sin. ⟦s5⟧ An oath is not against the precept: an oath which is not from the evil of him who swears, but from that of the unbeliever or the weak man, who otherwise does not believe.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *If according to man.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, acting reasonably, because it belongs to a man to believe, and not that a man should die like a beast, *I fought,* that is, I disputed. ⟦s3⟧ *Let us eat [0548A] and drink, for tomorrow we shall die.* ⟦s4⟧ This is on account of those who, as though nothing were to be after death, give their zeal to the belly only, like cattle, saying: Who has come thence? ⟦s5⟧ I have not heard the voice of any one coming thence. ⟦s6⟧ To whom it is said: Fool, if thy father should rise again, wouldst thou believe? ⟦s7⟧ The Lord of all has risen again, and thou dost not believe. ⟦s8⟧ He who willed to die and to rise again, that we all might believe one, lest we be deceived by many. ⟦s9⟧ Thou wouldst believe a father who will die again, and thou dost not believe one who is now immortal, who moreover has testimony in heaven, testimony on earth, testimony from the angels, testimony from those below.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Be not seduced,* etc., by the false apostles; who out of medicine seek a wound, and out of the Scriptures strive to twist a cord, whence they may cast the noose of death. [0548B] *They corrupt good manners,* etc. [n: (THEODOR.)] Here he has called good those light ones who are easily deceived.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Awake.* ⟦s2⟧ This do not, but *awake* from the body; and so you will be *just;* and afterwards: *Do not sin* by consenting to them, because they are ignorant of God.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *But some man will say.* ⟦s2⟧ Hitherto he has proved the resurrection of the dead by reasons, now he shows by the very nature of things that it can come to pass; as if he should say, by these things it is proved: but nevertheless someone depraved might speak thus, as though the resurrection of the dead could not come to pass by nature: *And if they shall rise: or with what body,* [var: Vulg. I Cor. XV, 35 *Quomodo resurgunt mortui* where Migne has *Et si resurgent*] as if he should say: It will not be able to be other than it now is, that is, passible and mortal. ⟦s3⟧ The Apostle answers: *Foolish man,* that is, thou who dost not attend to what thou daily seest in the grain.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0548C]VERS. 36.-- *Foolish man, thou: that which thou sowest.* ⟦s2⟧ So the dead man will be able to live, and in a better body: as that which thou sowest rises better.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *But God gives.* ⟦s2⟧ As therefore the bare grain is sown, and by the nod of God rises again in some manner clothed, having many increases with it, so the dead man by the power of God will be able to live, and to rise again in a better body. ⟦s3⟧ *To every one of the seeds.* ⟦s4⟧ So also for us he will refashion our ancient bodies, as Job says: *Whom I myself shall see, and not another.* ⟦s5⟧ But there will be a difference in glory and in dignity, although they be of the same nature. ⟦s6⟧ As not all flesh is of the same dignity, though of the same elements, and this is what he says:

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0548D]VERS. 39.-- *And the same flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ All flesh is body, but not conversely: as wood.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *Heavenly.* ⟦s2⟧ The bodies of those who rise again are heavenly; earthly before they die, because they are from Adam. ⟦s3⟧ And because Christ is heavenly, from him bodies are called heavenly, and are no longer called flesh. ⟦s4⟧ But from Adam, because he is earthly, bodies are named earthly.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *For star differs from star,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *In my Father's house there are many mansions,* even though the penny be the same, that is, eternal life for all. ⟦s3⟧ For the blessedness which the just receive is one; but the quality of the reward is unequal. ⟦s4⟧ And although those who rise again are of unequal brightness, yet God is all in all, [0549A] because God is charity, and it will come to pass through charity; that what each one has may be in common the possession of all.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *It is sown.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, although a man, from the time he is conceived until his dissolution, is in corruption, and afterwards swarms with worms, yet he will rise in incorruption.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 44.-- *A natural body.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, one which has nothing from the soul except that it is made to feel through it, as the beasts have. ⟦s3⟧ *It shall rise spiritual.* ⟦s4⟧ Passing over into the nature of spirit, that is, having certain spiritual properties, because it is nimble, light, and in no need of food. ⟦s5⟧ When he said that the body would pass over to incorruption, he makes mention of corruption, that the greater dignity of the resurrection may be shown. ⟦s6⟧ *If there is a body.* ⟦s7⟧ Truly a spiritual body will rise, because *if* it now is, *it is natural,* that is, it is established [0549B] to be so; and at some time *it will be spiritual.* ⟦s8⟧ For as we are natural because our carnal father was made into a living soul: so we shall be spiritual in the resurrection: because our spiritual Father was made, in his resurrection, into a quickening spirit.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 45.-- *Man.* ⟦s2⟧ Those two men, the first and the second, are in a manner the whole human race: born of the one, born again of the other. ⟦s3⟧ Christ is called Adam, because he is of the same matter; the last, because after him no other man succeeds who might be the head or the author of the human body.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But not.* ⟦s2⟧ He has said that our bodies will be spiritual; but lest any should doubt whether the natural can become spiritual, he proves it by the likeness of Christ; as if he should say, [0549C] he was made into a quickening spirit. ⟦s3⟧ *But not first* was there in him that *which is spiritual, but that which is natural, then the spiritual.* ⟦s4⟧ So also can it come to pass concerning us.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *The first man.* ⟦s2⟧ He is truly the first unto a living soul; because he was formed from the dust of the earth, natural and passible by nature. ⟦s3⟧ *The second man,* because *from heaven,* that is, because the divine nature was united to the human, therefore *heavenly,* that is, spiritual in the resurrection; and because the first father was earthly, such are all men. ⟦s4⟧ *Heavenly.* ⟦s5⟧ Christ is said to be so, because he was conceived and born not after the manner of men, but by the divine nod.
