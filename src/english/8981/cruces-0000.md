## 1. Conventions taken in this range

- `VERS. n.--` passes verbatim; the lemma is Englished; a fragmentary lemma is rendered as the
  phrase it stands for (brief convention 3). Expanded fragments in this range:
  *Paulus vocatus.* → "Paul, called"; *quia non multi.* → "for there are not many wise";
  *Ut non glorietur.* → "That no flesh may glory"; *Non enim judicavi.* → "For I judged not
  myself to know anything"; *Fundamentum enim.* → "For other foundation no man can lay";
  *Sic nos.* → "So let a man account of us" (both occurrences, identically);
  *Ab humano.* / *Humano.* → "By man's day" / "Man's day" (same fragment twice — expanded
  both, per the ⚑ ruling that the stump is expanded, not its twin contracted);
  *Saecularia igitur judicia.* → "If therefore you have secular judgments".
- **`*usque ad*` → `*as far as*`**, following the settled sibling practice in this volume
  (8986 ×7, 8987 ×3, 8990 ×1, 8993 ×9, 8995 ×6, 8998 ×19). All five occurrences in this range
  (0523A, 0523B, 0523C, 0524B, 0524D, 0527C, 0528A) are the abridgment formula — checked one
  by one against what stands on either side (addendum D). None is a lemma's own words: there is
  no `usque ad` inside any italic span in this range.
- **`pseudo`** (indeclinable, 0524D "de gloria pseudo", 0525C "de magisterio pseudo", 0528B
  "sequentes pseudo") is rendered **"the false apostles"** throughout, matching the work's own
  spelled-out *pseudoapostolorum* at 0525D. Not italicized: it is an abbreviation of a phrase
  the work itself writes out, not a technical term with no English equivalent.
- **`baptistae`** → "baptizers" throughout (0519D, 0524B, 0525C), not "Baptists".
- **Pattern 17.** The gloss's own voice is plural "you" for the Corinthians. "thou/thee" is used
  only where the Latin is grammatically singular: 0522A (*te facis*, *tu facis*, *tu non
  faceres*), 0525C–0525D (*te discernit*, *baptizatus es*, *gloriaris*, *accepisti*,
  *acceperis*), 0529B (*parcis tibi*, *te sibi fecit*, *evertis*, *peccas*). Two "ye" occur in
  this range: **none** — no archaic plural was used anywhere.
- Question-mark parity: 0000 = 1, 0001 = 0, 0002 = 1, 0003 = 5, 0004 = 0, matching the Latin
  twin band by band. No 8b recast was needed: every `?` in this range closes a **direct**
  question in Latin already.
- Punctuation was compared band by band (test 3), colons, semicolons, question marks and
  guillemets, and every band now matches its Latin twin exactly. **Two marks had been destroyed
  and were restored**: a colon at 0524C (*sanctos homines et angelos accipe:*) and a semicolon
  at 0527A (*diabolo eum vexandum traderet;*). Twenty supplied semicolons/colons — all of them
  Migne's commas quietly promoted — were reverted to commas.

## 2. VULGATE COLLATION — every lemma in 0519C–0529B

Comparand: `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, I Cor 1–7 read in full, plus Lc 8, Mt 13,
Mt 23, Mt 25, Mc 4, Sir 27, Jo 8.

### 2a. Divergences MARKED `[var:]` (4)

1. **0522B, chunk 0001, gloss on I Cor 2:12.** Migne prints *Qui habet, dabitur ei … Qui ergo
   non habet, et quod habet, auferetur ab eo*, and attributes it `[n: (Luc. VIII)]`.
   **Lc 8:18 reads** *Qui enim habet, dabitur **illi** : et quicumque non habet, **etiam quod
   putat se habere**, auferetur ab **illo**.* Migne's wording is instead **Mt 13:12** almost
   verbatim (*Qui enim habet, dabitur ei … qui autem non habet, et quod habet auferetur ab eo*).
   Material, because the gloss's whole point is *plene habere est scire unde habeat* — Luke's
   *quod putat se habere* is precisely the clause that would have carried it, and it is absent.
   ⚠ **The `[n: (Luc. VIII)]` was passed through verbatim and NOT corrected** (runbook false
   positive 2: an anaphora-bearing citation is never repaired in place). The mismatch is
   recorded here and in the marker, not in the note.
2. **0522D, chunk 0001, lemma of I Cor 2:15.** Migne prints *Spiritualis autem **homo**.*
   **I Cor 2:15 reads** *Spiritualis autem judicat omnia* — with no *homo*. Material: *homo* is
   the word the gloss then expounds (*Est vel vita vel scientia*, and the whole distinction of
   the spiritual man in life from the spiritual man in knowledge), and it builds the parallel
   with *Animalis autem homo* of 2:14, which is the previous lemma and is the Vulgate's own
   wording there. The added word is doing the work of the paragraph.
3. **0523B, chunk 0002, Augustine's proof-text.** Migne: *Vasa figuli probat fornax, et homines
   tentatio tribulationis **probat*** `[n: (Eccli. XXVII)]`. **Sir 27:6 reads** *Vasa figuli
   probat fornax, et homines **justos** tentatio tribulationis* — with *justos* and with **no
   second verb**. Material: the Vulgate restricts the trial to *the just*; Migne's line, adduced
   to prove that the fire tries every man's work, does not.
4. **0526C, chunk 0003, gloss on I Cor 4:15.** Migne: *Ne vobis dicatis Patrem in terra, unus
   est enim Pater Deus* `[n: (Matth. XXIII)]`. **Mt 23:9 reads** *Et patrem nolite vocare vobis
   super terram : unus est enim pater **vester qui in caelis est**.* Material: Migne's *Pater
   Deus* is the identification the gloss then argues from (*ne gratia Dei … sanctitati cujusquam
   hominis tribuatur*), and the Vulgate does not print it.

## 3. THE UNEXPRESSED SUBJECT — every supplied "he/she/it/they"

Sites where English had to name a subject the Latin leaves unexpressed, with what decides it.
Where two readings construe, both are stated.

1. **0519C, *Frater*.** *Per hoc removet, quod non malo animo ei notificaverit.* Rendered "By
   this he removes it, that **he** had made it known to **him**…". The subject of *removet* is
   **Paul** (he is the one writing); the subject of *notificaverit* is **Sosthenes** (the
   *frater* just named, and the man *qui inter eos versabatur* in the preceding lemma); *ei* is
   **Paul**. ⚠ **A second reading construes**: *notificaverit* = Paul, *ei* = an unnamed
   recipient, i.e. Paul removing the suspicion that *he* reported the Corinthians' faults out of
   ill will. The rendering leaves both open by keeping "he … him" and adds nothing.
2. **0520C, *Dei virtus*.** *Dum occisus diabolum vicit, et hominem liberavit… vel virtutem dat
   credentibus.* Supplied "he" = **Christ**, from *crux, id est mors Christi* eight lines above
   and from *Dei virtus* itself (I Cor 1:18, *Christum … Dei virtutem*, 1:24). Not in doubt.
3. **0521A, VERS. 19.** *Ideo non misit me in sapientia, quia praedixerat quod eam non
   reciperet…* Supplied "he" = **God** (the *Scriptum est enim* of Isa 29:14 quoted at I Cor
   1:19 is God's own speech, and *misit* answers *Non enim misit me Christus* of 1:17). ⚠ The
   sender at 1:17 is **Christ**, so "he" could be Christ throughout. Both construe; the English
   says only "he", which is what the Latin says.
4. **0521B–C, VERS. 27.** *Qui etsi primum elegerit pauperes … non tamen relinquit* — "he" =
   **God**, from *Deus humilitatem proponat* and *Venit enim humilis Deus* in the same sentence
   chain. Not in doubt.
5. **0524A–B, the two AUG. notes on *Templum*.** *Habitat itaque in singulis Deus* names its own
   subject; the following *Capitur autem habitans ab aliis amplius* and *in eis occulte agit*
   inherit **God**. Not in doubt.
6. **0524D, VERS. 23.** *Ut sicut haec nobis, ita nos Christo subjiciamur* — the first clause has
   no verb; "are subject" is carried over from *subjiciamur*, within the same sentence. Flagged
   as an ellipsis resolution, not a supplied subject.
7. **0523A, VERS. 8.** *tamen Deus magis per hunc quam per illum* — no verb; "gives" carried
   over from *dant* in the same sentence. Same class as 6.
8. **0523D.** *Qui autem aurum, argentum, lapides pretiosos de utroque igne securi sunt* — no
   verb in the relative clause; "have built" carried over from *superaedificaverunt* in the
   immediately preceding clause. Same class as 6.
9. **0527B.** *vel tradit, ut Spiritus sanctus … salvus sit eis* — subject of *tradit* is
   **Paul** (the *Apostolus* of *Sic Apostolus … Satanae tradit* four lines above); the object
   supplied as "him" is the fornicator of I Cor 5:5. Both from the lemma above, not the sentence.
10. **0527D.** *tollerentur potius, ne perverse malos evitando quisque … mittat ad gehennam* —
    subject of *tollerentur* is **mali** (from *auferendos esse malos*). Not in doubt.

## 4. PLATE REQUESTS — column, printed reading, why, and what would settle it

**No `[sic:]` was fired on any of these.** Each is raised here as a plate candidate and nothing
more. The reasoning is the volume's own: PL 114's marker sweep returned 65% false, and both
recent works in this volume that refused letter-level `[sic:]` came back 36/37 and 14/14. Three
of the six below are **non-words**, which addendum A says are worth raising and have been right
every time; three are **real words**, which addendum A says are Migne's until a plate says
otherwise, and they are rendered as printed.

### Non-words (hapax in the whole corpus — a `grep -r` over `src/latin/` returns one hit each, this work's)

1. **0519D · `ahos`** (VERS. 10, *invitat ahos ad horum similitudinem*). Not a Latin word.
   Certainly *alios*: the sentence contrasts *hos* (the praised) with the rest, and *horum*
   stands three words later. **Englished for its evident sense ("the others") and left unmarked.**
   Settled by: reading p.265 col. 0519D. If Migne prints *alios*, this is our transcription's
   l→h; if he prints *ahos*, it is his type.
2. **0520C · `devidebant`** (*per hominum se nomina devidebant*). Not a Latin word; *dividebant*
   is certain (*divisus est* is the lemma being glossed, twice, on the same column). **Englished
   as "were dividing" and left unmarked.** Same plate request, col. 0520C.
3. **0519C · `suffraganeiis`** (VERS. 2, *omnibus scribit suffraganeiis Corinthiis*). Not a
   Latin form; *suffraganeis* is certain. **Englished as "suffragan" and left unmarked.**
   ⚠ All three of these are on **p.265**, the one page in this range with a plate read on
   record, and that read was a checked zero. **It is worth knowing whether these three forms
   were within that read's scope** — if they were, all three are Migne's and the English above
   is a silent repair that should be reverted to carried type.
   ⚠ Note also that p.265's right column **clips the right margin in our scan** (measured on
   `per ipsum Ch`, `praedic`, `hominibu`, `conscient`, `diliger`). **No truncated word occurs in
   our chunk 0000** — I looked for the class and found none; the clipping falls in the Romans
   work that shares the page, whose text ends mid-page at 0519C.

### Real words that read oddly — rendered as printed, no marker (addendum A)

4. **0524A · `voluerit`** for expected `violaverit` (*sed si quis voluerit templum quod vos
   estis, disperdet illum*). I Cor 3:17 reads *si quis autem templum Dei **violaverit**,
   disperdet illum Deus*, and the gloss is expounding that verse word for word: with *voluerit*
   the clause has no sense at all ("if any man shall have wished the temple…"). **This is the
   strongest candidate in the range.** It is a real Latin form, so under addendum A it is
   Migne's until the plate says otherwise, and I declined the `[cj:]` as well as the `[sic:]`:
   an `[cj:]` asserts that Migne printed *voluerit*, which is exactly what has not been checked,
   and the loss of `-ia-` is precisely the shape our digitization produces. **Rendered
   literally.** Settled by: col. 0524A. ⛔ If Migne prints *violaverit*, this needs a tei-patch,
   not a marker, and the English sentence must be re-rendered ("shall have violated").
5. **0528C · `suo potestate judicis`** (*nolo redigi suo potestate judicis*). *suo* cannot agree
   with *potestate*. I Cor 6:12, the verse being glossed, reads *ego **sub** nullis redigar
   potestate* — so *sub* is what the construction wants, and *suo*/*sub* is a plausible
   misreading. Rendered "under a judge's own power", carrying *suo* as "own" rather than
   dropping it. Settled by: col. 0528C.
6. **0528D · `sanctificatus … Spiritus`** (*Non potest non esse sanctum corpus, quo
   sanctificatus utitur Spiritus*). *sanctificatus* agrees with *Spiritus*, which yields "the
   sanctified Spirit" — theologically impossible in a gloss arguing that the Spirit is God
   (0529A: *si enim non esset Deus, templum utique nosipsos non haberet*). *sanctificator*
   or *sanctificans* would give the required sense. **Rendered as printed** ("which the
   sanctified Spirit uses"), because it is a real, well-formed word. Settled by: col. 0528D.

### Punctuation / structure candidates

7. **0522D · the full stop in `sed spiritui. Dei subjectus certissime ac fideliter judicat`.**
   The stop splits what wants to be *sed spiritui Dei subjectus … judicat* ("but, subject to the
   Spirit of God, judges most certainly and faithfully"). **Pattern 8 obeyed: the stop is
   preserved and the English is broken in the same place** ("…but to the spirit. Subject to
   God, he judges…"). Settled by: col. 0522D, whether the plate prints the point.
8. **0524A–B · `Habitare autem ideo in animalibus hominibus et parvulis … quia in eis occulte
   agit`.** An infinitive with no governing verb (*dicitur*? *est*?). No verb was supplied; the
   infinitive is rendered as a verbal noun ("And his dwelling in sensual men … is for this
   reason, that…"), which adds only a copula that the Latin ellipsis licenses. Settled by:
   col. 0524B — a word may have been lost.

### ⭐ Suspiciously TIDY forms — the §4 normalization class, recorded as negatives

Per brief §4 and addendum C, a form that reads *too* well in a rough passage is the only handle
on the digitization's silent corrections. I read whole lines rather than target words throughout.
Nothing in this range struck me as tidied. Two forms I examined and **cleared**:
`querebantur` (0522D, for *quaerebantur*) and `tollerentur` (0527D, for *tolerentur*) are both
attested medieval orthography (Pattern 9), not tidying and not defects — no marker, no crux
beyond this line. **Recording the negative because the class is only ever found this way.**

## 5. Candidates RAISED AND REJECTED, with reasons

- **`personaliter legitur: examinatur` (0522C).** The sense the passage seems to want is
  *impersonaliter* — that *examinatur* is read as an impersonal. ⛔ **Rejected as a conjecture
  and rendered "it is read personally".** Migne prints a real word; the reading is arguable
  either way (the *examinatur* of I Cor 2:14 does have a personal subject in the second
  exposition Migne then gives, *quod animalis est*); and nothing licenses reversing a word to
  its opposite under Pattern 7a. Logged, not repaired.
- **`veritatis` for `veritas` (0527C).** *ut sinceritas mundam vitam faciat, et veritatis omnem
  fraudem excludat* — the pairing with I Cor 5:8's *sinceritatis et veritatis* makes *veritas*
  as a second subject very attractive. ⛔ **Rejected**: the genitive construes as printed
  (*omnem fraudem veritatis*, "every deceit of truth", with *sinceritas* still the subject), so
  there is no defect to mark and no need for a conjecture. Rendered on the genitive reading.
  ⚠ The rival — *veritas* as subject, "and that truth may exclude every deceit" — is named here
  and **lost because it requires changing a printed letter, while the printed text construes.**
- **`potentia rationalis` (0522B), nominative where the ablative *potentia rationali* is
  expected after *pro*.** ⛔ Rejected: a case mismatch with no English exponent (Pattern 9).
  Rendered "for the rational power of the soul itself"; no marker, no repair.
- **`Deo … judicat` (0522C)**, where *de Deo* is expected. ⛔ Rejected as a marker candidate for
  the same reason: rendered as printed ("who judges God according to the phantasy of bodies"),
  logged here. A plate read at 0522C would settle whether the *de* is Migne's or ours; it is a
  two-letter loss, so it belongs to the same class as items 4–5 above and takes no marker.
- **Column-band gaps.** 0519C → 0519D → **0520B** (no 0520A), and 0522D → 0523A across the
  chunk boundary. ⛔ Not reported as defects: runbook false positive 1. No text in this range is
  broken mid-word or mid-construction across an anchor. I checked every anchor in the range
  against the word it stands beside in the Latin (test 2b); three sit mid-construction and were
  placed mid-construction in the English on purpose: `privilegio [0519C] nominis`,
  `necessitate [0520D] instante`, `hospitium [0527B] non praebuerunt`.
- **Migne's verse ordering.** VERS. 5 is glossed at 0519D and VERS. 4 is not glossed at all;
  I Cor 4:8's *sine nobis* is glossed under **VERS. 9**, not VERS. 8. ⛔ Not reordered and not
  reported as an error — this is his arrangement (brief: "Migne's own printing is not yours to
  tidy"). Recorded so a blind reader does not raise it.
- **`Migne's own ( sic )`** — none occurs in this range. Checked.

### Negation audit (test 2 / 2a), run on the trigger population

Every printed negative was matched to the single Latin word it attaches to. The sites where a
negative sits on a **verb with a quantifier, correlative or predicate nearby** — 2a's exact
trigger — are these, and each was resolved from the sentence's own structure:

- **0519C** *non malo animo … sed ex charitate*: host is **malo animo**, not *notificaverit*.
  The *sed* clause (*ex charitate*) is a phrase, so the negative is on the phrase.
- **0520B** *si in nomine majorum non est gloriandum, **nec** in nomine illorum*: host of the
  second negative is **in nomine illorum**, carried by the parallel.
- **0520C** *ita **nec illius**, sed Christi esse cognoscitur*: host is **illius**, not
  *cognoscitur* — the point is whose the baptism is, not that it is unknown.
- **0521B** *non tamen relinquit sapientes, divites, nobiles*: host **is** the verb; the
  correlative is *etsi primum elegerit … non tamen relinquit*, so the scope is the verb.
- **0524B** *nec ubique habitans, sed in templo suo*: ⭐ host is **ubique**, not *habitans* —
  God is everywhere present but does not dwell **everywhere**. The English reads "yet not
  dwelling everywhere, but in his temple", with the negative on the quantifier. (Rendering it
  "not dwelling, but in his temple" would be the 8990 @0614B defect exactly.)
- **0526D** *Non solum ergo illum reum mortis, sed etiam inhaerentes illi, **non esse immunes***:
  the second negative is on **esse immunes** inside the *sed etiam* limb, not on *demonstravit*.
  Getting this wrong makes Paul deny the accessories' guilt.
- **0527D** *ostendit satis **non temere, et quolibet modo, sed per judicium** auferendos esse*:
  host is the adverbial pair *temere, et quolibet modo*, shown by *sed per judicium*.
- **0528B** *nequaquam … funditus deserant, sed vel aliis … committant*: host is the verb, and
  the *sed* limb confirms it.
- **0529A** *Non potest non esse sanctum corpus*: two negatives, both carried
  ("It cannot be that a body is not holy"). Neither was collapsed into an affirmative.
- **0526B** *nec illa elevant, nec ista frangunt*: both carried, each on its own verb.
- **0521B** *nec usus est eis* · **0525C** *Nihil ille habet a baptista, nihil tu* · **0526C**
  *non ut hoc nomen caeteris tollatur, sed ne … tribuatur*: all carried, hosts named.
