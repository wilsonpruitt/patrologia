## THE ARGUMENT.

[0615B]

The Thessalonians are Macedonians. These, having received the word of truth in Christ Jesus, persevered in the faith, [0615C] even in the persecution of their own fellow citizens. Moreover, they neither received false apostles, nor the things which were said by the false apostles. These the Apostle commends, writing to them from Athens, by Tichicus the deacon and Onesimus the acolyte.

## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *Silvanus and Timothy.* The salutation is prefixed with their names, but the sense and the words of this epistle are the Apostle's alone.

VERS. 2.-- *Grace be to you and peace.* After his custom he sets the salutation first, and this being set first, he gives thanks for their good things, calling to mind not only their faith and works, but also the manner of their conversion and their endurance of evils.

[0615D]VERS. 3.-- *Before God.* Thence [you are] so certain as though you already saw; and thus he encourages them.

VERS. 5.-- *In the Holy Spirit.* Because through me you received the gifts of the Spirit, not sparingly, but in fullness. *What manner of men we were,* etc. By this also, that we have suffered for an example to you, it is plain that God has loved and chosen you.

## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- *That it was not.* This you know concerning it, that it was not in worldly prosperity, which is vain, but in much solicitude on account of adversities: and therefore we had confidence not in ourselves, who are frail, but in the Lord.

[0616B]VERS. 2.-- *At Philippi.* The affliction which he here calls to mind he suffered because of the pythonic spirit which he had cast out of the girl.

[0616C]VERS. 3.-- *Our exhortation.* Here he seems to begin against the false apostles, calling to mind the truth of the Gospel, then his prudent conversation among us. *In guile.* Even truth is in guile when it is preached not for the love of truth and the profit of the hearers, but that glory or some other gain may be acquired.

VERS. 4.-- *Not as pleasing men.* Unless he himself work this in us, we cannot avoid it. For what strength to hurt the love of human glory has, he perceives not, save he who has declared war upon it: because, if it is easy for anyone not to covet praise while it is denied, it is nevertheless difficult not to be delighted with it when it is offered.

VERS. 5.-- *Neither.* In this he touches the false apostles, who wished themselves rather than the doctrine of God to be commended. But the Apostle, who sought glory not for the present, [0615D] but in the future, made himself lowly, that the preaching of God might be exalted. *In an occasion.* I say not, in covetousness, but neither did I do or say anything in which there might be an occasion of covetousness. Thence it is. *God is witness,* who knows the heart: for this is not so evident to men.

VERS. 7.-- *We might have been to you,* etc. So far does he weigh down the cause of the false apostles that he says he refuses it, though it was lawful for him to seek supports for the restraining of those who had neither the means nor the shame of demanding. But the debt of apostolic power he calls a *burden,* because of the false apostles, who, usurping it unduly, exacted it importunately of the common people. *Her own children.* A woman sometimes nurses others' children for hire, not out of love: but her own, out of affection.

[0617A]VERS. 12.-- *He called,* with the calling which is according to purpose.

VERS. 13.-- *Therefore.* Because he called you, we give thanks. *He called,* I say, which appears from this, that you quickly and firmly received the word etc. *The hearing of God.* That is, that which we have heard from God, or in which God is heard. *As it is the word of God.* [var: Vulg. *sicut est vere, verbum Dei*, "as it is in truth, the word of God"] For with so great devotion did they receive the word, that they proved themselves to have understood that it was the doctrine of God.

VERS. 14.-- *For you became imitators,* etc. Many things he has said against the false ones: now, lest they be moved on account of his own or the apostles' tribulation, he exhorts them.

VERS. 16.-- *It came.* Therefore they always fill up their sins, because the wrath of God has come, that they may be blinded. *Even unto the end.* Because at the end of the world not a few of the Jews will be converted.

[0617B] *In mouth, in sight,* etc. [var: Vulg. *aspectu, non corde*, "in sight, not in heart"; the Vulgate has no *ore*] The mouth and the sight were ceasing, because the Apostle could not address them face to face; but the heart and the solicitude rests not.

VERS. 18.-- *But he hindered.* That God permitted him to be hindered is for the greater crown of these men, because they stood firm in his absence.

VERS. 19.-- *What is,* etc. [n: (CHRYS.)] Of how great ardour is this? Never could a father and a mother, if they had come together at once and had mingled their longing, have been able to show their longing answering on equal terms to Paul's. I exult more, he says, on your account than on account of the crown. Consider therefore how great a thing it is that a Church should be present entire which was planted by Paul, and has driven roots.

[0617C] *Or a crown of glory.* If glory now, what before the true judge?

## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 2.-- *To confirm you.* For this he himself had desired to come.

VERS. 6, 7.-- *As we also you: therefore.* Because he announced these things, not because of his presence, we were consoled.

VERS. 9.-- *For what thanksgiving,* etc. For there is nothing so worthy as could suffice for the recompense of the salvation obtained for the Gentiles.

VERS. 13.-- *Without complaint.* That you complain not of evils, or that you be so innocent that no one can complain of you; and that you may also be. *In holiness.* That [0617D] is, existing thereby before God in the consummation of the virtues, not for the sight of men.

## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- *For the rest.* Thus far he has urged patience upon the perfect in his own or in their tribulation; now he urges cleanness upon the lesser, exhorting them to continence. *As.* By hearing and by understanding *from us,* that is, from me and Silvanus. *How you ought to walk,* etc., *so also you may walk,* by conducting yourselves rightly. *And to please God.* He pleases God who adorns the right faith which he has by the execution of the virtues and of perfect working. *So also you may walk.* The order is: we beseech that you walk as you have received. [0618A] *That you may abound the more.* This is not changed in the ordering of the letter.

VERS. 2.-- *For you know what precepts. [n: (HIER.)]* Sanctification calls the Christian back from all incontinence, for whom, being incontinent, it is not expedient to touch the holy body of Christ, especially since Moses, wishing to sanctify the Jews, commanded the whole people to contain themselves from every woman, that they might deserve to obtain the presence of God.

VERS. 4.-- *His own vessel.* That is, his wife, let him possess her by ruling her, and this, *In sanctification,* by abstaining from intercourse on the feasts, *In honour.* By not basely abusing her.

VERS. 8.-- *Who also gave,* etc. For the performing of these things he gave you the Spirit; that you despise not, therefore, so great a helper, [0618B] who dwells not in a body subject to sins. Or [he gave him] to us apostles, that the authority of preaching and of reproving might be the greater.

*But we beseech you brethren,* etc. The exhortation is not contrary to the aforesaid laws. For indeed it was happening that some indeed liberally supplied the needy, but others, because of their munificence, neglected to work. Rightly therefore he both praised those and gave to these useful counsel.

VERS. 11.-- *That you give diligence.* As if he should say: It is difficult to put away custom, but constrain yourselves. *And that you work with your hands.* Work is to be done, not idling: because it is both honourable, and as it were a light to the unbelievers, and you will not desire [sic: *de iderabitis*] another's property, much less ask for it or take it away.

[0618C]VERS. 12.-- *But we would not.* After the exhortation concerning continence, and after the correction from curiosity, he confirms that the dead are to rise again, both through Christ's resurrection and by his own authority.

VERS. 13.-- *Through Jesus will he bring with him.* Ever going on, by following him they came to the sleep of death, whence also it is written in another Epistle: *We who live.*

VERS. 14.-- *Them.* Not only those who shall be found alive, but also the dead, concerning whom you doubt. *Who have slept.* He calls Christ dead, the faithful sleeping, that they may the more believe the resurrection: he calls Christ dead, that when we have heard that he died and rose again, we also may hope that we shall rise again [0618D] through the power of his divinity.

VERS. 15.-- *For the Lord himself.* In his own person.

*With the trumpet of God. [n: (ANSELM.)]* This trumpet will rouse all and will terrify the enemies, will summon his own to battle, that they may vanquish the adversaries, and that [the adversaries] may flee from the face of the Lord and be sent into hell. And again, as the trumpet once called together the people of the Hebrews on the feasts: so this trumpet will call together the people of the saints to the solemnity of eternal gladness, God, he says, commanding, and with the voice of the archangel and the trumpet of God sounding. *He shall descend.* Not lowly as before, but commanding with a voice, either his own or the archangel's, or the angels', by which he will make the dead rise again, which is called a trumpet, because [0619A] it is manifest, or as though for battle, which shall be God's and Christ's, to which God will give efficacy.

VERS. 16.-- *In the clouds.* Because a cloud will take them up, as it took up Christ at the ascension, whence another interpreter says: *With bearing clouds.*
