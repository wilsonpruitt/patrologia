## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Of the times.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, whether in summer, or in winter, or by day or by night. ⟦s3⟧ *And moments.* ⟦s4⟧ Namely, at what hour of the day or of the night Christ is to come.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The day of the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ Here the day is men's, at their own pleasure, but then it will be the day of the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ *As a thief.* ⟦s4⟧ So unforeseen will he come to the wicked then, which happens also at the death of each one. ⟦s5⟧ As a thief, I say not by day but by night, when he comes more secretly, and men sleep, then he steals away all things: so that he who thought himself rich, on waking, may find nothing [0619B]. ⟦s6⟧ So many here well secure will then find that they have nothing of good. ⟦s7⟧ For this reason he began concerning the times, that he might warn them to beware for themselves, lest they be so caught.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *For when they shall say.* ⟦s2⟧ For, as is found in the words of Daniel: Antichrist and his ministers shall reign three years and a half, and Elias and Enoch shall be slain by him, as is had also in the Apocalypse: And then there shall be such tribulation as was not before, nor shall be after. ⟦s3⟧ Afterwards he shall be slain by Michael with the greatest part of his own.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Sons of light.* ⟦s2⟧ Faith is the light which makes the day, that is, the habit of the virtues; on the contrary, darkness is unbelief, night the habit of vices. ⟦s3⟧ *We are not of the night nor of darkness,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ That is, in things present [0619C] let us not grow torpid, but let the mind be watchful, considering these fleeting things, and let us use them soberly, not as our own goods but as given for sustenance. ⟦s5⟧ Truly we who are not of the night ought not to sleep. ⟦s6⟧ For he who sleeps, sleeps because of the night, that is, their vices and their sins have drawn them to this, that, forgetful of eternal life, they rest in these things; and they who are drunken, by using these immoderately; from the night of vices they have this.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Having put on the breastplate,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As the breastplate guards the vital parts, so faith and charity [guard] us against the appetite of earthly things.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *That you have them.* ⟦s2⟧ By ministering to them what is necessary, and by showing reverence to their dignity. ⟦s3⟧ For what does it profit to have honour without fruit? ⟦s4⟧ As riches beget negligence of salvation, so poverty, while [0619D] it seeks to be satisfied, turns aside from justice. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore in another epistle he enjoins that priests are to be honoured with double honour.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0620A]VERS. 15.-- *Evil for evil.* ⟦s2⟧ Then is evil rendered for evil, if he who ought to be rebuked is not rebuked, but is neglected by a wicked dissembling. ⟦s3⟧ *Towards all,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Generally, towards all. ⟦s5⟧ *Without ceasing.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, always live justly and desire eternal things. ⟦s7⟧ The just man never ceases to pray, unless he cease to be just. ⟦s8⟧ He always prays who always acts well. ⟦s9⟧ For the good desire itself is prayer; and if the desire is continual, the prayer is continual.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *In Christ.* ⟦s2⟧ The will of God has been shown, because these things were in Christ, in whom nothing displeased God. ⟦s3⟧ *Extinguish not the Spirit.* ⟦s4⟧ Because it is the will of God to do all these things, you who are greater, who have through the Holy Spirit the gift of understanding, hide it not, by which desert you also would lose it. ⟦s5⟧ You subjects, despise not [0620B] their prophecies; nevertheless receive not all things indiscriminately, but *prove* them, that is, examine them by reason, and hold what is found good. ⟦s6⟧ *Extinguish not the Spirit,* that is: If the Holy Spirit reveals anything to anyone's ear, forbid thou him not to speak what he perceives. ⟦s7⟧ The Holy Spirit who has been given to us we extinguish, not as to what is in himself, but in us, when by sinning we put him to flight.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *And may the God of peace himself sanctify you in all things,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, I thus admonish prelates and subjects, but God himself is he who is able. ⟦s3⟧ *Your spirit.* ⟦s4⟧ Let the reason be kept entire, by not consenting to the flesh. ⟦s5⟧ *And soul.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, let the sensuality be kept entire, by serving the reason. ⟦s7⟧ *And body.* ⟦s8⟧ That you do nothing by its ministry. ⟦s9⟧ Whence anyone may complain. ⟦s10⟧ Sometimes the soul is defiled [0620C] by an evil thought and yet the body is clean. ⟦s11⟧ And therefore he says that the spirit ought to be entire in man. ⟦s12⟧ But if an evil life, or an evil thought, intervene, there will not be spirit. ⟦s13⟧ Spirit and soul and body, of which man consists, namely that by which we understand, and that by which we live, and that by which we are visible and palpable, which again are called two, because often the soul is named together with the spirit. ⟦s14⟧ *Without complaint.* ⟦s15⟧ It is one thing to be without sin, which in this life was said of the Only-begotten alone. ⟦s16⟧ It is another to be without complaint, which could be said of many just men even in this life. ⟦s17⟧ For there is a certain manner of good life, of which even in this present conversation there could be no just complaint. ⟦s18⟧ *In the coming.* ⟦s19⟧ That is, even unto the end of life.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Faithful.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: I pray that he may sanctify, [0620D] that he may keep you sanctified; nor is there to be any doubt thereof, because he is true in his promises, who called you to this, that you should be holy.
