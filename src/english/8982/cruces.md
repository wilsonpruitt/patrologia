## §1 · THE LEMMA DIVERGENCE LIST — ALL 82 SPANS CHECKED AGAINST THE VULGATE

Every italic span in the work — the verse lemmata and every second (mid-paragraph) lemma —
checked word by word against the Clementine Vulgate of 1 Thessalonians **before** the English was
written, and each rendering then held fixed against every re-quotation of it in the gloss below.
**Agreements are listed too**, so this can be told apart from a report by someone who only listed
findings.

### 1a · The two that diverge — both marked `[var: …]`

| col | Migne prints | Vulgate (1 Thess) | marker + reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0617B | *Ore, aspectu,* etc. | 2:17 *ad tempus horae, **aspectu**, non corde* — **no *ore* at all** | ⭐ `[var: Vulg. *aspectu, non corde*, "in sight, not in heart"; the Vulgate has no *ore*]`. **Material, and the gloss proves the plate is not a slip:** its very first words are *Os et aspectus cessabant* — the gloss expounds **two** things, mouth and sight, and it can only be doing that because its lemma printed two. Rendered *In mouth, in sight,* etc. ⛔ Conforming this lemma to the Vulgate's *aspectu, non corde* would have deleted the word the gloss is written on, and it is the reading an eye that knows the verse reaches for first. |
| 0617A | VERS. 13 *Sicut est verbum Dei.* | 2:13 *sed (**sicut est vere**) verbum Dei* | `[var: Vulg. *sicut est vere, verbum Dei*, "as it is in truth, the word of God"]`. ### 1b · Divergences examined and deliberately NOT marked, with the reason

These are real differences from the Clementine text. Each was weighed against Pattern 14's bar
("differs … in a way that changes the sense") and declined. They are recorded because a blind
reader will meet them and must be able to tell a decision from an oversight.

- **0617C · VERS. 6, 7 *Sicut nos quoque vos: ideo.*** — Vulg. 3:6 *sicut **et** nos quoque vos*.
  The dropped *et* is a connective at the seam of a fragmentary lemma that splices the end of 3:6
  to the opening of 3:7; no content word is lost and nothing in the gloss touches it. Rendered
  *As we also you: therefore.*
- **0617D · VERS. 1 *Quomodo vos oporteat ambulare,*** — Vulg. 4:1 *quomodo **oporteat vos**
  ambulare*. Word order transposed only; identical sense, and the gloss (*recte conversando*)
  does not depend on the order. Contrast 7914 @1079B, where a reversed order **was** marked
  because the gloss turned on it.
- **0617D · *De caetero.*** — Vulg. *de cetero*. Orthographic (*ae*/*e*), Pattern 9. No marker,
  no italic carry.
- **0620B · *Et Anima.*** — Vulg. 5:23 *et anima*. Migne capitalizes *Anima* alone of the three
  (*Spiritus vester* … *Et Anima* … *Et corpus*). English capitalization of a common noun is not
  an exponent of anything and the facing Latin column shows the capital, so it is normalized:
  *And soul.* Recorded here so the normalization is visible, per the Pattern 9 argument for
  section labels.
- **0619A *Bajulis nubibus.*** — **not a Vulgate variant and correctly takes no `[var:]`**: Migne
  introduces it as *alius interpres dicit*, i.e. he is quoting a rival Latin version of 4:17 as
  such. Marking it against the Vulgate would tell the reader something the page already says.
  Rendered *With bearing clouds.*
- **0618C *Nos qui vivimus.*** — see §6; the reference, not the wording, is the crux.

### 1d · Fragment expansion — where the boundary was drawn, and why

Brief convention 3 expands a fragment only where the stump is not English. Ruth's shipped
practice is the control: it renders *Elevata.* as "She lifted up." and *Quocunque.* as
"Whithersoever." — minimal — and expands only *Non te.*, which is unrenderable. Applied here:

- **Rendered minimally** (renderable as they stand): *Quia non.* → **That it was not.** ·
  *Neque.* → **Neither.** · *Ideo.* → **Therefore.** · *Pervenit.* → **It came.** ·
  *Eos.* → **Them.** · *Fidelis.* → **Faithful.** · *probate,* → **prove,**.
  ⛔ *Quia non.* was **not** expanded to "That it was not vain": *inanis* is not in the lemma, and
  the gloss immediately supplies the idea itself (*quae inanis*). Supplying it in the lemma would
  have put a Vulgate word on the page Migne did not print there.
- **Expanded, because the stump is not English:** *Spiritum nolite.* → **Extinguish not the
  Spirit.** ("The Spirit do not." is not a sentence in any register.) The expansion is exactly the
  words of the second, whole lemma at 0620B (*Spiritum nolite exstinguere*), so the two lemmata
  read alike on the page, which is what the Latin means.
- **Verbs supplied to fragments on the Ruth model:** *Vocavit,* → **He called,** ·
  *Sed impedivit.* → **But he hindered.** (subject *Satanas*, 2:18 — see §3) ·
  *Vos enim imitatores,* → **For you became imitators,** · *Descendet.* → **He shall descend.**
- **Comma / `etc.` carried as printed** on every fragment that has one: *Quales fuerimus,* ·
  *Possemus vobis,* · *Vos enim imitatores,* · *Ore, aspectu,* · *Quae est,* ·
  *Quam enim gratiarum,* · *a nobis,* · *sic et ambuletis,* · *Qui etiam dedit,* ·
  *Rogamus autem vos fratres,* · *Induti loricam,* · *In omnes,* · *probate,* ·
  *Non sumus noctis neque tenebrarum,* · *Spiritum nolite exstinguere,* ·
  *Ipse autem Deus pacis sanctificet vos per omnia,* · *Quomodo vos oporteat ambulare,*.

### 1e · One rendering fixed across the work

***querela* = "complaint", never "blame."** It is the lemma at 0617C (3:13) and again at 0620C
(5:23), and the second passage **is an argument about the word**: *Aliud est esse sine peccato …
Aliud est esse sine querela … de quo … justa querela esse non possit.* Douay's "without blame"
at 3:13 would have broken the link between the two columns and left the 0620C argument glossing a
word that no longer stands above it. Both lemmata read **Without complaint.**

## §2 · NEGATION AUDIT — 49 particles carried, none invented

Every `non`/`nec`/`neque`/`nisi`/`nunquam`/`nullus`/`nihil`/`ne`/`noli(te)`/`nedum`/`nequimus`
Migne prints was located in the Latin and matched to its English. **The two lemmata that carry
negation on their face are exact:** *Non quasi hominibus placentes.* → **Not as pleasing men.**
(2:4, agrees with the Vulgate word for word) and *Non sumus noctis neque tenebrarum,* →
**We are not of the night nor of darkness,** (5:5, **both** negatives standing, and the *neque*
is not collapsed into the first).

Sites where the English could easily have lost one, and did not:

- **0616C** *Hoc **nisi** ipse operetur in nobis, vitare **nequimus*** → "Unless he himself work
  this in us, we **cannot** avoid it." Two negations in six words, both carried.
- **0616C** *amor **non** sentit, **nisi** qui ei bellum indixerit* → "he perceives **not**, save
  he who has declared war upon it." *nisi* rendered "save", not folded into the *non*.
- **0616C** *si cuiquam facile est laudem **non** cupere … difficile est tamen ea **non**
  delectari* → "not to covet praise … **not** to be delighted with it." The sentence's whole
  point is the pair.
- **0615D (2nd)** *Non dico in avaritiam, sed **nec** feci vel dixi* → "I say not, in covetousness,
  but **neither** did I do or say."
- **0615D (2nd)** *quibus **nec** facultas erat, **nec** pudor poscendi* → "who had **neither** the
  means **nor** the shame of demanding."
- **0619B** *qualis ante **non** fuit, **nec** post erit* → "as was **not** before, **nor** shall be
  after."
- **0619C** *nos qui **non** sumus noctis **non** debemus dormire* → "we who are **not** of the
  night ought **not** to sleep."
- **0620A** *Justus **nunquam** desinit orare, **nisi** desinat justus esse* → "**never** ceases to
  pray, **unless** he cease to be just."
- **0618B** *et **non** de iderabitis rem alterius, **nedum** rogetis vel tollatis* → "and you will
  **not** desire … **much less** ask for it or take it away." The negation survives the `[sic:]`
  beside it — see §4 and 7a⁗.

⚑ **One English "not" that answers no printed particle, declared:** 0617A
*convertentur Judaei **nonnulli*** → "**not a few** of the Jews will be converted." *nonnulli* is
one word meaning "several, a good many"; "not a few" is its literal English and the negation is
inside the lexeme, not a separate `non` on the plate. Flagged here so a particle-count check does
not read it as an invention. "Some" was the alternative and was declined as less literal.

## §4 · CARRIED TYPE — three `[sic: …]`, and what each swallowed

Each entry states what the marker took out of the clause and what was put back beside it in plain
words, per 7a⁗.

**1. 0618B · *et non de iderabitis rem alterius*** → `and you will not desire [sic: *de iderabitis*] another's property`

Pattern 10 split type for *desiderabitis*. The whole broken run is carried, spacing included.
⚠ **7a⁗ questions 1 and 2 both fire here**: the marked run is the clause's **only verb** *and* it
stands under a printed *non*. So the English carries "will not desire" in plain words beside the
marker — the negation is **outside** the quarantine, where a reader meets it. Conjecture:
*desiderabitis* (near-certain; the clause continues *nedum rogetis vel tollatis*, "much less ask
for it or take it away", which only makes sense as a climax on *desire*).

**2. 0619A (chunk 1, 5:1) · *qua hora dici vel noctis*** → `at what hour of the day [sic: *dici*] or of the night`

*dici* is a real Latin form (present passive infinitive of *dico*) with **no grammatical slot**
here: the phrase needs a genitive parallel to *noctis*. Pattern 12's first test — "it has no
grammatical slot" — so it is carried, not silently read. Conjecture: *diei*. ⚑ Rendering it
straight as "of the day" was the alternative and was declined: English would have shown the reader
nothing at all, which is Pattern 12's second test (it renders only by hiding the defect). The
sense "of the day" stands beside the marker so the phrase is not left with a hole where its noun
was.

**3. 0619D (chunk 1) · *ita egestas dum satutari quaerit*** → `so poverty, while it seeks to be satisfied [sic: *satutari*], turns aside from justice`

*satutari* is not a word. Carried under Pattern 7's non-word rule. Conjecture: **saturari**, "to
be filled/satisfied" — the antithesis is *divitiae* … *egestas*, riches breeding negligence and
want being driven from justice **by seeking to be filled**, and the *t*/*r* slip is a single
sort. ⚠ 7a⁗ q1: the marked run was the infinitive governed by *quaerit*, so the sense is supplied
beside the marker rather than left inside it.

**No `[d:]`, no `[ed:]`, no `[cj:]`, no `[nt:]`.** The three `[n:]` notes — `(CHRYS.)`, `(HIER.)`,

## §7 · PATTERN 17 — one number shift, and it is in the Latin

0620B moves from plural to singular inside a single gloss: *Vos subditi prophetias illorum
**nolite** spernere … sed **probate** … **noli** prohibere loqui quod sentit.* The plural
imperatives address the congregation; *noli* is singular and addresses one man — whoever has the
Spirit's word in his ear. Rendered "despise **not** … **prove** … forbid **thou** him not to
speak what he perceives." **Thou is the singular, and it recovers a distinction the plate makes
and modern English cannot.** Everything else in the work is plural *vos* and reads "you"; there
is no second-person singular anywhere else in either chunk.

### 11a · The two that matter, stated as MECHANISMS — the unexpressed subject again

Both repairs below are the same class, and it is the class that dominates this whole series. The
mechanism is worth more to the next translator than the two sites are.

**⭐ MECHANISM 1 — a bare accusative participle takes the person of the LEMMA, not of the
narration around it.** 0620D prints *Oro ut sanctificet, ut **sanctificatos** servet*. Nothing in
the sentence says who is sanctified. The surrounding gloss is third-person narration about God
(*sanctificet*, *servet*, *verus est*), so third person is what the ear reaches for, and "that he
may keep **them** sanctified" was written. It is wrong. **The antecedent of a bare accusative in a
gloss is almost always in the lemma above or in the verse the lemma comes from** — here 5:23
*sanctificet **vos** per omnia* — and this sentence then confirms it in its own closing words,
*qui vocavit **vos** ad hoc ut sancti **essetis***. The tell is countable: the wrong reading made
the gloss go 2nd → 3rd → 2nd person inside eleven words. **A supplied pronoun that forces the
sentence to change person and change back is wrong; look for one that does not.** Repaired to
"that he may keep **you** sanctified."

**⭐ MECHANISM 2 — getting the referent right is only half the job; if the Latin cannot express
it, the supply must be VISIBLE.** 0618D prints *suos ad bellum invitabit, ut **expugnent**
adversarios, et **effugiant** a facie Domini atque **mittantur** in gehennam.* All three
subjunctives grammatically share *suos*, and Christ's own fleeing the Lord's face into gehenna is
impossible, so the subject must shift to *adversarios* — **which is accusative and cannot be a
subject at all.** The original English said "and that **these** may flee", which is the right
sense but reads as though Migne had written a clean subject change. He had not: the construction
is broken on the plate. Repaired to "and that **[the adversaries]** may flee", using the supplied
-words bracket convention. **The rule the two mechanisms make together: name the referent from the
lemma, then ask whether the printed Latin can carry it — and if it cannot, bracket it, because
otherwise the repair is invisible and the plate's defect is concealed.**

### 11b · The four smaller repairs

3. **0618D, colon restored at a correlative joint.** Migne: *convocabat populum Hebraeorum**:**
   **sic** haec tuba convocabit…* The colon had been demoted to a comma. It is the hinge of a
   *sicut … : sic …* correlative and carries structure — the same shape as the lost *ita … ut* of
   the previous batch. ⚑ **Position beats tally**: a colon at a correlative joint is not
   interchangeable with a comma, whatever the corpus-wide punctuation rate is.
4. **0618A, VERS. 4 *Vas suum.*** Migne prints *Id est uxorem**,** possideat dominando ei**,** et
   hoc…*; the English had printed a colon for the first comma and a semicolon for the second.
   **Both of Migne's commas restored:** "That is, his wife, let him possess her by ruling her, and
   this, *In sanctification,*…" One of the two was a mark **supplied** where he prints a comma,
   which is Pattern 8's "never supply" and not a rate question.
5. **0617A, VERS. 16 *Pervenit.*** *quia ira Dei pervenit ut excaecentur* had been rendered "has
   come **upon them**". Migne's gloss prints no goal; the Vulgate (2:16 *pervenit … **super
   illos***) does. **The mildest form of the 7a″ conformation class, and still the class** — a
   phrase carried into the gloss from the verse the gloss is not quoting. Repaired to "because the
   wrath of God has come, that they may be blinded."
6. **0616A, *sed in multa sollicitudine **pro** adversis*** had been rendered "amid adversities".
   *pro* is causal; "amid" converts a cause into a circumstance for no gain. Repaired to
   "**on account of** adversities."
