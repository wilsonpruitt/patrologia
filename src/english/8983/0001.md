## CHAPTER II.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0627B]VERS. 1.-- *I beseech.* ⟦s2⟧ Hitherto concerning the false apostles, here concerning prayers. ⟦s3+4⟧ *First of all.* [var: Vulg. *primum omnium*] That is, that supplications be made before all things. ⟦s5⟧ Here the order of the Mass is shown, which is a special kind of prayer. ⟦s6⟧ *Supplications.* ⟦s7⟧ Or that prayer which is made beside or before the consecration. ⟦s8⟧ *Prayers.* ⟦s9⟧ That is, in the consecration itself. ⟦s10⟧ *Petitions.* ⟦s11⟧ Or interpellations, as it were of debt, that what is here transacted may be perfected in eternal life, as when the people is blessed by the bishop. ⟦s12⟧ *Thanksgivings.* ⟦s13⟧ Concerning things done, which conclude the whole as though the whole were from God: as, Let us bless the Lord. ⟦s14⟧ *For all.* ⟦s15⟧ That we may follow the clemency of God, because God wills all men to be saved, although according to justice he reprobates certain ones. ⟦s16⟧ *That quiet* from persecution.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *For this.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest anyone should think that these things are not [0627C] to be done for those whom the Church suffers, he adds: *For this is good.* ⟦s3⟧ And lest anyone should think that salvation could be had by the worship of the one God and by a good life without participation in the body of Christ, that is, without the faith of the Church. ⟦s4⟧ *One,* he says, *God, and one mediator,* that all men be saved may never come to pass save through the mediator, God the Word, which always was, yet according to the flesh was made.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The mediator of God,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This is that arbiter whom Job desires, saying: *Would that there were an arbiter for us.* [var: Vulg. Job 9:33 *Non est qui utrumque valeat arguere*, "There is none that may be able to reprove both"]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. IX de Civit. Dei, c. 15.)] A mediator between God and men, it behoved that he should have something like to God, etc., *as far as* another good one, who reconciles [0627D] enemies, Jesus Christ.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And in truth* Truth to come is the reality and the reward of faith. ⟦s2⟧ Dost thou ask, the reward? let faith go before. ⟦s3⟧ For we believe that we may know, we do not know that we may believe. ⟦s4⟧ For what is faith, save to believe what thou seest not? faith therefore is, to believe what thou seest not. ⟦s5⟧ Truth, to see what thou hast believed.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *I will therefore that men,* etc. [n: (AUG., serm. 93 de Tempore.)] Christ lifted up his hands for us upon the cross, etc., *as far as* that thy works may come into thy mind.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Anger,* toward one's neighbour; *contention,* toward God.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Not in plaited hair,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Lest by all these things, beyond the measure and the manners of their own person, they should give study to the occasion of stirring up [0628A] concupiscence, but rather through all good works let them be promising and showing outwardly the piety of the mind.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *A woman in silence.* ⟦s2⟧ He has said that the office of praying befits all, and in what manner, and for whom, and why, and that prayer must be made everywhere. ⟦s3⟧ Now he says that the office of teaching befits men alone, and determines what manner of men ought to be ordained thereto.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *To teach,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Behold, he not only taught that a woman should have a humble and honourable habit, but also denied her the authority of teaching, and commanded that she be made subject to the man, that as well in habit as in obedience she may be under the power of the man from whom she draws her origin.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Adam was not seduced. (AUG., lib. II de Gen. [0628B] ad litteram, c. ult.)* ⟦s2⟧ For since the Apostle calls him a transgressor in the likeness of the transgression of Adam, etc., *as far as* in no wise do I think that he could have been seduced.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *She shall be saved.* ⟦s2⟧ Although the woman was the cause of sin, yet she shall be saved, not only the virgin and the continent, but also the married woman, even if she never ceases from the work of marriage, if *through the bearing of children,* going on, she shall have departed out of this world. ⟦s3⟧ *If she continue in faith and love and sanctification,* that besides her own husband she know not another. ⟦s4⟧ *With sobriety.* ⟦s5⟧ That is, temperance, that she use even her own husband temperately.

## CHAPTER III.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *He desireth a good work.* ⟦s2⟧ A work, not a dignity, [0628C] a labour, not delights, not to grow greater by eminence, but to grow less by humility, that he may become the servant and minister of men for Christ's sake, that also, Rachel of fair countenance being left aside for a time, yet not forsaken, he may with Jacob go in unto Lia, blear of eyes, and may come down from the mountain with Moses to the plains, from the leisure of contemplation to the labour of action.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Blameless.* ⟦s2⟧ The same as to Titus, *without crime.* ⟦s3⟧ A thing almost against nature, as *without sin:* but let one be chosen, by whose comparison the rest may be called a flock. ⟦s4⟧ *The husband of one wife,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ That is, married but once after baptism. ⟦s6⟧ For if even before he had a wife who has died, it is not imputed to him, to whom, being wholly new, neither fornications nor the other things which were before are any longer a hindrance. ⟦s7⟧ *Sober,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ The ministers of the temple are forbidden [0628D] to drink wine and strong drink, lest their hearts be weighed down by drunkenness, and that the sense may be vigorous, may always be spare.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Not covetous.* ⟦s2⟧ Seeking nothing beyond food and clothing.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Lest into pride.* ⟦s2⟧ The priest of a moment knows not humility, the measures of persons, or to despise himself, has not fasted, has not wept, has not corrected himself, has not given out to the poor. ⟦s3⟧ Into arrogance, which is the ruin of the devil, they fall who in the point of an hour, being not yet disciples, become masters.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But it behoveth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This clause agrees with the beginning, that he be blameless not only among those of his own household, but among strangers, Jews, Gentiles, heretics, [0629A] so that they who disparage religion may not dare to disparage his right.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *No crime,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ A crime is a grave sin, most worthy of accusation and of condemnation.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The Church.* ⟦s2⟧ The calling together of many unto the worship of the one God. ⟦s3+4⟧ *The pillar of truth.* [var: Vulg. *columna et firmamentum veritatis*, "the pillar and ground of the truth"] Well sustaining the truth in herself, lest it fall, although she be afflicted.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *He appeared unto angels.* ⟦s2⟧ Not in God only does that which is hidden become known to the angels, but here also it appears to them, when it is wrought and made public; therefore he is said to have appeared to the angels not now in the form of lowliness but in power; he therefore who was before seen lowly, death being overcome, appeared in majesty, that the angels, acknowledging the mystery which before lay hid, declared [0629B] in the flesh, bending the knee to him as to God, might give thanks, because they had learned the truth. ⟦s3⟧ *In the world.* ⟦s4⟧ So that the perfidy of the world may not be able to merit pardon for its error. ⟦s5⟧ As the Lord says of the Jews: *If I had not come, and had spoken to them, they would not have sin,* etc. [n: (Joan. XV)].

## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Now the Spirit manifestly saith.* ⟦s2⟧ What things to come the Holy Spirit had revealed to him for the instruction and the warning of the Church; he keeps not silence, saying that there shall be men of pestilent doctrine, that the Church, being forewarned, may be watchful, lest it be circumvented by men of this kind, as if he should say: So is the house of God worthy, but *the Spirit manifestly saith.* ⟦s3⟧ And therefore, for its loftiness, lay not aside thy care.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0629C]VERS. 2.-- *Cauterized.* ⟦s2⟧ A cautery is an iron so called from taking heed.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Which God hath created.* [var: Vulg. *quod Deus creavit*] This is the doctrine of the saints, that of demons.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *It is sanctified.* ⟦s2⟧ The food becomes holy and wholesome, not hurtful to the soul or to the body, the word sanctifies, the prayer obtains, but the devil tempts through food.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For bodily.* ⟦s2⟧ It is here given to be understood that Timothy exercised himself in watchings, fastings, prayers and good works, not being so intent upon works of mercy. ⟦s3⟧ *Piety,* which works good things for the brethren and avails to merit God.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Command these things.* ⟦s2⟧ He admonishes him, that his advancement might in all things be an example to others, that in [0629D] a young man the discipline might seem wonderful, and grave, that his acts might excuse [him] - and that he be held not as a youth but as an elder, and that his elders in age might blush, if they did not show themselves after his pattern, grave in conversation and in manners.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Prophecy.* ⟦s2⟧ He called the inspiration of the Holy Spirit prophecy, through which he foreknew what he was about to do concerning this man and concerning the rest. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore he says: *Which was given thee by prophecy:* because, by the revealing of the Spirit, the Apostle knew that Timothy was worthy of the episcopal honour. ⟦s4+5⟧ *Of the presbyter.* [var: Vulg. *presbyterii*, "of the presbytery"] For *of the presbyters,* because they cannot be fewer than three. ⟦s6⟧ And he calls a presbyter a bishop; or because one lays on hands, the rest assenting.
