## CHAPTER V.

[0630A]

VERS. 1.-- *Rebuke not an elder.* Lest, taking it ill that he has been corrected by a lesser, he be more exasperated than profit.

VERS. 2.-- *As sisters.* That, seeing themselves to be admonished for love's sake, they may the more easily correct themselves. *In all chastity.* In addressing young women a young man is rightly admonished concerning chastity.

VERS. 3.-- *Honour widows that are widows indeed.* That is, those who are destitute of the help of all their own, and who cannot labour with their hands, whom poverty weakens and age wears out: to whom God is hope, and every work, prayer.

VERS. 4.-- *To parents.* The parents are the same who above are the sons and the grandsons. Whence also in the law of the world [0630B] a parent is bidden to succeed a parent by degree. To whom we render a mutual return, since we were brought up by our fathers, and nourish others by a like law.

VERS. 5.-- *Desolate, let her trust.* Seeing that from no quarter has she support, she is with her whole mind devoted to God, from whom alone she hopes for the help of life and of salvation

VERS. 6.-- *She that is in delights.* It is a kind of imposture to wish to have the honour of a widow, and not to obey God, and to do one thing and profess another, since she professes religion

VERS. 8.-- *Hath not care.* Care must be spent upon parents, as Jesus commended his mother to John.

VERS. 10.-- *The feet. [n: (AUG., in Tract. 58 in Joan.)]* Let us in humility do these things one for another, which he that is exalted did humbly, etc., *as far as* the effect of humility is confirmed. [0630C] *Of the saints.* Whose, I ask? Of those who are in tribulation, not of the saints simply: for there can be saints, and such as are cherished by all with the highest deference. Do not follow after these that abound in plenty of things, but the afflicted, and those who are unknown to many. Do not give these things to the prelate of the Church to be distributed, do thou rather minister, that thou mayest receive the reward not [sic: *nesolius*] of the outlay alone, but of the service: give out with thine own hands, do thou sow the furrow of piety.

VERS. 11.-- *Avoid.* That is, do not so far care for their vows of chastity as to undertake them to be maintained by the alms of the Church.

VERS. 12.-- *Having. [n: (AUG., de Bono viduitatis, cap. 8, 9, 10.)]* If in the conjugal bond chastity be preserved, damnation is not feared, etc., *as far as* [0630D] every woman of this kind is like the wife of Lot, who looked back.

*They have made void.* Even if not by marrying, yet by willing: not because marriages are condemned, but the fraud of the purpose, and the broken faith of the vow is condemned, and the will which departs from the purpose is damnable, whether marriages follow or follow not.

VERS. 15.-- *They are turned back after Satan.* By looking back from that excellent purpose of widowed chastity, or of virginal, they have fallen and perished.

VERS. 17.-- *Who well.* Here he treats of the honour of presbyters, as if he should say: That which I have said of widows, observe: and this also command concerning presbyters, that the presbyters [0631A] who rule well in life and in doctrine be accounted worthy.

VERS. 19.-- *Against a presbyter receive not an accusation,* etc. He who corrects is wont not to be loved, and therefore, an occasion being had, he is easily accused. Against this he admonishes, that an accusation against a presbyter is not to be received easily.

VERS. 20.-- *Before all,* etc. [n: (AUG., serm. 16 de Verb. Dom.)] Sometimes thou oughtest to reprove the sinner between thee and him alone, etc., *as far as* but when it is sinned openly, it must be reproved openly.

VERS. 21.-- *I testify before God.* That is, let God be my witness, that I have saved my soul for thee, beware.

VERS. 23.-- *Do not still drink water,* etc. Because he had admonished to chastity, on this occasion he forbids immoderate abstinence, [0631B] which he [Timothy] himself had entered upon.

VERS. 24.-- *Some men's sins,* etc. Because he had said that nothing is to be done without prejudgment, he determines where prejudgment is necessary, and where not, dividing goods and evils into manifest and hidden, as if he should say: therefore there is need of examination, because as some things are manifest, which it behoves not to examine, so some things are hidden, concerning which without previous examination a prejudgment cannot be made. *Made manifest.* [var: Vulg. *manifesta*, "manifest"] He says this of those things concerning which it is clear with what mind they are done, these go before unto judgment, because if judgment follows these, it is not rash. *They follow after.* As the evils which are hidden shall not themselves lie hid in their own time, when the time of Christ shall have come, in which they may be made manifest: so also of good things. Let us therefore judge of these: [0631C] let those be left to the judgment of God.

## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 1.-- *Whosoever.* It must be known that certain men preached a liberty common to all in Christ. Which indeed is true of spiritual liberty, not of carnal, as they understood it: therefore the Apostle here speaks against them, bidding servants be subject to their own masters. Let not servants demand what is said of the Hebrews, that they should serve six years, and be dismissed free without price, which is mystical.

VERS. 4.-- *Proud.* Pride assuredly, not reason, leads him, since he bursts forth into conflict for the seeking of favour, which is shunned by the meek and the good. *Languishing.* For he does not utterly die by refusing the faith, [0631D] nor does he grow strong unto the knowledge of the truth, but as with repeated attacks he languishes about questions and strifes of words. *Questions.* The question was whether he should be a servant, since all are sprung from the same fathers, and Christ has redeemed all.

VERS. 9.-- *For they that will.* This is said to those who will and are not, that they may not desire. Now to the rich another thing is said, namely, not to be highminded, but to give to the poor. *They fall into temptation and the snare of the devil, because the root of all evils,* etc. [var: Vulg. 6:9 *et in laqueum diaboli*; 6:10 *Radix enim omnium malorum est cupiditas*] There is no kind of evil which does not sometimes arise from covetousness, because the root of all evils is covetousness.

VERS. 10.-- *Covetousness.* In the Greek there stands, φιλαργυρία. [0632A] If therefore avarice, which properly is the love of money, is taken in place of the genus which is covetousness, a species is put.

*With many sorrows.* [var: Vulg. *doloribus malis*, "with evil sorrows"] For the avaricious sustain the torment of anxiety so much the more, the more they desire the things which either, being had, pass away, or which they cannot have.

VERS. 11.-- *But thou, man,* etc. [var: Vulg. *Tu autem, o homo Dei*, "But thou, O man of God"] He does not say that he fears this concerning that man, but he knew that this would come into the hands of the churches. *Flee these things.* For nothing is so harsh and so pernicious as if an ecclesiastical man, and most of all he who holds a lofty place, should give study to the riches of this world: because he harms not only himself but also the rest, to whom he gives a contrary pattern. *Piety.* Piety is that we condescend to the needy, which the covetous man does not do. [0632B] *Faith.* He who has a true faith concerning God does not desire to become rich amid miseries, nor is the world of more worth to him than God.

VERS. 13.-- *I charge thee.* In Timothy the Apostle speaks to all his successors. *And Christ Jesus.* By the example of Christ he exhorts.

VERS. 15.-- *Blessed and only,* etc. He says this not of the Father only, but of God who is the Trinity itself.

VERS. 16.-- *Who only hath immortality* He has it by nature, others from him by grace. *Inaccessible, whom none of men.* [var: Vulg. *lucem inhabitat inaccessibilem : quem nullus hominum vidit*, "he inhabiteth light inaccessible: whom no man hath seen"] Because none comes to it of himself, but to whom it is given, it is given by his gift, which the Prophet understood, saying: *Come ye to him, and be enlightened [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*, etc.

VERS. 17.-- *To the rich of this world.* Above he treated of [0632C] those who wish to become rich, saying that they fall into temptation and the snare of the devil, here he treats of those who are rich, admonishing them not to hope in riches. And because he said that evils come from covetousness, and persuaded the contrary, lest the rich should seem cast off, he determines. *Not to be highminded.* The Apostle did not dread riches, but their disease, that is, pride, which is the worm of the rich. Great is the mind which among riches is not tempted with that disease. He is great who does not therefore think himself great, because he is rich. But he who therefore thinks himself great is proud, is needy, swells in the flesh, cracks in the heart, begs, is puffed up, not full. *To enjoy.* For temporal things God gives in this life to use, eternal things to enjoy: the former, whence we may do good; the latter whence we may be made good.

[0632D]VERS. 19.-- *To lay up in store.* Unto this are riches sent before that shall not perish.

VERS. 20.-- *O Timothy.* Timothy, precious to God. Τίμιος precious, Θεὸς God.

*Profane.* As is, *hypostasis,* which word relates both to person and to substance. Whence also the heretics, by the multiplication of this name, wished to bring in that there are three substances. Understand profane words to be those which are against religion, others are not to be avoided

VERS. 21.-- *Promising, they have erred concerning the faith,* etc. For these love nothing so much as to promise knowledge, and to deride, as it were unskilfulness, the faith of true things (which little ones are commanded to believe).
