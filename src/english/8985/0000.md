## THE ARGUMENT.

[0689A]

Simon Peter declares that the wise are dead to this world through faith, and to those same men he makes manifest, more clearly than the light itself, how great is the greatness of piety.

## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *Simon. [n: (BEDA.)]* He writes this epistle to the same persons to whom he wrote the first also, etc., *as far as* they did not yield, let them not yield to the heretics.

*Servant and Apostle.* The person of the writer is commended.

*Equal. [n: (BEDA.)]* He acknowledges them as equal to himself, not on account of circumcision, but because the same grace and faith of Christ which they themselves had received, this also they exercised through works. *In justice.* This justice human [0689B] prudence did not find out, nor did the institution of the law teach it, but our Saviour showed it in the Gospel: *Unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. V)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *Grace unto you. [n: (BEDA.)]* In the first Epistle he wrote *be multiplied;* in this, *be fulfilled:* because he writes that one to beginners, this one to the more perfect. Grace is multiplied to those who advance well in this life through faith; it shall be fulfilled to those who arrive in the other life through sight. Whence he adds, *in the knowledge of God,* that is, that you may know the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom he sent.

VERS. 3.-- *How all things. [n: (ID.)]* So may the grace of Christ be fulfilled, etc., *as far as* but he restored the weak and inglorious by his own power and glory.

[0689C]VERS. 4.-- *He hath given us most great and precious promises.* That is, eternal life, which we may obtain through the knowledge of him. Or he gave the things which he promised us, concerning the conversion of others through us, or concerning the coming of the Holy Spirit, or concerning the power of miracles.

VERS. 5.-- *Minister in faith. [n: (BEDA.)]* Not only should you flee the concupiscence of the world, etc., *as far as* lest you be soft and dissolute.

VERS. 6.-- *But in knowledge, abstinence. [n: (ID.)]* When you shall have learned to do good things, in the hope of eternal life abstain forthwith from evil things, both in thought and in deed, lest the knowledge of heavenly things go for nothing, if anyone restrain not himself from the allurements of the world.

VERS. 7.-- *But in piety. [n: (ID.)]* Bestow not the works of piety upon your adversaries with a view to any other [0689D] thing save that of brotherly love, that you may pray for them that they may be converted, who by your teaching or by rebukes are unwilling to be converted.

VERS. 8.-- *For if these things be with you,* etc. Therefore hold these things and minister them to others, because if all these things be present with you in the affection of the heart, and also abound, that is, shall have prevailed against the wars of the vices, they will not constitute you void of virtues in this world, or without fruit in the world to come.

[0690A]VERS. 9.-- *He is blind and groping with the hand. [n: (BEDA.)]* The eye designates knowledge, the hand designates work, etc., *as far as* he could not foreknow, he fell.

*Receiving forgetfulness.* When a man falls from sin into sin, he seems to have forgotten the covenant which he entered upon with God in baptism.

VERS. 10.-- *You shall not sin at any time.* That is, you shall attain unto eternal life, where you shall sin no more. Or you shall shun criminal sins, which whoever has committed shall not have an inheritance in the kingdom of Christ; free from which he remains, with the Lord helping, who shall have given himself over to the aforesaid virtues. But without venial sins no one can be.

VERS. 11.-- *For so. [n: (BEDA.)]* Ezechiel, speaking of the building set upon the mountain, etc., *as far as, and there shall be seen [0690B] the God of gods in Sion [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*.

VERS. 12.-- *I will begin always to admonish you of these things.* That there may be for you an entrance open unto the Holy of holies, again and again will I admonish you, and I shall always be at the beginning, because there shall never be an end of admonition. What therefore it is not irksome to me to repeat the oftener, let it not be burdensome to you the oftener to hear.

*And indeed knowing. [n: (BEDA.)]* Why does he wish to admonish those who know, and are confirmed? For this reason, namely, that they may exercise in works the knowledge which they have, and may guard with fixed mind the truth by whose presence they are confirmed, lest at any time through masters of error they fall away from the simplicity and chastity of the faith.

VERS. 13.-- *But it is just,* etc. Therefore I will admonish, because I judge it just, by reason of this, that the Lord graciously called [0690C] me, and confirmed me, and admonished me to confirm others, saying: *Confirm thy brethren,* to rouse you up as though sleeping unto these things foretold, being always solicitous in admonishing.

VERS. 14.-- *The putting off of my tabernacle. [n: (BEDA.)]* Beautifully he calls it not death, but a putting off, because it is so for the servants of God to pluck out the bonds of the flesh, as it is for travellers, the journey finished, to go to their own home instead of the dwelling of a tent; as it is for men set on campaign, the enemy laid low, to return to their fatherland.

VERS. 15.-- *But I will give diligence.* Because I am soon to pass over, therefore meanwhile, while I live, I will give diligence that you may frequently, namely as often as the false ones shall press upon you and shall wish to draw you away, have these things in memory [0690D] after my decease, when you shall not have me present as a teacher; not to have them in any manner whatever, but so that you may make a memorial of them by fulfilling them in work, and by teaching others.

*After the decease,* etc. The decease of the saints is so called when they go forth to meet the bridegroom.

VERS. 16.-- *For not unlearned fables,* [var: Vulg. *doctas fabulas*, "learned fables"] etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Here he touches both the pagans and the heretics. The pagans feared not to call gods whatsoever delighted them. The heretics, having received the mysteries of the true God, thereafter were zealous not to attend to the divine [0691A] Scriptures, but rather to transfer them by ill interpretation to an erroneous sense.

*But made beholders,* etc. The transfiguration of the Lord and his brightness was a sign of his resurrection and of ours. By Elias, who appeared with him, is understood the resurrection of those who were before the law. By Moses, that of those who were under the law; by Peter, and James, and John, that of those who are under grace. Or, by Moses dead, that of the dead; by the living, that of the living.

VERS. 19.-- *And we have the more firm.* What I have said to you is not fabulous, but certain, and not only declared by these signs, but also for the confirmation of our preaching we have the prophetic word of David: *The Lord said to me, Thou art my Son [n: (Psal. II)]*, etc., more firm among the other prophets, [0691B] because he prophesied of Christ more clearly than the others.

*Whereunto you do well to attend. [n: (BEDA.)]* That you may be able to have the light of knowledge, etc., *as far as* in comparison with the life to come we have need of a lamp.

VERS. 20.-- *Understanding this first.* Attend to the prophetic word, for it is from God. Which can be proved by this, that prophecy is not written in such words and in such a manner of speech as men use in their own speech, and as secular writings are composed and interpreted. And truly the utterance of the prophets is not according to their own will, or that of the hearers, but according to the will of the Holy Spirit who was speaking in them.

VERS. 21.-- *For not by human will. [n: (BEDA.)]* As it was not in the power of the prophets always to have the [0691C] spirit, always to foretell things to come: so it was not of their power that they should teach whatsoever things they would, but they spoke those things alone which they had learned from the spirit. These things are therefore said, lest anyone expound the Scriptures according to his own pleasure.
