## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- *Perdition.* Deservedly do they bring perdition upon themselves who deny their Redeemer, or who preach him not such as the truth shows him, but such as they themselves feign him to be. And therefore, being strangers to the Redeemer, they look for nothing more certain than the pit.

VERS. 2.-- *The way of truth shall be blasphemed. [n: (BEDA.)]* Through the heretics the way of truth is wont to be blasphemed, not only in those whom, seduced, he associates to his own heresy, but in those also whom, through their most impure [0691D] deeds and the execrable sacrifices or mysteries which they perform, they stir up into hatred of the Christian name, while the unskilled suppose that all Christians are given over to villainies of this kind.

VERS. 4.-- *For if God spared not the angels. [n: (ID.)]* If God [spared not] the apostate angels, delivered over to the punishments of hell, etc., *as far as* they were hurried away into the depths of the abyss.

VERS. 5.-- *To the [sic: Or ginali] world. [n: (ID.)]* The world after the flood is the same which was before it also, etc., *as far as* the just shall receive the glory of [sic: *virae*].

VERS. 6.-- *And the cities.* These cities were in a twofold manner brought into fire, because first he reduced them to ashes by burning, together with the adjacent lands, [0692A] and when he had afterwards covered the places of the burning with the waves of the Dead Sea, he willed the surrounding region still to preserve the appearance of the ancient punishment. For there grow most beautiful fruits, which beget also in those who look upon them a desire of eating. If thou pluck them, they crumble and are dissolved into ashes, and stir up smoke as though they still burned. *Into ashes.* The fire which once punished the Sodomites signifies that the ungodly are to suffer without end. That the earth is smoking, that the most beautiful fruits have ashes within and a stench, intimates that carnal love, though it smile upon fools, yet they reserve to themselves nothing among things invisible but burning, or that the smoke of their torments may go up for ever and ever.

VERS. 7.-- *Lot oppressed. [n: (BEDA.)]* The unjust deeds and words of his neighbours tormented the just man, which, beholding, [0692B] he was not able to correct; but he himself kept himself in good works. Nor did he darken himself by the villainies seen or heard. He is likewise called just in seeing, or in hearing; because they saw nothing in him, they heard nothing of him, save what pertained to justice.

VERS. 9.-- *The unjust.* The false ones and their followers; and therefore do not follow them.

*Unto the day of judgment. [n: (BEDA.)]* Both before the day of judgment, loosed from the body, they pay their penalties, and in the judgment they look for heavier things, when they shall be tormented in body and in soul.

VERS. 11.-- *Where the angels.* There shall the unjust be tormented, where the evil angels, who before had been proud against God, and were punished thereupon, now bear not, but rather cast away, the judgment of being proud against God: which is execrable against themselves, because for it they are grievously [0692C] punished, and, terrified by the greatness of the punishments, they now desist from that judgment, to presumption [cj: *praesumptioni*; read *praesumptionis*, "of presumption"]: but these unjust ones do not yet desist. Or they shall be tortured there, that is, with God: where the angels, not good ones [cj: *non boni*; read *boni*, "the good angels"], endure not, but avenge, if anything execrable be said against them.

VERS. 13.-- *The reward of injustice. [n: (BEDA.)]* That is, punishment for evil works; and chiefly those who, while they serve the corruption of the flesh and are held by mad errors, blaspheme the conversation of the just, and cease not to detract from those who are of sound understanding.

VERS. 17.-- *These are they.* The false apostles are not to be imitated, because although they may seem useful, yet there is nothing in them but vanity and uncleanness.

VERS. 18.-- *Proud things.* Proudly saying that they are just, although it be vain, desiring carnally [0692D] and living luxuriously, they allure those who have fled a little from sins; who, though they are like unto them, yet in some measure decline their fellowship, and in some measure abstain from sins. *In the desires of the flesh,* etc. Every one is unclean who exalteth his heart, so that they who have fled a little from sins return to their own error and are zealous in luxury.

VERS. 20.-- *For if,* etc., *the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ,* etc. If, fleeing the defilement both of soul and of body which the world sends in; being again entangled (as before baptism) they are overcome by those defilements, it appears that their latter things are made worse than the former: because they offend God more than before baptism, [0693A] and they shall suffer greater punishments, because the promises made in baptism are lying ones.

VERS. 22.-- *For it hath befallen them. [n: (BEDA.)]* In the Proverbs it is set down with an exposition, etc., *as far as* it makes even those tears themselves foul before the eyes of God.

## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- *Behold, this to you.* As if he should say: I have already written another Epistle for the driving back of the false ones; and that I may show you my great solicitude, behold, for the same cause I write this second Epistle also.

VERS. 2.-- *That you may be mindful.* That is, that through those Epistles you may be mindful of those words which I myself, being present, foretold to you. *Of the words,* I say, received, [0693B] *from the holy prophets* which are confirmed by the authority of the holy prophets; and that you may be mindful of the precepts of the holy *apostles:* lest you let go the precepts which the holy apostles gave you in exchange for the preaching of those false ones; and which *precepts* are also *of the Lord and Saviour* because the Lord himself, who came to save us, appointed these precepts and commanded them to be kept.

VERS. 5.-- *It is hidden. [n: (BEDA.)]* Therefore they say that all things continue in the same manner, etc., *as far as* unto the day of judgment and of the perdition of ungodly men.

VERS. 8.-- *But this one thing.* It being proved that the judgment shall truly be, he shows how effectual it shall be [sic: *futurem*]. If it be said to you by the false ones: You can do what you will, because so great a time remains until the day of judgment, [0693C] that from the length of time the judge will not be able to know many things; believe them not, because whatsoever can be done in a thousand years, he sees it even as that which is done in one day. If they say: In a thousand years, by repenting, you shall not obtain pardon of your offences; believe them not: because one day of true penitence avails as much unto salvation as a thousand years, as it did for the thief.

*As a thousand years. [n: (BEDA.)]* In the acknowledgment of the divine power, etc., *as far as* whence also it is rightly subjoined: [0694A] VERS. 9.-- *The Lord delayeth not.* And because he sees all things, you may believe that the Lord delays not in fulfilling his promises, because the time is not yet in which he shall fulfil the things to be fulfilled. But he therefore defers, that he may first fill up the sum of the elect which he foresaw; whence in the Apocalypse: *And it was said to them that they should rest yet a little time, until their fellow servants be filled up [n: (Apoc. VI)]*.

VERS. 10.-- *The heavens with a great violence. The heavens,* that is, the saints; if therefore there be anything in them to be purged, it shall be purged by fire.

*The elements. [n: (BEDA.)]* [sic: *Quantor*], of which the world consists, etc., *as far as* and through a temporal punishment, they shall be dissolved into something better.

VERS. 13.-- *But new heavens. [n: (ID.)]* He saith not, [0694B] *others,* but that the old and ancient ones are to be changed into something better, etc., *as far as* we have it also in the prophets: *The light of the lamp shall shine there no more [n: (Isa. LXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Apoc. XVIII, 23 *lux lucernae non lucebit in te amplius*, "in thee"]

VERS. 14.-- *Be diligent to be unspotted and undefiled.* These are the watchings of which it is said: *Blessed are those servants whom the Lord, when he cometh, shall find watching.* He watches who keeps himself free from defilements, who, as much as in him lies, has peace with all men, who subjects all the allurements of the flesh to the governance of the spirit, enjoying in himself a most happy peace.

VERS. 15.-- *Our brother. [n: (BEDA.)]* He says that Paul had written to them, wherein he shows, etc., *as far as* and by these may stir themselves up unto advancement.

VERS. 16.-- *Difficult things.* In the Epistles of Paul, which are perverted by the heretics, etc., *as far as* certain men loosing the [0694C] reins to luxury, and many other things, etc.

*They pervert. [n: (BEDA.)]* There is no book of the Old or of the New Testament in which the heretics have not perverted something, either by taking away, or by adding, or by changing. The one remedy for the unlearned is, with humble steadfastness, to lend an ear to the words of the doctors; which steadfastness because the heretics have not, they are lifted out of the Church by the wind of pride like light chaff, unto their own perdition.
