## CHAPTER II.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *But I determined.* ⟦s2⟧ Sparing you I came not, *but I determined this same thing:* which I promised when I sent the first epistle, namely *that I would not come again:* or else there would again be sorrow as there was, their sins having been heard.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For if I make you sorrowful.* ⟦s2⟧ By correcting, by rebuking in whatsoever manner, either by epistle or by presence.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Not that you should be made sorrowful.* ⟦s2⟧ For to this end did the Apostle rebuke, that he might show with what love he loved them [0553B] over whose sins he grieved more than they did. ⟦s2⟧ He who rebukes not with this affection makes sorrowful. ⟦s3⟧ For he insults, who does not grieve with his brother.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *But if any one hath caused grief.* ⟦s2⟧ He commands that the fornicator, whom before he had declared must be delivered over to Satan, be received back, because he had been corrected. ⟦s3⟧ *But in part, that I may not burden,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ I lay the burden of sorrow not upon all, on account of those who have grieved for their sins.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *This rebuke, which is made by many, so that on the contrary rather.* ⟦s2⟧ Which, although in respect of the length of time or in some other manner it seem a punishment not sufficiently worthy, yet in this it is not to be esteemed small, that it is made by many, at whose gathering he was delivered over to Satan. ⟦s3⟧ Surely he suffers great grief who sees many abhor his offence. ⟦s4⟧ And so it suffices, that by many [0553C] he be rebuked.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *You should forgive and console.* ⟦s2⟧ Rightly does he command that a man afflicted for his sin be pardoned and be helped by consolations. ⟦s3⟧ For if, being corrected, he grieves in mind, he presently has the fruit. ⟦s4⟧ For this is true penance, to cease from sin.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *For to this end also did I write.* ⟦s2⟧ Since I have written for the receiving back of that man, for this also I wrote, that those whom I have found obedient to me in the casting out, I may know whether they will be obedient in all things.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *That we be not overreached.* ⟦s2⟧ Therefore we ought to forgive, lest we be deceived by Satan through too great harshness. ⟦s3⟧ For often, because of harsher censures, the weak man is cast headlong into despair.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0553D]VERS. 12.-- *And when I was come to Troas,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ After the interposition concerning the receiving back of the fornicator, he returns to them, showing that, as it was their fault that he came not to Corinth, so too that he did not advance at Troas, while Titus, hindered among them, was delayed, without whom he could not advance there, because perhaps Titus could use their tongue more expressly.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Because I found not Titus my brother.* ⟦s2⟧ Because being alone he could not instruct the faithful, and at the same time withstand the unbelievers making their din.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The odour of his knowledge,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, Christ, who, as an odour proceeding from the Father, makes known him who is invisible: as some thing which is not seen is known by its odour, and it is understood in what place it is. [0554A] Or understand by the name of odour either miracles or the words of doctrine by which men come to the knowledge of him.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *A good odour.* ⟦s2⟧ Namely, as of old the victim of the law, so now the true preaching and sincere report of the apostles. ⟦s3⟧ The odour of God the Father is Christ: the odour of Christ are the apostles, in whose life and preaching Christ is shown forth, not blasphemed. ⟦s4⟧ As it is said of the wicked: *The name of the Lord is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you.*

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The odour of death.* ⟦s2⟧ So that from our preaching death proceeds, to those who thereby rush into eternal damnation.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *As very many.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he touches the false apostles, who by corrupt teaching violated the truth, of whom Isaias says: *Thy tavern-keepers mingle water with the wine.* [var: Vulg. Is. I, 22 *vinum tuum mistum est aqua*, "thy wine is mingled with water," with no tavern-keepers]
