## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 2.-- *Commending.* That your light may shine before men. *Before God.* Not that they may be seen by men.

VERS. 4.-- *In whom.* The order of the words can be such: In whom that perish, the true and just God hath blinded the minds of the unbelievers of this world.

*The god of this world.* That is, God who not only rules the good, but also casts down the evil according to their desert, giving them what they will, that is, that they should not believe: which malice does not do, but justice. Or, the god of this world is pride or gluttony, or the devil the prince of the world, who over worldly men [0556B] bears rule.

VERS. 5.-- *For we preach not ourselves.* As if he should say: I speak well unto the glory of Christ, or: We have commended ourselves before God in the manifestation of the truth, and we do other such things; and this for Christ's sake.

VERS. 6.-- *Light to shine.* Which was in the separation of the elements. For darkness began from the time when the confused mass of heaven and earth began to be: afterwards, the light being added, which was done; it is rendered better. In which the disposition of the man who makes progress is signified, whence *you were heretofore darkness, but now light in the Lord [n: (Ephes. V)]*. *In the face of Jesus.* It has this sense: Since the divine nature falls not under sight, it is beheld, so far as is permitted, through the assumed humanity that shines back with the divine light and sends forth lightnings. And hence [0556C] it is also manifest that God did not send in unbelief, who has abundantly furnished to all the splendour of the light that gives understanding: but they indeed loved unbelief, while he, when they would not look, did not furnish them the ray. *Of the knowledge of the brightness.* The knowledge of the glory of God is that whereby we know that there is a light by which our darkness is enlightened.

VERS. 7.-- *We have.* Thus far he has treated of the height of knowledge: here of the frailty of the flesh, which is unto glory, although it seem the contrary. *A treasure.* The mystery of God, which is dispensed to the faithful, is hidden from the faithless; he affirms it to be a treasure.

VERS. 10.-- *Always the mortification,* etc. There is no doubt that in the martyrs Christ is slain, and that in [0556D] those who suffer for the faith, whether departures, or bonds, or stripes, are the passions of Christ, that his life also may be made manifest in their body. For the sufferings are they which show the merit unto the life to come, which Christ promised, whence in another place he says: *When I am weak, then am I powerful,* and again: *Through tribulations,* etc. *May be made manifest.* For certain men doubted concerning the resurrection.

VERS. 12.-- *Therefore death,* etc. Because through death we obtain the life of Jesus: therefore death is not useless, but works something great in us. But the life wherein you delight in earthly things works in you eternal death. Or 'therefore' passively, that is, for the hope of the life of Jesus death is wrought, that is, by the aforesaid sufferings [0557A] it is effected in us, that is, we permit it to be effected: but through this life is effected in you, that is, faith is planted.

VERS. 14.-- *With you.* Not you with us, as inferiors, but that you may be our equals, if you will. And I well affirm concerning you, that we do all these things for the instructing of you by our example. So all things look to you: that the grace of God abounding in us may abound in you, and through you in others, and through you many it may abound in thanksgiving, that both you and they may give thanks to God. Unto the glory of God, that is, to show God glorious.
