## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- *For the rest, brethren, pray.* He exhorts that prayer must be made, that God may vouchsafe to direct his doctrine with unwearied course and to pour it through the mouths of the apostles into the ears of the hearers, and from the ears into the hearts, that the seditions of the wicked may be curbed.

VERS. 2.-- *That we may be delivered.* From the false ones, lest they seduce ours, or lay snares for us. Or, that we may be delivered from importunate men, that is, from unbelievers who resist the word of God, and stand not in the haven of quiet. *And from wicked men,* who, being unbelievers, persecute. Therefore I say, *that we may be delivered,* because not yet [0623C] have all received the faith through me who are to receive it. *For faith is not of all men.* That is, not even with you praying will all believe, but only those foreordained unto the adoption of sons.

VERS. 6.-- *We charge.* He begins concerning the curious and the idle, admonishing the good to correct them.

VERS. 7.-- *You know how you ought to imitate us.* [var: Vulg. *quemadmodum oporteat imitari nos*, "how you ought to imitate us"] And since they do not these things, you ought to shun them. Or, [0624A] they walk not according to the tradition. For you know how I bore myself, which you do not see in them; as if he should say: There is no need that I should expound to you that tradition, because you know it, namely that we were not restless, as those are who mind other men's business, wandering hither and thither.

VERS. 9.-- *But that we ourselves.* It is to be noted that the Apostle taught not by words only, but exhorted also by deeds. For it belongs to a fit master to fulfil in works the things which he teaches in words.

VERS. 10.-- *For if any man will not work.* Certain men say that the Apostle enjoined this concerning spiritual works; otherwise, if he said this of bodily work, in which either husbandmen or craftsmen labour, he would seem to hold an opinion against the Lord, who says in the Gospel: *Be not solicitous, what you shall eat* [0624B] [n: (Matth. VI)], etc. But to no purpose do they strive to draw a mist over both themselves and others, so that they not only wish [sic: *velint*] to do what charity profitably admonishes, but not even to understand it, since the Apostle in many other places of the epistles most plainly teaches what he here means. For he wills that the servants of God work bodily whereby they may live, that they be not compelled by want to beg for necessaries. Nor is this against that which was said before: *Be not solicitous what you shall eat,* etc. For this was not said to this end, that they should not procure as much as is enough for necessity, from whence they honourably could, but not in order that [sic: *sed non ut*] they may look upon these things, nor do for the sake of these things whatever they are bidden to do by the proclamation of the Gospel.

VERS. 11.-- *Curiously meddling.* The Lord's discipline abhors this deed, for their god is the belly, [0624C] who with foul care provide necessaries for themselves.

VERS. 13.-- *Faint not.* Because although they work, yet they may want some things. And therefore he admonishes, lest those who had wherewith to supply necessaries to the servants of God should on this occasion grow slothful. For he does not come under reproof who is kindly in bestowing, but he who, when he could bear labour, wishes to lead his life in idleness.
