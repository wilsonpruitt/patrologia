## THE ARGUMENT.

[0633A]

Likewise he writes to Timothy concerning the exhortation to martyrdom, and concerning the whole rule of truth, and what shall come to pass in the last times, and concerning his own passion, writing to him from the city of Rome.

## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *Paul an apostle.* First the Apostle salutes, then he gives thanks for the good which he has, where he shows his own desire of seeing him. Afterwards he admonishes him to preaching, and to the patience of martyrdom, by his own example and in other ways. Thence he says what sort of men they shall be in the last days: at last, concerning the time of his own release, which is at hand. *By the will of God.* Not by my merits, as if he should say: In like manner thou also hast been sent freely, [0633B] and therefore art the more bound to the grace that is to be preached.

VERS. 3.-- *I give thanks.* He gives thanks for his [Timothy's] good, commending the grace of faith which is in Christ.

*Remembrance.* For Timothy wept when he was dismissed by Paul, being ready to go with him unto all perils. What therefore he had while present, let him hold while absent, *receiving also the recollection of the faith* [var: Vulg. *recordationem accipiens ejus fidei*, "receiving the recollection of that faith"] which may admonish thee, since it was firm even in women.

VERS. 6.-- *For which cause.* His faith having been commended, he exhorts him to preach it without fear and without shame. *By the imposition of hands.* That is, the grace of the Holy Spirit, which thou hast received for the establishing of the Church, for the accomplishing of miracles, for every observance of religion.

[0633C]VERS. 7.-- *Of fear.* Peter, being troubled by fear, denied Christ. Whence: *Fear ye not them that kill the body [n: (Matth. XXVI.)]* Of this fear we have not received the spirit, but of that fear of which the Lord says: *Fear him who is able to destroy both body and soul into hell.* For the Holy Spirit gives a fear not of the world, but of God, who is able to destroy into the hell of fire.

VERS. 8.-- *Be not thou therefore.* He teaches that constancy is to be had, and that there is no shame to be felt in the profession: for it is not such that one should be ashamed in it: because as Christ was seen a man, yet by his deeds he appeared God; although he was crucified, yet he rose again from the dead, and, while many beheld it and a cloud received him, ascended [0633D] into heaven. Where therefore weakness is supposed, there power appears.

VERS. 9.-- *Who hath delivered us.* It is fitting to render a return to him who delivers: And since we are not able worthily to answer his benefits, let us at least perform his embassy faithfully and urgently.

VERS. 10.-- *Who hath destroyed.* Death, as being conquered, is not to be feared for the faith of Christ. *But hath illuminated.* Whither these things look, he there opens: *Having a form. Through the Gospel.* [var: Vulg. 2 Tim. I, 13 *Formam habe*, imperative, "hold thou the form"] God showed forth incorruptible life, [0634A] which before was unknown; he showed it, I say, through the preaching of the Gospel, whose preacher is Paul.

VERS. 11.-- *Wherein I am appointed.* Here he exhorts by his own example. *A preacher, and an apostle, and a teacher,* etc. But what is the reason, since he continually calls himself the teacher of the Gentiles? he wishes, forsooth, to persuade that the Gentiles also have been called into the inheritance of God, and therefore are not to be driven back from the fellowship of the faith.

VERS. 12.-- *For I know.* As if he should say: Therefore *I am not confounded,* that is, I fail, or I do not suffer in vain, because he has promised and is truthful and powerful: and therefore I have believed that very one who will render to me. *Because he is able.* He is secure through the hope and the magnificence of the Saviour, because what he commends to him is in safety. And to him he commends his own salvation, that here suffering for him he may find salvation [0634B] with him when he shall have begun to judge, so that, punishing the unfaithful, he may pronounce this man worthy of eternal life.

VERS. 13.-- *Having a form.* Here he shows whither the things foretold about himself look. As if he should say: Thus I labour in this office committed to me: in like manner therefore thou also, having a form, etc.

*Of whom is Phigellus and Hermogenes.* These were with the Apostle feignedly, that they might find matter of calumny: but after they saw themselves made manifest, they withdrew from him.
