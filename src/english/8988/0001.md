## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 4.-- *No man being a soldier.* As if he should say: If thou wilt be a good soldier, do not entangle thyself in secular affairs, because no man, etc. *With affairs.* Secular affairs are when the mind is occupied with the care of gathering money [0634C] without labour of the body, as merchants and men of this sort: and lest this man should do that, because being weak he was not able to labour, he forbids it.

VERS. 6.-- *The husbandman that laboureth.* As if Timothy should ask: Whence then shall I live, since I cannot dig, and to beg I am ashamed? he subjoins *the husbandman that laboureth.* As if he should say, although thou dost not entangle thyself in secular affairs, yet there is whence thou mayest live: *Because it behoveth,* etc. *First to partake.* That is, to take. *Of the fruits.* That is, the necessaries from his hearers, because the labourer is worthy of his hire; as the husbandman or the vinedresser tastes the first fruits of his own labour, so the Apostle bids him who is first to take first, and so to distribute to the rest. For he ought first to fulfil his own need out of those things which he has received [cj: *qui*; read *quae*, "those things which"; the printed *qui* construes instead as "he who has received"], and afterwards [0634D] to minister to others out of those which remain over.

VERS. 8.-- *Be mindful.* Labour as I do, and this in the hope of eternal life. But he calls these things to mind not for Timothy's sake, but for the sake of the heretics whom he foresaw would come and would deny these things.

VERS. 9.-- *As an evildoer.* Here he notes the contumely which was heaped upon him. *But the word of God is not bound.* As if he should say: Although therefore I am bound in body, yet the word of God is not bound: I who [0635A] cease not to teach both those present by speech and those absent by letters.

VERS. 10.-- *Salvation,* etc., *with glory,* etc. As if he should say: You have been justified that here you may obtain salvation, and, after this life, heavenly glory.

VERS. 11.-- *A faithful saying.* It is this, that they shall obtain salvation and glory, for we are dead to the world, as Christ to the old man, and enduring, that is, martyrs.

VERS. 14.-- *Put them in mind of these things.* That thou mayest suffer these things foretold, I put thee in mind. Or, put thine own in mind of these things. *For it is profitable to nothing.* Contention, being less stable, often begets scruple. For in contention such things are wont to be objected, the argument of ill-will being polished, that they may stir the minds of unwise brethren. Nor can it [0635B] be but that contention wrings out something that is spoken against conscience, so that within, in the mind, he loses, and outwardly comes off victor. For no one suffers himself to be overcome, although he knows that the things he hears are true. There ought therefore to be conference among the servants of God, not altercation.

VERS. 17.-- *Of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus.* He gave out their names, that [Timothy] might beware of them spiritually, whom he designates both profane and impious and erring from the truth.

VERS. 18.-- *Saying that the resurrection is already past.* There are two resurrections. The first is that which takes place in the soul through faith, when he who was dead in unbelief and in other sins is made alive through faith and baptism. The other, which shall be of bodies, which shall be in the day of judgment. Of that resurrection which takes place in the soul through faith the Apostle here speaks, [0635C] when he says: *Saying that the resurrection,* namely of minds, *hath been made* in baptism and in faith, and that there shall be no resurrection of bodies to come. All sects which promise religion concede a resurrection of minds in faith. For otherwise they would not seem to be worth hearing. Therefore all, even those who founded the sect of some false religion, wishing to be believed, could not deny that resurrection of minds; all agreed about it. But many denied the resurrection of bodies, as do these whom the Apostle names.

VERS. 19.-- *The Lord knoweth who are his,* etc. That is, he loves and defends his own foreknown ones. In this is noted foreknowledge, by which those whom he foreknew would be conformable to the image of his Son, that is, he predestinated, [0635D] called, justified, glorified.

VERS. 20.-- *Vessels of gold and of silver.* For as wooden and earthen vessels avail that the golden and the silver ones may be cleansed, so the wicked are of profit toward the advancement of the good. The great house therefore is the Church, in which are vessels of gold and of silver, that is, the good and faithful and holy servants of God scattered everywhere and bound fast by spiritual unity, dwelling in the same communion of the sacraments. And wooden and earthen vessels, that is, those who are in the house in such wise that they are not in the framework of the house, nor in the peaceable fellowship, and yet are still bodily together with the good.

VERS. 23.-- *But avoid foolish questions and those without discipline.* Not all questions are to be avoided, but the unprofitable [sic: *mutiles*] ones and those without discipline.

[0636A]VERS. 24.-- *But the servant of the Lord must not wrangle, but be mild toward all.* They that contradict make clamour enough. Let not therefore the servant of God clamour against himself by the zeal of altercation. He willed that we should not be wanting to the correction of brethren, nor be eager for strife. For many men, when they are roused from sleep, wish to wrangle, [sic: *aurursus*] or again to sleep, when they are forbidden to wrangle.

VERS. 25.-- *Lest at any time God give them repentance to know the truth.* Correction profits nothing, unless the physician from above look upon him, that is, unless he bring it about that each man repent of his own sin. Which repentance is sometimes hindered by shame and by the fear of displeasing others, while the esteem of others delights a man more; than justice, by which each man humbles himself in repenting. And therefore not only when repentance is performed, [0636B] but also that it may be performed, the mercy of God is necessary. For that Peter might weep bitterly, the Lord looked upon him. And therefore the Apostle here says: *May God give them repentance [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*.

## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 2.-- *Lovers of themselves.* Not of the Lord, as Peter was, who being thrice questioned by the Lord thrice professed that he loved him, saying: *Thou knowest that I love thee.* Whence also the Lord entrusted to him the sheep to be fed: because in the feeding of sheep true love is shown, if a man seek not there his own things, but the things which are Christ's. And from this, that they love themselves and not God, as from a root, arise these evils which follow. *Ungrateful.* They are ungrateful who render evil things for good [0636C] to those who correct them.

VERS. 5.-- *Having an appearance.* Because they have the same sacraments as the godly. *But the power.* The power is charity out of a pure heart, a good conscience, and faith unfeigned.

VERS. 6.-- *Who penetrate.* For they entered the houses of women, and deceived them.

VERS. 7.-- *Ever learning, and never unto knowledge,* etc. But let us always walk in the way until we come thither whither the way leads; let us nowhere remain in it, until it bring us through to where we may remain. And thus both by seeking we press on, and by finding we arrive at something.

VERS. 8.-- *Now as Jannes,* etc. As if he should say: Such men seduce: but they always [0636D] resist the truth. And this is what he says: *Now as Jannes,* etc. Jannes and Mambres, two brothers, were the magicians of Pharao. Their names the Apostle did not find in the divine books, but in the apocrypha, from which this is taken. And these resisted Moses as far as the third sign, in which they failed, saying: *This is the finger of God.* And as they resisted Moses, so these, namely the heretics, resist me.

VERS. 13.-- *But evil men,* etc. Such are they who fear to hurt those before whom they speak, who do not prepare men for temptations, but promise the felicity of this world, which God himself promised us to the world [cj: *nos*; read *non*, "which God himself did not promise to the world"]. He foretells labours that shall come upon the world unto the end: thou, prosperous things. He, to strengthen thy heart, [0637A] came to suffer, to die, to be smeared with spittle, to be crowned with thorns, to bear reproaches, to drink gall and vinegar, to be fastened to the wood. All these things he bore for thee. He strengthens the weak, that when he has believed he may not hope for the prosperous things of this world; whence: *Son, when thou comest to the service of God, stand in justice and in fear, and prepare thy soul for temptations.*

VERS. 15.-- *Thou hast known the holy scriptures which thee,* etc. He signifies that it profits much to have knowledge of the Old Testament, where the person of Christ and the incarnation is intimated, which avails unto the salvation of men.
