## THE ARGUMENT.

[0609A]

The Colossians, like the Laodiceans also, are Asians, and they too had been forestalled by false apostles, nor did the Apostle himself come to them, but he corrects them by an epistle. For they had heard the word from Archippus, who also received a ministry among them. Therefore the Apostle, now bound, writes to them from Ephesus by Tichicus the deacon and Onesimus the acolyte.

## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *Paul.* After his custom he sets the salutation first. This being set first, he gives thanks for their good things, and, commending their faith and love, he prays that they may be made perfect in Christ, whose benefits and primacy he commends according to both natures; then, calling to mind the dignity of his own [0609B] ministry, he admonishes them not to withdraw from Christ, seduced through prophecy or the ceremonies of the law; last of all he instructs all together, morally, according to sexes and ages and conditions, and at the end he warns Archippus that he is to be careful of the ministry he has undertaken. *An apostle,* etc. Note that to all in dignity he here rightly names himself an apostle, because he was an apostle to these also, to whom he had preached through his own disciples.

VERS. 3.-- *We give thanks.* He shows the good things which they received, while he gives thanks for them.

VERS. 6.-- *As,* etc. That is, so purely and truly as it came through me and others into the whole world, and still remains in it, as if he should say: Not less has been done for you than in the other churches. Since the Gospel is in the world, it bears fruit there through good works, and [0609C] grows by the increase of knowledge and by the number of the faithful. Not by this do they have more than you, but God does this in the same manner as it is among you, and therefore you ought to add nothing further.

VERS. 7.-- *For you,* etc. Desiring whose salvation, he began to help Archippus.

VERS. 8.-- *In the Spirit.* Because you love for spiritual things, not for carnal.

VERS. 9.-- *That you may be filled in all.* It is a great thing to know fully what God wills, in all the matters of the active and the contemplative life.

VERS. 10.-- *In knowledge.* That they may not be ignorant of the form of their faith, that is, of the thing hoped for, whence they will be firm.

VERS. 11.-- *In all virtue.* You, I say, strengthened by God in all virtue, namely chastity and the rest. [0609D] Which he can well do who is the brightness of the Father, that is, the Son. Or according as he has given you clear knowledge.

VERS. 12.-- *Giving thanks.* Now he begins to say that the law does not profit but harms, and that Christ suffices for all good things: which can be known from this, that he himself and the other apostles (who had been in the observances of the law), having dismissed them, fled for refuge to Christ: As if he should say: We pray for you, giving thanks to God, for this reason, because he makes us worthy, which the law never could.

[0610A] *He has made us worthy.* Not in the law, but *in the light,* that is, through God who is light of light, by whose grace we are illumined. Or *in the light,* that is, in clear knowledge, from which he who turns aside falls into darkness. *Of the lot.* The will of God is the lot in the human race, with whom there is no iniquity, but he does all things by justice, though a hidden one.

VERS. 13.-- *He has snatched us from the power.* That is, from hell, in which we were held by the devil: as much from our own transgression as from Adam's. *And has translated,* etc. When, raising us from the depth of Tartarus, he brought us into heaven with his only Son. For now believers, who are devoted with fixed mind, going forth from the world, are brought into heaven with an angel of the Father's right hand for their guide. *The kingdom of the Son.* Of which he himself says: *My kingdom is not of this world;* [0610B] he did not say: It is not here, but, *it is not hence,* because he does indeed sojourn in the world, but is not of the world. For to his own kingdom he says, *You are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world.* They were of the world, when they belonged to the prince of the world.

VERS. 14.-- *Redemption.* The power of the devil being destroyed, the means of liberty was given to us by the pouring out of the blood of him who had no sin. So that, because the devil justly held those whom, guilty of sin, he had bound by the condition of death, he might justly let these go through him whom, guilty of no sin, the punishment of death undeservedly visited.

VERS. 15.-- *Who is the image.* In one way the image of a king is in a coin, in another in a son. We are the coin, in whom the image of God is Christ the Son. No image of God [0610C] ought to be worshipped, save that which is the very thing that he himself is, nor is that image to be worshipped instead of him, but with him.

VERS. 18.-- *The head of the body, the Church.* Because he stands to the Church as the head to the body. And since the Church began from Abel, how is he their head? Rightly, because he is the beginning of the Church, that is, its founder. *Who is the beginning.* Of the Church according to the divinity, that is, the founder of the Church, because he has illumined all the just, who from Abel as far as the last just man are begotten, by the power of his divinity and by the gift of his mercy. According to the humanity also he can be called the beginning of the Church, because it is founded upon the faith of his humanity. And rightly is he himself head and beginning, and ruler, and founder, because he himself is the firstborn, etc. *The firstborn,* etc. As he was born before all things of [0610D] God, that he might create all things, so as man he first rises again, that he may restore, that he may always be first and chief.

VERS. 19.-- *The fulness.* Because he can do all things of himself; whence: *As the Father has life in himself, so also the Son, who raises up and quickens whom he will,* [var: Vulg. John 5:26 *Sicut enim Pater habet vitam in semetipso, sic dedit et Filio habere vitam in semetipso*, "as the Father hath life in himself, so he hath given to the Son also to have life in himself"; the second half here conflates John 5:21 *sic et Filius quos vult vivificat*] thus he is shown to be perfect God.

VERS. 20.-- *To reconcile through him.* It pleased God to reconcile through him, and he himself made peace not gratuitously, but through blood, things heavenly and things earthly, whence entrance into heaven lies open [0611A] to man. *All things,* made through him, live in him their author.

VERS. 21.-- *Alienated and enemies* to his council, which comes to the same thing, because they did not receive the things which God commanded through Moses, inasmuch as they were devoted to their own idols.

VERS. 22.-- *He has reconciled in the body.* If, mortal and dead, he was able to reconcile, now, immortal, he can do all things.

VERS. 23.-- *If yet you continue.* Therefore he had said so many and so great things about Christ, that they themselves might place their hope in him alone, in whom are all things; that they might not think themselves to be made subject to any of the elements or of the angels: he alone is to be worshipped, and no one is to be reckoned in comparison with him. *In all.* Which new creature is now under heaven, yet is still a pilgrim, but nevertheless will come to [0611B] glory.

VERS. 24.-- *I fill up.* Those of the sufferings which Christ endured, or which he has now commanded to be endured, which are still lacking, I fill up, that is, I am ready to suffer.

VERS. 25.-- *I am made.* He says that he was made a minister of the Gospel by God through Christ, that the ministry begun by God might be fulfilled through a fit servant, which was unknown from the ages. *It has been given to me.* That I may fulfil what God preordained concerning you, namely that you should be saved through the incarnation. This he says, lest salvation should seem not to have been promised to the Gentiles. Or, *to me it has been given among you,* [var: Vulg. *quae data est mihi in vos*, accusative, "which was given me toward you"] for that which is sought from my own is as much as if it were sought from myself.

VERS. 28, 29.-- *Rebuking.* We rebuke the gainsayers [0611C] and overcome them, and we teach the obedient. *In all wisdom.* True wisdom is when Christ is acknowledged. *That we may present,* etc. *In which.* As if he should say: To my labour God joins miracles for confirmation.
