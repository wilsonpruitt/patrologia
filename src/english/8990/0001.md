## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- *I will,* etc. There follows: *For you.* For the Apostle was not less solicitous for those whom he had not seen than for those whom he had seen.

VERS. 2.-- *Instructed in charity.* Which God had toward us, who for us delivered up his Son. *Unto all the riches.* That is, the abundance of full understanding, where they may perfectly have understanding of things human and divine, as of the soul and of the supernal spirits. For otherwise no one ascends to the knowledge of God.

[0611D] *Of God the Father,* etc. That you may know that the Father is another person from the Son, although the same in substance with him. If you strive toward knowledge, have recourse to Christ alone, because in him are all the riches, but hidden, and therefore do not wonder if you are in Christ and have not yet found them, because they lie open not to all, but to those who ask and knock.

VERS. 4.-- *And this.* I say not only that you may strive toward those things which you do not yet have, but that no one may deceive, and by reason of worldly things draw away from the hope which is in Christ.

VERS. 5.-- *But also in spirit.* To the saints it is given to see distant things in the spirit. Let them therefore beware for themselves whose deeds are not hidden from the master.

[0612A] *Rejoicing.* By this he makes them more ready about the Gospel, because he says that he rejoices in the ordering of their conversation and in the stability of their faith, so that, knowing whence they please God, they might become firmer in it.

VERS. 7.-- *Rooted.* The lesser ones in the active life. *Above* others *built up in him.* That is, you greater ones who are in contemplation.

VERS. 8.-- *Deceit.* What the false preachers say about the law. *And vain.* Because in this falsehood there is no usefulness, which sometimes is wont to be in false things. *According to the elements.* He touches those who expound idols more shrewdly, saying, Juno is the air, Neptune the sea; therefore, when he had said *according to philosophy and vain,* [var: Vulg. Col. 2:8 *per philosophiam, et inanem fallaciam*, "through philosophy and vain deceit"] he adds, *according to the elements of the world:* as if admonishing that not worshippers of images of whatever kind, but also, as it were, [0612B] teachers and interpreters of the signs, are to be guarded against.

VERS. 9.-- *Corporeally.* Not because God is corporeal, but he has used a transferred word, because in a temple made by hands he dwelt not corporeally but in shadow, that is, by prefiguring signs. *Corporeally,* because in the body of Christ which he took from the Virgin, as in a temple, God dwells, but not so in the saints, because he is the head in which all the senses, reside; as it were touch alone, in those to whom the Spirit is given by measure, of whom it cannot be said: *The Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I)]*, nor is any of them called the only-begotten of God.

VERS. 12.-- *Buried together with him.* That is, baptized after the likeness of his burial. *Through the faith of the operation.* That is, [0612C] by believing the works of God. He expounds one of the works of God, which it is especially necessary for those who wish to rise again to believe, namely that God raised up Christ, and that by the example of Christ he will raise them up whence he subjoins: *Who raised up,* etc.

VERS. 14.-- *Blotting out.* Not only to you Gentiles has God granted pardon, but to us Jews also he has remitted the transgressions which are from the law. And this is, *Blotting out.*

*The handwriting.* He means the sin of Adam, which he took away and fixed to the cross, yet not that alone, others as well: because unless he took away this and the others, we should not be freed from death and hell. While the innocent one is slain, sin is crucified. *The decree,* [var: Vulg. *chirographum decreti*, genitive, "the handwriting of the decree"] is the sentence adverse in all men, by which it was said: *You shall die the death,* [var: Vulg. Gen. 2:17 *morte morieris*, singular; the plural *morte moriemini* stands at Gen. 3:4, in the serpent's mouth and there denied by *Nequaquam*] etc.

[0612D] *Fastening it.* That it may now cease, since all sacrifices are fulfilled in the offering of the true Lamb.

VERS. 15.-- *Despoiling the principalities and powers.* By taking away Abraham, Isaac and the rest of the just. *He led across,* he led them far from this kingdom to heaven. *Confidently.* That is, with hope of victory. *Triumphing,* that is, vanquishing them upon the cross, which is not in secret, but *openly.* For if anyone looks upon the perfection of the Church, the ancient enemy is plainly seen to be conquered. Or, *he led across the principalities and powers,* that is, he drove them out from the Church. *Triumphing over them in himself.* By his own death. For, one most true sacrifice having been offered for us, whatever of faults there was (whereby the principalities and powers held us fast for the paying of the punishments of life) [0613A] he extinguished: whence, therefore, the devil received outwardly the power of slaying the Lord's flesh, thence his inner power which held us was slain.

VERS. 17.-- *Which are a shadow,* etc. Then there were precepts of this kind, not to keep which was sin, now they are testimonies and the confirmation of truth. I [cj: *Ego*; read *Ergo*, "therefore"] the Scripture which then was the exactor of the works that signify, is now the witness of the things signified: and that which then was observed unto foreannouncement, is now recited unto confirmation. *But the body.* That is, the truth of this shadow is *Christ's,* that is, it pertains to Christ. Or all those things are the body of Christ, that is, they signify the body of Christ, either that which he took from the Virgin, or that which is the Church.

VERS. 18.-- *Willing.* That is, loving those things which he has not [0613B] seen. Or, *willing,* namely to urge *the things which he has not seen,* namely the ceremonies of the law, the reason of which he does not know. *Puffed up.* The puffed-up man understands no other thing than what sensuality grasps.

VERS. 19.-- *Compacted,* etc. Into one through conjunction, because they believe the same things and work the same things. And *supplied,* in serving under. *Through joints.* That is, through charity, without which the members do not cohere, nor serve one another, nor live. *Unto the increase of God.* God is increased by those returning, as he is diminished by those forsaking.

VERS. 20.-- *If therefore you are dead.* Thus far he has admonished those standing, lest they be seduced, now he assails those who were already keeping some things from the law; as if he should say: You, see [0613C] that you be not seduced, but do you say to others, Why do you keep the law? By the elements he means the creatures of this world. If, he says, when you received the faith of Christ, you were separated from sin, and from the elements of the world, that is, from abstinence and from every superstition of the creatures of the world. *Why yet,* etc. It can be taken *with Christ* and *as Christ.*

VERS. 22.-- *Which all things by the very use,* etc. If anyone uses them by the Jewish rite, so that he abstains from these and uses those, believing that he is thereby justified, they lead into death. These things, I say, now existing *according to the precepts and doctrines of men,* etc., not of God: for after the truth came, God now does not command these things, but the Pharisees both command them by authority and teach them by reasonings.

[0613D]VERS. 23.-- *In humility,* etc. That humility which is a virtue is not here understood, but, as it is understood from the Greek, that by which the mind is minded of low things, that is, of useful and earthly things, that is, by a casting down of the soul, and they are: *Not for the sparing of the body.* Because in those observances there is much labour, which is empty, and they are not: *In the honour* of God, whose counsel, is called void, nor of the saints, whose faith is destroyed and they are not: *Unto the satisfying of the flesh,* because they withdraw many things. *In superstition,* etc. The religion is feigned, that things which are false may seem true, which humbles minds upon the earth, lest they be raised to things above; and it harms the body, whose head is Christ: not in honour, because, cut off from the head, Christ, they fulfil the sense of the flesh. For the fattening of the carnal sense is human [0614A] tradition, because it says that that suffices which the prudence of the flesh delivers.
