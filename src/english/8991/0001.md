## CHAPTER II.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And you, when you were,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He calls to mind former things, that in good things they may be humbled, and that it may be certain that [0591A] if these things were given to enemies, they shall the more be given to friends, which the Apostle desires; as if he should say: He is the head of the whole Church and yours, because he hath quickened you together: when, that is, although you were dead. ⟦s3⟧ Or we who have believed have the hope of the inheritance, and you also, that is, you have believed. ⟦s4⟧ But God quickened you together, or raised up Jesus, etc.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The prince.* ⟦s2⟧ Namely Beelzebub, who is the prince of the malignant spirits, or, intransitively, the prince of spirits. ⟦s3⟧ He calls the prince of error the spirit of the air, in which he bears rule. ⟦s4⟧ *Of the air.* ⟦s5⟧ The murky lower air is that of the demons. ⟦s6⟧ *Of the spirit.* ⟦s7⟧ That is, of spirits: for the genitive singular is put for the plural. ⟦s8⟧ *Who worketh in the children of unbelief.* ⟦s9⟧ What? if not his own evil works: and among the first chiefly, because that very unbelief [0591B] and infidelity by which they are enemies of the faith, through whom he knows that they can be cleansed?

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *And we were by nature,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: since these foresaid things were in us, let not the Gentile despair but that he may become equal to the Jews. ⟦s3⟧ *Children of wrath.* ⟦s4⟧ Mortals are born with the wrath of God, because, Adam sinning, vice grew in place of nature. ⟦s5⟧ For the human race was held under a just damnation, and all were by nature children of wrath.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He hath quickened us together.* ⟦s2⟧ Or us Jews, and you Gentiles likewise, and this is: by whose grace you Gentiles are saved. ⟦s3⟧ And he raised up both together.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *He hath made to sit together,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not vainly, but faithfully, he already reckons as done that which he does not doubt shall be. ⟦s3⟧ For certainly Christ already sits in the heavenly places, but we not yet; yet we hold it by a sure hope.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0591C]VERS. 7.-- *In the ages to come, abounding,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For in the age to come the gift of God shall appear, in the remuneration of believers; which eye hath not seen, nor ear heard. ⟦s3⟧ And these are the abounding riches, that so much be given as the mind cannot search out.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And this not of yourselves,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Lest perchance thou say: I have deserved it, therefore I received it. ⟦s3⟧ Do not think that thou hast received by deserving. ⟦s4⟧ Grace went before thy merit: not grace from merit, but merit from grace. ⟦s5⟧ For if grace were from merit, thou hast bought it, thou hast not received it freely. ⟦s6⟧ Grace goes before all merits, so that my merits may follow the gifts of God.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *That no man may glory.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not say this for the reason that good works done with a pious thought are made void, since God renders to every man according to his works, and there is [0591D] glory of God to him that works good, but because works are from grace, not grace from works. ⟦s3⟧ It is good, therefore, for a man that he presume not upon the powers of his own will; because he who thought that without his help he could keep what he had given, having set out into a far country, and living prodigally, consumed all: and, worn down by misery, and returned into himself, said: *I will arise, and I will go to my father [n: (Luc. XV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Which good thought he would not have had, unless the most merciful Father had inspired that very thing into him in secret.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *For we are his,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ No one ought to glory, because we, created in faith and in works, are his making, that is, his work, or we are created through his making, that is, his operation: and so neither must one [0592A] glory of works through faith, since faith and all things are from grace.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *For which cause.* ⟦s2⟧ That you may know that all things are from the grace of God. ⟦s3⟧ He had compared the Jews and the Gentiles, in that both had been under sin, now he shows that the Gentiles were the more unworthy and had endured the reproaches of the Jews, so that, being now equal to the Jews, they may be the more beholden to the grace of God. ⟦s4⟧ *Sometime you.* ⟦s5⟧ In certain codices it is found to be written: *who,* in this place: *you who were Gentiles,* and according to this it is read suspensively as far as this place: *Now, however, in Christ,* etc.; the sense nevertheless remaining the same.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Strangers.* ⟦s2⟧ Hospes, as it were ostii pes, the foot of the door, because when one was received into a house, the master of the house, and he who was received, set his foot upon the doorway, and, right hands having been given, he swore that his entry was peaceable.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0592B]VERS. 14.-- *Who hath made both one.* ⟦s2⟧ For he himself is the corner stone, in whom two peoples, as walls coming to one another from a different angle, are joined as it were in the kiss of peace. ⟦s3⟧ *The wall.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, the obstacle, on this side from the law, on that side from idolatry.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Making peace,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He makes peace between them, that he may reconcile both to God the Father. ⟦s3⟧ *In himself.* ⟦s4⟧ Not otherwise than by the cross, that is, by death, did he slay the enmity which was between God and men, that is, he forgave sins. ⟦s5⟧ For the death of the Saviour profited all.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *You were far off,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If thou lovest God with affection, thou art near: if thou hatest, thou art far off. ⟦s3⟧ For it is not by regions that anyone is far from God, but by affections.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *You are not strangers.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, less worthy [0592C] in the faith, as those formerly received under the law. ⟦s3⟧ *And sojourners.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, foreign to God. ⟦s5⟧ *But you are fellow citizens.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, of the same right and dignity in the house of God. ⟦s7⟧ *Of the saints.* ⟦s8⟧ Not of carnal Israel. ⟦s9⟧ *And the domestics.* ⟦s10⟧ That is, of the household. ⟦s11⟧ *Of God.* ⟦s12⟧ To whom he reveals secrets.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *He himself the chief corner stone,* etc. [n: (AUG., in Psal. LXXXVI.)] Lest the Apostles or the prophets (in whom the city is founded) should hold to themselves, following on he says in himself: *He himself the chief corner stone, Christ Jesus;* for indeed the whole building rests upon Christ as upon the first foundation: which foundation is at the top, not at the bottom, as in a bodily structure.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *It groweth into a holy temple in the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ No one is so perfect that he cannot grow.
