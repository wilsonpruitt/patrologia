## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- *Then after fourteen years.* From [0573A] the passion of the Lord, when he had been converted to the faith, he came, as Bede shows, writing upon the Acts of the Apostles.

VERS. 2.-- *And I conferred with them.* He shows that he had no assurance of the Gospel unless it were strengthened by the authority of Peter and of the others. *Apart,* etc. I conferred with these separately, not publicly, lest a scandal of the faith should be born to the faithful of Judaea (who thought that the law was to be kept). At that time, when Peter had come to Antioch (although this is not read in the Acts of the Apostles), Paul withstood him to the face.

VERS. 4.-- *Brought in unawares.* By the Jews. For what the unbelievers could not do of themselves, they attempted through false brethren, whom they had sent in under the appearance of religion, that they might explore what liberty we have in the faith of Christ, [0573B] not that they might hold it, but that by some contention they might bring us back from it into the servitude of the law. But if it were so, the truth of the Gospel would not remain among the Gentiles, but all would judaize. And therefore here, to those with whom he compared the assertion of his preaching, he was in no wise willing to yield. But elsewhere, where it did no harm, on account of the scandal of the Jews, he circumcised Timothy. In all these things he both commends himself and proves that the law is not to be kept.

VERS. 5.-- *Nor for an hour.* He yielded not for an hour, who never yielded. But if he yielded at some time, how not even for an hour? Why also on account of the false brethren, if he was going to do it of himself? He yielded therefore on account of them: which of himself he would not do: humbling himself to the law, Timothy being circumcised, that the guile and the scandal of the Jews [0573C] might cease, who were ready to stir up a tumult and a sedition if he should receive that son of Judaea uncircumcised and ordain him bishop.

VERS. 6.-- *What they were sometime.* I do not run back to former things, because those which are now suffice; but they who seemed to be something conferred nothing. In which it appears that he is not inferior to them. But God is proved not to accept the person, because if it were so, I, Paul, before ungodly, upon whom such men would confer nothing, should not have been. Or that, *it maketh no matter,* but they brought me nothing. *Who seemed to be something.* That is, of some authority: because they had walked with the Lord and had been present at his transfiguration. They seemed, I say, *by these,* that is, by the false brethren. Because they who seemed to be something seemed to be so only to carnal men; [0573D] for they themselves are not something. Even if some are good ministers of God, Christ in them is something, not they themselves of themselves. For if they themselves were something of themselves, they would always have been something.

VERS. 9.-- *Pillars.* The Church is called the firm support of the truth: which wisdom also built, that is, established upon seven pillars, by which number is intimated either the whole body of preachers (because it is wont to be put for the universal) or the sevenfold operation of the Holy Spirit. *They gave the right hands.* Whence did he confer with them and receive right hands? Because he had the same word with them. The conference itself showed the unity of doctrine, otherwise the Church would not believe him who had not been with the Lord. *That we great ones.* [var: Vulg. Gal 2:9 *ut nos in gentes*, "that we unto the Gentiles"] That I might be first in the preaching [0574A] of the Gentiles, as Peter in the circumcision. And that, as the apostles were joined to Peter, so Barnabas might be joined to him.

VERS. 11.-- *I withstood.* This Paul would not dare, unless he felt himself not unequal.

VERS. 12.-- *For before they came,* etc. Certain [sic: *Qui dam*] emulators of the law, who venerated Christ and the law with equal right, whom fearing he did not mingle with the Gentiles. But if this alone were so, it would not be blameworthy. As Paul himself also yielded at some time on account of scandal. But in this there was error, that he compelled the Gentiles to judaize.

VERS. 14.-- *How dost thou compel the Gentiles.* Not by the command of one teaching, but by the example of his manner of life.

VERS. 15.-- *We.* His authority being commended, he now begins to treat by reasons that carnal observances [0574B] are not to be observed after Christ. As if to say: The Gentiles ought not to judaize, because we who availed in the law know that it does not justify: and therefore, having let it go, we flee to Christ.

VERS. 16.-- *But by the faith,* etc. He does not for this reason say that it is of faith, as though good works were made void, since God renders to every man according to his works: but because good works are of grace, and not grace of works: because faith working by love would work nothing, unless the love of God itself were poured forth in us by the Holy Spirit; nor would faith itself be in us, unless God gave it.

VERS. 17.-- *But if while we seek.* If we have in truth known that we cannot be justified save by passing over to Christ, does Christ then suffer us to slip back [0574C] to that which we were? But if he suffered it, he would surely be to us a minister of sin, that is, of our former state, as he would have been a minister of justice to those passing over to him.

VERS. 19.-- *For I through the law.* Understood spiritually. *Am dead.* That I may not live carnally. *That I may live to God.* Not to myself, but that I now can to God? because I am with Christ. *Nailed.* That is, the cross of Christ has quenched in me the ardour of sin, so that I am no longer stretched out toward it: as one having his hands fastened with nails.

VERS. 20.-- *I live.* I have in the virtues vigour for working well. *Now not I.* That sinner which I formerly was. *But Christ liveth in me.* That is, the newness of Christ appears in me. To what end then the law?
