## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

VERS. 27.-- *It is written.* That she is free and a mother he proves by the authority of Isaias, subjoining: *Rejoice.* To rejoice, to break forth, to cry out, pertain to liberty. *Thou bearest not.* He says this because at that time she brought forth as it were none. *Thou travailest not,* because as yet she had had no striving. *Rejoice,* in the heart. *Break forth.* Show forth outwardly the gladness of the mind. *Cry out.* Preach thy joy to others. *For many are the children.* This pertains to the mother. Isaias, beholding [0581D] in the time of Christ the Church of the Gentiles, before barren, then fruitful, addresses her, saying: O barren Church, who in the time of the law bearest not even by the power of a miracle, etc. *Of the desolate.* More are the children of the Church once desolate than of the Synagogue bringing forth in the meantime. This Isaias would say superfluously of the ancient Sara, he is therefore to be understood to say it of the Church. But the Church was barren among all the Gentiles, before that offspring which we behold arose. The Synagogue therefore is she that hath a husband, that is, the law. But the desolate, and wandering, and living without any power of a husband, is the multitude of the Gentiles, which also was barren, putting forth the germ of no lawful word. Nature corrupted by sin does indeed bring forth citizens of the earthly city, but the citizens of the heavenly city [0582A] grace brings forth, freeing nature from sin. *Which hath a husband.* For the Synagogue had a husband, that is, the law, and was once fruitful in children. But the barren Church, without her husband Christ, without any address of the bridegroom, lay long in the desert. But after she received the bill of divorce, and turned every ornament of her husband to the ornament of an idol, then the husband, the former girdle rotting, wove for his loins another belt, another girding out of the Gentiles, who, as soon as she was joined to a husband, conceived and brought forth. Whence Isaias cries out: *Enlarge the place of thy tabernacle, and stretch forth thy cords [n: (Isa. LIV)]* [var: Vulg. Is 54:2 *Dilata locum tentorii tui, et pelles tabernaculorum tuorum extende : ne parcas : longos fac funiculos tuos*, "Enlarge the place of thy tent, and stretch out the skins of thy tabernacles: spare not: lengthen thy cords"].

VERS. 28.-- *Isaac.* Laughter, that is, they who serve in gladness, not out of sadness or necessity.

VERS. 29.-- *He persecuted.* I do not think that we can easily [0582B] find where Ismael persecuted Isaac, but only this, that when the son of the Egyptian woman was playing with Isaac, Sara was indignant.

VERS. 31.-- *By which liberty.* The liberty by which we have been freed by Christ is the remission of sins, and the justification of faith working by love.
