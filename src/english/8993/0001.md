## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Of old.* ⟦s2⟧ It is not new. ⟦s3⟧ *God,* who is unchangeable then and now. ⟦s4⟧ *Speaking.* ⟦s5⟧ He says: *speaking,* in the present, because what was then is even now. ⟦s6⟧ *To the fathers.* ⟦s7⟧ That is, to those who are fathers to us in flesh and in the worship of God. ⟦s8⟧ *In the prophets.* ⟦s9⟧ For not they themselves, but God in them. ⟦s10⟧ *In divers manners.* ⟦s11⟧ That is, many times to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and the rest, and often to the same men. ⟦s12⟧ *And in many ways.* ⟦s13⟧ Because now by dreams, as to Daniel: now by an open voice, as to Moses: now by inward inspiration, as to David.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *In the Son,* who is greater than the prophets. ⟦s2⟧ For he is the fulfiller and sanctifier of the prophets. ⟦s3⟧ Thus then he is a prophet, in that he is also the Lord of the prophets. [0643C] So too Christ is an angel, in that he is also the Lord of the angels. ⟦s4⟧ *He appointed* unchangeably. ⟦s5⟧ *Heir of all things,* that is, possessor of every creature. ⟦s6⟧ For Jacob is no longer the portion of the Lord, and Israel his part: but absolutely all the nations of the world.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Who being the brightness of his glory,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Here he commends Christ according to his divine nature, showing him coeternal and coequal with the Father, and of the same substance with him, but other in person.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The figure of his substance.* ⟦s2⟧ Behold, otherwise, personally, as a figure from him whose figure it is. ⟦s3⟧ But equal, because not unlike, as he himself says: *The Father is in me;* and: *He that seeth me, seeth my Father also [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Note that these names, namely light, glory, are sometimes referred to the divine nature, sometimes to the person. [0643D] And when they are referred to the person; they are referred now to the Father, now to the Son. ⟦s5⟧ For we say: The Father is light, and the Son is light. ⟦s6⟧ Likewise the Father is glory, the Son glory, and these two are one glory and one light, not two. ⟦s7⟧ And the Son is called glory of glory, as light of light, and beginning of beginning, and God of God, yet not two gods, but one, not two beginnings, but one. ⟦s8⟧ But brightness and figure, as image also, are properly referred to the person of the Son and are said relatively.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0644A] *And upholding all things,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It was said above that through him he made all things. ⟦s3⟧ Here he now attributes to him the sum of authority, from this, that with authority he governs and holds together all things. ⟦s4⟧ For as all things were created by him, so through him, who is unchangeable, they are preserved. ⟦s5⟧ For the omnipotence of the Creator is the cause of subsisting to every creature, and if that power should at any time cease from ruling the things it founded, at once the form and nature of all things would fall together.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Purgation.* ⟦s2⟧ After he has shown the excellence of him by whom he created and governs all things, here he commends the kindness of his humility. ⟦s3⟧ For he puts us in mind of the cross and the resurrection and the ascension, where he says: *He sitteth,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ *On high.* ⟦s5⟧ In saying this, he does not shut God up in a place: but shows the man Christ loftier than all [0644B] and more eminent than all. ⟦s6⟧ So also by the right hand he did not give God a shape, but showed the likeness of honour. ⟦s7⟧ For he that hath confessed [cj: *Confessus*; read *consessus*, "the sitting together"] shows nothing else but equality of honour.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *So much better.* ⟦s2+3⟧ Yet in the psalm he says: *He was made a little less than the angels [n: (Psal. VIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps 8:6 *Minuisti eum paulominus ab angelis*, "thou hast made him a little less than the angels"] Here he is said to be better than the same. ⟦s4⟧ And as that was said according to his human nature, so this too could be understood to have been said according to the divine, were it not that *made* is added, by which it is opened that here he speaks of him according to the flesh. ⟦s5⟧ For according to that whereby he is acknowledged consubstantial with the substance of the Father, he was not made, but born: the discourse therefore is of him according to his human nature, according to which he was both less than the angels in his passion, and greater and better also in the fulness of grace, [0644C] of which the angels themselves also receive by measure.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A more differing name.* ⟦s2⟧ While he was mortal, he had a differing name, because the angels ministered to him. ⟦s3⟧ After he was immortal, more differing still. ⟦s4⟧ And because he could have this, and not for them, he adds *than they,* that is, greatly differing from the name of the angels, this is the name which is above every name, namely God, or Son, which is truly worthier than that by which they are called angels, that is, messengers. ⟦s5⟧ For the name of Son shows what is proper, not adoption, because unless he were the proper Son, the Apostle could not from this assert the greatness of his honour. ⟦s6⟧ But the proper Son is begotten of the Father himself. ⟦s7⟧ But if he were Son by the grace of adoption, he would be less not only than the Father, but even than the angels.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0644D]VERS. 5.-- *To day.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest the generation should seem past, he said to day. ⟦s3⟧ For although that day also may be understood on which Christ was born according to his manhood, yet because *to day* signifies presence, and in eternity nothing is past as though it had ceased to be, nor future as though it were not yet, but present only: because whatsoever is eternal, always is: it is more divinely taken of the everlasting generation of the Wisdom of God, which the most sincere and catholic faith preaches.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0645A] *I will be to him.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, his honour, or to that man who was to be born of the Virgin. ⟦s3⟧ Yet this can also be taken as said according to the flesh, for the flesh communicates with higher things, as the Godhead also with lowly ones.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Again he bringeth in,* visible by the flesh assumed, him who before was invisible in the world, which assumption is called a going forth from the Father: the bringing in unto the inheritance, where he is commanded to be adored.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And let them adore.* ⟦s2⟧ And again the Father says of the same Son: *When he bringeth in.* ⟦s3⟧ That is, when he shows him to be *within,* even unto the hearts of men, *to be led,* by faith; what does he say? ⟦s4⟧ *And let them adore.* ⟦s5⟧ The angels adore, because they obey his command and reverence him.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Angels.* ⟦s2⟧ Spirit is the name of a nature; angel, of an office. ⟦s3⟧ For those whom God founded as spirits, [0645B] by sending them as messengers he makes angels. ⟦s4⟧ And he uses them all for the unchangeable determination of his own sentence, whether the good by his grace, or the evil by their own will. ⟦s5⟧ And by this the will of God is the first and highest cause of all bodily forms and motions. ⟦s6⟧ For nothing is done visibly which is not either commanded or permitted, according to ineffable justice, out of the inner, invisible and intelligible hall of the supreme Emperor. ⟦s7⟧ Or those spirits are called angels when they are sent to announce light things: but when they are sent for vengeance, as in Sodom, they are called a burning fire. ⟦s8⟧ So too the ministers of the Church are fire while our vices are burned away, but angels while they announce the word of God.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *To the Son.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he commends Christ by his dignity together [0645C] with his power. ⟦s3⟧ *For ever.* ⟦s4⟧ Because he shall reign always, and the last judgment shall stand always; whence: *These shall go into everlasting fire, and those into life everlasting [n: (Matth. XXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 25:46 *in supplicium aeternum … justi autem*, "into everlasting punishment … but the just"]

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *He hath anointed thee.* ⟦s2⟧ According to his manhood, because the Godhead lacked nothing. ⟦s3⟧ The anointed signifies a king or a priest. ⟦s4⟧ *God* is repeated out of great love. ⟦s5⟧ Or according to Augustine: The one case is the vocative, the other the nominative, thus: O thou God the Son, thy God hath anointed thee, namely, the Father. ⟦s6⟧ In Latin it is thought to be the same case, but in Greek the distinction is most evident: whereby the one is understood as nominative, the other as vocative.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And thou.* ⟦s2⟧ After the excellence of his manhood, he returns again to the eternity of the Son. ⟦s3⟧ *In the beginning.* ⟦s4⟧ Because the Creator exists before the things created, without any beginning. [0645D] *Of thy hands.* ⟦s5⟧ The power of commanding, that is, the might of his will, is called a hand, because all things were made as he willed.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *They shall perish.* ⟦s2⟧ From that which they are, when the creatures shall be changed for the better. ⟦s3⟧ *But thou shalt continue.* ⟦s4⟧ So that, as he is eternal before all things, so, because he continues, he will be the same after all things are changed. ⟦s5⟧ *They shall grow old.* ⟦s6⟧ That is said to grow old which is worn away after the manner of a garment, as human flesh is, which nevertheless shall be changed for the better.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And as a vesture.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies the change of heaven. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *I saw a new heaven and a new earth [n: (Apoc. XXI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ *Thou shalt change them,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Because the works of God shall stand under eternity, lest they return to corruption. ⟦s6⟧ *But thou,* wholly unchangeable.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *To which of the angels.* ⟦s2⟧ He sets himself to treat [0646A] of the glory of his manhood. ⟦s3⟧ *Sit thou.* ⟦s4⟧ To the victorious Son one that hath confessed [cj: *confessus*; read *consessus*, "a session, a sitting together"] is offered by the Father. ⟦s5⟧ *Of thy feet.* ⟦s6⟧ By the feet eternal stability is signified, that is, unto eternity, where, his footsteps as it were set down, he stands firm by the power of his omnipotence.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Are they not all,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Assuredly. ⟦s3⟧ Understand therefore how great an honour is ours, that God sends his angels to us as ministers to friends. ⟦s4⟧ For although there is much difference between angels and men, yet he has made them near to us, because they are zealous for our salvation, run to and fro for our sake, discharge their own office on our behalf, this is the angelic work, the duty of the angelic function, that all things be done for the salvation of one's neighbours. ⟦s5⟧ But this is rather the work of Christ, because he commanded the angels, who are above us, to render their ministry unto our salvation.
