## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 2.-- *It did not profit,* etc. For having gone out of Egypt, when they had traversed much of the way, and had received many tokens of the power of God in Egypt, in the Red Sea, in the desert, they took counsel to send spies who should look into the nature of the land. *Which they heard.* To the explorers; who brought back evil things, they believed not; or distinguish it thus, of the *faith* taken *from those things which they heard.*

VERS. 3.-- *And indeed.* Here he treats what it signifies concerning the first rest. *By the works.* Because he had said that it had been announced [0650A] concerning the rest, he says when: namely, before the law, after the completion of the works of the seven days, by the rest of the sabbath; and under the law, when it was designated by the land of promise; and in the time, of grace, the Holy Spirit saying in David: *to day,* etc., as if, it has been announced concerning that rest, and thus *indeed* when the fulfilment of that true rest had not yet been signified, because no one had as yet obtained the rest. *Being perfected.* Which was when man was made, for whom all things were made, and he himself was made for the glorifying of God.

VERS. 4.-- *And he rested the seventh day.* [sic: *dia*] In six days he made all things, so that afterward nothing should be made save out of the matter then made, and after the likeness of the things made. On the seventh day he rested, which therefore signifies rest. So too in the sixth age Christ the man was born into the world for labour, [0650B] and having suffered on the sixth day and at the sixth hour of the day, on the seventh he rested. So too we, while we live under the works of the six days, labour; going out thence, we rest: if only our works be found very good.

VERS. 5.-- *And in this.* Of the second rest, which is signified by the figure of the rest of the land of promise.

VERS. 7.-- *A certain day, To day.* Of the third that is signified, and when it is given, though obscurely, which through Christ is promised to believers. *After so long a time.* But in this it is plain that this is another rest, of which David says *to day,* than that to which Josue led. For if it were that one, David would not speak of another; therefore it is certain that there is another to which Christ leads, which was signified by that Palestine.

VERS. 8.-- *Unto them,* the children of Israel, to whom it had before been announced [0650C] concerning the rest by a sign, that is, the land of promise; *had given rest, never,* that is, at no time; *thereafter,* that is, in the time following; *would he speak of another day,* that is, of the time of grace, in which it was to be given. Or, he would never afterward speak of another, namely rest, besides the land of promise. By saying, on this day, he means the same as he had said above, *to day,* etc.

VERS. 9.-- *A sabbath-rest;* that is, the true rest signified by the sabbath (that is, the festal gladness of full quiet) on which the Lord rested and which was observed in the law. For then we shall be perfectly at leisure, refreshed by a greater grace, and full of God. And we shall know perfectly that he is God. There we shall be at leisure, we shall see, and we shall love. We shall love and we shall praise: behold what shall be in [0650D] the end without end. For what other end is ours, save to come to the kingdom of which there is no end?

VERS. 11.-- *Into the same example.* Or, into such punishment as they had through unbelief; he says *example* that we may have our mind there, lest we likewise fall away.

VERS. 12.-- *The word of God.* Which came from the royal seats. *Living.* Whom the unbelieving think dead. *Reaching.* Coming with entire consideration unto the separation of the soul, that is, of sensuality, and of the spirit, that is, of reason. *The division of the soul.* By the soul we live with the beasts, by the spirit we understand with the angels. And therefore by the soul are understood carnal sins, that is, those which are done by an act of the body, as lust. But by the spirit, spiritual ones, [0651A] that is, those which are of the mind, as pride. But the word of God discerns between carnal sins and spiritual: who acts, what he acts, with what mind. *And of the spirit.* The word of God reaches with an inseparable knowledge, because it knows how sensuality is divided from reason, and itself from itself, when, being more given over to the lowest things, it is lower, or, called back from them, worthier. So too it sees how the spirit is divided from itself, whether while it gapes after God, thinking of the divine vision, or while lower down it considers heavenly things, or lower still on earth treats of worldly things to be rightly done, or how the spirit, that is, reason, is separated from sensuality, when that which is lower in itself surpasses that which is higher in her.

*Of the joints also.* A joint is called the joining of sensuality itself and of reason, which the Son [0651B] of God sees; namely how they cohere with one another in something, or this and that, that is, reason and sensuality, how in their differences they agree with one another, when the higher difference of sensuality by consenting agrees with the differences of reason. Or the lower difference of reason, pressed down and captive, sometimes consents to the lower difference of sensuality. *Of the marrow.* Marrow, which is the inner part, is here said to be whatsoever is more inward and more subtle in the soul or in the spirit, which the Son of God also sees. For he discerns thoughts, that is, what diverse things diverse men think. He discerns also the intentions of those thoughts, whither each tends, to good or to evil. And, that I may say more, no creature earthly or heavenly is invisible to him, but all things are naked and uncovered, because on every side fully seen. Likewise he calls joints [0651C] the conjunctions of thoughts: marrows, the intentions of them.

VERS. 14.-- *Having therefore.* Here he begins to speak of Christ the high priest, how he is sufficient, and how he is worthier than the old priest. Whom also he changes, and the law which is under him, by his own better law, and how he is necessary to us for justice and salvation.

VERS. 15.-- *For we have not,* etc. As if: Let us hold fast, and we ought to hold fast, because, since he is mighty, he is also merciful, having, as it were, experienced our infirmity. *Who cannot,* etc. It is impossible for a man who has not had experience of affliction, and has not sensibly borne all things, to know the afflictions of the afflicted. But Christ knows, not only in that he is God, [0651D] according to which he knows all things, but in that he is man and has borne like things.

VERS. 16.-- *In seasonable aid.* Now is the time of help, now is the time of gifts, when those who sin after baptism can through grace find repentance. But when the bride-chamber shall have been shut, when the king shall have come in to see those that recline, so that they who have been worthy of it shall have come to the bosom of the patriarch, then for the wicked it shall be the time of despair. But now, while the contest still remains and the palm hangs, there must be no despairing, but we must draw near with confidence, that is, without doubting, that by grace one may draw near, and seasonable aid be found. For there is a time which is not fitting.
