## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0652A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *For every high priest.* ⟦s2⟧ Because spiritual things do not so bring in the weak: as bodily things; from these he begins, wishing to show that the New Testament is better than the Old. ⟦s3⟧ But some things he sets down as common to Christ with the priests, some higher, pertaining to Christ alone. ⟦s4⟧ Some, further, more lowly, suiting those priests only.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For that he himself also.* ⟦s2⟧ Take away here the occasion of exalting oneself above others.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Neither doth any man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He strikes the covetous, who are not called, but thrust themselves forward.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He did not glorify himself.* ⟦s2⟧ The Son was glorified by the Father, when he said: *This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased [n: (Matth. III)]*. [0652B] Which glorification was foretold long before through the Prophet: *Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee [n: (Psal. II)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Thou art a priest.* ⟦s2⟧ Now Christ is a priest, not according to that whereby he was born of the Father, God with God, coeternal with him that begat him: but on account of the flesh consumed, on account of the victim which he offered for us. ⟦s3⟧ *According to the order of Melchisedech,* who was not temporal, Scripture keeping silence as to the beginning and the end of his life, in a figure of Christ who lacks beginning and end. ⟦s4⟧ And as he offered in bread and wine and once, so also Christ under the species of bread and wine gave his body and his blood to the disciples.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Prayers and supplications.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks of the action and the life of Christ, whose every action is the instruction of the faithful, and a prayer to God. ⟦s3⟧ Whatever Christ did, [0652C] they were prayers and supplications for all. ⟦s4⟧ The shedding of his blood was a mighty cry, in which he was heard, for the reverence of that same passion. ⟦s5⟧ The reverence is this, that being without sin he suffered for charity alone. ⟦s6⟧ *He was heard.* ⟦s7⟧ By this it is plain that through him we can obtain mercy, since he is now at the right hand of the Father.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *He learned,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, he willingly took upon himself obedience even unto death.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *To all that obey him.* ⟦s2⟧ So greatly does the passion avail, that it suffices for all unto salvation, which salvation can well be through him, because he himself *is called,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *He was made the cause of salvation,* not of any sort of salvation, but of eternal; nor to few, but to all; yet on this condition, that they obey him. ⟦s4⟧ For otherwise they will not be able [0652D] to obtain salvation. ⟦s5⟧ And he himself can well be the cause of salvation to the rest, since *he was called by God a high priest,* as he also himself showed in very deed by his works that he was such. ⟦s6⟧ For because he fulfilled the office of a high priest, and was obedient to the Father in all things, and in himself came to so great a consummation of all good things that it sufficed even for the salvation of others, therefore *he was called by God a high priest,* and this, *according to the order of Melchisedech,* namely, that all the order of divinity might be considered in him which could be considered in Melchisedech.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Not to be interpreted.* ⟦s2⟧ The discourse concerning the incarnate Son is hard to interpret, because you are weak in sense for understanding the deep mysteries.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *For whereas ye ought.* ⟦s2⟧ He inserts a rebuke [0653A] concerning their weakness, that they may labour to understand high things of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ *Which are the elements of the beginning.* ⟦s4⟧ The beginning of the discourses is simple teaching. ⟦s5⟧ Its elements are the matter of which it treats, as the nativity, the passion.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *For every one,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He who does not take in this: *The Word was made flesh,* how shall he take in: *In the beginning was the Word?* ⟦s3⟧ Every discourse upon this matter, wherein it is dealt with that it be not only believed but also understood, and that what is said be known, is burdensome: it more easily weighs them down than feeds them. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it comes about that spiritual men do not altogether keep silence of these things to the carnal, on account of the catholic faith, which is to be preached to all. ⟦s5⟧ Nor yet let them so forsake them that, wishing to bring these things through to an understanding not capable of them, they more easily make the discourse to be loathed in the truth than the truth to be perceived in the discourse.

## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0653B]

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Wherefore.* ⟦s2⟧ When they had been harshly rebuked and blamed, in that they wish always to learn these same things, again he coaxes them by numbering himself with them, exhorting them to perfection. ⟦s3⟧ *The word of the beginning of Christ,* wherein the untaught are to be catechized, as is the Symbol of faith and the Lord's prayer. ⟦s4⟧ *Not again,* that is, not formerly, that as they fell before, they should fall again. ⟦s5⟧ Or, *not again laying,* let us so lay now, that there be no need to lay further. ⟦s6⟧ *Foundation.* ⟦s7⟧ What he before called the word of the beginning, here he calls the foundation, in that it is first set before those coming to the Christian religion, so that upon this may be built what belongs to perfection. ⟦s8⟧ This foundation is divided into six, which [0653C] are: the beginning or penance, faith, the doctrine of baptisms, the imposition of hands, resurrection, and judgment. ⟦s9⟧ Whosoever converts any to the faith, first makes them repent, believe in God, be baptized unto remission, be confirmed through the imposition, and await the resurrection and the day of judgment.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Impossible.* ⟦s2⟧ But lest anyone suppose that a second or a third baptism can be had after sins, he adds: *Impossible,* not merely difficult, lest anyone should trust in this. ⟦s3⟧ *Those who,* that is, even those who have had these good things. ⟦s4⟧ Much less those utterly evil.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *To be renewed,* by baptism, whose power stands in the cross of Christ, whence there follows: *Crucifying again.* ⟦s2⟧ Because in baptism we are conformed to his death and to his burial. ⟦s3⟧ He who thinks to be baptized a second time, [0653D] a second time crucifies Christ (so far as in him lies), which is to have him for a show and a mockery, as he died once; so we in baptism die once to sins, not a second or a third time.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Often coming.* ⟦s2⟧ He says *often coming,* because if it is rare, it does not suffice: if it is constant, it grows cheap. ⟦s3⟧ *The rain.* ⟦s4⟧ Holy doctrine, the dew of the heavens, which the heavens rain upon the thirsting earth, that is, the preachers of the word of truth. ⟦s5⟧ *Bringing forth grass.* ⟦s6⟧ He signifies that they had often drunk in, through the law and the prophets, the word of heavenly doctrine, who not even so were ready to bring forth the buds of faith. ⟦s7⟧ *By whom it is tilled.* ⟦s8⟧ The tillers of the earth are they who with the spiritual ploughshare cut through the thickets, and with the sickle of the word of God cut away hurtful things. ⟦s9⟧ Or those by whom it is tilled. are [0654A] those same men who till themselves in good works, having imitated, that is, their elders, according to that: *And they brought forth the fruit of nativity,* because they imitate their fathers, who begot them in the faith.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *We trust.* ⟦s2⟧ Because as to the present the Apostle has nothing whereby to praise them; he lifts them up from the hope of things to come, to which he entices them by things past, and he refreshes their spirits and strengthens them, calling old things back into their mind, and makes them reckon that God has not forgotten the good things which they did. ⟦s3⟧ For as their former good things had been made dead and void through the evils that followed, so those same shall live again through penance and through other good things following.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Of the work.* ⟦s2⟧ He has testified great things of them. ⟦s3⟧ And he commends three good things in them, namely that [0654B] they made all their own things common to the saints. ⟦s4⟧ Behold the work, and this out of love, which informs actions; behold the eagerness, and this unto the glory of God; behold a pure and right intention.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *We desire.* ⟦s2⟧ He shows much love toward them, and shows the same care about each one, and bears care alike for the greater and for the lesser, and loves all.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *For to Abraham,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He says three things: that the promise was made; and that it was confirmed by an oath; and that it was already fulfilled in Abraham.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Unless blessing,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ There is an aposiopesis here, that is, a breaking off. ⟦s3⟧ As if: If I shall not do this, let me not be believed concerning anything.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Wherein God, willing more abundantly.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: [0654C] But if a man, who is a liar, is believed by an oath, how much more must God be believed, who cannot lie?

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *We flee for refuge.* ⟦s2⟧ He flees to hope who by the hope of blessedness despises tribulations.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Which as.* ⟦s2⟧ As an anchor holds a ship, so hope holds the soul, lest it be sunk in the salt sea of this world by consenting to iniquity; *and firm,* lest it so much as totter.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (AUG.)] Cast thy care upon God,* etc., *as far as* if for a time it is tossed, its anchor is hope.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Forerunner.* ⟦s2⟧ To this end did Christ run before, that we might follow. ⟦s3⟧ Who is well called a runner, because no fault hindered him, according to that: *He hath rejoiced as a giant to run the way [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. [0654D] And elsewhere: *In the way of sinners he hath not stood [n: (Ibid. I)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And a forerunner, because he ran before us, and for us.
