## CHAPTER VII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A priest of the most high God. [n: (CHRYS., hom. 35, in Gen.)]* Now he was perhaps a priest ordained by himself, for such were the priests in that time. ⟦s2⟧ Or because he was further advanced in age, he was reverenced by his own people. ⟦s3⟧ Or because he too was zealous to sacrifice, as also Noah, Abel Abraham, when they offered sacrifices.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Neither beginning,* etc., *nor having end of life.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ absolutely did not have a beginning nor an end. ⟦s3⟧ *For ever.* ⟦s4⟧ He says it thus because Scripture keeps silence that anyone had succeeded him. ⟦s5⟧ Or he speaks mystically of the priesthood of Christ.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0655A]VERS. 4.-- *But behold,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He dwells upon commending the dignity. ⟦s3⟧ Melchisedech and the excellence of his priesthood; preferring him to Abraham himself, the father of all, and to all the Levites.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And indeed of the sons of Levi,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: And from this behold how great he is, because some indeed of the sons of Levi, through this, that they receive the sacrifice (for otherwise they would not dare), have a commandment in the law, without whose authority they would never presume to take tithes.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *He testifieth.* ⟦s2⟧ As I do, or, the same in many places. ⟦s3⟧ *He liveth.* ⟦s4⟧ For he bore the type of our high priest, whose excellence of power is shown through his likeness. ⟦s5⟧ For through Melchisedech, in whom the figure went before, the priesthood of Christ is distinguished from the [0655B] priesthood of Levi; but in the figure of Christ it is said that Melchisedech liveth. ⟦s6⟧ Whence he is called a priest for ever, who offered himself, not out of any necessity, but out of the will of his own power.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Under him.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the law is in the hand of the priest, and through him is fulfilled the law which is said to consummate. ⟦s3⟧ This translation was prefigured in Samuel, who out of the tribe of Ephraim was chosen priest by the Lord, the sons of Heli being reproved, the Lord saying: He that shall honour me, I will honour him; and he that dishonoureth me, etc.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *For the priesthood being translated,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That another should rise, he proves thereafter both by the tribe and by the rite. ⟦s3⟧ By the tribe there: *For he of whom these things,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ By the rite there: *And it is yet far more,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ *For the priesthood being translated.* ⟦s6⟧ Because therefore the law and the priesthood were given alike, and by the same, and under the same promise, [0655C] what is said of the one must needs be understood of the other also.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *For it is manifest,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ From tribe to tribe the priesthood was translated, from the priestly to the royal, that the same tribe might be priestly and royal. ⟦s3⟧ And behold the mystery: first that tribe was royal, afterwards it was made priestly. ⟦s4⟧ So Christ was always king; but he was made priest when he took flesh, when he offered the sacrifice.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Not according to the law.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, according [cj: *secundum*; read *non secundum*, the lemma's negation governing the gloss] to the carnal understanding of the law, that he should act according to carnal observances; but according to the power of the law itself, that is, the spiritual understanding, which they that follow out have life eternal. ⟦s3⟧ There is a difference between priesthood, and priesthood. ⟦s4⟧ That one carnal, this spiritual. [0655D] That one temporal, this eternal.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *By so much.* ⟦s2⟧ From the praise of the priest he infers the commendation of the law. ⟦s3⟧ *Of a better.* ⟦s4⟧ Because it promises better things, that is, eternal; but the Old Testament, temporal things.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *But this man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As Christ remains, so also the law which he brought, in which is true remission of sins, and perpetual grace, which, because the old law could not give, it was shut out. ⟦s3⟧ And that it remains for ever he shows from the high priest, who is one, which he would not be, unless he were immortal, as in the law there were many, because they were mortal.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Whereby he is able also to save.* ⟦s2⟧ Having shown that through the priesthood of Christ the old priesthood and the law are destroyed, [0656A] he commends his act by putting down, by contrast, the working of the old priesthood.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Holy,* inwardly. ⟦s2⟧ *Innocent,* in hands. ⟦s3⟧ *Undefiled,* in body. ⟦s4⟧ *Separated from sinners,* that is, free from all sin.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Who hath not,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Since he is such, he has not need to offer daily. ⟦s3⟧ The Apostle did not say this as though Christ ever stood in need of it, but that he might remove from him what belongs to others. ⟦s4⟧ *For this he did once, in offering himself.* ⟦s5⟧ So great is the sacrifice, that although it is one and offered once, yet it suffices unto eternity.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *For the law maketh men,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ They have need to offer daily, because they are weak; he, offering himself once, suffices for all, because he [0656B] is perfect.
