## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The chief point. [n: (CHRYS.)]* The apostle Paul mingles lowly things with lofty, always following his Master, etc., *as far as* whom it behoves to discharge the office of a priest.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Such an one we have.* ⟦s2⟧ Here the chief point begins; and it ends there: *God and not man. ⟦s3⟧ In the heavens.* ⟦s4⟧ Or above the material heavens. ⟦s5⟧ Or, above thrones and dominations.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A minister of the holies.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he ministers to the saints increase of virtues, and life eternal. ⟦s3⟧ Or, in the heavens he ministers the glory signified through the holy of holies. ⟦s4⟧ *A minister of the true tabernacle.* ⟦s5⟧ That is, of the truth, which the old tabernacle prefigured; namely justice in the present Church, glory in [0656C] the Church to come. ⟦s6⟧ *Which,* the true tabernacle, *God hath pitched,* [var: Vulg. Hbr 8:2 *quod fixit Dominus*, "which the Lord hath pitched"] that it may remain for ever; but the shadowy one was not fixed, but portable; *and not man:* this he adds because God set up the old one also, but through a man as minister.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *That he may offer.* ⟦s2⟧ And since it is necessary that he have somewhat to offer; either that will be upon the earth, or heavenly: but not upon the earth, because if it were this, he would now not be a priest; therefore what he offers is heavenly.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *When he was,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *If then he were,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Because every one who offers, if there were some earthly man who could cleanse the human race, there would be no priest according to the order of Melchisedech, because they who offered the things of the law would be enough; but because they were mortal and sinners, and therefore not able to cleanse the human race, [0656D] Christ came, who should suffice. ⟦s4⟧ But now, that is, in the last age, Christ has obtained a better ministry of the priesthood, by as much as his law is better. ⟦s5⟧ Now from the priesthood and the sacrifice he passes to the difference of the Testaments.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *It was answered,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not when he was beginning, because he had already expressed many things by figures, but when he could not of himself consummate it, he consulted the Lord, who taught him of the figures, as also of the truth.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *And of a better Testament,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Now from the sacrifice and the priesthood he passes to the difference of the Testaments, preferring the New to the Old; in that the New was established in better promises.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *For if that former.* ⟦s2⟧ He proves that the Testament [0657A] of Christ is better than the Old: for that one is not free from fault, this is free. ⟦s3⟧ For if it had been free from fault, a second would not be given, but it is given. ⟦s4⟧ And so it appears that the one was imperfect, this perfect. ⟦s5⟧ *Had been faultless, then indeed.* ⟦s6⟧ He says this in such a way as though it had fault itself. ⟦s7⟧ Which is seen, while it commands without grace, and makes transgressors. ⟦s8⟧ *Would be sought,* by the Lord, who first instructed and brought up little by little, that afterwards he might give the perfect.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Which I made to their fathers.* ⟦s2⟧ And because he had made a special one for certain persons, he determines it. ⟦s3⟧ *Which I made in the day,* that is, in the time of the law, which was given after the going forth out of Egypt. ⟦s4⟧ What therefore he said to Abraham before the law: *In thy seed shall all nations be blessed [n: (Gen. XXII)]*, he afterwards fulfilled in the Gospel. ⟦s5⟧ For that [0657B] promise was spiritual and pertained specially to grace. ⟦s6⟧ *They continued not in the Testament.* ⟦s7⟧ He imputes it to their own fault that they continued not in the Testament, lest the law which they then received should seem to be blamed. ⟦s8⟧ For it is that same law which Christ came not to loose, but to fulfil. ⟦s9⟧ Yet it does not justify without grace, on account of the harm of the old man, which, because it is not healed by the letter that commands and threatens, that is called Old; this, New: on account of the newness of the spirit which heals from the vice of oldness, which he plainly opens when he says: *This is the Testament,* etc.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Giving my laws into minds.* ⟦s2⟧ Not in tables of stone, not in ink, but in the Spirit, by whose presence (who is the finger of God) charity is poured forth in hearts, which is the fulness of the law. ⟦s3⟧ Since indeed the just [0657C] are not terrified from without, but within they love justice. ⟦s4⟧ *Giving laws:* Behold the Testament. ⟦s5⟧ *I will write above.* ⟦s6⟧ As if: which ought to be above, and to rule over them. ⟦s7⟧ *And I will be to them a God.* ⟦s8⟧ That is, they shall live from me and to me, and in time to come they shall see me, which is perfection, that one should not teach another. ⟦s9⟧ This is the difference of the Old and the New, that the one is on stone, this on the heart; there the reward is earth; here the vision of God.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *He shall not teach.* ⟦s2⟧ What lay hidden in the letter, and the people learned through the traditions of the masters, this the Spirit at his coming taught the apostles, and this is what the Prophet says: *Each one shall not teach his neighbour, because all shall know [n: (Jer. XXXI)]*; as it is said in the Gospel: *Then he opened to them the understanding [n: (Luc. XXIV)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ *The greater.* ⟦s4⟧ Understand the greater either in time: as the former, [0657D] who awaited us that come after in the receiving of the penny; or more worthy in knowledge, or in virtue, namely they who were able to understand the incorporeal and unchangeable light, so far as one can in this life: which nevertheless the lesser were able to believe. ⟦s5⟧ When therefore that which is perfect shall have come, and that which is in part shall have been done away, then he who, having taken flesh, appeared to flesh, shall show himself to those that love him. ⟦s6⟧ Then all shall know him from the least even to the greatest, because even the least shall then know perfectly, when he shall have been instructed by himself, not by a greater.
