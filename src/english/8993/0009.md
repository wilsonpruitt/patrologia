## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *For a shadow.* ⟦s2⟧ He has proved that Christ [0660B] died once; but why? or once? ⟦s3⟧ Because the law could not make men perfect, therefore a lamb without spot was to be offered. ⟦s4⟧ *Not the very image.* ⟦s5⟧ That is, the truth, as in a painting, so long as one is laying on the colours, there is a kind of underlay. ⟦s6⟧ Or the underlay is a kind of shadow, and not an image; but when one has dipped the very flowers of the colours, then an image is made. ⟦s7⟧ *They that approach.* ⟦s8⟧ Although the high priests offered unceasingly, year by year, in the holy of holies with the same victims, yet by their own victims they could never be made perfect.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The worshippers once,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The one victim of Christ makes men perfect; and if thousands sin they need no other, because it suffices for all things, and washes every conscience from sins; which the old victim does not, for if it did this, there would be no need for it to be repeated. ⟦s3⟧ Just as a medicine, if it [0660C] be strong, and effectual for health, and able to drive off every sickness, works the whole cure when once applied; but if it is always being applied, it is a plain sign that it has not conferred health; and therefore, because by those sacrifices no one was cured, they were offered frequently.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *A remembrance,* not an absolution; an accusation of weakness, not a showing of power; therefore, that weakness might be shown, and that a memory of sins might be made, God commanded that those things should always be offered.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Thou wouldst not.* ⟦s2⟧ From the time of the passion, in which is the consummation of all victims, those things began to displease. ⟦s3⟧ He never destroyed them before, but was subject to them, when he was presented in the temple with victims. ⟦s4⟧ *But a body thou hast fitted to me.* ⟦s5⟧ The signs are taken away, because the truth has been shown forth, then was the time that [0660D] those should be taken away, and the truth should come. ⟦s6⟧ Nor was the Lord ever delighted in those odours, save in the faith and the desire of him that offered. ⟦s7⟧ Yet he commanded these things to be offered to himself, rather than that they should be offered to idols; and he imposed them for servitude, not for justification, and that they might be a figure of what was to come.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *They have not pleased.* ⟦s2⟧ In those figures the truth was foretold. ⟦s3⟧ They celebrate figures, many knowing the thing of the figure, but more not knowing it, which figures must cease when the thing has been shown forth.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Then.* ⟦s2⟧ When I saw that all those things displeased, and that a body fit for this was given to me. ⟦s3⟧ *I said: Behold I come,* to offer myself, that I may do and fulfil thy will, because thou art my God. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore I come [0661A] to fulfil [d: thy] thy will, because so it is written, and prefigured concerning me in the counsel of the Godhead, which is my head, I who am the book of the human race according as I am man, in which book let men read all things necessary for them, and therefore there is no need of the law.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Saying above.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: This authority notes not only that the old victims did not make men perfect, but that the victim of Christ alone did; but also the taking away of the Old Testament, and the establishing of the New, if the words be diligently noted; because, saying above, *thou wouldst not have those things,* he adds: *Then I said.* ⟦s3⟧ For behold, he takes away the first sacrifice and with it the Old Testament, and appoints as stable the new sacrifice and with it the New Testament.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Sanctified,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The purgation of sins [0661B] having been made, we are reconciled to God.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And every,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Lest the blood offered by the high priests once in the year should seem to have profited less, and the sacrifices made daily by the priests to make men perfect, he adds this, as if he should say: The high priest, offering year by year, does not come to consummation, so also every priest offers continually, and yet by these things is never consummated.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *He sitteth on the right hand.* ⟦s2⟧ Well can he consummate who so deserved to be exalted.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *As to the rest.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, concerning that which remains, namely the glory of the good and the punishment of the wicked. ⟦s3⟧ *A footstool.* ⟦s4⟧ Because, even if they now resist him, and seem not to be made subject, then they shall appear to all as subject to him, by whose command they shall lie under punishments.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And he also beareth witness to us,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Holy Spirit testifies that he has consummated for ever them that are sanctified, and that in the New Testament God will not remember sins. ⟦s3⟧ *After that.* ⟦s4⟧ As if he should say: After he had said that which is not of force for our proof, he added other things which confirm both the things we say: namely *giving laws,* which pertains to consummation; *and their sins and iniquities I will remember no more,* which pertains to sanctification.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *I will write over.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not say *I will write,* but *I will write over,* because those laws are over the strength of men, in the fulfilling of which none shall suffice, save by the help of grace. ⟦s3⟧ In these words it is said that he consummates by virtues, then he subjoins concerning sanctification.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0661D]VERS. 18.-- *But where,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not only can it be seen from this authority that Jesus sanctifies and consummates, but also that an oblation is now, after the time of grace, not to be made for sins, when remission has been made.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Through the veil.* ⟦s2⟧ Our faith is veiled, because faith has no merit where human reason supplies proof.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *In fulness of faith.* ⟦s2⟧ Our faith is perfect, since none of these things is now visible, neither priest nor victim. ⟦s3+4⟧ *Sprinkled in heart.* [var: Vulg. *aspersi corda*, "having hearts sprinkled"] By this it is shown that not faith alone, but life with virtue is sought. ⟦s5⟧ *Washed with water,* that is, with baptism.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *For he is faithful.* ⟦s2⟧ As God is faithful in [0662A] his promises, so he wills that we be faithful to him in our promises. ⟦s3⟧ Let us therefore have faith and confession, so far as in us lies.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *And let us consider,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The perfect man by exhorting others, the lesser by imitating the perfect. ⟦s3⟧ Thence the Apostle exhorts to those things which befit faith.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *As the manner is.* ⟦s2⟧ He blames those who rend the unity of charity, as though they could not dwell among others because of their own sanctity; who rather ought by their own example to have comforted them. ⟦s3⟧ *But comforting.* ⟦s4⟧ He comforts one that labours who persists equally in the labour, because the lightening of a labour is the effort, or the striving, of him that labours with him, as happens on a journey.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *For to us sinning wilfully.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, while we remain in the will of sinning, Christ, who is the victim for sins, does not profit [0662B] us even in this present time. ⟦s3⟧ Whereby it is plain that neither in the time to come. ⟦s4⟧ *After receiving the knowledge of the truth,* that is, although we had come to the faith, on account of which some are secure, as though they should never perish. ⟦s5⟧ He does not say, to them that will, but *wilfully,* because he is wilful who is assiduous in a thing; he is willing, who is so for a time. ⟦s6⟧ Or also, if we should sin by will, not to say by act. ⟦s7⟧ *There is now left no sacrifice for sins;* as in the old law it was granted that victims should be offered more often for sin. ⟦s8⟧ For Christ is not again to be immolated for sins, which was done once, and there is no need of a second time. ⟦s9⟧ But there is rather need to remain in faith and in truth. ⟦s10⟧ But if he does not do this, judgment awaits thee, unless thou be renewed through penance.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Under two or three witnesses.* ⟦s2⟧ Being convicted. [0662C] What then of those against whom the angels bear witness, and the saints, and their own conscience?

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Hath trodden under foot,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He treads Christ under foot who sins freely, without fear and without penance; and he who partakes unworthily esteems the blood polluted, if, being cleansed by it, he returns to his vomit and neglects to repent. ⟦s3⟧ He is injurious to the Spirit who does not receive his benefit thankfully. ⟦s4⟧ *Hath esteemed polluted.* ⟦s5⟧ Such is he who believes that sins are forgiven through carnal observances, from which the blood of Christ draws us back, and so he is polluted and pollutes us, if he draws us back from that from which remission is.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And to the Spirit of grace.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, to the Holy Spirit freely given; or, he does despite to him who gives grace; he who ascribes to carnal observances the remission which the Spirit himself makes, or who casts him off from himself [0662D] by living evilly.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *Revenge to me,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This is said of enemies, who do evil, but not of those who suffer evil. ⟦s3⟧ And here he also comforts them, saying that God remains for ever, and lives. ⟦s4⟧ Accordingly, even if now the tyrants do not receive what has been promised them; yet afterwards they shall receive it, and so it behoves them to groan together, but not us. ⟦s5⟧ For we fall into their hands, but they into the hands of God. ⟦s6⟧ For neither is it he that suffers who suffers any evil, but he that does it; nor is it he that is helped who receives the benefit, but he that bestows the benefit. ⟦s7⟧ Knowing these things therefore, let us be made patient to bear evils, and ready to do good.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0663A]VERS. 31.-- *Call ye to mind.* ⟦s2⟧ He invites them by their former deeds

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CHRYS.)] Just as the best of physicians, when they have cut others, etc., *as far as* but that ye may fall into the hands of God, *call ye to mind,* etc.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The former,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If as novices they endured, it is base if now they fail. ⟦s3⟧ *A great fight,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ They had sufferings, both from their own pains and from the pains of others, with whom they suffered together.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *And on the one hand indeed,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: You endure a fight of sufferings, both in your own sufferings and in your compassion for others, and these are the two parts of the sufferings that contend against us. ⟦s3⟧ *A spectacle.* ⟦s4⟧ Reproach is grievous, it is more grievous when it is made a spectacle to men, and one is upbraided not in secret.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0663B]VERS. 38.-- *He liveth. [n: (AUG.)]* Ye just, ye upright, rejoice in the Lord, for it becometh you, etc., *as far as* and he raises up him that fails, and crowns him that conquers.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *But we,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, we are not the sons of pagans, but of faithful prophets.
