## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- *Now faith is.* Here is the praise and commendation of faith, whose description he sets down. In which he notes three things, namely: what it effects in us, that it is the foundation of all good things, and that it is also of things that appear not. Concerning which it has been more fully said in the Epistle to the Romans: *Faith is the substance of things to be hoped for,* that is, the cause which will one day make the things to be hoped for subsist in us; which is to say: it will make us [0663C] obtain the good things to come. And faith is properly called *substance,* because it stands under the things to be hoped for, and makes them to be in believers in the other life. And it is the foundation of all good things, which no one can change, and without which there is no good building; or, it already makes the things to be hoped for to be in the heart of the believer. *The evidence,* that is, the certitude of things which appear not, and if anyone doubt of it, the proof, where the resurrection to come is further proved; because so believed the patriarchs, the apostles, and the other saints. Faith is the cause of the things to be hoped for, because it is the cause of the justice through which they are to be hoped for. And this is: *In this a testimony,* of justice, because on account of faith God testified that they were just. *The ancients:* He says this, not that they were ancients in age, but the forerunners of these, and so it is plain that faith is not new, but is to be held, since the ancients held it.

[0663D]VERS. 3.-- *By faith we understand.* He uses examples for the commendation of faith, to show that faith is of things that appear not.

VERS. 4.-- *By faith a more plentiful sacrifice.* He shows that the ancient fathers obtained the testimony of justice through faith, setting down another example for the commendation of justice. *Being dead he yet speaketh.* That is, he is matter for us of speaking, that we may give an example from his faith. For death has not extinguished the fame of his faith. From the faith and the patience of those who went before he comforts these men. Abel still speaks, because by his own example he admonishes others to be just. Death indeed slew him, but it did not slay with him his glory and his memory.

[n: (AUG.)] Neither did Henoch and Elias wither with old age through so long an age, [0664A] nor yet, etc., *as far as* before he had deserved to go out thence because of sin.

VERS. 6.-- *That he is.* To be belongs properly to God: because he immutably is.

VERS. 7.-- *By faith Noe,* etc. *And of the justice,* etc. That is, he was just from faith, as his fathers were, for unless he had been just, God would not have commanded this to him.

VERS. 8.-- *By faith he who liveth.* [var: Vulg. Hbr 11:8 *Fide qui vocatur Abraham*, "By faith he that is called Abraham"] Here he comes down to the fathers of the Jews and shows in them, as in those before, that all justice had its origin from faith. And he sets down the example of Abraham. *He obeyed,* God saying to him: « Go out from thy land, and from thy kindred. » This he had already done. For this was said to him when he had already gone out from the land of the Chaldeans, and was already settled in Mesopotamia. Therefore the Lord turned away not his body, but his mind from his [0664B] own land, that he should not wish to return. For he had not gone out thence in mind, but was held by the desire of returning; which desire had to be cut off, God commanding and helping, and he himself obeying.

VERS. 11.-- *Because she believed him faithful.* She laughed at first, though from joy, yet not with full faith: but afterwards, by the words of the angel, she was made firm in faith. Abraham also had laughed, when a son was promised to him, but that laughter was of wonder and of joy, not of doubt.

VERS. 12.-- *From one.* From a womb, or from Abraham, being dead. He calls Sara's womb dead for the work of conceiving, as regards the aged husband; or he calls Abraham himself dead for the work of begetting, namely as regards the aged woman. *As the stars.* This he says as regards the good, that is, those who by the light of virtues and [0664C] by multitude are likened to the stars. *And as the sand.* This he says as regards the wicked, that is, those who by instability and by number are likened to the sand. The sea is heathendom itself: the shore, its rites; to which some Jews were made near, yet not in heathendom.

VERS. 13.-- *And saluting them.* He uses a word transferred from sailors, who look out from afar upon the cities they have longed for, and which, before they enter them, they greet beforehand with a salutation. From the metaphor of sailors, therefore, he says, *And saluting them;* that is, rejoicing in them and desiring them, as sailors do when cities are seen from afar. And therefore they confessed that they themselves were pilgrims, who have nothing in a foreign country. *Strangers.* These are not of the country, and those men confessed that they were not of the world, nor that they had anything in the world, and therefore they confessed this. [0664D] But they who say this give to understand that they seek another country, which they themselves wished to signify. And lest anyone should think that they sought after that from which they had gone out, it is subjoined: *And if indeed,* etc.

VERS. 17.-- *When he was tried.* God did not try Abraham as one who was ignorant of his faith, but that Abraham himself and the world might know the truth and the firmness of his faith.

VERS. 19.-- *And for a parable.* By faith Abraham offered not only in the deed, but also in the signification. For he understood that Christ was signified, whose Godhead does not die; but the ram caught by the horns among the briers, that is, the flesh labouring among the Jews on the horns of the cross, could be offered to God, having been crowned beforehand with Jewish thorns.

[0665A]VERS. 20.-- *Isaac.* He had faith here, not only that the blessed one should be the better, but he also believed that the Christian people would supplant the blessing made and promised to the Jews.

VERS. 21.-- *He adored the top,* etc. Perhaps he had taken the rod from his son, when the son was swearing faith to him, and while he held it, after the words of him that swore, he straightway adored God. For he was not ashamed to bear for so long the ensign of his son's power, where a figure of a great thing to come was foreshadowed, and of the kingdom of Christ that was to be among the nations.

VERS. 22.-- *He gave commandment concerning his bones.* Adjuring the children of Israel that they should carry them away into the land of promise, and lay them up in the sepulchre of their fathers, namely in the double cave, in which are buried Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and the rest and the fathers, where also [0665B] Adam and Eve were resting. Which they willed for this reason, because they foresaw that Christ would be born in that land, that they might rise again with him.

VERS. 26.-- *The reproach of Christ.* This he said, either because Moses suffered for Christ, or because he signified the reproaches of Christ to come: or, because Moses endured them from his own brethren whom he wished to set free, as Christ did from his own whom he had come to redeem. Those men said to Christ: *Thou hast a devil [n: (Joan VII)]*. And these to Moses: *Wilt thou kill us, as thou didst kill the Egyptian [n: (Exod. II)]* [var: Vulg. Ex 2:14 *num occidere me tu vis, sicut heri occidisti Aegyptium*, "wilt thou kill me, as thou didst yesterday kill the Egyptian"] Which yet he bore patiently, as Christ also did.

VERS. 27.-- *Not fearing.* That he had first fled into the land of Madian was not fear, but faith; in that he believed that he would be delivered by God. He would not remain there, lest it should be [0665C] a cause of boasting, if he should cast himself into open danger, and tempt God, which is devilish. For the saints, so long as they have something they may do, ought not to tempt God, as the Lord also gave place to the Jews, who also commanded his own: *If they shall persecute you in one city, flee into another [n: (Matth. X)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 10:23 *Cum autem persequentur vos in civitate ista, fugite in aliam*, "And when they shall persecute you in this city, flee into another"]

VERS. 28.-- *By faith he celebrated the Pasch.* By this assuredly, because he so believed that the people were to be saved: they indeed from Pharao, we from the devil.

VERS. 29.-- *They passed through the Red Sea,* etc. So through baptism the faithful pass over, their sins being remitted; the Egyptians, as it were, being drowned with the devil the prince of darkness.

VERS. 30.-- *By the going round of seven days* being completed by the Levites bearing the ark of God, and sounding with brazen trumpets.

[0665D]VERS. 31.-- *Receiving the spies with peace,* etc. As if: So ought you also, by believing, to receive the two Testaments through the apostles sent by Christ as spies, and so you shall not perish with the unbelievers.

VERS. 32.-- *Of Gedeon.* He, having received the sign of the fleece from the Lord, went against the Madianites with three hundred men, and overcame them by night. *Samson.* He, as is read in the book of Judges, vehemently afflicted the Philistines. *Jephte.* He, when he was going to the battle against the enemies, vowed to the Lord that, if he should obtain a triumph over the enemies, on returning he would offer to God whatever should first meet him: and his daughter met him, whom he immolated.

[0666A]VERS. 33.-- *Who by faith.* Although he declines to say what each one did, yet he says what certain men wrought by faith. *Renewed promises.* The Apostle says *renewed promises* and not promises, because many promises were made to David, as to Abraham. Wisely he avoids the recital of the histories, lest he prolong his discourse, touching briefly on certain things. For neither was he altogether silent, nor in speaking was he made burdensome, he fled thickness and length, lest he weary the hearer.

VERS. 35.-- *And others were racked,* etc. In those Fathers of the Old Testament there were examples of patience, though rare, from the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias, whose blood the Lord says is to be required of those who should persist in the iniquity of their fathers (by whom those men were slain). And in the New [0666B] Testament, there have not been wanting men strong in temporal happiness, in which we experience the mercy of God.

VERS. 38.-- *Of whom the world was not worthy.* That is, of whose manner of life *the world was not worthy,* and therefore let them not be held cheap for the tribulations they endured: or, they were so cheap in men's eyes that they were thought not to be worthy to stand or to dwell among them. And both they and those before them did not at once receive the promised life, until Christ. Why not, is subjoined:

VERS. 40.-- *God providing some better thing for us.* Inasmuch as he would assume our nature, and would more gloriously rejoice with us. If they held the faith, who so long awaited us and rest, much more let us hold it, who receive it immediately after death.
