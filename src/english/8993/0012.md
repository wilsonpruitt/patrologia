## CHAPTER XIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1, 2.-- *Charity.* ⟦s2⟧ From this place the Apostle instructs morally, as if he should say: Because charity is to be had, let it remain in you, and *be not [0669B] forgetful of hospitality. ⟦s3⟧ For by this certain ones have lain hid,* as Lot, not knowing that they were angels, yet knowing who was in them.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *As though bound together with them,* that is, as you would wish help to be given to you if you were bound, or labouring.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Let your manners be.* ⟦s2⟧ After the Hebrews had lost their own goods, they wished to gather again: which the Apostle forbids. ⟦s3⟧ But lest they should say: What if necessaries should fail? he subjoins a consolation. ⟦s4⟧ *Without covetousness.* ⟦s5⟧ The covetous man is he who is tenacious in giving, greedy in receiving. ⟦s6⟧ *I will not leave thee.* ⟦s7⟧ This God says to everyone that hopes in him, as to Josue, so he is with us, *that we may confidently say,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ *Nor forsake.* ⟦s9⟧ He would be forsaken who should perish of hunger: but because this is not so, let not a man be greedy.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0669C]VERS. 6.-- *I will not fear,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Every adversary is a man, even the devil, who is a man from his office, namely in that he deceives man.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Be mindful,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because it is long to speak of each one, he admonishes them to follow their masters.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Jesus Christ,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This pertains to the sentence above, where he testified that God had said: *I will not leave thee, nor forsake thee.* ⟦s3⟧ For it could seem to them that this promise pertained to Josue only, to which he answers, that he will help us also as he helped him. ⟦s4⟧ As if: It is not to be doubted, because he who yesterday, that is in the past, helped Josue, today, that is in the present, helps, and will help those to come throughout all ages.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *We have an altar,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ We must not be zealous [0669D] for meats, because we have an altar, that is the body of Christ, in faith of which prayers and works offered are acceptable to God, *whereof they have no power to eat,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *The tabernacle,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ That is, the observances of the law, as they who distinguish meats and keep other legal things of this kind, which he marks by the name of the tabernacle, because they were imposed for a time; or, our body is rightly called a tabernacle, because we must abide in it for a time, and therefore it must not be served in desires [sic: *serviendium*].

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *For whose,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By a double allegory, of which one is of the Old and the other of the New Testament, he proves that the body of Christ is not to be eaten by those who serve the tabernacle. ⟦s3⟧ For to this end [0670A] the bodies of the animals are burnt outside the camp, and therefore Christ suffered outside the gate, that we, who wish to be cleansed from sins by his body and blood, being about to suffer reproaches and straits, may forsake the delights of the tabernacle. ⟦s4⟧ *Of the animals.* ⟦s5⟧ Christ was signified by those animals.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The gate.* ⟦s2⟧ The gate of the city is the senses of the body, in none of which did Christ sin: and therefore he suffered without. ⟦s3⟧ And let us, the door of the senses being shut to vices, suffer without.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *By him therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ Who is the way for us, from whom we take our example, let us run to martyrdom. ⟦s3⟧ *Of them that trust. [n: (AUG.)]* [var: Vulg. Hbr 13:15 *confitentium*, "of them that confess"] Ye therefore that fear the Lord, praise him, etc., *as far as* of which invisible sacrifice that visible rite was the sign.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0670B]VERS. 16.-- *Of beneficence.* ⟦s2⟧ The opinion of the depraved concerning gluttony being made void, he returns to morals. ⟦s3⟧ *Of beneficence,* that is, of bounty toward others. ⟦s4⟧ And if your substance has been taken away, yet out of those things which you have give alms. ⟦s5⟧ This he had said above: *Not forsaking the assembly.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *That with joy.* ⟦s2⟧ Let us assuredly do these things with joy, when we see men profit in the words of God. ⟦s3⟧ For then does the labourer work with joy in the fields, when he looks upon the trees, and sees fruit; when he looks upon the crop, and sees abundance. ⟦s4⟧ Then he understands that not without cause has he laboured, not without cause has he bent his back, not without cause has he worn his hands, not without cause has he borne cold and heat.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is not expedient for you.* ⟦s2⟧ I do not say: it is not expedient for them, because it profits them also to be made sorrowful concerning you. ⟦s3⟧ For those [0670C] prelates, when they are made sorrowful over your evils, that very sorrow is expedient for them, but not for you. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore let us, prelates and subjects alike, work good in the Lord's field, that we may alike rejoice over the reward.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Pray for us.* ⟦s2⟧ He strikes the pride and the elation of mind of certain bishops, who disdain to beg their subjects to pour out prayers for them. ⟦s3⟧ *We have a good conscience,* that is, our conscience does not accuse us, nor are we conscious to ourselves that we have laid snares for you, we have brought forth nothing with dissimulation, we have done nothing for the sake of gain. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore judge nothing amiss. ⟦s5⟧ *In all things.* ⟦s6⟧ The Apostle was accused of neglecting his own nation, but he shows that he has affection toward all Jews and [0670D] Gentiles, over whose past good things he rejoices, and for whom he wishes better things in time to come.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Doing, in you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For you can have nothing of good, unless he go before and follow after.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *For in few words,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: No one can refuse the length of the discourse, for this indeed was what the adversaries did, and therefore he touches on this covertly.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Salute all them that are set before you.* ⟦s2⟧ As he writes generally to all [sic: *ominibus*], so generally he wishes health to all, prelates and subjects. ⟦s3⟧ For to salute is to wish health.
