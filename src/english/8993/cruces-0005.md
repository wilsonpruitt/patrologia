## 2. The three markers, and why each rival lost

### 2a. `[var:]` at **0656C** — *fixit Deus* for Vulg. *fixit Dominus*

Migne prints the divine name **Deus** at Hbr 8:2 where the Clementine prints **Dominus**, and he
prints it **twice**: once in the closing-quotation span at 0656B (*Deus et non homo.*) and once as the
glossed lemma at 0656C (*fixit Deus,*). The gloss then keeps *Deus* a third time in its own prose
(*quia et vetus posuit Deus, sed per ministrum hominem*).

- **Why it is marked and not merely cruxed:** it is a substituted divine name in a proof-text, the
  class Pattern 14 exists for, and the three-fold internal agreement makes a one-off transcription
  slip in our source implausible.
- **The rival, and why it lost:** the rival was crux-only, on the ground that *Deus*/*Dominus* changes
  no argument in this gloss. It lost because the reader of our English is entitled to know that the
  words on Migne's page are not the words of the Vulgate at a verse the whole chapter is expounding;
  the sense-neutrality argument would suppress the divergence rather than record it.
- **One marker, not three.** It stands at 0656C, on the span Migne actually glosses.
- ⚠ **Plate gate:** this asserts what Migne's page reads. Col. **0656B–C** needs a plate read before ship.

### 2b. `[var:]` at **0658A** — *Tabernaculum enim sanctum* for Vulg. *Tabernaculum enim factum est primum*

The strongest lemma divergence in the stint. Hbr 9:2 opens *Tabernaculum enim factum est primum*;
Migne's lemma is *Tabernaculum enim sanctum.* — three words replaced by one, and the substituted word
(*sanctum*) is the very word the preceding verse's lemmata have just been glossing (*Sanctum,* …
*Saeculare.* at 9:1, and the verse itself closes *quae dicitur Sancta*).

- **Rival 1 (declined): read it as our source's loss** and mark `[ed:]`. Rejected — `[ed:]` requires a
  demonstrated hole, and there is no hole here: the lemma is a complete, well-formed phrase and the
  gloss that follows attaches to it.
- **Rival 2 (declined): crux only.** Rejected because the divergence is **material**: a reader taking
  the lemma as the verse would carry away a "holy tabernacle" that Hbr 9:2 does not contain.
- Rendered as printed, "For the holy tabernacle."; the `[var:]` stands beside it and replaces nothing.
- ⚠ **Plate gate:** col. **0658A** needs a plate read before ship. If the plate reads *factum est primum*,
  the marker is withdrawn and this becomes a Corpus Corporum patch.

### 2c. `[cj:]` at **0659A** — *Ac operibus mortuis* for *Ab operibus mortuis*

Hbr 9:14 reads *emundabit conscientiam nostram **ab** operibus mortuis, ad serviendum Deo viventi*.
Migne's lemma sequence for that verse runs *Obtulit semetipsum* → *Immaculatum* → ***Ac** operibus
mortuis* → *Viventi*, i.e. it walks the verse word by word; the lemma stands exactly where *ab* stands.

- *Ac* is a **real word**, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap and addendum A forbids a `[sic:]` on a
  corpus argument. Pattern 18 is the right instrument: the printed word is real and the faithful
  English misleads, because *ac* makes the fragment a conjunction where *ab* governs the ablative that
  immediately follows it.
- **The rival, and why it lost:** the rival was crux-only, on the ground that the fragment reads
  tolerably either way and the gloss (*id est peccatis*) treats *operibus mortuis* as a substantive
  regardless. It lost because `[cj:]` is **additive** — Migne's *Ac* keeps its English ("And by dead
  works") and the conjecture stands beside it — so nothing is concealed and the reader is not left to
  reconstruct why a preposition became a conjunction. The a/b–a/c confusion is also the cheapest
  possible type error, which is what makes the conjecture worth printing rather than burying.

## 3. Cruces where I fired nothing

### 3a. ⛔ **Two split-type candidates — the plate-read requests I most want honoured**

Both are **non-words as printed** (addendum A's class, which has been right every time about an error
existing) and both sit where a line break plausibly falls:

1. **0654A** — *quia patres suos, qui eos in fide **genu erunt**, imitantur.* Read *genuerunt*, "begot".
   As printed, *genu erunt* is "knee they-will-be", which is not Latin here.
2. **0655D** — *Sicut Christus **per manet**, ita et lex quam attulit.* Read *permanet*, "remains".

**Why no `[sic:]` on either, and this is a deliberate departure from Pattern 7's non-word ruling.**
Hard rule 8a (2026-08-28) is later and governs: a `[sic:]` accuses **Migne's type**, and our Latin is
Corpus Corporum's transcription, not the plate. A **space inserted at a line break** is the single most
likely digitization artifact there is, and the 2026-08-23 batch withdrew seven false `[sic:]` that each
publicly blamed Migne for exactly this class. The ship test therefore applies: unsure, cannot settle it
from the file, so the crux is the safe instrument. Both are rendered for their evident sense ("who begot
them in the faith", "As Christ remains"), which is what test 1 requires — nothing is swallowed.
⚑ **If a plate read confirms the split on the page, both become `[sic:]`; if not, both become
`data/tei-patches/8993.json` entries. Cols 0654A and 0655D.**

### 3b. **0652B** — *propter carnem **absumptam***

*Christus … non secundum id quod natus est de Patre … sed propter carnem absumptam, propter victimam,
quam obtulit pro nobis.* The doctrinal commonplace is *carnem **assumptam***, "the flesh he assumed",
and the whole sentence is about the incarnation.

**Declined, and here is why the conjecture lost the marker rather than the argument.** *absumptam*
(*absumo*, "consume, use up") is a **real, well-formed word**, and it construes in place: the parallel
member is *propter victimam quam obtulit*, and a victim's flesh being consumed is not nonsense in a
sacrificial sentence. Addendum A is 10 for 10 that a real word which reads oddly is Migne's until a
plate says otherwise, and both readers who raised refuted real-word cases in that batch were commended
for asking for the plate instead of patching on the argument. Rendered as printed, "on account of the
flesh consumed". **Plate-read candidate, col. 0652B.** ⚠ Note also brief §4/addendum C: if the plate
reads *assumptam*, the interesting finding is not the typo but that our file has the harder word — the
normalization class runs in both directions.

### 3c. **0655A** — *per hoc quod accipiunt **sacrificium***

Hbr 7:5, the verse being glossed, reads *de filiis Levi **sacerdotium** accipientes, mandatum habent
decimas sumere*. The gloss paraphrases it with *sacrificium*.

**Declined.** I raised it as a `[cj:]` candidate and then withdrew it: the printed reading is not merely
a real word, it is **true of the Levites** — priests do receive the sacrifices (Num. 18), and "because
they receive the sacrifice … they have a commandment in the law" is an argument, not a wreck. Pattern
18's bar is that the English asserts something **false**; this English does not. Rendered as printed.

### 3d. Migne's punctuation, preserved where it hurts

Three places where the printed mark makes the English read as an error, all carried through per
Pattern 8 with the mark in its printed position (band parity is exact for all four chunks):

- **0653D–0654A** — *Vel illi a quibus colitur**.** sunt illi iidem qui se colunt in bonis operibus.*
  A full stop stands between the relative clause and its verb. Carried: "Or those by whom it is tilled.
  are those same men who till themselves in good works". The lower-case "are" is deliberate — it mirrors
  Migne's lower-case *sunt*; supplying a capital would be tidier and would hide the mark's position.
- **0655A** — *Immoratur in commendanda dignitate**.** Melchisedech et excellentia sacerdotii ejus;*
  The same defect: *dignitate Melchisedech* is plainly one phrase and a full stop splits it. Carried:
  "He dwells upon commending the dignity. Melchisedech and the excellence of his priesthood;".
- **0658A** — the VERS. 2 gloss **ends with no terminal stop**: *intus significat Novum*. Carried; the
  English paragraph ends "within signifies the New" with no full stop. Never supply.

Also carried without comment in the text: *Distat inter sacerdotium**,** et sacerdotium* (0655C) keeps
its comma; *sicut et Noe, Abel Abraham* (0654D) keeps its missing comma between the two names.

**One comma NOT carried, and it is the only mark in the stint I could not place.** 0659D–0660A prints
*quod aliqua peccati sui**,** necessitate Christus oblatus sit*. *aliqua … necessitate* is a single
ablative phrase and English word order cannot admit a comma inside it without producing gibberish.
Rendered "that Christ was offered by any necessity of his own sin,". Logged here rather than adjudicated
in the text; comma parity is not a checked class, so nothing is concealed by this being a crux.

### 3e. **0653B** — *Non rursus,* **id est non olim**

The gloss on Hbr 6:1 reads *Non rursus,* id est non olim, ut sicut prius ceciderunt, iterum cadant.*
"Not again, that is, not formerly, that as they fell before, they should fall again" — the *ut* clause
states the outcome the *non* is refusing, so the sentence reads as a self-contradiction. *olim* is a
real word and construes ("not as of old"); no repair is attempted and none is licensed. Rendered as
printed, with both negations carried. **Candidate reading, not marked:** *non olim* for *non iterum* /
*non ita*. Plate-read candidate, col. 0653B.

### 3f. **0656C / 0656D** — a sentence printed twice, with its nouns swapped

*Jam a **sacerdotio et sacrificio** transit ad differentiam Testamentorum* closes the VERS. 4 gloss;
*Jam a **sacrificio et sacerdotio** transit ad differentiam Testamentorum* opens the VERS. 6 gloss, two
paragraphs later. Not Pattern 11 dittography — the runs are not adjacent, they are not identical, and
each sits in a different verse's gloss, so this looks like the recension repeating itself rather than a
compositor doubling a line. Both rendered in full, in place, with the noun order each one prints.

### 3g. Unexpressed subjects and objects I had to supply

Per the brief, each is named with what decides it:

- **0653A** *Nec tamen sic **deserant*** — "Nor yet let them so forsake **them**." Subject = *spirituales*
  from the preceding clause. The object is unexpressed; I read it as **the carnal** (*carnalibus*, the
  dative of the immediately preceding *ista carnalibus non omnino taceant*) rather than *ista*, because
  the following *ut volentes **ea** perducere* already carries *ista* as *ea*, so the two would collide.
  ⚠ **Both construe.** If a later reader prefers "forsake **these things**", the sentence is unharmed.
- **0655C** *quod quia vetus lex non potuit, exclusa est* — *exclusa* is feminine singular and agrees
  with ***lex***, so the thing shut out is the old law, not the grace. Rendered "which, because the old
  law could not give, it was shut out."
- **0655D** *sicut in lege fuerunt multi, quia mortales* — "because **they were** mortal"; the copula is
  unexpressed and the subject is *multi* (the many priests), not the law.
- **0658B** *Christus pro populo suo obtulit, pro se vero **non**, nisi in membris suis* — the elided verb
  is *obtulit*. Rendered "but for himself he did not, save in his members", keeping *non* on the verb.

### 3h. Negation attachment — the sites that fired test 2a's trigger condition

Every printed negative in the four chunks was walked to its host. Four sites had a quantifier,
correlative or pivot adjacent to a plausible verb and were proved from the sentence's own structure:

- **0653A** *ista carnalibus **non omnino** taceant* — host is *omnino taceant* as a unit ("do not
  altogether keep silence"), **not** *taceant* alone, which would say they keep silence entirely. The
  *propter catholicam fidem, quae omnibus praedicanda est* that follows requires that they do speak.
- **0654D** *Christus **omnino non** habuit initium neque finem* — host is *habuit*; *omnino*
  intensifies the negation rather than being negated. Rendered "absolutely did not have a beginning nor
  an end", with *neque* carried separately. (This is the 8960 @0734B class; English word order was
  checked for scope reversal.)
- **0657C** *justi **non extra** terrentur, **sed** intus diligunt justitiam* — the *sed* correlative
  fixes the host on ***extra***, not on *terrentur*. Rendered "are not terrified **from without**, but
  within they love justice." (The 8960 @0740B model, handled correctly.)
- **0659B** *in cujus morte constat **terrena non** promitti, **sed aeterna*** — the *sed aeterna* pivot
  fixes the host on ***terrena***. Rendered "it is certain that **not earthly things** are promised, but
  eternal", not "earthly things are not promised", which would be the same words in the wrong scope.
- **0659A** *non modo gentilium, sed et earum praevaricationum* — ordinary *non modo … sed et*; the
  second *non* four words later (*quas lex ostendebat, **non** solvebat*) attaches to *solvebat* and
  stays there.
- **0654A** *velint semper eadem ipsa discere* — ⚑ checked against the 8987 @0624B defect explicitly.
  There is **no** negation in or near this clause; *velint* is rendered positively, "they wish always to
  learn these same things."
