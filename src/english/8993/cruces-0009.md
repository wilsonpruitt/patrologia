## 4. `[cj: …]` — 0661D *vetata*

> *VERS. 20.-- Per velamen.* **Fides nostra est vetata**, quia fides non habet meritum, cui
> humana ratio praebet experimentum.

*vetata* is a perfectly good participle of *veto* — "forbidden" — so Pattern 12 has nothing to
wrap and Pattern 7 renders it. And the rendered page then says "Our faith is forbidden",
which is not what the gloss asserts. This is Pattern 18's founding shape exactly (*munde* /
*mundo* at 11081 @0030B).

- **The conjecture is *velata*, "veiled".** The lemma being glossed is ***Per velamen***, and
  the clause that follows is Gregory's commonplace that faith has no merit where human reason
  supplies proof — i.e. faith is *veiled*, and is meritorious precisely because it is.
- **The rival, and why it lost.** *vetata* read as "forbidden" was tested against a possible
  sense "our faith is fenced off / made inviolable" — it fails, because *veto* takes a person
  or an action as its object, not a thing held back from view, and because that reading leaves
  *Per velamen* doing no work in its own gloss.
- **The gloss is additive** (Pattern 18): Migne's word keeps its English and the conjecture
  stands beside it. Corpus frequency (`vetata` 1 · `velata` 0 · `velamen`/`velamine` 51) was
  **not** used to fire this — addendum A rules frequency worthless for real words. The
  argument is the sentence's own.

## 5. Dittography — 0661A

> Ideo venio [0661A] ad implendam ***tuam*** voluntatem ***tuam***…

Two identical copies of *tuam*, not adjacent, straddling *voluntatem*. **Per 11a the marker
sits on the INTRUSION, not on the second copy**, and the intrusion is the **first**: Heb 10:7
reads *voluntatem tuam* and the gloss quotes it in that order eight words earlier
(*ut faciam et compleam voluntatem tuam*), so the postposed copy is the true text and the
preposed one is a false start with no slot of its own. English: `to fulfil [d: thy] thy will`.
Looks like a compositor's doubling, not a textual one.

## 8. The unexpressed subject — every supplied pronoun, named

- **0660C** *quod non vetus* → "which the old **victim** does not". *vetus* is the neuter
  adjective standing for *hostia vetus*, from *Una hostia Christi* in the same sentence.
- **0660C** *Nunquam ea ante destruxit* → "**He** never destroyed them before". Christ, from
  *Probavit quod Christus fuerit semel mortuus* at the head of the chapter and from *in templo
  cum hostiis praesentatus* in the same clause.
- **0662C** *et ita pollutus est et nos polluit, si… nos retrahit* → "**he** is polluted and
  pollutes us". ⚠ Two candidates: *sanguis Christi* (the nearest nominative) and the man of
  *Talis est qui…*. **The man wins from the structure**: Christ's blood draws us *from* carnal
  observances, so it cannot be the subject of *nos retrahit* away from *unde est remissio*.
  Naming the blood would have made the page say Christ's blood pollutes us.
- **0664A** *Prius risit* → "**She** laughed at first". Sara, from Hbr 11:11's *Fide et ipsa
  Sara*, not from the sentence; the gloss never names her.
- **0665D** *Ipse, accepto signo velleris* → "**He**", Gedeon, from the lemma *De Gedeon*
  immediately above. Likewise *Hic* → Samson, *Hic* → Jephte, each from its own lemma.
- **0669B** *ut Loth nesciens esse angelos, sciens tamen quis in eis esset* → "as Lot, **not
  knowing** that **they** were angels, yet knowing who was in **them**". Both plurals are the
  *quidam* of the lemma.

**Pattern 17 (thou = singular).** *te terret* / *te exspectat* / *exceptus es* → **thou**
(singular in the Latin). *vobis · vestra · estis · erigite · dubitetis · Salutate* → **you /
ye** (plural). The switch at 0667C is Migne's own — *volens **te** amatorem boni esse* inside
a paragraph otherwise addressed to the plural — and it is reproduced, not levelled.
