## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1, 2.-- *If there be any consolation,* etc. If you wish to have consolation in the things of Christ, that is, in the remission of sins and in the other gifts; or: if you wish to console me, *if any comfort of charity* be yours, that is, if you wish that the charity of the churches which pray and do good things for the brethren may be to you a comfort and a strengthening in your adversities. *If any fellowship of the spirit.* On account of this fellowship, they upon whom the Holy Spirit first came spoke with the tongues of all nations. *If any bowels.* That is, if you have the affection of piety [0603B] toward me. I do not say compassion itself, that is, the showing forth of mercy. If these things, I say, you wish to profit you, *fulfil my joy,* that is, be humble, *of one mind,* which you will be if you have the same mind, etc.

VERS. 3.-- *Nothing through contention.* Note that there had been contentions among them. *Superior,* etc. Truly this is to be esteemed, because there can be in another something hidden whereby he is superior, even if our own good, whereby we seem to be superior, be not hidden.

VERS. 4.-- *Not the things that are their own.* Here he teaches how they may avoid contention and vainglory, and how they may esteem others superior, namely if they consider not their own good things, but those of others.

VERS. 5.-- *For let this be your mind,* etc. As if to say: [0603C] Let each one not seek his own advantage, but that of others, as Christ did. To patience and to humility I invite you. For you ought to be minded in yourselves in that which was in Christ. As Christ sought not his own things, but ours, so let us bear one another's burdens.

VERS. 6.-- *Who being in the form.* He sets high things concerning Christ before all else, that those in whom there is place for abasement may not disdain to be humbled. *He thought it not robbery.* By usurping what would not be his own, as did the devil and the first man. Or, he did not judge himself to be equal to God according to his humanity, which would have been to commit robbery, that is, to presume upon what is not his own. *But he emptied himself.* The height having been shown, he adds concerning the humility.

[n: (Aug., Ench. c. 35 et 36)]. But he emptied himself, [0603D] taking the form of a servant, etc., *as far as* it came about that the man Christ could have no sin.

*The form of a servant.* Not losing the form of God: the form of a servant came to him, the form of God did not depart: *He emptied himself.* To empty oneself is to show oneself visible out of the greatness of his own invisibility.

VERS. 7.-- *Made.* According to the form of a servant, he who was unmade according to the form of God, and also by his own *Habit.* That is, by his manner of life.

VERS. 8.-- *He humbled himself,* etc. [n: (CHRYS.)] Of his own accord the Son obeyed the Father, not as though into a servile, etc., *as far as* that he honoured the Father most of all.

VERS. 9.-- *For which cause.* The humility of Christ having been shown, [0604A] which appeared chiefly in the passion; now he shows concerning his glory, and what and how much humility merits.

VERS. 10.-- *That in the name.* Here he opens to whom the name of God has been given, to whom every creature bends the knee, that is, to God. But perhaps it might be said that the man was God by adoption, and thus Christ in part true God, in that in part he will be adoptive. But the creature does not bend the knee to an adoptive God, but to a true one. Yet it is agreed that Christ is true God, to whom the knee is bent, not adoptive.

VERS. 11.-- *That the Lord,* etc. Which here certain men deny, saying: How can it come to pass that a man should be in the glory of God the Father? but if he be adoptive, a man who is God cannot be in the glory of God the Father.

[0604B]VERS. 13.-- *For it is God.* Not even concerning the perseverance in good itself did God will his saints to glory in their own strength, but in himself: who gives them not only such help as he gave to the first man: without which they cannot persevere if they will, but works in them also the very willing, so that, since they will not persevere unless they both can and will, both the possibility and the will of persevering may be given them by the bounty of divine grace. For so far is their will kindled by the Holy Spirit, that therefore they can because they so will: therefore they so will, because God works that they may will.

VERS. 14.-- *And all things.* He joins patience to humility.

VERS. 15.-- *Of a depraved nation,* etc. That is, of the wicked, who deprave and pervert right order. [0604C] Who are called a nation, because they are not born again of water and the Holy Spirit. *As lights.* Well does he compare the saints to lights. For as the night does not extinguish the stars of heaven, so worldly iniquity does not darken the minds of the faithful cleaving to the heaven of Holy Scripture.

VERS. 20.-- *For I have no man,* etc. At this time there were many hirelings with the Apostle, but of sons or pastors none save Timothy.

VERS. 21.-- *All,* etc. These are the hirelings, who are to be borne with, because they have in their mouth the same things which the pastors also have, who assuredly are to be loved. But thieves, who preach false things, and robbers, who kill, are to be shunned.
