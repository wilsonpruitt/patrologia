## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Joy, and consolation.* [var: Vulg. *gaudium meum, et corona mea*, "my joy and my crown". The gloss printed beneath this lemma expounds the crown and nothing else, and leaves Migne's own word unglossed] That is, through whom I am gladdened in the present and shall be crowned in the future. ⟦s3⟧ For when the disciples are victors in the contest, the master will be worthy of the crown.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *I beseech Evodia,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ I admonish all, but especially Evodia and Syntyche: because these religious women were wont to receive preachers.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Names.* ⟦s2⟧ They who are distinguished by merits shall have diverse mansions in the eternal house. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: [0607B] *In my Father's house there are many mansions [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ *The book of life.* ⟦s5⟧ The predestination in which all who are to be saved have been predestined. ⟦s6⟧ As if: Do not, O Philippians, take it hard that I have not named you all one by one in my epistle, for although you are not written in it, yet in the book of life you are contained.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Rejoice,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Being gladdened over their faith and their works, the Apostle, that they may be the more eager, desires that they, advancing in this zeal, should rejoice in the Lord, and he repeats it, that he may show that he truly rejoices in them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Moderation.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, right measure, so that all joy may be in the Lord, and that others may learn this through you. ⟦s3⟧ Or, *let moderation be known to all,* faithful and unbelieving, that they may imitate it, lest they be able to reprehend. ⟦s4⟧ *The Lord is nigh,* that is, ready to give whatsoever is needful [0607C] in spiritual or in temporal things. ⟦s5⟧ Be solicitous and fearful about nothing. ⟦s6⟧ But your petitions concerning those things which you wish to obtain.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *But in every prayer.* ⟦s2⟧ Prayer and supplication are made in common for oneself or for others, concerning whatsoever things, but petition is made concerning things necessary by name. ⟦s3⟧ *Let them be made known.* ⟦s4⟧ This making known comes about by assiduity, and by watchfulness of prayer. ⟦s5⟧ Then at last peace keeps the hearts in Christ. ⟦s6⟧ He says this for this reason, because he who has peace with God does not fear an adverse mind. ⟦s7⟧ *With God.* ⟦s8⟧ Or to the angels, who offer them to God. ⟦s9⟧ Whence: When you were praying, I offered your prayer before God. ⟦s10⟧ Or, let them be made known for us with God through endurance, not with men through boasting.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And the peace of God,* that is, God, who is the highest peace, [0607D] which cannot even be thought, and therefore outside him there is need of nothing. ⟦s2⟧ *It surpasseth all understanding.* ⟦s3⟧ Ours, not that of those who always behold the face of the Father. ⟦s4⟧ Or, the peace whereby God himself is at peace *surmounts all understanding:* except his own, because neither can we know it thus, nor can any angels.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For the rest, brethren,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Thus far he has admonished against persecutors and against false preachers. ⟦s3⟧ But for the rest, in the conclusion, he sets forth the things which belong to perfection. ⟦s4⟧ *Whatsoever things are just.* ⟦s5⟧ This toward one's neighbour. ⟦s6⟧ *Holy,* in one's own life; and, that a lesser thing be named, *whatsoever things are lovely,* that is, worthy to be loved, [0608A] as a modest gait, humble speech and the like.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of good report. [n: (AUG.)]* For ourselves our own life, for others a good report is necessary. ⟦s2⟧ Accordingly whosoever guards his life from the crimes of shameful deeds does well to himself; but whosoever [guards] his good report is merciful toward others also.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *I rejoiced,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Here he calls to mind that they had often sent him what was necessary, whereby he shows that his own cheerfulness has been made the readier, because in that wherein they had become negligent they did it again with diligence applied, that, being made mindful of their apostle, they might send forth fruits to be stored in the heavenly granary. ⟦s3⟧ *At some time,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ At some time, because they were not able at every time. ⟦s5⟧ *You have flourished again.* ⟦s6⟧ By ministering to me what was necessary. ⟦s7⟧ Which ministration is called a flower, because from it the fruit of eternal life comes forth. ⟦s8⟧ *For me.* ⟦s9⟧ Because your compassion profits me. ⟦s10⟧ But lest [0608B] they should seem to have broken off for some evil cause, he adds: *now you have flourished again,* but before you were occupied and hindered by certain adversities.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Not as though.* ⟦s2⟧ Not on his own account, but he rejoices over their advancement.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *I know both how to be brought low.* ⟦s2⟧ He who is not broken by want is not drawn back from the giving of thanks. ⟦s3⟧ He who is not inflamed with the desire of temporal things knows how to be brought low, that is, to suffer need. ⟦s4⟧ *To abound.* ⟦s5⟧ He who is not exalted by the things received, who does not wrest them to the use of vainglory, who does not alone possess what he has received, but mercifully divides it with the needy, knows how to abound. ⟦s6⟧ He who, when food is received, [gives it] not to the gorging of the belly, nor gives more to the flesh than necessity demands, knows how to be filled. ⟦s7⟧ He who bears the lack of food [0608C] without murmuring, and does not, for the necessity of sustenance, do anything whereby the soul may run into the snare of sin, knows how to hunger.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Not that I seek.* ⟦s2⟧ The gift is the thing itself which is given, as a present, food, drink and the like. ⟦s3⟧ But the fruit is good works, and the right will of the giver. ⟦s4⟧ Whence the Lord in the Gospel did not say simply: *He that receiveth a just man or a prophet,* but added: *in the name of a just man or of a prophet.* ⟦s5⟧ For to receive a prophet or a just man is the gift. ⟦s6⟧ To do this in the name of a just man or of a prophet is the fruit. ⟦s7⟧ The raven and the widow are read to have fed Elias. ⟦s8⟧ But by the raven, which [did it] not in the name of a just man, he was fed with the gift, by the widow, with the fruit, because she knew that she was feeding the man of God. ⟦s9⟧ *But the fruit.* ⟦s10⟧ That is, the good and right will joined to good working. [0608D] For he does not so much rejoice that his own need has been relieved, as he rejoices with them over their fruitfulness.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *For an odour.* ⟦s2⟧ The things which have been sent by you please God as a most sweet odour. ⟦s3⟧ They are compared also to prayer, which is the incense of God. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it is said: *Give alms, and behold all things are clean unto you [n: (Luc. XI)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *May he fulfil all your desire.* ⟦s2⟧ Not in earthly things, but in glory. ⟦s3⟧ Or, by the glory of Christ the desires of the saints are fulfilled, so that Christ himself may thence be glorious.
