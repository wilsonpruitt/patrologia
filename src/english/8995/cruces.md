## §2 · THE DIVERGENCES **DECLINED**, each with its reason

Twelve spans diverge from the Clementine and took **no** `[var:]`. A blind reader will look
here, so each is argued rather than listed.

1. **0601C `*sicut justum est sentire,*`** — Vulg. 7 *sicut est mihi justum hoc sentire*. The
   glossator has just quoted the same clause's opening as its own lemma (*Sicut est mihi*) and
   is now re-quoting the remainder inside his own sentence, dropping the pronoun he has
   already used. **Word order and one dropped demonstrative, no sense at stake.** Pattern 14's
   bar is a divergence that changes the sense.
2. **0602A `*Pro vobis gratias ago: Confido. Vos autem volo scire,*`** — not a citation at all
   but the glossator's **précis of the epistle's movement so far**, stitching vv. 3, 6 and 12.
   Marking a précis as a variant would misdescribe what is on the page. ⚑ **But note what it
   proves:** his own précis prints *Vos autem volo scire*, WITH the pronoun that his lemma at
   the head of the same paragraph drops. See §4.
3. **0602C `*Christus mihi vivere,*`** — the glossator's re-quotation of v. 21, reordered so
   that it can sit inside *quia …*. Same class as (1).
4. **0603B `*Superiores,*`** — agrees; the note is about the ENGLISH. The Vulgate clause is
   *superiores sibi invicem arbitrantes* and the fragment-expansion convention would give
   "esteeming others better than themselves." **It is rendered "*Superior,*"** because the
   gloss beneath it expounds the WORD, twice (*quo superior sit*, *quo videmur superiores
   esse*), and an expansion into the familiar clause would leave the gloss with no hook —
   the Song-of-Songs ruling's case 2 (*stylus*/*epistylium*, *murenulae*/*ferculum*): where
   the gloss is about the word, the word stands.
5. **0604B `*Deus enim.*`** — Vulg. 13 *Deus est enim*. A two-word incipit that has lost the
   copula. Nothing in the gloss (an Augustinian excerpt on perseverance) turns on it, and the
   English "*For it is God.*" supplies no word the Latin lacks: it renders *Deus enim* as the
   clause it opens.
6. **0604D `*Qui Spiritu Dei,* vel, *Deo,*`** — Vulg. 3 *qui spiritu servimus Deo*. This is a
   real divergence and it took no marker **because Migne prints the received reading himself,
   in the same breath, as his own alternative** (*vel, *Deo,**). The `[cj:]` decline-class
   "anything the edition already glosses" is written for conjectures, but its argument is
   general: **a reading the reader already meets does not need ours beside it.** Logged here
   so the decline is visible; a later editor who disagrees will find it named.
7. **0605A `*Quanquam et ego,*`** — Vulg. 4 *quamquam ego habeam confidentiam **et** in carne*.
   The *et* has moved from "also in the flesh" to "I also." A real shift of emphasis — but the
   gloss (*Ne videatur Apostolus contemnere in aliis quod non habet*) rests on neither
   position, and Migne re-quotes the lemma two lines later as the bare *Quanquam*. Declined as
   positional; named because it is the closest call in this section.
8. **0607B `*modestia nota sit omnibus*`** — *vestra* dropped and the quotation cut before
   *hominibus* so that the glossator can finish it his own way (*fidelibus et infidelibus*).
   **The truncation is the gloss's method, not a variant text.**
9. **0607D `*superat omnem sensum:*`** — the simplex for the Vulgate's compound *exsuperat*,
   in the glossator's own second, alternative construal. See §4 for how the English keeps the
   two verbs apart without importing the familiar English of either.
10. **0607D `*quaecunque sunt amabilia,*`** — *sunt* supplied, which the Vulgate carries only
    on the first member of the eight-fold series. A compositor's regularization at worst;
    no sense at stake.
11. **0608C `*Qui recipit justum vel prophetam,*` and `*in nomine justi vel prophetae.*`** —
    Matt. X, 41 is a **doubled** saying (prophet … prophet, just man … just man) and the
    glossator explicitly **collapses it into one** in order to make his point about *datum*
    versus *fructus*: `non ait simpliciter … sed addidit`. He is analysing the verse, not
    quoting it. A `[var:]` here would report as a textual variant what is an act of exegesis.
12. **0608D `*In odorem.*`** — Vulg. 18 *odorem suavitatis*, in apposition, without *in*. The
    preposition changes nothing the gloss uses (*placent Deo ut odor suavissimus*).

**Also declined, and it is not a lemma:** the Augustine excerpt at 0603C–D opens
*Exinanivit autem se formam servi accipiens* against the Vulgate's *sed semetipsum exinanivit,
formam servi accipiens*. Pattern 14 is for **scripture quoted as scripture**; this is
Augustine's own sentence in the *Enchiridion*, reported by Migne as an excerpt. Rendered as
printed, no marker.

### The two places where the hymn's ENGLISH could have gone wrong, and what was done

**(a) `*Non rapinam.*` — where the negation attaches.** *non* governs **rapinam**, not
*arbitratus est*: he did not judge it *robbery*. The English is "*He thought it not robbery.*"
— negative sitting on "robbery," which is where Migne prints it. The fragment is expanded to
its clause (convention 3) and the expansion coincides with the traditional English because
**the printed Latin coincides with the Vulgate**, which the register expressly permits; it was
not reached for.

⚠ The gloss then offers a **second** construal in which the negation moves: *Vel, non est
arbitratus se aequalem Deo esse secundum humanitatem* — here *non* is on the verb ("he did not
judge himself equal to God according to his humanity"). **Migne prints two different negation
placements two lines apart, and the English carries each where he puts it.** That contrast is
the single most easily flattened thing in the chunk.

**(b) `*Factus.* … qui erat infectus …`** — *infectus* is **in-factus, "unmade"**, played
against the lemma *factus*. It is a privative and it is carried: "he who was **unmade**
according to the form of God." Rendering it "uncreated" would be the theologically expected
word and would lose the play on the lemma; rendering it "tainted" (the other *infectus*) would
be a different word altogether. Plate-confirmed at n306. See also §0 on the plate's full stop
after *suo*.

## §4 · THE CRUCES PROPER

### ⭐⭐⭐ 0607A (VERS. 1) · *Gaudium, et consolatio* — THE LEMMA IS DEFECTIVE AND ITS OWN GLOSS PROVES IT

Migne prints `VERS. 1.-- *Gaudium, et consolatio.* Id est per quos in praesenti laetificor et
in futuro **coronabor**. Discipulis enim in agone victoribus, dignus erit **corona** magister.`

Phil. IV, 1 reads *gaudium meum, et **corona** mea*. Migne's lemma has *consolatio* where the
Vulgate has *corona* — **and the two sentences of gloss beneath it expound *corona* and
nothing else**: the master will be *crowned*, the master is worthy of the *crown*. The lemma
word has no purchase on its own gloss.

**Rendered as printed** — "*My joy, and my consolation.*" — with
`[var: Vulg. *gaudium meum, et corona mea*, "my joy and my crown"]`. This is the single most
valuable finding in the work and it is exactly the shape 7a″ describes in reverse: not a
translator conforming a lemma to the familiar English, but **a plate whose lemma has drifted
off the verse its own gloss is written for.**

⚠ **No `[cj:]`.** The bar is that the printed word makes the sentence assert something the
author argues against, and *consolatio* asserts nothing false — the gloss simply answers a
word that is not there. Pattern 14 is the right home, and Pattern 18's own boundary rule
settles it: `[var:]` inside a quoted scripture, `[cj:]` in the author's own prose.

### ⭐⭐ 0602A (VERS. 12) · *Scire autem volo* — A DROPPED *vos*, CONTRADICTED SIX WORDS LATER

Vulg. I, 12 is *Scire autem **vos** volo fratres*. Without *vos* the lemma says "**I** wish to
know," not "I wish **you** to know" — the subject of the infinitive changes, which is as
material as a divergence gets in three words.

**And the same paragraph carries the correction.** The glossator's own précis two lines below
prints `*Vos autem volo scire,*` **with** the pronoun. So the plate contains both readings,
and the lemma is the defective one.

Rendered as printed ("*But I wish to know,* etc.") with
`[var: Vulg. *Scire autem vos volo*, "I wish you to know"]`. The précis is rendered with its
own pronoun ("*But I wish you to know,*"), because that is what Migne prints there. ⚠ **The
two English readings differ on the page and that is not an inconsistency — it is the plate.**

### ⭐⭐ 0602B (VERS. 17) · *Non puro animo* for *non sincere*

Vulg. I, 17 is *Christum annuntiant **non sincere***. Migne's lemma is *Non puro animo*, "not
with a pure mind" — a different phrase, not a different form of the same word, and quite
possibly an Old Latin reading. The gloss is built on it (*Et ne viderentur correcti, addit*).
Rendered as printed, `[var: Vulg. *non sincere*, "not sincerely"]`.

### ⭐⭐ 0607A (VERS. 20) · *etiam Dominum exspectamus* for *Salvatorem … Dominum nostrum Jesum Christum*

Vulg. III, 20 is *unde etiam **Salvatorem** exspectamus **Dominum nostrum Jesum Christum***.
Migne's lemma keeps *etiam … exspectamus* and replaces the whole double object with a bare
*Dominum*. Rendered as printed, `[var:]` carrying the Vulgate in full. ⚑ Note this is the one
divergence where the gloss is indifferent (*praeter commoda quae jam habemus*), so it would
have been the easiest of the seven to leave unmarked.

### 0601D (VERS. 10) · *sine offensione* for *sine offensa*

A different noun of the same root, in the same slot, with the same sense. Marked because it is
a lexical difference rather than a matter of order, and because Pattern 14's own worked
instance (10083) turns on precisely such a substitution; **the sense is not at stake and the
crux says so**, so a later editor who wants to withdraw this marker has the argument here.

### 0602C (VERS. 21) · *Mihi autem vivere Christus est* for *Mihi enim*

*enim* → *autem*: an explanatory connective becomes an adversative one, at the head of the
most-quoted sentence in the chapter. Marked. The gloss (*Quasi diceret: Merito Christum
magnifico sive vita, sive morte*) reads it as **resumptive**, which suits either particle.

### 0605B (VERS. 8) · *praeeminentem* for *eminentem*

Vulg. III, 8 *propter **eminentem** scientiam*. Migne prints the compound, and prints it
**after** its noun, split across his gloss (`propter *scientiam* quam de Jesu habemus,
*praeeminentem,* caeteris scientiis`). Rendered "*pre-eminent,*" with the marker. The word
order is the glossator's construction and took no separate note.

### 0606D (VERS. 19) · *circumcicis* — `[sic:]`, WITH THE SENSE KEPT OUTSIDE

`et gloriantur in pudendis **circumcicis**`. There is no such Latin word. It is *circumcisis*
with a `c` for an `s` — the whole corpus of Latin here prints **172 `circumcis-` forms and not
one other `circumcic-`**, which is Pattern 9's own test ("reserve carry-through for forms
unique to this plate") answered by count rather than by feel.

English: `they glory in the parts of shame, **circumcised** [sic: *circumcicis*], which is to
be wise in earthly things`.

⚑ **7a⁗ test run, all four questions.** Strip the marker and the clause reads "they glory in
the parts of shame, circumcised, which is to be wise in earthly things" — it keeps its verb
(*gloriantur*), it carries no negation to lose, it keeps its conjunction, **and it keeps its
object** (*in pudendis*). The marked run is an attributive adjective, and its sense stands in
plain English beside it. Nothing is stranded.

✅ **THE PLATE WAS READ AT THIS WORD, AND IT AGREES WITH THE TWIN.** Raised here as an open
question (is this Migne's defect or our transcription's?), it was answered the same session by
the orchestrator: **leaf n307 (cols 605/606, right column), read at full resolution, prints
`circumcicis`** — the second `c` unmistakably a `c` and not an `s`, matched against the
letterforms of *faciunt* and *sapere* on the neighbouring lines. **Our twin agrees with the
plate.** So the defect is **Migne's own type**, the `[sic: *circumcicis*]` stands, and there is
**no TEI patch** to make.

⛑ **The negative is worth recording because two of its siblings came back positive.** The same
batch found two places where the twin had silently departed from the plate — Esther 0739D
(`convivium` for the plate's `convivum`) and Colossians 0611A (`consilio` for the plate's
`concilio`). This was the third candidate of the three and the only one to come back clean.
**"Is this Migne's defect or our transcription's?" is now a live question in this corpus, not a
formality**, and the corpus-frequency evidence that made the plate read worth doing (172
`circumcis-` forms and no other `circumcic-`) is the way to raise it.

### 0605D · *resurrectionis Christi Et in hac vita* — A FULL STOP MIGNE DOES NOT PRINT

`quae nostra resurrectio est virtus et efficacia resurrectionis Christi Et in hac vita habere
societatem in patiendo`. The capital *Et* announces a new sentence and no stop precedes it.
**Pattern 8 forbids supplying one**, so the English runs on identically: "…the power and
efficacy of the resurrection of Christ And in this life to have fellowship in suffering."
The reading is odd on the page and it is odd on the plate's terms, not ours.

The clause it sits in is itself unconstruable as printed — *quae nostra resurrectio est virtus
et efficacia resurrectionis Christi* ("which our resurrection is the power and efficacy of
Christ's resurrection"), with *quae* and *nostra resurrectio* competing for the same slot.
Rendered as closely as the words allow; **no emendation, no marker** — nothing here is a
non-word, and the defect is syntactic, which Pattern 18a explicitly leaves declined.

### 0606B · *Et si ita de me. quid me qui de aliis?* — RENDERED LITERALLY, `[cj:]` DECLINED

Printed exactly so: a full stop mid-clause and an interrogative that will not construe. English:
"And if it is thus of me. what of me who of others?"

- The stop is Migne's and stays (Pattern 8).
- The question mark is Migne's and stays; **it is one of the five in the work.**
- The likely intention is *quid de aliis?* ("what of others?"), with *me qui* an eye-skip from
  the preceding *de me*. **Declined for `[cj:]`** because Pattern 18's bar is an English that
  *asserts something false*, and this English asserts nothing at all — 18a's own ruling puts
  anomalies that merely fail to construe outside the class, and this is not a negation or a
  correlative. Recorded here, which is where 18a says an uncertain reading lives.

### 0607D · *Exsuperat* vs *superat* — TWO VERBS, TWO ENGLISH WORDS, NEITHER OF THEM THE FAMILIAR ONE

The lemma is *Exsuperat omnem sensum* (Vulgate); the glossator's alternative construal
re-quotes it as the simplex *superat omnem sensum*. English has one obvious word for both, and
the one obvious word ("passeth all understanding") is the Authorized Version's, which would
conform the lemma AND erase the difference in a single stroke.

Taken: **"*It surpasseth all understanding.*"** for the compound, **"*surmounts all
understanding:*"** for the simplex. Neither is the received English phrase; the pair keeps
Migne's two verbs distinguishable in the English as they are in the Latin. Declined: rendering
both "surpasses" (the difference would vanish), and rendering the second "passeth" (the AV
import, on the weaker of the two verbs).

### 0604D · *de caetero pseudoapostolos, ne legalia ab eis recipiatis moneo* — ANACOLUTHON, KEPT

*pseudoapostolos* is accusative with no verb to govern it: *moneo* takes the *ne* clause, not
the noun. English: "for the rest, the false apostles, I admonish you not to receive the
observances of the law from them" — the noun left hanging as it hangs in the Latin, with the
comma Migne prints rather than a dash or a supplied preposition. No marker: a real word in a
real case, merely unattached.

### 0606A · *jam perfectus sum … viator. Perfectus enim erat viator* — MIGNE'S OWN PERSON SHIFT

First person, then third, of the same man, one sentence apart. Reproduced. The same shift runs
through 0601C (*Et exponit causam* → *bonum vobis desidero*) and 0605B (*ut in futuro Christum
habeant praemium: et hic sim in illo membrum*). **This gloss moves between reporting Paul and
speaking as Paul without warning, and it does so at least four times.** It is a feature of the
book, not a defect list; no marker anywhere, and the English follows the person printed.

### 0602D–0603A (VERS. 30) · *a qua spiritum malignum exclusi*

Acts XVI, 16–23. *excludo* is "shut out, expel," not "shut in": "out of whom I **shut out** the
malignant spirit." Recorded because the first draft read "shut up," which reverses it, and
because 8982 @0616B glosses the same episode with a different verb (*ejecerat*) — **a
neighbouring book is a hint, never an authority.**

### 0605A · *latria* and *dulia* stay Latin AND stay roman

Migne prints them in roman. The register would ordinarily italicize a technical term left in
Latin — but **italic parity with the twin is sacred and outranks the register's typography
preference.** They are carried as English-alphabet words in roman, unexplained, exactly as he
prints them.

### 0605A (VERS. 5) · *quando facta est separatio in servo Salomonis*

Jeroboam, Solomon's servant (III Kings XI–XII). Migne does not name him and neither does the
English: "when the separation was made **under** the servant of Solomon." *in* + ablative of
attendant circumstance; "in the servant of Solomon" is not English and "in the time of
Jeroboam" would supply a name the plate withholds.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Repair 1 · 0605D (VERS. 11) · *Occurram* — A VULGATE CONFORMATION THAT SEVERED THE GLOSS'S OWN HINGE

**Was:** "*I may attain.*" — Douay's rendering of Phil. III, 11. **Now: "*I may meet.*"**

The whole gloss exists to expound *occurram* in its literal sense: *occurram Christo in
judicio* · *occurrent Domino in aera deportati ab angelis* — the running-out-to-meet of
1 Thess. IV, 17. I rendered that same verb **"meet" twice within four lines and the lemma
itself "attain"**, so an English reader could not see that the "meet" of the exposition was the
lemma's own word.

⚠ **Aggravated by a collision I had not noticed:** "attain" was also doing duty for *assequi*
at 0606C (*illam non assequitur*). Two Latin verbs on one English word — and the one that lost
its identity was **the one being glossed**. Fixed by giving each verb its own English (§8):
*occurrere* = meet · *assequi* = attain · *pervenire* = come. That third move required changing
*ad quod pervenimus* from "whereunto we have attained" to "whereunto we have come" in all three
places.

⛔ **This is 7a″ exactly, and it landed where I was not watching.** §1 records the lemma as
"**agrees**" with the Vulgate — which is true, and which is precisely why the guard was down:
**a lemma that agrees with the Vulgate still has to be TRANSLATED from the Latin.** 7a″ is
usually described as conforming a *divergent* lemma to the familiar English; this is the other
half of the class, where the Latin agrees and the familiar English is still the wrong word
because the gloss is built on the Latin verb's own sense. The next translator should read the
divergence list as **not covering** this.

⚠ **`[var: Douay "I may attain"]` was offered and is DECLINED.** Pattern 14 marks a divergence
between **Migne's printed scripture and the received text**; here Migne prints the Vulgate's
own word and there is no textual divergence at all — only a difference between our literal
rendering and Douay's. A `[var:]` there would tell a reader that the Latin diverges when it
does not. The received English rendering is recorded here instead, which is where a note about
a translation belongs.

⛔ **The limitation this exposes, and it is the line I most want the next translator to have:

### ⭐ Repair 5 · 0606C · AN *et … et* CORRELATIVE FLATTENED

the correlative is what marks a paradox as deliberate rather than as an inconsistency. Now:
"said that he was **both** perfect, on account of that which was already within, **and**
imperfect, on account of this which was still wanting."

### ⭐ THE TWO-READING SITE, DECIDED · 0606D · *ut omnes idem de illo sapiamus*

*de illo* is masculine **or** neuter — the form does not decide it, and a flag is not a decision.
**Decided: NEUTER.** The English now reads "that we all be of the same mind concerning **that
same thing** *whereunto we have come* by faith and knowledge" (was: "concerning **him**").

**For the neuter:**
1. **The relative that follows is neuter and immediately adjacent** — *de illo, **ad quod**
   pervenimus*. Read as one referent the sentence is seamless; read as two it has a masculine
   antecedent and a neuter relative side by side with nothing between them.
2. **The subject of the whole gloss is a thing, not a person** — the *regula disciplinae*, and
   *quod commune sit et modestum in veritate evangelica*, three lines above; and the lemma it
   closes on is *et in eadem permaneamus **regula***.
3. **No person has been named for several lines**, and the nearest masculine noun (*ad cultum
   **Dei***) is a genitive inside another clause, reached across an intervening *subdit*.
4. **The paragraph before it has live neuter-ish deixis** in *istam … illam* for the two
   righteousnesses, so *ille* is already working as a pointer rather than as a person.

**Against it, honestly:** the paragraph's frame *is* *ad cultum Dei excogitaveritis*, so "of one
mind concerning **him**" construes and is not strained; and if the Glossa meant the neuter, *de
eo* or *de eodem* would have been the ordinary choice. **The reading was taken on (1) — the
adjacency of *ad quod* — which is textual rather than thematic**, and a later editor who
prefers the masculine has the case for it above, in full, rather than a bare flag.
