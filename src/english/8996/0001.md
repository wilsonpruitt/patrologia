## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 1.-- *Paul. [n: (BED.)]* On the Acts of the Apostles he says that Paul came to the faith in the second year after the passion and resurrection of Christ, etc., *as far as* he set out to the teaching office of the Gentiles according to the agreement of the apostles.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG. lib. de Spiritu et litt., c. 7)]. ⟦s2⟧ He chose this name for no other reason, etc., *as far as* Saul is interpreted *disquiet* and *temptation*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AMB.)] Further, he is said to have done this after the manner of the holy apostles, who, advancing in virtues, [0469C] were called by a changed name, that by the very name also they might be new, as also Cephas and the sons of thunder.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIER. Com. in Ep. ad Philem.)] Paul took this name to himself from the first spoil which he brought to the Church, namely from Sergius Paulus the proconsul, whom he converted in Cyprus.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A servant.* ⟦s2⟧ Out of a servant and a lowly man God made an apostle and a lofty one; so be you also lowly: *For he that humbleth himself shall be exalted [n: (Luc.XIV)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] By setting down both, he testifies that the person of God and man is one. ⟦s2⟧ The one he sets down for the Jews, the other for the Gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ For "Christ": this name is known to the Jews, by which name they see the Son of God designated, and hear him promised in the law; and though thou shouldst preach another to them, they would not believe. ⟦s4+5⟧ But to the Gentiles, [0469D] because they had not read of it in anything, he sets the Saviour first, that is, Jesus. "Christ," although it is not a proper name but a name of the sacrament, like "prophet" and "priest," is nevertheless rightly set first, being known to the Jews: even though these names were bestowed upon many, yet they were only a figure; for he alone is the true King and Priest.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Called.* ⟦s2⟧ Not by himself, but by God; or "called," named by men by the privilege of the name. ⟦s3⟧ *An apostle.* ⟦s4⟧ Behold, from a lowly man a lofty one. ⟦s5⟧ *Separated.* ⟦s6⟧ From the doctrine of the scribes and Pharisees: this against the Jews; or, from the other apostles, in body and not in mind, whence: *Separate [0470A] unto me Barnabas and Paul for the work whereunto I have taken them [n: (Act. XIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Acts 13:2 *Saulum*, "Saul," where Migne prints *Paulum*.]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The gospel. [n: (HAYM.)]* It is a good announcement, etc. ⟦s2⟧ *Of God.* ⟦s3⟧ Not devised by man.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0470B]VERSE 2.-- [nt: Whatever Walafrid expressed in this his Gloss out of the exposition of the Epistles of Paul by Haymo, bishop of Halberstadt, let the reader seek among the works of that same Haymo in the Patrologia, vols. CXVI, CXVII and CXVIII. Edit.] *Which before* its fulfilment: it did not come as something sudden, but as something promised long before. ⟦s2⟧ Whence in this the word is true: *Because one is he that soweth, and another he that reapeth.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 3.-- *Who was made unto him.* ⟦s2⟧ Who was made according to the flesh, by the work of the Holy Spirit from a virgin, that is, born. ⟦s3⟧ The impiety of the heretics is met, who, understanding this chapter with a blunted heart, take Christ to be only a man. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore by adding *according to the flesh,* he reserved to the divinity its own dignity, in which Christ is the Word of God, through whom all things were made.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Who was, etc., *as far as* that he might fittingly appear to carnal men, clothed.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID. Expos. in hanc epist.)] Christ therefore is one person of a twofold substance; nor yet can God and [0470C] man be called a part of this person: otherwise the Son of God, before he took the form of a servant, was not whole, and grew when the man was added to his divinity.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 4.-- *Who was predestined. [n: (AUG., Tract. 105 in Joan.)]* Predestined was that so great and so lofty and supreme raising up of human nature, that it had nothing higher to which it might be lifted: just as for us the divinity itself had nothing lowlier to which it might lay itself down, than the assumed nature of man with the infirmity of the flesh even unto the death of the cross.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. I de Praedest. sanct., c. 15.)] The most illustrious light also of predestination and of grace is the mediator himself of God and men, the man Christ Jesus, etc., *as far as* that he who was to be according to the flesh the son of David, might be in power the Son of God.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [0470D] *Of the dead, of Jesus Christ.* "Of Christ" for "of him" or "of his own": he has set a proper name in place of a pronoun after the Hebrew manner, a way of speaking most usual in the Scriptures, especially the older ones, as: *Moses did as the Lord commanded Moses.*

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CHRYS.)] That is, we did not furnish this by our own power, that we are apostles, nor indeed did we obtain this dignity by our many sweats and labours, but we found grace.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 5.-- *Among all the nations.* ⟦s2⟧ Not to the Jews only, with whom (because they were called without the law) they ought not [0471A] to act under the law. ⟦s3⟧ *For his name.* ⟦s4⟧ For his glory, not for ours, as you do.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 6.-- *The called of Jesus Christ.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, by him, namely by Jesus Christ. ⟦s3⟧ A genitive for an ablative, after the Greek manner.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 7.-- *To the called saints.* ⟦s2⟧ They were not called for this reason, that they were holy, but were made holy for this reason, that they were called; and called for this reason, that they were beloved.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 8.-- *I give thanks.* ⟦s2⟧ To give thanks is to perceive that all things have been given by God, and for that to praise him with heart, voice and deed.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To my God. [n: (AUG., lib. de Ovibus.)]* For this utterance can belong to none but the saints, whose God he is called, as the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. ⟦s2⟧ For although he is by nature the one God of all, yet he is said to be theirs properly [0471B] by grace, who by the merit of faith and of justice are proved to be his worshippers.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For you all.* ⟦s2⟧ While for others, first for you, from whom great profit comes through Christ the mediator, through whom God has given you all good things.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Because your faith. [n: (AMBR.)]* He rejoices with them over their good beginning, whereby he shows his charity toward them, and exhorts them to advance. ⟦s2⟧ And he says that he does all these things because their faith profits many.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 9.-- *A witness.* ⟦s2⟧ That you have faith I impute to the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ For I pray to him for what is to be had; or he sets forth when he gives thanks, namely in prayers; how? without ceasing.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 11.-- *For I long.* ⟦s2⟧ That what he could not urge [0471C] by words, being present he might urge by the power of miracles.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 12.-- *To be comforted.* ⟦s2⟧ Just now I am desolate, as you also are: even if you do not perceive it, but thus, we should be comforted.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 13.-- *Brethren.* ⟦s2⟧ Because they are reborn, and some upright; see above: *To the called saints.*

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 14.-- *To the Greeks.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the Jews he is silent, because he is the teacher of the Gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ *To the Greeks.* ⟦s4⟧ He puts the Greeks first, because from them all worldly philosophy took its beginning. ⟦s5⟧ *To the barbarians.* ⟦s6⟧ He calls barbarians those who are as it were outside the law, namely those who are neither Hebrews, nor Greeks, nor Latins. ⟦s7⟧ To none is this preaching more especially owed. ⟦s8⟧ *A debtor,* because he was sent to all.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 16.-- *For it is the power of God,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because by signs the [0471D] incredible preaching is confirmed: which is not the case among false heralds.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 17.-- *For the justice of God. [n: (AMBR.)]* It is that which freely justifies the ungodly through faith without the works of the law, etc., *as far as* who deny that this is the Christ whom God promised. ⟦s2⟧ *Is revealed. [n: (AUG.)]* He approaches the Gentiles according to their former state, etc., *as far as* that, puffed up of themselves, they fell into idols. ⟦s3⟧ *From faith,* etc., [n: (AMBR.)] of God who promises, unto the faith of the man who believes him, etc., *as far as* unto the faith of realities and of sight.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Faith is that by which those things are believed which are not seen, etc., *as far as* unto the faith of realities by which we shall obtain the things believed.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As it is written.* ⟦s2⟧ In Habakkuk [n: (cap. II)] it stands thus: [0472A] *But the just shall live by his own faith.* ⟦s3⟧ But this and many more such testimonies which he sets down seem to disagree with the translation of the Hebrew verity which we now use: because sometimes he takes from the LXX interpreters, sometimes, as speaking by the same Spirit as the prophets, he takes the sense only, using his own words and his own arrangement.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 18.-- *Upon all ungodliness.* ⟦s2⟧ Ungodliness is to have sinned against God, iniquity against men; or the ungodly man is the unbeliever, like an idolater; the iniquitous man is he who by the depravity of his work departs from equity. ⟦s3⟧ *Of those who the truth.* ⟦s4⟧ As if he should say: Why is there wrath upon them for ungodliness, since they have not known God? ⟦s5⟧ To which he answers: They were indeed able to know, but they themselves are unwilling, preferring to abide unjustly in their own pleasure. ⟦s6⟧ *They hold back the truth of God.* [0472B] Because, as it is said, that which is knowable of God is in them; as if he should say: they have in themselves whereby they may know, namely natural reason. ⟦s7⟧ *They hold it back in injustice.* ⟦s8⟧ Good is that which they hold, but evil is where they hold it; for they hold the truth, but in iniquity. ⟦s9⟧ Hast thou found God? thou hast found the truth, and holding him back in injustice; even what thou hast known through the works of God, thou hast lost through the works of man.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 19.-- *That which is known of God. [n: (GREG., lib. X Moral., c. 4.)]* Known is that which we can attain from the disposition of this world and from the natural order. ⟦s2⟧ But unknown of God is that which lies hid from every creature: as the account of his substance or of his nature, and the mystery of man's deliverance.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is manifest in them. [n: (AUG.)]* Because not only did [0472C] natural reason avail for this, but God also helped daily, lest nature alone should seem to suffice.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 20.-- *From the creature. [n: (GREG., lib. XXIX Moral., c. 8.)]* From man by excellence, because he excels among the other creatures, or because of the agreement which he has with all creatures: for he is locally in a place with bodies, he feels with the animals, he understands with the angels; whence: *Preach the gospel to every creature [n: (Marc. XVI)]*.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *When they had known,* etc. [n: (AUG., Serm. LV de Verb. Dom.)] They saw whither they must come, etc., *as far as* for what God had given freely, he took away from the ungrateful.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 21.-- *Darkened. [n: (AMBR.)]* For a cloud of error covered their heart, because, whereas they ought to have honoured the Creator the more from those beautiful things [0472D] which he made, they were blunted, having forsaken him.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 22.-- *They were made fools.* ⟦s2⟧ Behold how the heart was darkened, which is the punishment: wise in the natures of things, foolish in God.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 23.-- *And they changed. [n: (AUG., epist. CXIX, c. 6, ad Januarium.)]* Here he condemns the images of the ungodly. ⟦s2⟧ Some worshipped the earth, others the sun and things of this kind.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Into the likeness of an image.* ⟦s2⟧ He heightens the folly, to show the full measure of their dullness.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of a man and of birds,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It was the custom of the Romans from of old to adore the images of men, as of Romulus, of Jove, and of others, chiefly from the coming of Aeneas into Italy. ⟦s3⟧ But of birds and of four-footed beasts and of serpents, [0473A] from the time when Alexandria was conquered by Augustus and subjugated to Rome.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AMBR.)] For the Babylonians first called the figment of Bel a god, etc., *as far as* and birds, because the pagans hold raven-rites sacred.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. VII de Civit. Dei, c. 33.)] Through this one and true religion (of Christ), it could be laid open that the gods of the nations are most unclean demons, desiring to be thought gods under the occasions of departed souls or under the appearance of worldly creatures.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 24.-- *He delivered them up,* etc. [n: (AUG., lib. de Grat. et libero arbit., c. 21.)] It is manifest that God works in the hearts of men, to bend their wills, whether to good things, according to his mercy, or to evil things, according to their deserts, by his own judgment assuredly, sometimes [0473B] hidden, sometimes open, but always just.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That they may afflict. [n: (GREG., Homil. 11 in Ezech.)]* A sin which is not washed away by the lamentation of penitence, etc., *as far as* God permits that the blinded mind should slide into others also.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 25.-- *Because they exchanged.* ⟦s2⟧ He pursues at greater length, part by part, the fault and the punishment, that he may match them. ⟦s3⟧ *Because they exchanged.* ⟦s4⟧ He shows part by part that they changed the glory of God, namely that they thought him God who was not, and worshipped and served the creature rather than the Creator. ⟦s5⟧ And he pursues the parts of the uncleanness, that women into women, males into males: in which he matches the punishment to the fault, that as they sinned against the creating nature, [0473C] so they might be punished in their own nature; whence he concludes: *And the recompense.*
