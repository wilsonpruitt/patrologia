## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They exchanged the truth. [n: (AMBR.)]* The name of God, who is true, they gave to those who are not gods.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] The truth of the creature is from God, but is not God, and this they turned into a lie, worshipping creatures as the Creator.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they worshipped,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ But lest they should say, I do not worship the images, but the things they stand for, such as the sun, he adds: *And they served the creature.* ⟦s3⟧ Here he condemns the things which they understood in the images. ⟦s4⟧ *Amen.* ⟦s5⟧ As if he should say: It is true, that to the true God is blessing unto the ages: but to the gods of the nations ungodliness gives honour for a time. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore what they say is falsehood.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 26.-- *Passions.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, pleasures which, although [0473D] they delight, are nevertheless passions, of a nature not to be named.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The natural use. [n: (AUG., lib. III cont. Jul. Pelag., c. 20.)]* Dost thou think, Julian, that the Apostle has from this praised lust, etc., *as far as* it is against nature and shameful.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 27.-- *And the recompense,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] But few see these punishments, etc., *as far as* they are both sins and punishments. ⟦s2⟧ *It behoved.* ⟦s3⟧ Here the cause for which they were delivered up is shown without any ambiguity.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 28.-- *And as,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Again, because they do not yet come to their senses, they fall into other things for the same fault. ⟦s3⟧ By *as,* he likens the punishment to the sin, in that as they sinned in the knowledge of God, so they were punished in their own understanding. ⟦s4⟧ *They did not approve.* ⟦s5⟧ Because they did not know how to hold God in their acknowledgment, [0474A] which reason approved: or because they thought that God did not know and neglected evils. ⟦s6⟧ *He delivered up,* etc.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 29.-- *With all iniquity.* ⟦s2⟧ He enumerates the parts, that he may accuse malice more openly, when someone contrives harm to another. ⟦s3⟧ Wickedness is rashness, when one dares what he cannot do, or want of self-restraint. ⟦s4⟧ Contention is the assailing of truth by the boldness of shouting. ⟦s5⟧ Malignity is an evil will, when it can go no further, or the not returning of thanks for benefits. ⟦s6⟧ *Detractors:* who deny the good things of others, or pervert them.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Hateful to God.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest whispering or detraction be thought a light thing, because they consist in words, he adds concerning them: *Hateful to God,* that they may understand that by whispering and detraction alone they can incur eternal damnation. ⟦s3⟧ *The haughty.* ⟦s4⟧ The haughty are those who are unwilling to endure a superior, or an equal.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0474B]VERSE 31.-- *Disordered.* ⟦s2⟧ Disorder of the body indicates the quality of the mind.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 32.-- *They are worthy of death.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest they be thought to be afflicted only with those punishments in which they take delight, he adds the last, eternal death. ⟦s3⟧ *Who consent to them that do them,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ To consent is to keep silence when thou couldst reprove; or to foster error.
