## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *According to the flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, from the works of the law. ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: Shall we say that he was just from them? ⟦s3⟧ No, because if he is just from carnal observance, he has the eternal glory that follows from justice: but he has it from himself, not from God. ⟦s4⟧ Or: this justification is in the opinion of men, and not with God. ⟦s5⟧ But toward God [0481D] he has glory: therefore he was not justified from works. ⟦s6⟧ If therefore he was not justified from works, whence was he justified? ⟦s7⟧ It follows, and he says whence, etc.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *He believed.* ⟦s2⟧ To believe was for him a sufficient cause of justice, and it is so for others; yet to him who has time for working, a reward will not be given according to grace only, but according to the debt of his own working; but to him who has not time for working, if he believes, faith alone suffices for justice, and so for salvation, according to the grace set before all, or according to what God laid down as law beforehand.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *But to him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Abraham is just from faith: but to him who works those carnal things, [0482A] or any good things, that he may merit grace, if there is a reward for him, it is not from grace, but from the debt of his own merit. ⟦s3⟧ But to him who does not do these carnal things or any good things, but only believes, faith suffices for justice; and thus he attributes to others what he said of Abraham, namely that if he is just from works, he has glory, but not with God, and therefore he is just from faith.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to grace.* ⟦s2⟧ If it is grace, it is given freely, or it stands freely. ⟦s3⟧ Thou hast done nothing good, and remission of sins is given thee. ⟦s4⟧ Thy works are looked into, and all are found evil.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But according to debt. [n: (AUG.)]* Do not flatter thyself concerning merits, etc., *as far as* let us do it by his own gift and bounty.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *But to him that believeth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He does not say *to him.* [0482B] For he believes him who believes that the things he speaks are true, which the wicked also do. ⟦s3⟧ To believe him is to believe that he is God, which the devils also do. ⟦s4⟧ But to believe in him is, by believing, to love him, by believing to hold him dear, by believing to go into him, and to be incorporated into his members.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the purpose.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: The ungodly man is justified without preceding works, and this according to the purpose of the grace of God, that is, according to the grace of God set before all believers, or according to what God laid down long beforehand.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Forgiven.* ⟦s2⟧ To forgive, to cover, not to impute, are words of the same reckoning and sense. ⟦s3⟧ *Iniquities.* ⟦s4⟧ By iniquities is understood original sin, namely the *fomes* of sin, which is called concupiscence [0482C] or concupiscibility, or the law of the members, or the languor of nature, and by other names. ⟦s5⟧ And that, before baptism, is fault and punishment; but after baptism, punishment and not fault, which makes the little one apt to covet, but not to covet, and makes the adult even to covet; which indeed in baptism is remitted and passes away as to guilt, but remains as to act. ⟦s6⟧ But it is called original because it is drawn from the corrupt condition of our origin, and because we were conceived by the corrupt law of conceiving, out of the sin befalling the first man, that is, by the concupiscence of the flesh; and therefore we are guilty of that sin which is called original.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sins are covered. [n: (AUG.)]* Do not so understand what he said, that sins are covered over, as though they were there and alive. [0482D] He said *covered*, therefore, that they might not be seen; and for God to see a sin is to punish it.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *In circumcision,* that is, at the time of circumcision the faith of Abraham was reputed unto justice.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *But in uncircumcision,* that is, at the time of uncircumcision.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *For not through the law was the promise.* ⟦s2⟧ Other promises were made to Christ than to Abraham. ⟦s3⟧ For to Abraham they were made thus, that those who should imitate him in that manner would be blessed by Christ; but to Christ not so, but so that he should not only be the exemplar and form of the ordering of morals and of works, but that he should bless with an eternal blessing those who imitate him. ⟦s4⟧ *To his seed.* [0483A] That is, to all believers who are the seed of Abraham, not by flesh, but by imitation. ⟦s5⟧ Or to Christ, to whom the promise is said to have been made, because it was to be fulfilled in him.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *For if,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Truly Abraham is not the heir of the world through the law: because his sons through the law are not those sons who are called heirs, namely as possessing what he himself has. ⟦s3⟧ For if this were so, these incongruities would follow from it: that faith would be made void, by which it stands that Abraham was just; that the promise would be abolished, that is, unfulfilled, so that there are no heirs: for the law makes no heirs, since rather it works wrath, and so transgression; for from it comes transgression, because without it there is none. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore, because if these things would follow from the law, then it is from faith that they are heirs, and so the promise is firm.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0483B]VERS. 15.-- *The law works wrath.* ⟦s2⟧ That it might make offenders guilty, not by an efficient cause, but by grace not helping; or it would make them guilty by temporal punishments, it would work wrath, because [it works] transgression; and in what manner this is, he opens, saying:

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For where,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] He shows that there cannot be transgression unless there has been a law; yet not that, if there is a law, there will in every kind be transgression: for it can come about that there is a law and there is no transgression, but not conversely.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Nor transgression.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not say: There is no iniquity. ⟦s3⟧ For everyone who passes the law by is unjust, but not the contrary. ⟦s4⟧ For those who have not received the law can be called unjust, but they cannot be called transgressors.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Not to him who is of the law.* ⟦s2⟧ Because such a man as this has fallen away from grace, and therefore for the sake of faith one must withdraw from the law, [0483C] that the promise may be firm: for the law, without the grace of the promise, not only does not take away sin, but even increases it.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Who against hope.* ⟦s2⟧ He begins to commend the faith of Abraham, that we may imitate him who through faith received justice and such a promise.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *So shall thy seed be,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It must be known that Abraham first knew God as three and one, and through this merit of faith he obtained the remission of all sins. ⟦s3⟧ But after justice, he received a promise from God that he should become the father of many nations. ⟦s4⟧ Then again a promise was made to him that his seed should become as the stars, etc.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *He was not weakened.* ⟦s2⟧ Some, puffed up by the reasoning of the world, neglected God: therefore the things that are impossible to the world [0483D] God did, that believing through these they might be saved.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *In the promise.* ⟦s2⟧ Isaac, promised against the nature of human generation, was for a sign that the Son of God was to be born of man against human nature; and that the sons of men, through the spirit of regeneration, were to be begotten out of sinners into sons of God, out of mortals into immortals.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *In him who raised up Jesus,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He has fitly entered upon the discourse concerning the Lord, and says: he died not for his own sins, for in that manner he ought not to have risen again, just as neither did any man before him, but for our sins, that he might free us from them. ⟦s3⟧ In the same way as he also restored Abraham and the womb of Sarah, [0484A] already dead through old age and barrenness, to life and to the procreation of children.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Who was delivered up.* ⟦s2⟧ The death of Christ alone signifies the destruction of the old life, and in the resurrection alone is the new life signified, which begins from justification and will be perfected in immortality.
