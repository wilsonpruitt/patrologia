## CHAPTER VII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *According to the inward. [n: (AMB.)]* Because the flesh is drawn by descent from Adam it has sin in itself; if the soul were drawn by descent, sin would dwell in it also: [0494A] because Adam's soul sinned. ⟦s1⟧ But if the soul had sin in itself, man would not know himself.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *But I see. [n: (AUG.)]* It belongs to the body of death that the law of the members fight against the law of the mind, while the flesh lusts against the spirit. ⟦s2⟧ But the law of sin does not reign, when its desires are not obeyed. ⟦s3⟧ *The law in the members.* ⟦s4⟧ Rightly is the tinder of sin called a law: because it was lawfully brought about that he who did not obey his superior should not be served by his inferior, that is, the flesh. ⟦s5⟧ *Captive.* ⟦s6⟧ From the flesh, in part: because the mind fights back, and delights together in the law of God. ⟦s7⟧ So it is read of the Apostle himself: the mind consents not to sin, death contends, the mind consents not. ⟦s8⟧ There is in me something dead, something alive: what is to be hoped from thence?

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0494B]VERS. 24.-- *Unhappy.* ⟦s2⟧ Not in the mind, but in the flesh. ⟦s3⟧ *Who shall deliver me?* ⟦s4⟧ He shall be delivered when this mortal shall put on immortality, where no concupiscence shall remain.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Therefore I myself.* ⟦s2⟧ Here is the third act in the law of the spirit. ⟦s3⟧ But because he says that he has been delivered by grace, lest anyone grow slack in his security, as though sin should hurt no further, he determines in what way the deliverance is, if he shall have remained in grace. ⟦s4⟧ Otherwise sin will seize him again. ⟦s5⟧ *I myself.* ⟦s6⟧ I in the flesh, I in the mind: not two, but one out of both; with the mind I consent not, with the flesh I lust.
