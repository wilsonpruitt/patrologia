## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0494C]VERS. 1.-- *Nothing therefore now.* ⟦s2⟧ Even if before, not now: since [they are] in Christ. ⟦s3⟧ *Nothing of damnation.* ⟦s4⟧ Because through the grace of baptism and the laver of regeneration there has been loosed both the guilt itself with which thou wast born, and whatsoever thou hast committed before.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Even if they have desires of the flesh to which they do not consent, etc., *as far as* but this good regeneration has wrought.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For the law of the spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he shows by what grace delivers, namely by the Holy Spirit, because [it is] *the law of the spirit.* ⟦s3⟧ It is the Holy Spirit who teaches what is to be done and what is not, who is the giver of life, and this through Christ who gives the Spirit: and he sets forth how, in Christ.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *For what was impossible.* ⟦s2⟧ He shows how in [0494D] Christ the deliverance was made. ⟦s3⟧ *Through the flesh.* ⟦s4⟧ Through carnality, which was rebelling, and on the hearing of the law was the more incited; thus the law of the letter was made weak through the flesh, and therefore it was impossible for it to deliver from the law of sin and of death.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sending his own Son. [n: (AUG.)]* Not where he was not: who is everywhere, etc., *as far as* because he appeared visible to the world.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Into the likeness,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not into sinful flesh; because his mother conceived him not by concupiscence but by grace. ⟦s3⟧ It was not therefore sinful, because it was neither conceived in the concupiscence of lust, nor had in itself the cause of sin, nor did he sin in it. ⟦s4⟧ In punishment therefore it is like to ours, not in the quality of sin.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0495A] *And of sin. [n: (ORIG.)]* That is: By the victim of his own flesh which he offered on the altar of the cross, he condemned sin, and reduced it to nothing in his own flesh.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He condemned.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, he destroyed the devil, that is, for the sin which he works in the flesh of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ Or sin, that is, he condemned the tinder proceeding from the sin of Adam, that is, he weakened it.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *That the justification.* ⟦s2⟧ So is sin condemned through Christ that the justice which the law of Moses promised might be consummated.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For they that are according to the flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ He that walketh according to the flesh is not just, because neither is he who is according to it. ⟦s3⟧ Why not? because he desires carnal things as the highest goods, which is repugnant to justice.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *For the prudence.* ⟦s2⟧ He who savours these things which are of the flesh [0495B] is not just; for the prudence of the flesh is death. ⟦s3⟧ *Life and peace.* ⟦s4⟧ Life indeed referred to this, *the affection of the flesh is death;* but peace to that which follows: *the affection of the flesh is enmity toward God.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Because the wisdom of the flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ By enumerating these things he deters from obedience to the flesh.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is an enemy.* ⟦s2⟧ While it thinks God can do nothing beyond what is seen in the natures of things.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *If yet.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks ambiguously, because they are not yet of perfect faith, but there is hope of their being perfected. ⟦s3⟧ *The Spirit of God.* ⟦s4⟧ The Spirit of Christ, because the Spirit of God and the Spirit of Christ are the same.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *The body indeed.* ⟦s2⟧ By the name of the body he signifies the whole man, as Ezekiel conversely [0495C] by the name of the soul, when he says: *The soul that hath sinned,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ And the sense is: If the spirit of Christ is in you, although the body be dead, that is, although the man be dead because of sins, that is, in sins, yet the spirit, that is, the soul which had been ungodly, lives: because it is joined to God, who is life, and this because of justification, that is, the remission of sins, which is from the Holy Spirit. ⟦s4⟧ *Dead,* he says, not mortal. ⟦s5⟧ For before sin the body was animal, that is, mortal, namely able to die. ⟦s6⟧ But which it was not necessary to die: now it is called dead because of sin, that is, because it is necessary to die. ⟦s7⟧ *Because of sin* to be shunned, this weakness is kept back, that thereby he may the more fear to sin.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *But if the Spirit. [n: (AUG.)]* Mark that the Holy Spirit, etc., *as far as* even as who shall declare the generation [0495D] of the Son?

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall quicken also your mortal. [n: (ID.)]* Behold the fourth act. ⟦s2⟧ In the first action, etc., *as far as* because we had forsaken God who is above us.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Mortal.* ⟦s2⟧ Not dead, because in the resurrection, when this mortal shall put on immortality, not only the necessity of dying, which it now has because of sin, but not even the mortality which the animal body had before sin, shall remain.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Because of him that dwelleth.* ⟦s2⟧ It appears most evidently that man deserved, because of sin, the death not only of the spirit but also of the body, and that through the Holy Spirit both were delivered by Christ.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *But if by the Spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ Either by the Holy Spirit [0496A] or by our own: which nevertheless is not so, save by the working of the Holy Spirit.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *They are driven.* ⟦s2⟧ He says they are driven, not they are ruled. ⟦s3⟧ For it is more to be driven than to be ruled: for we say that one is driven, as scarcely doing anything; but that he is ruled, as already doing something, who is therefore ruled that he may act rightly. ⟦s4⟧ But they are driven, not because they do nothing of themselves, but because, that they may do something, they are impelled by the force of grace.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. de Correp. et Grat., c. 2.)] Let them not therefore deceive themselves who say: To what end for us, etc., *as far as* let them rejoice that they have received.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *For you have not received,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ You ought to be sons, not servants, because you have not received the spirit of bondage: which makes men serve unwilling through fear of punishment.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But you have received the spirit of adoption.* ⟦s2⟧ It is the same spirit, [0496B] but because of diverse works it is differently named: he is called the spirit of wrath to the Egyptians, to whom the water divided by the spirit did harm. ⟦s3⟧ The son of Israel is not called [a spirit] of wrath, to whom the divided water was of profit. ⟦s4⟧ He is the spirit of fear to those who, convicted by the law of their sins and of their own weakness, did not understand the grace of the spirit. ⟦s5⟧ He is the spirit of liberty by his working to those to whom regeneration unto life eternal, and faith which works through love, has been imparted. ⟦s6⟧ One spirit therefore, which makes two fears. ⟦s7⟧ Which also make two kinds of servants. ⟦s8⟧ For there is a servant who is also a son, who fears the Lord and honours the father, whence Malachi: *If I am Lord, where is my fear? ⟦s9⟧ If father, where is my honour? [n: (Malach. I)]*. ⟦s10⟧ And there is a servant who fears punishment, but does not love justice. ⟦s11⟧ And because mention is made of fears, it is to be known [0496C] that besides the natural fear, which is in all, there are four fears, namely: the worldly, which is evil and not from God; and the servile, which is good and from God, but is not sufficient; the initial, which is good and sufficient; and the chaste, which is good and perfecting, of which it is here treated: *For it begets us sons of God. ⟦s12⟧ In whom we cry.* ⟦s13⟧ This again is from the gift of the Spirit, which makes us so to cry *Abba,* in Hebrew, *father* in Latin. ⟦s14⟧ *Abba, father.* ⟦s15⟧ Because of the mystery, the Apostle set down both. ⟦s16⟧ And in his passion Christ said, *Abba, father,* because the Church is of both peoples, and suffering with her he showed in himself that her sons (the martyrs, namely) ought not to despair, if at the time of passion any sadness from human frailty should creep in.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *That we are the sons of God.* ⟦s2⟧ This the Holy Spirit makes us [0496D] to recognize, while in the Church remission of sins is made; which although the whole Trinity works, yet it is understood to pertain to the Holy Spirit, because he is common to the Father and the Son, and the union of both; through whom is made that fellowship and unity by which we are made one body of the only-begotten Son of God. ⟦s3⟧ For unity joins us together, which charity makes, which is not save from the Holy Spirit. ⟦s4⟧ These things are said that we may love unity, and fear separation.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Heirs.* ⟦s2⟧ Even though God does not depart, which is spoken according to human laws. ⟦s3⟧ Or he will depart, so that he be not seen through a glass in a dark manner as now, but face to face. ⟦s4⟧ It can also be said that Christ our head, departing to the passion, left us [0497A] the possession of ecclesiastical peace, as he himself says: *My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Christ also is our inheritance. ⟦s6⟧ Whence: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance [n: (Psal. XV)]*, etc.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *If yet we suffer with him.* ⟦s2⟧ To mortify the deeds of the flesh, whence life shall be, no one can without trouble, for which patience is necessary.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *For the expectation of the creature.* ⟦s2⟧ The creature: they are the good, who remain in the creation of God, or because they are the cause of all things. ⟦s3⟧ Corruption is vanity, to which the creature is subjected. ⟦s4⟧ For all things that are born are weak and corruptible, and thereby vain: because they cannot keep their own state.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] What he says, *for the expectation,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is so to be understood that neither sense, etc., *as far as* it labours [0497B] and lies under corruption.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Not willingly,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Willingly it sinned, but not willingly does it bear the penalty, in that it is subjected to deceit: but because of the justice and the clemency of him who both punishes sin, and purposes to heal.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Because the creature itself also. [n: (AUG.)]* Now it is a creature only, since the form of the sons is not yet perfected, etc., *as far as* for man, having lost the seal of the image, remained only a creature.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Shall be delivered.* ⟦s2⟧ He shall be revealed; it awaits [sic: *revela*]; he subjoined, shall be delivered. ⟦s3⟧ All these things through the Spirit.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Every creature. [n: (AUG.)]* Man. ⟦s2⟧ Every creature is either corporeal, etc., *as far as* although not in all men. ⟦s3⟧ *Every creature. [n: (AMBR.)]* All the elements fulfil their offices with labour, which is [0497C] for our sake: whence they shall rest when we have been taken up; and therefore let us prepare ourselves diligently.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It groaneth.* ⟦s2⟧ It is always new in this, which is marked by the inchoative verb, and for desire of the heavenly fatherland. ⟦s3⟧ *It travaileth.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, it grieves.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Until now. [n: (AUG.)]* Because although some one in Abraham's bosom has ceased to grieve, yet still in those who are not yet delivered every creature groans.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Not only,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] That is, not only in the man of the body, but we also in our own selves apart from the body, the troubles of the body excepted.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Not only. [n: (AUG., in expos. propos. in 53. in epist. ad Rom.)]* Not only that which in men is called merely a creature, etc., *as far as* or with trouble disquieting.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0497D] *The first-fruits of the spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, of those whose spirits have already been offered to God as in sacrifice, and kindled with the divine fire of charity. ⟦s3⟧ *Adoption.* ⟦s4⟧ That the body also may receive the benefit of adoption, and that in every part they may be sons of God.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *For by hope.* ⟦s2⟧ He therefore says it that the merit of hope may seem the greater and therefore ought to save.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The Spirit helpeth our infirmity. [n: (AUG.)]* Because we groan and ask to be delivered, but lest we ask either before the time or contrariwise, the Spirit helps. ⟦s2⟧ What prayer obtains is evidently the gift of God, but to pray also is of grace.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Unutterable.* ⟦s2⟧ For how should that be uttered which is desired, when it is unknown? [0498A] Yet it is not altogether unknown. ⟦s3⟧ For if it were utterly unknown, it would not be desired. ⟦s4⟧ And again, if it were seen, it would not be sought with groanings.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He searcheth. [n: (AMBR.)]* Not as though seeking what he knows not, to whom nothing is hidden, and to whom the prayer of all spirits is known.
