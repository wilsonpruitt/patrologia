## CHAPTER VIII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *But we know that to them that love,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Not only does the Holy Spirit do this, but he also makes all things turn out unto good for them that love him, whether prosperous or adverse; for such a God comforts by prosperous things, exercises by adverse. ⟦s3⟧ Profitable therefore are the evils which the faithful piously endure, either for the amending or the taking away of sins, or for the exercising and proving of justice, or for the showing forth of the misery of this life. ⟦s4⟧ For although the death of the flesh came originally from the sin of the first [0498B] man, yet the good use of it has made most glorious martyrs. ⟦s5⟧ And therefore not only it, but all the evils of the world also, had to remain when sins were remitted, that man might have wherewith to contend for the truth, and whence the virtue of the faithful might be exercised.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the purpose.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: That they are called is from nothing else than from the predestination of God. ⟦s3⟧ And thence it is that *all things work together unto good:* not from merit.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *For whom,* God, *he predestinated,* that is, prepared by grace bestowed that they should believe the word of preaching: those *he foreknew,* that is, long before knew beforehand, *to be made conformable to the Son,* who is the image of the Father, in every way the same with the Father. ⟦s2⟧ Whence it is said: *He that [0498C] seeth me seeth the Father also [n: (Joan. XIV)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the image.* ⟦s2⟧ That we may bear the image of the heavenly, as we have borne the image of the earthly. ⟦s3⟧ Or *the firstborn.* ⟦s4⟧ Because before every creature he was not made but born, and because he was the first born without sin, and the first to rise again impassible, in which he has brethren.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *He called.* ⟦s2⟧ The outward calling is made through preachers and is common to the good and to the bad. ⟦s3⟧ But the inward is of the elect only. ⟦s4⟧ Of the outward it is said: *Many are called, but few chosen.* ⟦s5⟧ By calling predestination is fulfilled. ⟦s6⟧ To call is to help the knowledge concerning faith, or to prick him whom he makes to hear, and this is of the elect only. ⟦s7⟧ *He magnified.* ⟦s8⟧ This of the foreknown, not of the others, who, although they believe for a time and seem good, yet because they do not abide, are not magnified. [0498D] *He magnified,* or *he shall magnify:* but this is to come, and yet it is spoken as past: because God is said to have done the things which from eternity he disposed to do; whence Isaias: *Who hath made the things that are to come [n: (Isa. XLIV)]*. ⟦s9⟧ To such all things are unto good, even that they go astray, that thereby they may be the more wary, and not trusting in themselves. ⟦s10⟧ Four things are here mentioned: predestination, calling, justification, magnification. ⟦s11⟧ One is in God, and three in us. ⟦s12⟧ For our predestination is not in us, but hidden with him in his foreknowledge. ⟦s13⟧ But the three remaining are in us.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *If God be for us. [n: (AUG.)]* God is for us, that he should predestinate us before we were, that he should call us when we were turned away, that he should justify us [0499A] when we were sinners, and glorify us when we were mortal.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *He delivered up.* ⟦s2⟧ This is said because the Father delivered up the Son; but the author of this cup is also he himself who drinks it. ⟦s3⟧ Whence the Apostle: *Christ loved us, and delivered himself up for us [n: (Ephes. V)]*. ⟦s4⟧ If therefore the Father delivered up the Son, and the Son himself, what did Judas do? ⟦s5⟧ The Father in delivering up the Son did well; the Son in delivering up himself did well; Judas in delivering up his master for avarice did ill. ⟦s6⟧ For that which has been bestowed upon us out of the passion of Christ shall not be reckoned to the malice of Judas; he shall have the wage of malice, Christ the praise of grace. ⟦s7⟧ *All things.* ⟦s8⟧ It is less to give us all things than for our sake to deliver Christ up to death. ⟦s9⟧ And so all the higher things, namely God the Trinity, are [0499B] ours for the seeing and the enjoying. ⟦s10⟧ Things equal, namely the angels, to whom we shall be made equal in the time to come, for the living together; but now we are inferior. ⟦s11⟧ Things lower also are ours for the ruling. ⟦s12⟧ And likewise certain higher things too, as the sublime angels and all the heavenly ministries of God, are ours, not by dominion, but by use; as the things of a lord are the servants', for sustenance, namely, or ornament, or aught else of this kind.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *At the right hand of God.* ⟦s2⟧ In the honour wherein he is God, whose petition cannot be contemned. ⟦s3⟧ *He maketh intercession.* ⟦s4⟧ While daily he offers to the Father's countenance the man whom he assumed, and the grievous kind of death which he bore for us.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *Who shall separate us,* etc. [n: (CYPR.)] None [0499C] of these things can separate believers, etc., *as far as* that we also may suffer for his sake: *But in all these things we overcome.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *Neither angels, nor princes, nor virtues.* ⟦s2⟧ These orders are taken of the evil angels, and of the good if it might be done.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Neither height, nor depth.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, neither prosperity nor adversity, whence Solomon: *Riches and poverty give me not: lest, being filled, I become a liar, or, being poor, I rob, and forswear the name of God [n: (Prov. XXX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Many sin through height, many through need. ⟦s4⟧ Against this Ahaz is set forth in Isaias, who would not ask a sign either in the height or in the depth. ⟦s5⟧ The remedy is from Christ, who was neither lifted up by the power whereby he is equal to the Father, nor troubled at the casting out of [0499D] death, who is a sign in the height and in the depth: lest he should either be exalted by royal power, or troubled by hardship, as it then was on account of the war of the Assyrians.
