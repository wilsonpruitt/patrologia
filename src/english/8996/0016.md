## CHAPTER XI. (cont.)

[n: (AUG., lib. de Spirit, et lit., c. 36 in fine.)] I know that, as there is no impossibility, so neither is there iniquity with God, etc., *as far as* that he might first permit them to be concluded in unbelief.

[n: (ID.)] Sometimes the grace of God is bestowed upon the sons of unbelievers, etc., *as far as* and if not in this life, certainly in the other, etc.

*His ways.* That is, the fulfillments of his disposing, because not even in the carrying out can what he has disposed be known.

*Counsellor.* The counsellor of the Father is his only-begotten [0510A] Son, who is also called the Angel of great counsel. But of men and of angels none was a counsellor to him.

VERS. 35.-- *Or who hath first.* Although we cannot know the judgments of God, at least this is certain, that of grace alone he gives good things, and not from any preceding merit.

[n: (AUG.)] God has been made a debtor, not by receiving anything from us, etc., *as far as* and this thou hast done, who hast helped them that labored, etc.

VERS. 36.-- *For of him,* etc. *Of, through, in,* denote the distinction of the persons; but the same pronoun *him* repeated, the identity of substance; and because he is such, the glory is to him, not to the just themselves.

[n: (AUG., lib. de Natura boni, cap. 27, Ex ipso.)] Not all things are of his own substance, etc., *as far as* he willed each one of these [0510B] three to be referred to each one of them.

*All things.* When we have heard that *all things are of God,* we ought assuredly to understand all natures, and all things that naturally are; for neither are those sins of him which vitiate nature, which are born of the will of them that sin.

## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 1.-- *I beseech you.* Thus far he has sufficiently humbled both parties, and has annulled the glory drawn from their former state; now, the business being as it were finished, he thereafter turns to morality. *I beseech you.* Thus far, lest the one should be proud against the other; from here, that the one should labor for the other's profit. *I beseech you by,* etc. That is, by the apostolate mercifully committed to me by God, or [0510C] the mercy wrought and shown to you by God.

*A victim,* etc. [n: (AUG., lib. X, de Civit., cap. 6.)] A true sacrifice is every work that is done, etc, *as far as* he adds, saying: *But be ye reformed in the newness of your mind,* etc.

VERS. 2.-- *Be not conformed.* A general prohibition. *But be ye reformed.* A regular command. There continence, here justice. The one pertains to not coveting, this to loving; the one to turning aside from evil, this to doing good. For by not coveting we are stripped of the oldness, and by loving we are clothed with the newness. *Be ye reformed.* Because in Adam the image of God was in part lost, so that it may begin to be reformed by him by whom it was formed. For it cannot reform itself as it was able to deform itself.

[0510D]VERS. 3.-- *For I say.* This is the sacrifice of Christians, that the many should be one body in Christ. For this is shown in the Sacrament of the altar to the faithful by the vow of the Church, which is signified in that thing which she offers. For as bread is made of many grains, so the holy Church consists of the many members of the faithful, who rejoice together in the participation of their Creator; by whose gift they are holy and are one, which is also shown in what follows, where he says: *We being many are one body in Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]*. Which cannot come to pass unless they be wise unto sobriety, which is here enjoined. *To be wise unto sobriety.* That is, that it should not go beyond the bounds of justice, that which may be useful to us alone, and may hurt no one.

[0511A] *The measure of faith.* According to the faith which God distributes by measure; this is the faith that works through charity, without which no man's works are to be reckoned anything. To Christ the Spirit was not given by measure, but to all the rest it is given, and being given is added to; whence John says of Christ: *For God doth not give the Spirit by measure,* to the rest it is divided, not indeed the Spirit, but his gifts.

VERS. 4.-- *For as in one. [n: (AUG.)]* No one will be able to be just so long as he has been separated from the unity of this body, etc., *as far as* but that they may possess them with the piety of using them well.

VERS. 5, 6.-- *And every one members one of another, having,* etc. To this end having differing goods. Or: we are severally members, therefore let this be done in the differing [0511B] gifts; and what those graces are from the diversity of which we are called members, and in what manner in them we ought to serve one another in turn, he opens, saying: *Whether prophecy according to the proportion of faith, or ministry,* etc. *Having*: let us be members of others, that we may prophesy to them. I say *having according to the proportion of faith,* because prophecy is given according as their faith, or the faith of the hearers, requires. Note that in the enumeration of the graces he begins from prophecy, because the first proof that our faith is reasonable is that believers, having received the Spirit, prophesied.

VERS. 8.-- *He that showeth mercy.* This showing of mercy is the daily medicine for daily wounds, even though they be the lighter ones; yet *judgment without mercy shall be done to him that doth not mercy [n: (Jac. II)]*, and this is assuredly just, [0511C] that it be forgiven to him that forgives and given to him that gives. But whosoever, as being over-just and secure, awaits judgment without mercy, provokes wrath against himself. This mercy is manifold, namely to pardon him that sins, to succor him that is oppressed, etc.; and therefore he subjoins concerning mercy, setting down one member of it when another member has been set before, but where *he that giveth, in simplicity.* Simplicity excludes hypocrisy, cheerfulness shows joy, and reliance upon the hope of what is to come.

VERS. 11.-- *Not slothful.* Cursed is he that does the work of God negligently; the slothful man is without hope in the divine converse. And therefore, that the cold of sloth may be driven off, *Be ye fervent,* etc.

*Fervent in spirit. [n: (THEOD.)]* He called grace the Spirit, [0511D] etc., *as far as* for this he said. *Instant in prayer.* That is, doing this assiduously.

*Serving the Lord,* etc. As if he should say: Although I have said that ye should be fervent in spirit, yet do not thrust in the words of God everywhere and unseasonably, but be fervent with the opportunity of the time, so that ye may speak the words of faith in fit places, to fit persons, and at a fit time. *Fervent.* The Spirit is fire, with which charity is fervent, whence the Lord says: *I am come to send fire on the earth, and what will I, but that it burn? [n: (Luc. XII.)]* [var: Vulg. Luke 12:49 *nisi ut accendatur*, "but that it be kindled," where Migne prints *nisi ardeat*]

VERS. 12.-- *Rejoicing in hope.* Being certain of things to come they rejoice, and therefore are patient in tribulation.

VERS. 16.-- *Consenting.* Which is not done with the mouth only, because then is there true humility, if it is not in the tongue [0512A] alone, lest, presuming upon themselves, they should not suffer with others, but reproach them.

VERS. 19.-- *Give place.* He does not command that the saints should be unwilling to be avenged by God, which the saints cry out for in the Apocalypse, not indeed for the glutting of hatred, but because it is written: *The just shall rejoice when he shall see the revenge.* But he commands that they, not avenging themselves, give place to the wrath of God, who says: *Revenge to me, and I will repay.* If therefore a good man wills that God punish his enemy, this is not to render evil for evil. Since therefore both the just man and the evil man wish to be avenged by God on their enemies, wherein are they distinguished? The good man desires rather that his enemy be corrected than punished, and when God punishes he is not delighted at his enemy's pain, but that pleases him which pleases the Lord also, because he [0512B] loves him, and it is God whom he follows in this: *Who raineth upon the just and the unjust [n: (Prov. XXV)]*; and yet even in this world he more sharply reproves the obstinate and at the end will damn them, to whom accordingly vengeance is to be reserved. He gives place to the wrath of his adversary who permits his adversary to do what he will.

VERS. 20.-- *But if he hunger.* Note the strangeness of the conjunction *but.* For it is to be carried on as though neither conjunction had been interposed. Or it can also be read thus, so that neither conjunction is superfluous, as if he should say, not only do ye not defend yourselves, but if, etc.
