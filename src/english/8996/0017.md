## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Let every soul. [n: (AUG. Expositio prop. 720 ex epist. ad Rom.)]* What the Apostle says here he admonishes most rightly, etc., *as far as* yet let every man [0512C] be subject to him that is above him.

*For there is no power.* Power is from God; but the things that are from God are ordained; therefore power is ordained. And so he that resists the power resists the ordinance of God. Of a good power it is plain, that God perfected it. Of an evil one it can reasonably be seen, since both the good are purged by it, and the evil are damned, and it is itself cast down into the worse. And know that by the name of power there is sometimes understood the power itself which is given to someone by God, sometimes the very man having the power; which let the diligent reader distinguish.

*And those that are, are ordained of God.* That is, reasonably disposed by God, whence: *God casteth not away the mighty, since he himself also is mighty [n: (Job. XXXVI)]*.

[n: (GREG.)] For he desires to imitate God who the summit [0512D] of power, etc., *as far as* because it is by no means a just power, but a depraved act, that is condemned.

VERS. 2.-- *But he that resisteth. [n: (AUG.)]* What if he command what is not lawful? Here indeed he shall despise the power, by fearing a greater power; therefore if the emperor command one thing, and God another, that one being despised, God is to be obeyed.

VERS. 3.-- *Princes.* The Apostle says that those are princes who are created for the correcting of life and the forbidding of adverse things, having the image of God, that the rest may be under one. *They are not a terror to the good,* etc. If the prince is good, he does not punish the well-doer, but loves him. But if he is evil, he does not harm the good man, but purges him. The evil subject therefore ought to fear, because princes are appointed [0513A] that they may punish evils. *Thou shalt have praise from it.* If it is just, by its own praising; if unjust, by its affording thee an occasion of praise.

VERS. 4.-- *For he is God's minister.* Therefore rulers are given, that evils may not be done. *An avenger unto wrath.* That is, for the avenging of God's wrath, or an avenger for the showing forth of God's wrath; because this punishment indicates that those who persist in evil are to be punished more grievously.

VERS. 6.-- *For therefore.* Ye ought to be subject, because for this too, that is, for the showing of subjection, ye render tribute, which is a sign of subjection. He does not say, ye pay, but ye render, as to men who will make return; because they make return while they fight for their country, and while they conduct judgments, ye render, serving God. For by this, that ye give them tribute, ye serve God, because they are ministers [0513B] of God. Or ye render tribute, because they are ministers of God, that the good may be praised, the evil punished.

VERS. 7.-- *Tribute.* Or tribute is what is paid to lords. A toll is what is conveyed to the lord's house.

VERS. 8.-- *To no man anything.* Peace toward all, love among brethren. For otherwise they are not truly brethren. *That ye love.* Mention is made here of charity, because it avails unto this humility. For charity alone is that which, even when repaid, always keeps its debtor. For it is repaid when it is bestowed, but it is owed even if it has been repaid, because there is no time at which it is not to be bestowed. Nor when it is repaid is it lost, but rather it is multiplied by repaying; for it is repaid by having, not by lacking, as money which cannot be repaid unless it be had, nor be had unless it be repaid; nay rather, when it is repaid [0513C] by a man, it grows in the man; and so much the greater is acquired, the more men it is repaid to. *He loveth his neighbor.* Since there are two precepts of charity, one is often put for both, because neither is God loved without the neighbor, nor the neighbor without God, nor can be. The love of the neighbor appears in deeds, the love of God lies more hidden, and therefore he sets down this one more particularly, of which the Lord says: *A new commandment I give unto you, that ye love one another,* where that greater commandment of the love of God seems to be passed over. But by those who understand well, each is found in the other; because he who loves God cannot despise him whom God commands to be loved, and he who loves his neighbor holily and spiritually, what does he love in him but God? *His neighbor, hath fulfilled the law.* Every man must be understood [0513D] by neighbor, because there is no one with whom evil is to be wrought. He therefore who loves men ought to love them either because they are just, or that they may be just. For so also he ought to love himself, namely, either because he is just, or that he may be just. For he who loves himself otherwise loves himself unjustly, because he loves himself to this end, that he may be unjust, therefore to this end, that he may be evil: therefore he no longer loves himself; for he who loves iniquity hates his own soul.

VERS. 11.-- *And this knowing.* Namely, that it is the hour; or, as certain books have it: knowing this time, namely of grace. Now from the time when we have had love; it is the hour, that is, the opportunity, but a brief one. *To rise from sleep.* Sleep is negligence or ignorance. *For now.* Causes interposed, why we ought to [0514A] rise. *Is nearer.* He that works well is near to eternal life. For baptism is unto pardon, a good life unto the crown. Or *nearer*, because we are more nigh to death.

VERS. 12.-- *The night is past.* Again it is shown why they ought to rise: because the *night* of unbelief and ignorance *is past*, that is, has been; but now it is not, whose memory admonishes, that ye may beware. *But the day*, that is, eternal blessedness, *is at hand*, since sins are remitted, since ye are just through grace. The *night* is the old man, who passed away in baptism; the *day* is Christ the sun of justice, by whose light they have learned the truth, that they may know what is to be done. *The works of darkness.* Evil works, which have come out of the darkness of ignorance, or love the darkness, and lead to the darkness

[0514B]VERS. 13.-- *Not in rioting.* Note that in these things the Romans are touched; and therefore they have no right of boasting.

[n: (AUG.)] But of these diseases pride is the mother, and the greediness of the human mind, which also often begets hypocrisy; this is not resisted, unless by frequent testimonies of the divine books fear and charity be struck in.
