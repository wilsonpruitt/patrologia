## CHAPTER XIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Now him that is weak.* ⟦s2⟧ It belongs to a perfect faith to distinguish no food, nor to think it unclean, because *all things are clean to the clean.* ⟦s3⟧ But certain weak ones judged that some things were to be abstained from, and so there was contention among the Romans; whence he enjoins that such be taken up, not cast off, but by bearing with them, by example and by word, to be raised up [0514C] to faith. ⟦s4⟧ And if it is unknown with what mind anyone does a thing, not to dispute about it. ⟦s5⟧ *Not in disputes.* ⟦s6⟧ He is not to be condemned whose thought is not open, or of whom we know not what sort he will afterward be.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *He that is weak.* ⟦s2⟧ He is weak who distinguishes foods, thinking some clean, others unclean, because the Jews were forbidden to eat; and he says this man is to be left to his own judgment, lest he eat with scruple. ⟦s3⟧ Or, although the faith be perfect which believes all things lawful, yet let the weak man, prone to a fall, who is easily laid low by the assault of lust, eat herbs, that is, thin and dry foods, which are not the fuel of vices, and let him abstain from those things by which lust is stirred up. ⟦s4⟧ For the richness of flesh-meats and delicious feasts provoke the body to lust.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0514D]VERS. 3.-- *He that eateth.* ⟦s2⟧ Above he enjoined that we should not dispute about the thoughts of others, here he enjoins that we should not despise them.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let him not judge. [n: (AUG., lib. II de Ser. Dom. in monte.)]* Judgment is to be made of manifest things, not of hidden things, etc., *as far as* whatsoever human foods indifferently to be taken.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *To his own Lord he standeth.* ⟦s2⟧ Concerning the unknown heart of another's servant thou hast not to judge, but his own Lord, to whose honor he that stands, stands; to whom belongs also the fall of him that falls, who can raise up or judge. ⟦s3⟧ *He standeth, or falleth,* he therefore says: because it is ambiguous; perhaps he stands who is thought to fall. ⟦s4⟧ *He standeth or falleth.* ⟦s5⟧ By this he shows that he does not know with what mind that man conducts himself in doubtful matters. ⟦s6⟧ *But he shall stand.* ⟦s7⟧ Here he intimates that ambiguous things [0515A] we ought to turn to the better part, and to wish rather the safety than the death of the accused, and to have hope of good in the future, even if it be otherwise in the present. ⟦s8⟧ *But he shall stand.* ⟦s9⟧ Because neither if he eats is he guilty, nor if he does not eat, provided he does this out of devotion.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For one.* ⟦s2⟧ Above he said that some eat all things, others not all; and speaking of the kinds of foods he says this, that some abstain at every time, others at some certain time, and he says that either may rightly be done. ⟦s3⟧ *In his own sense.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, let him be left to his own judgment, lest, having suffered scandal, he withdraw from charity, which is the mother of all virtues.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *He that regardeth.* ⟦s2⟧ *In his own sense* I say, as one abounding: because he that regardeth a day, whether every day, or a day interposed, regardeth it unto the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ *And he that eateth, eateth unto the Lord.* [0515B] That is, unto the honor of the Lord, whose created things he believes to be all clean, and because, being about to serve him, he is sustained by them.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *For none of us liveth to himself.* ⟦s2⟧ For he who is bridled by the law lives not to himself, but to God, who gave the law. ⟦s3⟧ If there were no law, each man would live to himself. ⟦s4⟧ *To himself.* ⟦s5⟧ But unto the Lord, by whose judgment he is damned or crowned; or, *to himself*, that is, unto his own praise, but unto God's. ⟦s6⟧ For as of his greatness there is no end, so of our praise there will be no end. ⟦s7⟧ For when we shall have died in this flesh, we shall not cease to praise God. ⟦s8⟧ And according to this he treats of the faithful only. ⟦s9⟧ *Or to himself*, that is, to be judged by himself, but by the Lord: according to this it is taken of both the good and the evil.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Before the tribunal.* ⟦s2⟧ This is said from a likeness: for the tribunal is the seat of the judge, which is placed on high, [0515C] that the judge may be seen by all and himself from the eminent place may be able to behold all. ⟦s3⟧ So also Christ will sit as it were on a tribunal, because he will be seen by all, and he himself will see the consciences of all, and will judge their causes.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Let us not therefore any more.* ⟦s2⟧ Let us not usurp to ourselves what does not pertain to us. ⟦s3⟧ Let us not reprehend those things which we know not with what mind they are done; or even if they are open, let us not so reprehend them as to despair of their soundness. ⟦s4⟧ For in these two things judgment is rash, and it is either pride or lack of evidence, whence it is said: *Judge not, and ye shall not be judged [n: (Luc. VI)]*.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *I know and am confident.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: I say, Lay ye no stumbling-block before your brethren. ⟦s3⟧ But I do not say a stumbling-block or a scandal because the food is unclean; [0515D] because I know by this, that there is confidence in Jesus, who after he came loosed us from the law. ⟦s4⟧ He puts *common* for *unclean*, as drawn from the vessels which before were common to all uses, of which some, afterward dedicated to the sacrifices, were called no longer common, but holy; or it is drawn from the Jews, because the Jews themselves were called the proper people of God, but all others common and unclean.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Let not then our good be blasphemed.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he that has good works, if he be reprehended in the least matter, his good is obscured, and his good begins to be blasphemed on account of the evil. ⟦s3+4⟧ Whence Ezechiel: *The justice of the just shall not profit him, if he shall have erred [n: (Ezech. XXXIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Ezek. 33:12 *non liberabit eum in quacumque die peccaverit*, "shall not deliver him in what day soever he shall sin," where Migne prints *non proficiet ei, si erraverit*] *Let not then our good be blasphemed.* ⟦s5⟧ Eating, good in itself, [0516A] is blasphemable while it harms others: and because it harms, abstain, because food is not the cause of the kingdom of God.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink.* ⟦s2⟧ Not the use of foods, but concupiscence is to be bridled. ⟦s3⟧ It matters not what foods, or how much, a man takes for the fitness of men and of his own person and for the necessity of his own health, but how easily and with how glad a mind he does without them, when it is either fitting or necessary to do without them.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Joy in the Holy Ghost. ⟦s2⟧ For he that,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Not indeed in feasts, as many are wont, but in the Holy Ghost this joy arises out of brotherly peace; as in dispute there is anger, where the Spirit is not, because he is in the peaceable alone; by whose grace also these three are in a man: justice, peace and joy, which in these Romans [0516B] were not

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Pleaseth God, and is approved of men.* ⟦s2⟧ According as the joy is in the Holy Ghost. ⟦s3⟧ But he is held approved of men according to justice, and to peace also, because dispute begets discord; therefore, rash judgments being dismissed, let us follow after the things that are of peace, not of discord, and that are of edification, that the rest may be edified, or that those already edified may be preserved toward one another, the Jew toward the gentile, and the gentile toward the Jew.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Is offended.* ⟦s2⟧ Disturbed, not knowing what he should hold. ⟦s3⟧ He is offended who departs from the right faith.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Blessed.* ⟦s2⟧ He calls him blessed who does nothing other than what he approves as useful, but he judges that man to be condemned by his own judgment who does not do what he says he ought to do.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0516C]VERS. 23.-- *But all.* ⟦s2⟧ Yet not everything that is done with faith is good, because the ignorance which comes of fault does harm. ⟦s3⟧ The whole life of unbelievers is sin, because everyone who lives or acts unfaithfully sins vehemently, and nothing is good without the highest good, where the knowledge of eternal truth is lacking. ⟦s4⟧ There is false virtue even in the best morals. ⟦s5⟧ Works which seem praiseworthy are, apart from faith, such as great strength and a most swift running off the road. ⟦s6⟧ *Is not of faith.* ⟦s7⟧ Our faith wills that a man do that which he well understands is to be done, and it is sin which is done otherwise than as it has been approved.
