## Markers placed

### 0512D — `[sic: *nonlicet*]` (chunk 0017)
Migne's text as we hold it prints *Quid si jubeat quod nonlicet?* — *non licet* fused into a
single run. Pattern 10 (split/fused type is carried whole) with Pattern 12's marker. **7a⁗
compliance:** the quarantined run carries the clause's negation, so the English supplies it in
plain words beside the marker — "what [sic: *nonlicet*] is not lawful?" The question the whole
paragraph answers (obey God, not the emperor) requires the negative.

### 0512D — `[sic: *cor tempto*]` (chunk 0017)
*cor tempto illo obtemperandum est Deo.* The run *cor tempto* is *contempto* split at the wrong
point (Pattern 10, split direction; cf. *ur sam*, *Ad dam*). The run is the ablative absolute's
participle, i.e. the clause's whole force, so per 7a⁗ question 1 the sense rides in plain words
beside the marker: "[sic: *cor tempto*] that one being despised, God is to be obeyed."
Conjecture: *contempto illo*.

### 0517C — `[sic: *daban*]` (chunk 0019)
*exemplum bonae conversationis daban fidelibus.* *daban* is not a word; conjecture *dabant*
(the clause's only finite verb). Carried untranslated per Pattern 7's non-word class, with the
verb supplied beside it per 7a⁗ question 1: "they [sic: *daban*] gave to the faithful an example
of good conversation."

## Readings rendered as printed, no marker

### 0507C — *in fine Christi* (chunk 0015)
*quando Judaei Christianos sequentur in fine Christi.* Rendered literally, "in the end of
Christ." Conjecture *in fide Christi* ("in the faith of Christ"), which the surrounding argument
(the Jews imitating the gentiles in good, Moses returning to his mother and brethren) wants.
Declined for `[cj:]` under Pattern 18a: the printed reading is obscure rather than false, and
*in fine* answers to *in fine saeculi* three words earlier, so it may be the author's own echo.

### 0506D — *deferente gratia* (chunk 0015)
*non immissa malitia, sed deferente gratia.* Rendered "but by grace giving way," the literal
force of *deferre*. Conjecture *deserente gratia* ("grace deserting"), which is the Augustinian
commonplace. Not marked: *deferente* is a real word occupying its slot (Pattern 12's
`fuges`/`munde` boundary).

### 0507A — *Sicut seipsos acetum mihi obtulerunt* (chunk 0015)
Accusative *seipsos* where the sense wants nominative *ipsi*. Rendered "Just as they themselves
offered me vinegar" — English has no exponent for the case slip (Pattern 9), so there is nothing
to mark and nothing to undo. Logged so a later sweep does not churn it.

### 0510D — *fidelibus voto demonstratur Ecclesiae* (chunk 0016)
Will not construe cleanly. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow: "shown in the Sacrament
of the altar to the faithful by the vow of the Church, which is signified in that thing which she
offers." Conjecture candidates (*quotidie* for *voto*; *votum … Ecclesiae* as subject) are not
decidable from our text. No repair made in the English.

### 0510D — *quod, justitiae terminos non egrediatur quod nobis solum utile sit* (chunk 0016)
The doubled *quod* leaves the relative stranded. Rendered literally with the printed sequence
preserved. Negation *non egrediatur* carried.

### 0511C — *et fretum spe futuri* (chunk 0016)
*fretum* is a bare participle with no expressed subject. Rendered "and reliance upon the hope of
what is to come." No repair.

### 0512C — *Non sunt timore boni* (chunk 0017)
Migne prints ablative *timore*; the Vulgate (Rom 13:3) has dative *timori boni operis*. Rendered
"They are not a terror to the good." **This is treated as Pattern 9 orthography** (final *-e* for
*-i* is standard medieval practice, not a defect of this plate), not as a silent repair of a case.
Flagged here so the judgment is visible; if a plate read shows *timore* is Migne's own oddity rather
than routine spelling, the right treatment is `[var:]` and the ablative rendering, not a marker.

### 0513A — *ocultis* for *occultis* (chunk 0017)
Attested medieval spelling. Translated normally, no italics, no marker (Pattern 9).

### 0515A — *quicunque humani cibi indifferenter sumi* (chunk 0018)
The tail of an *usque ad* abridgment; the infinitive *sumi* has no governing verb because Migne's
abridgment cuts before it. **No verb supplied** (the 8981/8978 *usque ad* precedent): rendered
"whatsoever human foods indifferently to be taken." Not a defect — an abridgment artifact.

### 0517D — *consumationem* beside *consummationem* (chunk 0019)
Both spellings inside one sentence (the gloss's own *consummationem saeculi*, then Matt 28:20 as
*consumationem*). Attested variant; translated normally (Pattern 9).

### 0518A — *quibus credere debeant Romam* (chunk 0019)
*Romam* accusative where *Romani* (nominative subject) would be expected. Rendered as accusative
for locative, "whom they ought to believe at Rome" — an attested late/medieval usage, so the
printed word is rendered rather than replaced. Alternative (*Romani*, "whom the Romans ought to
believe") not adopted: it substitutes a different word.

### 0519B → 0520A — the column jump (chunk 0019)
The anchor sequence runs …0519A, 0519B, **0520A**, 0520B, skipping 0519C–D. **Not a defect.**
Cols 0519C–D carry the opening of *I Corinthians* (idno 8981, already shipped); Migne divides the
page between the two works. Reproduced in order verbatim; the sentence *plene tamen a nullo fuerunt
[0520A] scita* runs across the jump intact, which is the check.
