## THE ARGUMENT.

[0671A]

The apostle James instructed the holy clergy concerning the cultivation of the heavenly precepts, and the rule of catholic observance, and concerning the majesty of unconquered patience, and concerning the revelation of very many things, and concerning the commissioning of masters.

## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *James.* This man, after the apostles had been sent forth to preach, took up the care and government of the church of Jerusalem. *To the twelve tribes.* To have a special solicitude concerning these belonged to the twelve apostles, who discharged the embassy for the Lord among the circumcision. Whence Paul also says: *James, and Cephas, and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave the right hands,* etc. For that reason he writes to them especially, [0671B] and because he himself had been ordained bishop over them by the rest of the apostles.

VERS. 2.-- *All joy.* Be not indignant if the wicked flourish in the world, if you suffer, because it belongs not to Christian truth to be exalted in temporal things, but rather to be brought low. The wicked have nothing in heaven, you nothing in the world. In the hope of that good toward which you strive, whatever may befall on the way, you ought to rejoice. *When you shall fall into temptations.* He begins from the more perfect, concerning outward temptations.

VERS. 3.-- *The trying of faith,* etc. [n: (BED.)] The same sentence, but in reversed order, is read in Paul, etc., *as far as* by which faith is proved perfect.

VERS. 6.-- *But he that wavereth.* He who, pressed by the conscience of sin, doubts concerning the heavenly rewards, when the wind of temptations comes upon him, easily deserts the [0671C] state of faith, and, according to the will of him that tempts, is swept at the blast of the wind through divers errors of vices.

VERS. 10.-- *But the rich.* He calls that man rich who puts his whole hope in riches; it is not harmful to have riches, but to love them. *But let the rich man glory.* Ironically. As if: He ought not to glory in his own glory, wherein he is proud and oppresses others, because it must be ended and humbled in hell. Or, *Let him glory in his humility,* that is, not in pride, but let him humble himself by serving others out of his riches. *For as the flower of the grass.* The just man flowers as the palm, the unjust as the grass: because the one remains, the other quickly passes away. The flower of the just man is hope, which awaits fruit, the root of the just man is charity, which remains unmoved; the root of the wicked is covetousness, his flower the delight of temporal things.

VERS. 11.-- *The rich in his ways.* The ways of the rich man are the temporal [0671D] things by which he seeks to be made blessed, which shall quickly be destroyed. The heat of the sun is the coming of the severe Judge: either unforeseen at each man's death, or in the judgment, in common. In which the just man shall remain as a fruitful tree.

VERS. 12.-- *Blessed is the man.* Because it is hard to exhort to contempt of the world, he adds concerning the greatness of the reward: [0672A] *Blessed is the man,* etc. Not only ought you to suffer stripes and prisons, but also the loss of temporal things, which are vile and transitory, and their lovers are to be punished.

VERS. 13.-- *Let no man, when he is tempted. [n: (BED.)]* Thus far concerning outward temptations, now, etc., *as far as* and that man sins as it were of necessity.

*For that he is tempted by God.* God sends the outward temptation for the proving of his own, but the inward, by which theft, adultery, murder are often conceived, God does not send.

VERS. 14.-- *Every man is tempted.* There are two kinds of temptations: one which proves, according to which God tempted Abraham. The other which deceives, according to which God tempts no man. When upon [0672B] the foundation we build wood, hay, stubble, the devil lays fire upon it. Let us build gold, silver, precious stones: and he will not dare to tempt; nor yet does he altogether desist, but he sits in secret places that he may kill the innocent.

*Bringeth forth death.* As he who, being tempted, overcomes, [has] the rewards of life; so he who, enticed by concupiscences, is overcome, deservedly incurs the ruin of death.

VERS. 17.-- *Every gift,* etc. [n: (BED.)] After he had taught that vices are not to us from God, but from ourselves, etc., *as far as* the perfect gift can be understood.

*Nor of alteration.* No shadow shuts off his light, so as to send in any evils. Always the goods of light are in God, but his gifts in us are sometimes such as supervening sins overshadow.

[0672C]VERS. 18.-- *Of his own will.* Every good is from God, and you have not come to this by your own merits, but by the sole grace of the divine will. *That we might be some beginning,* etc. By this begetting let us not think that we are that which he himself is, but a certain primacy among creatures he granted to us by adoption. Whence another translator: *That we might be the firstfruits of his creatures.*

VERS. 19.-- *Slow to anger.* Let no one be angry rashly and without cause, whether against sinning subjects, or against any brethren whatsoever, or against the prosperity of the wicked, because the ripeness of wisdom is not perceived save with a tranquil mind. And he who judges in anger, even if he judges justice, yet cannot imitate the justice of the divine examination, into which disturbance does not fall.

[0672D]VERS. 21.-- *Wherefore casting away,* etc. He has admonished them to seek out doctrine, for the receiving of which, and that they may be able to advance in it, he now exhorts to cleanness of body and of soul. For he who declines not from evil cannot do good. *The engrafted word.* That which by preaching we lay upon your hearts, or [0673A] the word which was engrafted and sown in the day of redemption, when God begot you; now receive it more perfectly, and fulfil in works what you hold in mystery.

VERS. 23.-- *To him that considereth the countenance.* He who purposes in his mind to consider in the Scriptures, as in a glass, the countenance of his nativity, how man is born, how frail, or what he shall be, of how brief an age, set in how many miseries, has contracted great compunction and a will of repenting; but straightway, seduced by some temptation, he forgets his compunction, and returns to sins. To this inconstancy he compares him who gladly hears the word and neglects to fulfil it. And there is a likeness between him who of his own accord, without a teacher, has applied himself to the Scriptures, and him who from another [0673B] hears the Scriptures, since neither has fulfilled them.

When a boy is born he wails, whereby is signified the grief of the soul, and that it enters into life unto the miseries of the flesh, which grief the soul afterwards forgets, being accustomed to the enticements of the flesh.

VERS. 26.-- *Not bridling his tongue,* etc. [n: (AUG., Serm. 3 ad fratres in eremo.)] What else is wordiness? etc., *as far as* and do thou diligently attend: *He that bridleth not his tongue, his religion is vain,* etc.

VERS. 27.-- *And to keep himself unspotted from this world.* By *saeculum* he understands the world, or all the things which are in the world, as are the concupiscence of the flesh, and of the eyes, and the pride of life.
