## CHAPTER II.

[0673C]VERS. 1.-- *Do not hold the faith in respect of persons. As if he should say:* The Lord does not invite the rich to the faith and despise the poor, but he chose the poor in this world, and endowed them with faith. The world casts out the poor man, courts the rich; the faith of Christ on the contrary teaches that all the glory of the rich is as the flower of the grass, while mercy toward the poor flowers unto eternity. *Of the Lord of glory.* He names him the Lord of glory, that his commands may be obeyed, who repays with everlasting glory that which is given to the poor for love of him.

VERS. 5.-- *Hearken. [n: (BED.)]* Attend more diligently, because not those who are richer in the world, etc., *as far as* yet these he has rendered illustrious and noble by the expectation of the kingdom to come.

*Hath not God. [n: (BED.)]* God chose the poor, who elsewhere says: *Fear not, little flock, for it hath pleased your [0673D] Father to give you a kingdom.* He chose poor parents also, by whose office he himself should be nourished.

VERS. 6.-- *Do not the rich.* More openly he shows whom he had called rich above, namely those who prefer riches to Christ, and, themselves strangers to the faith, oppress by power those who believe, and drag them to the judgment-seats of the more powerful. And there is another cause why they are not to be chosen, because they bring evils upon the faithful.

VERS. 7.-- *They blaspheme.* In the times of the apostles many of the Gentiles and of the Jews, and especially the richer, are found to have done these things.

VERS. 8.-- *If yet the law.* Because he had spoken harshly and concerning contempt of the rich, and in a manner contrary to the divine law, which commands that all be loved, [0674A] lest the rich be thought to be despised altogether, he adds: as if he should say, for the aforesaid causes they are not to be chosen, but if you fulfil this law which says: *Thou shalt love thy neighbour [n: (Matth. XXII)]*, you do well; because, although the rich are not to be chosen on account of their riches, yet they are not the less to be loved for God's sake.

*Royal.* That is, excellent, or because it is common to all, and embraces all. Or that which says: *Thou shalt not confound the person of the poor, nor honour the countenance of the mighty [n: (Levit. XIX)]* [var: Vulg. *Non consideres*, "Thou shalt not regard"], and elsewhere: *There shall be no difference of persons: you shall hear the little as the great, because it is the judgment of God [n: (Deut. I)]*.

VERS. 10.-- *But whosoever.* Lest they should think it a contemptible sin to transgress the law in this one matter, he adds: *But whosoever,* etc. *But offend [0674B] in one, he is become guilty of all.* Truly he who offends in one point is a transgressor of the whole law, because he who sins acts against the author of the law, whom he slights in his own law, and against charity, which is the cause and mother of the whole law. Let us see this sentence from a like case: If anyone should offend me, he would offend all my brothers and friends in me, and in a manner would sin against all. So he who neglects one commandment renders useless to himself the rest, which, when fulfilled, seemed to help.

VERS. 12.-- *So speak ye. [n: (BED.)]* Since it is an evil that a rich man be chosen on account of his riches, etc., *as far as* which calls the willing to penance and remits sins.

As if he should say: Speak well, and do well: because if you neglect it, you shall be more grievously condemned than those who were [0674C] in the time of Moses, because from him to whom more is committed, more is required. By how much the greater mercy a man obtains from the Lord, by so much the more unjustly does he deny mercy to his needy neighbour, and the more justly does he pay the penalties of impiety.

VERS. 13.-- *But mercy exalteth itself. [n: (BED.)]* As in the judgment he shall grieve who has not shown mercy, so he who has shown it, being rewarded, shall exult and rejoice.

VERS. 14.-- *What shall it profit,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Here he disputes more at large concerning the works of mercy, that he may console those whom he had terrified by the preceding sentence, teaching by what remedies daily sins are expiated, without which life is not passed. Lest those who offend not in one point only, but in many, be found in the judgment guilty of all.

[0674D]VERS. 18.-- *But some man will say.* Not only for the aforesaid reasons ought you to be incited to work well, but also for this, lest you suffer reproach from others, because someone, having taken confidence from his own works, that he may show that faith alone avails not, will say by way of reproach: *Thou hast faith,* etc.

VERS 19.-- *Thou believest,* etc. He has proved that those who have not works have not true faith; now he makes plain of what sort the faith is that they have, lest they should make much of such a faith. *And the devils believe, and tremble. [n: (BED.)]* It is written, etc., *as far as* they are to be reckoned slower and more insolent than the devils.

[0675A]VERS. 20.-- *But wilt thou know, O vain man? [n: (BED.)]* It having been proved that faith without works is dead, etc., *as far as* let them imitate in temptation and in working.

VERS. 21.-- *Abraham our father,* etc. [n: (ID.)] That Abraham is said to have been justified by faith without works, etc., *as far as* he is set forth as an example of both. *Offering his son Isaac upon the altar?* A great temptation, when he was bidden to slay his son; a great faith, when even of the dead he believed that he could receive seed; a great work, when he did not hesitate to offer his best beloved.

VERS. 22.-- *Thou seest that faith did co-operate with his works, and by works,* etc. Because he offered him in whom he had received the promise, the great power of faith appears: because, although he offered him, he believed that the promise was to be [0675B] fulfilled, since he believed that God was able even to raise the dead.

*In like manner also Raab the harlot. [n: (BED.)]* Lest they should plead that they were not able to imitate the works of so great a father, etc., *as far as* and was numbered in the generations of the Saviour.
