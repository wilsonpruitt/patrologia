### 1b · Divergences RECORDED but NOT marked, with the reason (11)

- **0671A · Gal. 2:9** *dexteras dederunt* ‖ Clem. *dextras dederunt mihi, et Barnabae
  societatis*. `dexteras`/`dextras` is one word in two forms; the tail is Migne's `etc.`
  truncation. No English exponent, no marker.
- **0671B · Jac. 1:6** *Qui **autem** haesitat* ‖ Clem. *qui **enim** haesitat*. A bare
  connective swap. **Declined**: nothing in the gloss turns on "but" against "for", and
  Pattern 14 fires on divergence that changes the sense. Recorded so the reader knows it was
  seen.
- **0671C · Jac. 1:9–10** *Dives autem **glorietur*** — the glossator lifts *glorietur* out of
  v. 9 (*Glorietur autem frater humilis*) to complete v. 10's elliptical *dives autem*. That
  is the Glossa's citing practice, not a variant text.
- **0671C** *flos **feni*** ‖ Clem. *flos foeni*; **0674A** *Quicunque* ‖ *Quicumque*;
  **0676D** *Nunquid* ‖ *Numquid*. Orthography, Pattern 9. Named here as a class so a later
  reader does not re-raise them one at a time.
- **0672A · Jac. 1:14** *Unusquisque tentatur* ‖ Clem. *Unusquisque **vero** tentatur*; and
  **0678A · Jac. 4:6** *Superbis resistit* ‖ Clem. ***Deus** superbis resistit*. Lemma
  truncation, not variation.
- **0673B · Jac. 1:26** Augustine's tail quotes the verse as *Qui **non refrenat** linguam
  suam* where the lemma four lines above prints the Vulgate's participle *non **refrenans***.
  Both forms stand on the page, both are rendered as printed, and the difference is legible to
  the reader without a marker.
- **0674A · Deut. 1:17** *ita parvum ut magnum audietis … quia **judicium Dei** est* ‖ Clem.
  *ita parvum audietis ut magnum, **nec accipietis cujusquam personam**, quia **Dei judicium**
  est*. Word order twice, plus an abridging omission. No assertion changes.
- **0675A · Jac. 2:21** *Offerens **filium suum Isaac*** ‖ Clem. *offerens **Isaac filium
  suum***. Word order only; rendered in Migne's order.
- **0675B · Jac. 2:25** ***Raab*** meretrix ‖ Clem. ***Rahab***. A proper-name form. Kept as
  Migne prints it, on Ruth's *Lia* precedent (Migne's name forms stand).
- **0678D · Job 31:38** *Si adversum me terra mea clamat **et** sulci ejus deflent?* ‖ Clem.
  *…clamat, **et cum ipsa** sulci ejus deflent :*. Two words dropped and the colon set as a
  `?`. The punctuation is Migne's and rides as printed (Pattern 8); the omission changes
  nothing the gloss uses.
- **0680B · Jac. 5:20** *Salvabit animam **suam** a morte* ‖ Clem. *animam **ejus***. **This
  one matters and needs no marker of ours**, because Migne prints the attribution himself:
  *Quidam codices habent*. The gloss's whole point (*per hoc … sibi conquirit*) depends on
  *suam*, and the reader is told on the page that it is a codex variant.

### 1d · The one scripture divergence that is neither Migne's nor the Clementine's

**0675C · Prov. 24:16.** Migne prints *Septies **in die** cadet justus et resurget*; the
Clementine has *Septies **enim** cadet justus, et resurget*. The added *in die* comes from
Luke 17:4 (*septies in die*), and **the gloss depends on it**: the next clause reads *sicut
quotidiana est offensio, ita quotidiana est medela*. Rendered as printed ("Seven times in the
day"). **No `[var:]`**, and the reason is a judgment, not an oversight: this is not Migne's
compositor slipping but the glossator quoting a conflated form he then argues from, and a
marker saying "Vulg. lacks *in die*" would invite the reader to discount the very word the
argument turns on. The divergence is recorded here instead, where the argument can be given.
⚑ Note also that the gloss's closing *sed impii **corruunt** in malum* echoes the same verse's
*impii autem **corruent** in malum* in the present tense, outside any italic lemma.

Each is a form unique in `src/latin/` against a frequent correct twin, so each is carried
under Patterns 7/10/12 **and** Englished beside the marker under 7a⁗ (the marker never stands

- **0676A · *sapientiem*** for *sapientem*. Corpus control: **1 `sapientiem` (this site) against

## §3 · THE `[cj: …]` — 0678A, a dropped negative in the gloss on *Propter quod*

Migne prints, on Jac. 4:2 (*propter quod **non** postulatis*):

> *Propter haec, scilicet quod Deum digne postulatis; si enim illum pia intercessione
> postularetis etiam temporalia ad usum necessaria, et non solum sempiterna daret.*

Rendered as printed, the gloss says the readers **do** ask God worthily, which is the opposite
of the verse it glosses and is contradicted by its own next clause: *si … postularetis … daret*
is contrary to fact, so they are not asking. A *non* has fallen out of the setting.

`[cj: *postulatis*; read *non postulatis*, "you ask not"]`

**Which word, and why the rival lost.** The alternative was to gloss *digne* (read *non
digne*, "not worthily"), which also construes. It is rejected because the lemma itself is
*Propter quod*, whose verse reads *propter quod **non** postulatis*: the word that fell out is
the verse's own *non*, standing where the verse stands it, before the verb. Glossing *digne*
would put our conjecture on a word the verse does not carry. The marker opens on *postulatis*,
which is verbatim in the Latin twin, so the convention's guard is satisfied — the 7561 @0648
precedent for a **dropped** word exactly.

**Bar check.** Pattern 18a: a lost negation is inside the class the extension admits, and
incoherence is not a defence there.

## §4 · MIGNE'S OWN, RENDERED LITERALLY AND LEFT STANDING

### 0678A · *negligunt Sed humilibus* — a full stop that is not there

Migne runs *…qui Deo subdi poenitendo negligunt Sed humilibus dat gratiam…* with **no mark
between the two sentences** and a capital *S*. Pattern 8 forbids supplying one, so the English
runs on identically: *…neglect to be made subject to God by repenting But to the humble he
gives grace…*. ⚠ **Do not "fix" this**: the missing stop is the record. Wants a plate read.

### 0673C–0674D · `VERS 19.--` without the period

At 0673C the run of verse addresses is `VERS. 1`, `VERS. 5`, `VERS. 6`… and at 0674D it prints
`VERS 19.--`, alone in this work. Reproduced verbatim (the address passes through untranslated).
⚑ Not unique to this work: `src/latin/8980/0003.md` prints `VERS 14.--` the same way, which is
some evidence the form is Migne's or Corpus Corporum's habit rather than a one-off here. Wants
a plate read.

### 0675C · *alii male docendo, et **aliis** male vivendo*

*Se illis … alii male docendo, et aliis male vivendo, et aliis modis* — the second member wants
the nominative *alii* to answer the first, and prints the dative/ablative *aliis*, which then
sits oddly beside the genuine ablative *aliis modis* three words later. Rendered for the sense
the parallel requires ("some by teaching ill, and others by living ill, and in other ways") and
**not marked**: nothing is broken type, and Pattern 18 is scoped away from case/number slips
that merely fail to construe (18a's declined list). Logged so a plate reader can settle it.

### 0676B · *concinantur* — read as *concinnantur*, and the rival named

*…cuncta fere facinora, aut **concinantur**, ut latrocinia, stupra: aut patrantur … aut
defenduntur…* Corpus control: `concinantur` occurs **once**, here; `concinn-` forms occur nine
times (`concinnant`, `concinnare`, `concinnatum`, `concinnabat`, `concinnandas`…), and single-`n`
`concin-` forms in the corpus are all from *concino*, "to sing together" (`concinamus`,
`concinat`). Rendered **"are contrived"**, i.e. read as *concinnantur* with the medieval
simplification of the geminate, which is a Pattern 9 orthographic convention and not an
emendation. ⛔ **The rival was to render *concino* literally — "almost all crimes are sung
together, as robberies, ravishments" — and gloss it `[cj:]`. It is rejected** because the
triad *concinantur / patrantur / defenduntur* is a contrive-commit-defend sequence in which
only *concinnare* has a slot, and because "sung" is not a sense *concino* carries of a crime in
any Latinity. Stated here rather than hidden, because the reader meets an English word whose
Latin looks like a different verb.

### 0672B · *nec tamen omnino desistit*

Negation on *desistit*, scoped by *omnino*, with the *sed* correlative answering
(*sed sedet in occultis*). English keeps the scope: "nor yet does he **altogether** desist" —
not "does not desist at all", which would deny the *sed* clause. Checked against the 8960
@0734B failure shape and cleared.

### 0672B · *Sicut qui tentatus superat, praemia vitae*

The first limb of the comparison has no verb at all in Migne; the second supplies *incurrit*.
Rendered with a bracketed English copula, "[has] the rewards of life", rather than promoting a
verb into the running text.

### 0678C · *sic aerugo vestras carnes*

The same shape: *sic aerugo vestras carnes* has no verb. Rendered "so rust [consumes] your
flesh."

### 0680A · *quid ergo multi fideles multis orationibus?*

The `*usque ad*` tail ends on a verbless question. **Not completed** — rendered elliptically,
"what then of many faithful men with many prayers?", keeping Migne's question mark. The
abridgment removes words; it does not license us to supply a verb (the *ferias* lesson).

### 0678A · *Quasi dicat, lex non fecit*

Two readings construe: "the law did not do so" (i.e. did not forbid detraction, which is what
the detractor implicitly asserts) and "the law did it not [well]". **The first is taken**, on
the strength of the immediately preceding *Quae detractionem prohibuit*, which supplies the
verb's content. The other is not excluded and nothing else in the paragraph turns on it.

## §5 · SUPPLIED SUBJECTS AND PRONOUNS — every one, with its evidence

The dominant defect class, so each supplied person is named here rather than assumed.

- **0671A** *Iste … suscepit* → "This man" = **James**, from the lemma *Jacobus* immediately
  above.
- **0671A** *Idcirco eis scribit* → "he writes" = **James**, from the same lemma and from
  *ipse … ordinatus erat episcopus* in the same sentence.
- **0673D** *Pauperes etiam parentes quorum nutriretur officio elegit* → "by whose office **he
  himself** should be nourished". The unexpressed subject of *nutriretur* is **Christ**, not
  the poor and not the parents: the sentence's *elegit* has the same subject as the preceding
  *Pauperes elegit Deus **qui alibi ait*** — the God who speaks Luke 12:32 — and the only
  person whom chosen parents nourish is the incarnate Lord. Named in the English ("he himself")
  so the reader is not left to bind it to *parentes*.
- **0672D–0673A** *Monuit … hortatur nunc* → "he" = **the Apostle**, the running subject of the
  gloss's verbs of comment (*subdit*, *addit*, *monet*, *arguit*) throughout all four chunks.
- **0676A** *si impetus dirigentis bene **eam** gubernat* → "governs **it**" = **the tongue**,
  from the lemma *lingua modicum quidem membrum est* quoted three lines above.
- **0678C** *ut **hanc** visibiliter intuendo* → "by beholding **it**" = **the rust**
  (*aerugo*, feminine), from the lemma *Et aerugo* that opens the verse. The alternative
  antecedent, *poenarum*, is genitive plural and cannot take a feminine singular accusative.
- **0673B** *quem dolorem postea obliviscitur, consueta illecebris carnis **anima*** → "the
  soul", printed in the Latin; not supplied.
- **0679A** *Non dicit … sed* → "**He** does not say" = the Apostle; and *quia ad patiendum …
  invitat* the same.

## §7 · RENDERING DECISIONS THAT A READER MIGHT QUERY

### 7a · The three bare *Nolite* lemmata — Ruth §1.2 applied, with one stated exception

- **0673C** `*Nolite. Quasi dicat:*` (Jac. 2:1). The italic span in Migne **swallows the
  glossator's own formula** *Quasi dicat:*; the span is preserved exactly as printed, so the
  English italic reads *Do not hold the faith in respect of persons. As if he should say:*
  The bare *Nolite* is expanded to the phrase it opens (Jac. 2:1 *nolite in personarum
  acceptione habere fidem*), on Ruth's *Non te* → "Let no man see thee" precedent: "Do not."
  is not English.
- **0675B** `*Nolite.*` and `*Nolite plures.*` (Jac. 3:1) are rendered **minimally**, "*Be ye
  not.*" and "*Be ye not many.*", and this is the exception to the rule just applied. The
  reason: Migne glosses the bare prohibition first and *plures* second, so expanding both to
  the verse ("Be ye not many masters") would collapse two distinct lemmata into one identical
  English and destroy the distinction the double lemma exists to make. The rule bends where
  obeying it would erase what it is protecting.
- **0674C** `*Superexaltat autem.*` is rendered "*But mercy exalteth itself.*" — the subject
  *misericordia* supplied from Jac. 2:13, the verb's own extent kept. The fuller "above
  judgment" was declined as more supply than the fragment reaches.

### 7b · 0673B · *Per saeculum intelligit mundum* — the one added italic span

The gloss defines *saeculum* by *mundus*, and English has one word for both, so a faithful
all-English rendering ("By the world he understands the world") explains nothing. The glossed
word is therefore carried in italic Latin: "By *saeculum* he understands the world, or all the
things which are in the world." This is the *stylus*/*epistylium* (11551) and
*murenulae*/*ferculum* (10804) precedent — where the gloss **is** the word, the word stands in
Latin. ⚑ It is the only place in this work where the English carries an italic span the Latin
does not, and the band check at 0673B reports it (LA 4 / EN 5); that delta is this and nothing
else.

### 7c · 0679A · *Sufferentiam Job* rendered "the patience of Job"

*sufferentia* is "endurance", and the familiar English is exactly the pressure 7a″ warns
about. The traditional wording is nevertheless allowed **here** because Migne's Latin is
verbatim Clementine (Jac. 5:11 *Sufferentiam Job audistis*), which is the register's own
scripture-echo licence and Ruth's *Aethiopia praeveniet* precedent. Nothing in the gloss turns
on patience against endurance; what it turns on is *sufferentiam* against *finem*, and that
contrast is carried.

### 7d · 0676A–B, 0675C · *magisterium* fixed as "mastership"

*magisteria* / *magisterium* occurs four times (0675B ×1 plural, 0676B ×2, and the argument's
*mandatione magistrorum*). One English is used throughout — **mastership**, with *magistrorum*
as "masters" — because the chapter's whole argument is about the one office and elegant
variation would break it.

### 7e · 0671B · *A perfectioribus incipit de extrinsecis tentationibus* — a call declined

*a perfectioribus* may be "from the more perfect [men]" or "from the more perfect [matters]".
Rendered literally, "He begins from the more perfect, concerning outward temptations", which
carries both. **Declined** rather than resolved: nothing later in the work disambiguates it,
and the sequence it announces (outward temptations first, inward at VERS. 13 *Hactenus de
tentationibus exterioribus*) works under either reading.

### ⭐ BATCH RULING — lemma expansion is NOT the defect; CONFORMATION is
This class was raised independently by **three of the four blind readers in this batch** (here, in
1 Peter at 0687C, and as a six-site cluster in Ecclesiastes), each proposing that expansions be
contracted to the printed catchword. **The boundary was already settled last batch, tested from both
sides — one reader flagged expansions as repairs, another flagged a non-expansion as a defect, both
declined — and it stands: the defect is never expansion or its absence, it is CONFORMATION.**
Convention 3 licenses the expansion; what is forbidden is following the familiar English Bible where
Migne's own plate diverges from it. None of the sites raised in this batch is that.
⚑ Worth noting for the next brief: **the readers' "control" is often not a control.** A single-word
lemma that is already complete English (*Sepultura.* → "Burial.") is not the same class as a clipped
lemma whose governed word is missing (*Multas.* → "Many [cares]"), and comparing the two manufactures
an inconsistency that is not there.
