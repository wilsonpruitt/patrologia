## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *In the beginning was the Word.* ⟦s2⟧ Against those who, on account of Christ's temporal nativity, said that Christ had not always been, he begins with the eternity of the Word, saying: *In the beginning was the Word.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *All things were made by him. [n: (AUG.)]* He is not a creature, through whom every creature was made, etc., [0356C] *as far as* which all agree unto the power of the Divinity.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And without him was made nothing. [n: (ORIG.)]* Thus he divides it: *And without him was made nothing.* ⟦s2⟧ The order is: *There is nothing which was made without him.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *What was made, was life in him. [n: (AUG.)]* John Chrysostom: *And without him was made nothing. ⟦s2⟧ What was made.* ⟦s3⟧ Hilary: *Without him was made nothing, which was made in him.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *There was a man sent.* ⟦s2⟧ After he had ascended by a lofty flight beyond every nature into the height of theology, because he had contemplated that the Word had always been with the Father, he presently descends to the lowliness of human nature, and particularly of the Word's forerunner, against those who said that human nature had been in [0356D] the first parent wholly lost and brought to nothing. ⟦s3⟧ *A man sent.* ⟦s4⟧ A man, not an angel, as the heretics will have it, sent from God. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore he was able to speak of God.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *He was the true light.* ⟦s2⟧ Every man is said to be that nature in man which was made to the image and to the likeness of God. ⟦s3⟧ All the other things which are in man are the lower parts of man, and are in a manner outside the man. ⟦s4⟧ In that, namely, the soul, as in a certain higher world, the true light shines, and it enlightens those coming into that world and being born again through grace, because [0357A] they whose life and conversation is in the heavens are as it were in another world.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And his own received him not.* ⟦s2⟧ From the beginning of the world there was no time in which there were not receivers of the divine word; whence he adds: *But as many as received him.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *To be made the sons of God.* ⟦s2⟧ A wondrous power, that they who were sons of the devil should, set free through him, be called both sons of God and brethren of Christ. ⟦s2⟧ But by what order they are made sons of God, and how this generation differs from the carnal, is subjoined: *Who not of bloods.*

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *And the Word was made flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ But that we may securely believe that men are born of God, he subjoins that God is born of man: by this taking on of infirmity our infirmity is healed, that we may be able to be made sons of God. [0357B] And this is it: *And the Word was made flesh.* ⟦s3⟧ That which was above descended below. ⟦s4⟧ What wonder: if that which was below ascended into that which is above? ⟦s5⟧ Nor is it a wonder if men are born of God, since God was born of man. ⟦s6⟧ *Full of grace and truth.* ⟦s7⟧ The man Christ was full of grace, because, with no merits going before, from the time he was conceived the Godhead was in him, filling that man with all fullness, redounding into all the saints; whence his mother also is called not only the mother of a man, but also the mother of God. ⟦s8⟧ Nor is it a wonder if a man is also called God, since God is also called man. ⟦s8⟧ And this is called grace and truth, because it was so promised of old and now exhibited: namely, that God should be in the man, filling him with every good, and through him his own.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0357C]VERS. 15.-- *This was he of whom I said.* ⟦s2⟧ By the demonstrative pronoun it is intimated that Christ was present in that place. ⟦s3⟧ For often the Lord himself, still unknown, before he was baptized and preached, was wont to come to John. ⟦s4⟧ *Who is to come after me.* ⟦s5⟧ Who is born after me, who is to preach after me, to baptize, to die. ⟦s6⟧ Not that he was not already born when he said these things, since he had even been baptized: but he was to come in preaching, in death, but not in resurrection. ⟦s7⟧ *He was made before me, because he was prior to me.* ⟦s8⟧ From the Greek, he was made in my presence, that is, he appeared to me indeed, namely, because he was prior to me.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And of the fullness.* ⟦s2⟧ The forerunner's testimony having been set forth, the evangelist returns to the testimony [0357D] of his own assertion. ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: *The Word was made flesh: we saw his glory, full of grace and truth, and of his fullness we all have received.* ⟦s4⟧ Grace, that we may believe in him, and truth, by which we shall understand him. ⟦s5⟧ *We have received. [n: (AUG.)]* Not only the grace which is plainly given gratis, not for merit, as the faith which works through love, by which the just man lives, but in return for that one, grace, that is, eternal blessedness, which is also called the wage of justice, and grace also, because the merit is from grace. ⟦s6⟧ *Grace for grace.* ⟦s7⟧ Grace is given in the conceiving of the good through grace. ⟦s8⟧ Grace simply, which is not of merit; if afterwards we work with it, we grow accustomed to good things, and then grace is given for the former grace.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0358A]VERS. 17.-- *By Moses.* ⟦s2⟧ By Moses the servant, the foretelling; by the Lord Jesus, the fulfilling of salvation.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *No man hath seen God at any time.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, no mere man living in this body has seen God as he is; whence it was said to Moses: *Man shall not see me and live [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*, that is, no one living in the body shall see my divinity. ⟦s3+4⟧ Whence God added these things to Moses: *Stand in the hole of the rock, and when I shall have passed through, thou shalt see my back parts [n: (Ibid.)]*. [var: Vulg. Ex 33:21 *stabis supra petram*; 33:22 *ponam te in foramine petrae … donec transeam*.] The rock, that is, Christ; the hole, the gibbet of the cross; *I shall have passed through*, that is, I shall pass over from the world to the Father. ⟦s5⟧ The back parts: his birth, his passion, his resurrection, his ascension, through which, being believed, we come to his blessedness and to the sight of him. ⟦s6⟧ That which is read, that God appeared to men either visibly [0358B] to the eyes of the flesh, or invisibly in prophetic visions, is to be understood as done through a creature made subject to him; for the visions of the prophets were wrought by a certain spiritual creature, in which they are declared to have seen God.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The only-begotten Son,* etc. ⟦s2+3⟧ What the sum of grace and truth may be he briefly subjoins, namely, the knowledge of the Trinity itself; whence: *This is life eternal, that they may know thee the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent.* [var: Vulg. Jo 17:3 *Haec est autem vita aeterna: ut cognoscant te, solum Deum verum*, "the only true God."] Which in this life is not full, because no one in the flesh has seen God as he is. ⟦s4+5⟧ Whence neither Moses himself, much less was grace given through him, as he himself also, after all the things seen in the figure of God, said: *Show me thyself [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*, [var: Vulg. Ex 33:13 *ostende mihi faciem tuam*, "show me thy face"; 33:18 *Ostende mihi gloriam tuam*, "show me thy glory."] but the Son, who is in the secret of the Father, has declared him, *because no one knows the Father but the Son, and he to whom the Son shall have willed to [0358C] reveal him [n: (Luc. X)]*. [var: Vulg. Lc 10:22 *et nemo scit quis sit Filius, nisi Pater: et quis sit Pater, nisi Filius*; cf. Mt 11:27 *neque Patrem quis novit, nisi Filius*.] He himself declares to his own what is to be held concerning the Trinity of the Godhead, and how it is to be come to, and he leads them into it.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And this.* ⟦s2⟧ Both this and the other testimonies of John that follow were given after Christ had been baptized, when Jesus was now coming to invite the disciples, whom John by his testimonies sends to him. ⟦s3⟧ A testimony against this, that Christ was hidden: hidden, because lowly; lowly, that he might be able to be despised and killed, that he might redeem the world. ⟦s4⟧ *Who art thou.* ⟦s5⟧ The Jews thought John to be the Christ who was promised in the law. ⟦s6⟧ Others thought him to be Elias, because of his exceeding abstinence and chastity, and his solitary life, and his most sharp rebuking of offences, and the most severe terror of the vengeance to come. ⟦s7⟧ Others said that he was one of [0358D] the prophets raised up again, because of the grace of prophecy.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *He confessed.* ⟦s2⟧ He confessed, as he says afterwards, not that he was the Christ. ⟦s3⟧ He did not deny what he was, that is, that he was the forerunner, as he says when questioned afterwards: *I am the voice of one crying.* ⟦s4⟧ The order of the words is: and he confessed and did not deny, when they sent.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Art thou Elias?* Since all knew the name of Christ, they knew also that Elias was to go before him. But Elias himself is to go before him as about to judge in power; John is figuratively Elias, going before the same one as about to be judged: so that there are two heralds, as there are two advents of the same one. Whence: *I am not Elias,* that is, not the herald pointing to his power; but I show the [0359A] lowliness at which you take offence. As if he should say: Serve the lowly one before the exalted one judges. Behold, neither am I the Christ, nor a judge on the side of power.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not say, I am a man, or John, or the son of Zacharias. ⟦s3⟧ He does not regard his human subsistence or his generation, but, exalted beyond all these things as the forerunner of the Word, he forsakes all things that are contained within the world. ⟦s4⟧ He ascends on high, made the voice of the Word, and confesses in himself no substance beyond the abundance of grace by which he surpasses every creature, that he may be the voice of the Word. ⟦s5⟧ The voice is the interpreter of the mind; but the mind, that is, the understanding of all things, is the Son of God. ⟦s6⟧ John therefore is the voice and interpreter of this mind, because he first showed him to the world: *Behold the Lamb of God. ⟦s7⟧ Isaias,* prophesied this of [0359B] John: *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness,* in which the word sounds, that is, prophecy, and the showing forth and the lamp of the Word present.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Of the Pharisees.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, of the chief men of the Jews, who do not seek doctrine, but envy it. ⟦s3⟧ Who nevertheless come to John for this reason, that they hear that he has preached the common resurrection of all, which they themselves also believed; whence they themselves also agree with Christ in many things. ⟦s4⟧ They gave hearing to the Apostle Paul also in many things.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Why then dost thou baptize.* ⟦s2⟧ The Pharisees heard in the prophets that Christ would come and would baptize, knowing that the Jordan had borne the figure of baptism, and that Elias and Eliseus had crossed it with dry feet; and they did not doubt that the figure of baptism had gone before [0359C] in Elias and Eliseus, whence they now thought that these had risen again and had baptized. ⟦s2⟧ Whence they do not ask, Art thou one of the prophets? but only: Art thou the prophet? that one, namely, who prefigured baptism.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *I baptize in water.* ⟦s2⟧ Let not what I do be imputed to boldness, because I wash bodies with water, I do not take away sins; so that, as I am forerunner in being born and in preaching, so also in baptizing: but he is in the midst of you who takes away sins.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Whose I am not worthy,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He does not say, I will not loose. ⟦s3⟧ For he does loose, since he made him manifest and disclosed many things concerning his divinity and his humanity; but, I am not worthy, because he reckons himself unworthy for the doing of this, in which humility is simply shown.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0359D]VERS. 28.-- *These things in Bethania.* ⟦s2⟧ There are two Bethanias. ⟦s3⟧ One beyond the Jordan, the other round about, not far from Jerusalem, where Lazarus was raised. ⟦s4⟧ That which is beyond the Jordan signifies human nature under the natural law before the streams of grace, which grace was abundant after the incarnation of Christ; which nature had been created to this end, that it should obey the law of nature, in which John baptizes, because he does not free from sin. ⟦s5⟧ He baptizes beyond the streams of the divine gifts, which had not yet begun in Christ to descend into human nature. ⟦s6⟧ That which is on this side of the Jordan signifies the same human nature obedient under the law of grace, which is nearest to Jerusalem, that is, to the vision of peace, through charity, [0360A] in which Christ raises the dead man, because he frees from sin.
