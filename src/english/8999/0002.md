## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

VERS. 29.-- *The next day John saw Jesus.* Typically. John, that is, the people of grace, on the next day, that is, after the law, knows the lamb by whom it is redeemed. *Coming to him, and he says.* Not only by the steps of the body, but also by the approaches of his own inward contemplation, because he deigned to be known by John according to his divinity and his humanity. *Behold the Lamb of God,* etc. Three things the lamb supplies to those who possess him: the milk of simple doctrine, by which little ones are nourished; the wool, that is, the increases of the virtues; the eating of his flesh. *The sins of the world.* [var: Vulg. Jo 1:29 *peccatum mundi*, singular.] The sin of the world is called original sin, which is common to the whole world, by which the whole of human nature, founded at once and together, transgressed the divine laws through disobedience [0360B] in paradise; and that original sin, and the several sins added on top of it, grace looses.

VERS. 30.-- *After me there comes a man.* Christ is man, in which he agrees with us and pays on our behalf; and Son of God, in which he agrees with the Father, and by forgiving sins reconciles us to him.

VERS. 31.-- *That he may be made manifest.* This is the lamb who takes away sins, which I am not, whom also I now know better; but for this reason do I baptize, that he may be made manifest to Israel. For lest, on account of the strangeness of it, they should be the less baptized by Christ in the Spirit, he prepared the way for him by the practice of baptizing; whence, after men had come to him who is the way, his baptism ceases. In John's baptism also the humility of Christ is made manifest, which is the fulfilling of all justice; for as it was needful that Christ [0360C] should be born and die, so also that he should be baptized. For if he himself, who has no need, is baptized by a servant, let not men, however wise and holy, disdain to be baptized by the Lord, without whom they remain in their sins. *Baptizing.* Christ is baptized by John, that all humility, which is all justice, may be shown. But not he alone, lest the baptism of John, in which God-and-man was baptized, should seem holier and greater than the baptism of Christ, in which men are baptized. But others are baptized by John, so that the practice of baptizing may prepare the way for the baptism of Christ.

VERS. 32.-- *And testimony.* As he said that he had come to make Christ manifest, so he bore testimony, namely: *he saw the Spirit.* In which he learned what he did not know before. He had come also to preach penance, that [0360D] is, to make the preparation for Christ's preaching; but he sets down only the preparation of baptism by itself, because the baptism of Christ is the highest sacrament among the rest. *The Spirit descending.* Not that he then first descended, but that by this he might be shown to descend and to remain in those baptized in water and not otherwise, and that John through him might understand more excellently concerning Christ.

VERS. 33.-- *And I knew him not. [n: (AUG.)]* The dove having been seen, he says that through it he learned what he did not know before, etc., *as far as* whence the Apostle: *In labour and in mind they are different, in working undifferentiated.*

[n: (HIER.)] He shows that John did not know, by the baptism of Christ, that is, that the world was redeemed; for he knew [0361A] him to be the Son of God and born in the flesh. Aug. says that John was ignorant of this only: whether he would keep to himself the power of baptizing, or would grant it to some one of his own. *And remaining. [n: (AUG.)]* Spiritually the Spirit is said to remain in Christ, from whom he never withdrew through sin, in whom he always lived wonderfully and wonderfully wrought; into the saints he comes, and through sins he withdraws. Yet he always remains in them for something, as Christ promised them, namely, that they may always press on in good things. For something he does not remain, namely, that they should prophesy from him or work miracles; and therefore this which he says is the first sign of recognizing Christ.

*Who baptizes.* Christ baptizes in the Spirit, not only by remitting sins in the water, but also by cleansing afterwards, and by kindling love, and by filling with gifts.

[0361B] *This is the Son of God.* Here he testifies him to be the Son of God whom above he was calling a man: so that from him there may be had a testimony of either nature.

VERS. 35.-- *The next day John was standing again.* Typically. John stands, the law ceases, yet bearing testimony to Christ. Jesus walks, that is, grace, gathering from this side and from that.

VERS. 37.-- *They followed Jesus.* They followed, that they might now hear him rather than John, not yet however cleaving to him wholly, but that they might see where he dwells, that they might come to him constantly and be taught by him; and afterwards they followed perfectly, when he called them from the boat. Two follow, because they who follow ought to be kindled with a twofold charity.

VERS. 38.-- *Turning.* As it were from behind they had seen him, but [0361C] as it were he offered them his face when he came down from his majesty, that he might be able to be seen, when in place of the shadow of the law he showed forth the light of the Gospel. *What seek ye?* He asks, not as one ignorant, but that they who answer may have a reward: he asks the thing, not the person, *what*, not *whom*, lest he should seem to show himself; they answer the person. *Where dwellest thou?* Mystically. They wish it to be shown them in whom Christ dwells, that they may make themselves like to them. Or: they who see the incarnation devoutly ask that it be shown them how the eternal dwelling is with the Father.

VERS. 39.-- *Come.* As if to say: it cannot be unfolded in speech, but is shown in work; come by believing and by working, and you shall understand; come by the testimony of John, and withdraw from the law, and you shall receive grace. *That day.* He shows the New Testament, or the present life. *And it was [0361D] about the tenth hour.* Because they had come from the law, the decalogue sent them to Christ; at the tenth hour they seek to be taught by him, because none other teaches the law but he who gave it. And he teaches with mercy, and those things are fulfilled which they could not fulfil; or the tenth hour indicates the evening of the world.

VERS. 40.-- *The brother of Simon Peter, one of the two who had heard from John, and had followed.* The brother younger in birth believes first, for the order of faith is not an empty thing. Perhaps for dignity Andrew is said to be the brother of Peter, upon whom, that is, upon whose faith, the Church was to be founded.

VERS. 41.-- *He findeth first.* True piety at once announces the treasure it has found to its brother, that as he is a brother by [0362A] blood, so he may be a brother also in faith. *We have found the Messias,* etc. Christus in Greek, Messias in Hebrew, anointed in Latin: for he was truly and in a special way anointed by the Holy Spirit, and from that anointing Christians are named. That Jacob, after the vision, set up a stone for a pillar which the pouring on of oil anointed: already by divine gift he figuratively understood, through that rock, the rock to come, namely Christ, who was to be anointed with the Holy Spirit; and he prefigured that the Church was so to be founded upon that rock, etc.

VERS. 42.-- *Thou art Simon the son.* First he says the name as he has it from his parents, that the change may be signified when from Simon he calls him Peter. But in the change is marked the liveliness of the mystery; yet neither is the former name without power. For Simon is *obedient*, son of grace or of the dove, when he comes to Jesus, following his brother; [0362B] whence from the rock, that is Christ, he is called Peter, weak in him, in the figure of the Church which is founded upon the rock.

*Thou art Simon. [n: (CHRYS.)]* He changed the name for this reason, that he might show that it is he himself who gave the Old Testament and changed names: because he called Abram Abraham, and Sarai Sara, and Jacob Israel. Therefore upon many he laid names even from their birth, as upon Isaac and Samson; but upon others after the naming which comes from their forebears, as upon Peter and the sons of Zebedee. For those in whom virtue was to shine out from their first age received their names from that time; but those in whom it was afterwards to be increased, upon them the naming was set afterwards.

*Son of Joanna,* etc. Jona and Joanna are said indifferently. Jona is interpreted *dove,* Joanna *grace.*

[0362C]VERS. 43.-- *Into Galilee, and he found Philip.* Galilee is interpreted transmigration, or revelation. For man passes over from the vices to the virtues, and afterwards from virtue to virtue, and so revelation is made to him, that the God of gods may be seen in Sion. About to call a disciple therefore to follow him, that is, to imitate him, he goes out into Galilee, intimating by the place itself that, as he himself advanced in wisdom, and as through sufferings he entered into glory, so also do his followers.

VERS. 44.-- *From Bethsaida the city.* Not in vain is the city named, nor in vain is it said to be the city of Philip, of Peter and of Andrew. For it sounds *house of hunters*, in which is marked the disposition and the office of these men: for they are hunters in the taking of souls; whence Philip, before he becomes an apostle, of his own accord calls Nathanael.

[0362D]VERS. 45.-- *Philip findeth,* etc. Nathanael, most skilled in the Scriptures, understands what others do not, namely, that from Nazareth there comes forth a singular Nazarene; but because he was most learned, he was not chosen into an apostle: for all the apostles were first from the unlearned, that the wise might be confounded. *Whom Moses wrote of.* Like a wary hunter he builds up the truth for his brother by many reasons, namely, that Jesus is he who is foretold in the law and the prophets. The son of Joseph, who is of the house of David: so that through him (not that he was born of him), together with Mary his mother (whom he knew from the prophets to have brought forth as a virgin), he may be marked as being of David.

VERS. 46.-- *And Nathanael said to him.* Either he asks in doubt, or he affirms, as one skilled in the law, that from a city [0363A] of so great a name there can be some good man, or an outstanding teacher, or the supreme Saviour. For Nazareth is interpreted flower, or bud of cleanness, or holiness. And to either utterance there is fittingly subjoined: *Come and see,* etc.

VERS. 47.-- *Behold an Israelite indeed.* Guile, that is, dissimulation. For if he has sins, he confesses them. He does not therefore deny him to be a sinner, but praises the confession in him. But the Pharisees are guileful, who proclaim themselves good when they are evil.

VERS. 48.-- *Whence knowest thou me?* Knowing that the Lord speaks of his conscience, he asks not out of indignation, but in wonder: whence, and from what power. *I saw thee under the fig tree. [n: (AUG.)]* Nathanael, that is, the gift of God, is the true people of Israel, the gift of God, which was [0363B] hidden under the fig tree, that is, made subject under the habit of sinning and the shadow of death, as the first parents covered themselves under the leaves of the fig tree; where he had been seen and sought by the Lord before he saw and sought the Lord, and even before he called him through the apostles.

VERS. 49.-- *Rabbi, thou art the Son of God.* Because Nathanael knew that Christ, though absent, had seen what he himself had done in another place, that is, how and where he was called by Philip, which is a token of Deity, he confesses him not only master, but also the Son of God, and king of Israel, that is, Christ: in which he is proved to be an Israelite, as it is said, that is, one seeing God.

VERS. 51.-- *You shall see heaven opened.* Nathanael the Israelite is said to be about to see heaven opened, and the angels of God descending and ascending, as of old the patriarch [0363C] Jacob saw the ladder, and the angels ascending and descending, who through the blessing was called Israel. *The angels of God.* Often this miracle appeared, that angels ascended and descended upon Christ. *Upon the Son of man.* Christ calls himself the Son of man, affirming what is less, namely his humanity; Nathanael and Peter call him the Son of God, which is greater, affirming his divinity. Behold the confirmation of either nature in Christ. These, confessing his deity, deserve to ascend; he, asserting his humanity, testifies that he came to redeem. *Upon the Son of man.* He confirms that he is man, lest any should err, believing him to be only God, as even Peter did when he said: *Far be it from thee, Lord.*
