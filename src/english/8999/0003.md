## CHAPTER II.

[0363D]

VERS. 1.-- *And on the third day. [n: (AUG.)]* After the showing forth of Christ through the testimonies of John, he himself shows himself by a miracle.

*And on the day.* It signifies that on the third day, that is, in the time of grace, after the time, that is, before the law and under the law, Christ came through the flesh to the nuptials of the Church.

VERS. 2.-- *And there was called.* In which he shows that the nuptials which he made are not to be despised, and he intimates the sacrament of marriage.

VERS. 3.-- *Failing.* The wine fails, that, wine being wonderfully made from water, the power of the hidden Godhead may appear.

[n: (AUG.)] Greater is the power in the creation and governance [0364A] of heaven and earth, etc., *as far as* through which the power that works also in the greater things is brought back to memory.

VERS. 4.-- *What is it to me. [n: (ID.)]* In these words he does not deny his mother, as the heretics say, etc., *as far as* he understands the entrance to God, he drinks the wine.

VERS. 6.-- *Two or. [n: (ID.)]* It belongs to one making an estimate to speak thus, so that he should call the same vessels two-measure which he also calls three-measure, etc., *as far as* sown abroad by the three sons of Noe.

VERS. 7.-- *And they filled them.* That the waterpots were filled at his command signifies that that Scripture also is from the Lord; but while Christ lies hidden in it under the veil, that is, under the covering of the letter, it tastes of insipid water. But when the veil is taken away, it is wine that inebriates. He could indeed have filled them, empty as they were, [0364B] with wine, he who creates all things out of nothing; but he chose rather to make wine out of water, that he might teach that he does not destroy the law but fulfil it, nor in the Gospel does he do or teach other things than those which prophecy foretold.

VERS. 9.-- *And when he had tasted.* The *triclinium*, that is, three ranks of those reclining, standing apart from one another in height: that is, teachers, the continent, the married. The chief steward is some man skilled in the law, who drinks the spiritual sense of the law. Whence to open the spiritual sense in the Scriptures to disciples is to turn water into wine, as when after the resurrection he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures. *The water made wine.* By the water, the baptism of John; by the wine, the passion of Christ: whence the wine also is carried first to the chief steward, that is, to John, who drank the passion before Christ did.

[0364C]VERS. 11.-- *This he did. [n: (HIER.)]* In the beginning of the signs which, being mortal, he was to give to mortals, he turned water into wine; and as, being now immortal, he opened the understanding of the disciples, that in the law and the prophets they might understand Christ, so he first steeped carnal minds with the savour of wisdom, being about afterwards to fill them, when raised again, with a greater glory.

VERS. 12.-- *After this,* etc. After the beginning of the signs, concerning the wine, he subjoins another token of the divine majesty in the casting out of the sellers from the temple. Typically. The Son of God, as lowly, goes down to Capharnaum, that is, into the world, according to that whereby he has a mother and brethren.

VERS. 13.-- *And the Pasch was at hand.* We keep the Pasch when we pass over from the vices to the virtues. To this Jesus comes when he daily visits the Church, and considers the deeds [0364D] of each man, and casts out those who, among the saints, either do good things feignedly or evil things openly. By the oxen that plough are signified the preachers of heavenly doctrine; and these they sell who preach not from the love of God, but for temporal gain. Innocent sheep offer their own fleeces for the clothing of others; by these are signified the works of cleanness and of piety, which are sold when they are performed for human praise. The Spirit appeared as a dove, whence by the dove the Spirit is understood, which the Simoniacs sell. They give coins in loan in the Church who serve, not the heavenly things feignedly, but the earthly things openly. All these are cast out from the portion of the lot of the saints, who either do good things feignedly or evil things openly; and they are now scourged with the cords of their sins unto correction; [0365A] and if they remain uncorrected, at the end they shall be bound. He casts out the sheep also and the oxen, because he shows the life and doctrine of such men to be reprobate. And he overturns the tables, because at the end the very things which they loved are destroyed. *And he went up.* If he himself who gave the decrees of God keeps them, he teaches with how great care men ought to be free for prayers and for the solemnities of God. He is read to have gone up to the Pasch twice: once in the first year of his preaching, John not yet imprisoned, which is what is treated of here; a second time when he was going to his passion; and in both he cast the sellers out of the temple.

VERS. 14.-- *And he found in the temple.* If in the figurative temple, which is a house of prayer, he forbids the trading that is thought honourable, since it is of those things which are offered in the temple, how much more does he forbid [0365B] drinking-bouts and the other graver things?

VERS. 15.-- *Of little cords.* The cords by which they are cast out are the increases of evil actions; whence Isaias: *Woe to you that draw iniquity with cords of vanity [n: (Isa. V)]*. And Solomon: *Each man is bound fast with the cords of his own sins [n: (Prov. V)]*. [var: Vulg. Pr 5:22 *et funibus peccatorum suorum constringitur*, with no *quisque*; Migne prints *funiculis* and *quisque*, and sets *contringitur*, read at the plate.]

VERS. 16.-- *Who sold doves.* Not only they who sell doves, but others also make the house of God a house of trade: that is, all who seek the things that are their own, not the things that are Jesus Christ's.

VERS. 17.-- *The zeal of thy house. [n: (AUG.)]* Good zeal is a fervour of the mind by which the mind, human fear being cast away, is kindled for the defence of the truth. He is eaten by it who busies himself to correct whatever crooked things he has seen; and if he cannot, he bears with them and groans.

[0365C]VERS. 18.-- *What sign dost thou show us, seeing thou doest these things? He answered.* Because they seek a sign, why does he cast the accustomed traffic out of the temple? And he says: *Destroy.* As if he should say: Because this temple signifies the temple of the body, in which there is no stain, and deservedly do I cleanse this figurative one, I who am able by divine power to raise up that one when it has been dissolved by men in death.

VERS. 20.-- *They said therefore.* Carnal men were minded carnally, while he was speaking spiritually. Which the Evangelist opens to us: *But he spoke of the temple of his body.*

*Forty and six. [n: (AUG.)]* Anatole in Greek is the east, dysme the west, arctos the north, etc., *as far as* which have the number of six forty-six times.

*Built. [n: (AUG.)]* The building of Solomon was completed in seven [0365D] years, etc., *as far as* whence it is said that the elect are to be gathered from the four winds.

VERS. 23.-- *And when.* He went to the assemblies, that by words and by signs he might convert the more. And because some, moved by the signs alone, believed in him, truly indeed, yet in such a way that they could afterwards be scandalized in his passion; and because they were not yet born again of water and of the Spirit, they were not worthy men to whom Jesus should entrust himself and with whom he should dwell familiarly. For he knew what was in them, better than the man himself of himself. This faith befits catechumens, who believe in him; but because they are not born again, the communion of the body and blood of Christ is not entrusted to them. Of such was Nicodemus, who, believing, says that he came from God, because of [0366A] the signs which he had seen; but he was not yet born again, and therefore he came by night, not by day, because he was not yet enlightened by the heavenly light.
