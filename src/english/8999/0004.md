## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- *And there was a man,* etc. This man was one of those who had believed through the signs. *Nicodemus by name.* Nicodemus, the victory of the people, or the victor of the people, bearing the figure of all those who, from the Jewish people believing in Christ, overcome the world through faith.

VERS. 2.-- *He came,* etc. Night signifies the letter of the law, or the ignorance of the heart, or fear. And perhaps he came by night because, as a master in Israel, he would blush to speak openly, or from dread of the Jews. Because he had prudently noted the manifest signs, he asks more fully after the mysteries [0366B] of faith; and therefore he deserved to be taught concerning the deity of Christ and his twofold nativity, concerning the passion, the resurrection and the ascension, concerning the second generation and the entry into the kingdom of heaven, and concerning many other things.

VERS. 3.-- *Amen, amen I say.* To one who does not ask he expounds the second nativity, lest Nicodemus should believe that faith alone, on account of which, the signs being seen, he had believed in Jesus, suffices for salvation without the power of spiritual regeneration and without the sacraments. Also, because Nicodemus understood him to be a mere man, like John the Baptist or any one of the prophets, therefore Jesus began to instruct him concerning the second regeneration, by which he understands him to be the Son of God, and himself able to be made a son of God.

[n: (AUG.)] The second nativity of which Jesus teaches is spiritual, [0366C] which is of God and the Church unto life; but that man is minded only of the carnal, which is of Adam and Eve unto death. But as he says that the carnal cannot be repeated, so neither can the spiritual, by whomsoever it is performed, be repeated. For they are born of the seed of the true Abraham, whether through the free woman or through the handmaid.

*Unless a man.* The visible sacrament of water is necessary for the washing of the visible body, as the doctrine of invisible faith is necessary also for the sanctification of the invisible soul.

*Shall have been born again. [n: (CHRYS.)]* That is, if thou hast not been born from above, and hast received the certainty of the doctrines somewhere, thou wanderest outside and art far from the kingdom of heaven; showing and pointing to himself here, since it is not only this that is seen, but there is need of other eyes [0366D] for the seeing of God.

VERS. 5.-- *Of water.* Of water, the visible sacrament, and of the Spirit, the invisible understanding: so that a man may visibly receive the symbol of baptism, and may bring to completion the spiritual understanding of that same symbol. Or: of visible water and of the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 6.-- *That which is born.* He distinguishes this spiritual nativity from the carnal, saying: *That which is born. Of the flesh.* That is, that which is from the first man by the succession of the flesh is carnal. By the name of flesh he signifies not only the flesh, but the whole carnal man. So, on the contrary, the whole spiritual man, in soul and body, is called spirit. *And that which,* etc. By the word and by the sacrament the Spirit is invisibly present, whereby he who is born [0367A] of Adam unto death is born a son of God unto life: who is called not only spiritual, but spirit, because, as the substance of a spirit is invisible, so invisibly, through the visible sacrament, he is made a son of God. *Is spirit.* But do not wonder if I so call what is born spirit, because, as the spirit breathes where it will, and thou knowest not whence it comes or whither it goes, so is everyone that is born of the Spirit.

VERS. 8.-- *The Spirit where he will.* By which he has in his power whose heart he shall enlighten. Thou hearest his voice in the Scriptures, or when someone full of the Holy Spirit speaks; and even if thou hearest it, thou knowest not whence it comes, that is, how it entered that man's heart, or how it returned, because its nature is invisible. So is everyone that is born of the Holy Spirit, because, by the invisible [0367B] working of the Spirit, he begins to be what he was not, so that the unbeliever knows not whence he comes and whither he goes, that is, by what grace he comes into the adoption of a son, and goes into the kingdom of God. And because these things are unknown to the carnal man, he asks again? How can these things be done, not having the spiritual understanding within the letter of the law; he thinks impossible what is said of the nativity through the Spirit, whence he is reproved by the Lord: *Thou art a master,* etc. *And his voice,* etc. The voice of the Spirit is the voice of Christ, which Nicodemus heard; but because he is not yet born of the Spirit, he knows not whence it comes or whither it goes, that is, by what occasion he wills a man to be born in the Spirit, or whither it goes, that is, to what perfection he says that man is born of him. For he who is born of him is made one spirit with him by grace, [0367C] as he himself also is one spirit by nature.

VERS. 10.-- *Thou art a master,* etc. He does not insult him, so that he who is set over all should be held superior to him; but because he wills him to be born of the Spirit, which he cannot be unless he is humble, he humbles one swollen with his mastership. As if he should say: Proud prince, thou knowest nothing; humble thyself as did he who emptied himself, and, born of the Spirit, thou shalt know what now thou knowest not.

VERS. 11.-- *We speak what we know.* That is, because he himself is the knowledge, the speech and the word of the Father. *And what we have seen we testify.* Because he himself is the vision, and the true testimony and truth of the Father. Which, being in the plural, intimates the mystery of the Trinity.

VERS. 12.-- *If I have spoken to you earthly things. [n: (AUG.)]* And because you do not receive earthly things, how can you take in the heavenly, [0367D] that is, the heavenly nativity of the Spirit? since you not only do not believe, but do not even understand earthly things, namely what I said of the temple of my body (which I took from the earth), to be destroyed by you, to be raised up by me.

VERS. 13.-- *But he that descended.* This pertains to the second nativity in the Spirit, because no one ascends in Christ to the Father but he who is born of the Spirit. No one is saved through the death of Christ but he who is born of the Spirit. No one is born of the Spirit but in the death of Christ. And when he says the Son of man, he gives us the assurance of ascending, since we are sons of men.

VERS. 14.-- *And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the desert.* He puts forward his own death, which is the origin [0368A] and the cause of having the things aforesaid, and in faith of it he instructs him. And because he was carnal, and versed in the law, therefore he brings forward a certain part of the law, which he shows to be a figure and significative of his own death, so that in this way it may be plain of the other parts of the law also that they are in figure. And because death is understood by the serpent, and Christ dead is called a serpent, whom he who beholds with faith is set free; as by the beholding of that other one in the figure they are set free from the fiery serpents, which signify the incitements of the vices. *So must the Son of man be lifted up,* etc. He invites the master of the law to the signification of the law. And because he had spoken of the Son of man, lest Nicodemus should think this only, he subjoins that the same is also the Son of God. *For God so loved.* He opens the cause of human salvation, namely, the love of God the Father, from whom all [0368B] restoration is through his Son in the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 16.-- *The world.* Cosmos in Greek, which in Latin is rendered *mundus*, is called adornment. By the world, therefore, human nature is signified, not because according to the body it consists of the four elements, but because according to the soul it has been adorned by God and made to the image of God; wherefore God loves it to this end, that he may make it eternal.

*That he would give his only-begotten Son, that everyone who believes,* etc. Behold, he says the same of the Son of God which he said above of the Son of man, so that by him through whom we were founded in the deity, through the same we may be restored in the man.

VERS. 19.-- *And this is the judgment.* Why is he who does not believe judged? Because the light came which rouses [0368C] men and admonishes them to know their own evils, in which all men are; but some hate the light that admonishes, and flee, lest their evils which they love should be reproved. Others, admonished by the light, accuse their own evils, which is to do the truth, and so they are enlightened. *For theirs were.* He who is in darkness and loves the light is not evil; but he is called evil, and his works evil, who, after the light has come, hates the light and loves evil things And this is what he says: *For their works were evil.* Works, that is, impiety, and unbelief, and hatred of the eternal light, and the unwillingness to look upon it, but the will to remain in the darkness of sins.

VERS. 21.-- *But he that doeth.* Having shown by what justice he is condemned who does not believe, he shows by what justice [0368D] he is saved who believes. *But he that doeth.* That is, he to whom his own evils are displeasing, and who accuses them, does not pardon himself, does not spare himself, but comes to the light. And he begins to act with God who accuses his own evils, because the beginning of good is the accusation of evil. Therefore his works, these namely, that he accuses himself as God accuses, he manifests, because they are done in God; for unless the light of God shone upon him, he would not be displeasing to himself.

VERS. 22.-- *After these things.* Not immediately after the disputation with Nicodemus, which took place in Jerusalem, but when a space of time had passed he returned from Galilee into Judea. *Into the land of Juda, and there he abode,* etc. [var: Vulg. Jo 3:22 *in terram Judaeam: et illic demorabatur cum eis*.] Morally. Where there is confession of sins or of the divine praises, thither comes Jesus, and his disciples, [0369A] that is, his doctrine and his enlightening, where he abides, cleansing from offences; whence there follows, *and there he stayed and baptized.* [var: Vulg. Jo 3:22 *et illic demorabatur cum eis, et baptizabat*; the same clause stands above, in the lemma, as *illic demorabatur*.]

*And there was.* Christ now baptizing, John still baptizes, because the shadow still remains. Nor ought the forerunner to cease until the truth be made manifest.

VERS. 24.-- *For John was not yet cast into prison.* Behold, he plainly notes the deeds of Christ before John was imprisoned, which the others passed over, beginning from those things which were done after John was sent into prison, when, the baptism of the forerunner ceasing, the baptism of Christ openly began.

VERS. 25.-- *And there arose a question from among John's disciples,* etc. While John preaches and his school endures, Christ preaches and works in hiding, [0369B] but baptizes openly, so that a question may arise concerning the baptism of John and the baptism of Christ, and that it may be resolved by the testimonies of John.

VERS. 26.-- *And they came to John and said to him: Rabbi,* etc. Wishing to accuse Christ, whom a little before they had seen baptized by John.

VERS. 27.-- *John answered, and said,* etc. Because beyond the custom of human nature I was born of parents of advanced age, you think me to be beyond men. But know that I am a mere man, and he is God, from whom I have whatever I have. From me, weakness; from him, the sublimity of grace. Do you think that I would say anything against my own testimony? I do not baptize unto the remission of sins, but he does. *He cannot.* As if he should say: I, a mere man, have nothing [0369C] except what is given from God, that is, to baptize unto penance, not unto remission; because I am not the Christ, but he is, as you know that I testified to you; therefore run ye to him.

VERS. 28.-- *I am not the Christ.* I am not anointed by the Father with the fullness of the Spirit, but of his fullness I have received, that I might run before him.

VERS. 29.-- *This joy therefore.* In this my joy is full, that I have been made the friend of the bridegroom, and because I stand and rejoice.
