## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

VERS. 25, 26.-- *I know that the Messias cometh, who is called,* etc. Now advanced further by the words of him who taught her, she knows by whom she ought to be perfectly instructed, that is, by the Messias. *He will tell us all things. Jesus saith to her.* He will show in what manner, the temple and the mountain being despised, we may adore in spirit and in truth, and how, the shadows being cast away, we may be illumined by the truth. *I am he that speak.* Him whom thou awaitest as one to come, know that he has come; and, having brought with thee the husband who is to govern thee, labour to learn the things that are necessary to faith. And because she already knew who could teach her, although she did not recognize him who was already teaching her, she was worthy that he should manifest himself to her; [0374C] whence he says: *I am the Messias,* which manifestation once received, she has nothing further to seek; whence follows: *She left therefore her water-pot,* etc. But the Evangelist interposes somewhat concerning the disciples.

VERS. 27.-- *And they wondered that.* They did not wonder that he was speaking with a woman, with such as he had been accustomed to speak with, but that it was with a stranger, being ignorant of the mystery of the Church that was to be of the Gentiles. And they suspect no evil, but wonder at his clemency, because he teaches a Gentile woman in error, as *he who came to seek that which had perished.*

VERS. 28.-- *She left therefore.* Having heard *I am he,* the woman, now holding Christ in her understanding as the head of the man, left the water-pot, that is, covetousness, and ran to preach the gospel. Here let those who are to preach the gospel learn first to lay aside [0374D] the care and the burden of this world.

VERS. 29.-- *Come and see.* Step by step she leads those rude ones to the truth; nor does she at once affirm him to be Christ, lest they should be angered and take offence.

VERS. 33.-- *Hath any man brought.* They did not dare to question the Lord, fearing lest they should be reproved by him if they questioned incautiously.

VERS. 34.-- *My meat is.* They did not understand the food, just as the woman above did not understand the water; whence the master instructs them, not by a circuit as he did her, but now openly, saying: *My meat is to do,* etc. *His will.* The Father's. The will of the Father is that men believe in him, that is, in the Son whom he himself sent. The work of the Father is our salvation. Christ's meat, therefore, is our [0375A] faith and our salvation. He does the will, then, by teaching men to believe in him; he does the work by bringing the mystery of redemption to maturity.

VERS. 35.-- *Do you not say.* Since he seeks the faith and the salvation of the peoples, he grows fervent for the work and disposes to send labourers. *Lift up your eyes.* Consider with the understanding of the mind that, the winter being past, that is, unbelief, the warmth of faith is at hand and hearts are made ready, that you may gather from them the works of justice; as if he should say: Help toward the fulfilling of the Father's will. And well can you do it, because the harvest is ready, that is, see the Gentiles believing easily.

VERS. 36.-- *That both he that soweth.* Both were needed, the sower and the reaper, because in this the word appears probable, that one sows and another reaps; [0375B] for unless they had been made ready through the prophets, they would not have heard the apostles.

VERS. 38.-- *I have sent you.* I make you reapers there where others have sown, that is, in Judaea, where the first crop was gathered, whence some going forth have sown in the whole world, whence another harvest rises as it were from the grains, to be gathered at the end of the world by the angels as reapers. *They have laboured, and you have entered into their labours.* For many were the labours of the patriarchs and the prophets, in all of which was the prophecy of Christ, and they suffered many things as it were in the cold of the sowing.

VERS. 40.-- *When they were come.* First they heard the report, afterward they beheld him present; and it is not enough for them, but they make him abide with them, that they may be instructed by his own words, which they prefer to the words of the woman. In [0375C] all which, on the contrary, the hardness of the Jews is reproved, who do not believe even at miracles. *And he abode there two.* Morally. So daily among those who are without, Christ is announced through the woman, that is, the Church, and they believe through that report. Thence he abides with them for two days without mention of night, that their light may be instructed by the two precepts of charity.

VERS. 42.-- *And they said to the woman.* After the Lord, by the presence of the flesh, the Jews being spiritually taught, had poured the grace of his own knowledge into the woman, that is, into the law, they said: now not on account of thy speech do we believe.

VERS. 43.-- *For we ourselves have heard.* Because at the first, while he tarried among them, he had wrought miracles, and yet not any believed in him save a few; whence, upon the sign that was seen, his disciples believed in him, and no others: and therefore from the Samaritans, [0375D] who believed easily with no sign seen, he returns to these, that they who did not believe through the former things may now believe. It signifies that from the Gentiles, easily converted, he shall return at the end of the world to the Jews, who are his own country, because among them he had not before had honour.

VERS. 44.-- *That a prophet.* Allegorically. Christ's country is the people of the Jews, where he is without honour, because few of these believe what is said of the Galileans, Christ's fellow-citizens. The Church of the Gentiles, believing easily, is like the Samaritans; and these are many, by the word of preaching alone, those by miracles, and few. Behold why he came, and therefore he returns: because he testified that a prophet has not honour in his own country; otherwise it would seem that he ought not to have returned to these, where he has not honour, [0376A] but to have remained with those among whom he was well received.

VERS. 46.-- *And there was a certain ruler whose son.* This ruler may figure some teacher of the Jewish nation, who at length, after the full conversion of the Church of the Gentiles, to which the Lord, driven out by the Jews, passed over through his disciples, prays for his son, the Jewish people, whom he knows will not receive the faith save at the end of the world, that he may be healed of the long infirmity of his faithlessness.

VERS. 47.-- *And he besought.* Already believing that he could heal his son; but because he does not believe him to be present to the son through the Godhead, which is everywhere, he asks that he would come down and, being present in body, heal him; whence he is convicted of unbelief: *Unless signs and wonders,* etc. On the contrary the centurion is praised, who says: *Say the word, and my servant [0376B] shall be healed [n: (Matth. VIII)]*; whence: *I have not found so great faith in Israel.* Or perhaps he was desiring to try what manner of man Christ was; whence he is reproved: *Unless signs and wonders.* For the salvation of his son he himself believed, and his whole house.

VERS. 48.-- *Unless signs.* In this he is convicted of unbelief, that he did not believe him to be everywhere; and by this man's hardness is signified the hardness of the Jews, who do not believe even when miracles are seen. Truly, on the contrary, by Samaria is signified the faith of the Gentiles, which believed by the word alone; and this, as far as regards the allegory, shall endure in the faithless Jews from Christ's first coming until after the reign of Antichrist.

*And wonders.* *Prodigium* as it were *porro dictum*, spoken far off, as it were that which signifies something. Or, *prodigium* as it were *procul a digito*, far from the finger, because it prefigures something remote and to come.

[0376C]VERS. 49, 50.-- *Come down.* As though he could not heal otherwise unless he were present. Whence the Lord, showing that he is not absent where he is invited, heals by his command alone; whence: *Go, thy son.* He does not go to the ruler's son, lest he should seem to honour riches; he promises to go to the centurion's servant, he who does not despise the nature of man: wherein he destroys the pride which in men weighs not nature, but the things that lie open outwardly. *He himself believed.* Not the other citizens; wherein is signified the fewness of the faith of the Jews, although they had seen signs; whence he infers: *This is again the second sign that Jesus did.* Just as above the multitude of the Gentiles, who were to be converted by the word alone, was signified in the Samaritans.

VERS. 54.-- *This is again the second sign that Jesus did [0376D] when he was come out of Judaea into Galilee.* Of all the miracles which Jesus did in the year before John's death, John makes mention of none but these, and of Christ's disputation with the Jews, which is set down above. For the other Evangelists have plainly told the things which he here passes over in silence.
