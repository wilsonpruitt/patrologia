## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And Jesus.* ⟦s2⟧ Because it was his custom to teach in the temple by day, and in the evening to return to Bethania, which is on the Mount of Olives.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically: It signifies the height of mercy, whence comes to us the chrism, that is, the anointing. ⟦s2⟧ In the morning he returns to the temple. ⟦s3⟧ The morning is the rising of the new light after the darkness of the law, in which he comes to open and to give mercy to the faithful, that is, in his temple. ⟦s4⟧ The sitting is the humility of the Incarnation, by which he has mercy and teaches; whence the people came the more willingly to him, but the hostile Pharisees tempt him concerning justice, whether he will speak against it. ⟦s5⟧ They knew him to be meek and a preacher of mercy, whence he was pleasing to the people; [0389C] whence they thought he would say that the adulteress was to be let go, and in this he would be called an enemy of the law, contrary to Moses and to God its author, and, along with the adulteress, guilty of death. ⟦s6⟧ But if according to the law he should say that she was to be stoned, they would deride him, as though he had not the meekness which he preached, for which he was loved. ⟦s7⟧ But he himself is caught by neither calumny; rather, his meekness being kept, he answers what belongs to true justice: *He that is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her.* ⟦s8⟧ Yet he does not judge at once, but bowing himself down he writes with his finger on the ground.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *They bring.* ⟦s2⟧ Mystically: The woman taken in adultery signifies the Synagogue, which adulterated the law of Moses by following the traditions of the fathers. ⟦s3⟧ Or her who, the law of Moses being let go, now follows the faith of Christ, whence the Jew accuses her as an adulteress. ⟦s4⟧ Or she signifies [0389D] the Church, which, forsaking God, had fornicated with demons, whom the Jew accuses because she comes to Christ, and she was reputed as it were a new kind of idolatry.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *But Jesus bowing himself down.* ⟦s2⟧ What he wrote on the ground, whether their words or something else, is not determined, according as he bows himself down and writes with his finger. ⟦s3⟧ Yet he teaches that, before judgment is made, we ought to bow ourselves, that is, to condescend in judgment, or by acting to use discretion.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And again bowing himself down.* ⟦s2⟧ The sentence of justice having been given, by which they, conscious of their evils, were struck, he writes again on the ground after his custom, turning his face elsewhere: that it might be free for them to go out, whom he foresaw [0390A] would go out sooner than ask more questions. ⟦s3⟧ And he teaches us, as before the correction of another, so also afterwards, to search ourselves humbly, lest the same thing, or something else like it, be in us.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Neither will I condemn thee.* ⟦s2⟧ But because she could fear lest he who is without sin should punish her, he says to her, as one merciful forgiving past sins: *Neither will I condemn thee,* as one just forbidding that she sin any more.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Again therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ The tempters being repulsed and the woman's fault healed, he adds more openly what he can do out of his Divinity, lest they doubt concerning him as a man whether he can forgive sins. ⟦s3⟧ *I am the light of the world.* ⟦s4⟧ Which is covered with the cloud of the flesh, and so is made bearable to men; whence I can both forgive sins and also enlighten [0390B] a man.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Testimony.* ⟦s2⟧ They think that he is not to be believed. ⟦s3⟧ As if: He alone testifies of himself, forgetful of the lamps sent before, and of John the Baptist himself, who showed evidently concerning him.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *My testimony is true.* ⟦s2⟧ My testimony is therefore to be held true, because I know whence I come, that is, because it is confirmed by the testimony of the Father. ⟦s2⟧ I came sent by the Father, yet not forsaking him, and, my ministry fulfilled, I return to the Father, nor yet do I leave the world.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *You judge according to the flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ Because you know not that, therefore you judge according to the flesh, that is, you think me a man who is not to be believed in his own praise, and you even condemn me.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0390C] *I judge not any man.* ⟦s2⟧ I judge not, as it were, according to the flesh, as we do [cj: *ut nos*; read *ut vos*, "as you do" -- Christ is answering *Vos secundum carnem judicatis*]; or I do not judge at all at this first coming, but save; but because at the second coming he will judge, he adds this: *And if I do judge,* etc.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Because I am alone [n: (AUG.)]* Therefore true, because not alone, but I and the Father. ⟦s2⟧ Behold therefore two in persons, though one in substance; which is: Therefore my judgment is true, because I am the true Son of God; whence it is proved that he is the Son, because he is not alone, but the Father is with me.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *And in your law it is written. [n: (ID.)]* Having spoken of the judgment, that his own and the Father's testimony is true, he proves it from the authority of the law. ⟦s2⟧ For it is said, etc., *as far as* as Susanna when accused had.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *They said therefore to him.* ⟦s2⟧ Judging according to the flesh, [0390D] they took Christ's Father carnally; but the man was in the open, the Word of the Father in secret. ⟦s3⟧ *Where is thy Father.* ⟦s4⟧ As if they should say: We see thee, but not the Father with thee. ⟦s5⟧ To whom on the contrary it is said: But neither do you know me; because if it is the man whom you see, you do not know what I am in secret, and therefore neither do you know the Father. ⟦s6⟧ But if you knew me, which is first, know me, and so you would know the Father, because we are one. ⟦s7⟧ *Perhaps also the Father,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ He does not doubt, but as it were mocks and rebukes those who doubt; as a master to a servant, perhaps I am thy lord.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *These words,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Gazae, riches; Phylaxe, to keep. ⟦s3⟧ Typically: The treasury is the mysteries of the Scriptures, which are in the temple, that is, in Christ. [0391A] These secret things he opened to the apostles after the resurrection, but in them he now speaks by parables, the Jews not understanding. ⟦s4⟧ *And no man apprehended him.* ⟦s5⟧ That is, speaking of his Godhead.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *I go.* ⟦s2⟧ As it were of my own accord, and therefore you labour in vain. ⟦s3⟧ He goes thither whence he had come, and whence he had not departed. ⟦s4⟧ *You shall seek,* therefore persecuting him taken up into the heavens, not desiring to have him as friends do. ⟦s5⟧ Whence he foretells them their punishment: *You shall die in your sin.*

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Will he kill himself?* ⟦s2⟧ Foolish words, as if, if he spoke of death, they could not follow him unto death. ⟦s3⟧ He was speaking therefore not of death, but of the glory to which he was going through death; whence to those carnal men he adds:

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *You are below.* ⟦s2⟧ And therefore you savour of the earth, [0391B] as the serpent you eat earth.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *I said therefore to you.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he expounds what it is to be of the world, that is, to be sinners, unbelievers, savouring earthly things; whence he expounds this to them. ⟦s3⟧ *For if.* ⟦s4⟧ But on the contrary he gives hope to those who will believe. ⟦s5⟧ *You shall believe.* ⟦s6⟧ He says, you shall believe, not, you shall perceive, because no one now grasps the unchangeable essence. ⟦s7⟧ *I am, you shall die,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ He does not say what: neither Christ, nor the Son of God, nor anything else of this kind; but to these men he uses the word of the eternal essence, as of old to Moses, whom he was sending to deliver Israel. ⟦s9⟧ As if he should say: I who sent Moses to deliver the children of Israel, am come myself to deliver the lost. ⟦s10⟧ But all other things which are sent, are not truly.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *The beginning. [n: (AUG.)]* Believe me to be the beginning, [0391C] etc., *as far as* he himself is the right hand, the strength, the wisdom, and the Word of the Father.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Behold what it is to be: because the beginning abides in itself, makes all things new, and cannot be changed.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I speak to you.* ⟦s2⟧ And I have many things to speak to you, teaching if you shall have believed, accusing if you shall not have believed; and I who now judge not, but save, have afterwards to judge. ⟦s3⟧ I have, I say, but the whole from the Father, who is truthful of himself, from whom I am the truth which he begot of himself; and therefore I say true things, and I judge. ⟦s4⟧ Behold how he gives glory to the Father, to whom he is equal, that he may teach us who are less to give glory to God.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *And they understood not.* ⟦s2⟧ When he says, *he that sent me is true,* they did not understand that he spoke of the Father, because they still had their eyes closed; and therefore [0391D] he adds to them: *When you shall have lifted up the Son,* etc., where he shows that some were there who after his passion would believe and would know who he was.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Because I am.* ⟦s2⟧ To be is the unchangeableness of the divine substance. ⟦s3⟧ But lest he himself who speaks should be understood to be the Father, he adds: *And of myself I do nothing;* because I am not of myself, but of the Father.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Because I,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That equality itself always is. ⟦s3⟧ Always, not from some beginning and thenceforward, but without beginning and without end. ⟦s4⟧ For the generation of God has no beginning of time, because through the Only-begotten times were made.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *Many believed in him.* ⟦s2⟧ Faith is to believe what thou seest not; truth is to see what thou hast believed. [0392A] We do not know that we may believe, but we believe that we may know: therefore Christ as man on earth casts faith, afterwards he will give himself to be seen, which is the reward of faith.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *Truly disciples.* ⟦s2⟧ To this height you shall come out of faith, which is humble, and is despised, and is a grain of mustard seed.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33, 34.-- *Free.* ⟦s2⟧ This word the Lord set from liberty, not as it is for the most part wont to be said in Latin, that someone is freed from infirmity. ⟦s3⟧ And the Lord had not said: you shall be free, but: *The truth shall make you free;* in which word nevertheless they understood nothing but carnal liberty. ⟦s4⟧ *He answered them,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Not those who believed, but others in the crowd who did not believe, who, having heard *shall make you free,* were indignant that they were called servants, thinking that he had spoken of carnal liberty. ⟦s6⟧ *And we have served no man.* [0392B] They lie. ⟦s7⟧ Joseph served in Egypt; the fathers in Egypt served in the mud; these men serve the same by the tributes of Caesar.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Amen, amen I say,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Amen in Hebrew is said in Latin to be "true," which no interpreter has changed, lest, being stripped bare, it should grow cheap, but that it may keep its honour by being secret; and it is as it were an oath, whence Jerome upon Isaias: The Lord swears by himself, since he in no wise makes void the sentence of his mouth and the word which he has once uttered; that is, he swore by himself.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *But the servant,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In this he terrified us. ⟦s3⟧ What hope of liberty then is there for those who are not without sin?

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *I know,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord having expounded against [0392C] those who gloried in the vain liberty of the flesh, and having expounded the true servitude of sin and the true liberty through himself the Son, granting to these men, namely that they are sons of Abraham through the flesh, he shows that such children are not sons of Abraham by the imitation of faith and of justice, but are sons of the murderer the devil, seeking to kill him, and so are truly servants.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *They answered and said to him.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he reproves them for doing the works of their father, confessing their father to be Abraham, they provoke him to speak some evil things against Abraham, that it may be for them an occasion of doing what they wished; but the Lord praises Abraham and condemns these degenerate men, saying: *If you are the children of Abraham,* etc.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *But now you seek,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He does not say (what he could) that he is the Lord of Abraham, lest it be an occasion [0392D] of calumny, nor that he is the Son of God; but that which they envy, and that which they are able to kill.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *You do the works.* ⟦s2⟧ Now he hints at another father than Abraham, whose sons they are, not by nature, but by imitation. ⟦s3⟧ *They said,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ *We,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Now understanding that the Lord spoke not of the origin of the flesh but of the life, and knowing from the usage of the Scriptures that fornication is spoken of spiritually, when the soul is subjected to false gods, they change their answer and are now silent about father Abraham, lest it be said to them: Why do you glory in him whose life you do not imitate? ⟦s5⟧ And they say that God is their father, wishing to see what he could say to them in answer to this.
