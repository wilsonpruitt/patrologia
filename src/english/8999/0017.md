## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- *Now there was.* While he tarries beyond the Jordan, Lazarus falls sick, and the sisters send to him there. These sisters of Lazarus are a figure of the Jews already received into eternal blessedness, who for the healing of their brother, that is, for the rest still wandering in the darkness of ignorance, beseech the Lord out of their most familiar manner of acting. *Lazarus was sick.* Lazarus is a figure of the Jews who were weak in the faith of Christ: Martha and Mary signify the teaching of the good Jews.

VERS. 3.-- *Behold, he whom thou lovest,* etc. Lazarus, by whom the sinner is signified, who is loved by the Lord, because he came not to call the just but sinners. The sisters [0399B] are the sinner's good thoughts, which pray for the loosing of his sins.

VERS. 4.-- *For the glory of God,* etc. That is, that the Son of God may be glorified. Behold, he calls himself God, against those who say that the Son of God is not God.

VERS. 5.-- *He loved.* The Saviour of the sick and the raiser of the dead loved Lazarus, because he was sick and dead.

VERS. 6.-- *Two days.* That the four days might be fulfilled. For on the first day, on which it was announced to him, the sick man died beyond the Jordan; for two days he remained: behold, three; but on the fourth day he came.

VERS. 7.-- *Let us go into Judea.* Mystically: it signifies that faith is to return to the Jews at the end through Elias and Enoch.

VERS. 8.-- *The disciples say to him.* Terrified, wishing [0399C] to give counsel to their master that he should not die, as elsewhere Peter also: *Far be it from thee, Lord,* this shall not be unto thee. But as Peter is praised for what is another's: *Blessed art thou, Simon Bar-Jona, because the Father hath revealed it to thee,* he is blamed for what is his own: *Go back behind me, Satan, because thou savourest not the things that are of God, but the things that are of man.* [var: Vulg. Mt 16:23 *Vade post me Satana*, "Go behind me," and it adds *scandalum es mihi*, "thou art a scandal unto me"] So too he rebuked these, saying that he is the day in which they ought to walk lest they stumble, and that walking without him they stumble, as now unwilling that he should die who came to die for men. But he says that they are the hours which follow the day, not days, but the day enlightens them. Twelve is a consecrated number which remains complete: for when Judas was cast down, who did not shine among the hours, another is substituted.

VERS. 9.-- *Are there not twelve hours. [n: (HIER.)]* Christ is the day, and in another manner the apostles are the day, as Christ is, [0399D] etc., *down to* they are understood to be the twelve apostles.

VERS. 15.-- *That you may believe.* Not that they should only now begin to believe, but that they might believe more robustly. For their faith was always increased by miracles.

VERS. 17.-- *And he found. [n: (AUG.)]* Mystically: there are four days of death. A man is born, etc., *down to* but mercy does not leave behind even such as these.

VERS. 22.-- *But now also I know.* This I know, that thou canst raise him if thou wilt; which I leave to thy judgment. But I do not presume, nor do I ask that thou raise him, since I know not whether the resurrection be useful to be done.

VERS. 25.-- *Yea, though he be dead.* Jesus raised three dead men: one in the house, that is, in [0400A] the heart; a second at the gate, that is, in the mouth; a third in the tomb, that is, in the deed and the habit of sinning. *He shall live.* As Abraham, Isaac and Jacob live, whose God he is called as of the living. The believer, though dead, lives, as the unbeliever, though living, is dead.

VERS. 28.-- *Secretly.* Because if the Jews had heard that she had been called by Jesus, she would not follow, and so they would not see the miracle. But because they thought that they were going to their brother's tomb, they followed. *And calleth thee.* By reason of brevity in Martha's words he shows what was not said before, that is, that the Lord had called Mary.

VERS. 31.-- *They followed.* The evangelist took care to relate this, that we might see what occasion brought it about that the resurrection of the man four days dead should have more witnesses.

[0400B]VERS. 33.-- *He groaned in the spirit.* Note the power: he himself groaned and troubled himself, whom another could not trouble, as he took upon himself all things of this kind because he willed it. And it signifies how greatly a man ought to be troubled when pressed by so great a mass of sin. But when the sinner, reckoning what good things of God he has received and what evils he has rendered, groans in spirit and is troubled, Christ groans in him and is troubled, because faith concerning Christ is Christ in the heart. But faith groans in a man while he rebukes himself for his sins.

VERS. 34.-- *Where have you laid him?* He who though absent knew that he was dead knows also where he is buried; but speaking thus, he signifies that he as it were does not know the lost man; whence it is said: *I know you not [n: (Matth. VII)]*. And: *Where is Adam? [n: (Gen. III.)]*

[0400C]VERS. 38.-- *Jesus therefore,* etc. He shows the difficulty of the quickening of such a sinner. *Now it was a cave.* Morally: every sinner pressed by the mass of his sins and weighed down by his own law, and now stinking by his own evil habit, openly corrupts others by his bad example: the sisters are certain thoughts, because sometimes, thinking that he has done ill, he grieves.

VERS. 39.-- *Lord, by this time he stinketh.* She feared that, the stone being taken away, the stench would rather be stirred up than her brother raised.

VERS. 42.-- *But for the people,* etc. By these words he shows that he seeks the glory of the Father, as in his other works. For because according to his manhood he was less than the Father, he asks of him the raising of Lazarus, and therefore says that he was heard, and this for the sake of the bystanders, that they might so [0400D] believe him the Son of God.

VERS. 43.-- *With a loud voice.* He groaned, he wept, he cried out, because he rises with difficulty whom the mass of an evil habit presses; but nevertheless after the great voice he rises, that is, by the great grace of God he is quickened while he repents.

VERS. 44.-- *And forthwith he came forth.* While the sinner despises, he lies buried; when he repents, he rises; when he confesses, he comes forth, as though going out of hiding into the open. *Loose him.* Christ by himself quickens within; to his ministers he grants the power of loosing.

VERS. 48.-- *They will believe in him. And the Romans will come.* And because they perceived that his teaching was against the temple and the laws of the fathers, they feared lest, if all should believe [0401A] in Christ, none would remain who would defend the city of God and the temple against the Romans. *And they will come to Rome.* They fear for temporal things, not for eternal, and they lost both. Jerusalem is the place which they lost; they lost their own nation, scattered everywhere.

VERS. 51.-- *This of himself. [n: (AUG.)]* Note that even the wicked foretell things to come by the spirit of prophecy, etc., *down to* which is not lawful save to the high priest, as is read of Zacharias.

VERS. 52.-- *For the nation.* Of the Jews, namely, in which there were sheep: this only did Caiphas [say]. But because the evangelist knew that there were other sheep, he added: *But that he should gather together the children of God that were dispersed.* Children by predestination, as also the sheep in Israel; for they were not yet, because [0401B] they had not yet believed.

VERS. 55.-- *And it was nigh.* The figural Pasch being at hand, in which the true Lamb is now to be immolated, there was counsel among the Jews concerning the killing of him, and he himself draws near to the place of the passion.
