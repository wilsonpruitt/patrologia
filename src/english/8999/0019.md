## CHAPTER XIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *That he should pass.* ⟦s2⟧ The evangelist interprets for us the name of the pasch, that is, a passing over, and its signification: because as they passed out of Egypt, so Christ out of [0404D] the world. ⟦s3⟧ So we also, marked with the blood of the Lamb, from vices to virtues, from the world to heaven. ⟦s4⟧ *He loved them unto the end.* ⟦s5⟧ Unto the end, that is, unto Christ who is the consummating end, that is, that by love of him they may pass whither he himself passes. ⟦s6⟧ Or unto the end, that is, unto death: not that his love is ended by death, but that he loved so far that love led him even unto death, wherein he gave an example that all should remain in the truth of God unto the end.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And when supper was done.* ⟦s2⟧ The supper signifies the passion. ⟦s3⟧ For as after supper there is no dinner, so also Christ, after he has once suffered, does not suffer again. ⟦s4⟧ He rises from supper; after the passion he lays aside his garments; according to his former infirmity he girded himself with a linen cloth, [0405A] his flesh being now immortal; he puts water into a basin, that is, the grace of baptism; he washes the feet and wipes them from sins in baptism; *he took his garments,* that is, the Jews at the end of the world, whom he had first laid aside as blinded.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *He riseth from supper.* ⟦s2⟧ From that heavenly refreshment with which he was refreshed among the angels he lays aside his garments, the glory of eternal blessedness. ⟦s3⟧ *And layeth aside his garments.* ⟦s4⟧ He passed over his loftiness, being about to place his lowliness beneath it, that lowliness might be perfectly commended to man, since one so lofty humbled himself so far that he washed even the feet of the traitor.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *After that he putteth.* ⟦s2⟧ He poured his blood upon the earth, that he might cleanse the steps of believers, which were soiled with earthly sins.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0405B]VERS. 8.-- *Peter saith to him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because of the loftiness of the promised mystery he does not yet permit it to be done; he cannot yet endure that God be humbled at his feet, being struck through with dread. ⟦s3⟧ *He answered.* ⟦s4⟧ He who is not washed through baptism and the confession of penance has no part with Jesus.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Simon saith to him.* ⟦s2⟧ Peter's error here profited us. ⟦s3⟧ For he erred that we might not err. ⟦s4⟧ He erred when he offered his whole self to God to be washed, whom God corrected, showing that one once baptized is not to be baptized again.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *He that is washed.* ⟦s2⟧ From these words we understand that the apostles were baptized, although we do not read where or when. ⟦s3⟧ *He that is washed.* ⟦s4⟧ He that is washed by baptism is clean. ⟦s5⟧ But because he is again soiled by the dust of his earthly dwelling, [0405C] there is again need to wash the feet, which the Lord washes by interceding for us, and we ourselves by praying. ⟦s6⟧ He also who in leisure studies the truth, while he goes to open to the bridegroom knocking in others, soils his feet, because the truth is heard humbly, but cannot be preached without danger; but these feet the Lord washes for his disciples.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And when he had sat down, he said to them again: Know you what I have done to you? ⟦s2⟧ You call me,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ As before by example, so now also by word the master of truth instructs them. ⟦s4⟧ And that he says great things of himself is not arrogance, but truth which profits the hearers, as Paul also does.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG.)] Incautious are the humble who ensnare themselves in a lie while they shun arrogance, because they set themselves against the truth which they forsake. ⟦s2⟧ For he who under compelling [0405D] necessity speaks true and good things of himself is joined so much the more to humility as he is allied to truth.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *I speak not of you all.* ⟦s2⟧ You shall not all do that whereby you may be blessed; but he that eateth my bread, not that he may profit but that he may lie hid, *shall lift up his heel against me,* that is, shall tread upon me, that the Scripture may be fulfilled which foretold this, not that it compels, but because it did not keep silence concerning what was to be.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *From henceforth I tell you.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Hitherto I have borne with him and kept silence, but now I mark the traitor before that is done which is soon to be done, so that when it has been done you may believe that I am he of whom that Scripture foretold.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Amen, amen I say to you.* ⟦s2⟧ After [0406A] he had by his own example taught them humiliation and the suffering of the heel, he subjoins an honour wherewith he may console them, that he himself and the Father are received in them.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *When Jesus had said these things* Our infirmity was truly troubled in him, not his own power, as it also truly suffered. ⟦s2⟧ But he willingly took the trouble upon himself, lest the servant troubled by necessity should despair. ⟦s3⟧ And that he is troubled signifies that spiritual men are to be troubled out of charity, when an urgent cause is compelled to separate even the cockle from the wheat before the harvest.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *And when he had dipped the bread.* ⟦s2⟧ The dipping of the bread signifies the feigning of Judas, who came to the supper a feigned friend. ⟦s3⟧ For the things that are dipped are not cleansed but stained. ⟦s4⟧ But if the dipping signifies something good, damnation not undeservedly [0406B] followed one ungrateful to that same good. ⟦s5⟧ *He gave it to Judas, son of Simon the Iscariot,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ Not, as some think, did Judas now receive the body of Christ. ⟦s7⟧ For it must be known that the Lord had already distributed to all the sacrament of his body and blood, among whom Judas also was; then by the dipped morsel the traitor himself is pointed out, etc.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *That which thou dost.* ⟦s2⟧ He did not command, but he foretells evil to Judas, good to us, because he wills this to be done quickly, not so much hastening to that man's punishment as to the salvation of the faithful.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Because he had the purse.* ⟦s2⟧ In which the offerings were examined for the needs of their own and of others in want. ⟦s3⟧ Wherein a form is given to the Church of keeping what is necessary, which is bidden not to think of the morrow, lest it serve for earthly things, or through fear of want forsake [0406C] justice.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *And it was.* ⟦s2⟧ The night befits the sacrament. ⟦s3⟧ For he who went out was a son of darkness, doing the works of darkness.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Now is the Son of man glorified. [n: (AUG.)]* Judas going out, for whom it was said: *You are not all clean,* etc., *as far as* as though that were already done which is soon to be.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Little children. [n: (ID.)]* Because he had said that God would quickly glorify him, etc., *as far as* this cannot be taken so, because what follows would not agree.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Whither I go you cannot come.* ⟦s2⟧ The apostles, not yet confirmed in spirit, could not now follow the Lord, that is, die for the Lord and for justice, or come to the immortal life to which he himself was going; but they were to follow afterwards. ⟦s3⟧ And how they might [0406D] be fit and go where he goes before, he teaches.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A new commandment I give. [n: (AUG.)]* Not that this is not in the old law, *Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself,* etc., *as far as* which love makes a man die to the world.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *That you love one another, as I have loved you.* ⟦s2⟧ Let not that love of God be thought passed over which is in the love of the neighbour; for in the neighbour nothing is loved but God, as Christ also loves God in us; and not that we have this, but that we might have it, as a physician in a sick man loves not the disease but the health which he comes to give.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *Lord, whither,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Having heard "whither I go," and having understood the way by which he can follow, that is, love, [0407A] Peter says that he is ready to follow. ⟦s3⟧ And because the Lord knew this mind of his, he said that he could not now, wherein he did not take away the hope of the future, but confirmed it. ⟦s4⟧ *But thou shalt follow hereafter.*

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *My life.* ⟦s2⟧ He presumes much who promises to lay down his life for him who had not yet given his life for Peter, as though he could go before.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Knowing his own will, but not his own strength, as a weak man he boasts of his will, but the physician looked upon his infirmity. ⟦s2⟧ The one promised, the other foreknew; and he who knew not was hearing, he who foreknew was teaching.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *The cock shall not crow,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Peter begins the threefold denial before the first cockcrow, but completes the whole of it before the second cockcrow.
