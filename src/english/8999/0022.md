## CHAPTER XVI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS 1.-- *These things have I spoken,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ After he had taught by his own example to bear hatreds, wherein they know what they ought to do but cannot, after he had also promised the Spirit by whom they will be able to resist, he adds also: *These things have I spoken,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Which is an example concerning the Spirit who [0412D] will confirm them, so that, charity being thus poured forth in your hearts, there may be to you much peace and no stumbling-block.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *But the hour cometh.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: If, driven out by them, you gain so many for me (whereat you ought to rejoice), so that it is not enough for them to drive you out. ⟦s3⟧ But let them not suffer [you] to live, lest you gain all, so that you turn [them] away from Judaism, in which they believe the God of Israel and his holy things to be forsaken.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *But these things.* ⟦s2⟧ After he had spoken of their sufferings to come and of the Spirit the comforter to be given them, he subjoins: *These things I said not,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Which, if we take it of the sufferings, he had foretold them not only after the supper but also before the next Passover, and from the beginning of the sending of the twelve apostles; but concerning the consolation of the Spirit to come to them he had not foretold, because he himself [0413A] with his bodily presence was their advocate or comforter; but because he is now about to go, it was needful that he should say that he would come who should confirm them against those who cast them out and killed them.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *I go to him.* ⟦s2⟧ So openly that no one asks what he discerns by bodily sight; they saw him ascending in a cloud, certain of his glory, they who before, when the passion had been foretold, had asked: *Lord, whither goest thou?*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *For if,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not that, placed on earth, he could not give him, but because they are not fit to receive him, unless they cease to know him according to the flesh.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But if.* ⟦s2⟧ To your minds thus caught up to heavenly things the Spirit will be present, and together with him the Father and the Son. ⟦s3⟧ For, made spiritual out of carnal, they were to possess the Trinity more capaciously, as had been promised.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0413B]VERS. 8.-- *He shall convince.* ⟦s2⟧ Not that the Son does not convince, but because the Spirit will give them charity and will drive out fear, so that there may be to them a liberty of convincing.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] He is convicted of sin who does not believe in Christ. ⟦s2⟧ He is convicted, etc., *as far as* lest they be judged along with the prince of the world whom, judged, they imitate.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And of justice.* ⟦s2⟧ Not of its own, but of the justice of believers is the world convicted, by comparison with whom it is condemned. ⟦s3⟧ Whence he does not say: The world shall not see me, but *You shall not see me,* you apostles. ⟦s4⟧ Against the unfaithful who say, How shall we believe what we do not see? ⟦s5⟧ It is true justice to believe what is not seen, so that they who saw the man believed him God. ⟦s6⟧ And when through the ascension the form of the man was withdrawn from their sight, then in every part was fulfilled: *The just liveth by faith.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0413C]VERS. 10.-- *And now,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, never shall you see me such as now, that is, mortal, to be judged, but glorified, a judge. ⟦s3⟧ And this is the justice of faith, concerning which the Apostle: *And if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him no longer [n: (II Cor. V)]*, which, because the world will not receive it from these men, it is convicted.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Of judgment.* ⟦s2⟧ Because if they follow him who is condemned they are condemned with him; and lest they should believe themselves to be spared, they are terrified by the punishment of that proud one.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *All truth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He promises what the Spirit is to do wholly, not that in this life the whole be accomplished.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of himself,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Because he is not of himself, because the Father alone, etc., *as far as* he always knew, knows [0413D] and will know.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *He shall glorify me.* ⟦s2⟧ Because by making you spiritual he will declare how the Son is equal to the Father, whom according to the flesh, as a man, they had known; or, by taking away fear, he will make you announce my glory to the whole world, which glory profits not me, but men.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of mine.* ⟦s2⟧ Not that the Spirit is less than the Son. ⟦s3⟧ But why he said this, he explains: *All things whatsoever the Father hath are mine.* ⟦s4⟧ Therefore I said that he shall receive of mine. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore the Spirit receives from the Father, whence the Son receives, because in this Trinity the Son is born of the Father, the Spirit proceeds from the Father. ⟦s6⟧ But he who is born of none and proceeds from none is the Father alone.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0414A]VERS. 15.-- *All things,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Christ speaks concerning the Spirit. ⟦s2⟧ *The Spirit of the Father,* etc., *as far as* so openly is it shown that the Spirit is of the Father and of the Son.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *A little while.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, a small time remains until this, that I suffer and be shut in the sepulchre, and again a small time until you see me raised up. ⟦s3⟧ Or it is a small time, that is, the three days in which, shut in, I shall not see. ⟦s4⟧ And again a small [time], that is, forty days, in which, raised up, I shall be seen. ⟦s5⟧ *Because I go.* ⟦s6⟧ This is because it is time that, mortality laid aside, I should bring human nature into the heavens.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Because you shall lament.* ⟦s2⟧ When I have been slain, over which the enemies will rejoice. ⟦s3⟧ In like manner all good men lament amid the hardships of this world; but the lovers of the world rejoice. ⟦s4⟧ But the good are compared to a woman in travail who has brought forth in [0414B] childbirth; but she is glad over the fruit, and rejoices more over the coming of the offspring to be than she is sad over the present pain.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *If you shall ask anything. [n: (AUG.)]* Thus indeed is that to be understood which he says, he will give you: that those benefits are to be known as signified which properly pertain to those who ask. ⟦s2⟧ For the saints are heard for themselves, but not for all others, whether friends or enemies.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He will give you.* ⟦s2⟧ In the world to come you shall not ask, but now ask, and there shall be given you what pertains to you, even if not to all those for whom you ask; and therefore it is said, to you.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *These things in proverbs.* ⟦s2⟧ The things I have said thus far I have said as it were in proverbs (which men are wont to hear but not to understand), because you are natural men. ⟦s3⟧ *But the natural [0414C] man who perceiveth not the things that are of the Spirit of God [n: (I Joan. I)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 2:14 reads *Animalis autem homo non percipit ea quae sunt Spiritus Dei*, "the natural man perceiveth not the things that are of the Spirit of God"; Migne prints a relative where the received text has the subject noun and the finite verb]. ⟦s4⟧ Whatsoever things are said of an incorporeal and unchangeable substance, he holds as it were for proverbs. ⟦s5⟧ *But the hour cometh.* ⟦s6⟧ In which, the Spirit being given, I will make you spiritual, so that with the most certain understanding of the mind (though not as in the world to come) you may see God; and then I will announce openly concerning the Father, so that you may understand him and me to be of the same substance; and then, *Ask in my name.* ⟦s7⟧ Because then you will truly know and understand that if as man I ask the Father, as God I hear together with the Father; whence he says: *And I say not to you, that I will ask the Father.*

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *For he himself.* ⟦s2⟧ The Father loves us because we love the Son, since from the Father and the Son we have received that we should love the Father and the Son: charity having been given through the Spirit, [0414D] whom we also ourselves love together with the Father and the Son, he who loves first works in us that he be loved.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Again I leave the world.* ⟦s2⟧ He left the world by bodily presence. ⟦s3⟧ He goes to the Father, because he led manhood into the invisible things of the paternal majesty. ⟦s4⟧ He left the world, because he took away from the sight of the lovers of the world what they had seen. ⟦s5⟧ He returns to the fatherland, because he taught his lovers that he is to be believed equal to the Father.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *His disciples say to him.* ⟦s2⟧ Since the hour which he promised had not yet come: *Behold, now he speaketh plainly* [var: Vulg. Jo 16:29 has *loqueris*, "now thou speakest plainly"; the disciples' address in the second person]. ⟦s3⟧ Those things which the Lord knows to be proverbs to men who do not understand: they to such a degree do not understand [cj: *nobis*, "for us"; read *non* -- Augustine, *Tract. in Ioh.* 103, *illi usque adeo non intelligunt*], that they do not even understand that they do not understand. ⟦s4⟧ Admonishing them concerning which [0415A] infirmity of theirs, he answered: *Now you believe,* etc.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *Now we know that thou knowest all things.* ⟦s2⟧ They show openly that, speaking of them, he was chiefly treating the things which it delighted them to hear; and the things which they themselves wished to ask, these he himself, anticipating, brought forward of his own accord; and therefore, because he knew their thoughts, they confess that he knows all things, as God and the Son of God. ⟦s3⟧ *And thou needest not,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Since thou knowest all things, there is no need that thou shouldst question anyone from whom thou mightest know anything. ⟦s5⟧ Or even that thou shouldst be questioned by anyone, foreknowing what that man wishes. ⟦s6⟧ But if he ever questioned or was questioned, there was need not for them, but for others.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *Now you believe.* ⟦s2⟧ As little ones, to whom whatsoever lofty things are still proverbs, you believe; but there comes [0415B] the hour of the passion, and now it is near, in which you will be so troubled that what you now believe you will forsake, not only in body, but even in the faith of the mind; whence: *We hoped that he would have redeemed Israel.*

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6⟧ VERS. 32.-- *And I am not,* etc. Behold, they have made progress, but he wishes them to be stretched out to something greater, so that when they shall have said: *Thou camest forth from God,* he may say: *The Father is with me,* lest they should so think him to have gone forth from God as to think that he had also withdrawn. *Because the Father is with me.* He had said, *I leave the world and I go to the Father,* now he says: *The Father is with me,* which to one who understands is a word, to one who does not understand a proverb. To whom, if it is not food, it is nevertheless the nourishment of milk. From which it comes that they knew him to know all things.
