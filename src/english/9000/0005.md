## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- *And he was led. [n: (AMBR.)]* He was led into the desert with this purpose, that he might provoke the devil; for unless that one had contended, this one would not have conquered for me; with this mystery, [0253D] that he might free Adam from exile; with this example, that he might show us that the devil envies those who reach out toward better things.

*Into the desert.* Historically: into that desert which is between Jerusalem and Jericho, where he had figuratively said that Adam had been overcome by the devil.

VERS. 2.-- *He was hungry. [n: (BEDA.)]* It is not written of Moses or of Elias, etc., *as far as* but he hungers not so much for the food of the body as for the salvation of the soul.

VERS. 3.-- *If thou be the Son of God. [n: (AMBR.)]* He had known that the Son of God would come, but he did not think that he had come through the weakness of the body, etc., *as far as* he who is not fed with this word does not live.

VERS. 4.-- *It is written.* He teaches that one must fight rather with doctrine than with miracles, with humility rather than with power, [0254A] and that nothing is to be done at the devil's pleasure, nor out of any regard for displaying his power.

VERS. 5.-- *And he brought him.* Luke follows the thing as it was done, setting avarice in the middle and pride last, according to the manners of the present time, because pride rages after all the virtues. Matthew does not follow the order of the history, but that of the temptations of Adam.

VERS. 6.-- *To thee will I give.* He says these things out of arrogance, not because the whole world is his, for there are some good men. *It is delivered to me.* Power is not from the devil, but it is nevertheless exposed to the snares of the devil. Nor is the ordering of the powers evil on that account, because the powers are exposed to evil.

VERS. 7.-- *Thou therefore. [n: (BEDA.)]* The devil, saying to the Saviour: *If falling down thou wilt adore me,* hears in return that he himself ought rather to adore him, his Lord and [0254B] his God.

VERS. 8.-- *If thou be the Son of God.* In all the temptations he does this, that he may learn whether he be the Son of God. But Christ so tempers his answer as to leave it in doubt: *Cast thyself down.* Him whom neither gluttony nor avarice overcame, vainglory tempts, in case perhaps the very boasting of his own victory might avail to cast him down. *Cast.* This word befits him who busies himself to hurl all men headlong. In which he is shown to be weak, since he can harm no one unless that one has first cast himself down.

VERS. 9.-- *It is written.* Because he had put forward an example from the Scriptures, he is overcome by examples from the Scriptures. But the devil uses the testimonies of the Scriptures through the heretics, not to teach, but to deceive.

VERS. 10.-- *To the angels. [n: (BEDA.)]* This prophecy is not [0254C] of Christ, but of a holy man. And even if it were of Christ, he ought also to have remembered what follows: *Thou shalt walk upon the asp and the basilisk, and thou shalt trample under foot the lion and the dragon [n: (Psal. XC)]*. But of the help of the angels he speaks as though to one who is weak; of his own treading down he keeps silence, like a shuffler.

VERS. 13.-- *And being ended. [n: (ID.)]* The three temptations being overcome, he says that every temptation was ended, etc., *as far as* yet he dreads to press on, lest he be more often triumphed over.

VERS. 14.-- *And the fame went out.* He is honoured not only by those present because of the miracles they had seen, but among the absent his fame was being spread abroad.

VERS. 15.-- *He taught.* After the miracles, behold wisdom, by which he teaches, and by which teaching, through the preceding miracles, [0254D] he magnifies.

VERS. 16.-- *And he rose up to read. [n: (BEDA.)]* He who had come not to be ministered unto but to minister, etc., *as far as* or he might correct them by the testimony of the reading of the prophecy.

VERS. 18.-- *The Spirit. [n: (ID.)]* In the earlier parts of the prophecy the Saviour, speaking of the calling of the Gentiles and the confirmation of the Church, etc., *as far as* because the Spirit of the Lord is upon him.

*To preach the gospel to the poor,* etc. He understands the Gentiles, who were truly poor. For they had neither the knowledge of God, nor the law, nor the prophets, nor anything else among their goods, and they were destitute of all the other spiritual riches.

VERS. 20.-- *And when he had folded. [n: (BEDA.)]* He read in the hearing [0255A] of those who were present, etc., *as far as* but according to the capacity of the hearers he commits the word to the teacher to be dispensed.

VERS. 23.-- *Physician.* As [they call him] the son of a carpenter, so by the same madness of error; they call him a physician, but in their error the truth lies hidden. For he was truly the son of a carpenter: of one who through him made all things in the beginning, who works in the Holy Ghost and in fire, who in the great house of this world makes vessels of diverse kinds. He is a physician also, because through him all things that are in the heavens and that are on earth have been restored. Who says of himself: *They that are in health need not a physician, but they that are ill [n: (Matth. IX)]*.

VERS. 24.-- *Amen I say to you. [n: (BEDA.)]* Not only the Lord and head of the prophets, etc., *as far as* he turned away the miracles of his power.

VERS. 25.-- *In the days of Elias. [n: (ID.)]* As if he should say: It is not [0255B] contrary to the example of the prophets, etc., *as far as* because there was no abundance for the knowing of the Godhead.

VERS. 26.-- *A widow. [n: (ID.)]* The widow to whom Elias is sent figures the Church of the Gentiles, etc., *as far as* in which the bread of eternal life is prepared for us.

VERS. 27.-- *Naaman the Syrian. [n: (ID.)]* Naaman, who is interpreted comeliness, signifies the people of the nations, etc., *as far as* because it behoves the baptized to be confirmed by partaking of the Lord's body.

VERS. 29.-- *And they led him. [n: (ID.)]* The Jews, worse disciples, with the devil for their master, etc., *as far as* they are preserved as though shaped by the hand of the divine Sculptor.

VERS. 31.-- *And he went down,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Neither moved by indignation, nor offended by their crime, etc., *as far as* nor is the law dissolved, if there be a renewal of a man now falling.

[0255C]VERS. 33.-- *And in the synagogue. [n: (ID.)]* In the synagogue there is a man having an unclean spirit, because he had lost the Holy Spirit. For the devil had entered in, whence Christ had spiritually gone out.

VERS. 34.-- *I know thee.* By his works he could be proved the Son of God. But did he not say to the Apostles: *Greater things shall you do?* The solution: by works, that is, by the manner of the working. Others were saying: *In the name of Jesus,* etc. He himself was saying: *I have said to thee,* etc.

VERS. 35.-- *And when he had thrown him. [n: (BEDA.)]* He who was to be cured is allowed to be thrown into the midst by the demon, so that the power laid open may invite more to salvation.

VERS. 36.-- *And with power.* Not as men, who cast out demons in the word of God, but by his own authority [0255D] he works mighty deeds.

VERS. 38.-- *Into Simon's house.* The house of Peter is the circumcision committed to his apostolate. The mother-in-law is the Synagogue, which is in a manner the mother of the Church committed to Peter. She is sick of a fever, because she laboured under the heats of envy, persecuting the Church. But Jesus, standing over her, heals her, because where the fear of God begins to be, such burning is wont to depart.

VERS. 40.-- *And when the sun was down. [n: (BEDA.)]* The setting of the sun is the passion of Christ, through which more possessed by demons are healed than he had healed sick people while living before in the flesh; because living in the flesh he taught few of the Jews, but after the resurrection he appeared to the Gentiles.

VERS. 41.-- *Thou art the Son of God. [n: (ID.)]* When the devil saw him wearied [0256A] with fasting, etc., *as far as* because, *if they had known the Lord of glory, they would never have crucified him [n: (Matth. I)]*.

VERS. 42.-- *And when it was day.* Mystically: in the manifest light of the resurrection he is sought by the companies of believers; and being found in the desert of the Gentiles, he is held back, that he may not go away.
