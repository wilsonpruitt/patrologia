## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass.* ⟦s2⟧ Neither by time nor by place is the crowd restrained from its zeal for being healed; evening presses on, and yet it follows; the lake meets them, and the crowd presses, and therefore he went up into Peter's ship.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He stood beside. [n: (BEDA.)]* The lake signifies the present world. ⟦s2⟧ But the Lord is now no longer in the lake, as being now no longer in passible flesh, but beside the lake, because in that flesh in which he suffered he came to the stability of perpetual [0256B] rest.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *He saw two ships. [n: (ID.)]* The two ships, mystically, signify the circumcision and the uncircumcision, which he saw, because in each people *he knoweth who are his,* whose hearts comes forth from the waves of the world to the tranquillity of the life to come, as it were to the shore, by living mercifully.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *He asked him. [n: (ID.)]* That he first asks the ship to be drawn back a little from the land, etc., *as far as* it pertains to the more remote Gentiles to whom it was afterwards preached.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Launch out into the deep. [n: (AMBR.)]* Although it is commanded to others that they let down their nets, etc., *as far as* or the Church is instructed and armed from the flowing gifts of the Holy Ghost.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *At thy word,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Unless by the word of grace [0256C] the instruments of disputations have been let down, etc., *as far as* even amid the scandals of persecutors.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And they filled. [n: (ID.)]* This filling grows even unto the end of the world, etc., *as far as* and such humility deserves the merciful consolation of the Lord.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *From henceforth. [n: (ID.)]* This pertains specially to Peter himself, to whom is expounded what the catch of fishes signifies. ⟦s2⟧ But as then he took fishes by nets, so at some time he shall take men by words, wherein Peter is a type of the whole Church.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *In one of the cities.* ⟦s2⟧ Here two comings into Capharnaum are noted. ⟦s3⟧ The first, when John had been imprisoned, when he wrought those three miracles in succession; the second, when he came down from the mountain, the apostles having been chosen, when this miracle of the leper is wrought.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0256D]VERS. 13.-- *He touched.* ⟦s2⟧ Because of humility, that he might teach us to despise no one for any blemish of the body. ⟦s3⟧ He touches, not disdaining; he commands, not distrusting; he adds the testimony of the work. ⟦s4⟧ *I will.* ⟦s5⟧ He says it because of Photinus, he commands because of Arius, he touches because of Manichaeus.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *He charged him. [n: (BEDA.)]* He bids it be kept silent, and it cannot be kept silent. ⟦s2⟧ Not that he willed something and could not, but he gives an example that his own should wish to lie hidden in the great things they do, yet that they may profit others, be published against their will.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And the crowds came together,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The healing of this man drives many crowds to the Lord. ⟦s2⟧ For that he might show himself healed within and without: he does not keep silent the benefit received, even when bidden. ⟦s3⟧ But [0257A] Mark also says, discharging the evangelist's office: *Straightway going forth, he began to publish and to spread abroad the word [n: (Marc. I)]*. [var: Vulg. Mc 1:45 reads *At ille egressus coepit praedicare, et diffamare sermonem*, "But he going out began to publish and to spread abroad the word."]

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And he was praying.* ⟦s2⟧ When he prays, he forms the contemplative life; when he heals, the active.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] He heals as God, he prays as man. ⟦s2⟧ In the city, etc., *as far as* let him speak silently through inward desires.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *And it came to pass.* etc., [n: (ID.)] Where he sat and taught Luke passed over for the sake of brevity, etc., *as far as* which he had made his own not by being born, but by making it illustrious with his powers.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And there were. [n: (AMBR.)]* Among the remedies of the other weak ones, as they came together, etc., *as far as* and let the halting steps of our actions be reformed by the remedy of the heavenly word.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Men carrying.* ⟦s2⟧ Allegorically: that is, raising to higher things the mind of men, though sluggish through the weakness of [0257B] the outward man; by whose supports in turn one is taught both to lift himself up and to humble himself more easily before Jesus, worthy to be seen by the Lord's regard. ⟦s3⟧ For God looks upon humility.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they sought,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] They desire to offer, but with the crowd interposed they are shut out, because often the soul, etc., *as far as* because it is by the devotion of faith that one descends to Christ's humility, and comes to the knowledge of Christ.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And through the tiles. [n: (ID.)]* The house of Jesus is described as roofed with tiles, because under the contemptible veil of the letter, if there be present a teacher to unlock divine things, the power of spiritual grace is found. ⟦s1⟧ And that he is let down with his bed signifies that Christ must be known by a man still set in this flesh.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0257C]VERS. 20.-- *Whose faith. [n: (BEDA.)]* Each man's own faith avails much, since through another's faith a man has been saved within and without.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] This healing of the paralytic signifies the salvation of a soul sighing after God, etc., *as far as* which are prudence, fortitude, temperance, justice.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Man, they are forgiven,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Thou art a man, a sinner; I am God, who forgive sins, who by the merit of others release thy sins for thee. ⟦s3⟧ Let him therefore who is burdened with sins call in the Church to pray for him.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Because for the fault of the soul the body also was made infirm, therefore, about to heal the body, he first heals the soul.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Who can forgive?* ⟦s2⟧ No one forgives sins but God, who forgives also through those to whom he has given the power of forgiving. ⟦s3⟧ Since therefore Christ [0257D] forgives, he is truly proved God. ⟦s4⟧ They render true testimony to God, but by denying the person of Christ they are deceived. ⟦s5⟧ For faithlessness can confess, it cannot believe. ⟦s6⟧ And so testimony is not wanting to the Godhead, faith is wanting to salvation. ⟦s7+8⟧ But God, willing to save sinners, both shows himself God by the knowledge of hidden things, and, answering them by the wonder of his deeds: *Why do you think evil things in your hearts?* [var: Vulg. Lc 5:22 reads *Quid cogitatis in cordibus vestris?*, "What is it you think in your hearts?"; *mala*, "evil things," stands at Mt 9:4.] By the same majesty and power by which I behold your thoughts, I can forgive men their offences. ⟦s9⟧ From yourselves understand what the paralytic obtains. ⟦s10⟧ But because you do not know spiritual grace, nor believe what has been done within, let it be proved by a visible sign that it is of no less power, that in the Son of man [0258A] you may know the hidden power of majesty whereby he can forgive sins as God.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *But when he knew,* etc. [n: (AMBR.)] The great priest saw leprosy in the hearts of the Jews, and showed them worse than that leper whom, when cleansed, he bade offer himself to the priest. ⟦s2⟧ But these the high priest rejects, lest their leprosy defile others also.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *I say to thee, arise. [n: (BEDA.)]* To arise is to draw the soul away from carnal desires. ⟦s2⟧ To take up the bed, etc., *as far as* by the release of the penalty understand the remission of the fault.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *And astonishment. [n: (AMBR.)]* The unbelieving look on him as he rises, they marvel at him as he goes away, and they choose rather to fear the miracles of the divine work than to believe; for if they believed, they would not fear, but would love. ⟦s2⟧ *For perfect [0258B] love casteth out fear [n: (I Joan., IV)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Jo 4:18 reads *perfecta caritas foras mittit timorem*; Migne prints *dilectio*, "love", for *caritas*, and adds *enim*.]

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Levi. [n: (BEDA.)]* The same Levi who is also Matthew. ⟦s2⟧ But Luke, etc., *as far as* he was changed from a publican into an evangelist.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA, AMBR.)] He left his own, who was seizing what was another's. ⟦s2⟧ Nor did he only leave the gains of the tolls, but he also despised the danger that could come from the princes of the world, because he had left the accounts of the tolls unsettled. ⟦s3⟧ And because he reserved to himself absolutely no regard or thought of this life, he deserved to be a faithful steward of the Lord's talents.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] By the election of Matthew is expressed the faith of the Gentiles, who before gaped after worldly things, but now refresh the body of Christ with assiduous devotion. ⟦s2⟧ By the pride of the Pharisees is intimated the envy of the Jews, [0258C] which is tormented at the salvation of the Gentiles.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *Why,* etc. [n: (AMBR.)] Every question from the law, which is tenacious of justice, has not, etc., *as far as* and his walking and all that he did serve for salvation.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AMBR., BEDA.)] He who receives Christ into the inward dwelling is fed with the greatest delights of overflowing pleasures. ⟦s2⟧ And so the Lord enters, and reclines in the affection of him who has believed. ⟦s3⟧ But again the envy of the faithless is kindled, and a likeness of the punishment to come is prefigured. ⟦s4⟧ For while the faithful feast and recline in the kingdom of heaven, faithlessness shall be tormented with fasting. ⟦s5⟧ It is shown also how great a difference there is between the rivals of the law and of grace, because they who follow the law shall suffer an everlasting hunger of mind. ⟦s6⟧ But they who have received the word in their inward parts, refreshed by the abundance of the heavenly nourishment and fountain, [0258D] cannot hunger and thirst; and therefore they who fast in mind murmur, saying: *Why with publicans,* etc.? ⟦s7⟧ Sons of the devil, they pour abroad the venom of the serpent. ⟦s8⟧ The serpent uttered the first word, saying to Eve: What indeed did God say: *Eat ye not of every tree? [n: (Gen. III.)]* [var: Vulg. Gn 3:1 reads *Cur praecepit vobis Deus ut non comederetis de omni ligno paradisi?*, "Why hath God commanded you that you should not eat of every tree of paradise?"] These men imitate him.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *A physician.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ the physician who, by a wondrous manner of healing, *was wounded for our iniquities.* ⟦s3⟧ By this medicine the venom of the serpent is shut out. ⟦s4⟧ He who uses this medicine does not remain fasting.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *To call the just. [n: (BEDA.)]* He calls those just who, *not knowing the justice of God and willing to establish their own, are not subject to the justice of God;* [var: Vulg. Rom 10:3 reads *Ignorantes enim justitiam Dei, et suam quaerentes statuere*, "For not knowing the justice of God, and seeking to establish their own."] and, presuming upon the law, [0259A] do not seek the grace of the Gospel; but sinners, who, attending to their own evils and thinking that they cannot be justified by the law, submit themselves by repentance to the grace of Christ.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Why do the disciples. [n: (BEDA.)]* Matthew says that the disciples themselves asked this. ⟦s2⟧ But it must be known that both asked.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Spiritually: the disciples of John and of the Pharisees fast, because they who the works of the law, etc., *as far as* he eats with sinners, that thou mayest understand the grace and the power.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *For can you. [n: (ID.)]* As long as the bridegroom is with us and we are in gladness, neither can we fast, etc., *as far as* then shall the children of the bridegroom fast, desiring the coming of the Judge.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0259B]VERS. 35.-- *But the days will come. [n: (ID.)]* As if he should say: You will bring it about that mourners fast, because you are, etc., *as far as* as long as the Lord dwelt in the flesh with the disciples.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Then they shall fast,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Concerning both fasts, that which is in tribulation and that which is in joy, he answers: *When the bridegroom shall be taken away,* then shall they be in sorrow and in mourning, until consolation be granted through the Holy Ghost; and when that gift has been received, the fast which is made through gladness they, being now renewed unto the spiritual life, shall celebrate. ⟦s3⟧ And here he treats first of the fast which pertains to the humility of tribulation. ⟦s4⟧ But that which pertains to the joy of the mind (when the mind is suspended toward spiritual things, and is therefore estranged from bodily foods) he signifies by the similitudes that follow, showing that [0259C] to the carnal, and for that reason still drawing the old sense with them, this kind of fasting is not fitting.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *From a new garment. [n: (BEDA.)]* To the old garment are compared the disciples, who are still the old man. ⟦s2⟧ To whom the new piece, etc., *as far as* whence the Apostle: *Put off the old man with his deeds, and put on the new [n: (Ephes. IV)]*. [var: Vulg. Col 3:9 reads *expoliantes vos veterem hominem cum actibus suis*, "stripping yourselves of the old man with his deeds," and Eph 4:24 *et induite novum hominem*, "and put on the new man"; the quotation joins words from the two places.]

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *Wine.* ⟦s2⟧ By wine we are refreshed within, by the garment we are covered without. ⟦s3⟧ The garment therefore is the good works which we do outwardly, by which we shine before men. ⟦s4⟧ The wine is the fervour of faith, of hope and of charity, by which we are reformed within, in newness of sense, in the sight of God.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Into bottles. [n: (BEDA.)]* They are compared also to old bottles, which are more easily burst by new wine, that is, by spiritual precepts, than they contain it. ⟦s2⟧ But they shall be [0259D] new bottles when, after the ascension, having received the Spirit, they shall be renewed by praying with desire of his consolation.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Otherwise: to a soul not yet renewed, but persevering in the oldness of malice, the sacraments of the new mysteries ought not to be committed. ⟦s2⟧ Otherwise: the old bottles are the scribes and Pharisees; the new piece and the new wine, the precepts of the Gospel, which the Jews cannot bear, lest a greater rent be made, with whom the Galatians mingled the precepts of the law, new wine into old bottles. ⟦s3⟧ But they suit the apostles, not those who, depraved by the traditions of the elders, are unable to keep the sincerity of Christ's precepts; whence it is subjoined: *And no man,* because to the Jews, steeped in the spittle of the old life, the precepts of the new grace were loathsome, because, heaped up with the traditions of the elders, [0260A] they were not able to perceive the sweetness of spiritual words.
