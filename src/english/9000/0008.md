## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] He says that the letter of the sabbath had begun to be loosed, etc., *as far as* after the second, from that on which the preceding things were said or done.

[0260B] *Through the corn,* etc. Mystically: the field is the world; the crop of the field, the fruitfulness of the human race; the ears, the fruits of the Church, with which the apostles are filled, feeding themselves on our progress.

*The disciples plucked,* etc. The disciples, not having space to eat because of the importunity of the crowds, hungered as men, but by plucking the ears they solace their want, which is a sign of a more austere life, to seek not prepared dishes but simple foods. And note that the apostles already destroy the letter of the sabbath, against the Ebionites, who receive the other apostles, but reject Paul as a transgressor of the law.

[n: (BEDA.)] Mystically: the disciples, hungering for the salvation of men, pass through the corn, that is, through the regions which, etc., *as far as* convicting the doctors of the law of not knowing [0260C] the law.

VERS. 2.-- *They said to them,* etc. Others say that these things were objected to the Lord himself. But they could have been objected by different persons both to the Lord himself and to the disciples, and to whomsoever it was objected it looks chiefly to him. *On the sabbaths.* The sabbaths signify keepings of feast; either those which are kept in this world by those who give themselves to the superstitions of the Jews; or those which shall be kept in the world to come, when in perpetual solemnity we shall eat the good things of the earth.

VERS. 3.-- *Have you not read this,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] How is that objected to the Lord as a crime, which in a servant is not held for a crime? The true king, etc., *as far as* all the sons of the Church are priests, anointed with the Holy Ghost that they may offer themselves.

VERS. 4.-- *He went into the house of God.* Great is the grace [0260D] of hospitality: with the peril of death set before it, the mind of the priest does not turn the guest away. And so we ought to take upon ourselves the perils of others. But the true priests do not shut out the true David from the hospitality of the mind, not even for the peril of death.

VERS. 6.-- *A man. [n: (BEDA.)]* Mystically this man signifies the human race dried up in the fruitfulness of good work, through the hand stretched out to the tree in the first parent, which the innocent hand stretched out on the cross heals. And well was the hand withered in the Synagogue, because where there is a greater gift of knowledge, there the transgressor lies under a greater fault.

VERS. 7.-- *They watched. [n: (ID.)]* Because he had excused, by the probable example of the master, the destruction of the sabbath which they were charging against the disciples, [0261A] now, watching the master himself, they wish to calumniate him, that if he heal not, they may accuse him of cruelty or of weakness; if he heal, of transgression.

VERS. 9.-- *I ask you. [n: (BEDA.)]* Forestalling the calumny which they were preparing for themselves, he reproves them, etc., *as far as* God is not the author of evil, but those whom he does not save he is said to destroy.

VERS. 10.-- *Stretch forth. [n: (ID.)]* The weakness of an unfruitful soul is cured by no better order than by the bounty of alms; whence it is said: *Let not thy hand be stretched out to receive, and drawn back to give [n: (Eccles., IV)]*. For he stretches out his hands to God in vain, being about to pray for his sins, who does not stretch these out to the poor according to his power.

VERS. 11.-- *Folly.* Because they were moved by envy alone, [0261B] for there was no fault either in a man's stretching out his hand, or in Jesus' healing. It was great folly to plot the death of him whose benefits they very greatly needed.

VERS. 12.-- *He went out into a mountain to pray.* The mountain on which he chooses and teaches the apostles signifies the height of justice, because they were to be instructed and to preach, that they might not remain among the weak, but be raised to things above. So also the law was given on a mountain. He leads the apostles up more eminently, that from the mountain the mountains may first receive the peace to be announced to the people. So also Moses, alone understanding the law spiritually, ascended the summit of the mountain on which God was.

VERS. 13.-- *Disciples.* Of whom he chose twelve, whom he named apostles, that is, sent in his own place; whence: *As the Father hath sent me, I also send you [n: (Joan. XX)]*. Ascending into the mountain he calls to himself, he chooses whom he wills, [0261C] because it is not of their zeal but of divine grace that they were called to the apostleship; whence: *You have not chosen me, but I have chosen you [n: (Ibid., XV)]*. He chose not the wise, not the rich, not the noble, lest he should seem to have chosen them for prudence, or to have redeemed them with riches, or to have drawn any to grace by the authority of power and nobility.

VERS. 14.-- *He surnamed.* Not then for the first time, but long before, when he is said to have been brought by Andrew: *Thou shalt be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter.* But Luke, wishing to name the names of the twelve apostles, since he had need to say Peter, wished briefly to hint that he was not so called before; but that the Lord so surnamed him, that it should not be thought done by chance, but that from the change the sacrament of the rock might be commended to the hearers.

[0261D]VERS. 15.-- *Matthew.* Matthew, for humility's sake, places himself in the order beneath his fellow Thomas, though by the other evangelists he is set before him.

VERS. 17.-- *And coming down,* etc. The Lord, about to choose apostles, went up into the mountains; about to teach the crowds, he returns to the plains, because the crowds are not able to see Christ except in what is lowly, and rarely is it found either that a crowd follows the Lord to higher things, or that any weak man is cured on the mountain; but when the fever of lusts has been quenched and the light of knowledge kindled, each one step by step goes up to the height of the virtues.

*The sea coast.* Not from the neighbouring sea of Galilee, because they do not set this in the place of the miracle, but it is named from the great sea. In which Tyre and Sidon also [0262A] could be comprehended. But because they are cities of the Gentiles, they are deliberately set down by name, that it may be intimated how great is the power of Christ, which stirs even foreign cities.

VERS. 18.-- *That they might hear.* If we say that there are two discourses, he went up into the mountain, and had his disciples there; and afterwards he came down and made this one to the crowd. If we say there was one, it must be understood thus, that he went up into the mountain, and after a little came down, and there made the discourse, where there was a level place.

VERS. 19.-- *To touch him.* He touches Christ who faithfully believes in him. He is touched by Christ who is made firm in him. He who so touches and is touched is healed by the power of his spirit.

VERS. 20.-- *And he, lifting up,* etc. Although he speaks generally to all, he lifts his eyes specially upon the disciples, [0262B] that they who receive the word of God with the intent ear of the heart may more widely receive the light of the inward savour; to whom, sitting on the mountain, he opens his mouth, that he may utter great things upon them; standing on the plain he directs his eyes, that they may patiently understand what they hear. *Blessed are the poor,* etc. They who cannot yet climb the citadel of consummated virtue are meanwhile to be cherished with the beatitude of a general perfection, that little by little advancing to better things, while they gladly listen to the Lord standing on the plain, they may at some time loftily ascend to him sitting on the mountain. For those upon whose hearts he still presses to tame them, these he addresses as it were standing. Those whom he finds exercised by long endeavour and now teachable, to these he imparts mystical things as one resting at ease.

[n: (BEDA.)] What in Matthew is fully said by eight [0262C] beatitudes in the sermon of perfection to the apostles on the top of the mountain, this in Luke, etc., *as far as* but because he excelled in faith, by faith he deserved the primacy before the rest.

VERS. 21.-- *You that now hunger.* He who hungers has compassion on him that hungers: by compassion he gives freely, by giving freely he becomes just; he makes himself equal to his inferiors, he shuts out guile, he seeks the truth. *You that now weep.* He who for the riches of Christ's inheritance, for the bread of eternal life, for the hope of heavenly joys, desires weeping, hunger and poverty, is blessed; much more blessed he who does not fear to keep these virtues amid adversities.

VERS. 23.-- *Rejoice and be glad.* Amid the hatreds of hearts, amid the reproaches of tongues, amid the hands of persecutors, [0262D] be conversant with a gladder heart, in view of the reward on high. *For according to these things.* By the example of the prophets he strengthens them, because those who speak the truth are wont to suffer persecution.

VERS. 24.-- *But woe,* etc. Matthew on the mountain describes only the beatitudes of the upright; but Luke on the plain describes also the woe of the wicked, because hearers still untrained are to be driven to good things by threats and terrors, while for the perfect it is enough to be invited by rewards. *But woe.* Since above the kingdom of heaven is said to be the poor's, it appears by the contrary that he estranges himself from this kingdom who seeks his consolation in temporal things. Nor are riches so much in fault as the love of riches.

[0263A]Mystically: the rich man is the Jewish people, or the heretic, or the philosopher of the world, who, delighted with the abundance of words and the patronage of eloquence, having gone beyond the simplicity of the true faith, have laid up useless treasures, disputing according to the usage of the world about the generation of Christ; who in the world to come shall recognize the neediness of their faith, and, wasted with eternal fasting, shall know that the food of faithlessness which they belch forth in the present is the cause of so great a punishment. And there shall be a time when they shall bewail their laughter. To whom it is well said: *Woe, when men shall bless you;* because while here they please the powerful, they subject themselves to a perpetual curse, because as the poor, the hungry, the weeping are proved by the wickedness of the evil, so those given to riches, to feasts and to laughter, by the evil retinue of those who fawn upon them, are cherished unto a greater punishment.

[0263B]VERS. 25.-- *You that are filled,* etc. You who among the rich abound the more, like the rich man who was clothed in purple and fine linen and with feasts, who seeks a drop of water from Lazarus' finger.

*Woe to you that now laugh,* etc. Solomon: *The heart of the wise man is where there is sadness, the heart of fools where there is gladness [n: (Eccl. VII)]*; teaching that folly is to be ascribed to those who laugh, prudence to those who weep.

VERS. 26.-- *Shall bless you,* etc. A great part of the punishment of sinners is that their crimes are not only not reproved, but are moreover praised as though well done.

VERS. 27.-- *Love your enemies.* When it has been said what they can suffer from enemies, he adds how they ought to bear themselves toward those enemies. And here the Church is not fed with milk, but strengthened with the stronger food of charity. It is not [0263C] enough not to hate, but what is beyond human nature is commanded, to love. And whereas the law commands a return of vengeance, the Gospel bestows charity for enmities, kindness for hatreds, prayers for curses, patience for persecutors, and even the grace of recompense for those who hunger.

VERS. 28.-- *Pray for them that calumniate you.* That the prophets seem to imprecate, these are not the wishes of men desiring it, but foretellings of things to come: for they themselves prayed for their enemies.

VERS. 29.-- *And to him that striketh thee.* Since charity is patient, it ought to sustain the patient bearing of him that beats; and since it is kind, it ought not to answer curses. If it *seeketh not her own,* it ought not to resist him that plunders; if it *envieth not [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*, it ought not [0263D] to hate an enemy. *Offer them,* etc. The physician of souls commands those whom he instructs to heal their neighbours to bear all things that can avail for the salvation of their neighbours. And it pertains to mercy that, as from sick and frenzied sons, they should suffer many things, if their health require it, until the infirmity pass. What is so wonderful as to offer the cheek to him that strikes? Nay, every assault of the angry man is broken, wrath is calmed; and through patience he is invited to repentance. *A garment.* What is said of the garment and the coat is to be done also in other things which by any right we call temporally our own. For if this is commanded concerning necessary things, much more is it enjoined to despise superfluous things.

[0264A]VERS. 30.-- *Ask not again.* Because *charity is patient, is kind [n: (I Cor. III)]*; it not only bears bravely the injuries of an enemy, but also kindly anticipates the favour of a friend, that they may be drawn by other kindnesses.
