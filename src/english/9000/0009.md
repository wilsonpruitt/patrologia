## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)

VERS. 31.-- *And as you would.* Lest he should seem to dissolve the law, he keeps in benefits the return which he neglects in injuries. Nor does he say, as they do, but *as you would that men should do to you, that you also should do to them,* that the return may be the fuller, since the act is matched with the wish. And now extend the works of charity not only to friends, but even to enemies, that they may begin to be friends; and first extend the affection of love, and afterwards the works of charity.

VERS. 32.-- *And if you love.* The more eminent your degree of profession, the more needful it is that the care [0264B] of virtue be more abundant, that you embrace in the bosom of charity even those who do not love.

There are two kinds of benefit: either when we give kindly what we give, or when we lend to one who will repay.

VERS. 35.-- *But yet,* etc. After he has reproved as unfruitful the love and mutual benefit of sinners, he now shows how these are to be done fruitfully by the faithful, as if he should say: Although such love and mutual benefit profit not sinners, yet do you love, not only by the leading of nature, as they love friends, but by a higher degree of virtue, your enemies. The philosophers divide justice into three parts: one toward God, which is called piety; another toward parents, or the rest of the human race; the third [0264C] toward the dead, that the due rites of burial be paid to them. But the Lord, having gone beyond the oracle of the law and the summit of prophecy, extended the office of piety even to those who have done hurt.

*Hoping for nothing thereby: and it shall be.* Not fixing the hope of a reward in man. For whether he repay what you have lent, God will nevertheless repay also, at whose bidding you did it; or whether he repay not, your inheritance shall be for ever.

VERS. 36.-- *Be ye therefore merciful,* etc. He rains upon the evil, the earth does not deny its produce, the same sun enlightens the good and the evil.

Mystically: he waters the people of the Jews with prophetic rains, and upon those not deserving it the ray of the eternal sun has shone. If therefore they have believed, be merciful to them, by teaching and by lifting them up, and do not rashly judge, [0264D] lest anyone conscious of his own offence be compelled to pass sentence upon another.

VERS. 37.-- *Judge not.* There are certain middle and uncertain things, in what mind they are done, because they can be done well and ill. We know not either what he will be like who now appears evil, of whose correction to despair, and to reprove him as though cast off, is rash.

Concerning open things which cannot be done with a good mind, it is permitted us to judge.

VERS. 38.-- *For with the same measure.* It can be taken generally of all things which are done by mind, by hand, by tongue, because God will render according to the works of each.

[0265A] *For with the same, [n: (AMBR.)]* God will not repay with an unjust measure, but it is as if he should say: The very rashness of judging and the unjust measure hurts thee, punishes thee, and often not him about whom thou doest this. Or if thou judgest and measurest rightly, it profits thee, it saves thee, even though sometimes not him upon whom thou bestowest these things.

VERS. 39.-- *He spoke.* By which he invites to the giving of alms and to the forgiving of injury. *Can he.* If anger has blinded thee against a violent man: if thou shalt be covetous against him that asks, canst thou, with thy mind vitiated, heal his vice? And not only he who has done thee injury, but thou also who knowest not how to bear it, shalt be guilty. But if his wickedness finds thee tranquil, both he shall repent, and thou shalt be crowned for thy patience, because thou hast taken care to lead the blind man with a seeing eye, that is, with a serene heart, [0265B] to the light.

VERS. 40.-- *As the master.* God does not avenge his own injuries, but by bearing with persecutors he willed to make them milder; the disciples, who are men, ought to imitate this rule of perfection.

VERS. 41.-- *And why seest thou,* etc. Truly a sinner is not able to chastise a sinner, because they who, forestalled by pride, or by hatred, or by some other vice, judging these things light or nothing, gravely rebuke those whom they see disturbed from the state of the mind, either by anger or by some slight sin, such men as these love rather to blame and to condemn than to correct and to amend. *Thou considerest not.* The open eye sees ill, namely its own boasting, while it wishes to heal others, but being blind it is not able to look upon itself, while in this it falls the worse.

[0265C]VERS. 42.-- *Hypocrite.* If thou wilt reprove anyone, first see whether thou art like him. And if thou art, groan alike with him, and be not willing that he should obey thee, but admonish him to strive alike. But if thou art not like him, yet because thou once wast, or couldst have been, condescend, and reprove not out of hatred but out of mercy. Rarely therefore, and not without great necessity, are rebukes to be applied, and not save out of regard for God, when the beam has been removed from the eye.

For these men undertake to accuse all things out of hatred or envy, and wish to seem counsellors, without the example of their own amendment. But they ought first to take away the beam of envy, or of malice, or of dissimulation, from the hidden place of their heart, that they may be able to cast out the mote of anger, or of any fault of envy, from their brother's eye.

[0265D]VERS. 43.-- *There is no tree,* etc. A tree good or bad is not nature, which was created good by God in all things, but a good or a bad will. The fruits are the works, which can neither be good of a bad will, nor bad of a good will. *Nor a tree.* If thou wilt have true justice and not feigned, fulfil in deeds what thou showest in words, that thou mayest be a good tree, and be adorned with good fruits. Because although the hypocrite feign, he is not good who does evil works; and if he reprove the guiltless, he is not therefore evil who does good works. And note that here the discussion is of manifest things.

VERS. 44.-- *For every tree.* The evil are known by their own fruit, while they oppress the good, and blaspheme God by their deeds if not by their words: and chiefly they are discerned by their impatience [0266A] in the time of adversity. For fasts and prayer and things of this kind belong to the feigning as also to the good: but the sheep ought not to lay aside their own fleeces, even if sometimes wolves cover themselves with them. *Is known.* Not by the garment, but by the works which are done by them, are they known. Because as the thorn does not make a grape, so a good tree makes what is good, a bad tree what is bad, and not the contrary. *For neither of thorns.* Not of thorns, that is, of the cares of the world, figs, that is, the sweetness of the resurrection, nor of the bramble, that is, of the pricking of vices, a grape, that is, the fruit of the soul. Which, as a grape near to earthly things is corrupted, in higher places ripens. Or from the flesh, which brings forth thorns and thistles, we do not gather Christ, who hung upon the tree like a grape.

The thorn and the bramble, to the heretics, from whom [0266B] no wise man will be able to find holiness or virtue, but they tear and bloody those that draw near. Or the thorns and brambles are the cares of the world and the prickings of vices. The grape and the fig, the sweetness of the new manner of life which Christ hungers for in us, and the fervour of love which gladdens the heart of man.

Not of thorns and brambles a grape or a fig, because the mind still pressed by the custom of the old man can indeed feign, but cannot bear the fruit of the new man. But if at any time the deeds or words of the evil profit the good, the evil do not do this, but it comes of them by the counsel of God.

VERS. 45.-- *Bringeth forth good.* He loves his enemy. He gives to everyone that asks, and things of this kind.

*Bringeth forth evil,* etc. He hates his friend, he takes away what is another's, [0266C] he does not give his own, and things of this kind, which God judges according to the intention of the heart. And truly, because it proceeds from a good or a bad heart, the fruit ought to be judged good or bad, because out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks what lies hidden within, that is, the effect proceeds outwardly, as well in words as in deeds. This is as regards the divine examination, to which the mouth of the heart speaks, because he is not ignorant from what intention words proceed. Because even the words which outwardly seem good, because they proceed from an evil root, he judges not to be good; whence he subjoins: *And why call you me Lord?* As if he should say: Why do you boast that you put forth the leaves of a right confession, who show no fruits of good work? *Out of the abundance.* By the speech of the mouth the Lord signifies all the things which are brought forth from the heart by act or by thought. [0266D] For a word is wont to be put for a deed; whence: *There was no word which he did not show them [n: (Isa. XXXIX)]*.

VERS. 46.-- *And why,* etc. This calling seems the fruit of a good tree, but because it does not proceed from the fatness of charity, God judges it not good. But what the true discernment of good or of evil fruits may be, he sets forth under another figure:

VERS. 47.-- *Everyone that cometh,* etc. After he has long disputed of the openly evil, and of the truly and the feignedly good, he likens good hearers of the word to himself, the evil to the devil. Because as Christ builds, instructs and governs one Church out of the various persons of men, being about to lead it into life eternal; so the good hearer builds for himself by various virtues a dwelling on high, [0267A] being about to rejoice in the world to come with Christ at its dedication. And as the devil draws those who obey him from the heavens to earthly things and to sins, where there is no foundation, because evil is without substance, and always casts them down into worse things: so the evil hearer, whether he be initiated in the mysteries of Christ, or wholly a stranger, cast down as it were into a well without a bottom, finds not where to hold himself, but when he has come into the depth of evils, he despises.

VERS. 48.-- *He digged deep.* He digs deep who by the precepts of humility roots out utterly from the hearts of his own all earthly things, that he may not serve God for the sake of anything fleeting, that he may have in them an unshaken dwelling. *He laid a foundation.* Foundations in the plural, the doctors; in the singular, the foundation, the doctor of doctors [0267B] Christ.

Morally. The foundations of the house are the intentions of a good manner of life, which through Christian humility, the rubbish of superfluous thoughts having been drawn off, the perfect hearer sets within himself, doing spiritually in himself what Christ does generally in the whole Church.

*A flood.* This flood he elsewhere calls the gates of hell. *And the gates of hell shall not prevail against it [n: (Matth. XVI)]*. This flood happens in three ways, because each man is tempted by his own concupiscence, or by the wickedness of false brethren, or by the open assault of those without. *Beaten upon.* By the rush of the river can be understood the crisis of the last judgment, when each Church has been consummated. *Everyone that exalteth himself [0267C] shall be humbled, and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted [n: (Luc. XIV)]*. *That house.* Our several houses are daily beaten by the unquiet of unclean spirits, or of wicked men, or of one's own mind, or of the flesh. And in so far as they trust in their own strength, they are bowed down: in so far as they cleave to the most unconquered rock, they cannot be shaken.
