## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)

VERS. 49.-- *Upon the earth,* etc. The devil builds upon the earth the world which is set in wickedness, because he draws it to the love of earthly things. He builds without a foundation, because sin does not subsist in its own nature, since evil is not a substance, which nevertheless, wherever it happens, grows together in a good nature, or without a foundation, that is, without a bottom. As therefore he who is plunged into a well is held at the bottom of the well, so [0267D] the fallen soul stands as it were in a certain place of the bottom, if it holds itself within some measure of sin. But since it cannot be content with the sin into which it falls, while it is daily led to worse things, as in a well into which it has fallen it does not find a bottom whereon it may be fixed. *It fell down, and there was made a ruin,* etc. [var: Vulg. Lc 6:49 reads *et continuo cecidit: et facta est ruina domus illius magna*, "and immediately it fell, and the ruin of that house was great."] Every conscience which does not remain with its hope fixed in God is not able to persist in temptations, and is the more driven the more it is disjoined from things above in those things which are of the world. And truly all the evil, or the feignedly good, become worse when any temptation presses in.
