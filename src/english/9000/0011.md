## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 1.-- *When he had fulfilled.* Beautifully, where he has completed his precepts, [0268A] he teaches the form of carrying out his precepts. For at once the servant of a Gentile centurion is offered to the Lord to be healed, in whom the Gentiles, who were held sick in worldly servitude, are set forth as those to be saved by the benefit of God. *He entered into Capharnaum. Of a centurion,* etc. He did not enter before he had brought his words to an end, although he did not enter immediately after these words. For before he entered, in that very interval the leper was cleansed, as Matthew sets it down.

VERS. 3.-- *He sent to him the elders,* etc. By divine providence the elders of the Jews are sent, and in their presence the sick man is healed, that they may be without excuse if they have refused to believe.

The centurion sends friends, lest by his own presence he should seem to burden the Lord's modesty, and to have challenged [0268B] service with service. But it signifies that we who from among the Gentiles believe in the Lord cannot ourselves come to him whom we cannot see in the flesh, but to him who sits at the Father's right hand. The Gentile people, like a centurion, is thronged about with the soldiery of the virtues and with the perfection of spiritual things, which is signified in the hundred. Lofty, seeking nothing earthly from the Lord for himself and for his own, but only the joys of eternal salvation: and these make supplication to the Lord for servants, that is, for those who are still pressed down in fear by the spirit of servitude, that, as they are little by little advanced to higher things, *perfect love may cast out fear [n: (I Joan. IV)]*.

Matthew says that the centurion came near, which is to be understood of faith. For by faith one truly draws near to God, and that faith is commended in the centurion by the Lord: [0268C] *I have not found so great faith in Israel.* But Luke follows out the order in which it was done, that we might be compelled to understand in what manner Matthew says he came near.

VERS. 5.-- *Our nation.* Mystically. The Gentile people (whose figure the centurion bears) has Jesus not far from the house, although it does not dare to invite him under the roof, because, *near to them that fear him is his salvation.* He who rightly uses the natural law, so as to work the good things he knows, draws near thereby to him who is truly good. But to those whom the crimes of Gentile error had bound, that can be fitted which is said elsewhere: *for some of these came from afar [n: (Marc. VIII)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *And Jesus went.* Being about to heal powerfully by his word alone, he deigns, for the commendation of humility, to visit [0268D] the sick man; and he who would not go to heal the ruler's son, lest he should seem to have honoured riches, goes to a servant, lest he should be thought to have despised the servile condition. Faith shines out in his works while he heals, but humility works more in the affections while he goes. *I am not worthy.* Because of the conscience of his Gentile life, he thought himself to be burdened rather than helped by the Lord's condescension, and that he could not have Christ as a guest, since, although endowed with faith, he was nevertheless not yet steeped in the sacraments. But because divine grace knows how to give what human weakness does not presume, as a figure of the Gentile people he deserved, not yet catechized, both that his faith should be praised by the Lord and that his servant should be healed.

VERS. 8.-- *For I also,* etc. If I, a man set under another, [0269A] can command those beneath me, how much more thou, who, having angels serving thee over all things, canst through their ministries, without the presence of thy body, say to sickness that it depart, and it will depart, and to health that it come, and it will come.

VERS. 9.-- *Not even in Israel.* This man's faith is set even before the more chosen and those who see God. Even in this one man the faith of the Gentiles is preferred to Israel.

VERS. 10.-- *And they returned.* Matthew explains it more plainly, that when the Lord said: *As thou hast believed, so be it done to thee; let the boy be healed [n: (Matth. VIII)]* [var: Vulg. Matt 8:13 "Et sanatus est puer in illa hora," and the boy was healed in that hour; the printed reading has the subjunctive]. But it is the custom of blessed Luke to abridge what has been plainly set out by others, or even of set purpose to pass over what has been briefly said by others, or to elucidate more skilfully what has been omitted. *They found the servant.* The master believing, the servant is healed, because the merit of the master [0269B] can avail even for servants, not only by the merit of faith, but also by zeal for discipline.

VERS. 11.-- *And it came to pass,* etc. After the sick man healed he raises the dead, because sickness goes before death.

*Which is called Naim.* Naim is a city of Galilee at the second milestone from Thabor toward the south, next to Endor, which is a large village. And Naim is interpreted *a wave* or *a commotion.*

VERS. 12.-- *And when he drew near,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] When the Word made flesh, the Gentile people through the gates of faith, etc., *as far as* which he commands both meanwhile in the few of the Jews converted, and at last in the fulness.

[0269C] *Behold a dead man was carried out,* etc. The dead man, who is carried out before many outside the gate, signifies him who sins criminally, and who does not cover his sin in the chamber of the heart, but by the evidence of deed or of speech publishes it abroad to others, as it were through the doors of his own city. Whom mother Church bewails as an only son, for although she is gathered out of many persons, yet mother Church is one virgin, while the sons are single. The gate by which he is carried out is some one of the senses, by which a man falls into sin. As he who looks in order to lust, and he who [lends himself] to hearing idle or filthy things, and he who lends his tongue to quarrels.

*The only son to his mother. [n: (AMBR.)]* Adam is carried from the earth by the four elements, of which man consists. The bier is touched, etc., *as far as* when he thinks upon avarice, or when for excess of fever [0269D] he cannot be touched.

VERS. 13.-- *And a crowd.* Many with the Lord, many with the widow, so that, the miracle being seen, many may become praisers and witnesses of God.

VERS. 15.-- *And he sat up.* The Lord raised three dead: the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue in the house, that is, in thought; the only son of his mother at the gate, that is, in word; Lazarus in the tomb, that is, in deed.

VERS. 16.-- *He hath visited.* Since he both appointed the Word to be embodied once, and daily sends the Holy Spirit into the hearts of men, that they may be raised up.

VERS. 18.-- *And they told. [n: (AMBR.)]* Not with a simple heart, but at the prompting of envy, etc., *as far as* [0270A] and, being corrected, they may believe in Jesus and, at their master's questioning, say for themselves: *Thou art he that is to come.*

[n: (BED., GREG.)] He does not say, who hast come, etc., *as far as* because a lapse of love does not hinder faith, for such a lapse is religious.

VERS. 22.-- *And answering he said to them: Go and relate to John what you have seen,* etc. [var: Vulg. Luke 7:22 "quae audistis et vidistis," what you have heard and seen] Jesus, knowing that no one can fully believe without the Gospel, because as faith begins from the Old Testament, so it is fulfilled in the New. Being questioned about himself, he signified what he was not by any word, but by his deeds, answering not to the things about which he was questioned, but to the scandal of the messengers.

VERS. 23.-- *Blessed.* As if he should say: I do indeed marvellous things, but I do not disdain to suffer abject ones, and men must beware lest they despise death in me, who [0270B] venerate the signs.

*He that shall not. [n: (GREG.)]* He who shall not have doubted of the power of the godhead. This is not against John, whom he presently commends so highly, but he calls back from faithlessness those who believe not, and he sets forth to John what he was asking, because *God of saving; and of the Lord, of the Lord are the issues from death [n: (Psal. LXVII)]*. So many signs and mighty works having been seen, the faithful man could not doubt concerning his death, but only wonder; but the mind of the unbelieving bore a scandal in him, when it saw him dying even after the miracles.

VERS. 24.-- *What.* When he had admonished John's disciples that they must believe in the Lord's cross, turning to the crowds he summons the poor to virtue, lest, lifted up in heart, unstable in mind, weak in counsel, they should prefer specious and fleeting things to things eternally useful, but that they should rather take up the cross [0270C] than the world's trappings. And therefore he praises the person of John, who, setting aside the love of life, did not change the form even of justice through fear of death. The world is compared to a desert, because it is still untilled and barren, into which one must not so go forth as to think that men puffed up with the mind of the flesh and empty of virtue, and boasting of the glory of a frail world, are to be imitated.

*A reed by the wind. [n: (BED.)]* By the reed is meant the carnal mind, which, when it is touched by favour or by detraction, is bent to whatever side. But John is not a reed shaken by the wind, because neither did favour make him soft nor any man's anger make him harsh, nor did prosperity lift him up, nor did adversity bow him down.

VERS. 25.-- *Clothed in soft garments.* By the [0270D] garment the human body is understood. Therefore by the prophetic example he exhorts to the virtue of a virtue to be undergone. Behold they who are in costly apparel. Those whose limbs are loosed and made flabby by riches, strangers to the heavenly kingdom, grow old under the sway of demons (who are the kings of darkness) within the dwellings of this world.

VERS. 26.-- *More than a prophet.* Because he is an angel: not by nature, but by office, who is sent to announce the judge from on high.

VERS. 28.-- *But I say.* In this he is not preferred before the patriarchs and all men, but is shown equal to the rest of the saints. *But he that.* Every saint who is already with God is greater than he who is still in the world. Or: I, whom the opinion of many makes the lesser, [0271A] am in the Church of the saints set before him. Or, the least angel of heaven is better than any man whatsoever.

VERS. 29.-- *And all the people.* If this is understood to have been said by the Lord, the people is understood to be designated as hearing John. If it is said to have been interposed by the evangelist, it remains to be understood as hearing the Lord himself, discoursing upon John's greatness.

VERS. 31.-- *To whom then,* etc. John being praised, he passes to the rebuking of those who neither were moved by John's preaching, or who despise the preaching of Christ himself.

VERS. 32.-- *They are like to children.* The children are teachers humble in spirit. The marketplace is the synagogue, or Jerusalem itself, in which the ordinances of God's precepts were established, because they were wont to reproach their fellow tribesmen that neither, [0271B] drawn by psalms, did they rise up to the praises of God with a devout heart and with agility of limbs (which the dancing denotes), nor by threnodies (that is, by the lamentations of the prophets over the destructions already wrought or yet to be wrought) were they turned to penance.

VERS. 34.-- *The Son of man is come eating and drinking.* He comes as then, so now also; you spurn both ways of salvation. We have lamented to John, we have sung to Christ.

VERS. 35.-- *Wisdom is justified.* He shows that the sons of wisdom understand that justice lies neither in abstaining nor in eating, but in the evenness of mind that bears want and temperance, and does not corrupt itself through abundance. *The kingdom of God is not meat and drink [n: (Rom. XIV)]*. Of which not the use but the [0271C] concupiscence is reproved. *Justified,* etc. Whatever you think me, I nevertheless, the power of God and the wisdom of God, am understood to act justly by the apostles, to whom the Father revealed the things he hid from the prudent.

VERS. 36.-- *He asked.* As he had set it forth in words, so in deeds also he establishes that wisdom is justified by all her sons, because she is justified even by Mary repenting after her fault.

VERS. 37.-- *In the house.* In whosesoever inner house thou shalt have known wisdom and justice reclining, run back to the feet, that is, to the furthest part of wisdom; seek with tears, confess thy sins, spread out thy hair, that is, strew before him all the dignities of thy body; kiss, that is, thou shalt speak nothing but wisdom. *She brought.* As many delights as she had in herself, [0271D] so many holocausts she found out of herself.

[n: (HIER.)] The alabaster box of ointment, that is, the body with faith, etc., *as far as* that is, the sins of the Jews.

VERS. 38.-- *She began to water.* Mary the sister of Lazarus performed the same office twice: once in Galilee, when she first draws near with humility and tears, where she received the remission of sins. The second time, in Bethania, no longer named a sinner, but chaste, and therefore devout: there she anoints the feet, here the head.
