## CHAPTER VII. (cont.)

VERS. 43.-- *He to whom more.* According to men, he perhaps offended more who owed more. But by the Lord's mercy the case is changed, so that he who owed more should love more, and yet obtain grace. *Thou hast judged rightly.* The Pharisee is convicted by his own sentence, [0272A] because, like a frenzied man, he carries the rope by which he is to be bound.

VERS. 44.-- *I entered into the house.* I entered, flesh being assumed, into the people of the Jews.

VERS. 46.-- *My head with oil thou didst not anoint.* And although that people confessed that it believed in God and not in a man, yet it neglected to proclaim with worthy praise that very power of the godhead which appeared through the miracles. But the Gentile world, while it believes the mystery of the incarnation, proclaims with the highest praise even his weak things.

VERS. 47.-- *Her sins are forgiven her.* In the house of the Pharisee, that is, in the keeping of the law and the prophets, it is not the unbelieving Pharisee but the repentant sinful woman who is glorified, because the ardour of charity burned away in her the rust [0272B] of her offences.

VERS. 49.-- *And they that were together began.* The sick woman being healed, they sicken over her salvation, to others.

VERS. 50.-- *Thy faith hath made thee safe.* Faith made her safe, because she did not doubt that she could receive what she asked, since she had already received hope from him from whom she was seeking salvation.
