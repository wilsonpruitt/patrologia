## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass.* After the manner of an eagle stirring up her young to fly, the Lord little by little clothes his own disciples in the nest of faith with the feathers of the virtues, by which afterwards they may be able to fly more loftily and to instruct others. First he teaches in the synagogues, works miracles, spreads his fame everywhere, receives the crowds, heals, instructs; here he makes disciples, out of whom he chose twelve, [0272C] whom at first he teaches with the crowd present, and bestows on the wretched his accustomed benefits; but now he keeps them alone with him, that they may hear him more familiarly; to them alone he expounds the more hidden mysteries; and so at last, when they have been strengthened by the showing of his powers as by the protection of wings, he gives them the power of healing, and sends them to preach the kingdom of God.

VERS. 2.-- *Mary who is called Magdalen.* When Mary was making the journey with the Lord, and is recorded to minister to him, he calls her by the celebrated name of Magdalen: where he describes the same woman as a sinner, he reverently calls her by the general name of woman, lest the mark of her former error should darken a name of such renown as we venerate today. *Mary,* a bitter sea, because of the ingrafted roaring of penitence, by which she herself, or we, bewail our several [0272D] evils. *Seven demons.* That same Church of the Gentiles, cleansed from the filth of vices, which Mary signifies, Joanna also signifies: formerly given over to the worship of idols, but now redeemed by the mercy of Christ and filled with grace, and which was before as it were the wife of the devil.

VERS. 3.-- *And Joanna,* etc. Joanna, the Lord her grace, or the Lord merciful. whose is all that we live. *Of Herod's steward.* When any malignant spirit labours to deceive on behalf of the devil's kingdom, he stands as it were the steward of the most impious Herod. *Of their substance,* etc. It was an ancient custom of the Jews, and it was not accounted a fault, that women should feed and clothe their teachers out of their own substance. Here, because this could give [0273A] scandal among the Gentiles, Paul records that he abstained from it. They ministered therefore carnal things to the Lord, whose spiritual things they reaped, that he might give a form to preachers, that they ought to be content with the food and clothing they receive from the peoples.

VERS. 4.-- *And when a crowd,* etc. As often as the word "crowd" is inserted in the Gospel, just as a diversity of men, so also a diversity of wills is intimated. For not all followed Christ with the same will, but for different causes: some for good, some for evil; yet to all the Lord bestows his benefits, by teaching, by feeding, by healing. *The sower went out,* etc. The Son of God went out from the bosom of the Father, to which there was no access for a creature, that he might sow his own seed; he came into the world, that he might give testimony [0273B] to the truth. Whence, according to the other evangelists, when about to speak this parable he is recorded to have gone out of the house, to have gone to the sea, and to have gone up into a ship, insinuating by the posture of his body the very thing he insinuated by the course of his discourse.

VERS. 5.-- *By the wayside.* The way is the heart worn down and dried up by the constant passage of evil thoughts, so that it cannot receive the seed of the word nor make it germinate, but it is trodden down by the worst thoughts, and snatched away by the demons. Who are therefore called the birds of the air, because they are of a heavenly and spiritual nature, or because they course through the air.

VERS. 6.-- *Upon the rock.* The rock is the untamed heart, pierced by no ploughshare of true faith, in which there is not true love nor the virtue of perseverance.

[0273C]VERS. 8.-- *A hundredfold,* etc. Since the number ten is wont to be taken for perfection (because the keeping of the law is contained in its ten precepts), the number a hundred, which arises from ten multiplied, is set for great perfection: therefore that heart bears fruit a hundredfold which by the love of its neighbour attains the active life, and by the love of God the contemplative, and is adorned with the perfection of spiritual virtues.

VERS. 9.-- *This parable.* Note that this is the first parable which is set down with its own interpretation. And one must beware, wherever the Lord, being asked by the disciples privately, expounds his own discourses, lest we should wish to understand either something else, or more, or less, than what has been set forth by him. And therefore he deigns to show this parable by himself, that he might make known that he speaks figuratively, [0273D] and might teach that the significations of things are to be sought even in those which he was unwilling to expound.

VERS. 10.-- *Seeing.* The teachers of the Jews, who, although they have been blinded by the desert of their own perversity, falsely think themselves to be seeing.

VERS. 12.-- *But that which by the wayside.* They are sown by the wayside who deign to receive it with no faith, with no understanding, not even with any occasion of profit tempting them. But they are sown upon the rock and among thorns who do indeed hear, and approve the profit of the word, and taste the desire of it; but, lest they should come to that which they long for, they hold them back either by terrifying through adverse things or by flattering with prosperous ones. By these three kinds [0274A] are designated all who do not do the word they have heard.

VERS. 13.-- *For a while they believe.* Many, hearing a discourse against avarice or luxury, say that despisers of the world and chaste men are blessed: but when desirable forms are set before their gaze, presently whatever they had rightly thought departs from them.

VERS. 14.-- *They are choked.* Because pleasures and riches, by their importunate thoughts, strangle the throat of the mind, as it were the entrance of the vital breath, so that a good desire cannot enter to the heart.

VERS. 15.-- *On the good ground,* etc. Upon good hearers who take pains to hear the word, and lay it up in the belly of memory, and so in due time bring forth the fruit [0274B] of working, being patient amid the prosperity and adversity of the world. And the good ground renders fruit with patience, because it does good things, and bears with an even mind the evils of its neighbours; and while they humbly look up to the scourges, after the scourges they are received into rest.

VERS. 16.-- *And no man.* These words are set down according to the likeness of human custom.

VERS. 17.-- *For there is not,* etc. As if he should say. Let not the fear of carnal men hold you back, nor yet the evil opinion which is now held of you among unbelievers; because although you appear vile, one day your brightness will appear, when *the Lord will bring to light the hidden things of darkness,* then you also will be crowned, and your adversaries will be punished.

[0274C]VERS. 18.-- *For he that hath,* etc. These things seem to be said specially to the apostles, to whom out of charity it is given to know the mysteries, and to the faithless Jews (who through parables hear what they do not understand). It can also be taken generally, because both the clever man, by being negligent, deprives himself of true wisdom, and the simple diligent man, by labouring, tastes it. Because to him who has the love of the word, the sense also of understanding what he loves will be given. But he who has not the love of hearing, even though by natural talent or by studious labour he thinks that he understands, will yet taste no sweetness of true knowledge. *And whosoever hath not.* Therefore the slothful man often receives talent, that he may be the more justly punished for his negligence, who scorned to know what he could have attained without labour; and sometimes the studious man is pressed by slowness of understanding, that he may find the greater [0274D] rewards the more studiously he labours.

VERS. 24.-- *And it ceased, and there was a calm.* Note that every creature perceives its Creator. He commands, he rebukes, and they perceive him commanding; not that, according to the heresy of certain men, they are living things, but that things which with us are insensible are sensible to the majesty of their maker.

VERS. 25.-- *They wondered one to another.* Not the disciples, but the sailors and the others who were in the ship wonder. Whence Matthew: *But those men wondered, saying: What manner of man is this? [n: (Matth. VIII.)]* But if we say that the disciples wondered, they too are rightly called men.

*To the country of the Gerasens.* Gerasa, a city of Arabia [0275A] beside Mount Galaad in the tribe of Manasses, not far from the lake of Tiberias, signifies the Gentiles whom after his passion and resurrection the Lord visited through his preachers; whence Gerasa, or Gergesi, casting out the settler, that is, the devil by whom it was formerly inhabited, or, a stranger drawing near, who was far off in time.

VERS. 27.-- *In the sepulchres.* What else are the bodies of the faithless but certain tombs of the dead, in which the words of God do not dwell?

VERS. 28.-- *Jesus, Son of God.* Arius contends that he is a creature, whom the demon confesses to be the Son of God. The Jews say: *In the prince of demons he casteth out demons,* whom the demons deny to have anything in common with themselves. It is a torment to the devil to cease from injuring a man, and by how much the longer he had possessed him, by so much [0275B] the more hardly does he let him go. Let anyone therefore who has been injured by the devil take care to break his yoke quickly, because his dominion is escaped the more easily the more quickly it is escaped.

VERS. 29.-- *He was bound with chains.* The chains and fetters are the hard and heavy laws of the Gentiles, by which, even in their commonwealth, sins were restrained. Yet when these laws were broken, they were led by desire to those crimes which exceeded common usage.

VERS. 30.-- *Because many demons entered,* etc. The many demons which oppress one man signify the many cults of idolatry in one people of the Gentiles. On the contrary it is said: *The multitude of believers had one heart and one soul [n: (Act. IV)]*. Whence fittingly in Babylon the unity of tongues was rent, and in Jerusalem it was made one.

[0275C]VERS. 31.-- *And they besought.* The demons knew that by the Lord's coming they were one day to be plunged into the abyss: not themselves foreseeing things to come, but recalling the things said of them by the prophets.

VERS. 32.-- *A herd of swine.* The swine are men devoid of speech and of reason, given over to filthy deeds, feeding on the mountain of pride. And unless a man has lived after the manner of a swine, the devil receives no power over him, or receives it only for proving him, and not for destroying him.

VERS. 34.-- *They fled and told it in the city and in the villages.* The herdsmen fled, because neither the philosophers of the Gentiles nor the rulers of the Synagogue can bring medicine to the perishing; and although they flee the Christian law, yet by being astonished and by wondering they proclaim its power.

[0275D]VERS. 35.-- *And they went out to see what was done, and they came to Jesus, and found the man,* etc. It signifies the multitude, delighted with its own comely life, indeed honouring the Christian religion, but unwilling to endure it, while they say that they cannot fulfil it. They wonder also at the faithful people healed of its former manner of life, and sitting at the Lord's feet, that is, with a sound judgment of mind beholding the footsteps of the Saviour which it is to follow, and clothed with the pursuits of the virtues which, being deceived, it had lost.

VERS. 37.-- *And they besought him.* Conscious of their frailty, they judge themselves unworthy of the Lord's presence, not receiving the word of God, and not sustaining the weight of wisdom in a firm mind; and therefore he was not troublesome to them any longer, but [0276A] returns, because he does not deign to heal the unwilling, but quickly leaves the weak, to whom he sees his own presence to be a burden.

VERS. 39.-- *Return to thy house,* etc. Allegorically. After the remission of sins one must return into a good conscience, and must serve the Gospel, for the salvation of others also, that thereafter he may rest with Christ; lest, when he now wishes to be near with Christ, he neglect the mystery of preaching that is fitted to the deliverance of the brethren. Whence the Apostle: *I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ [n: (Philip. I)]*, a thing much better, *but to abide in the flesh is needful for you.*

VERS. 43.-- *Upon physicians.* The physicians are the demons, who, as though taking counsel for men, demand to be worshipped in God's stead, and whom the Gentile world had served.

[0276B]VERS. 44.-- *She touched the hem.* He touches the hem who perfectly loves and believes the mysteries of the Incarnation, until he comes to the grasping of greater things. If we consider how great our faith is, and understand how great the Son of God is, we see that, in comparison with him, we touch only the hem, and cannot reach the upper part of his garment.

*And immediately,* etc. Being about to raise the dead girl, he returned to make faith; first he heals the woman with the issue of blood. So Elizabeth, barren, is declared as about to bear, that the Virgin about to conceive may be believed.

VERS. 45.-- *Who is it.* He does not ask that he may be taught what he does not know, but that the woman's faith may appear.
