## CHAPTER IX.

[0277B]

VERS. 1.-- *And Jesus, having called them together.* The power of signs being first granted, they are sent to preach the kingdom of God, that the greatness of the deeds might also bear witness to the greatness of the promises, and that the power shown might give credit to the words, and that they who preached new things might also do new things.

VERS. 3.-- *Take nothing.* In Mark it is read, *that they should take nothing for the way but a staff only [n: (Marc. VI)]*. But it must be known that both were commanded by the Lord: that they should carry nothing but a staff, and that they should not carry even a staff. Not to carry a staff is not to be anxious about the things which are necessary for human sustenance, because these are owed to preachers by those to whom they preach, when they can be received without scandal. To carry [0277C] nothing but a staff is to use that power of receiving necessities which has been given to preachers. *Two coats.* A single garment is whatever is necessary to a man according to the character of the place or the time. A double one is what is beyond necessity. Allegorically, in the two coats he forbids duplicity.

VERS. 4.-- *There abide.* Do not run about through the houses and change the rights of an inviolable hospitality. Whence according to Matthew, the house in which they ought to remain must first be chosen, that the necessity of changing lodging may be taken away.

VERS. 5.-- *The dust of your feet.* The dust is shaken off in testimony of their labour, that they entered into the city, and that the preaching reached as far as them. [0277D] Or, it is shaken off, that they may receive nothing from those who have spurned the Gospel, not even the necessaries of life.

VERS. 8.-- *John is risen.* They who said that John, of whom they had seen no sign, had risen again, by the utmost malice denied that Jesus had risen again; they had seen many marvellous works, in whose passion many wonderful things had happened.

VERS. 10.-- *They told.* The apostles tell the Lord what they had done, and how they had taught, and how John suffered while they themselves were teaching. Whence it follows: *And taking them.* The nourishment of heavenly grace is imparted not to the idle, not to those residing in the city of the Synagogue, that is, of secular dignity, but to those seeking Christ amid the deserts. For those who do not turn away in loathing are received by Christ, and both spiritually and bodily, [0278A] if they have need, they are healed. And note, that after the mention of John's passion is completed, Christ refreshes, because after the failing of the law the food of the Gospel begins. Finally, after the woman who bears the figure of the Church has been healed, and after the apostles have been sent to preach, mention is made of the refreshment, because through the apostles true refreshment takes place in the Church.

VERS. 11.-- *They followed him.* Not on beasts, not in carriages, but by the proper labour of their feet, that they might show the ardour of their mind. *He healed.* No one receives the food of Christ unless he has first been healed, because after the remission of sins the heavenly nourishment is bestowed.

VERS. 12.-- *Now the day began.* He refreshes them when the day has declined, because, either as the end of the ages draws near, or when the sun of justice had set, we were [0278B] saved from spiritual famine. And note the order of the mystery: first medicine is applied to the wounds by the remission of sins; afterwards the nourishment of the heavenly table is dispensed.

VERS. 13.-- *Unless perhaps.* The apostles had not yet understood that the faith of a believing people is not for sale, but they had known that we are rather to be redeemed by his own free foods.

VERS. 14.-- *Five thousand.* By the five thousand are signified those who, being still in a secular habit, know how to use well, through the five senses of the body, the outward things which they possess. Who are fed with five loaves, because such must still be instructed by the precepts of the law, and restrained by legal fear. For those who renounce the world entirely both are four thousand, because of the evangelical [0278C] doctrine, and are refreshed with seven loaves, that is, with the grace of the sevenfold Spirit.

VERS. 15.-- *And all to sit down.* According to another evangelist the five thousand sit down upon the grass, because those who still have bodily senses are delighted with better things. *For all flesh is grass.* The four thousand recline upon the earth, for it is more to press the earth than to lie upon grass.

VERS. 16.-- *He distributed to his disciples.* He calls forth the apostles to the breaking of bread, that, while they testify that they have none, the greatness of the sign may become the better known. He insinuates that through their teaching fasting hearts are daily to be fed.

VERS. 17.-- *And they were filled.* That the people, eating, [0278D] is filled signifies that hunger is to be driven away for ever from the elect, because he who has received Christ's food will not hunger. That the apostles minister announces the future distribution of the Lord's body and blood. *Twelve baskets.* Why is more left over to the five thousand men, and less to the four thousand? Because the four thousand were with Christ for three days, and therefore received more of the heavenly pasture.

VERS. 18.-- *And it came to pass.* After the miracle, when the crowds wished to make him king, the crowd being dismissed, he fled and went up into the mountain alone to pray; and before the disciples went up into the boat, they were present with him. But he alone prays to the Father. For the saints can be joined to the Lord by faith and love, and can behold him as more excellent than men, and can follow his manner of life [0279A] in the flesh with the steps of humility; but he alone penetrates the incomprehensible secrets of the Father's position, and he alone prays what he knows must be prayed. But he is not found to have prayed with the disciples, but prays alone, because human hearts do not grasp the counsel of God, nor can anyone be a partaker of the inner things with Christ. *Whom do the crowds say that I am?* Being about to search out the faith of the disciples, he first inquires the opinion of the common people, lest the faith of the apostles should seem to be established by common opinion, but by the acknowledgment of the truth. Rightly are they named crowds who bring forward different opinions about the Lord, whose speech and understanding is unstable and wandering; and distinguishing his own from them he says. *But whom do you say that I am?*

VERS. 19.-- *John the Baptist.* The opinion about John [0279B] was perhaps for this reason, because, placed in his mother's womb, he perceived the Lord's presence. About Elias, because he was caught up into heaven, and is believed to be about to come. About Jeremias, because he was sanctified in his mother's womb.

VERS. 20.-- *But you.* He who discourses ought to consider the persons of his hearers, lest he be derided before he is heard. *The Christ of God.* He embraced all things, who expressed both the nature and the name. For in the name of Christ there is the expression both of the godhead and of the incarnation, and the faith of the passion. The heretic, although he does not deny the name of Christ, nevertheless denies Christ, since he does not confess all the things that are Christ's.

VERS. 21.-- *He charged them lest,* etc. He bids the disciples be silent, that he may deceive the prince of the world, and that he may avoid boasting and teach humility. At the same time, lest the disciples, still rude [0279C] and imperfect, should be crushed by the weight of a greater preaching.

VERS. 23.-- *And he said.* The things that pertain to faith in the Lord's nativity or passion he opened to the disciples alone, apart; but these things to all. *Let him deny himself.* Unless a man fall away from himself, he does not draw near to him who is above himself: and he does not lay hold of the newness to which he is called, unless he slay the oldness in which he was born; and when he ceases from vices let him also seek out virtues. Whence there is subjoined:

VERS. 24.-- *For he that. [n: (GREG.)]* As in time of persecution the soul must be laid down, so in peace earthly desires must be broken, that by how much a man seems the more secure, by so much he may be the more careful for his own keeping.

VERS. 26.-- *Him the Son of man.* The voice of profession does not suffice for the [0279D] proof of faith, a voice which the profession of the generality defends from shame.

VERS. 27.-- *Some standing here.* He who stands with Christ does not taste death, because he who has merited fellowship with Christ will not have even the slightest sense of eternal death, and for him the order of living is not interrupted even in death. *Till they see the kingdom.* Because it was hard to offer the soul to dangers and the body to death, he sustains the weakness of the human mind by the reward of present things, lest it be broken by despair or weariness. For when the eternal glory has been seen, even in passing and for a brief moment, they are nevertheless rendered stronger against the adversities of the world. He promises therefore that the glory to come is to be seen in passing upon earth, that the eternal glory may be the more surely hoped for in heaven. Or [0280A] he promises that some of the disciples will see the spreading of the Church before they depart from the world by death, so that, raised up in the present against the glory of the world, they may be the more surely believed to be about to reign in heaven.

VERS. 28.-- *After these words.* Others, who count only six days, signify that after the six ages of the world the saints must rest from labour. Luke, who counts in both the first day on which it is promised and the last on which the promise is fulfilled, hints that we shall rise on the eighth. For as Christ, after the sixth day of the week on which he ascended the cross, and after the seventh day of the week on which he rested in the sepulchre, rose on the eighth day, so we, after the six ages of the world in which we suffer and labour for the Lord, and after the seventh of the rest of souls which meanwhile is passed in another life, shall rise in the eighth age. *He took Peter.* [0280B] He names three only, because *many are called, few chosen [n: (Matth. XX)]*, and those who keep the faith of the Trinity inviolate deserve to rejoice in the eternal vision. He who knows merits knows that the three apostles were more perfect than the rest. They signify those who have faith, hope, charity. Or by Peter, prelates or the married; by James, the penitent or the active; by John, virgins or the contemplative.

VERS. 29.-- *And he was made.* Being transfigured, he did not lose the substance of true flesh, but showed the glory either of his own or of our resurrection; and such as he then appeared to the apostles, such will he appear after the judgment to all the elect. For in the judgment itself he will appear both to the good and to the evil in the form of a servant, that they may see him whom they pierced. [0280C] *And his raiment.* The Lord's garments are the saints who have put on Christ. Which garment, while the Lord dwelt on earth, seemed contemptible and like that of others, but shines when the Lord ascends the mountain, because *it hath not yet appeared what we shall be, but at length we shall be like to him, when we shall see him as he is.* [var: Vulg. 1 John 3:2 "Scimus quoniam cum apparuerit, similes ei erimus: quoniam videbimus eum sicuti est," We know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like to him, because we shall see him as he is] Or the garments of the Word are the discourses of the Scriptures, and certain clothings of the divine understanding. Because as he himself appeared to those apostles in another form and his garment shone, so also to the eyes of thy mind the sense of the divine readings already grows white.

VERS. 30.-- *Behold two men.* It is to be noted that not the bodies or the souls of Elias or Moses appeared, but that those bodies were formed out of some underlying creature: it can also be believed that this was done [0280D] by angelic ministry, so that angels took upon them their persons. A dead man and a living man appear, that they may signify that Christ was to die and afterwards to live, and that we should live to Christ and die to the world. To the apostles also he gives a sign from heaven, that their faith may be increased. But to the scribes tempting him he would not give one, that they might be hardened. *And they were Moses and Elias, seen.* By Moses are signified those of the lower regions; by Elias, the heavenly; by the apostles, the earthly, who shall come to the judgment.

VERS. 31.-- *They spoke of his decease.* Moses, that is the lawgiver, and Elias, the most eminent of the prophets, appear with the Word, that they may show that he is the one who spoke in the law and the prophets, and whom the law and the prophets promised. Who were seen not in the lowest places, but on the mountain, [0281A] because only those lofty in mind perceive the majesty of Scripture, which was fulfilled in the Lord.

VERS. 32.-- *They were heavy with sleep.* They are weighed down with sleep, because the sense of the human body is oppressed by the splendour of the incomprehensible godhead. Or they are weighed down with sleep, that they might see the glory of the resurrection after the rest of the body; and the saints will see the majesty of the Lord the more truly in proportion as they rejoice in the immortality of their flesh, in which they had conquered death. Then they shall behold Moses and Elias in glory, because they will better understand how *one iota or one tittle shall not pass from the law [n: (Matth. V)]*, and how the Lord did not destroy the law and the prophets, but fulfilled them.
