## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And after these things,* etc. [n: (BED.)] Just as in the apostles there is the form of bishops, etc., *as far as* just as the sun is wont to accomplish the three-day circuit of its light in seventy-two hours.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He sent them two and two.* ⟦s2⟧ By this, that he sends them two and two, it is intimated that no one ought to take up the office of preaching who has not charity toward another. ⟦s3⟧ Or they are sent two by two according as the animals were sent into the ark two by two, that is, male and female, unclean before in carnal generation, but cleansed by the sacrament of the Church through spiritual grace in the preaching of the disciples.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0284B]VERS. 2.-- *The harvest indeed is great, but the labourers are few.* ⟦s2⟧ The harvest is the crowd of believers; the labourers are the apostles and those who follow them. ⟦s3⟧ And although the harvest has been sown by the word of God, still it requires the toil of tillage and the careful service of a labourer, lest the birds of heaven scatter the seeds that have been sown.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *As lambs among wolves.* ⟦s2⟧ As the wolf lies in wait for the sheep, so does the heretic for the faithful. ⟦s3⟧ And as the wolf goes about the sheepfold by night, not daring to enter, scouting out the dog's sleep, the shepherd's absence or sloth, so the heretic tries to deceive the faithful by the night of his own interpretation, not entering the Church, and strives either to avoid the pastors of the Church, or to kill them, or to send them into exile. ⟦s4⟧ The wolf, stiff by the nature of its body, cannot easily bend itself; so the heretic is not wont to call back the purpose [0284C] of his hard heart from error.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Carry therefore neither purse nor scrip.* ⟦s2⟧ So great ought a preacher's confidence in God to be, that even if he does not foresee the means of the present life, he yet knows most certainly that they will not fail him, lest, while his mind is taken up with things temporal, he preach the eternal things less. ⟦s3⟧ *Salute no man by the way.* ⟦s4⟧ He bids them make haste and press on, lest they be turned aside from the journey enjoined them by conversation with someone they meet. ⟦s5⟧ Nor does he say simply, *Salute no man,* since it is a usage of human kindness to salute others, but he added, *by the way,* because it is not the diligence of greeting that is taken away, but the obstacle that hinders devotion is done away with, so that, when divine things are commanded, human things may be set aside for a little.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And in the same house.* ⟦s2⟧ If your peace be [0284D] received, it is fitting that you remain in it, receiving carnal wages from those to whom you offer heavenly things. ⟦s3⟧ Behold, he who forbade purse and scrip grants means from preaching, yet in such a way that they be content with the food and drink set before them. ⟦s4⟧ *Remain.* ⟦s5⟧ There must be no removing from house to house with a roving readiness, so that constancy may be preserved in the love of hospitality, lest the close bond of friendship once grown up be easily dissolved. ⟦s6⟧ *For he is worthy.* ⟦s7⟧ Note that two wages are owed to the one work of preachers: one on the way, which sustains us in labour; another in the fatherland, which rewards us in the resurrection.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And into whatsoever city.* ⟦s2⟧ Having described the various lodging of a house, he teaches what they ought to do in cities as well: namely, to have fellowship in all things with the godly, [0285A] and to be kept apart in all things from the company of the ungodly.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Even the dust.* ⟦s2⟧ He bids them shake off the dust: either as a testimony of the earthly labour which they undertook for them in vain; or that they may show that to such a degree do they seek nothing earthly from them, that they do not even suffer the dust from their land to cleave to them.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *That for Sodom in that day.* ⟦s2⟧ The Sodomites, although they burned in vices and were inhospitable, yet among them no guests such as the prophets among the Jews, such as the apostles, were found. ⟦s3⟧ Lot, though just among them, still taught nothing, wrought no signs. ⟦s4⟧ And therefore *to whom much has been given, much is required of him, and the mighty shall mightily [0285B] suffer torments [n: (Sap. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Lc 12:48 *cui multum datum est, multum quaeretur ab eo*, future; the second clause is Sap 6:7, and the two are joined here under one citation].

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Woe to thee, Corozaim.* ⟦s2⟧ Corozaim, Bethsaida, Capharnaum and Tiberias are cities of Galilee, on the shore of the lake of Genesareth. ⟦s3⟧ These the Lord laments, because after so many miracles and mighty works they did not repent: and they are worse than the Gentiles, who squander only the natural law, because after their contempt of the written law they did not fear also to trample upon the Son of God and, ungrateful, to spurn his grace. ⟦s4⟧ *And sitting in ashes.* ⟦s5⟧ We see the words of the Saviour fulfilled, for whereas those foresaid cities, with the Lord present, would not believe, Tyre and Sidon were once friends of David and Solomon, and afterwards, when the disciples of Christ preached the Gospel, devoutly received the faith. ⟦s6⟧ But why it was preached to those who would not believe, and not preached [0285C] to those who would believe, he knows who knows all things.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *He that heareth you heareth me.* ⟦s2⟧ In hearing or in spurning a preacher of the Gospel, let each man know that he spurns or hears not persons of no account, but the Saviour himself, because in the disciple the master is heard, and in the Son the Father is honoured.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Lord, even.* ⟦s2⟧ They confess well, offering honour to the name of Christ: but because, their faith being still weak, they rejoice in mighty works and are lifted up by the working of signs, they are made afraid by an example and called back to humility. ⟦s3⟧ For if the devil was cast headlong from heaven for pride, much more shall these, brought forth from the earth, be humbled if they have been proud.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Upon serpents and scorpions.* ⟦s2⟧ Serpents [0285D] are evil men or demons who cast the venom of wicked persuasion against virtues at their beginning. ⟦s3⟧ Scorpions are those who strive to corrupt at the end virtues that are to be brought to completion.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Rejoice not in this, that spirits unto you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ To cast out evil spirits and to work other mighty deeds is sometimes not the merit of him who works them, but the invocation of the name of Christ does this, either to the condemnation of those who invoke it, or to the profit of those who see and hear: where, though men despise those who work the signs, they yet honour God, at whose invocation the miracles come to pass. ⟦s3⟧ He forbids them to rejoice over the humbling of the devil, who fell through pride, but let them rejoice over their own exaltation: [0286A] so that whence those, being lifted up, fell, these, being humbled, may ascend. ⟦s4⟧ *But rejoice.* ⟦s5⟧ If anyone has done heavenly or earthly works, by these he is, as though adorned with letters, fastened eternally in the memory of God.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *In that same hour.* ⟦s2⟧ When he was speaking of the subjection of unclean spirits and of the names written in heaven, he showed by what means so great a loftiness was given to men, namely by the humility of the Christian faith. ⟦s3⟧ *I confess to thee, O Lord.* ⟦s4⟧ I give thanks, because thou hast opened to the apostles the sacraments of my coming, which the scribes and Pharisees, who seem wise to themselves, do not know. ⟦s5⟧ *Thou hast revealed them.* ⟦s6⟧ He does not say, to the unwise and dull, but to little ones, that is, to the humble, to prove that he condemned swelling, not sharpness. ⟦s7⟧ *For so.* ⟦s8⟧ He does not render a reason why he chose [0286B] these and reprobated those, but says that it so pleased God. ⟦s9⟧ In which we receive an example of humility, that we should not rashly discuss the counsels above.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *All things are delivered to me,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Humility being commended, through which one comes to faith, a passage concerning that same faith is beautifully joined on, when he says that all things are delivered to him, not the elements which he himself created, but those little ones to whom the Father revealed the sacraments of the Son. ⟦s3⟧ When thou hearest all things, thou acknowledgest one almighty, not discoloured, not degenerate from the Father. ⟦s4⟧ When thou hearest delivered, thou confessest the Son, to whom by the nature of one substance all things are by right his own, not conferred as a gift through grace.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *That many prophets.* ⟦s2⟧ The prophets and the just saw the glory of the Lord from afar, through a glass [0286C] and in a dark manner; but the apostles, having the Lord present and asking of him what they would, were not taught through angels or through various kinds of visions.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *A lawyer stood up.* ⟦s2⟧ The doctor of the law, who was lying in the law without faith, rises that he may stand: he stands while he calls him Master, but even in standing he tempts God by keeping silent about what he was.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *He answering said.* ⟦s2⟧ He said what he had read, but did not fulfil what was written, because he had present with him the Lord whom he ought to love, yet he calls him Master, not God.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *But he, willing.* ⟦s2⟧ The lawyer, that he may be said to have answered wisely, first confesses that he is ignorant of the commandment of the law, because, refusing to be humbled and willing to justify himself, he cannot see the hidden things [0286D] that are revealed to the little ones of Christ; to whom Christ so tempers his answer that both everyone who does mercy may be understood to be a neighbour, and especially the Son of God himself, who through his humanity has been made neighbour to us, may be designated. ⟦s3⟧ *Who is my.* ⟦s4⟧ No one is nearer to man than God, who knows him within and without and can care for all things; but every unbeliever or tempter has neither God nor man for a neighbour.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A certain man,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] That man is understood to be Adam in the human race, etc., *as far as* he was nearer than the priest or the Levite of the same nation.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *And it happened,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The priest announces the law of God: the law indeed came down through Moses [0287A] into the world, and conferred no health upon such a one. ⟦s3⟧ The Levite came down, who shows the type of the prophets, but he too heals no one, because the law reproves sins, but passes by, since it does not bestow pardon.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Being moved with mercy.* ⟦s2⟧ This is not said of the priest and the Levite, because the law has not mercy, but judgment and vengeance.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *He brought him to an inn.* ⟦s2⟧ He calls it not a house but a stable, by which name he marks the miseries and the stenches of this life, lest a man rejoice in this exile as though in his fatherland.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *He that did.* ⟦s2⟧ Kinship does not make a neighbour, but mercy, which mercy is according to nature. ⟦s3⟧ For nothing is so according to nature as to help a partner of one's nature.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0287B]VERS. 38.-- *It came to pass, as,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ A discourse having been held about the love of God and of neighbour, an example of both loves is set down, not so much in word alone as in the showing forth of work. ⟦s3⟧ For by these two sisters two spiritual lives are signified. ⟦s4⟧ By Martha, a devotion busy in works, by which we are joined to our neighbour in charity. ⟦s5⟧ By Mary, the religious intention of the mind upon the word of God, by which we sigh in the love of God. ⟦s6⟧ The active life gives bread, either bodily to one that hungers, or of doctrine to one that is ignorant; it corrects the erring, calls back the proud to humility, dispenses what is expedient for each. ⟦s6⟧ The contemplative holds fast the charity of God and of neighbour, but rests from outward action, cleaves to the desire of the Creator alone, and, all cares being trodden underfoot, burns to see the face of the Creator, and longs to be mingled [0287C] with the citizens above, who rejoice in eternal incorruption in the sight of the Lord. ⟦s7⟧ *Into her house.* ⟦s8⟧ When Jesus enters into the house, the wicked life, even if once it had been there, flees away; there remain two innocent lives, the laborious and the leisured, and between them, in the midst, is the very fount of life.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Sitting beside.* ⟦s2⟧ The more humbly she sits, the more abundantly does it fall. ⟦s3⟧ Water flows together into the valley from the swellings of the hill. ⟦s4⟧ Mary sits, because the contemplative life, the tumults of the vices being stilled, now enjoys the longed-for quiet of the mind in Christ. ⟦s5⟧ Martha stands, because the laborious active life sweats in the contest.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *But Martha.* ⟦s2⟧ Mary was intent on how she might be fed by the Lord; Martha was intent on how she might feed the Lord: the latter prepares a banquet for the Lord, in the Lord's banquet the former already takes her delight.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0287D]VERS. 41.-- *And answering.* ⟦s2⟧ Mary does not answer, but, as one at leisure, commits her cause to the judge. ⟦s3⟧ For if she were preparing words to answer, she would slacken her intentness on hearing. ⟦s4⟧ *Martha, Martha.* ⟦s5⟧ The repetition is a token of love, or perhaps of an intention to admonish, that she might listen more attentively.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *But one thing is.* ⟦s2⟧ The one is set before the many. ⟦s3⟧ For the one is not from the many, but the many from the one; and yet many things are necessary to those who tend toward the one. ⟦s4⟧ *Mary the best.* ⟦s5⟧ Martha's part is not blamed, since it too is good, but Mary's part is praised, and why it is best is added below.
