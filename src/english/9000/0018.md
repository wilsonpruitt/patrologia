## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass.* After the history of the sisters [0288A] (who signify the two lives) he brings in the Lord as having prayed, and as having instructed the disciples to pray, because both the prayer which he taught contains in itself the mystery of either life, and the perfection of those lives is to be obtained not by our own strength but by prayers. *When he ceased.* For this reason the Saviour is often brought in praying, that he may lead the disciples on to the pursuit of prayer. Whence, the prayer being ended, he is asked by the disciples, that he may teach how they ought to pray.

VERS. 5.-- *Which of you hath a friend.* After he had set forth the form of prayer to those who asked, he lays upon them also instancy and frequency in praying, so that prayer may be offered not only by day but also by night; for he who at midnight asks loaves of his friend, and persists in the intention of asking, is not cheated of his prayer.

VERS. 7.-- *Trouble me not.* He does not take away the hope of obtaining, [0288B] but the more vehemently kindles the desire of praying, by showing the difficulty of obtaining. *The door is now.* The door which Paul asks to be opened to him for the speaking of the mystery of Christ, and which John saw opened to him, is the understanding of the divine word, which is shut in the time of a famine of the word of God, when understanding is not given. And the children, that is, those who, dealing out evangelical wisdom like bread, preached it through the whole world, are now with the Lord in eternal rest; and yet by praying it is brought about that he who prays receives understanding from the Lord himself, even if there be lacking a man through whom wisdom may be preached. *I cannot rise. [n: (AMBR.)]* No one fears that he may waken a sleeper, whom he knows to be always watching in prayer.

VERS. 8.-- *Yet because of his importunity,* etc. [0288C] Many are the snares, and heavy is the sleep of the body, because if the mind begins to sleep, it loses the vigour of its own strength. Shake off therefore thy sleep, that thou mayest knock at the door of Christ, that when thou hast entered there may be opened to thee the treasures hidden and in darkness in Christ Jesus.

VERS. 9.-- *Ask, and it shall be given you.* If a man who is a friend rises and gives, compelled not by friendship but by weariness, how much more does God give, who without weariness most bountifully bestows what is asked. Lest therefore the soul, turned back from the vanity of error, waste away any longer in spiritual want, let us ask the banquet of the word, let us seek the friend who may give, let us knock at the door where hidden things are kept. For he gives great hope, who in promising does not deceive. *For every one that asketh receiveth.* There is need of perseverance, that we may obtain. But if it is given to him that asks, and he that seeks [0288D] finds, and to him that knocks it is opened, then as for him to whom it is not given, and who does not find, and to whom it is not opened, it appears that he has not sought and asked and knocked well.

VERS. 10.-- *Seek, and you shall find.--The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him in truth, and he will do the will of them that fear him [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*. Others, who do not so ask, James rebukes, saying: *You ask and receive not, because you ask amiss [n: (Jac. IV)]*, because you persevere in your sins.

VERS. 11.-- *And which of you, if he ask his father bread, will he give him a stone,* etc. [n: (BED.)] Mystically. Bread signifies charity, on account of the greater appetite for it and its necessary use, etc., *as far as* let him stretch himself forth to those things which are before.

[0289A]VERS. 13.-- *How much more.* An open comparison. For if a sinful man, still weighed down with frail flesh, does not refuse to give temporal things to his sons when they ask, much more does the heavenly Father bestow in heaven goods that do not fail upon sons endowed with his fear and his love.

VERS. 14.-- *And he was casting out.* After the words of preaching which he set before the humble, he works miracles also, so that those who do not believe him preaching may believe when they have seen miracles. Matthew says that this demoniac was blind as well. In whom three miracles are wrought by the Lord: he sees, he speaks, he is freed from the demon. And what was then done carnally in one man is done daily in the conversion of the Gentiles, so that first, the demon being cast out along with all the filth of idolatry, they may see the light of faith, and then mouths that were silent before may be loosed to the praise of God.

[0289B]VERS. 15.-- *In Beelzebub.* King Ninus, the founder of Nineveh, consecrated a statue to Belus his father, and appointed divine honours for him; and the Chaldeans, receiving the likeness of that image, called it Bel. The Palestinians, receiving it, called it Baal, the Moabites Beelphegor. But the Jews, worshippers of the one God, called it Beelzebub for a derision of the Gentiles. For Baal means man, Zebub a fly; whence Beelzebub, that is, the man of flies, or one having flies, on account of the filth of the gore that was sacrificed in his temple. The Jews also asserted that the prince of demons dwelt in this image, by whose power they said that Jesus drove out demons.

VERS. 18.-- *But he, when he saw their thoughts.* This the Lord has reserved as proper to himself, that he should behold [0289C] and judge thoughts. It is proper to the Jews to seek signs, whence Paul: *The Jews require signs, the Gentiles wisdom [n: (I Cor. I)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 1:22 *et Judaei signa petunt, et Graeci sapientiam quaerunt*, "and the Greeks seek after wisdom"]. *But he, when he saw.* He answers not to their words but to their thoughts: in which he gives a sure sign of his own power, by which he searches out the secrets of hearts. According to the letter it is plain that every kingdom, solid and firm, if it is divided into parts, is reduced to a wilderness, emptied of its inhabitants; and house shall fall upon house, when the inhabitants of one house are either oppressed or punished by the household of another house. But whither these things tend spiritually he sets forth when he adds: *And if Satan also be divided against himself.* If I, as you say, cast out demons in the prince of demons, then the kingdom of the devil is divided against itself, and so the power of his malice [0289D] cannot stand. But if a demon cannot drive out a demon, you say falsely that I cast out demons in the prince of demons. But if he can, as you say, look to yourselves, that you go out from his kingdom which is divided, lest you be involved alike in its ruin. But by what power the Lord casts out demons, lest they think him the prince of demons, let them attend to what follows.

VERS. 19.-- *But if I in Beelzebub cast out demons, your sons.* He calls the apostles the sons of the Jews, who among the other miracles they had received from the Lord drove out demons also. Which casting out they assigned not to the devil but to God, being conscious to themselves that they had learned nothing of evil arts from the Lord. Therefore they themselves, choosing the ignoble and contemptible things of the world, in which [0290A] there was no crafty malice, shall sit *upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.* Or, *sons,* that is, the exorcists of that nation, who by invoking the name of the Lord drove out demons. As though he said: If the casting out of demons in them is imputed not to the devil but to God, why should not the same work in me have the same cause? *Therefore they shall be your judges.* Not by the power of judgment, but by comparison, since they assign the casting out not to the devil but to God. *But if.* As though he said: A divided kingdom cannot stand, but the kingdom of Satan is divided, as you say, in that Satan drives out Satan; therefore, etc.

VERS. 20.-- *But if by the finger.* The Son is called the hand or the arm of the Father; the Holy Ghost is called the finger, on account of the difference of the various gifts which are given [0290B] to men through the Holy Ghost, as the fingers are distinct one from another; nor does the inequality of the members do harm. For although the arm is greater than the finger, yet the arm with the finger is one body. By this finger the law was written on the tables. This finger the magicians confessed in *Aegyto.* *Assuredly is come,* etc., *the kingdom.* That is, the judiciary power *of God:* by which he will separate the good from the evil in the day of judgment. Or, *Kingdom,* that is, the beatitude above. As though he said: If I cast out demons in the Spirit of God, know without doubt that the entrance of the heavenly kingdom is laid open to them that believe.

VERS. 21.-- *When the strong man his court.* As though he said: If I, as you say, were casting out demons in Beelzebub, then I should be consenting in something to the works of the devil; but in no way do I consent with him, but rather I hasten to overcome him and to cast him out [0290C] from my faithful ones. *When* the devil, *the strong man,* to do harm, *armed,* with many spiritual wickednesses, *keepeth,* in his own servitude, *his court,* that is the world, which is set in wickedness, in which, until the coming of the Son of God, the devil bore rule without contradiction, and used an ill-pacified sovereignty in the hearts of the unbelieving.

VERS. 22.-- *But if a stronger.* He calls himself the stronger, who laid him low not by a concordant peace and working, as they slanderously alleged, but by a mightier power, and freed the world from his dominion. The arms of the devil which he took away are the most crafty wiles of spiritual wickedness. The spoils are the souls deceived by him, which Christ the victor distributes, which is the mark of one triumphing, because *leading captivity captive, he gave gifts to men, [0290D] some indeed apostles, others evangelists (Ephes, IV)*, etc. [var: Vulg. Eph 4:8 *captivam duxit captivitatem*, a finite verb; and Eph 4:11 reads *quosdam quidem apostolos, quosdam autem prophetas, alios vero evangelistas* — the prophets are absent here].

VERS. 23.-- *He that is not,* etc. And truly my works do not agree with the works of Satan, because I myself in nothing agree with him, but we are altogether contrary, because I am humble, kindly, willing to save souls; he is proud, envious, desirous to destroy. I by my preaching gather in the virtues; he scatters and severs from the unity of the Church. *And he that gathereth not.* These things may over and above be understood of all heretics or schismatics, who are all shut out from the kingdom of God.

VERS. 24.-- *When the unclean spirit.* Because you withdraw yourselves from my kingdom, attributing my works to the devil, [0291A] therefore the devil dwells in you, as in his own kingdom, more grievously than at the beginning. *Is gone out.* The devil went out from the Jews, when that people received the law and the worship of the one God, and he passed over to the Gentiles, who were dry and barren of the fatness of the Holy Ghost and of the love of neighbour. In whom now he does not find rest, because now he is driven out from the hearts of the Gentiles when the faith of Christ has been received, when he says: I will go back to the Jews whom I formerly left. And he finds that people swept with brooms, that is, with the superfluous observances of the Pharisees and the ceremonies of the law, which now avail nothing after the coming of Christ. And that he may possess it the more firmly, he takes to himself the whole company of demons. And the Israelites are now worse, blaspheming Christ in their little assemblies, than they had once been in Egypt before the law was received, because it is a greater unbelief [0291B] not to receive him when he comes than not to believe in him who is to come.

*Seeking rest, and he findeth not.* Because he who feeds upon depraved thoughts and deeds perceives that in the minds of the faithful all his temptations come to nothing. Then he says: *I will return into my house whence I came out.* I will return to that man's conscience, whence I had been cast out in baptism, and I will subject my former possession to my accustomed lordship.

VERS. 25.-- *And when he is come he findeth it swept with brooms.* Because when he has gone back to a reprobate of this kind, his offences compelling it, he finds the conscience of the sinner cleansed from vices in baptism, but heaped up with no good work.

VERS. 26.-- *Then he taketh seven.* The fulness [0291C] of all the vices. Which spirits, namely such as these, are called *more wicked* than the devil, because, though the devil is evil, yet those who, driven on by their own deserts, are made over to vices, are not only evil, but strive besides to make a display of the appearance of goodness through hypocrisy by affected virtues. *And the last state of the man becometh.* It were better to be ignorant of the way of truth than after acknowledging it to be turned back again.
