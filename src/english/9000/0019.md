## CHAPTER XI. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Blessed.* ⟦s2⟧ Here Mary is praised, who bore the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ And the perversity both of the Jews then present and of the heretics to come, who deny the true humanity of Christ, is confuted, because she confesses the true Son of God against the blaspheming Jews, and testifies him a true son of man, consubstantial with his mother. ⟦s4⟧ For unless he were one flesh with his mother, in vain [0291D] would that womb of the Virgin and those breasts be called blessed. ⟦s5⟧ *And the paps.* ⟦s6⟧ According to the natural philosophers, from the same fount there flows both milk for children to be nourished and seed for children to be begotten; therefore he could be conceived of the seed of the Virgin who could be nourished by her milk.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Yea rather.* ⟦s2⟧ As though he said: Not only is Mary to be praised, because she bore the Word of God in her womb, but she is most of all blessed, because she kept the precepts of God in her work. ⟦s3⟧ So also all are blessed who conceive the word of God by the hearing of faith, and bring forth and nourish the keeping of good work in their own heart or in their neighbours'. ⟦s4⟧ *Who hear.* ⟦s5⟧ In praise of the Church. ⟦s6⟧ On the contrary the Pharisees are condemned, who sought neither to hear nor to fulfil the word of God, but to blaspheme it.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0292A]VERS. 29.-- *He began to say.* ⟦s2⟧ Certain men were slandering his works, to whom he has replied thus far. ⟦s3⟧ Others, tempting him, were seeking a sign from heaven, and to these he replies here. ⟦s4⟧ *A sign is sought, and a sign shall not be given to it,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ To the disciples he gave a sign of his divinity, both when he was transfigured before on the mountain, and afterwards when, as they looked on, he was lifted up into heaven. ⟦s6⟧ But to unbelievers, a sign from the lower passion of his humanity.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *The queen of the south shall rise.* ⟦s2⟧ Here, the people of the Jews being condemned, the mystery of the Church is set forth, which is gathered together from all the ends of the world: in the Ninevites through penance (which does away with sin), in the queen of the south the zeal of receiving wisdom (which guards against sin), that she may know the words of the true Peaceable One. ⟦s3⟧ For the Church stands upon two things: that thou either cease to sin, [0292B] or know not how to sin. ⟦s4⟧ *In the judgment.* ⟦s5⟧ Not a thousand years before the judgment, as the Jews feign, but in the judgment itself shall she rise. ⟦s6⟧ *With the men. [n: (BED.)]* Behold, a common resurrection is shown, of the good as well as of the evil. ⟦s7⟧ *Shall condemn.* ⟦s8⟧ Not by the power of judgment, but by the comparison of a better deed, because, leaving her empire behind, she came into Judea to hear a man famed for wisdom, whereas the Jews do not hear but blaspheme the true Wisdom of God dwelling among them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the preaching.* ⟦s2⟧ Jonas preached for a few days, Christ for a long time. ⟦s3⟧ He to strangers, Christ to his own fellow-citizens. ⟦s4⟧ He wrought no sign, Christ wrought many.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *No man. [n: (BED.)]* He who in the figure of the queen and of the Ninevites set the Church before the Synagogue, etc., *as far as* they will not enter by believing the open gate of the light.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0292C]VERS. 35.-- *Take heed therefore lest,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ After the instruction of right faith he instructs also concerning good works and good intention, so that not only works but also the thoughts and intentions of the heart may be made clean; as though he said: I kindle the lamp of faith in your senses and set it in the Church. ⟦s3⟧ But you, so far as in you lies, kindle your own lamps, that is, cleanse the intentions of your heart, that from a clean intention your works also may be pleasing. ⟦s4⟧ These things, spoken particularly against the hypocrisy of the Pharisees who craftily sought signs, instruct us generally according to the moral sense.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *And as he was speaking.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not say, as he was speaking these things, but *as he was speaking.* ⟦s3⟧ In which it is intimated that he turned aside to the Pharisee not immediately when these words were finished, but with other things coming between, because when this [0292D] discourse was finished, while he was still speaking, behold his mother and his brethren were seeking to speak to him. ⟦s4⟧ Concerning whom, after he had answered the man who announced them: *He that doth the will of my Father, he is my brother and sister and mother,* [var: Vulg. Mt 12:50 *Quicumque enim fecerit voluntatem Patris mei, qui in caelis est, ipse meus frater* — Migne's opening and *hic frater meus* follow Mc 3:35, which however reads *voluntatem Dei*] he afterwards went in to the Pharisee's banquet.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Why not washed.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jews frequently wash their hands before they eat, holding the tradition of the elders, and returning from the market place they do not eat unless they be washed.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Of the cup.* ⟦s2⟧ Mark relates that the Jews were wont to observe the washings of cups and pitchers and beds and brazen vessels. ⟦s3⟧ The cup is a glass vessel; the dish is earthenware, of clay. ⟦s4⟧ By this is figured the frailty of the human body, in which outwardly they make a show of holiness [0293A] and feign justice: what was without they washed, within most wicked. ⟦s5⟧ *But that which is within you.* ⟦s6⟧ Let no one think that fornication and bodily vices of this kind are alone grievous, and that spiritual vices are light, such as avarice, anger, pride, and the rest, as the Pharisees thought.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *But yet that which remaineth.* ⟦s2⟧ Now the good teacher teaches how the contagion of the body ought to be cleansed. ⟦s3⟧ And here is more fully set out what he had said briefly above about cleansing the eye of the heart. ⟦s4⟧ For this whole passage is directed to this end, that it may invite us to the pursuit of simplicity, and condemn the superfluous and earthly things of the Jews, who by understanding the law according to the body are likened to the glass and the dish because of their frailty. ⟦s5+6⟧ In which, although they sin grievously, yet to them the absolution of sin is promised, if they strive to do mercy. [0293B] What remains over from necessary food and clothing, give to the poor. ⟦s7⟧ Or, I give counsel: what alone is left after so great crimes, give alms. ⟦s8⟧ The first alms is to heal your own souls by believing in me who cleanse hearts; and when hearts have been cleansed through faith, all outward things also will be clean. ⟦s9⟧ He who wishes to give alms in an ordered way begins from himself, and gives it first to himself. ⟦s10⟧ For alms is a work of mercy, whence it is said: *Have mercy on thy own soul, pleasing God.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *You pass over judgment.* ⟦s2⟧ He has briefly touched upon many of their vices, who, intent upon tithing worthless things, have not the fear of the judgment to come nor the charity of God. ⟦s3⟧ They pass over judgment, because they do not refer all [0293C] that they do to the judgment. ⟦s4⟧ They pass over charity, because they do not love God from affection. ⟦s5⟧ But again, lest it make you, zealous for faith, negligent of works, he sets down the perfection of the faithful man, that after faith he may work.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 44.-- *Who are as monuments.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he rebukes the superstition of those same teachers, who outwardly make a show of the appearance of right doctrine, but hide the foulness which they carry within.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 45.-- *Answering, however.* ⟦s2⟧ Hearing the word of God, the Pharisee out of an evil conscience thinks that an insult is being done to him, and, the punishment of the faithless being called to mind, understands that he is to be condemned.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *Woe to you.* ⟦s2⟧ They are not reproved because they adorn the monuments of the prophets, but because they imitate the slayers [0293D] of the prophets; by building sepulchres they accuse their fathers' crimes. ⟦s3⟧ But by persecuting Christ and the apostles they imitate the crimes of their fathers, and the sentence of damnation which they were pronouncing upon their fathers they turn back upon themselves.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 48.-- *Truly.* ⟦s2⟧ You build for the sake of catching the favour of the common people, as though you shuddered at their crimes; but by the very work you show that you consent to their treachery, because you do not do this good thing well, but for vainglory. ⟦s3⟧ *But you.* ⟦s4⟧ For this reason: because you are imitators of them, that you may fill up what was wanting to your fathers, namely that you may persecute me and mine, whom they persecuted in the prophets.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 49.-- *Prophets and apostles, and some of them they will kill, [0294A] and persecute.* ⟦s2⟧ Heretics lie, who say that the founder of the Old Testament is one and of the New another.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 50.-- *From the generation.* ⟦s2⟧ All the evil are one generation, one city, one body of the devil, as all the good are called one generation and one body.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 51.-- *As far as the blood of Zacharias.* ⟦s2⟧ Why as far as this man, when after him many were slain before the birth of Christ, and, Christ being born, the children were straightway destroyed by this generation? ⟦s3⟧ But because Abel was a shepherd of sheep, and was slain in the field, and Zacharias a priest slain in the court of the temple, they signify two orders of martyrs, the laity and those who are made over to the service of the altar.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0294B]VERS. 52.-- *Because you have taken away the key.* ⟦s2⟧ The key is the humility of Christ, and they who have it understand the Scriptures. ⟦s3⟧ This the lawyers neither themselves understand by the law and the prophets, nor do they wish it to be understood by others. ⟦s4⟧ To enter the key is not to be content with the surface of the letter but to penetrate as far as the hidden things that are to be understood. ⟦s5⟧ Or: a teacher, if he scandalizes by his example those whom he builds up by his word, neither himself enters the kingdom of God and shuts out those who were able to enter.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 53.-- *And as he was saying these things unto them, the Pharisees and the lawyers began.* ⟦s2⟧ How true were the charges of their treachery and dissimulation and impiety which they heard, they themselves bear witness, who at so great a thundering whirlwind do not come to their senses, but assail the teacher of truth.
