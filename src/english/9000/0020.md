## CHAPTER XII.

[0294C]VERS. 1.-- *Beware.* To this leaven belong all the things which he disputed above in the house of the Pharisee; whence also the Apostle: *Not with the old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice [n: (I Cor. V)]*, etc. *Beware of the leaven.* A beautiful passage for the holding of simplicity and the emulating of faith, that we may not after the manner of the Jews put forth one thing in affection and feign another with the voice, since we know that in the day of judgment there can be no hiding-place for fraud.

VERS. 2.-- *Nor hidden, that shall not be known.* Therefore do not emulate dissemblers, because assuredly the time will come, in the day of judgment, in which both your virtue and their hypocrisy shall be revealed to all; whence there follows. *For the things which you have spoken in darkness shall be said in the light.* Not only in the world to come shall all things lie open, but even in the present time they shall be publicly preached in the whole Church throughout the world [0294D]: the things which in darkness, that is, in the shadow of oppressions and of prisons, you have spoken, when your acts shall be read on high. *For the things which you have spoken in darkness shall be said in the light. [n: (AMBR.)]* What you have spoken in darkness, that is, in fear, shall be said in the light, that is, in the confidence of truth when the Holy Ghost has been received. And what you have spoken in the ear, that is, in secret, shall be preached upon the housetops, that is, when the dwelling-place of the flesh has been trodden underfoot.

VERS. 4.-- *And I say to you. [n: (ID.)]* Since the cause of faithlessness is twofold, etc., *as far as* so fortitude is the foundation of faith.

VERS. 5.-- *But I will show you.* Because there are two kinds of persecutors, one of those who persecute openly, the other of those who fraudulently flatter. As above [0295A] against hypocrisy, so here he arms against the executioners. This whole passage instructs unto the undergoing of suffering for the confession of the Lord, either by contempt of death, or by hope of reward, or those who will abide, in the denunciation of punishment.

VERS. 6.-- *Are not five. [n: (AMBR.)]* Five sparrows, the five senses of the body; or two according to Matthew, the body and the soul, etc., *as far as* there is no doubt that the contemplation of the faithful will prevail with him.

*They are sold for two farthings.* A dipondius is a weight composed of two asses. What in numbers is one, that in weights is the as; what is two, that is the dipondius. *And one of,* etc. He had breathed in the disposition of simplicity, he had raised up the strength of the mind, faith alone was wavering; this he strengthens from the meaner things. As the Apostle says: [0295B] *Doth God take care for oxen? [n: (I Cor. IX.)]* But care is one thing, knowledge another.

VERS. 7.-- *Yea, and the hairs.* Not by the act of counting, but by the faculty of knowing, from the likeness of money which is numbered that it may be kept. Wherein is the great providence of God, that neither small nor idle words lie hidden from him.

VERS. 8.-- *Every one whosoever.* Lest anyone in the time of persecution should think the faith of the heart sufficient for himself, he says: *Every one whosoever shall confess.* A man confesses Jesus with that charity which works through love; he denies who does not obey the commandments. *The Son of man.* The Son confesses before the Father him who through the Son has access to the Father. He is denied by the Son before the Father, to whom the Son and the Father are not made manifest in the power of the Godhead. And lest one condition be thought to belong to all, [0295C] both to those who deny of set purpose and to those who deny out of weakness or ignorance, he adds: *And every one that speaketh a word in the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him.*

VERS. 10.-- *But to him.* To him also who, being scandalized in the weakness of the flesh, shall have suspected me to be a mere man or a glutton, it shall be remissible; but he who, not acknowledging the grace of the Holy Ghost (by which penance is inspired and a return is made to the congregation), shall have remained with an impenitent heart, since this is now not human but devilish, for him it shall be irremissible.

VERS. 11.-- *And when they shall bring you into the synagogues, and to magistrates and powers.* The terror of such assemblies is wont to take away freedom of speech. *Be not solicitous.* Offer your will for Christ. But Christ will speak for himself.

[0295D]VERS. 13.-- *Master.* Upon one who was commending the grace of the peace and unity above, this man wishes to thrust the trouble of an earthly division; whence also he is called a man, that is, earthly. For those among whom there is a zeal and contention of this kind are men, and walk according to man. Among brethren it is not a judge in the midst but family affection as arbitress that ought to divide a patrimony, although the patrimony of immortality is not money to be sought by men.

VERS. 14.-- *Who hath appointed me.* I am not the God of dissension but of peace and unity, who came to make men at peace with the angels, that many may have one heart and one soul. Not that they may be divided, but that they may have all things in common, and that there be not anyone in need among them. [0296A] He who does not gather with me is a divider of brotherhood and an author of dissension.

VERS. 15.-- *Of all covetousness.* No one would think it a crime to divide an inheritance, to gather fruits into barns; but the judge above declares that this too is covetousness, whence also he adds: *of a certain rich man.* As above against the Pharisees and hypocrites, so here, on the occasion of a foolish petition, he disputes against covetousness. *Not in abundance. Man liveth not by bread alone, but in the word of God [n: (Deut. VIII)]* [var: Vulg. Deut 8:3 *non in solo pane vivat homo, sed in omni verbo quod egreditur de ore Dei*, "in every word that goeth forth from the mouth of God"], nor is temporal life prolonged by a multitude of riches.

VERS. 16.-- *And he spoke,* etc. After the precept he subjoins an example for the avoiding of covetousness for temporal things. *The plentiful fruits of a certain rich man.* The rich man is not blamed because he tilled the ground, or laid up his fruits [0296B] in barns, but because he placed the confidence of his life in them, and did not lay them out upon the poor, that he might be received by them into everlasting dwellings.

VERS. 17.-- *What shall I do,* etc. Reckoning the fruits to be his own and the goods his own, he does not lay out upon the poor what is over and above, but is eager to keep it for his own luxury against the time to come, and knows not who will make use of what he has gathered.

VERS. 19.-- *My goods, and I will say. [n: (AMBR.)]* Those are not a man's goods which he cannot carry away with him. For mercy alone is the companion of the dead.

*Take thy rest.* Behold, to the plague of covetousness is joined the plague of sloth, so that he did not even call upon God for temporal things.

VERS. 20.-- *But he said.* For God to speak is to check a man's depraved contrivances by a sudden chastisement. *Thou fool.* Because thou wast promising thyself long seasons [0296C] of riches, this night, snatched away, thou shalt leave to others what thou hast gathered. *They require,* etc. In the night the soul is taken away, which in the darkness of the heart would not foresee what it could suffer.

VERS. 21.-- *And is not rich toward God.* He is rich toward God who, despising things transitory, distributes to the poor; whose expectation is the Lord; whose substance, that is, the possession of a conscience by which he is sustained and fed, is with God, not in the purses of earth.

VERS. 22.-- *And he said to his disciples.* He strengthens the apostles through faith in the hope of things eternal, because there are many whom the love of things present and the despair of things to come make sluggish. *Be not solicitous.* It would be more perfect to give all to the poor, and to make friends of the mammon of iniquity; but because for the most part the devotion of faith is called back by weakness, [0296D] he does not forbid foresight, by which bread is prepared in the sweat of the face, but he forbids the solicitude which disturbs the mind and calls it back from things eternal.

VERS. 24.-- *Consider.* The saints are deservedly compared to the birds, who, having nothing in the world and not labouring, seek eternal things by contemplation alone, being already like the angels. *How much more.* If to the birds, which do not labour, nor claim the common fruits for themselves more particularly by any lordship, the providence of God bestows what is needful, it is true that the cause of our want is shown to be covetousness, in that we claim common things as though our own, and grudge them to others.

VERS. 26.-- *If then neither.* If you cannot work even in the increase [0297A] of your own body, an operation which is the least thing for God in comparison with the spiritual creature, why are you solicitous about the rest, which are greater, that is, about the preservation of the soul.

VERS. 27.-- *Consider the lilies.* An open comparison. Lilies do not, like other fruits, require any tillage of husbandmen. By the lily also the heavenly beatitude is wont to be signified, to which the Lord invites us. Lilies are born not in the woods but in gardens; so we too ought to be born in the gardens of the virtues. *Nor Solomon. [n: (AMBR.)]* Solomon is here said to be clothed, and elsewhere to be covered with glory: he who used to clothe with the glory of works the weakness of bodily nature, covered over as it were with a certain strength of mind.

*Was clothing as one.* What purple of kings, what [0297B] painting of weavers can be compared to flowers? The colour itself is called the garment of the flower, as it is said: Redness has covered it.

VERS. 28.-- *To-morrow into the oven.* To-morrow in the Scriptures is put for the future, as Jacob says: *My justice shall hear me to-morrow [n: (Gen. XXX)]* [var: Vulg. Gen 30:33 *Respondebitque mihi cras justitia mea*, "and my justice shall answer for me"].

VERS. 29.-- *Seek not what you shall eat, or what you shall drink.* He does not say: Seek not, or be not solicitous about food or clothing, but more expressly *what you shall eat or what you shall drink.* In which those seem to be reproved who, despising the common food or clothing, seek finer or harsher meats or garments than those among whom they live. *Be not lifted up on high.* Solicitude for food or clothing, which are for necessity, being forbidden, he forbids also the pride which is wont to follow from these things when they are superabundant: therefore do not seek these things, because such solicitude [0297C] makes men like the unbelieving.

VERS. 30.-- *And all these things.* Because all things belong to the sons, therefore all these things shall be added to you, even when you do not seek them, so that neither in the present nor in the future may any grace be lacking to the faithful; from whom, if these things are taken away, it is for a testing; if they are given, it is for a giving of thanks, because *all things work together unto good for the good.* [var: Vulg. Rom 8:28 *diligentibus Deum omnia cooperantur in bonum*, "to them that love God"]

VERS. 32.-- *Fear not.* Seek the kingdom, and do not distrust concerning the kingdom; because the Father with a well-pleased goodness will give you the kingdom, not by your own merits. *Little.* Either on account of the comparison with the greater number of the reprobate, or rather on account of the devotion of humility, through which humility the Church grows, through which one comes to the kingdom.

[0297D]VERS. 33.-- *Sell.* Not only should you not fear that the necessaries of life may be lacking to those who fight for the kingdom's sake, but sell also what you possess for the sake of alms, which is the way to the kingdom, since through it sins are redeemed. Not only share your food with the poor, but sell also your possessions, so that, when all that is yours has once been despised for the Lord's sake, you may afterwards work with the labour of your hands, whence you may live or may give alms. *Give alms.* He who for heaven's sake despises all worldly things, let him sell what he has and distribute it; he who is not of so great virtue, let him give alms out of what he has. *That faileth not.* Temporal things either fail of their own frailty, or, if anything solid remains, such as precious stones, it can be taken away by thieves. [0298A] But things given for Christ's sake bring an eternal fruit in the heavens.

VERS. 34.-- *For where your treasure is.* Universally, both of money and of all pleasures. The glutton's treasure is his belly, where he has his heart; and so of the rest. For if it is on earth, the heart is below; if in things heavenly, it is fixed in Christ. For it is necessary that where the treasure of love has gone before, there the affection of thought should follow.

VERS. 35.-- *Let your loins be,* etc., *and lamps.* The one without the other does not please God, when a man either does good things while luxury is not yet bridled, or lives chastely without exercising himself in good works. And that you may be able to be drawn away from the love of things temporal, and may do the good things you do with a simple intention of things eternal, gird your loins, that is, bridle the motions [0298B] of carnal desires, and have your lamps burning, that in your working you may burn with the love of God alone and may offer an example to others.
