## CHAPTER XII. (cont.)

VERS. 36.-- *Waiting.* Whether you avoid evils or do good, you are cleansed in nothing, but only await the coming of the Redeemer. *When he shall return.* The Lord went to the marriage when, after the resurrection, the new man joined to himself the multitude of angels: from whom he returns when he is made manifest to us through the judgment. Whence too he fitly subjoins concerning those that wait: *That when he cometh, they may open to him immediately.* [var: Vulg. Lc 12:36 reads *ut, cum venerit et pulsaverit, confestim aperiant ei*, "when he cometh and knocketh"; the gloss at once expounds the knocking (*pulsanti*) that the lemma as printed does not carry.] He does not wish to open to the judge who knocks, who, fearing to see angry the one whom he despised, dreads to go forth from the body. He opens who bears with the judge glad and untroubled, and rejoices at death drawing near; whence it is subjoined: *Blessed are those servants whom, when [0298C] he cometh,* etc.

VERS. 37.-- *Watching.* He watches who keeps his eyes open in the true light, that he may avoid the darkness of negligence; who also works what he has believed; who is solicitous in the care of the flock committed to him. But what is owed to those that watch he subjoins: *Amen I say to you.*

VERS. 38, 39.-- *And if he shall come in the second,* etc. He calls them watches from the likeness of men keeping guard. In the night of this world we ought always to be solicitous against the enemy, and to await the light that is to come, that is, the coming of the judge. The first watch, therefore, is the guarding of boyhood, the second of youth, the third of old age. But if anyone has neglected to watch in boyhood, let him not despair, but let him come to himself even in youth, or at least at last in old age, because the merciful judge [0298D] patiently awaits our delays. He, to shake out the sloth of the mind, brings in the likeness of outward condemned men, that by this the soul may be roused to the guarding of itself. Whence, *But this know ye, that if the householder knew.* While the householder knows not, the thief digs through the house, because, while the spirit sleeps from the guarding of itself, unforeseen death bursts into the dwelling of the flesh and drags it to punishments. But he would withstand the thief if he watched, because, taking precaution against the coming of the judge who comes secretly, he would meet him by repenting.

VERS. 40.-- *At what hour you think not, the Son of man will come?* The Lord willed the last hour always to be unknown, that it may always be suspected, and that men may always be prepared for it.

[0299A]VERS. 41.-- *And Peter said to him.* Since the Lord had set forth two things, namely both that he would come suddenly, and that they by waiting ought to be ready, Peter asks whether those lofty ordinances of the heavenly life, which are necessary for men preparing themselves, pertain to the apostles alone and to such as they, or to all who are to be saved.

VERS. 42.-- *And the Lord said.* Answering the things asked, the Lord says that the first and the second judgment will be present to all, and that each severally is to be rewarded or condemned according to his merits. Then he adds what he had chiefly asked, namely that the grace of the virtues which he had brought to the world is to be followed by each as far as he can, which is in that place: *I came to send fire upon the earth. Who dost thou think to be the faithful steward?* As if he said: To you, who have been appointed stewards, this [0299B] more especially belongs concerning watchfulness and preparation, that you may prepare both yourselves and others for the coming of the judge, because few are they who well administer the stewardship committed to them. *And prudent, whom the Lord hath set over his household, that,* etc. How rare is he that serves the Lord for the Lord's sake, feeding the sheep of Christ not for gain but out of love of Christ, prudent for what is to come, providing for himself, whom he hath appointed; that is, how rare is he who is called by God as Aaron was, and has not rather thrust himself forward, and who feeds not himself more than the sheep!

VERS. 43.-- *Blessed.* As if he said: Few are the faithful. But he who shall have been found faithfully ministers the allowance of the word, and, watching in the care of the flock, shall be glorified with eternal blessedness.

[0299C]VERS. 44.-- *Over all that he possesseth.* As great as is the distance of merits between good hearers and good teachers, so great is that of rewards also. The good hearers he makes to sit down, and passing by he ministers to them; the stewards he sets over all that he possesses, not that they alone, but that beyond the rest they may have things eternal, both for their own life and for the guarding of the flock.

VERS. 45.-- *But if the servant shall say.* Note that it is reckoned among the vices of the servant that he thinks his lord's return slow. Among the virtues of the good it is not counted that he hoped for it soon, but only that he ministered faithfully. Nothing therefore is better than that we patiently bear to be ignorant of what cannot be known, yet labour that we may be found fit.

*And to eat.* That is, to be taken up with all the crimes and enticements [0299D] of the world, which make the mind to go astray; or literally, if he be given over to gluttony and drunkenness.

VERS. 46.-- *And his portion.* The wicked shall be punished with the unfaithful, but with a difference. For those who sin knowingly shall be punished more grievously; less grievously, those who sin out of ignorance. And among the unjust each shall have a damnation the more tolerable, the less iniquity he had.

VERS. 48.-- *With few.* The mildest punishment of all is that of those who beyond original sin have added no offence besides.

[n: (AMBR.)] Many, thinking that they shall be beaten less if they know not, etc., *as far as* but they are judged as despisers.

[0300A] *And to everyone.* Deservedly the wicked man who knows shall be beaten more, and he who knows not, less, because to the one more, to the other less, has been committed: and of everyone to whom more is committed, more is required.

VERS. 49.-- *Fire,* etc. As if he said: Thou wast asking whether I admonish all men, with loins girt and lamps burning, to await the coming of the Lord? But I, who came into the world only for this, that I might kindle men from the desires of the world to heavenly things, will nothing else save that the radiance of this burning may light up all the parts of the world as far as the end of the age, and that it may so blaze in the hearts of the faithful that it be not quenched by any onset of waves or of blasts.

*But I have a baptism,* etc. And that this fire may be able to be kindled in the hearts of the faithful, so that they may be able perfectly to despise all temporal things [0300B] and themselves also, I must first be baptized with a baptism, that is, be steeped in the anointing of my own blood: for the Spirit would not be given unless Jesus were first glorified. *And how.* So great is the condescension of the Lord that he testifies there to be in him an eagerness for devotion to be poured into us, and for perfection to be consummated in us, and for the passion to be hastened for us: he who, though he had in himself nothing whereat to grieve, was yet straitened by our troubles, and at the time of death put forward a sadness which he had taken on not from the fear of his own death, but from the delay of our redemption. *Until it be accomplished.* He who is straitened as far as the accomplishment is untroubled about the accomplishment, nor is he straitened about it; but rather the death of the body is the loosing of anxiety, not the heaping up of grief.

[0300C]VERS. 51.-- *Think ye.* How, after the baptism of the passion and after the coming of the fire, the earth is to burn, he says: Because for the faith of Christ the whole world is divided against itself, and every house had faithful and unfaithful, these fighting against the faith, those for the faith.

VERS. 52.-- *In one house.* Each single man is one house, either God's or the devil's. And so a spiritual man is a spiritual house. *Divided.* The first cause is that of religion toward God, the second that of dutifulness toward one's neighbour. For thou oughtest to set human things after divine. Kindred pledges are not therefore to be renounced, but God is to be preferred to all. For indeed the pledges of nature are God's benefits, nor ought the benefit that is received to be loved more than God, by whom the benefit received is preserved. *Three against two.* That is, the son and the daughter and the daughter-in-law, against the father and [0300D] the mother; and two against three, that is, the father and the mother, where the mother-in-law also is understood, against the son and the daughter and the daughter-in-law.

VERS. 54.-- *And he said.* When he had instructed the apostles also concerning his first coming, intimating what was done in it, and had admonished them concerning the coming to be, how they ought to await it, he turned his discourse to the crowds, among whom were the Pharisees, as Matthew indicates: because, neither believing the first coming to have taken place nor rightly awaiting the second, they were seeking a sign from heaven, tempting him. These therefore he reproves, because if they were willing to attend, they could have from the words of the prophets a sure sign of both comings. *When you see.* Mystically. The cloud rising from the west signifies the flesh [0301A] of Christ rising again from death, from which time the shower of the Gospel preaching has been poured out upon all lands. The south wind blowing before the heat signifies the lighter tribulations before the judgment.

*A shower cometh.* Literally the sense is plain: they who from the changing of the elements can foreknow the state of the breezes could also know the time of both comings, both from the sayings of the prophets and from the magnificence of his works.

VERS. 56.-- *Hypocrites,* etc. And lest any unlearned men in the crowd, flattering themselves upon their want of skill, should excuse themselves, saying that they knew not the law and the prophets, he adds that they can discern by natural reason either that he who does works which no other had done is above man and is God, or that after so many injustices of the age [0301B] the just judgment of the Creator will come.

VERS. 58.-- *And when thou goest.* When in this life thou preparest thyself to see the appeased face of the judge, although the word of God be contrary to thy flesh, yet use it as a guard given thee on this journey, lest, being despised, it accuse thee. *With the adversary.* Our adversary in the way is the word of God, contrary to our carnal desires in the present life, from which he is set free who is humbly subject to its precepts: otherwise the sinner, guilty from his contempt of the word, will be held at the judge's examination, and the judge will deliver him to the exactor, that is, to the devil; because he will permit the devil to drag his soul to punishment, and the exactor himself, who exacts the punishment for the guilt which he suggested, will thrust it back into hell.

[0301C] *Give diligence,* etc. These things too pertain to the treading down of the world's enticements and to the awaiting of the judge's coming. As if he said: Since it is certain that a strict judge will come, live therefore here in such wise that you be not condemned in that judgment.

VERS. 59.-- *Thou repay.* He repays always by suffering punishments for his sins, but never by obtaining pardon.
