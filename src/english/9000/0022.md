## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And there were present.* While the master of truth was instructing those present how they ought to prepare themselves for the coming of the judge, and how they that do not prepare themselves are to be condemned, tidings are brought him of the Galileans, who as it were justly sought this for the cause that they had not well prepared themselves: and the Lord answers both that they truly [0301D] had been sinners and had justly perished, and that other sinners shall likewise perish, if they take no heed that they are sinners. *Bringing tidings.* As if they said: It appears that these men were sinners and to be condemned for ever, who by so wicked a death were slain by an impious governor in the midst of their own sacrifices. *Of the Galileans.* The Galileans pay the impious penalties of their own crimes by a wicked death; yet it was not that death itself that harmed them, so that they should be sent into the second death, but their wicked life. And they were punished for the correction of the living, that when the pestilent man is scourged the fool may become wiser, or for an example to those who will not be corrected, and who therefore shall perish most wretchedly.

VERS. 3.-- *All,* etc. This was fulfilled after the Lord's passion, when the Romans so laid waste the impenitent [0302A] Jews, beginning from Galilee, that not only the courts of the temple but even the inner parts of the house, whither the Galileans had no access, were defiled with human blood.

VERS. 4.-- *As those eighteen.* These men of Jerusalem, like the Galileans also, were not the only sinners, but were punished for the terror of the rest; and they, crushed by the fall of the tower, signify that the Jews who would not repent were to perish together with their own walls. Nor is it without purpose that they are eighteen, which number among the Greeks is expressed by iota and ita, that is, by the same letters with which the name Jesus begins. And it signifies that those Jews chiefly were to perish who would not receive the name of the Saviour. That tower signifies him who is the tower of strength, which is rightly in Siloe, because Siloe, which is interpreted [0302B] *sent,* signifies him who, sent by the Father, came into the world, who shall crush all upon whom he shall fall.

VERS. 6.-- *A fig tree.* In the beginning of the human race Adam and Eve, when they had sinned, made themselves aprons of fig leaves. By the leaves of the fig tree, therefore, sins are understood. And they were under the fig tree as under the shadow of death, of whom it is said: *They that sat under the shadow of death, light is risen to them [n: (Isa. IX)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 9:2 reads *habitantibus in regione umbrae mortis, lux orta est eis*, "to them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death, light is risen"; the *qui sedebant* form belongs to Mt 4:16, and neither witness has *sub umbra* or *lumen*.]

VERS. 7.-- *Seeking fruit. The vineyard of the Lord sabaoh is the house of Israel,* in which house the Synagogue was founded as a fig tree in a vineyard. But he who permitted the one sinning in the vineyard to be plundered by wayfarers commanded this fig tree also to be cut down. In it he seeks fruit, not as though he knew not that it was absent, but he shows in a figure that in the Synagogue [0302C] it ought by now to have fruit. Nor does he seek before the time, who comes through three years. He who through Moses established the Synagogue, himself appearing in the flesh teaches often in the Synagogue, seeks the fruit of faith, but finds it not in the Pharisees.

*And he said to the dresser of the vineyard.* The dresser of the vineyard: these are the teachers and the apostles, by whose prayers and admonitions the unceasing care of God is supplied; to whom the Lord often complains of the unfruitful nation of the Jews, that through the three years of his visitation, that is, in the edicts of the law, in the testimonies of the prophets, in the grace of the Gospel, it has been negligent.

*Why doth it also the ground,* etc. By the ground occupied by the barren fig tree may be signified the Jewish people, which is pressed by the harmful shadow of its rulers, that it may not see the light of truth, [0302D] and is hindered by their examples, that it may not grow warm with the sun of love; whence [n: (Matth. XXIII)]: *Woe to you, Scribes and Pharisees,* etc. He occupies the ground who does not work with good works the place that he holds, who by the example of his crookedness affords a hindrance to the rest.

VERS. 8.-- *Until I dig,* etc. That is, until I make the root of the unfruitful mind humble, by striking into it the fear both of present afflictions and of eternal damnation. For a ditch is in the lowest place, and rebuke, while it shows the mind to itself, humbles it. He knows how to till the ground who knows how to reprove vices. He understood that the hardness and pride of the Jews was the cause of their barrenness. And so he promises that hard hearts are to be dug with apostolic mattocks, and proud hearts to be called back to humility. [0303A] *And put on dung.* That is, I will bring back to their mind the abomination of the evils they have done, and will rouse up the grace of compunction, with the fruits of good work, as it were from the richness of the dung. For the sins of the flesh are called dung, which are laid to the root of the tree when the memory of the thought touches the conscience of its own crookedness; and while a man repents thereof, he returns, as it were by the touch of dung, to the fruitfulness of work.

VERS. 9.-- *And if indeed.* Although the order of repentance required that he should add "it shall be spared," or something of the kind, yet he subjoined nothing, but left the sentence hanging.

*But if not.* That is, if it shall not bear fruit, he at once annexed the judgment of the damnation to come, because he saw men more prone [0303B] to deny than to believe.

VERS. 10.-- *And he was teaching.* This similitude of the fig tree the Lord is said to have taught in the synagogue, that it might be intimated that the parable belonged to the Synagogue itself. For that he seeks fruit in the fig tree and finds none signifies that he lends the word to the Synagogue and she does not receive it; yet understanding that she is not to be rooted out wholly, but that a remnant is to be saved through faith, straightway in the same place there follows the healing of the primitive Church under the figure of the bowed woman. For the fig tree spared signifies the same thing as the woman set upright. *On the sabbaths.* On the sabbaths he teaches and raises up her that was bowed, because through the word of preaching he leads those raised up from temporal things to eternal into the rest of the resurrection.

[0303C]VERS. 11.-- *And behold a woman,* etc. This woman could not be raised up save after eighteen years, because human nature will not be able to lay aside this corruption and be raised up to put on the incorruption of eternal blessedness, unless it first fulfil the law which is contained in the ten precepts, and the grace in which the resurrection of Christ has been wrought and our own is awaited.

*Years,* etc. The number six, in which the creature of the world was perfected, signifies the perfection of works, and multiplied by three it renders eighteen; and it signifies those who in the three times of the Lord's visitation, namely before the law, and under the law, and under grace, have perfection of works. In this time therefore Judea was bent away from the uprightness of her mind, she who had known earthly things rather than heavenly, works.

[0303D]VERS. 14.-- *Because on the sabbath.* God rested on the sabbath from the works of the world. Whence Moses also commanded that on the sabbath men should keep holiday from servile and harmful action, prefiguring the time in which our secular works, but not indeed the religious, that is, of praising God, shall cease. In sign of this thing the Lord heals on the sabbath also. But the ruler of the synagogue, not understanding this, is deceived and deceives, because the law forbade not to heal a man on the sabbath, but to do servile works, that is, to be weighed down with sins.

VERS. 15.-- *Hypocrites.* He rightly calls them hypocrites, that is, dissemblers, who, while they desire to be seen as masters of the people, are not ashamed to put the healing of a man after the care of a beast. *Doth he not loose his ox.* Mystically. The ox that knew its owner and the ass that knew [0304A] its master's crib signify the Jewish and the Gentile people, each of which, loosed from the bonds of sin, laid aside the thirst and the heat of this world by a draught from the Lord's fountain. In these two animals, therefore, the Lord foretells the calling of the two peoples, the Jews withstanding it.

VERS. 16.-- *And this woman,* etc. A daughter of Abraham: every faithful soul is a daughter of Abraham, the Church gathered to the faith out of both peoples, which, the time of the law and of the Lord's resurrection being fulfilled, broke the bonds of a long captivity through the sevenfold grace of the Holy Spirit: and so it is the same thing for the ox or the ass to be loosed from the manger and led to drink, as for the daughter of Abraham to be raised up from the bond of a harmful bowing down

VERS. 17.-- *All were ashamed.* They who are ashamed of the sayings [0304B] of the Saviour show themselves like to the barren fig tree. They therefore who rejoice at the miracles belong to the daughter of Abraham, that is, to the Church, glorifying God for her raising up. *The adversaries.* The Scribes and Pharisees, who can neither openly gainsay the truth nor, because of envy, are willing to believe. *All the people rejoiced.* The simple and the humble, who love the words of truth and the miracles; and these are a figure of the Gentiles, who, the Jews being blinded, are to be gathered to the faith through humility. Whence the Saviour also commends their faith under the comparison of the grain of mustard seed.

VERS. 18.-- *He said therefore.* Since there were present those who rejoiced and glorified, he spoke to the commendation and the instruction of those who through humility and fervour of faith are to be exalted in the Church.

[0304C]VERS. 19.-- *It is like,* etc. The grain of mustard seed is a cheap and simple thing, but if it be crushed it pours out its own strength. The kingdom of heaven, therefore, that is, the faith through which one comes to the kingdom, is compared to a grain of mustard seed: because at first it is humble and as it were abject and of bitter savour, because of the weakness of Christ and the scandal of the cross; but crushed by adversities it pours forth the grace of its own power and fills those standing about with its odour, since even after death it preached the glory of the resurrection and of the ascension through the whole world. Or the body of Christ is the grain of mustard seed, which Joseph took and buried in the garden, and which afterwards grew in the resurrection and the ascension. It spread out its branches, sending preachers through the whole world, in whose words and consolations the faithful take breath from the weariness of the world.

[0304D]VERS. 21.-- *Which being taken,* etc. The woman is the Church, whose meal we are, as many of us as are ground, by the exercise of fear and of hope, as by an upper and a nether millstone, that we many may be one bread in Christ. The Church therefore hides the leaven of love in three measures of meal, because she bids us love God with the whole heart, with the whole soul, with the whole strength.

VERS. 22.-- *And he went,* etc. Everywhere he preached without distinction, not discerning persons, but seeking salvation for them that believe.

VERS. 24.-- *Strive to enter,* etc. As if he said: Truly they are few, and those with great labours, because unless the intention of the mind be fervent, the wave of the world is not overcome, by which the soul is always called back to the depths.

[0305A]VERS. 25.-- *But when,* etc. Christ the householder, though by his Godhead he is everywhere, is said to be already within to those whom, present in heaven, he gladdens by the sight of himself; but he is as it were without to those whom, hidden, he helps as they strive in this pilgrimage. He will enter therefore when he shall bring the whole Church to the contemplation of himself. He will shut the door when he shall take away from the reprobate the place of repentance, who, standing without, shall knock; that is, being separated from the just, they shall implore in vain the mercy which they despised. *You shall begin to stand without.* Strive now, in the time of mercy, because in the time of judgment the place of repentance is taken from the reprobate, which is now open to all that seek it dutifully.

*I know you not.* The light knows not the darkness, that is, it does not look upon it, because if it looked upon it, there would be no darkness.

[0305B]VERS. 26.-- *We have eaten,* etc. The feasting of festivals does not profit, which the piety of faith does not commend. The knowledge of the Scriptures does not make him known to God whom the iniquity of his works shows to be unworthy. Or it is to be understood simply of the Jews, who, spurning the mysteries of the faith, think themselves acceptable to God because they feast before the Lord in the temple upon the victims offered, and give ear to the reading of the prophets. Or mystically: he eats and drinks before the Lord who eagerly receives the food of the word.

*In the streets,* etc. Scripture in its more obscure places is food, because it is broken as it were by expounding and is swallowed by eating. For it is drink in the more open places, where it is drunk down just as it is found. Both the hidden things, therefore, and the open they testify that they have [0305C] understood, who, when the judge reproves them, complain of what they have eaten.
