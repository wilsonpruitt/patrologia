## CHAPTER XIII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *There shall be weeping,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Weeping is wont to be stirred up by heat, the gnashing of teeth by cold, because there they shall pass from the waters of snow to excessive heat. ⟦s3⟧ Or the gnashing betrays the feeling of one indignant, that he repents too late of what he stubbornly did amiss. ⟦s4⟧ Or they shall gnash with their teeth who here shall rejoice in gluttony. ⟦s5⟧ Those eyes shall weep which here shall wander through concupiscences. ⟦s6⟧ And note, through the weeping which belongs to the eyes and the gnashing of the teeth, the true resurrection of the wicked.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *And they shall come.* ⟦s2⟧ Many who are fervent at first grow torpid afterwards. ⟦s3⟧ Many who are cold at first suddenly take fire. ⟦s4⟧ Many despised in this world are to be glorified in the world to come. ⟦s5⟧ Others glorious among men are to be condemned in the world to come. [0305D] Or the Gentiles, who had long lived without faith, are to be called to the faith, and the Jews to be execrated.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *Say ye to the fox,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The fox is a crafty animal, lurking in a hole for ambush, foul of smell, never going by straight paths. ⟦s3⟧ All these things suit the heretics, whose type Herod holds, who strive to quench Christ, that is, the humility of the Christian faith, in them that believe. ⟦s4⟧ *Behold I cast out demons,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ As if he said: Neither Herod nor the heretics, of whom he himself is the figure, shall be able to disturb what I purpose to do. ⟦s6⟧ And I purpose to cast out demons from the hearts of men, that, leaving vanities behind, they may believe in me; and to perfect healings, that they may live according to my precepts; afterwards I am consummated in my body, [0306A] which is the Church, which, glorified on the day of the resurrection, I shall bring to the fellowship of the angels.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Nevertheless I must.* ⟦s2⟧ Herod shall not be able to disturb it but that I so act and am so consummated; nor shall he be able to work the death of my body, which must come to pass in Jerusalem, where not he but Pilate bears rule. ⟦s3⟧ *To-day and to-morrow,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ The first day is to renounce vanities through the grace of God. ⟦s5⟧ The second, to be in accord with the truth in manners and in life. ⟦s6⟧ The third is the final glorification.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Jerusalem,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Where my prophets are, there I, the head of the prophets, must be sacrificed; for which sin she herself shall justly be destroyed, and with that destruction of theirs I grieve as a devoted father. ⟦s3⟧ *How often would I have gathered.* ⟦s4⟧ As if he said: I would, and thou wouldest not: and as many as [0306B] I have gathered by my will, which is always effectual, I did it while thou wast unwilling, because thou hast always been ungrateful. ⟦s5⟧ *Thy children.* ⟦s6⟧ In this, that he says: *How often would I have gathered thy children,* etc., he bears witness to all the prophets sent by him in time past. ⟦s7⟧ *Even as a bird,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ Beautifully does he who had called Herod a fox, while he was plotting his death, compare himself to a bird, because the deceitful fox always lays ambushes for birds.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *Your house.* ⟦s2⟧ The temple or the city, because, the Lord being slain, the Romans, plundering as it were an empty nest, took away both the place and the nation. ⟦s3⟧ *You shall not see me,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Unless you do penance and confess me to be the Son of the almighty Father, you shall not see my face at the second coming. ⟦s5⟧ *When you shall say,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ This indeed the crowds said when the Lord was coming into Jerusalem. [0306C] But because Luke does not say that the Lord afterwards returned to the Galileans, so that this might be chanted to him by them, he compels it to be understood mystically of the coming of his brightness.
