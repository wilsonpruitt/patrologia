## CHAPTER XIV.

*And it came to pass,* etc. Ὕδωρ , water, whence the dropsy, a watery disease beneath the skin, born of a defect of the bladder, with swelling distension and fetid breath. And it is proper to the man with the dropsy that the more he drinks, the more he thirsts; and therefore he is compared to the man whom the overflowing flux of carnal pleasures weighs down, and he is compared also to the covetous rich man.

VERS. 3.-- *Is it lawful,* etc. On the sabbaths especially Jesus teaches and works, not only because of the spiritual sabbath, but also because of the more crowded gathering of the people, [0306D] seeking the salvation of all.

VERS. 4.-- *But they held their peace.* Rightly do they hold their peace, who see that whatever he had said would be said against them. For if it is lawful, why do they watch him? If it is not lawful, why do they care for their cattle? *But he.* He therefore heals the man with the dropsy before the Pharisees, and straightway disputes against avarice, that he may convict them of the avarice through which they violate the sabbath and ill interpret the law. For on the sabbath one must keep holiday not from good but from evil works, whence it is said: *No servile work shall you do therein [n: (Lev. XXIII)]*, that is, sin. So in the eternal rest there shall be holiday from evils only, not from good things.

VERS. 5.-- *Which of you.* By a fitting example he solves the question, that he may show that they violate the sabbath [0307A] in a work of covetousness who accuse him of violating it in a work of charity. *An ox into a pit.* Fittingly does he compare the man with the dropsy to an animal that has fallen into a pit, because he was perishing by a harmful humour. And well does he name the ox and the ass, that he may signify either the wise or the dull, or both peoples, namely the Jewish, pressed by the yoke of the law, and the Gentile, tamed by no reason but stolid and brutish; all of whom, sunk in the pit of concupiscence, he draws out: *For all have sinned and do need the grace of God [n: (Rom. XXXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Rom 3:23 reads *egent gloria Dei*, "do need the glory of God."]

VERS. 7.-- *And he said.* After he had convicted them of avarice and of the violation of the sabbath, he begins to teach concerning the grace of the Gospel those present who were fit, inviting them to humility against the swelling which the Pharisees had in the law, or intending in his teaching [0307B] how they ought to choose the places at table in the Church.

VERS. 8.-- *When thou art invited:* When, called through the grace of faith by a preacher, thou hast joined thyself to the members of the Church, do not, by glorying in thy merits, exalt thyself as though loftier than the rest. *Lest perhaps one more honourable.* He gives place to one more honourable invited after him, who, made too secure by confidence in his own manner of life, is quickly outstripped by the nimbleness of those who followed him in Christ. And he holds the last place with blushing, etc., when, coming to know better things of others, he humbles whatever lofty thing he had thought of his own working, saying with the Prophet: *But being exalted, I have been humbled and confounded [n: (Psal. LXXXVII)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps 87:16 reads *conturbatus*, "troubled."]

VERS. 9.-- *And then thou begin,* etc. And then, blushing that thou didst once presume greater things of thyself, thou shalt begin to be humbled unto repentance, and to know thyself [0307C] of lesser merit, lest thou now begin to seek what is kept for thee at the end, because the inheritance to which men hasten in the beginning shall be wanting in the last blessing.

VERS. 10.-- *Then shalt thou have glory,* etc. Daily the Lord, while he visits and judges his guests, bestows such gifts of his Spirit upon the humble that the company of the faithful glorifies them with wonder; and, astonished, it leaps forth into the praise of its author, saying: *But to me thy friends, O God, are made exceedingly honourable [n: (Psal. CXXXVIII)]*.

VERS. 12.-- *And he said.* According to the order of the letter. After he had admonished those invited how they ought to sit at table, he admonishes the inviter also whom he ought to invite, lest the temporal spending of goods which he makes [0307D] pass away in vain, or perhaps increase his sin: but let him so spend temporal things that through them a way may lie open to things eternal. *And to him that had invited him.* He speaks to the chief of the Pharisees, who perhaps might grow proud because he had called so many to the banquet as though out of mercy; or, as may be gathered from the Lord's words, he had rather invited them for this, that he might be invited by them in turn, and so no special recompense would follow. *Do not call thy friends.* That brethren and friends and the rich should hold banquets for one another he does not forbid as though it were a crime, but he shows that it does not avail for the rewards of life. And there are certain banquets of such rich men which receive not only requitals in the present but even damnation in the time to come; whence the Apostle: *Not in rioting [0308A] and drunkenness [n: (Rom. VIII)]*, that is, in luxurious banquets, which are either celebrated by the contribution of all or are wont to be furnished by turns among companions, where no man is ashamed to do or say anything unseemly. *Lest perhaps.* As if he said: If thou purposest to invite that thou mayest be invited, therein thou canst be cheated; but if thou distribute to the poor, thou shalt not be cheated of thy reward.

VERS. 13.-- *Call the poor.* He that calls the poor shall receive a reward in the time to come. He that calls friends and brethren and the rich has received his reward. But if he does this for God's sake, after the example of the sons of Job, then, as in the other offices of brotherly love, he himself who commanded it rewards it. He that calls the wanton for the sake of lasciviousness shall not lack punishment.

VERS. 14.-- *In the resurrection.* Though all are to rise again, [0308B] yet the resurrection of the just is called singular and as it were their own, since they are to be changed in it.

VERS. 15.-- *When one had heard these things,* etc. Allured by the sweetness of the resurrection he had heard of, he breaks forth into the praise of it, and asserts that men shall be truly blessed therein, although he does not well understand by what merits that blessedness may be attained. *Blessed,* etc. *If any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever [n: (Joan. VI)]*. If any man, perfectly incorporated into the sacrament of the incarnation, shall have deserved to enjoy the vision of the divine majesty, he shall rejoice in eternal blessedness. But because many receive it only so far as faith, as it were by praying, yet loathe to attain its sweetness truly, their torpor is reproved.

VERS. 16.-- *A certain man,* etc. The Word incarnate [0308C] has prepared for many the fulness of inward sweetness, but few come, because many who are subject to him through faith gainsay him continually by living ill. *A great supper.* This banquet is called not a dinner but a supper, because after the dinner of which Matthew treats a supper remains, and after the supper no banquet remains: from the dinner many are cast out, and from the supper no one.

VERS. 17.-- *And he sent his servant.* The hour of the supper is the end of the world; whence the Apostle: We know *upon whom the ends of the ages are come [n: (I Cor. X)]*. In this end the servant is sent, that is, the order of preachers, to those invited through the law and the prophets, that, loathing being put away, they might prepare themselves to taste the supper, because all things are now ready. For Christ being sacrificed, the entrance of the kingdom [0308D] lies open.

VERS. 18.-- *And they began,* etc. They therefore make excuse, because the kingdom is shut against no man save him whom the profession of his own voice shall have shut out. And every man excuses himself who loves earthly things more than heavenly, even if he say that he is reaching toward heavenly things. *I have bought a farm.* The farm is earthly substance. He goes out therefore to see the farm who thinks of outward things alone for the sake of substance; and this is what is first sought by the lovers of the world, namely the substance of the body.

[n: (AUG.)] In the farm that is bought, lordship and pride are noted. For to have a farm, etc., *as far as* the love of earthly things is birdlime to spiritual wings.

[0309A] *I pray thee.* While he says: *I pray thee, hold me excused,* humility sounds in his voice while he scorns to come, pride in his deed.

VERS. 19.-- *Yokes of oxen.* The five yoke of oxen are the five senses of the body, which are rightly called yokes, since they are doubled in either sex; and because they know and touch not inward things but outward only, by them curiosity is rightly designated, which, while it searches out the life of a neighbour without, knows not its own inward parts. And the mind of a curious man, the more skilled it is in what is another's, the more ignorant it is of its own. Nor do the words of the man excusing himself disagree with the signification of his own vice, when he says: *I go to try them.* For trying is wont to belong to curiosity.

VERS. 20.-- *I have married a wife.* Because many take wives not for the sake of fruitfulness but for the desires of the flesh, [0309B] therefore by this thing the pleasure of the flesh is designated, for the sake of which the fastidious man refuses to come to the supper of God.

VERS. 22.-- *It is done,* etc. Many such of the Jews have we gathered to thy supper, but these do not fill the place of thy banquet; there remains a place where the great number of the Gentiles may be received.

VERS. 23.-- *Go out into the highways and hedges,* etc. Note that in this third invitation it is not said, Invite, but Compel them to come in. For they who, broken by the adversities of this world, return to the love of God, and are corrected from the desires of the present life, do not enter unless compelled. But some are called and despise it, who receive the house of understanding but do not fulfil it in work. Some are called and come, who fulfil in work the [0309C] understanding they have received.

VERS. 25.-- *And there went crowds,* etc. The crowds that follow Jesus as he makes his way to Jerusalem signify those who, invited or compelled, come to the supper, and with their whole intent imitate Jesus going before them to the vision of eternal peace. These therefore he instructs, who ought with sincere love to seek that country, and for its sake to renounce the affections of carnal love.

VERS. 26.-- *If any man come.* We can at once both hate and love: so that we love both those joined to us by acquaintance and by the divine precept, and those whom we suffer as adversaries in the way of God we may not know, by hating and fleeing them. *Yea more.* Then do we rightly hate our own soul, when we do not consent to its carnal desires, but struggle against them and break them.

[0309D]VERS. 27.-- *And whosoever doth not carry.* The cross is carried in two ways: either when the flesh is afflicted by abstinence, or when the mind is afflicted by compassion for a neighbour. *And come after me.* For if a man tears his flesh for empty glory, or has compassion on his neighbour carnally so as to favour his fault, he seems to bear the cross, but he does not follow God.

VERS. 28.-- *For which of you.* After the Lord had given to those following him into Jerusalem lofty precepts concerning contempt of the world and the casting away of the body, he adds a precept concerning perseverance, for which patience with humility is necessary. And this by the likeness of one that builds. *To build a tower.* Because he had given lofty precepts, he subjoins a comparison of a loftiness to be built. [0310A] *Reckoneth the charges.* He reckons the charges who discerns that temporal money must be paid out, and the heart turned away from the covetous desires of the world, and the soul made ready against the adversities of the world.

VERS. 29.-- *All that see.* As the Apostle says: *We are made a spectacle to the world, to angels and to men [n: (II Cor. IV)]*. And in everything that we do we ought to consider our hidden adversaries, who lie in wait for our works; and unless we watch against them, we suffer as mockers those whom we have as persuaders to evil.

VERS. 31.-- *Or what,* etc. After the similitude of the tower to be built, he adds the similitude of a greater matter, of a king about to fight. In which it is shown that by the fulfilment of the preceding commandments no man can attain to glory [0310B] unless the sentence of the severe judge be tempered by mercy. For in the sight of God every man is imperfect. *If he be able.* He meets God as it were with ten thousand who offers the works of the ten commandments which he has performed outwardly. But God comes against him as it were with a doubled number, when he examines him, scarcely prepared in work alone, at once concerning both work and thought. And therefore, while he is still far off through the delay of the judgment, we, considering the insufficiency of our frailty, ought to send before us, for the obtaining of the severe judge's peace, an embassy of tears, of good works, of holy affection.
