## CHAPTER XIV. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *So likewise,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He infers this from the foregoing similitudes. ⟦s3⟧ Since he cannot build well who does not reckon the charges, nor can he safely go to meet another [0310C] who does not send an embassy before him, therefore neither can he be my disciple who does not renounce all things.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Good.* ⟦s2⟧ Truly, whoever wishes to be my disciple must renounce all things, for so he will be good salt, that is, worthy to be set in the number of the apostles, for the seasoning of the hearts of his hearers with spiritual wisdom.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *Nor into the dunghill.* ⟦s2⟧ Salt makes the ground barren. ⟦s3⟧ Whence the wrath of conquerors used to sow with salt the cities they had destroyed, that no shoot might spring up in them. ⟦s4⟧ So the apostles, the kingdom of sin being destroyed, restrain the shoot of vices. ⟦s5⟧ Salt seasons food and dries flesh. ⟦s6⟧ Preaching keeps human nature unharmed for its Creator from the worms and the rottenness of vices.
