## CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 1.-- *To the disciples.* Because the Pharisees, being proud and avaricious, just as they denied pardon to those who asked it, so [0314C] denied money to those in need; therefore the Saviour now speaks to them, now in their hearing speaks to the disciples, concerning mercy. *A certain man.* A bailiff, properly, is the keeper of a farm, but the word is put here for a steward, that is, a dispenser, who dispenses the whole substance of the house. For in this dispenser we ought not to take all things for imitation, namely that we should act fraudulently toward God. But on the contrary these similitudes are given, that we may understand that if he who wrought fraud could be praised by his lord, how much more does he please God who does those works according to his precept.

VERS. 6.-- *A hundred cadi.* A cadus in Greek is called in Latin an amphora, holding three urns. A chorus is filled with thirty pecks. It can simply be taken thus: whoever [0314D] relieves the need of a poor man, whether by a half or by a fifth part, is to be endowed with the wages of his own mercy.

VERS. 8.-- *And the Lord commended.* If the dispenser of the unjust mammon is praised by the Lord's own voice because out of an unjust thing he made ready righteousness for himself, and, though he suffered loss, yet he praises his prudence, not because he had acted fraudulently against his lord, but because he had acted prudently for himself: how much more shall Christ, who can sustain no loss and is inclined to clemency, praise the disciples, if they have been merciful toward those entrusted to them. *The children of this world.* Whose works a man does, his son also he is called; whence: *The children of this world,* that is, of darkness; and the children of light, of eternal life.

[0315A]VERS. 9.-- *Make unto you.* Mammon, in the tongue of the Syrians, is the riches of iniquity, because they were gathered out of iniquity. If therefore iniquity well dispensed is turned into righteousness, how much more do the feasts of the divine word, in which there is no iniquity, lift up a good dispenser into heaven.

VERS. 10.-- *He that is faithful.* In money that is to be shared with the poor man. *And in that which is greater.* Cleaving to the Creator, and made one spirit with him. But he who does not well dispense the temporal things he possesses, empties out for himself the glory of eternal things, over which he swells.

VERS. 11.-- *If therefore,* etc. He who does not give to his brethren for their use what was created by God for all, will be unjust in dividing spiritual money, so that he divides the Lord's [0315B] teaching not according to need, but according to persons.

VERS. 12.-- *And if in that which is another's.* If you do not well dispense the carnal riches which slip away, who will give you the true and eternal riches?

VERS. 13.-- *No servant.* Rebuking avarice, he says that the lovers of money are not able God with love; he therefore who loves God despises money: for it avails not to love things transitory and things eternal at once.

*Or he will hold to the one. [n: (AUG.)]* He will not indeed love. For no man loves, etc., *as far as* and he flatters himself concerning his goodness and his impunity.

*You cannot,* etc. He does not say to have riches, but to serve them. He serves who guards them as a servant; but he who has shaken off the yoke of servitude distributes as a lord. He who serves mammon assuredly serves him who, being set over these things by the desert of his own perversity, [0315C] is called by the Lord the prince of the world.

VERS. 14.-- *Now they heard.* The Lord was admonishing the scribes and Pharisees not to be proudly wise, not to presume upon their own righteousness, but to receive penitent sinners: and to redeem by alms their own sins into which they might chance to fall. But they deride the teacher of mercy, of humility and of thrift, as though, being harmful, he were commanding things less useful and never to be done, or else things useful indeed, but, being superfluous, were pressing upon them things already accomplished by themselves.

VERS. 15.-- *And he said,* etc. While you despise sinners as weak and as men without hope, and believe yourselves perfect in all things and free of every frailty, and to have no need of the remedy of almsgiving. But how justly this height of swelling [0315D] is to be condemned, he sees who enlightens the hidden things of darkness, and makes manifest the counsels of hearts.

VERS. 16.-- *The law and the prophets.* They derided him as he disputed against the love of money, as though it were contrary to the law and the prophets: since in the law many rich men pleased God, and since the law promises to those who keep it the good things of the earth; whom he meets, teaching that there is a distance between the law and the Gospel: because in the law lesser things are commanded to carnal men for the sake of the earth, and in the Gospel greater things are commanded to spiritual men for the sake of heaven.

*And every one.* Great is the violence, when by birth from earth he seeks to possess by virtue heaven, which he could not hold by nature, when he despises even [0316A] the tongues of those who deride, as the Lord is made void by the Pharisees. Every one who does violence hastens with vehement zeal, he does not slacken with a torpid affection. There is therefore a religious violence of faith, and a criminal sloth. Let us then do violence to nature, that it may not sink us to earthly things, but raise itself to things above.

VERS. 17.-- *It is easier.* Although I say, *The law and the prophets until John,* yet I do not preach the destruction of the law: because it is easier for the greatest elements of the world to pass than the least precepts of the law, because even those which seem light or superstitious are not empty of spiritual sacraments, since all things are recapitulated in the Gospel also.

VERS. 18.-- *Every one* etc. [n: (AMBR.)] Not every wedlock is from God. For Christian women are not [0316B] joined to Gentiles, since the law forbids it. Where there is an unequal wedlock, the law of God is not there.

VERS. 19.-- *A certain man.* Against the avaricious mockers he builds up by an example what he had set forth, namely that the rich man is tormented among those below, who did not make Lazarus his friend, by whom he might have been received into eternal tabernacles; and it seems rather a narrative than a parable.

*Who was clothed in purple,* etc. [n: (GREG.)] Purple is the colour of royal apparel, from sea-shells, etc., *as far as* because he knew nothing of the bowels of pity, because he was unwilling to redeem the precepts with a price.

VERS. 20.-- *And there was a certain.* If we see anything blameworthy in the poor, we ought not to despise them, because perhaps those whom weakness of manners wounds, the medicine of poverty heals. *Full of sores.* At this man's [0316C] sores the fastidious rich man shudders, lest amid precious banquets and perfumed guests he should endure the stench of sores with the dogs licking them.

VERS. 21.-- *Desiring.* In one thing two judgments of God are fulfilled: from the sight of the poor man a heap of damnation is brought upon the rich man who does not pity; and again from the sight of the rich man the poor man, tempted daily, is proved, whom, for his own greater proving, poverty together with sickness, and the beheld plenty of the rich man, and no consolation applied to him, afflict. *The dogs.* In the tongue of the dogs there can be signified the tongue of flatterers who lick wounds, while with wicked favour they praise the evils which we blame in ourselves.

VERS. 22.-- *Into Abraham's bosom.* Abraham's bosom is the rest of the blessed poor, whose is the kingdom [0316D] of heaven, into which after this life they are received. The burial of hell is the depth of punishments, which devours the proud and the unmerciful after this life. *And he was buried.* In the law not niggardliness but rapine is condemned; in the Gospel the rich man is tormented because he did not give what was his own.

VERS. 23.-- *And lifting up.* The unfaithful see afar off, while, set in the depth before the day of judgment, they mark the faithful above them in rest, whose joys afterwards they cannot behold. What they descry is far off, because they do not reach thither by merit.

*He saw Abraham. [n: (GREG.)]* It is to be believed that before the retribution of the last judgment the unjust in rest, etc., *as far as* and they are tormented by the punishment of those whom they loved uselessly.

[0317A]VERS. 24.-- *That he may dip.* He who did not give to Lazarus the crumbs falling from the table, desires that water be distilled into his mouth from the tip of that man's finger; and he who gave not the least things in life, in hell seeks the least things.

VERS. 25.-- *Because thou hast received. [n: (AUG.)]* Note that this rich man had something of good, out of which he received a transitory good in this life, and Lazarus had something of evil, but the want of fire purged it.

VERS. 26.-- *And in all these things.* Just as the reprobate wish to pass over from their punishments to the glory of the saints, so the just wish in mind through mercy to go to those set in torments, that they may free them; but they cannot, because the souls of the just, though in the goodness of their own nature they have mercy, are yet now joined to the righteousness of their Author and are bound with so great a rectitude that they are moved by no [0317B] compassion toward the reprobate.

VERS. 27.-- *I beseech thee therefore.* After hope for himself is taken from the burning man, his mind runs back to his kinsmen, because the punishment of the reprobate sometimes uselessly instructs their mind unto charity, so that then at last they love even their own spiritually, who here, while they loved their sins, did not even love themselves. There is kept therefore for the rich man unto punishment both the knowledge of the poor man whom he despised, and the memory of the brethren whom he left behind, that he may be the more tormented by the beheld glory of the despised and by the punishment of those he uselessly loved. *That thou wouldst send.* Too late does this rich man begin to be a master, when he has now neither time for learning nor for teaching.

VERS. 31.-- *If Moses.* They who despise the words of the law, by how much the more subtle the precepts of the Redeemer, who rose from the dead, are, by so much the more hardly will they fulfil these, [0317C] and him whose sayings they refuse to fulfil, him without doubt they refuse to believe.
