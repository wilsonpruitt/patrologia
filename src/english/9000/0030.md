## CHAPTER XVIII.

[0322B]VERS. 2.-- *A certain judge.* This parable exhorts to praying and to believing, and to presuming not upon ourselves but upon God.

VERS. 3.-- *And a widow.* This widow can bear a likeness of the Church, which seems desolate until the Lord come, who nevertheless even now bears care for her in secret. *Avenge me.* Although the just are admonished to pray for their persecutors, yet, righteousness being safe, they seek this vengeance upon the evil: that all the evil may perish, either by being converted to righteousness, or by the loss through punishment of that power by which they now, against the good, so long as this very thing is expedient for the good, are able to do somewhat even temporally. And when the just desire this end to come, although they pray for their enemies, yet they are not absurdly said to desire vengeance.

[0322C]VERS. 8.-- *But yet.* I say that I will execute the vengeance of the just, but they will be exceedingly few, because when on the day of judgment I shall appear in the form of the Son of man, so great will be the rarity of the just that the ruin of the whole world must then be hastened, not so much for the cry of the faithful unjustly condemned, as for the torpor of those justly to be condemned. *Thinkest thou.* In the word the unbelief of doubting is rebuked; the Divinity does not form an opinion. For he through whom all things are knows all things, but by doubting he rebukes the hearts of the unbelieving.

VERS. 10.-- *Two men.* Because the parable by which he invited to prayer he so concluded as to say that, when the Judge comes, faith would hardly be found on earth. Lest anyone should flatter himself with a superfluous knowledge of faith, or even with a confession of it, in this parable he shows more diligently [0322D] that not the words of faith but the works are to be examined by God, among which humility chiefly reigns; and because the proud do not have it, presuming upon their own righteousness and despising the weak, they, as though empty of faith, are not heard when they pray.

VERS. 11.-- *God, I give thee thanks,* etc. There are four kinds of swelling: when either a man reckons that the good which he has he has of himself; or, if he believes it given by God, he thinks it given for his merits; or when he boasts that he has what he has not; or when, despising the rest, he seeks to be seen singularly to have what he has. With this plague the Pharisee laboured, who therefore went down not justified, because ascribing to himself singularly the merits of good works, he set himself before the publican.

[0323A]VERS. 12.-- *I fast twice.* Behold, the Pharisee has an eye to the showing of abstinence, to the obtaining of mercy, to the returning of thanks to God; to the keeping of humility he has none. And what does it profit if the whole city be guarded, and one opening be left through which the enemies may enter?

VERS. 13.-- *And the publican.* As a humble man, he does not dare to draw near, that God may draw near to him: he does not look up, that he may be looked upon; he presses down his conscience, but lifts up his hope; he strikes his breast, he exacts penalties of himself that God may spare; he confesses that God may pardon; God pardons what he acknowledges. *God, be merciful.* The publican praying humbly belongs to those of whom it is said: Shall not God revenge his elect crying to him? The Pharisee boasting of his merits belongs to those of whom it is added: [0323B] *The Son of man coming, thinkest thou, shall he find faith on earth?*

VERS. 14.-- *For every one,* etc. The controversy of the Pharisee and the publican being set down, the sentence of the judge is set down, that pride may be shunned.

VERS. 15.-- *And they brought.* After the controversy spoken, after the sentence given by the humble master, infants are offered, that it may be shown that an innocent and simple age belongs to grace. The apostles rebuke, not because they were unwilling that infants should be blessed by the hand and voice of the Saviour, but because, not yet perfect in faith, they thought that he was wearied, after the likeness of men, by the importunity of those who offered them.

*They rebuked.* This perhaps for the reason that they wished the people of the Jews, from whom they were according to the flesh, to be saved first; yet they also made supplication for the Chanaanite woman. [0323C] They knew therefore the mystery, that the calling of both peoples was due, but perhaps they did not yet know the order.

VERS. 16.-- *Suffer the children.* Let no one forbid the sick to come to the physician. These have as yet committed nothing in the branch, but they have perished in the root. They who cannot plead for themselves have committed their cause to their elders. Let the physician touch, and *let him bless the little ones with the greater [n: (Psal. CXIII)]*. *For of such is.* He does not say, of these, but of those who have from endeavour the innocence and simplicity which these have by nature, because it is not age that reigns, but manners. For it is not a virtue not to be able to sin, but not to will it. As a child does not persevere in anger, when hurt does not remember it, does not covet a woman, does not think or speak evil, so you, unless you have such innocence and purity of mind, [0323D] shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.

VERS. 17.-- *The kingdom of God.* That is, we are bidden to receive the teaching of the Gospel as a child does. A child in learning does not contradict his teachers. He does not compose reasonings and words to resist, but faithfully receives, obeys and rests.

VERS. 18.-- *And he asked him. [n: (BEDA.)]* This ruler had heard that those like children were worthy, etc., *as far as* to those who wish to be perfect he adds: *Sell all and give to the poor [n: (Marc. X)]*.

*Good master.* Because he said a good master, and did not confess him God or the Son of God he [Christ] says that even a holy man, in comparison with God, is not good. But Christ does not refuse the testimony of goodness, [0324A] but excludes the goodness of a master apart from God.

VERS. 19.-- *And he said. [n: (AMBR.)]* A crafty question, and therefore a keen answer. For the tempter, etc., *as far as* since by these words it is defined that a merciful rich man is against nature.

*Thou knowest the commandments.* This is the chastity of childish innocence, which is set before us for imitation, if we wish to enter the kingdom of God.

VERS. 23.-- *He was rich.* No rich man, that is, none trusting in his riches, shall enter in, as Jesus expounds according to Matthew. Not therefore those who remain rich, but those who have ceased to be so, shall enter in.

VERS. 25.-- *For it is easier.* Mystically. It is easier for Christ to suffer for the lovers of the world, than for the lovers of the world to be converted to Christ. Christ is called a camel, [0324B] because, humbled of his own will, he took up the burdens of our infirmity. The needle is a pricking, by which he signifies the straits of the passion, by which passion, as by a needle, he deigned to sew up again the as it were rent garments of our nature, that is, to repair them after the fall. Otherwise: it is easier for the Gentile people, deformed by the hump of sins, to enter by the narrow way of life, than the Jew who glories in his merits and is unwilling to humble himself to take up the light burden of Christ.

VERS. 26.-- *Who can?* They understand that all who love riches, even if they have them not, are to be reckoned in the number of the rich: for otherwise, since the crowd of the poor is greater, they would not say: *Who can be saved?*

VERS. 29.-- *No man.* He who shall have let go carnal things for the Lord shall receive spiritual things. Mark says: *A hundred times as much [0324C] now,* etc. [n: (Marc. X)], that is, in this life full of persecutions they taste the joy of the kingdom, and enjoy the sincere love of all the elect.

VERS. 30.-- *He shall receive,* etc. He who shall have let go all riches for the Lord, and shall have despised the affections of the flesh, will receive much more in the present, and from brethren and sisters bound together by the spiritual glue of his own purpose he will receive in this life a much more gracious charity.

VERS. 31.-- *He took.* Foreseeing that the disciples would be troubled by his passion, he foretells both the penalty of the passion and the glory of the resurrection, that when they beheld him dying they might not doubt that he would rise again. *And they shall be accomplished.* In this he shows that whatever the prophets foretold had regard to the mystery of his dispensation. In which he strikes the heretics, who say that [0324D] Christ preached a thing contrary to the law and the prophets, and that the God of the Old *Testatamenti* is one and of the New another. He confutes also the madness of the pagans, because they deride his cross, since he both showed the time of his approaching passion as one foreknowing things to come, and approached the place as one not fearing death.

VERS. 34.-- *And they,* etc. The disciples are not able to understand the discourse of the Lord's passion, so often repeated, for this cause, that of him whom they most desire to see they cannot bear to hear the death, and him whom they know to be not only an innocent man but also true God, they do not think able to die.

VERS. 35.-- *A blind man.* The blind man signifies the human race, which, shut out from the brightness above, suffers the darkness of its own [0325A] damnation, but is healed by the Lord drawing near to Jericho. Jericho is interpreted *moon*, by which the failing of mortality is understood. When therefore the Word of God takes up the infirmity of our flesh, man returns to the things that are to be known. The blind man sits by the way when he begins to believe in him who says: *I am the way [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. He begs, when he asks.

VERS. 39.-- *And they that went before,* etc. They who go before Jesus as he comes signify the blind man of carnal vices, which scatter the thought of man and disturb the voice of prayer, that Jesus may not be able to come to enlighten the heart of man; but he, now feeling himself weighed down by the phantasm of his former vices, and the voice of his prayer hindered, lest [the Lord] be entreated on his behalf, cries out the more ardently.

VERS. 40.-- *And standing,* etc. Passing by, he hears the blind man; standing, he enlightens him, because through his humanity he suffers with the voices of our [0325B] blindness, but through the power of his Divinity he pours into us the light of grace. *To be brought unto him.* He who somewhat perceived him passing by, while in prayer he suffers crowds of phantasms, pressing the more vehemently upon prayer is led to him as he stands, and receives light, because God is fixed in a firm heart, and so the lost light is repaired.

VERS. 41.-- *What wilt thou,* etc. He does not ask out of ignorance, but for this cause, that he may be asked, that he may rouse the mind to prayer. Understanding which, the blind man subjoins: *Lord, that I may see.* The blind man does not ask for gold or anything temporal, but only for light. Let us therefore imitate him whom we have heard was healed in mind and in body; let us ask of the Lord the light of the eternal vision, and he will say to us: *Receive thy sight, thy faith hath made thee whole.*

VERS. 43.-- *And immediately he saw.* He sees and follows, [0325C] who works the good which he understands, and imitates Jesus present or passing by. And such a one not only profits toward God for what he has obtained, but also kindles others to the praise of God. *And all the people.* The people praises God not only for the obtained gift of light, but also for the merit of the faith that obtained it, because it praises not only that he enlightens powerfully and mercifully, but because he hears the cry of a faith that cries firmly.
