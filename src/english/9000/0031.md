## CHAPTER XIX.

VERS. 1, 2.-- *And entering in,* etc. Behold, the camel, having laid down the burden of its hump, passes through the eye of the needle, while, despising the revenue got by frauds, it climbs up to the narrow gate which leads to life. And because by the devotion of faith he supplies, by the climbing of a tree, what he had too little of by nature for seeing the Saviour, [0325D] justly he receives what he did not dare to ask, the blessing of the Lord's welcome which he had desired. Mystically, Zacchaeus, who is interpreted *justified,* signifies those believing from among the Gentiles, who had been pressed down by the business of temporal things, and were least, but were sanctified by the Lord.

*And he was rich.* Who should despair of himself, when this man came to God whose revenue was from fraud? By him it appears that not every rich man is covetous.

VERS. 3.-- *Because of the crowd.* What crowd, but the confusion of an unskilled multitude, which cannot see the summit of wisdom? Zacchaeus therefore, not in the crowd, but having passed beyond the common people, beholds the Messiah whom he desires.

VERS. 4.-- *A sycomore.* The sycomore is a tree, [0326A] like the mulberry in its leaves, but surpassing it in height, and it is interpreted *the foolish fig,* that is, the cross. Which, as a fig, feeds believers; as foolish, is mocked by unbelievers. The little man climbs it when anyone humble and conscious of his own weakness glories in the virtue of the cross, and through this praiseworthy foolishness discerns the Lord passing near; because if not yet so solidly as he now is, yet hastily, and as it were in passing, he strains toward the light of heavenly wisdom.

*Because he was to pass that way. [n: (ISID.)]* In that part where the sycomore was, or where he was to believe, etc., *as far as* he stood out as in the fruitful height of a work.

VERS. 5.-- *And when he was come.* Through the heralds of his word sent before, in whom Jesus was and was speaking, he came to the people of the nations, which, already lifted on high by faith in his passion, burns to acknowledge his divinity also; [0326B] looking up he sees, for he chose him who through faith had been raised from earthly things and who was choosing him, and loves him who loves him. *Come down.* That he is bidden to come down from the sycomore, and so to make ready a lodging in his house, this is what the Apostle says: *Although we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him not. For if he died of weakness, yet now he lives by the power of God [n: (II Cor. V, XIII)]* [var: Vulg. 2 Cor 13:4 *crucifixus est ex infirmitate*, "was crucified out of weakness"; and 2 Cor 5:16 *sed nunc jam non novimus*]. *To abide.* The Lord once abode in the house of the chief of the Pharisees, that is, [d: that is,] he was teaching in the synagogue of the Jews.

VERS. 7.-- *They murmured.* It is manifest that the Jews always hated, or did not understand, the salvation of the Gentiles. Whence also it is said against Peter: *Why didst thou go in to the Gentiles that have uncircumcision [n: (Act. IX)]* [var: Vulg. Acts 11:3 *ad viros praeputium habentes*, "to men that have uncircumcision"]?

VERS. 8.-- *But standing,* etc. While others were calumniating him as a sinful man, Zacchaeus himself also standing, [0326C] that is, persisting in that truth of faith which he had begun, proves that he was not only converted from a sinner, but had even lived among the innocent, since he lived innocently even before his conversion, and he who before his conversion could give the half, after his conversion can give the whole also. *If I have taken anything,* etc. The first thing is to restore according to the law, then to give out to the poor what remains. This fruit of life the publican gathers from the sycomore through wise folly, in that he restores what he has seized, leaves what is near, despises things visible, and longs to follow the footsteps of him whom he does not yet see.

VERS. 9.-- *He is a son.* Not by flesh, but by faith, because as Abraham at God's command forsook his country for the hope of a future inheritance, so also this man, who acquires by hope a treasure in the heavens.

[0326D]VERS. 10.-- *He came,* etc. Even to those murmuring he does not disdain to expound his mysteries: that he himself came upon earth chiefly for the sake of the repentance of sinners, which is to be looked upon, where also he calls himself the Son of man, that he may intimate to us the dispensation of his mercy.

VERS. 11.-- *These things to them,* etc. The repentance of the rich publican having been received and commended, he teaches by a parable that the repentance of sinners pleases him more than the righteousness of the proud, and that he will reign more widely among the Gentiles, who are humble concerning their ignorance of the law, than among the Jews, who are proud concerning the righteousness of the law.

*Because that.* Not only those who persevere as just, but also those who are converted from injustice, belong to the children of the promise.

[0327A]VERS. 12.-- *A nobleman went away.* This is the nobleman to whom the blind man cried: *Have mercy on me, son of David,* and to whom the trumpets cried: *Hosanna to the son of David: blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel,* [var: Vulg. Matt 21:9 ends *hosanna in altissimis*, and *rex Israel* stands only at Jo 12:13] who was appointed king by the Father, to whom the Gentiles were given for an inheritance. *A far country.* Leaving the blinded Jews, he passes over to enlighten the Gentiles, who cry to the Lord from afar (because from the ends of the earth), or because *salvation is far from sinners [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*: God, who is everywhere present, is far from those who worship idols; but they too have been made near in the blood.

VERS. 13.-- *And having called ten.* He calls ten servants, when he chose disciples steeped in the letter of the Decalogue. He gives ten pounds, when after the resurrection he rises and opens their understanding, that it may spiritually understand [0327B] the sayings of the law The mna weighs ten drachmas. And every word of Scripture, because it suggests the perfection of the heavenly life, shines as with the weight of the hundredfold number. *Trade ye.* Offer to the peoples the words of the law sifted by mystical interpretation, and receive from them the confession of faith and uprightness of manners.

VERS. 15.-- *And it came to pass.* Daily Christ, having received the kingdom from the Father, returns, because he always beholds the state of the Church journeying on earth. To this servant he lends money for trading; in another he examines the measure of the work accomplished. This one, labouring prudently, he endows with ampler grace; that one, following after wanton things, he deprives even of what he had given. But in the judgment many who seemed fit for teaching will, for their own negligence, [0327C] be reckoned among the unlearned. Certain men, a devotion of simpler observance, will receive the highest rewards among the apostolic. *For he that receiveth a prophet in the name of a prophet shall receive the reward of a prophet [n: (Matth. X)]*.

VERS. 16.-- *And the first came.* The first servant is the order of teachers sent into the circumcision, who received one pound to trade with, because he was sent to preach one God, one faith, one baptism. But he gains ten while by teaching he joins to himself the people set under the law.

VERS. 17.-- *Thou hast been faithful in a little.* Little is whatever of gifts we receive in the present, in comparison with those to come. *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. *Ten [0327D] cities.* The ten cities are souls coming through the words of the law to the grace of the Gospel, over whom he is then set to be glorified who shall have worthily commended to God the money of the word for them; whence the Apostle: *What is our hope, or joy, or crown of glory? Are not you before the Lord Jesus [n: (I Thes. II)]*?

VERS. 18.-- *And another,* A good order: being about to call the Gentiles, and about to send the Jews to be slain, who would not have Christ reign over them, he set this comparison first, lest it should be said: He had given nothing to the people of the Jews whereby they might be made better, so that what should be exacted from him who receives nothing? Out of one pound he makes five, who has moral things on account of the five senses.

VERS. 20.-- *And another,* etc. He who was sent [0328A] to the uncircumcision, to whom the Lord entrusted one pound, and the same faith which was committed to the circumcision, to be preached. Which makes five pounds, because it converts to the grace of the evangelical faith the Gentiles that were enslaved to the five senses of the body. *In a napkin,* etc. He lays up the money in a napkin who, being fit for preaching, either refuses to undertake the office of preaching, or, having undertaken it, does not discharge it well, but hides with himself the gifts he has received under the idleness of the time, saying: It is enough for me that I render an account of myself; why should I preach to others, or be compelled to render an account of others? since even with God those are inexcusable to whom the law was not given, nor did they sleep when the Gospel had been heard, because through the creature they were able to know the Creator.

VERS. 21.-- *Thou reapest what.* For God reaps where he did not [0328B] sow, that is, he holds guilty of impiety even those to whom he has not ministered the word of the law or of the Gospel. Fearing therefore the peril of the judgment, I will rest from the administration of the word.

VERS. 22.-- *Thou knewest,* etc. What he had thought to have said for an excuse is turned into a fault. If thou hadst known me so hard and cruel, why did that thought not strike fear into thee, that thou shouldst know I would seek my own the more diligently?

VERS. 23.-- *The money.* That is, the evangelical preaching, and thou didst not make it known to the prepared hearts of the faithful. *Mine to the bank.* Not thine. Whence the Apostle: *If any man speak, let it be as the words of God [n: (I Petr. IV)]*. *With usury.* He who buys the money of the word from a teacher by believing must needs pay it back with usury by working. Or, [0328C] he pays usury on the loan of the word he has received who, from what he hears, studies to understand other things also.

VERS. 24.-- *Take away,* etc. He loses the grace conferred who has neglected to share it with others by preaching. But it is increased to him who has laboured in it. As the royal chrism which Saul lost by being proud, David merited by obeying. For the Spirit of the Lord who departed from Saul was directed from that day forward into David.

VERS. 26.-- *And he shall abound.* Mystically: when all Israel shall be saved, then the fulness of spiritual grace, which now we exercise sluggishly, shall be abundantly conferred upon the teachers of that time.

VERS. 28.-- *And having said these things.* The parable finished, he goes to Jerusalem, to show that the parable had been set forth concerning the fate of that same greatest city, which [0328D] not long after, being itself also about to kill him for hatred of his kingdom, was to perish by an enemy's slaughter.

VERS. 29.-- *To Bethphage.* Bethphage is a hamlet of priests on the Mount of Olives, and is interpreted *the house of the mouth.* Bethany is a farmstead or city on the side of the same mountain, and is called *the house of obedience.* These the Lord, being about to come to Jerusalem, exalted by his presence, because before his passion he filled many, by teaching, with the gifts of devout confession and of spiritual obedience. Which are well placed on the Mount of Olives, that is, in the Lord himself, who cherishes us with the unction of spiritual chrisms and with the light of conscience and of mercy. And because he does not wish a city set above itself to be hidden, he sends the disciples into the village which is over against them, that through [0329A] them he may penetrate the fortifications of the whole world set over against him.

VERS. 30.-- *Entering in you shall find.* The preachers, entering into the world, find the people of the nations, entangled in the bonds of unbelief, free and wanton. *Upon whom no man,* etc., that is, no rational teacher has put the bridle of correction, who might either restrain its tongue from evil, or compel it to go into the narrow way of life. No man, by persuading what was useful, bestowed the garments of salvation whereby it might *be warmed.* *No man sat,* because none corrected its folly by pressing it down. *Tied.* Tied with the bonds of unbelief, in which, made over or bound fast, he was serving a lord by error; but he whom not nature but fault had made lord could not claim the mastery for himself. And therefore when the Lord is spoken of, one is acknowledged. For, [0329B] although there be many gods and lords, yet in general there is one God and one Lord.

VERS. 33.-- *And as they were loosing him,* etc. Despise not this little ass, because as under the clothing of sheep there are ravening wolves, so under the spoils of the body (which is common to us with the beasts) a mind full of God is quickened. *They said.* He had many masters, who, given over not to one superstition, but at the pleasure of unclean spirits, was hurried away to various errors. *Why do you loose him.* Those who had spoken against the loosing of the colt are quiet when the name is heard, because the masters of errors, who withstand the teachers coming for the salvation of the Gentiles, defend their own darkness, until, with miracles bearing witness, the power of the true owner stands forth. But after the power of the Lord's faith appears, the complaints of the adversaries giving way, [0329C] there is brought forward an assembly that may bear God in its heart.
