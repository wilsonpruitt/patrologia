## CHAPTER XIX. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *The garments.* ⟦s2⟧ The garments of the apostles are the teaching of the virtues, or the discernment of the Scriptures, and grace spiritually, wherewith they cover the hearts of men, before naked and cold, that they may deserve to have Christ for their rider. ⟦s3⟧ *They laid them on.* ⟦s4⟧ While the people of the Gentiles carries Christ, the disciples strew their garments in the way, because, putting off the covering of their own body, they make ready a way for the simple by their own blood, that with unhindered step of mind they may press on to Jerusalem, whither Christ leads, that is, to the true vision of peace.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *At the descent.* ⟦s2⟧ As the Lord came down from the Mount of Olives, that is, as he who was in the form of God humbled himself to the weakness of our flesh, the crowds come down with him, [0329D] that is, he is humbled who need his mercy, that they may be exalted by him. ⟦s3⟧ *Rejoicing.* ⟦s4⟧ Often he entered the city of Jerusalem, but was not called king, which he had always fled, save only now when he went up to suffer. ⟦s5⟧ Which was done for this reason, that he might the more stir up their envy against himself, because the time of the passion was now at hand. ⟦s6⟧ *For.* ⟦s7⟧ Chiefly for the raising of Lazarus, which had lately been done. ⟦s8⟧ For on that account also the crowd came to meet him, because they had heard that this sign had been done by him. ⟦s9⟧ They praise him also for the signs and the teaching which he wrought when he went up for the day of the feast of tabernacles, which John relates.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *Blessed.* ⟦s2⟧ The crowd, that after a little was to crucify him, calls him king, and declares that the awaited son of David according [0330A] to the flesh has come, so that a testimony against themselves is wrung from them unwilling. ⟦s3⟧ *Peace.* ⟦s4⟧ As at the Lord's birth the heavenly companies of men and of angels sang: *Glory to God in the highest. ⟦s5+6⟧ And on earth peace to men,* etc. [var: Vulg. Luke 2:14 *Gloria in altissimis Deo*; *in excelsis* is the reading of Luke 19:38, the verse here glossed], so to the same, soon to triumph, men sing together: Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Master.* ⟦s2⟧ Wondrous madness of the envious! ⟦s2⟧ Him whom they had known to teach true things, when they hear him called master, they judge that his disciples are to be reproved, as though they themselves were the better taught, those disciples whom he had instructed who by his miracles appears to be God.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *The stones.* ⟦s2⟧ When the Lord was crucified and the apostles held their peace for fear, the rocks that were rent, the tombs being opened, openly [0330B] cried out that he was God and the Lord of the world.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically: if blindness has befallen Israel, so that it falls silent from the praise of God, the people of the Gentiles, their stony heart softened, shall believe and announce their Creator.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 44.-- *And they shall not leave.* ⟦s2⟧ For when the perverse man adds perversion, he builds as it were stone upon stone. ⟦s3⟧ But when the soul is hurried away to vengeance, such a building of thoughts is scattered, and this for this cause, *because thou hast not known the time.* ⟦s4⟧ For God visits the perverse soul, sometimes by a precept, sometimes by a miracle. ⟦s5⟧ But because in its pride it despises, and does not blush for its own evils, at the last it shall be delivered to its enemies, with whom in the eternal judgment it is gathered into the fellowship of damnation. ⟦s6⟧ *Unto peace,* etc. ⟦s7⟧ While thou givest the flesh to pleasures, not foreseeing the evils to come, thou hast present goods [0330C] which can be for a temporal peace. ⟦s8⟧ And why this, he subjoins, namely, because it does not foresee the evils that are to come upon it. ⟦s9⟧ For if it did foresee them, it would not have rejoiced in present prosperities.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 45.-- *And going in.* ⟦s2⟧ After the evils had been enumerated, going into the temple he cast out those that sold and bought, intimating by this that the ruin of the people was chiefly from the fault of the priests; for while he strikes those that sell and buy, he shows whence the root of perdition proceeded.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *And he was teaching.* ⟦s2⟧ The house of God is the conscience of the faithful, which he makes a den of thieves who, having left the simplicity of holiness, plots to do that whereby he may be able to hurt his innocent neighbour. ⟦s3⟧ But on the contrary the Lord instructs the minds of the faithful to beware of evils. ⟦s4⟧ *They sought to destroy him.* ⟦s5⟧ Therefore they were not able [0330D] to destroy him, because they feared the tumult of the people; or therefore they wished to destroy him, because, their own office of teaching being neglected, they saw the whole people flowing together to his doctrine.
