## CHAPTER XX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *By what authority.* ⟦s2⟧ They doubt of God's power, and would have it understood that what he does is the devil's. ⟦s3⟧ Whence they add: *Or who is he,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ In which they plainly deny the Son of God, whom they believe to work signs not by his own powers, but by another's.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *I will ask you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He could have refuted the calumny of the tempters by an open answer, but he prudently asks a question, that they may be condemned either by their own silence or by their own sentence.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0331A]VERS. 4.-- *The baptism of John,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He whom you confess to have had his prophecy from heaven bore witness to me, and from him you have heard by what authority I do these things. ⟦s3⟧ Understanding which, they doubt what they ought to answer.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Neither do I,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For two causes the truth is to be hidden from those who ask it. ⟦s3⟧ Either because they are less able to hear it; whence it is said: *I have yet many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now [n: (Joan. XVI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Or because they ask out of hatred and contempt; whence it is said: *Give not that which is holy to dogs [n: (Matth. VII)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *And he began.* ⟦s2⟧ The chief priests and scribes having been refuted out of their own question, the Lord pursues what he had begun, and, in their hearing, addresses the common people, which hears his words more gladly, bringing in [0331B] a parable whereby he may both convict them of impiety and show that the kingdom of God is to be transferred to the Gentiles.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A vineyard.* ⟦s2⟧ Morally: *The vineyard of the Lord of sabaoth, the house of Israel [n: (Isa. V)]*: [var: Vulg. Isa 5:7 *Vinea enim Domini exercituum domus Israel est*, "of the Lord of hosts"] the husbandmen are the labourers who were hired to till it at the first hour, the third, the sixth and the ninth. ⟦s3⟧ *And he into a far country,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ He who is everywhere is the more present to those who love him, and is absent to those who neglect him; and he was absent a long time, lest the exaction should seem wrongful. ⟦s5⟧ For the more indulgent the liberality, the more inexcusable the obstinacy.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And at the season.* ⟦s2⟧ The first servant who is sent is Moses the lawgiver. ⟦s3⟧ Who for forty years sought from the tillers the fruit of the law which he had given, but they beat him and send him away empty.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And he added,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The second servant is David [0331C] the prophet, who after Moses invited the husbandmen of the vineyard by the measure of psalmody and the sweetness of the harp to the exercise of good work; but him also they treat with contumely and send away empty, saying: *What part have we in David, or what inheritance in the son of Isai [n: (Reg. XII)]*? ⟦s3⟧ Yet he entreats for this vineyard, that it be not utterly rooted up: *O Lord God of hosts, turn and look down from heaven, and see, and visit this vineyard [n: (Psal. LXXVII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ The third servant is the choir of the prophets. ⟦s5⟧ Who rebuked the people, and foretold the evils that hung over the vineyard; but them also they persecuted and killed.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Whom when they had seen.* ⟦s2⟧ He plainly proves that the Jews crucified the Son of God not through ignorance, but through envy. ⟦s3⟧ For they understood that this was he to whom it was said: *Ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thy inheritance, [0331D] and the utmost parts of the earth for thy possession [n: (Psal. CXVII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And therefore, taking counsel for themselves, they say: *Behold, the whole world is gone after him [n: (Joan. XII)]*, etc. ⟦s5⟧ The inheritance of the Church, therefore, which the Father did not leave by dying, but which he himself, bought by his own death, possessed by rising again, this the Jews wish to snatch away, while they endeavour to extinguish the faith which is through him, not being subject to the justice of God, but wishing to establish their own. ⟦s6⟧ *This is the heir:* Christ is the heir, because he outlived his own death, and of the testaments which he himself made he obtains, as it were by inheritance, the profits in our advances.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And having cast him out.* ⟦s2⟧ Mark changes the order: *They killed him and cast him out of the vineyard (Marc. [0332A] XII)*. ⟦s3⟧ In which their obstinacy is noted, who after the resurrection had refused to believe the apostles preaching, but, casting him out as it were a useful corpse, gave him to the Gentiles to be taken up.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *God forbid.* ⟦s2⟧ They contradict the sentence which they know was spoken against their own unbelief. ⟦s3⟧ For what they were carrying in their mind they understand to be contained in the parable. ⟦s4⟧ Since therefore the Jews deny that it would be just that the divine knowledge of the law (which they themselves despised) be transferred to the Gentiles, mark what the Saviour answers.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *What is this,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ How shall this prophecy be fulfilled? ⟦s3⟧ Except that Christ, rejected by you, is to be preached to the Gentiles who will believe, so that in himself, the one corner stone, he may found two walls. ⟦s4⟧ *The builders.* ⟦s5⟧ Those who above are husbandmen are here called builders, [0332B] because they who were bidden to till the people that it might bear fruit were bidden to set it up as a house worthy of the Lord himself for its indweller.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Every one that shall fall.* ⟦s2⟧ He who believes and yet sins falls upon the stone, and is not ground to powder, but is broken, because by patience he is reserved unto salvation. ⟦s3⟧ But he upon whom the stone falls, that is, he who utterly denies, is ground to powder, so that not even a shard remains. ⟦s4⟧ Or he falls upon it who now despises and does injuries: he does not yet utterly perish, but is shaken, so that he does not walk upright. ⟦s5⟧ The stone falls upon him whom, coming in judgment, it crushes.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And they sought,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ They seek to kill him as a liar, in which the things he had said are proved true. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself is the heir, and they the wicked husbandmen, who [0332C] are held back by human fear alone until his hour should come.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *And watching him.* ⟦s2⟧ What for fear of the people they cannot do by themselves, they wish to accomplish by empty helps, so that they may seem free from his death.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Is it lawful for us,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ When lately under Augustus Judaea had been made tributary to the Romans, the Pharisees, who applauded themselves for their righteousness, who gave tithes and first-fruits to God, denied that tributes were to be paid to a man; for which sedition their kingdom and their whole country were afterwards destroyed.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Whose image hath it,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ One is the image of God, another that of the world. ⟦s3⟧ If then Christ had not the image of Caesar, why did he pay the tribute? ⟦s4⟧ He did not pay of his own, but rendered to the world the things that were the world's. ⟦s5⟧ And thou, if thou dost not wish [0332D] to be liable to Caesar, do not have the things that are the world's. ⟦s6⟧ If thou hast riches, thou art liable to Caesar. ⟦s7⟧ If thou wishest to owe nothing to an earthly king, leave all that is thine and follow Christ. ⟦s8⟧ And well does he determine that first the things that are Caesar's are to be rendered. ⟦s9⟧ For no one can be the Lord's, unless he has first renounced the world.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Render therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ As Caesar exacts the impress of his own image, so also does God the soul sealed with the light of his countenance.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *And there came to him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ There were two heresies among the Jews: that of the Pharisees, who set foremost the righteousness of traditions and observances, whence also they were called the separated from the people; and that of the Sadducees, who likewise claimed for themselves what they were not, that is, just men.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0333A]VERS. 28.-- *Master.* ⟦s2⟧ They who do not believe the resurrection of bodies, and think that souls perish with bodies, invent the baseness of a tale, which convicts of raving those who assert the resurrection. ⟦s3⟧ But it may be that this once happened in their own nation. ⟦s4⟧ *That he may take,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ In the law of the letter one was compelled to marry in this life. ⟦s6⟧ But in the law of the spirit chastity is commended.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *But they,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Although all, both good and bad, are to rise again and to remain without marriage, yet the Lord, that he may stir men up to the glory of the resurrection, discourses of the elect alone. ⟦s3⟧ But if in the resurrection they shall neither take wives, then those bodies shall rise which are able to marry and to take in marriage. ⟦s4⟧ Which is said of those who have generative members, but shall be enslaved by no pleasure or necessity [0333B] of marrying. ⟦s5⟧ And note that although he speaks secret mysteries with liers-in-wait and tramplers, yet he does not give what is holy to dogs; but there were present many fit persons who ought not to be neglected because of the uncleanness of others. ⟦s6⟧ *Neither shall they marry,* etc. ⟦s7⟧ Since marriages are for the sake of children, children for the sake of succession, succession for the sake of death, where therefore there is no death, neither are there marriages.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *But that the dead shall rise again.* ⟦s2⟧ After he had satisfied the question proposed concerning the conditions of the resurrection, he treats of the resurrection itself against their unbelief.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *And after that,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Refuted in argument they ask no more, but openly seize him and deliver him to the Roman power; from which we understand [0333C] that the poisons of envy can indeed be overcome, but hardly be laid to rest.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *How do they say,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord, being about to complete his precepts, at the end of his testament concludes the faith, that we should believe the Lord Jesus, true God and true man, to sit at the right hand of the Father.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 43.-- *Till I make.* ⟦s2⟧ That his enemies are made subject by the Father signifies not the weakness of the Son, but the unity of nature, whereby in the one he works otherwise. ⟦s3⟧ For the Son also makes the enemies subject to the Father, because he glorifies the Father upon earth.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 46.-- *Beware of the scribes.* ⟦s2⟧ For a twofold reason we are bidden to beware of those greedy of vainglory: lest either we be deceived by their pretence, or be inflamed by their example to the love of temporal things. ⟦s3⟧ *And they love salutations [0333D] in the marketplace.* ⟦s4⟧ He does not forbid being saluted in the marketplace, nor the first seats, to those to whom they are due by the order of their office, but to those who love them unduly. ⟦s5⟧ For he reproves the mind, not the rank. ⟦s6⟧ Nor indeed is he without fault, if he wishes to take part in the lawsuits of the marketplace who desires to sit in the chair of Moses.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who devour,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Mark: *Under the pretence of long prayer,* they who, that they may appear the more religious, pray at greater length, where from any weak persons whatsoever, and from those troubled by the conscience of their own sins, they may receive as patrons both praises and moneys; whose prayer is turned into sin, so that not only can they not intercede for others, but neither profit themselves unto salvation; nay rather, for those very prayers they shall be the more damned. ⟦s3⟧ *These shall receive damnation.* ⟦s4⟧ In this he intimates that those also who stand praying in corners, [0334A] that they may be seen by men, do indeed deserve damnation, but these a greater.
