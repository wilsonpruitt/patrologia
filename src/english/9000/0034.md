## CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 1.-- *And looking on,* etc. He who had warned that the seekers of primacy and of vainglory must be guarded against, discerns also with a sure weighing those who bring gifts into the house of the Lord, that he may repay each one according to the intention of the heart.

VERS. 4.-- *Out of their abundance.* The Jew casts in out of abundance, who, presuming upon his own righteousness, prays thus within himself [n: (Luc. XVIII)]: *God, I give thee thanks that I am not as the rest of men.* The Church casts all her living into the gifts of God, she who understands all that she intimates to be not of her own merit but of the divine gift, saying: *God, [0334B] be merciful to me a sinner.*

VERS. 5.-- *And some.* After the widow's offering, in which the Church of the Gentiles is signified, the destruction of the temple is subjoined, so that, with the veil of the concealing letter, the hidden sacraments of the Church may begin to be disclosed. For God took care to overthrow the city and the temple and all figurative things, lest anyone after the coming of Christ should run back to them.

VERS. 7.-- *They asked him.* Because to those praising the buildings of the temple he had foretold that all was to be destroyed, the disciples (as Mark says), Peter, James, and John and Andrew, ask apart the time and the signs of the foretold destruction.

VERS. 8.-- *For many.* When the destruction of Jerusalem was at hand, there were many leaders who said that they were Christs [0334C] and that the time of liberty was drawing near. In the times of the apostles also many heresiarchs came forth, who among their other falsehoods preached that the day of the Lord was at hand, whom the Apostle also refuted.

VERS. 9.-- *Wars and seditions.* These abounded in Judaea from the passion of the Lord; and lest the apostles, terrified by them, should forsake Jerusalem and Judaea, they are warned *that the end is not immediately,* but after the forty-second year.

VERS. 10.-- *Then he said.* Of these, some are from heaven, some from the earth, some from the elements, some from men. Mystically. Kingdom against kingdom, and the pestilence of those whose speech creeps like a canker, and the famine of hearing the word of God, the shaking of the earth, the separation from the true faith, ought to be understood of heretics, who, fighting against [0334D] one another, make the victory of the Church. *And great signs.* These also are found fulfilled at that time in Josephus, who relates that a star like a sword hung over Jerusalem for a year, and that chariots and armed horsemen fought in the air for forty days, and that a heifer brought forth a lamb among the hands of those sacrificing.

VERS. 13.-- *And it shall happen,* etc. Of those who either persecute, or are not changed by their living. For the death of the just is to the good a help, to the wicked a testimony of damnation, so that the perverse may perish without excuse from that whence the elect take an example that they may live.

VERS. 16.-- *By parents,* etc. It is a greater torment when a man suffers from those upon whom he counted, [0335A] because along with the loss of the body the evils of lost charity torment him. And because the things foretold of the affliction of death are hard, a consolation concerning the joy of the resurrection is subjoined.

VERS. 18.-- *And a hair.* As if he said: Why do you fear that that should perish which hurts when cut, seeing that not even there can that perish in you which does not hurt when cut? For the flesh when cut hurts, the hair does not hurt.

VERS. 19.-- *In your patience.* Patience is the root and the guardian of all the virtues. In it therefore we possess our souls, which possess the body, when we govern those souls by reason unto suffering. But true patience is to endure with equanimity the evils of another, and to be moved by no grief against him who inflicts the evils.

VERS. 21.-- *And they that are in the countries.* The Ecclesiastical [0335B] History relates that the Christians who were in Judaea, when the destruction was at hand, were warned by an angel, and dwelt beyond Jordan in the city of Pella, until the desolation of Judaea should be fulfilled.

VERS. 23.-- *For there shall be distress,* etc. This distress and wrath endures to this day for that people scattered through all the nations. Yet it will not endure for ever.

VERS. 24.-- *Until they be fulfilled,* etc. That is, *Until the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in, and so all Israel be saved [n: (Rom. XI)]*, and as it were return rejoicing to the throne of its own birth. For what follows when the times of the nations are fulfilled and all Israel is saved, the Lord sets down in order. For these things also the apostles asked according to Matthew: not only the time of the overthrowing of the temple, but also the sign of his coming and [0335C] of the consummation of the world.

VERS. 25, 26.-- *And there shall be signs.* These things are of his coming itself, when all the virgins, wise and foolish, roused by an unwonted cry, trim their lamps, that is, count over their works with themselves, for which with the greatest fear they await the now impending outcome of the eternal decision. As the last judgment draws near, the sound of the sea and of the waves is confounded, and the round world is stained with husbandmen pressing on every side; the greatest lights of heaven, their rays struck with a new horror, veil their troubled face, because, as their own end draws near, the elements as if in dread waver and roar.

VERS. 27.-- *And then they shall see.* Him whom, set in humility, they would not hear, so that they may then feel his power the more strictly, the more that now they do not incline their hearts to his [0335D] patience. But because these things were said against the reprobate, he presently subjoins matter for the consolation of the elect.

VERS. 28.-- *But when these things begin to come to pass.* That is, when the plagues grow thick, when the terror of the judgment is shown by the shaking of the powers. *Lift up your heads.* Gladden your hearts, because, while the world to which you are not friends is being ended, the redemption which you seek is at hand. But that the world ought to be despised is made plain by a provident comparison.

VERS. 29.-- *See the fig tree.* He compares the kingdom of God to summer, because then the clouds of our sorrow shall pass away, and the days of eternal life shall shine under the brightness of the sun. For as from the fruit of the trees the nearness of summer [0336A] is known, so also from the ruin of the world the kingdom of God is known to be near. From these words it is shown that the fruit of the world is ruin. For it grows to this end, that it may fall; it buds to this end, that whatsoever it shall have budded it may consume with disasters.

VERS. 33.-- *Heaven and earth.* Nothing in the nature of bodies is more lasting than heaven and earth. Nothing passes more swiftly than speech. Words, so long as they are unfinished, are not words; if they be finished, they now are not at all: for they cannot be finished save by passing away. He says therefore: *Heaven and earth shall pass away.* As if he said: Everything that with you is lasting and without change is not lasting unto eternity, and everything that with me is seen to pass is held fixed and without passing, because my speech, which passes, expresses abiding [0336B] judgments. *But my words shall not pass away.* And since my words are thus without doubt to be fulfilled, see that, against the attestation of my words, you be not given over to surfeiting and drunkenness and the cares of this world, as certain men will be in the time of the impending judgment: of whom it is said: *When they shall say: Peace and security, sudden destruction shall come upon them [n: (I Thess. V)]*.

VERS. 37.-- *And he was,* etc. What he taught by word he confirms by example. He who, before the universal judgment and the uncertain departure of each man, warned men to renounce the pleasures and cares of the world, and to watch and to pray, himself, when his own passion was at hand, presses on with teaching, with watchings and with prayers, and those for whom he was to suffer he calls forth by his word and commends to the Father by his prayer. So we also, when among prosperous things we live justly and devoutly, let us not among [0336C] adverse things despair of the divine mercy. By day we teach in the temple, because we offer to the faithful the form of good work; by night we abide on the Mount of Olives, because in the darkness of straits we breathe again with the consolation of spiritual joy.
