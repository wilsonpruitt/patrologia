## CHAPTER XXII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *It drew near,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *Which is called the Pasch,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The Pasch is named not from the passion, but from the passing over, because on that day the destroyer passed through Egypt, or the Lord, delivering his people; by which name it was signified that the Lamb was on this day to pass out of the world to the Father. ⟦s4⟧ But between the Pasch and the unleavened bread this is the difference, that the Pasch is called that day alone on which the lamb was slain at evening, that is, the fourteenth moon [0336D] of the first month. ⟦s5⟧ The days of the unleavened bread were from the fifteenth moon to the twenty-first day of the same month. ⟦s6⟧ But the evangelists set them down indifferently, both the day of the Unleavened Bread for the Pasch, and the Pasch for the days of the Unleavened Bread; whence Luke here: *The feast day of the Unleavened Bread, which is called the Pasch [n: (Luc. XII)]*, because the days of the Pasch also were commanded to be celebrated with unleavened loaves. ⟦s7⟧ And we too, as it were keeping a perpetual Pasch, are bidden always to pass out of this world: the lamb having been immolated at evening on one day, there follow the seven days of the Unleavened Bread, because Christ, having suffered once for us in the fulness of times, commanded that throughout the whole time of the world (which is run in seven days) men should live in the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0337A]VERS. 2.-- *And they sought,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Matthew witnesses that these things were done two days before the Pasch, when the chief priests and scribes were gathered into the hall of Caiphas.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *He entered.* ⟦s2⟧ John says that after the morsel had been given by the Lord, Satan entered into Judas. ⟦s3⟧ But Satan had assailed him before the morsel also; and him whom he had first entered that he might deceive him, he afterwards entered that he might more fully possess one now delivered over to him.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And the day came.* ⟦s2⟧ Although he was crucified on the following day, that is, on the fifteenth moon, yet on this night on which the Lamb was immolated he both delivered to his disciples the sacraments of flesh and blood to be celebrated, and, held and bound by the Jews, hallowed the beginning of that same immolation, that is, of his passion.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0337B]VERS. 10.-- *Behold, as you go in.* ⟦s2⟧ As God, foreknowing all things, he speaks with his disciples of the things that are done elsewhere. ⟦s3⟧ And well does a man carrying a pitcher of water meet those who are to prepare the Pasch, that it may be shown that the mystery of this Pasch is to be celebrated for the washing of the world through the water of baptism, where for the worshippers of the true Pasch the typical heat is taken away from the threshold, and the baptism of the life-giving font is consecrated for the taking away of sins. ⟦s4⟧ The pitcher signifies the perfect measure of the fulfilled time in which it was fitting that these things be done. ⟦s5⟧ *A man a pitcher.* ⟦s6⟧ He is designated without a name, that he who is chosen by the Lord may be reckoned poor and ignoble. ⟦s7⟧ Whose holiness appears in this, that in his chamber the Lord with his disciples rests, with the delight of sublime virtues. ⟦s8⟧ *Follow him.* ⟦s9⟧ The names are of set purpose passed over, [0337C] both of the water-bearer and of the master of the house, that it may be signified that leave is to be given to all who wish to be steeped in the sacraments of Christ (which is to celebrate the Pasch), and who seek to receive Christ into the lodging of their own mind.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *A large dining room.* ⟦s2⟧ Spiritual is the law, which, going forth out of the straits of the earth, receives the Saviour in a lofty place. ⟦s3⟧ He who remains in the letter keeps the Pasch in the innermost places; but he who follows the water-bearer, that is, the herald of grace, into the house of the Church, passing beyond the surface of the letter, prepares a refreshment for Christ on the throne of the mind, while he understands that all the sacraments of the Pasch, and the other decrees of the law, were written of him.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *With desire I have desired,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He desires first to eat the typical Pasch, and so to declare to the world the mysteries of his [0337D] passion, that he may both stand forth as the approver of the ancient Pasch, and, showing that this had belonged to the figure of his own dispensation, the shadow should now cease at the coming of the truth. ⟦s3⟧ In figure of which thing the manna failed after the children of Israel had eaten of the fruits of the land, nor did they use that food any more.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *I will not eat.* ⟦s2⟧ I will no longer celebrate the Mosaic Pasch, until it be fulfilled, spiritually understood, in the Church, which is the kingdom of God. ⟦s3⟧ In this kingdom Christ eats to this day, when he exercises spiritually in his own members those things which Moses commanded the untaught people to observe carnally.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *And having taken the chalice.* ⟦s2⟧ This chalice belongs to the old Pasch, to which he was setting an end. ⟦s3⟧ Having taken which, [0338A] he gives thanks, namely for this reason, because the old things had passed away and all new things were to come. ⟦s4⟧ *He gave thanks.* ⟦s5⟧ He shows what each man ought to do under the scourge of his own fault, since he himself bore with equanimity the scourges of another's.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *For I say to you.* ⟦s2⟧ As the typical eating of the lamb, so also he denies that he will drink the typical drink of the Pasch, until, the glory of the resurrection being shown, the kingdom of God, that is, the faith of the world, shall come; that by the changing of these two sacraments he may teach that the other sacraments or commands of the law are to be transferred to a spiritual observance. ⟦s3⟧ Yet it can be taken simply, that from the hour of the supper until the time of the resurrection, at which he was to come in the kingdom of God, he was not to drink wine. ⟦s4⟧ For after the resurrection he ate and drank with his disciples.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0338B]VERS. 19.-- *And having taken.* ⟦s2⟧ The solemnities of the old Pasch being finished, he passes over to the new, which he desires to be frequented in the Church in memory of his own redemption, that in place of the flesh and blood of the lamb he may substitute the sacrament of his own flesh and blood under the figure of bread and wine. ⟦s3⟧ He breaks it and reaches it out, to show that the breaking of his own body would not be without his own will. ⟦s4⟧ He gives thanks for the old things ended and for the new to be begun. ⟦s5⟧ *Bread,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ Because bread strengthens the body, wine produces blood.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *But yet,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As he foretold of the passion, so he foretold also of the betrayer, that, seeing he could not lie hid, he might repent of the deed. ⟦s3⟧ Nor does he mark him out by name, lest, being openly corrected, he should become the more shameless. ⟦s4⟧ He casts the fault therefore into the number, that he who is conscious of it may do penance [0338C] he foretold the penalty also, that whom shame had not convicted, the punishments denounced might restrain. ⟦s5⟧ *The hand of him that betrayeth me.* ⟦s6⟧ After the example of Judas he betrays the Son of man who presumes to violate that inviolable sacrament of the Lord's body. ⟦s7⟧ He sells him who, having neglected the fear and the love of him, is convicted of loving things fallen and criminal

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *And there was,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The cause of the strife is unknown to us, but it is not incredible, etc., *as far as* let us see what the spiritual master has commanded.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AMBR.)] Let us therefore beware exceedingly, etc., *as far as* that there be no boasting about preferment, but a contention about humility.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *And he said to them.* ⟦s2⟧ The kindly master does not [0338D] accuse them of the strife begun, but describes the form of humility which they should follow; as if he said: You, after the manner of worldly men, wish to arrive at the possession of the kingdom through preferment; but the summit of the virtues is reached not by power, but by humility. ⟦s3⟧ But it is needful that the subject show correction, and mercy a mother, and discipline a father, or lest strictness be rigid, or mercy be remiss.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *For which is greater,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ To the words of the exhortation to be fulfilled he sets forth an example from himself. ⟦s3⟧ Hence John writes [n: (Joan. XIII)]: *If I have washed your feet, I your Lord and master,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ And in the word of ministering there can also be all the things which the Lord did in the flesh, not only by preaching or by bringing miracles to bear, but [0339A] also by laying down the soul which he took for us, that we too may learn to lay down our lives for one another.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *You have continued with me.* ⟦s2⟧ My weakness did not trouble you, but you persevered in the good you had begun. ⟦s3⟧ As therefore to me persevering unto death the Father gave *a name which is above every name [n: (Phil. III)]*, so I also will lead you persevering with me to the eternal kingdom. ⟦s4⟧ It is to be understood that Judas had already withdrawn, and that many others had already gone back, when the words of the incomprehensible sacrament had been heard, at the time when the Lord was speaking these things.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *And may sit upon thrones.* ⟦s2⟧ This is the change of the right hand of the Most High: that they who, being humble, now rejoice to minister to their fellow servants, may then, being exalted, be fed at the table of the Lord with perpetual banquets; and that they who in temptations, [0339B] being unjustly judged, remain with the Lord, may there come with him as just judges over their own tempters. ⟦s3⟧ For by how much they are the more despised here, by so much shall they rise there to a greater height of power. ⟦s4⟧ *Judging the twelve.* ⟦s5⟧ By the twelve, not twelve persons, but the multitude of those judging is signified, on account of the two parts of the sevenfold number, by which universality is signified, that is, three and four, by which twelve is multiplied.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *And the Lord said.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest the twelve should glory that they alone out of so many thousands were said to have continued with the Lord in temptations, he warns them that they could have been crushed by the same storm, had they not been protected by divine grace. ⟦s3⟧ But when Satan desires to tempt, and to shake them as one who cleanses wheat by winnowing, [0339C] we are taught that no one is tempted by the devil except by God's permitting it. ⟦s4⟧ *Behold, Satan,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Satan asks for the good that he may sift them, when he pants with the heats of malice for their affliction. ⟦s6⟧ For wherein, envying, he seeks their temptation, therein, entreating, he asks as it were their proving.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *But I have prayed.* ⟦s2⟧ Not that thou shouldst be tempted, but that thy faith fail not, that after the fall of denial thou mayest rise again by penance to thy former state. ⟦s3⟧ For it is profitable for the saints to be tried by temptations also, that either it may appear, when they are tempted, how strong they were, or that, their own weakness being known through temptation, they may learn to become stronger, and so, when they have been proved, may receive the crown of life.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Who said to him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because the Lord had said that he had prayed for Peter, Peter, conscious of his present affection [0339D] and of his fervent faith, but ignorant of the fall to come, does not believe that he can fall away from him. ⟦s3⟧ But he who knows what is in man, lest anyone should incautiously trust in his own state, or the more incautiously distrust because of a fall, as God foretells the manner, the time and the number of his denial, and as merciful promises the help of his own defence.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *When I sent you.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not instruct the disciples by the same rule of living in the time of persecution as in the time of peace. ⟦s3⟧ For when the disciples were sent to preach, he commanded that they should take nothing on the way, ordaining namely that he who *announces the Gospel should live by the Gospel.* ⟦s4⟧ But when the moment of death was at hand, and that whole nation was persecuting both the shepherd and the flock together, he decrees a rule suited to the time, [0340A] permitting the necessaries of living, until, the madness of the persecutors being lulled, the time of evangelizing should return.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *But now,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In this an example is given to us also, that sometimes, when a cause presses, we may without fault leave off certain things from the strictness of our purpose. ⟦s3⟧ For example: if we journey through inhospitable regions, it is lawful to carry more for provision than we should have at home. ⟦s4⟧ *And he that hath not.* ⟦s5⟧ He bids them either to take the sword they have, or, not having it, to buy it; that readers may know that the power of resisting was not wanting to the disciples, but that rather the love of suffering was in the master; where, even if there were no other cause for drawing the sword, this one can suffice, that, the servant's ear having been cut off, it should be healed by the Lord's touch. ⟦s6⟧ In which we are warned [0340B] that we, being struck, should not maintain anger, but rising again should take up faith.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *Behold two swords.* ⟦s2⟧ Two swords are brought forth, one of the New, the other of the Old Testament, wherewith we are fortified against the snares of the devil. ⟦s3⟧ And it is said: *It is enough.* ⟦s4⟧ Two swords suffice for a testimony that the Saviour suffered willingly. ⟦s5⟧ The one, which should teach both that boldness to contend for the Lord was in the apostles, and that, the ear having been torn off by the stroke of the sword, mercy and the power of healing were in the Lord even when he was about to die. ⟦s6⟧ The other, which, being by no means drawn from the scabbard, should show that they were not permitted to do all that they could for his defence.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *And going out.* ⟦s2⟧ The Lord, about to be betrayed by a disciple, goes to the accustomed place, where he could easily be found, lest he should be thought to have feared death. ⟦s3⟧ And well does he lead out [0340C] to the Mount of Olives the disciples steeped in the mysteries of his blood and of his body, that he might mark out all who are baptized in his death as to be confirmed by the highest charism of the Holy Spirit.
