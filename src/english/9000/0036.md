## CHAPTER XXII. (cont.)

VERS. 40.-- *Pray.* It is impossible for a man not to be tempted; nor does he say, that you be not tempted, but, *lest you enter into temptation. Pray,* that is, that temptation may not overcome you and hold you within its nets.

VERS. 41.-- *And he himself,* etc. The precept of praying having been given to the apostles, he himself also was drawn away from them. He alone prays for all who alone was about to suffer for all, intimating that his prayer stands as far from ours as his passion also does. *A stone's cast,* etc. In this he mystically intimates that they should direct the stone unto the selfsame point, that is, [0340D] that they should carry as far as him the intention of the law, which had been written on stone: for as far as him can that stone reach, since *the end of the law is Christ to everyone that believeth unto justice [n: (Rom. X)]*.

VERS. 42.-- *He prayed, saying.* He prays not so much from fear of suffering as from mercy toward the former people, that he might not drink from that people the cup proffered him. Whence he says also pointedly: *This one,* that is, the cup of the people of the Jews, who have no excuse of ignorance in the matter of my death, since I am daily announced to them in the law and the prophets. If therefore it can come to pass that the multitude of the Gentiles believe without the destruction of the Jews, I refuse the passion. But if they must be blinded that others may see, not my will but thine be done.

*Father, if thou wilt,* etc. [n: (AMBR.)] For me he grieved, when for [0341A] himself he had nothing to grieve over. For he took upon him my sadness that he might lavish upon me his own gladness. Confidently therefore do I name sadness, I who preach the cross.

*Nevertheless,* etc. As the Lord drew near to the passion, he took upon himself the voice and the fear of the weak, that he might draw them off: and again through obedience he showed the fortitude of the mind, so that when what we do not wish to be done presses upon us, we may so ask through weakness that it be not done, that yet through fortitude we may be ready to fulfil the Creator's will rather than our own.

VERS. 44.-- *And his sweat became.* With death drawing near he expressed in himself the struggle of our mind, we who deservedly suffer a certain force unto terror, when after a little while we are about to find what may last for ever. [0341B] *Of blood running down to the ground.* By the earth watered with blood it is declared that he was already obtaining the effect of his prayer: namely, that he might cleanse by his own blood the faith of the disciples, which earthly frailty was still convicting; and that whatever of scandal that faith had endured from his death, all this he might himself blot out by dying; and moreover might raise up to life the whole world, dead in its sins.

VERS. 47.-- *That he might kiss him.* The Lord receives the kiss, not that he may teach us to dissemble, but that he might not seem to shun even the betrayer, and might the more affect the betrayer, to whom he would not deny the offices of love. Whence it is said: *With them that hated peace I was peaceable [n: (Psal. CXIX)]*.

VERS. 49.-- *Lord, shall we strike with the sword.* [0341C] The order is this: when they had asked, saying, *Lord, shall we strike with the sword,* then he answered: *Suffer ye thus far.* That is, let not what is to come move you. They are to be permitted to go forward thus far, that they may seize me and the things written of me be fulfilled. But between the delays of the words of those asking and of him answering, Peter, stirred by a greater eagerness of defence, struck on the Lord's behalf. But things which could be done at the same time could not be said at the same time. It is not therefore said, Suffer ye thus far, as though the deed of Peter had pleased the Lord, since in Matthew he is read to have said: *Put back thy sword into its place,* which it appears Jesus said after the deed.

VERS. 50.-- *The servant,* etc. Mystically: the servant is the people of the Jews, made over, namely unduly, to their princes, [0341D] who in the Lord's passion lost the right ear, that is, spiritual understanding. Content with the left, that is, with the cheapness of the letter, Peter not taking it away, but the divine judgment disclosing that it had been taken away. Which ear is restored, by the Lord's mercy, in those who chose rather to believe.

VERS. 52.-- *They were come to him,* etc. According to the other evangelists the chief priests and the magistrates had not come to Jesus, but had waited in the hall of Caiphas. But because these men so dealt with the Lord's slaying that they might seem as it were guiltless, since he was betrayed by a disciple, seized by a tribune and by the crowds, condemned by the governor, crucified by the Romans, and Barabbas even chosen by the peoples, the evangelist, wishing them above all [0342A] to be the guilty, by whose counsel all these things were being carried on, says that they came to seize the Saviour, they who came by the power of their own commanding. *As to a robber,* etc. It is foolish to seek with swords and clubs one who of his own accord offers himself to their hands, and to hunt out by a betrayer, as one lurking by night and shunning your eyes, him who daily teaches in the temple. But for this reason do you gather against me in the darkness, because your power, with which you are armed against the light, is in darkness.

VERS. 54.-- *Of the high priest.* Of Caiphas, who had bought for himself the pontificate of a single year at a price from the Roman prince: yet first to Annas, as John says. *He followed afar off.* Deservedly afar off, he who close at hand was about to deny. It signifies that the Church would imitate [0342B] Christ's passion far differently, because the Church suffers for herself, Christ for the Church.

VERS. 55.-- *A fire in the midst.* The fire of cupidity, kindled at the prompting of the malignant spirit, was arming in the hall of Caiphas the tongues of the faithless to deny and to blaspheme God. For what the malignant synod was carrying on within, in the house of the high priest, this the fire kindled outside amid the colds of the night was typically showing. Peter, numbed for the hour by this cold, desired as it were to be warmed by the coals of the servants, because he sought the solace of temporal advantage in the fellowship of the faithless. But this faithlessness he left when the Lord looked upon him; and after the resurrection, at the catch of fishes, when he had seen the coals laid and the fish laid upon them, he set his heart aflame with the coals of love.

[0342C]VERS. 56.-- *A certain maidservant.* Why does a maidservant betray him first, when men could better have recognized him? Unless that this sex might be shown to have sinned in the Lord's slaying, being about to redeem through the passion. And therefore a woman first received the mystery of the resurrection, and keeps the commands, that she might do away the old error of the transgression.

VERS. 57.-- *But he denied him.* Not on the mountain, not in the temple, not in his own house does Peter deny, but in the praetorium of the Jews, in the house of the high priest, where there is no truth, where Christ is taken and bound. What else than that he should err, whom the doorkeeper of the Jews let in? Eve ill led Adam in; a woman ill brought Peter in.

VERS. 59, 60.-- *Man, I know not what thou sayest.* Not only does he deny Christ who says that he is not the Christ, [0342D] but also he who, though he be one, denies that he is a Christian; and so is fulfilled what the Lord says: *Thou shalt deny me. He is Galliaeus.* Men of Jerusalem and Galileans are alike Hebrews and of one tongue, but each several province has its own properties, and cannot change the homeborn sound of its speech. *And immediately.* After the third denial the cock's crow follows. The sacraments of things are designated by the state of the times. At midnight he denies, at the cock's crow he repents. After the resurrection, under the light, him whom he had thrice denied he thrice professed that he loved; because he who had erred in the darkness of forgetfulness corrected it by the remembrance of the light now hoped for, and by the presence of that same true light fully raised up whatever he had supposed.

[0343A]VERS. 61.-- *And the Lord turning.* By his look he provoked him to tears, as it were bringing back to his mind how often he had denied, and what he had foretold him; nor could he remain in darkness upon whom the light of the world looked.

VERS. 62.-- *And going forth without.* Shut off from the council of the impious he washes his guilt, who was permitted not within, and for this reason, to waver: that in the chief of the Church a remedy of penance might be founded, and that no one might dare to trust in his own virtue.

VERS. 63.-- *They mocked him,* etc. The prophecy was fulfilled: *With a rod shall the cheek of the judge of Israel be smitten.* And likewise: *I have given my cheek to blows.* [var: Vulg. Is 50:6 *genas meas vellentibus*, "my cheeks to them that plucked them"] But he who was then beaten by the buffets of the Jews is now beaten by the blasphemies of false Christians. They veiled his face, not that he might not see their crimes, but that they might hide from themselves (as they once did [0343B] to Moses) the grace of the knowledge of him: *For if they believed Moses, they would believe the Lord also [n: (Joan. VIII)]*. This veil remains upon their hearts until this day, but from those who believe it has been taken away: when he died the veil of the temple was rent.

VERS. 64.-- *Prophesy.* A contumely against him who called himself a prophet. But both heretics and Jews, who until this day deny Christ, and evil Christians who exasperate him by reprobate deeds, while they suppose that their thoughts and works of darkness are not seen by him, say as it were in mockery: *Prophesy: Who is it that struck thee?*

VERS. 66.-- *If thou be the Christ.* They do not desire the truth, but prepare a calumny. They understood that the Christ would be born of David a mere man. [0343C] According to this, therefore, they ask, so that if he should say, I am the Christ, they might calumniate him for arrogating royal power to himself.

VERS. 67.-- *If I shall tell you.* He had often said that he was the Christ. He had also asked them how they said that Christ was the son of David, when David calls him Lord, that by this he might provoke them to believe him God: but they were willing neither to believe him speaking, nor to answer him asking, nor to let him go who was guiltless.

VERS. 69.-- *And hereafter it shall be.* They who sought to calumniate the seed of David hear yet more: that through contempt he is to come to glory.

VERS. 70.-- *You say.* So does he temper his answer [0343D] that he both speaks the truth and gives no opening to calumny. He chose rather to prove himself Christ the Son of God than to say it, that the ground of condemning might be taken away from those who confess the very thing they lay against him.

VERS. 71.-- *For we ourselves have heard.* Where he said: *The Son of man shall be sitting at the right hand of the power of God.* By their own sentence do they condemn themselves who deliver to death him whom by the testimony of their mouth and of their work they acknowledge to be God.
