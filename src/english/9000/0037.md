## CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- *They led him away.* They handed him over to the Roman power, that in this manner the Jews might show themselves strangers to his killing, wherein not their innocence but their madness is shown.

VERS. 3.-- *And Pilate.* etc. Since two things had been objected [0344A] against him, namely that he forbade the assigned dues to be rendered, and that he called himself Christ, Pilate was able to hear what he had said about the tributes: *Render the things that are Caesar's to Caesar,* and thence, counting this as an open falsehood of no weight, he asked only about what he did not know.

*Thou sayest it.* With the same word with which he answered the chief priests he answers the governor also, that by their own sentence both may be condemned.

VERS. 4.-- *I find no cause.* The day before he suffered, the Lord says to the disciples: *The prince of this world cometh, and he hath not anything in me [n: (Luc. XX)]*. But since the prince, that is Pilate, absolves him, in whom he finds nothing worthy of condemnation, see what the Jews do, who search out the truth not from love of equity, but labour to condemn the just man at the goad of envy.

[0344B]VERS. 5.-- *He stirreth up the people.* They accuse not him, but themselves. For to have taught the people, and by teaching to have stirred them from the former sluggishness of the body, and by such an act to have passed through from Galilee as far as Judea, that is the whole land of promise, from end to end, is a judgment not of crime but of virtue. And so Pilate, thinking that the Saviour was not even to be questioned about this, but rather having got the occasion, desires to make himself free from judging him.

VERS. 7.-- *And when he knew it.* Lest he should be forced to give sentence against a guiltless man, and one whom he knew to have been handed over out of envy, he sends him to Herod to be heard, that he might be either absolved or condemned by his own tetrarch; but, lest any excuse should remain to the Jews, Herod too, who was a Jew by birth and by religion, was permitted to show what he thought [0344C] about Christ, so that both regions, both Judea, in which Christ was born, and Galilee, in which he had been reared, might be shown to have conspired in his slaying.

*But he answered him nothing.* He was silent and did nothing, because both Herod's unbelief did not deserve to see divine things, and the Lord was avoiding ostentation. And in Herod are signified all the impious, who do not believe the law and the prophets, and cannot see the works of Christ in the Gospel. Therefore, while the chief priests accuse him, he answers few things before Pilate, none before Herod, lest, washing away the charge and being dismissed by the prince, he should put off the profit of the cross.

VERS. 11.-- *And he mocked him.* That he is despised and mocked in a white garment signifies that for the sins of the whole world [0344D] the lamb was offered up in chaste flesh, and suffered, and was buried: that he is mocked here clothed in a white garment, and, according to the other Evangelists, in a purple or scarlet one, marks the twofold kind of martyrdom with which the Church is adorned in her sufferings, so that she is white in action, red with blood. For he himself, blooming with the little flowers of his members, brings forth lilies in peace, roses in war. *And he sent him back to Pilate.* He is sent to Herod, he is sent back to Pilate; and although each of the two does not pronounce him guilty, yet each complies with the goads of another's iniquity.

VERS. 12.-- *And they were made friends.* The abominable compact which Herod and Pilate struck in the killing of Christ, their successors keep by hereditary right, while Jews and Gentiles, disagreeing in mind as they do in race and religion, nevertheless agree in persecuting Christians [0345A] and in destroying the faith of Christ. In the type of Herod and Pilate, who were made friends through Jesus, the figure of the Jews and of the Gentiles is preserved: so that through Christ's passion a concord of both peoples was to come about, yet in such wise that the Gentile should first receive the kingdom of God, and pass on to the Jew the devotion of his own faith, that they too might clothe with the glory of his majesty the body of Christ, whom before they had despised.

VERS. 14.-- *You have presented.* Mark how great an eagerness Pilate has to release Jesus. First, when they accuse him, he says that he has found no cause in him; then, when they persist in what they have begun, he sends him to Herod, to explore whether he can find any wickedness in him, or whether he decides that he should be released. Then, when Herod's will is known, and no crime found in Jesus, [0345B] he seeks the sentence of the people, to whom one must be released at the Pasch. Again also a third time, wishing to release him, he asks. But the more carefully he seeks to release him, the more criminal does he find them for his death.

VERS. 15.-- *No, nor Herod either.* To reproach the hardness of the Jews or of the Gentiles, Pilate confesses that neither he nor Herod found anything in killing and mocking Christ, but that he complied with the shouts of another's cruelty.

VERS. 16.-- *I will chastise him therefore,* etc. Pilate absolves Jesus, whom he proves guiltless; but that the Scriptures might be fulfilled, he absolves indeed by judgment him whom nevertheless he crucifies in a mystery.

VERS. 18.-- *Away with this man,* etc. Because in place of Jesus they chose a robber, in place of the Saviour a murderer, in place of the giver of life [0345C] a taker of it away, they deservedly lost salvation and life; and they gave themselves over to robberies and seditions so far that they lost both the homeland and the kingdom which they had preferred to Christ, and to this day have not received back the liberty of soul or of body which they had sold. *Barabbas,* etc. Barabbas, who is interpreted the son of their master or of their father, is himself Antichrist, whom they will prefer to Christ, they to whom it is said: *You are of your father the devil.* Pilate is interpreted the mouth of the hammerer, and signifies the devil, who is the hammer of all matter, through whom God permits the vessels to be tested, and at last crushes him, when he breaks him in the final damnation.

VERS. 21.-- *Crucify him, crucify him.* Great cruelty: they seek not only to kill but to crucify him, [0345D] so that with hands and feet fastened to the wood he might be slain by a lingering death, lest the pain should end too quickly, and that he might long be seen upon the cross.

VERS. 22.-- *I will chastise him,* etc. This chastisement he offered to the desires of those abominable men by way of mockery and scourging, that they might not rage as far as crucifying. This both the words of John the evangelist and the very column testify, to which the Lord was bound, which to this day shows sure signs of the Lord's blood to those who look upon it. But although Pilate does this, the insatiable fury of the sacrilegious does not cease, but presses the harder, because they had seen the accusation which they had brought against the Lord emptied out by Pilate's careful questioning. At length the shameless men turn themselves to entreaties alone, [0346A] that what they had not been able to do by accusing or as it were by reasoning, they now accomplish by demanding and by shouting and by accomplishing.

VERS. 25.-- *And he released,* etc. The author of sedition and of murders is released to the Jews, that is, the devil, who for his fault had been driven from the homeland of light and sent into the prison of darkness; and therefore they cannot have peace, because they did not choose the author of peace.

VERS. 26.-- *They laid hold of one,* etc. First the Lord carried the cross, but afterwards it was laid upon Simon, whom they met as they went out; and this fittingly: *For Christ suffered for us, that you should follow his steps [n: (I Petr. II)]*. But he who wishes to follow him, let him take up his cross and follow. But because Simon [0346B] was not an Israelite but a Cyrenean, by him the Gentiles are designated, formerly strangers and guests to the testaments, but now in the faith citizens and of the household of God and coheirs of Christ; whence Simon is interpreted obedient, heir of Cyrene. But Simon came from the country place, that is from the village (*pagus*), from which come the pagans, who, strangers to the city of God, are ignorant of city conversation. But Simon, going forth from the village, carries the cross after Jesus, when the peoples of the Gentiles, having forsaken pagan rites, obediently embrace the footsteps of the Lord's passion.

VERS. 27.-- *There followed him,* etc. But they do not follow the cross with the same mind: the wicked follow that they may look with gladness on him dying; the women, that they might weep for him whom they desired to live, as about to die, as dying, and as dead. Nor is the lamentation of the women alone brought in on this ground, [0346C] since many men also were grieving, but because the female sex, as more contemptible, could the more freely display before the chief priests who were present what it felt against them. But because *the Lord knoweth who are his [n: (II Tim. II)]*, passing by the crowd of the raging people, he turns his eyes and his face to the women who loved and lamented him. *Who bewailed him.* They were sorrowing for a mortal life, which was to be ended by death, not for the blind men who were killing the physician.

VERS. 28.-- *Daughters of Jerusalem.* Note, when he calls them sons of Jerusalem, that not only those women who had come with him from Galilee, but also many women, citizens of that same city, had attached themselves to him. *Weep not.* Do not lament me as about to die, whose resurrection can quickly loose death, whose death will destroy death and the very [0346D] author of death. Rather wash yourselves and your offspring with worthy fountains of tears, lest with the faithless you be condemned in vengeance for my cross.

VERS. 29.-- *Behold, the days shall come.* The days of the Roman siege and captivity. It is natural, when captivity threatens and the enemy's slaughter rages through the fields and the cities, that all who are able to escape seek out any high or hidden refuges in which they may hide themselves.

VERS. 30.-- *Fall upon us.* This can be understood, by way of surplus, of those who in either sex have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven.

VERS. 31.-- *For if in the green wood.* The green wood is Christ and his elect, the dry the impious and sinners; whence he says: If then I, who have committed no sin, who [0347A] am deservedly called the tree of life and bear twelve fruits of grace in each several month, do not go out of this world without the fire of the passion, what torments await those who are empty of fruits, and moreover do not fear to give the tree of life to the flames? And *if now is the time that judgment should begin at the house of God, and all who wish to live godly in Christ suffer persecution,* what is the end of those who do not believe the Gospel? [n: (I Petr. IV; I Tim. III)].

VERS. 32.-- *And there were led also.* That is fulfilled which was said: *And he was reputed with the wicked [n: (Isa. LIII)]*. In death he is reputed with the wicked, that in the resurrection he might quicken the wicked, he who, made man for the sake of men, gave to men the power to become the sons of God.

VERS. 33.-- *The place of Calvary.* Outside the gate of Jerusalem [0347B] were places in which the heads of the condemned were struck off, and they were called places of Calvary, that is, of the beheaded. There he is crucified, that, there was the ground of the condemned, the standards of martyrdom might be raised up. *And the robbers,* etc. Execrable in the deed is the iniquity of the Jews, which crucifies the Redeemer of all as though a robber; yet in the mystery he is a good robber, who lay in wait for the devil, to carry off his vessels. By him who is on the right hand is signified the mortification of the flesh for heavenly glory; by him who is on the left, the mortification of the flesh for some cause of this world.

VERS. 34.-- *And Jesus,* etc. Because Luke set himself to write Christ's priesthood, rightly does the Lord intercede with God for his persecutors by the right of a priest, and open paradise to the robber who confesses him; nor does he pray in vain, [0347C] but obtains it for those who believed after his passion. He does not pray for those who through envy and pride understood him to be the Son of God, and deny and crucify him, but for those who, having a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge, knew not what they did.

*Dividing.* Of these John speaks more fully. The fourfold garment signifies the Church, which consists of four parts, spread through the whole world, distributed equally, that is, harmoniously, to all the parts. The tunic allotted signifies the unity of all the parts, which is held together by the bond of charity. *Lots.* In the lot the grace of God is commended. For thus it came in one to all, when the lot pleased all; because the grace of God also comes to all in unity, and when the lot is cast it is credited not to anyone's person or merits, but to the hidden [0347D] judgment of God.
