## CHAPTER XXIII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *He saved others.* ⟦s2⟧ By their own sentence they condemn themselves, who even against their will confess that he saved others. ⟦s3⟧ He could indeed have saved himself, but because he himself is the Christ, the elect of God, he was unwilling to come down from the cross. ⟦s4⟧ For he who came for this, that he might be crucified for us, for this reason neglected to save himself from the cross: because by dying he took care to save, along with the rest of sinners, even those who crucified him.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *And the soldiers also mocked him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Note that the Jews deride the name of Christ and of the Son of God, handed down to them by the authority of Scripture. ⟦s3⟧ But the soldiers, ignorant of the Scriptures, insult not Christ the elect of God, but the king of the Jews. ⟦s4⟧ *And offering him vinegar.* ⟦s5⟧ Vinegar [0348A] were the Jews, degenerating from the wine of the patriarchs. ⟦s6⟧ With this vinegar the soldiers give the Lord to drink, whom at their prompting they deliver to death.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *And there was also,* etc. ⟦s2+3⟧ These three tongues were pre-eminent above the rest: Hebrew, because of the Jews glorying in the law; Greek, because of the wisdom of the Gentiles; Latin, because of the Romans holding command. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore, will they or nill they, the Jews, every kingdom of the world, all worldly wisdom, and all the divine sacraments of the law bear witness that Jesus is the emperor of those who believe and confess him. ⟦s5⟧ *This is the king of the Jews.* ⟦s6⟧ The title which witnesses that Christ is king is set not below but above the cross, because although on the cross he was suffering in the weakness of a man, yet above the cross he was shining in the majesty of a king; and therefore, when he had been sacrificed, the title was set above him, that it might lie open to all who wished to read, [0348B] that is, to believe, that through the cross he did not lose his empire but strengthened it.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *He rebuked him.* ⟦s2⟧ The rebuke itself reveals the coming scandal of the cross even among believers, of whom some are on the right hand, others on the left.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *Lord, remember me.* ⟦s2⟧ Great grace shines out in this robber. ⟦s3⟧ He has no member free from the torment except his heart and his tongue; the whole of what he has free, he offers. ⟦s4⟧ With the heart he believes, with the mouth he confesses. ⟦s5⟧ He had faith, who believed that he would reign whom he saw dying beside him. ⟦s6⟧ He had hope, who asked for entrance. ⟦s7⟧ He held charity firmly, who both convicted the robber concerning his own iniquity and preached to him the life which he had come to know. ⟦s8⟧ Great is his virtue: he confesses him whom he sees dying in human weakness, whom the apostles were denying, [0348C] whom they had seen working miracles by divine power.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 43.-- *Amen I say to thee.* ⟦s2⟧ A most beautiful example of a conversion to be sought after, in that pardon is so quickly loosed to the robber, and the grace is more abundant than the petition. ⟦s3⟧ For the Lord always bestows more than he is asked. ⟦s3⟧ He was asking: *Remember me,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ The Lord says: *This day thou shalt be with me,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Quickly does the Lord pardon, because that man is quickly converted.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 44.-- *And there was darkness,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The sun drew back its rays, either that it might not see the Lord hanging, or that the impious blasphemers might not enjoy its light. ⟦s3+4⟧ And note that at the sixth hour, that is, when the sun was about to withdraw from the centre of the world, he is crucified; at daybreak, that is, when the sun rises, he rises again, because *he died for our sins, and rose again for our justification [n: (Rom. XVIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Rom 4:25 *qui traditus est propter delicta nostra*, "who was delivered up for our offences"] When Adam [0348D] had sinned after midday, that is, when the light of faith had declined, the voice of the Lord is heard; at the same moment of time, therefore, at which he had then shut paradise against the sinning Adam, the Lord now opens it to the penitent robber.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 45, 46.-- *The veil of the temple,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This was done as the Lord breathed forth his spirit, but Luke anticipates, wishing to join the miracle of the sun to the miracle of the veil. ⟦s3⟧ And the veil is rent, that the ark of the testament and all the sacraments of the law which were covered might appear and pass over to the Gentiles. ⟦s4⟧ Josephus says that the angelic powers, once the guardians of the temple, then cried out together: *Let us flee from these seats. ⟦s5⟧ Father,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ Calling upon the Father, he declares himself the Son of God. ⟦s7⟧ Commending his spirit, he intimates not a failure of power, but confidence in the same [0349A] together with a share of power. ⟦s8⟧ For he loves to give glory to the Father, wherein he forms us, that we may give glory to the Creator.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *And the centurion seeing.* ⟦s2⟧ Seeing that he thus had the power of sending forth his spirit, which none has but the Maker of souls, the very man who had crucified him, in the very scandal of the passion, confesses him the Son of God, whom the Jews after so many miracles refused to believe. ⟦s3⟧ Whence by the centurion is designated the faith of the Church, which, when the veil of the heavenly mysteries had been unlocked by the Lord's death, straightway affirms him just and the Son of God, while the Synagogue is silent.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 48.-- *Striking their breasts.* ⟦s2⟧ That they strike their breasts as a sign of penitence and of mourning can be understood in two ways. ⟦s3⟧ For either they were grieving that one whose life they had loved [0349B] had been unjustly slain; or, remembering that they had procured his death, they were trembling that he was the more glorified in that death. ⟦s4⟧ But whatever cause compelled them to beat the breast, let us see the distance between nation and nation. ⟦s5⟧ The Gentiles, fearing God as the Lord died, glorify him with the voice of open confession; the Jews, only striking their breasts, go home in silence.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 50.-- *Named Joseph.* ⟦s2⟧ Joseph is worthy, through the nobility of his worldly power, to receive the body; through the justice of his merits, to bury it.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 52.-- *This man went,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not the apostles, but Joseph, just and steadfast, and Nicodemus, in whom was no guile, bury him. ⟦s3⟧ Such too is the burial of Christ, which has in it no fraud or iniquity; and in this the shuffling of the Jews is blocked. ⟦s4⟧ For if [0349C] the apostles had buried him, they would say that he was not buried whom they said was carried off.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 53.-- *In a winding-sheet.* ⟦s2⟧ From the simple burial of the Lord the ambition of the rich is condemned, who cannot do without riches even when dead.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically: he wraps Jesus in a clean winding-sheet who receives him with a pure mind. ⟦s2⟧ Hence also the custom of the Church holds that the sacrifice of the altar be celebrated not on silk, not on dyed cloth, but on pure linen.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And laid him.* ⟦s2⟧ Well, then, is Christ laid in the tomb of a just man, that the Son of man may have where to lay his head, and may rest in the dwelling of justice. ⟦s3⟧ Well in a new one, that he who receives Christ may walk in newness of life. ⟦s4⟧ No one buries Christ save he who believes in Christ.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In a monument that was hewn.* ⟦s2⟧ The Lord alone is enclosed in the tomb, [0349D] that his burial and resurrection might be shown unlike the frailty of our nature. ⟦s3⟧ He was buried, but for as long as he willed; he rose again, but when he willed. ⟦s4⟧ *Wherein not yet.* ⟦s5⟧ If he were laid in a monument where another had been laid, another would be feigned to have risen. ⟦s6⟧ Hewn in the rock, lest, had it been built of many stones, and the foundations of the tomb dug away, he should be said to have been carried off by theft.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 54.-- *And it was the day,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ On the sixth day man was made; on the sabbath God rested. ⟦s3⟧ Crucified therefore on the sixth day, he fulfils the secret of the preparation. ⟦s4⟧ Resting on the sabbath in the sepulchre, he awaits the event of the resurrection. ⟦s5⟧ So must we in the sixth age of the world suffer for the Lord. ⟦s6⟧ In the seventh, that is, when we die, the body remains in the tomb, [0350A] the soul with the Lord in rest after its labours, until in the eighth the bodies rise again.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 55.-- *The women.* ⟦s2⟧ When the other acquaintances of Jesus withdrew after the body was taken down, the women alone, who loved more closely, were looking upon the office of the burial, that at a fitting time they might offer the gift of their devotion, awaiting what Jesus had promised; and therefore they were the first to see him.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 56.-- *And returning.* ⟦s2⟧ Having looked upon the Lord's burial, they return and prepare spices and ointments: they who, having read, heard and called to mind the Lord's passion, turn themselves to the works of the virtues in which Christ delights, and on the sabbath keep silence. ⟦s3⟧ Being about to come to the Lord after the sabbath with their gifts, when the preparation-day of the present life is finished they await, rejoicing in blessed rest, the time [0350B] of the resurrection, at which they run to meet Christ with the fragrant spices of their deeds. ⟦s4⟧ *And on the sabbath,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ It had been commanded that the silence of the sabbath be kept from evening until evening. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore the women, when the Lord was buried, so long as it was lawful to work, are busied on the preparation-day in making ready the ointments until the setting of the sun; and, the sabbath being over at the going down of the sun, that coming in the morning they might anoint him, because on the evening of the sabbath, the moment of night already coming on, they could not go to the monument.
