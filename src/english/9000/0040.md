## CHAPTER XXIV. (cont.)

VERS. 43.-- *And when he had eaten.* He takes away all evasions [0354C]: he was seen, he was touched, he ate; and lest in anything he should seem to have deluded the human senses, he puts his hands to the Scriptures, so that even if it should seem, as the pagans say, that by magic arts he was able to do what he willed, they may at least see that he could not have prophesied before he was born.

VERS. 45.-- *Then he opened.* After the sight, after the touch, after the law recalled to mind, he opened their understanding, that they might understand what they see and read; then, after the truth of his own body had been commended, he commends the unity of the Church: *And that there should be preached in his name.*

VERS. 46.-- *It behoved,* etc. It behoved that the ministers who were to preach penance and remission [0354D] unto all nations should begin from Jerusalem. Not only because to them were entrusted the oracles of God, and because theirs is the adoption of sons, and the glory the testaments, and the law-giving; but for this reason also, that the Gentiles, entangled in various errors and crimes, might by this token above all of the divine loving-kindness be roused to the hope of pardon: that he grants pardon to those who crucified the Son of God.

VERS. 49.-- *But stay you.* After he had perfectly instructed the disciples about the power of preaching, although he could of a sudden, if he willed, have strengthened them, he put it off, saying: *But stay you.* That he might give an example to those who follow, lest the imperfect presume to preach before the time. He sits in the city who binds himself within the cloisters of the mind, lest by speaking he wander abroad outside; so that when he has [0355A] been perfectly clothed with the divine power, then he may go forth as it were out of himself, by instructing others.

VERS. 50.-- *And he led them out.* Passing over all that was done through the forty days, he silently joins to the first day of the resurrection the last, on which he ascended into heaven. And beautifully, being about to bless the disciples, he leads them out to Bethany, which is called the house of obedience: because he who came down on account of the disobedience of the perverse, ascended on account of the obedience of the converted.

VERS. 53.-- *In the temple.* That in the place of prayer, amid the devotions of praise, with hearts made ready in all things, they might await the promised coming of the Holy Spirit. So [0356A] let us, after the solemnities of the passion and the resurrection have been celebrated in Jerusalem, that is, in the vision of peace, seek Bethany with the Lord for our leader, that with a quiet mind we may be steeped in the sacraments of his body and blood, and may take care to be a house of obedience.

*Praising and blessing.* Luke, who took upon himself to set forth Christ's priesthood more fully than the rest, and therefore began his Gospel from the ministry of the temple through the priesthood of Zachary, beautifully completed it in the devotion of the temple, when he concludes with the apostles there, ministers, namely, of the new priesthood to come, not in the blood of victims, but in the praise of God and in blessing.
