## §1. THE POLICY THIS STINT APPLIED — stated before the findings, so it can be argued with

### 1.1 Markers

- **`[sic:]` fired: ZERO.** Deliberately, per the launch brief §2. Every suspect word — non-word,
  broken word, or real word that reads oddly — is in the **declined-oddity list at §5**, with its
  column, named and *unattributed*. I do not know whose any of them is, and I say so per site.
  On John the 23 leaf-reads spent on fired markers produced no corrections and the nineteen
  declined oddities produced all seventeen; I am doing what John's stints did.
- **`[cj:]` fired: ZERO.** Two sites tempted one (0280A *dilationem*, 0279A *positionis*). Both are
  real, well-formed Latin words, which is exactly the class the brief tells me to log and not mark.
- **`[var:]` fired: THREE.** Each rests on `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, cited book
  chapter:verse, and each is a **quoted clause**, not a single word:
  1. **0268D** Matt 8:13 — Migne *sanatus **sit** puer*, Clementine *Et sanatus **est** puer in illa
     hora*. Mood, and the gloss's whole point is that "Matthew explains it more plainly", i.e.
     reports the healing.
  2. **0270A** Luke 7:22 — Migne *renuntiate Joanni quae **vidistis***, Clementine *quae **audistis
     et** vidistis*. A dropped pair of words inside the clause, not at the `etc.` truncation.
  3. **0280C** 1 John 3:2 — Migne *nondum apparuit quid erimus, **sed tandem** similes ei erimus,
     **quando** videbimus eum sicuti est*, Clementine *…**Scimus quoniam cum apparuerit,** similes
     ei erimus: **quoniam** videbimus eum sicuti est*. A whole clause replaced.
- **Materiality test for `[var:]`** (Pattern 14, "in a way that changes the sense"): I declined it
  for orthography (*coelo/caelo*, *quicunque/quicumque*, *fenum/foenum*, *Plusquam/plus quam*), for
  a dropped connective at the head of a quotation begun mid-verse (*enim*, *autem*), for abridgment
  at either end of a span, for word order inside a truncated address, and for lexical doublets that
  do not move the sense (*dilectio*/*caritas*, *vestimentis*/*vestibus*, *Cupio*/*desiderium
  habens*). All 22 such declines are listed with reasons in §3.

⚑ **I CHECKED THE INSTRUMENT BEFORE REPORTING, TWICE, AND BOTH SIGNALS WERE FALSE.**
1. A `nation` grep over my Latin returns **four hits** — `incarnationis` ×3 (0272A, 0275B/0276B,

   is the class convention 4 expressly permits ("their own nation").
2. A raw `usque ad` grep returns **eleven**, not ten. The eleventh, **0277C** *et **usque ad** illos
   praedicatio pervenit*, is ordinary prose and **unitalicised** — the same class as 8999's
   unitalicised "down to". I rendered it "as far as them" anyway, so no drift signal exists either
   way, but the count is 10 formula + 1 prose and a later sweep should not read 11 as 11.
3. Related: the one **"throng"** in my English (0268B, "is thronged about with the soldiery of the
   virtues") renders the verb ***stipatur***, not *turba*. This is 9001's documented near-miss;
   do not sweep it.

⭐ **No inherited convention had to be broken.** No exception was forced by Luke in this range.

- *gentes* took **Gentiles** throughout because every instance in capita VII–IX is inside the
  Judaei/gentes typology: the centurion as *gentilis populus* (0268A–0269A), *plebs gentium*
  (0276C), the Gerasene demoniac read as *gentes quas … Dominus per praedicatores visitavit*
  (0275A), and Paul's scandal *in gentibus* (0273A). Even *gentium leges* at 0275B, which looks
  generic, sits inside that same allegory and is the laws of the Gentile peoples whom the demoniac
  figures.
- ***gentilitas*** (0268C *errore gentilitatis*, 0272A, 0276A) is a **sixth word** on this axis and
  the brief does not rule it. I rendered it **"the Gentile world"** (and *errore gentilitatis* as
  "the crimes of Gentile error"), never "heathenism", so it stays inside the *gentes* family and is
  distinguishable at the merge. `ethnicus` does not occur here, so "heathen" appears **zero** times
  and the fourth word is untouched.

## §4. CRUCES BY COLUMN — Migne's own, rendered as printed, no marker

**0269B — *Credente Domino sanatur servus, quia potest meritum Domini etiam servis suffragari.***
Migne capitalises *Domino*/*Domini*, and the easy reading makes this Christ. **It cannot be.** The
sentence's argument is that a *master's* merit avails for his *servants* ("not only by the merit of
faith, but also by zeal for discipline"), and the master in this pericope is the centurion, who is
the one who believed. Rendered "The **master** believing, the servant is healed … the merit of the
**master**." A mechanism-5 site, decided on the argument's own structure rather than on the capital
letter. **No emendation is involved**: *dominus* carries both senses and only English must choose.

**0270D — *Prophetico ergo exemplo ad virtutem subeundae virtutis hortatur.*** *virtutem …
virtutis* in four words, and the second occurrence has no work to do. Rendered as printed ("he
exhorts to the virtue of a virtue to be undergone"). I do not know whether this is a compositor's
repeat of a word from the line above, a scribal one in the recension, or the abridger's own
clumsiness, and I assert none of the three.

**0270D — *Ecce quae in veste pretiosa.*** Lc 7:25 reads *Ecce **qui** in veste pretiosa sunt*, and
Migne prints *quae*. ⚑ **This span is NOT italicised in the Latin**, so it is not in the brief's
inventory and no lemma test reaches it — but it is a quotation, and it is the only unitalicised one
in the range. A relative's gender has no English exponent (Pattern 9), so nothing changes in the
English; recorded so the divergence is not invisible.

**0270D — *exteros coelestis regni … consenescunt.*** *exteros* is accusative plural with no verb
to govern it; the clause's own verb is *consenescunt*, intransitive. Rendered as an apposition
("strangers to the heavenly kingdom, grow old…"), which is the closest the printed case allows.

**0271B — *Venit sicut tunc, ita et nunc, utramque viam salutis **respuitis**.*** A second-person
plural verb dropped into a third-person sentence, with no address. Kept ("you spurn both ways of
salvation"); the next sentence (*Lamentavimus … cantavimus*) is first-person plural, so the passage
is already changing person and the shift may be the abridger's compression of a homiletic original.

**0271C — *osculare, id est, nihil nisi sapientiam **loqueris**.*** Five imperatives (*recurre,
inquire, confitere, expande, osculare*) and then a future indicative. Rendered "thou shalt speak",
which is what is printed; *loquaris* would give the jussive the series wants, and I do not assert
it.

**0272B — *Sanata aegra, de salute ejus **aliis** aegrotant.*** *aliis* is dative/ablative and
cannot be the subject *aegrotant* requires. The obvious reading is *alii*, "others sicken over her
salvation" — which is exactly what the verse says (*coeperunt qui simul accumbebant dicere intra
se*) — and I declined to adopt it, because adopting it is the silent repair Pattern 7a exists to
forbid. Rendered "they sicken over her salvation, to others", which is deliberately awkward and
true to the page.

**0272D — *Joanna, Dominus gratia ejus, vel Dominus misericors**.** cujus est omne quod
vivimus.*** A full stop mid-sentence, followed by a lowercase *cujus*. Preserved exactly (Pattern
8, and 9001 @0202D is the precedent: the stop there was **our source's**, at Migne's line break).
⚑ **This is the site in my range I most want read at the plate**, because a stop that appears at a
line turn and a stop Migne set are indistinguishable in our file and only one of them is a defect.

**0273D — *vel per adversa terrendo, vel prospera blandiendo retardant.*** *retardant* is active
with no expressed subject: grammatically it must be the *adversa*/*prospera*, but the two gerunds
name the means, not the agent. Rendered with an unspecified "they hold them back". A mechanism-5
shape that has no safe resolution; recorded rather than resolved.

**0274A — *Quia voluptates et divitiae suis importunis cogitationibus guttur mentis, ne bonum
desiderium ad cor possit intrare quasi aditum vitalis strangulant flatus.*** The word order splits
*vitalis … flatus* across the verb and puts the purpose clause inside the object phrase. Every word
construes; only the order is violent. Rendered in the order English needs, with nothing added or
dropped, and logged because a later reader will meet the Latin and think something is missing.

**0274B — *Et bona terra cum patientia fructum reddit … et dum humiliter flagella **suspiciunt**,
post flagella in requiem **suscipiuntur**.*** Singular *terra* governs the first two verbs and
plural verbs govern the last two. Kept as printed; the sentence has slid from the ground to the
hearers it figures.

**0275A — *unde Gerasa sive Gergesi colonum ejiciens, id est diabolum a quo prius incolebatur vel
advena propinquans, qui longe erat in tempore.*** Two Hebrew etymologies run together with no main
verb: *Gerasa* = "casting out the settler", *Gergesa* = "a stranger drawing near". Rendered as
printed, with the two glosses left in apposition. *Gergesi* is a form I cannot vouch for (§5.2).

**0276A — *non capientes verbum Dei, et **in firma** mente pondus sapientiae non sustinentes*.**
Printed as two words. It construes as printed ("not sustaining the weight of wisdom in a firm
mind") and I rendered that, but *infirma* is equally available and would give "in an unsteady
mind", which suits *Conscii fragilitatis* better. **A word-division ambiguity is the shape our
source manufactures most often** (the hyphen-rejoin class), so this is a §5 entry and not a marker.

**0277D — *…Jesum resurrexisse negabant, multa mirabilia opera viderant, in cujus passione multa
miranda contigerant.*** The middle clause has no connective and no relative, and then *in cujus*
takes an antecedent the sentence never supplies in that position. Rendered as printed, asyndeton
and all.

**0278B — *Nondum intellexerant apostoli fidem populi credentis non esse venalem, sed **noverant**
nos potius esse redimendos per suas escas gratuitas.*** *Nondum intellexerant … sed noverant* is
self-contradictory as it stands. ⛔ **I did not supply a negative**, which is precisely the repair
7a forbids, and I did not soften *sed*. Rendered "had not yet understood … but they had known".
If a negative was lost here it was lost before our file, and the plate is the only thing that can
say so.

**0279A — *incomprehensibilia paternae **positionis** arcana*.** *positio* is a real word and gives
"the Father's position", which is not what the sentence wants; *dispositionis* would give "the
Father's disposing". A `[cj:]` was available and is declined per §1.1. Rendered as printed.

**0280A — *visuros **dilationem** Ecclesiae antequam per mortem recedant de mundo*.** Read as
*dilatio* = "delay", the sentence is nonsense; read as the attested medieval *dilatio* =
*dilatatio*, "spreading", it says exactly what the following clause says (*in praesenti contra mundi
gloriam erecti, certius credantur in coelo regnaturi*). I took the **attested medieval sense**
(Pattern 9: the test is attestation, not classicism) and rendered "the spreading of the Church".
**This is a rendering decision, not an emendation** — the printed letters are unchanged — but it is
a decision, and it is logged so it can be reversed.

**0280C — *ita et oculis mentis tuae jam **divinarum** sensus albescit **lectionem***.**
*divinarum* is genitive plural feminine and *lectionem* is accusative singular: **they cannot
construe together**, and the sentence needs *lectionum*. I rendered the genitive plural that the
adjective demands ("the sense of the divine readings"), and I say plainly that I am rendering
against one printed letter. **No marker**: one letter is suspicion of our file as much as of his.

**0281C — *cujus perfectus ostenditur audita voce dicentis*.** *perfectus* as a bare masculine
nominative has no referent; *perfectio* would give "whose perfection is shown". Rendered "whose
perfect one is shown", which is what the case allows.

**0283B — *presbyteros Baal morte affecit*.** 3 Kings 18 has Elijah killing the ***prophetas***
Baal, and *presbyteros* is a real word in the wrong place. Not a lemma, so no `[var:]` is even
available; rendered "the elders of Baal" and logged.

**0283D — *sed eum cujus patrem sciebat mortuum, illum patrem de quo dicitur: Obliviscere domum
patris tui, etc.*** The sentence never reaches a main verb: *sed eum … illum patrem …* dangles
after *elegit innocentem*. Rendered as printed, with the anacoluthon intact.

**0284A — *quid fiet illis qui nulla utilitate saepe **visitat** domos suorum quas dereliquerunt.***
Plural relative subject, singular verb, and a question that Migne closes with a full stop.
**Both kept**: "what will become of those who often, with no profit, **visits** the houses of their
own people which they had forsaken**.**" The period is Pattern 8, not 8b — 8b covers an *indirect*
question English cannot mark, and this is a direct one whose mark Migne simply does not print.

Decided on **case and gender**, then checked against the sense; the easier reading is named where
it differs. **Sites 12–19 are in chunks 0015 and 0016**, which is the half of a range that John's
census never reached.

### 4b. NEGATION — carried, including where it hurts

All of `non/nec/neque/nisi/haud/nunquam/nullus/nihil` in the range appear in the English and none
was added. The sites where carrying it produces awkward or contradictory English, and where I
carried it anyway: **0270C** (*nec … nec … nec … nec*, four limbs on John the reed, all four kept)
· **0271B** (*nec psalmis … nec threnis*, both kept, and the second *nec* governs a long
parenthesis that could easily swallow it) · **0273C** (*nulla fide, nullo intellectu, nulla saltem*
— three, kept) · **0275C** (*nisi quis more porci vixerit … non accipit … non autem ad perdendum*)
· **0277B** (*ut nihil nisi virgam ferrent, **et ut nec virgam ferrent*** — the gloss's point is
that the two commands contradict, so flattening either kills the paragraph) · **0278B** (*Nondum
intellexerant … sed noverant*, §4: the contradiction is on the page and stays there) · **0282A**
(*qui nec fidem confitentur, nec ipsius fidei audire volunt sermonem*) · **0283A** (*non sacramenta
… et adversum nos non sunt … et Dominum non sequuntur* — four negatives in one sentence with two
different scopes).

⚑ **7a⁗ is not at issue anywhere in this range**, because nothing is quarantined: zero `[sic:]`,
zero `[cj:]`, and the three `[var:]` sit **beside** a complete rendering and carry no force of their
own. Every verb, negation and conjunction is in the running English.

⚑ **And per the brief's closing warning, I re-read every ruling above against the English I actually
shipped.** The four that were most at risk of the John @0409B failure (a crux that says the right
thing over an English that does not) are 0269B *master*, 0272B *aliis*, 0278B *sed noverant*, and
0280A *dilationem*: all four read in the shipped chunks exactly as this file says they do.
