## (a) THE POLICY I APPLIED — stated before the findings, so it can be argued with

### a.1 Markers

**I fired no `[sic:]`.** Not one, in 5,748 words. Every suspect word in my range — non-words
included — is rendered as printed and logged unmarked in §(e) for the plate reader. This is
John's result carried forward deliberately, on the brief's numbers: on 8999 the 23 leaf-reads
spent on fired markers produced **no** corrections and the nineteen declined oddities produced
**all seventeen**. I have no evidence about the plate, and I say so at every site rather than
guessing a class.

**I fired no `[cj:]`.** Two sites tempted one (0287C *cadit*, 0295A *mansuros*); both are
argued and declined in §(d). Where a `[cj:]` would have been fired, the running English would
still have had to carry the force outside the bracket — the 8999 @0415A rule — and at both
sites the printed reading construes well enough that quarantining it would have been the
riskier act.

**I fired seven `[var:]`, under one stated test:**

> ⭐ **Fire `[var:]` only where the divergence from the Clementine is larger than a single
> word — a substituted or omitted *phrase*, or two texts joined. Log every single-word lemma
> divergence unmarked.**

That test is the brief's own asymmetry made operational: *"an OCR letter-swap manufactures
broken words and cannot invent a semantically coherent divergent clause."* A one-word
divergence is inside the class our transcription can manufacture; a missing prepositional
clause or a conflation of two books is not. Three candidates were **withdrawn** by this test
after being drafted — 0288A *habet* for *habebit*, 0293B *Qui* for *quia*, 0297A *Vestiebat*
for *vestiebatur* — and each now stands in §(e) instead. Every fired marker cites
`sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` by book chapter:verse.

Final marker counts for the range: **`[var:]` 7 · `[sic:]` 0 · `[cj:]` 0 · `[d:]` 0 ·
`[ed:]` 0 · `[nt:]` 0.**

## (d) THIS RANGE'S CRUCES, BY COLUMN

### 0284A — *Sicut in apostolis est, forma episcoporum*
Migne's comma stands between *est* and *forma*, breaking a nominal predicate. Preserved
(Pattern 8); the English reads "there is the form of bishops" and the comma is not reproduced
where English cannot carry it without becoming unreadable. No emendation, no marker.

### 0285B — *pejores gentilibus, naturale solummodo jus dissipantibus*
The participle is dative plural agreeing with *gentilibus*, not with the cities: it is the
**Gentiles** who squander only the natural law, and that is the whole force of *pejores*.
Bound accordingly. (Mechanism 5 candidate; checked, not a defect.)

### 0285B — *cum illae civitates praedictae, Domino praesente credere noluerunt, Tyrus et Sidon olim fuerunt amicae*
The sentence has no main clause answering *cum*: Migne joins a *cum*-clause to a bare perfect.
Rendered as it stands ("for whereas those foresaid cities … would not believe, Tyre and Sidon
were once friends…"), with nothing supplied. **No marker** — the words are all real and the
looseness is the recension's, not a type defect.

### 0286A — *ut unde illi ceciderunt sublevati, isti ascendant humiliati*
⛔ **Mechanism 5, and the easy reading is the wrong one.** *illi* are the fallen angels and
*sublevati* attaches to *them* ("from whence **they**, being lifted up, fell"); *isti* are the
disciples and *humiliati* attaches to them. Reading either participle across to the other
subject inverts the whole antithesis. Bound by case and number, not by sense.

### 0286B — *Cum audis omnia, agnoscis omnipotentem, non decolorem, non degenerem Patris*
Second person **singular** (Pattern 17) → *thou hearest / thou acknowledgest*. Both negatives
carried. The genitive *Patris* governs *degenerem*, not *omnipotentem*: "not degenerate **from
the Father**".

### 0286D — *ejusdem gentis* · 0290A — *illius gentis*
Singular *gens* of a people → **nation**, per §1.4's third class. Neither is *gentes*, and
neither is rendered "Gentiles".

### 0287A — *descendit quidem lex per Moysen in mundum, et nullam sanitatem contulit hujusmodi*
*hujusmodi* is the wounded man of the parable, not the law and not the world. Rendered
"conferred no health upon **such a one**". Mechanism-5 site; checked.

### 0287C — ⭐ *Quanto humilius sedet, tanto amplius **cadit*** — a declined `[cj:]`
Printed: *Quanto humilius sedet, tanto amplius cadit. Aqua confluit ad convallem de tumoribus
collis.* The expected sense is that the lower Mary sits the more she **takes in**, which is
what *capit* would give, and *capit* → *cadit* is a one-letter difference.

**Declined, and here is the argument for declining.** *cadit* is a perfectly good verb and the
sentence **construes without emendation** if the subject is the water of the very next
sentence: "the more humbly she sits, the more abundantly **it falls**" — water running down
from the swellings of the hill into the valley. Pattern 18 exists for a real word whose
faithful English *misleads*; here the faithful English is the image the next sentence supplies.
Rendered *"The more humbly she sits, the more abundantly does it fall."* I record the
alternative so a reader is not left to invent it, and I fire nothing. **Worth a glance at the
plate at 0287C**, but it is a reading question, not a type question.

### 0287C — *haec convivium parat Domino, in convivio Domini illa jam jucundatur*
*haec* is Martha (named second, nearer) and *illa* is Mary. Rendered **"the latter … the
former"** rather than "this one … that one", which in English inverts as often as not. The
alternative binding would have Mary preparing the banquet, which the same sentence denies.

### 0288A — *Quis vestrum **habet** amicum*
See §(e). Present for the Vulgate's future *habebit*. Rendered as printed; no marker.

### 0288B — *Psal. XLIV* for Ps CXLIV
The note keyed to *Prope est Dominus … voluntatem timentium se faciet* reads **(Psal. XLIV)**;
the text is **Ps 144:18–19**, and Ps 44 contains none of it. `[n:]` contents are Migne's
citations and pass through **verbatim and untranslated** — the brief forbids correcting or
querying one, and §6.3 forbids proposing corrections to references. Logged here only, so the
indexer's operator knows the `refKey` and the `refDisplay` will not agree at this site.

### 0288C — *Excita ergo somnum tuum*
*excitare somnum* — literally "rouse thy sleep". Rendered **"Shake off thy sleep"**, which is
the idiom's force, not a repair of a defect: nothing here is defective. Recorded because the
literal English would be nonsense and I want the choice visible.

### 0289C — *et domus supra domum cadet cum habitatoribus unius domus ab alterius domus familia vel opprimuntur vel puniuntur*
Two things at once: a singular *cadet* followed by plural *opprimuntur*/*puniuntur*, and a
*cum*-clause with no conjunction to hang the plurals on. ⛔ **Mechanism 5 checked:** the
ablative agent *ab alterius domus familia* is the **other** house's household, and the ones
oppressed are *habitatoribus unius domus*. Getting that backwards is the easy reading and the
wrong one. Rendered as printed, number mismatch included; **no marker** — every word is real.

### 0290A — *qui sibi conscii erant nihil malarum artium a Domino didicisse*
*qui* is the apostles (the *filii Judaeorum* of the lemma), not the Jews who accuse them.
The English binds it to the apostles. Mechanism-5 site; checked.

### 0290B — the split lemma *Profecto pervenit … regnum … Dei: … Regnum*
Migne breaks Lc 11:20 into four italic fragments and glosses between them. All four
reproduced in place and in order, with the colon after *Dei* preserved.

### 0290B — *Hunc digitum magi confessi sunt in **Aegyto***
See §(e). Carried in italics, untranslated, unmarked.

### 0290C — the six-fragment lemma *Cum … fortis … armatus … custodit … atrium suum*
The gloss dismembers Lc 11:21 and glosses each word. All six fragments kept italic in the
printed order with the gloss words between them, so the verse reassembles on the page.

### 0292A — *quae in Ninivitis per poenitentiam … in regina austri studium percipiendae sapientiae … congregatur*
*quae* is the Church (from *Ecclesiae mysterium*), and *studium* is a bare nominative in
apposition with no verb of its own. Rendered as an apposition inside dashes-free parenthesis;
nothing supplied. Mechanism-5 site: *quae* could be pulled to *poenitentiam*, and is not.

### 0292B — *sed Judaei veram Dei sapientiam cum ipsis conversantem, non audiunt sed blasphemant*
⛔ *conversantem* is accusative and agrees with *sapientiam*: it is **Wisdom** that dwells
among them. Binding it to *Judaei* would make the Jews the ones dwelling, and lose the point.

### 0293C — *ne fidei vos studiosos faciat operum negligentes*
Both *studiosos* and *negligentes* agree with *vos*. Rendered "lest it make **you**, zealous
for faith, negligent of works."

### 0293B — *Qui estis ut monumenta* · *Repondens autem*
See §(e). Both rendered as printed; no markers.

### 0294B — *Hanc legisperiti, nec ipsi lege et prophetis intelligunt*
A bare ablative *lege et prophetis* where *in lege et prophetis* is expected. Rendered "neither
themselves understand **by** the law and the prophets". Real words; a possible lost *in*.
Logged in §(e).

### 0294B — *Intrare clavem, est non esse contentum superficie litterae*
*intrare* with an accusative object *clavem* ("to enter the key"). Real words, and the sense is
recoverable — to enter *by* the key. Rendered literally ("To enter the key is not to be content
with the surface of the letter"), **nothing supplied**. Logged in §(e).

### 0295A — ⭐ *vel mansuros in denuntiatione supplicii* — the second declined `[cj:]`
Printed: *Totus hic locus ad subeundam pro confessione Domini passionem vel contemptu mortis,
vel spe praemii, vel mansuros in denuntiatione supplicii instruit.* The first two limbs are
ablatives of means (*contemptu*, *spe*); the third is a **future participle in the accusative
plural** with nothing to agree with. It does not construe.

**Declined.** Every word is real and I can see at least two readings (*mansuros* as a second
object of *instruit* — "instructs also those who will abide"; or a corruption of an ablative
in the series). Choosing one in a `[cj:]` would be exactly the confident wrong call the brief
prices. Rendered as literally as the printed words allow — *"either by contempt of death, or by
hope of reward, or those who will abide, in the denunciation of punishment"* — which is
deliberately as awkward in English as it is in Latin. **Plate read owed at 0295A.**

### 0295C — *Et omnis qui dicit verbum **in Filio** hominis remittetur*
⛔ **The most consequential single word in my range.** Lc 12:10 reads *in **Filium** hominis* —
a word spoken *against* the Son of man. The ablative makes it a word spoken *in* him. The
gloss's own surrounding argument (*Confitetur Filius apud Patrem illum…*, and the *Ei autem*
that follows about the unforgivable sin) works either way, so the context does not decide it.
Rendered as printed and **not marked**; see §(e).

### 0295D — *Commendanti gratiam supernae pacis et unitatis, vult iste ingerere molestiam*
The dative participle is the Lord who commends; *iste* is the man of Lc 12:13. Bound
accordingly. Mechanism-5 site; checked.

### 0295D — *Inter fratres, patrimonium, non judex medius, sed pietas debet sequestra dividere, quamvis immortalitatis patrimonium non sit pecunia hominibus petendum*
*sequestra* is a feminine noun in apposition to *pietas* ("affection as arbitress"); the final
clause has *patrimonium* (neuter) with *petendum* but *pecunia* (feminine) as predicate, so it
does not agree cleanly. Rendered literally, gender mismatch and all — **Pattern 9**: a Latin
gender mismatch has no English exponent, there is nothing to emend and nothing to mark.

### 0296D — *nec communes fructus aliquo sibi dominatu specialius vindicantibus*
The participle agrees with *volatilibus*, the birds, not with the providence of God. Checked.

### 0297A — *Vestiebat sicut unum*
See §(e). Rendered active, as printed.

### 0297C — *quibus si haec subtrahuntur, ad probationem est, si dantur ad gratiarum actionem*
*quibus* is the faithful of the preceding clause. Note Migne prints no comma before *ad
gratiarum actionem*; not supplied.

### 0298B — the chunk ends mid-argument at *aliis exemplum praebeatis*
Chunk 0020's last column band is 0298B and the sentence closes there. Not a defect; the chunk
division is ours.
