## (a) THE POLICY THIS STINT APPLIED — stated before the findings, so it can be argued with

1. **No `[sic:]`, none, on any ground.** The brief's measured finding governs: on John, 29,727
   words produced zero fired `[sic:]`, the 23 leaf-reads spent on fired markers produced **no**
   corrections, and the nineteen DECLINED oddities produced **all seventeen**. Every suspect word
   in this range — real word reading oddly, non-word, fused or split run alike — is **rendered as
   printed, logged in §(e), and left unmarked**, with no claim about whose defect it is. I do not
   know whether any of them is Migne's type or Corpus Corporum's transcription, and I say so
   site by site rather than in a preamble only.
2. **No `[cj:]` either.** Pattern 18's bar is that the printed word must make the sentence assert
   something the author is arguing *against*, and 18a's extension reaches negations and
   correlatives only. Four sites in this range are strong conjecture candidates (§e.1, e.4, e.11,
   e.12) and **none** of them is a negation or a correlative; each is a word that leaves the
   sentence obscure rather than false. All four are declined and logged with the conjecture named,
   which is where 18a says an uncertain reading belongs.
3. **`[var:]` is the one marker I fire, and only where a word or clause is REPLACED by different
   matter that changes what the verse says.** Each of the four rests on
   `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, cited book chapter:verse, and each sits on a **quoted
   clause** rather than a single word — the class the brief's table shows standing 88 of 88 across
   three Glossa gospels. **Declined, explicitly:** abridgment (a lemma truncated, or closing in
   *etc.*), orthography (*sepes*/*saepes*, *Usquedum*/*usque dum*), preposition variants
   (*super*/*supra*, twice), and every **single-word** substitution — *ei* for *et*, *esse* for
   *est*, an added *se*. A one-word `[var:]` on an unread column is the trap 9003's collation
   exposed, where the digitization *manufactured* the divergence a collation then dutifully found.
4. **Migne's wrong citation ADDRESSES take no marker.** Three notes in this range point at
   chapters that cannot be right (`Rom. XXXV`, `Rom. VIII` for Rom 13:13, `II Cor. IV` for
   1 Cor 4:9). Notes are reproduced verbatim and untranslated; the words Migne quotes agree with
   the Clementine in two of the three, so there is no divergence to mark — only an address to log
   (9001's precedent at 0192B, where a wrong address alone took no `[var:]`).
5. **Pattern 7 literalism, including where it hurts.** *nihil mundamini*, *petierunt*, *domum
   intellectus*, *cognitione conjunctos*, *operatur* stranded at a sentence end, *affectum* for
   *affectuum* — all rendered as the printed words allow, none repaired, none smoothed by a
   substituted English word. Where the printed sentence will not construe cleanly the English is
   correspondingly hard, and the crux says so.
6. **Punctuation follows the plate (Pattern 8).** Migne's `?` closing the fragment *Qua hora non
   putatis Filius hominis veniet?* rides as printed on what is a statement. The paragraph at
   0304A ending *…noxiae inclinationis erigi* has **no terminal stop in the Latin and none was
   supplied in the English.**
7. **§1 conventions carried from Mark and John, from the first line.** *turba* → **crowd**
   (5 sites), *multitudo* → **multitude** (1 site, 0298B, and it is the only one); *daemonium* →
   **demon** (0305C, twice), *diabolus* → **devil** (0301B, twice); *gentes* → **Gentiles** in the
   Judaei/gentes opposition (0304B, 0304A, 0307A, 0309B), singular *gens* of the Jewish people →
   **nation** (0302C *infructuosa gente Judaeorum*, 0306B *tulerunt locum et gentem*); **no
   `natio` occurs anywhere in this range** — checked with word boundaries, because a bare grep for
   `natio` matches *damnatio*, *inclinatio* and *examinatio*, which is the instrument error the
   brief warns about, and it fired here nine times out of eleven. *mysterium* → mystery,
   *sacramentum* → sacrament, 1:1. **`usque ad` → "as far as"**, every occurrence: two splice
   formulas (0299C in the AMBR. extract, 0308D in the AUG. extract) and two ordinary uses
   (0300A *usque ad finem saeculi*, 0300B *usque ad perfectionem*).
8. **The gloss's own voice is not archaised.** *dicit*/*ait* → **says/said**, no `saith`. Every
   `-eth` form in the range was audited: all of them sit inside an italic scripture lemma
   (*cometh*, *knocketh*, *possesseth*). One `-eth` in the gloss voice was written and then
   removed — *"it behoveth thee"* at 0300C is now *"thou oughtest"*, which keeps Pattern 17's
   required `-est` on a singular address without archaising a third-person gloss verb. The `-est`
   forms that remain (*goest*, *wouldest*, *mayest*, *purposest*) are all Pattern 17 singulars.
9. **Pattern 17 in the second person.** The gloss switches number inside single paragraphs and the
   English follows the morphology, not the addressee: *thou* at 0300C (*posthabeas*), 0301B
   (*vadis, praeparas, tuae carni*), 0307A–0308A (*invitatus fueris, te junxeris, extollas,
   incipias, distribueris*); **you** at 0301C (*vivite … damnemini*) and 0309B (*Voca pauperes*
   context, plural imperatives).
10. **The Greek is reproduced in Greek.** `Ὕδωρ` stands at the head of 0024 exactly as printed,
    space before the comma included, not transliterated and not translated away (§d, 0306C).

## (d) THIS RANGE'S OWN CRUCES, BY COLUMN

### 0298B — *nihil mundamini* (0021)
*Sive vitatis mala sive facitis bona, nihil mundamini, sed solum adventum Redemptoris
exspectetis.* Rendered **"you are cleansed in nothing"**. *mundamini* is a well-formed passive,
but "whether you shun evils or do good, you are cleansed in nothing" argues against the sentence's
own second limb. A conjecture that construes is *nihil moremini*, "delay not at all, but await
only the coming of the Redeemer" — one minim's difference. **No marker**: the printed word is
real, the sentence is obscure rather than false, and Pattern 18a keeps that class in the crux.
Logged for the plate (§e.1).

### 0298B — the Lc 12:36 lemma, `[var:]` FIRED
See §c.3. The gloss's *judici pulsanti* expounds words the printed lemma does not carry.

### 0298D — *exteriorum damnatorum similitudinem* (0021)
Rendered **"the likeness of outward condemned men"**. The simile actually introduced is the
householder and the thief, i.e. **outward losses**, which is *damnorum*; *damnatorum* is a real
word and is rendered as one. No marker (§e.2).

### 0299C — the anacoluthon at VERS. 43 (0021)
*Iste autem qui repertus fuerit fideliter annonam verbi ministrat, et in curam gregis vigilans,
aeterna beatitudine glorificabitur.* The relative clause takes an indicative *ministrat* where the
construction wants a participle, so the sentence has two finite verbs and no clean subject. The
English carries the join as printed ("he who shall have been found faithfully ministers … and,
watching …, shall be glorified"). Nothing is emended (§e.3).

### 0300A, 0300B — *usque ad* used four ways in two columns
Two are the recension's splice formula inside patristic extracts (0299C AMBR., 0308D AUG.); two
are ordinary prepositional uses in the gloss's own prose (*usque ad finem saeculi*, *usque ad
perfectionem*). **All four render "as far as"** per §1.6, which reads naturally in each. Recorded
because a later sweep looking for the formula will find four hits in this range and only two are
the formula.

### 0301B — *ne contemptus te accuset* (0021), a mechanism-5 site
*contemptus* is nominative masculine and agrees with **sermo Dei**, not with *te*. The English
reads **"lest, being despised, it accuse thee"** — the word of God accusing, not the reader being
accused for contempt. The easier reading here is the wrong one, which is the class the brief names
as dominant.

### 0301D — *petierunt* for *perierunt* (0022)
*nuntiatur ei de Galilaeis, quod quasi juste ideo petierunt, quod non bene se praeparaverunt.*
Rendered **"who as it were justly sought this for the cause that…"**. The same sentence continues
*et illos vere fuisse peccatores et **juste periisse***, and the next clause *alios peccatores
**perituros***, so *perierunt* is what the argument wants — one letter away. **No marker, no
`[cj:]`**: the printed word is real, and internal corroboration proves an error exists, never
whose it is (§e.4).

### 0302A — *ex iota et ita* (0022)
The Greek for eighteen is ιη´, iota + **eta**. Migne prints *ita*, which is also the ordinary
medieval Latin NAME of the letter η (Byzantine *ita*), so this is very possibly correct as
printed. Rendered as printed, **"by iota and ita"**, and flagged only so a plate reader can
confirm the sort (§e.5).

### 0302B — *Vinea Domini sabaoh* (0022)
A **non-word**: *sabaoh* for *sabaoth*. It sits inside an italic lemma, where a second italic
cannot nest, and it is carried as printed. It could be Migne's dropped final sort or a Corpus
Corporum loss; **I do not know which and assert neither.** No `[sic:]` (§e.6). The Vulgate's own
*exercituum* at Is 5:7 is discussed at §c, span 46, and took no `[var:]`.

### 0302B — the Is 9:2 proof-text, `[var:]` FIRED
See §c, span 45. Three words replaced in a clause carrying its own address.

### 0302C — *in Synagoga crebris docet* (0022)
*crebris* is an ablative plural adjective with no noun; the sense wants *crebris [sermonibus]* or
an adverb. Rendered **"teaches often in the Synagogue"**, which is the least that the printed
words allow without supplying a noun. Logged (§e.7).

### 0303C — *…curvata fuit, quae magis terrena quam coelestia noverat, operatur.* (0022)
Two finite verbs, the second stranded at the end of the sentence with no subject of its own.
Rendered as printed, with *operatur* left where Migne sets it: **"…was bent away from the
uprightness of her mind, she who had known earthly things rather than heavenly, works."** The
conjecture that construes is *noverat **operari*** ("knew how to work earthly things rather than
heavenly"). **No marker** (§e.8).

### 0304A — a paragraph with no terminal stop (0022)
VERS. 16 ends *…quod est filiam Abrahae a vinculo noxiae inclinationis erigi* with **no full
stop**. Pattern 8: none supplied. The English paragraph ends the same way, mid-air.

### 0304B — *Deum de sua erectione glorificantem* (0022), mechanism-5 checked
The participle is accusative feminine and agrees with *filiam Abrahae, id est Ecclesiam* — so it
is **the Church** that glorifies God for her raising up, not the rejoicing bystanders, who are the
easier antecedent. Rendered accordingly.

### 0306C — `Ὕδωρ`, the only Greek in the work (0024)
*Ὕδωρ , aqua, inde hydropis, morbus aquosus subcutaneus.* The Greek is reproduced **verbatim, in
Greek**, with the space Migne sets before the comma. *hydropis* is set as a nominative where it is
the genitive of *hydrops*; the English renders it **"the dropsy"**, the one English word that
keeps the ὕδωρ derivation visible (dropsy < hydropsy < ὕδρωψ), so that the etymology the sentence
exists to make still works in the English. The choice is recorded rather than silent (§e.10).

### 0307A — the Rom 3:23 proof-text, `[var:]` FIRED; and `(Rom. XXXV)`
*gratia* for *gloria* — see §c, span 96. The note's address names a chapter Romans does not have;
reproduced verbatim, unmarked (§e.15).

### 0307A/B — the Ps 87:16 proof-text, `[var:]` FIRED
*confusus* for *conturbatus* — see §c, span 100.

### 0307B — *Honoratiori post invitato locum dat, qui … praeitur* (0024), mechanism-5 site
*qui* is the man who **gives** place — the over-confident earlier guest — and *praeitur* is
passive, so he **is outstripped** by the nimbleness of those who followed Christ. The easier
reading binds *qui* to the more honourable newcomer and makes the verb active; that reading is
wrong on both the case and the voice. Rendered per the morphology.

### 0309B — *domum intellectus accipiunt* (0024)
*Quidam autem vocantur et contemnunt, qui domum intellectus accipiunt, sed opere non implent.*
Rendered **"who receive the house of understanding"**. The parallel three lines later —
*qui acceptam **intelligentiam** opere adimplent* — wants ***donum*** *intellectus*, "the gift of
understanding," and *domum*/*donum* differ by one sort. Real word, so no `[sic:]`; not a negation
or correlative, so no `[cj:]`. Logged (§e.11).

### 0309C — *cognitione conjunctos* (0024)
*ut et cognitione conjunctos, et divino praecepto diligamus.* Rendered **"those joined to us by
acquaintance"**. The passage is about hating father, mother, wife and children while loving them,
so the expected word is ***cognatione***, "by kinship". One letter. No marker (§e.12).

### 0310B — *piorum affectum* (0024)
*legationem lacrymarum, bonorum operum, piorum affectum* — three genitives, and the third is an
accusative singular where the series requires *affectuum*. English has no exponent for the case,
so the rendering **"of holy affection"** is unavoidable and the defect is invisible in the English;
that is exactly why it is logged here (§e.13).

### 0310B — the 1 Cor 4:9 quotation, `[var:]` DECLINED
Order and connectives only; see §c, span 137. The address `(II Cor. IV)` is wrong for the verse.

### 0310C — *spiritualia sapientia* (0025)
*ad condienda corda auditorum spiritualia sapientia*: a neuter plural adjective against an ablative
singular noun. Rendered **"with spiritual wisdom"**; Pattern 9 governs — English carries no
exponent for the disagreement, there is nothing to emend and nothing to mark (§e.14).

### Mechanism-5 census — ALL FIVE CHUNKS, not just the first two
The brief's warning that a stint's apparatus is silent about what it never looked for is answered
here explicitly. Every site in this range where a pronoun, relative or participle could bind to
either of two nouns was checked on case and gender rather than on sense:
**0021** — *ne contemptus te accuset* (0301B, above) · *a quo liberatur* (0301B: *quo* = *sermo
Dei*, the adversary of the lemma, not the judge) · *qui cum in se nil haberet* (0300B: *qui* = the
Lord, not the devotion) · *quem judex exactori … tradet* (0301B: *quem* = the sinner).
**0022** — *quam tamen non ex toto exstirpandam* (0303B: *quam* = the Synagogue) ·
*Deum de sua erectione glorificantem* (0304B, above) · *quorum … uterque* (0304A: the two peoples)
· *quae … vincula … erupit* (0303D–0304A: the Church, not the soul).
**0023** — *quam in die resurrectionis glorificatam* (0306A: *quam* = the Church, his body) ·
*cui destructioni eorum … condoleo* (0306A: the destruction, not the prophets).
**0024** — *qui … praeitur* (0307B, above) · *cum vix illum praeparatum … discutit* (0310B: God is
the subject of *discutit*, *illum* the object; the easier reading makes the man the examiner) ·
*quos habemus ad malum suasores* (0310A).
**0025** — *ne in eis germen oriretur* (0310C: *eis* = the salted cities).
No re-binding was found that the English had taken the easy way on; the two named above (0301B,
0307B) are the sites where the easy reading was available and refused.
