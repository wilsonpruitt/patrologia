### a.2 The line I drew on `[var:]`, so the twenty declines below can be argued with

`[var:]` **fires** where a whole word or clause of the received text is **replaced by different
matter, or removed from inside the quotation**, in a way that changes what the verse says.
`[var:]` is **declined**, and the divergence logged here instead, where the difference is:

  (i) a **clip** at either end of the quotation — Migne's citing practice throughout this
      recension, and the commonest single class in my range;
  (ii) an **inflection, a single letter, or a word order** — the class in which Corpus Corporum
      can manufacture a Vulgate divergence that a collation then dutifully "finds"
      (the `judicabit`→`judicavit` trap), and the class in which English frequently has **no
      exponent at all**, so the marker would be the only place the claim lived;
  (iii) an **adaptation to the citing sentence** — case, person, mood, or a finite verb made out
      of an infinitive so the verse can sit inside the gloss's own syntax.

⚑ The two markers I did fire are neither (i) nor (ii) nor (iii). *hora*→*die* is one substantive
noun exchanged for another at the verse being glossed; the loss of *uxores* is a word taken out
of the **middle** of a clause, which changes the object of *habent* from wives to possessions
generally — and it is the second sense the gloss then argues from.

## (d) THIS RANGE'S CRUCES, BY COLUMN

### CHUNK 0026 — cols 0310D–0314B

**0311B · `hic majoris misericordiae et majoris gaudii` — MECHANISM 5, not resolved, rendered
adverbially.** The sentence is *majus videtur impios justificare quam justos creare et conservare,
quia etsi utrumque aequalis potentiae, **hic** majoris misericordiae et majoris gaudii*. Read as a
demonstrative pronoun, *hic* at its ordinary value is the LATTER of the two — creating and
preserving the just — which reverses the clause the sentence has just made (*majus videtur impios
justificare*). Read as the adverb, it commits to nothing. **I rendered it adverbially: "here there
is greater mercy and greater joy."** Named because the contextually easy English ("this is of
greater mercy", meaning the justification of the wicked) is a construal the printed pronoun does
not license, and it is the reading a later reader will want to substitute.

**0311C · `Quod ubi inter homines claruit, domum evertit` — MECHANISM 5, not resolved.** Two
candidate subjects for *evertit*: *Quod* (= *verbum in carne*, and the subject of *claruit*), and
*Dei sapientia*, the main-clause subject two clauses back — who is also the woman of Lc 15:8, the
one who lights the lamp and sweeps the house. **There is no morphological discriminator**:
*quod* is neuter nominative, and *evertit* carries no gender. Rendered "And when this shone forth
among men, it overturned the house", which leaves both open exactly as the Latin does.

**0311C · `evertit` for the Vulgate's `everrit`.** Lc 15:8 has *everrit domum*, "sweeps the
house"; the gloss's own word is *evertit*, "overturns". ⚑ **Almost certainly not a defect**: the
explanation attached to it is *quia conscientias hominum de reatu suo perturbavit*, which wants
overturning and not sweeping. It is unitalicised gloss prose, not a lemma, so 7a″ does not reach
it. Logged only because a mechanical collation will flag it.

**0311D · `Qui nec post culpam a Creatore periit` — MECHANISM 5, resolved.** *Qui* is masculine
singular and there are two masculine candidates in the preceding clause, *decimus electorum
numerus* and *homo*. Bound to *numerus*: it is the nearer, and the reason clause in the same
sentence (*eum sapientia Dei … ex lumine testae reparavit*) belongs to the drachma-and-number
image, not to the man. Rendered "And that number did not perish from its Creator".

**0312A · `a quo negatur`.** Declined oddity 1; see §(e). Rendered "by whom he is denied".

**0312A · `naturalibus solum ingenii … beneficium` — a case with no host.** Declined oddity 3.

**0312B · THE PARAGRAPH ENDS WITH NO FULL STOP, AND SO DOES OURS.** *Haec est prodiga vita, quae
amat fundere et spatiari in pompis exterioribus, relinquens eum qui sibi est interior* — Migne
prints no terminal mark. Pattern 8: never supply. The English stops where he stops. **Not a
defect to repair, and not a build error.**

**0313B · `munite`.** Declined oddity 2. Rendered as the imperative it is — the two purpose
clauses that follow (*ut et opus ornetur … et cursus … praeparando*) construe with it.

**0313D · `litterali sensu, contentus,` — Migne's comma falls between the ablative and its
adjective.** Carried, mark for mark: "in the literal sense, content, working earthly things,
awaiting earthly things." Pattern 8 forbids deleting a printed mark, and this is the class of
comma that gets tidied away without trace.

**0314A–0314B · convention 4 audited.** Every *gentes*-family word in this chunk stands in a
Judaei/gentes opposition — *cur Deus laetetur in assumptione **gentium*** against Israel outside

**0316D · ⭐ `injusti in requie` IS NOT A DEFECT, AND IT READS AS ONE.** The Gregory extract
prints *Credendum est, quod ante retributionem extremi judicii **injusti in requie**, etc.* — as
it stands, that the unjust are at rest before the judgment, which contradicts the whole column.
It is not a dropped negative and it is not broken type: **Gregory's sentence continues *injusti
in requie **justos conspiciant***, and the abridgment's *etc.* cuts the clause before its
object.** The fragment is a truncation artifact of the recension's own splicing. Rendered as the
fragment it is ("the unjust in rest, etc."). **No marker, no conjecture, and no plate read is
owed on this column for this reason.** Recorded because a blind reader will otherwise find it
and file it as a polarity defect.

**0316D–0317A · `A longe vident infideles` — MECHANISM 5, resolved, and the easy reading is the
wrong one.** *infideles* and *fideles* are both nominative-equals-accusative plural, so the
morphology does not decide the subject. The next clause does: *fideles super se in requie
attendunt* puts the faithful above and at rest, so it is the **unfaithful** who see and the
faithful who are seen. The tempting English — "the faithful see the unfaithful afar off" — would
have inverted heaven and hell in a sentence about the day of judgment.

**0317A · `In sinum Abrahae … in quo … recipiuntur` — MECHANISM 5, named, not decisive.** *quo*
may be *sinus* (masculine) or *regnum* (neuter); both are available and the sentence names both.
Rendered so that English "which" follows "the kingdom of heaven", the nearer candidate and the
idiomatic one. I do not claim it is settled.

**0317C · `Redemptoris praecepta qui ex mortuis surrexit` — MECHANISM 5, resolved by number.**
*surrexit* is singular, so *qui* is the Redeemer and not the precepts, despite standing next to
them. Rendered "the precepts of the Redeemer, who rose from the dead".

### ⚑ THE ONE CONVENTION EXCEPTION FORCED BY LUKE — 0316A, and the Latin that forced it

Convention 6 fixes the abridgment's splice formula *usque ad* as **"as far as"**. My range has
**six** occurrences of the formula proper — 0313A (AMBR.), 0315B (AUG.), 0316B (GREG.), 0316D
(GREG.), 0323D (BEDA.), 0324A (AMBR.) — each italic and each following `etc.,`. All six are
rendered **"as far as"**. Two further, non-formula, unitalicised occurrences in ordinary prose
are also rendered "as far as" and read naturally: *in verbo **usque ad** peregrinationem nostram
condescendens* (0313A) and *ire crescendo **usque ad** perfectiora* (0319D).

**The exception is span 66, at 0316A:**

> *Quamvis dicam,* **Lex et prophetae usque ad Joannem,** *tamen non praedico legis
> destructionem.*

This *usque ad* is **Luke 16:16 itself** — an italic scripture lemma quoted inside the gloss's
own sentence, not a splice mark, and the very verse the chunk's VERS. 16 address (span 63, *Lex
et prophetae.*) heads. Rendered **"The law and the prophets until John."** Applying convention
6 here would put the splice formula's English inside a lemma where nothing is spliced, which is
the opposite of what the convention exists to mark. Mark's cruces record two such look-alikes on
that book and decided them per occurrence; this is the same decision, and it is stated here so
that a mechanical sweep does not "normalise" it back.
