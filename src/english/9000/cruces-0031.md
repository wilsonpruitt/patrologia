## (a) THE POLICY I APPLIED — stated before the findings, so it can be argued with

**1. No `[sic:]`. Not one, on any word, however good the internal argument.** The launch
brief's numbers govern: on John the 23 leaf-reads spent on *fired* markers produced zero
corrections, and the nineteen *declined* oddities produced all seventeen. My attribution guess
carries no evidential weight, and the brief's within-work table (cols 381–390 four of four
Migne's; cols 399–415 five of five ours, ~200 columns apart in one book) says no prior may be
inherited from a neighbouring range either. **Every suspect word in this range is in §(e),
named, unattributed, with the leaf that would settle it.** Where a defect could be Migne's type
or Corpus Corporum's transcription I name both and assert neither.

**2. No `[cj:]` either.** Pattern 18's bar is that the printed word must make the sentence say
something the author is arguing against, and 18a scopes the extension to negations and
correlatives. Three sites in my range came close and all three are declined in §(e), with the
reason: **@0332A *utile cadaver*** (a coherent reading exists — the corpse they discarded was
in fact of use, and the Gentiles took it up, which is the chapter's whole argument),
**@0335A *illuc* for *illud*** (a pronoun slip, not a negation), and **@0338D *aut ne … aut***
(a genuine correlative disturbance, but I cannot name the true reading without the plate, and
"where a reading is merely uncertain, it stays in the crux").

**3. `[var:]` only for a divergent quoted CLAUSE I can cite** against
`sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` by book chapter:verse. **Five fired.** I declined every
one-word apparent divergence — *aut*/*vel*, *enim* added, *converte*/*convertere*,
*resurgent*/*resurgant*, *intraverit*/*intraret*, *non*/*nondum*, dropped *ergo* — and every
pure word-order or truncation case, following the peer stint that got this right this run.
A single word is suspicion of our file; a semantically coherent divergent clause is evidence
about Migne.

**4. One `[d:]` fired**, at @0326B (*id est id est*), under Pattern 11: an unmarked repetition
would read as *our* build defect. ⚠ **I do not know whose doubling it is** — the marker's
tooltip says Migne's plate, and I record here that the plate has not been read. It is also in
§(e) as an oddity so the reader can settle it at leaf 168.

⚑ **`-est` second-person forms** (*knewest*, *reapest*, *givest*, *wishest*, *mayest*) are
**Pattern 17**, not archaism drift: each renders a Latin second-person **singular** in the
gloss's own voice (*noveras*, *metis*, *das*, *vis*, *resurgas*), and Pattern 17 makes *thou*
a number marker, not a reverence marker. They are not the class convention 1 forbids.

⚑ **Both instrument warnings in the brief fired in my range and were caught.** A bare
`\bturba` grep here matches *turbatam* (@0335C, *turbata faciem velant*) and *turbatis*
(@0333D, *conscientia turbatis*) — both the verb *turbare*, neither a crowd. A bare `natio`
grep matches *damnationem* three times and *damnationis* once. My table above counts the noun
forms only.

**6. Negation fidelity (§4 / 7a).** Every `non`, `nec`, `neque`, `nisi`, `nullus`, `nihil`,
`nequaquam`, `necdum` the Latin prints stands in the English, including where carrying it
leaves the sentence odd. Two notes: @0340A *nonnunquam* is one lexeme meaning "sometimes" and
is rendered so — flagged here to pre-empt a particle count reading it as a lost negation; and
@0332D *Non enim potest quis esse Domini, nisi…* is rendered "no one can be the Lord's, unless
he has first renounced the world", which is the English exponent of *non … quis*, not a
dropped particle.

**7. Pattern 7 / Pattern 8 literalism, three visible costs, all deliberate.** @0327B carries
Migne's missing full stop (*legis dicta Mna decem drachmas appendit*), @0338C carries a second
(*poenitentiam agat praedixit et poenam*), and @0338C ends a paragraph with no stop at all
(*et criminosa*). Supplying the stops would have been a silent repair of exactly the class 9001
@0202D was nearly published with. @0329D carries a singular verb against a plural relative
(*humiliatur qui … indigent*) rather than smoothing it to "they are humbled".

  say what the author is arguing against, and here it does not. Logged, not marked. Leaf 171.

  An ablative absolute with no participle to complete it (*sublato*? *remoto*?). Rendered
  literally ("so that, with the veil of the concealing letter, the hidden sacraments … may
  begin to be disclosed") without supplying a verb. Leaf 172.
- **@0336B–C punctuation.** Migne's `Sic et nos cum … non desperemus` is an *ut*-less
  hortatory after a *cum*-clause; rendered as printed, not smoothed to an indicative.
