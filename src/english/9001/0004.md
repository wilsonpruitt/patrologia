## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- *The withered hand. [n: (BEDA.)]* Which was stretched out to the forbidden fruit, and withered from good work, and was restored by the sap of good works through the hands of Christ stretched out upon the cross.

[n: (HIER.)] This man signifies the covetous, who, unwilling to give, wish to receive, to plunder and not to bestow: to whom it is said that they should stretch forth their hands, that is, he who *stole, let him now steal no more, but rather let him labour, working [0189B] with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have wherewith to give to him that suffereth need [n: (Ephes. IV)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *And they watched him. [n: (BEDA.)]* Because he had plausibly excused the disciples: they watch himself, that if he heal on the sabbath they may accuse him of transgression: if he heal not, of cruelty or of weakness.

*It is lawful to do good on the sabbath days. [n: (ID.)]* He shows that they violate the precepts of the law by a wicked interpretation, thinking that on the sabbath one must keep holiday from good, whereas the law says, etc., *as far as* he set before them the likeness of the sheep, and concluded that *it is lawful to do good on the sabbath.*

*A soul. [n: (ID.)]* By the part he signifies the whole man. Either because he did this for the soul's sake, or because this healing of the hand signifies the salvation of the soul.

*To destroy. [n: (ID.)]* Not that he who is supremely merciful could [0189C] destroy anyone, but for him not to save is to destroy. As he is said to have hardened the heart of Pharao, not that he hardened it, but that he did not soften it when it was hardened.

VERS. 5.-- *Stretch forth thy hand. [n: (BEDA.)]* The unfruitful weakness of the soul is not better healed than by the bestowing of alms; whence: *He that hath two coats, let him give to him that hath none: and he that hath meat, let him do in like manner [n: (Luc. III)]*. And in Ecclesiasticus: *Son, let not thy hand be stretched out to take, and shut to give [n: (Eccl. IV)]*. For in vain does he spread out his hands to the Lord in prayer, who does not stretch them out to the widow that asks.

*And the hand was restored unto him.* A great zeal of wickedness, they reckon it for a crime., that at his word the hand was healed. Nor even on the sabbath can he be convicted [0189D] of having laboured, who spoke and they were made: whereas they themselves on the sabbath carry food, hand round cups, and the rest that is necessary for living.

VERS. 6.-- *And going forth.* They who cannot do it by themselves seek companions of iniquity. *With the Herodians.* The ministers of Herod, because of the enmities which he himself was exercising against the man, pursued with snares and hatreds Jesus, whom John preached.

VERS. 7.-- *He retired.* He flees as man, because his hour had not yet come, and there was no place for his passion outside Jerusalem.

[n: (BEDA.)] From the synagogue he retires to the sea, etc. He leaves Judaea, he comes to the Gentiles with his disciples, that is, when the apostles preached he approached the hearts of the Gentiles: and so [0190A] he received many that came to him, and gave the salvation they had desired.

*And a great crowd. [n: (ID.)]* The teachers of the people and the ministers of the king pursue him with hatred: the unlearned common folk follow him with love, and very many deserve to be healed.

VERS. 9.-- *A small ship.* The Church gathered out of the Gentiles, having passed over the waves of the world in mind: which, the more it widens the bosom of the heart for the receiving of grace, the more securely it treads upon the waves of the world. *Lest they should throng him.* They throng him who trouble the peace of the Church. They touch him who receive the faith with a true heart and with love, and they who touched him are reported to have been healed: fleeing those who thronged him, he went up into the little ship.

VERS. 11.-- *And the unclean spirits,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Each of the two, both they that had plagues, and the unclean spirits. [0190B] But the sick with a holy intention of health: the possessed, or rather the demons in them: compelled by fear not only to fall down, but also to confess his majesty. For, terrified by his presence, they could not hide him whom they now well knew to be the Son of God. Sounder than the Arians, who even to-day deny that he is the Son of God.

VERS. 12.-- *That they should not make him known. [n: (ID.)]* Not only the demons, who confess unwillingly, or those healed by him, who confess of their own accord: but the apostles also, who were to preach in all the world after the passion, are bidden to be silent before the passion, lest, his divine majesty being preached, the passion should be deferred, and so the salvation of the world.

VERS. 13.-- *And going up. [n: (ID.)]* After he had forbidden unclean spirits to preach him, he chose holy men, [0190C] who should preach him and cast out wicked spirits.

This mountain signifies the height of justice, in which they were to be instructed and which they were to preach, by the place he admonishes them to be raised up to heavenly things, and so he gave the law also upon a mountain.

[n: (HIER.)] It is the custom of divine Scripture to speak allegorically: but let us so apply high things to our own understandings, that the truth of the history, etc., *as far as* they are called into the mountain who are lofty both in merit and in word, that the place may accord with their high merits.

VERS. 14.-- *That they should be twelve.* God loved the beauty of Jacob when the Most High looked upon the nations, and divided the sons of Adam. He appointed the bounds of the peoples according to the number of the children of Israel, that they might be upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel. To whom [0190D] was given the power which the Son received from the Father according to the flesh, that they also might do the works which he himself did, and might do greater than these.

[n: (BEDA.)] He gave also the power of raising the dead, as Matthew says, that the power shown, faith, etc., *as far as* because *tongues are for a sign not to believers, but to unbelievers [n: (I Cor. XIV)]*.

VERS. 16.-- *And he imposed. [n: (ID.)]* Not then for the first time, but when he said to him who had been brought by Andrew: *Thou shalt be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter [n: (Joan. I)]*. But here the evangelist makes thee attentive, saying that, etc., *as far as* or hearing the sadness of death, that is, when it is said to him: *Another shall gird thee and lead thee whither thou wouldst not [n: (I Cor. IV)]*.

[0191A]VERS. 17.-- *And James.* Who has supplanted the desires of the flesh. And John, who received by grace what others receive by labour. *And he imposed on them the name Boanerges, which is, the sons of thunder.* Whose lofty merit deserves to hear upon the mountain the thunder of the Father thundering through the cloud concerning the Son: *This is my beloved Son [n: (Matth. XVII)]*, that through the cloud of the flesh they also might scatter upon the earth the fire and the lightnings of Christ into rain. for the Lord made the lightnings into rain, that mercy might quench what judgment burns; whence: *I will sing to thee mercy and judgment, O Lord [n: (Psal. C)]*.

*James.* Who, at the Lord's calling, supplanted the care of the flesh, and, when Herod slew him, despised the flesh itself.

[0191B] *John. [n: (BEDA.)]* In whom is grace, or *the grace of the Lord,* who, for the favour of that special love which he earned by the glory of virginity, reclined upon the breast of his Redeemer in the passion.

*Sons. [n: (ID.)]* Of whom one, thundering, uttered the theological voice (which no man before had known how to bring forth): *In the beginning was the Word,* etc. [n: (Joan. I.)] Which he left so heavy with weight, that if he had wished to thunder somewhat more, not even the world itself could have contained it.

VERS. 18.-- *And Andrew. [n: (HIER.)]* Who manfully does violence to his own perdition, that he may always have in himself the sentence of death, and his soul always in his own hands.

*And Philip. [n: (ID.)]* Who is *the mouth of a lamp,* who enlightens by his mouth what he has received, to whom is given the work of an enlightened [0191C] mouth. This kind of speech Scripture often records, when names are set down for the sake of mysteries: which the evangelist now, earnestly aiming at it in the person of God, inserts.

*And Bartholomew. [n: (ID.)]* Who is the son of him that suspends the waters, of him namely who said: *I will command my clouds that they rain not [n: (Isa. V)]*. But the name of a son of God is acquired through peace and love: for *Blessed* are *the peacemakers, for they shall be called the sons of God [n: (Matth. V)]*. And: *Love your enemies* that you may be *the sons of God [n: (Luc. VI)]*. [var: Vulg. Lc 6:35 reads *filii Altissimi*, "the sons of the Most High."]

*And Matthew.* Who is *given:* to whom is granted not only the remission of sins, but also to be in the number of the apostles, *that the lion and the ox may eat together, and the wolf with the lamb [n: (Isa. XI)]*.

Note, that the other evangelists put Matthew before, [0191D] and do not call him a publican. He himself sets himself after Thomas, and calls himself a publican.

*And Thomas. [n: (ID.)]* Who is *an abyss.* For many know deep things, but do not utter them. *Whence I know a man in Christ,* etc., even to: *which it is not lawful for a man to utter [n: (II Cor. XII)]*.

[n: (BEDA.)] Thomas an abyss: because he penetrated the depth of the power in the resurrection. Or a twin, which in Greek is called *didymus,* that is, doubting, because of his doubting heart, since he scarcely believed the resurrection.

*And James the son of Alpheus.* That is, of the learned or of the thousandth, of whom *at his side a thousand shall fall, and ten thousand at thy right hand [n: (Psal. XC)]*.

[n: (BEDA.)] James the son of Alpheus, who is also called the brother of the Lord: [0192A] because Mary of Alpheus was a sister, etc., *as far as* immediately after the resurrection he is ordained bishop of Jerusalem, as being learned or the son of a learned man.

*Thaddeus.* The keeper of the heart. Him Luke, in the Gospel and in the Acts of the Apostles, names Jude of James, because he was the brother of James the brother of the Lord. Whence he is called the son of Mary the mother of James and Joseph and Jude and Simon.

*[n: (HIER.)] And Thaddeus,* who is *a little heart,* that is, a tiller of the heart, who keeps his heart with all watchfulness. For by cleanness of heart God is seen, as through clean glass.

*And Simon. [n: (BEDA.)]* Simon the Cananean, from Cana a village of Galilee, who is also Simon *Zelotes,* that is, the zealous. Cana is zeal; the Cananean, Zelotes.

[0192B] *And Simon the Cananean.* Who is also Zelotes, Simon laying down or having sorrow. For *Blessed* are *they that mourn, for they shall be comforted [n: (Matth. V)]*. He fulfils a threefold mourning who seeks the comfort that is to come. He weeps for his own sins with David and Mary. With Paul he weeps with them that weep. And he weeps much with John, who said: *And I wept much: because no man was found worthy to open the book and to loose the seals thereof [n: (Apoc. V)]*. [var: Vulg. Apc 5:4 closes *nec videre eum*, "nor to look upon it"; the words about loosing the seals stand at Apc 5:2.] But he is called Zelotes whom the zeal of the house of God eats up, as Phinees: *And the slaughter ceased [n: (Num. XXV)]*.

VERS. 19.-- *And Judas Scariot.* Who does not blot out his sin by penance, nor is it blotted out by remembrance. Whence: *And let not the sin of his mother be blotted out. Let it be against the Lord continually [n: (Psal. CVIII)]*.

[0192C]Judas is *confessing* or *glorious;* Scarioth, *the memory of death.* There are many confessors in the Church, proud and boastful, of whom the first is Simon Magus, and other heretics, whose deadly memory is kept in the Church: that it may be blotted out.

[n: (HIER.)] Judas Scarioth, from the village in which he was born, or from the tribe of Issachar. Issachar is *a wage,* namely the price of perdition: Scarioth, etc., *as far as* whom the example of one humbles unto fear, as the fall of Lucifer humbled the angels, that *Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom [n: (Jer. IX)]*, but *he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord [n: (I Cor. I)]*.

*And they come,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] They who have undertaken the care of others ought carefully to guard the gift of conscience, that they may rule first themselves, and thence others.

[0192D]VERS. 20.-- [n: (HIER.)] The house in which they come together is the primitive Church. The crowds which hinder the eating of bread are sins and vices, because *he that eateth the body of the Lord unworthily, eateth and drinketh judgment to himself, not discerning the body of the Lord [n: (I Cor. XI)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 11:29 reads *qui enim manducat et bibit indigne*, "for he that eateth and drinketh unworthily," with no mention there of the body of the Lord.] Whence the Lord is turned into fury when he says: *Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, you shall not have life [n: (Joan. VI)]*.

VERS. 21.-- *And when they had heard,* etc. The crowd throngs him, his own despise him as one mad, in which the salvation of the Gentiles is marked, and the envy of the Jews is reproved, of whom: *He came unto his own, and his own received him not [n: (Joan. VI)]*.
