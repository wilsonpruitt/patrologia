## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And again he began to teach. [n: (BEDA.)]* Going out from the house where he is said to have a demon, he teaches by the [0194B] sea, that he might show that for the guilt of unbelief he would leave Judaea and pass over to the Gentiles. ⟦s2⟧ Whence it is fitly set before, that while he was preaching in the house, his mother and his brethren stood outside.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIER.)] He began to teach by the sea, that the place might indicate hearers bitter and unstable. ⟦s2⟧ And therefore, etc., *as far as* who sweetens bitter things, turns sad things into joy, sickness into health.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Going up into a ship,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The Church, which is in the sea among unbelievers, which God enlightens by the grace of his visitation and consecrates as a dwelling beloved to himself.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And all the crowd was about the sea. [n: (HIER.)]* These are they who have lately come to hear the word, and thence, by the devotion of their mind, have followed away from the bitterness of the reprobate as from the [0194C] sea: but they are not yet with the Lord in the ship, because they are not yet steeped in the heavenly mysteries or sacraments which they desire.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Many things in parables.* ⟦s2⟧ A parable is a comparison made, under some likeness, of things that differ from one another in nature. ⟦s3⟧ He speaks after the manner of his own prudence, that they who could not savour heavenly things might have been able to perceive them through an earthly likeness that they had heard. ⟦s4⟧ In Greek it is called a likeness when we point out, by certain comparisons, that which we wish to be understood, as we compare iron with some hard man, and a swift man with winds and with birds. ⟦s5⟧ The same is done in the thirtyfold, sixtyfold and hundredfold fruit, that is, in the law, in prophecy and in the Gospel, because in the mouth of three witnesses, that is, of Moses, of Elias and of Jesus, the mystery of the kingdom shown on the mountain stands firm.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0194D]VERS. 4.-- *Another fell by the way side,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Luke: *And it was trodden down, and the birds of the air devoured it [n: (Luc. VIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The way, the mind, worn by the fear of carnal thoughts: whence the seed perishes and bears no fruit, and is snatched away by the birds, that is, by the demons, who are called birds of the air either because they are of a heavenly and spiritual nature, or because they dwell in the air.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And these,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He sets down the differences of those who hear the word. ⟦s3⟧ But there are some, as the Gentiles, who do not even hear.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *The lusts after other things,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Which is a thing commanded. ⟦s3⟧ For it is commanded: *Thou shalt covet wisdom: keep the commandments [n: (Prov. VII)]* [var: Vulg. Prov. VII, 2 reads *serva mandata mea, et vives*, "keep my commandments, and thou shalt live", with no clause about coveting wisdom; the nearest received wording, Eccli. VI, 37, has the desire of wisdom given, not commanded], etc. ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *Blessed is the man that feareth the Lord, in his commandments he shall delight [0195A] exceedingly [n: (Psal. III)]*. ⟦s5⟧ These desires are good: he that desires the other things goes astray.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *A candle,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] The candle, the word of God. ⟦s2⟧ The bushel or the bed, the hearing of the disobedient. ⟦s3⟧ The candlestick, the apostles, whom the word of God enlightened. ⟦s4⟧ The hidden and the secret, which is made open when it is handled by the Lord.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] The bushel, our life measured by the divine foreknowledge. ⟦s2⟧ The bed, the flesh, in which, etc., *as far as* because he sets the lusts of the flesh before it.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Is it not that it may be set upon a candlestick? [n: (ID.)]* That the preaching of truth may bear rule, and the service of the body lie subject beneath it, through which nevertheless the teaching may appear the loftier, as the candle by the candlestick.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *For there is nothing,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if [0195B] he said: Be not ashamed of the Gospel, because there is nothing hidden now which shall not be made open in the judgment, *when God shall enlighten the hidden things of darkness, and shall make manifest the counsels of hearts [n: (Matth. X)]*; where there is praise for you from God, but for them everlasting punishment.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *With what measure,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] For according to the measure of faith the understanding of the mysteries is divided to each one, and the gift of knowledge shall be added to us, and virtues also shall be added.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *For he that hath,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, he that has faith shall have virtue, and he that has the work of the word shall have the understanding of the mystery. ⟦s3⟧ And on the contrary, he that has not faith fails of virtue; and he that has not the work of the word lacks the understanding of it; and he that does not understand loses the hearing, as though he had not heard.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0195C]VERS. 26.-- *So is the kingdom of God. [n: (HIER.)]* Divine Scripture shows the heavenly sacraments through earthly likenesses, as is plain throughout the whole body of the Gospels. ⟦s2⟧ *The kingdom of God* is the Church, which, etc., *as far as: Since the harvest is at hand,* that is, the consummation of the age, in which the just shall rejoice, they that have sown in tears.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *He putteth in the sickle. [n: (VICT. ANT.)]* The harvest indeed is the time of the consummation, the sickle, the word of God, more piercing than any two-edged sword. ⟦s2⟧ But the sickle in this place does not denote, etc., *as far as* for all bear wheat, though not all equally much.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *As a grain. [n: (HIER.)]* This seed he compares again to a grain of mustard, which is least in fear, great in charity, which is greater than [0195D] all herbs, because God is charity, and all flesh is grass, and let him that is weak eat herbs. ⟦s2⟧ And it makes the branches of mercy and of compassion. ⟦s3⟧ Under whose shadow the poor of Christ, who are the living creatures of heaven, delight to dwell.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It shall have been sown.* ⟦s2⟧ By Christ or by man in the earth, or in the field, that is, in his own heart. ⟦s3⟧ This man is our sense and our mind, which, cherishing the grain of preaching with the moisture of faith, makes it sprout in the field of its own breast. ⟦s4⟧ *It is less,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ As one preaching things contemptible, and at the first he is not believed, because he preaches God a man, dead, crucified, this, compared with the eloquence and the wisdom of the philosophers, is found less.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0196A]VERS. 32.-- *It groweth up,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Which in height, in breadth, in length of years surpasses the nature of herbs: it is high, because it lifts up to heavenly things; broad, because it has taken possession of the whole world; full of years, because it shall not be able to be ended. ⟦s3⟧ *Than herbs. [n: (BEDA.)]* The philosophic disciplines, which grow up so much the more openly, become the more worthless: because, like the pot-herbs of the grasses, they will quickly fall.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Without a parable. [n: (ID.)]* Not that he made no discourse plain to the crowds, but because no discourse of his is found in which something of parable is not found.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But apart, the disciples. [n: (HIER.)]* For they were worthy to hear the mysteries in the innermost sanctuary of wisdom, who, the tumults of their thoughts being removed, abode in the solitude of the virtues. ⟦s2⟧ For wisdom is to be learned in a time [0196B] of leisure.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *In the ship. [n: (BEDA.)]* Allegorically: the sea, the surge of the age. ⟦s2⟧ The little ship, the cross, by which we mount the waves of the world and come to the shore of the heavenly country. ⟦s3⟧ Other ships, etc., *as far as* for those ships perhaps had lately been made and only brought down into the harbour, or had been brought back to harbour after the perils of the sea.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *In the stern. [n: (HIER.)]* The stern with dead skins holds the living and keeps off the waves, and is made solid with wood, etc., *as far as: A great calm;* the peace of the Church after her afflictions, or the contemplative after the active life.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sleeping. [n: (BEDA.)]* While the disciples are sailing Christ falls asleep, because, while the faithful are meditating on the rest of the kingdom to come and, by the favouring breath of the Holy Ghost or by the [0196C] oarage of their own effort, are casting the waves of the world behind their backs, suddenly the passion of Christ comes upon them, and as he goes up, etc., *as far as* but let us run back to the pilot, let us diligently rouse him, who does not serve the winds but commands them, who will still all things, who will grant the harbour of salvation.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Peace, be still.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that every creature feels the command of the Creator. ⟦s3⟧ Not that all things are animate, as the heretics lie, but that by the majesty of the founder the things which are insensible to us are sensible to him.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *Why are you fearful? [n: (BEDA.)]* As if he said: Why do you fear while I am present? ⟦s2⟧ To which is like that which, after the resurrection, *he upbraided their unbelief and hardness of heart, because they who saw him rise again did not believe [n: (Marc. XVI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And again: [0196D] *O foolish and slow of heart to believe in all things which the prophets have spoken! ⟦s4⟧ Ought not Christ to have suffered, and so to enter into his glory [n: (Luc. XXIV)]*?

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they feared.* ⟦s2⟧ Matthew: *But the men wondered, saying: What manner of man is this,* etc. [n: (Matth. VIII.)] Not therefore the disciples, but others who were in the ship, were wondering, doubting of him whom the sea and the wind obeyed. ⟦s3⟧ Or even the disciples themselves are rightly called men, because they did not yet know the power of the Saviour.
