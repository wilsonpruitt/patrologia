## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- *And they came,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Gerasa, a notable city of Arabia beyond Jordan, joined to mount Galaad in the tribe of Manasses, not far from the lake of Tiberias, [0197A] into which the swine were cast headlong, signifies, etc., *as far as* and he that was far off has been made nigh in the blood of Christ.

VERS. 2.-- *A man with an unclean spirit. [n: (ISID.)]* That most desperate man is the people of the Gentiles whom the Apostle enumerates. Puffed up, proud, unclean, bloody, an idolater, dishonourable, etc., *as far as* for when the fool is scourged the wise man becomes wiser, or more prudent.

VERS. 3.-- *And not even with chains,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Heavy with chains and fetters, and the two laws of the Gentiles are signified, by which sins were restrained in their commonwealth. The chains being burst (as Luke writes), he was suddenly driven by the Lord into the desert, because, those laws also being transgressed, they were hurried by lust into those crimes which now exceeded the common usage.

[0197B]VERS. 7.-- *Torment me not. [n: (ID.)]* It is a great torment to him to cease from the injuring of a man, and by so much the more grievously does he let go as the longer he possesses.

VERS. 9.-- *What is thy name? [n: (BEDA.)]* He asks not as though ignorant, but that, the public plague which he had endured being confessed, the power of him that heals may appear the more welcome. But the priests also of our time, who cast out demons by exorcism, are wont to say: that the sufferers cannot be healed otherwise, unless, so far as they are able to understand, they plainly confess all that they have endured from the demons by sight, by hearing, or by any sense of body or of mind, waking or sleeping.

*My name is Legion. [n: (ID.)]* It signifies the Gentile people made over to countless cults of idolatry: against which is, etc., *as far as* but more [0197C] sects than tongues confound the reprobate by setting them at variance.

VERS. 12.-- *Send us. [n: (ID.)]* Luke: *And they besought him that he would not command them to go into the abyss [n: (Luc. VIII)]*. They knew therefore that it would one day come to pass that by his coming they should be sent into the abyss, not as things now to be, etc., *as far as* for unless a man live after the manner of a swine, the devil receives no power over him, unless perchance to prove him, but not to destroy him.

VERS. 13.-- *The herd was cast headlong. [n: (BEDA.)]* Because, the Church being now made glorious and the people of the Gentiles set free from the dominion of the demons, they carry on their sacrilegious rites in hidden places, etc., *as far as* since for the salvation of one man two thousand swine are drowned.

VERS. 14.-- *But they that fed them.* Because certain chief men of the impious, although they flee the Christian law, yet by being amazed at its power [0197D] and wondering at it, preach it among the Gentiles.

VERS. 15.-- *And they see him. [n: (BEDA.)]* Luke: *Sitting at his feet.* which signifies that they who have been corrected from their sins behold, with fixed intention of mind, the footsteps of the Saviour which they are to follow.

*Clothed.* With the zeal of the virtues, which he had lost by a maddened mind. Whence the prodigal son, returning to his father, is straightway clothed with the first robe together with the ring: because whosoever shall have repented with a true heart can, by the gift of Christ's grace, recover the former works of justice from which he had fallen, together with the ring of unviolated faith.

*And they feared.* For many, delighted with the old sweetness, say that they honour the Christian law but are unwilling to suffer for it, [0198A] because they cannot fulfil it, yet admiring the faithful people healed from their former manner of life.

VERS. 17.-- *And they began to beseech him. [n: (RAB.)]* As Peter: *Depart from me, O Lord, for I am a sinful man,* etc. [n: (Luc. V.)] Lest they touch the ark of God, as Oza.

VERS. 19.-- *Go into thy house. [n: (BEDA.)]* So let each one, after the resurrection from sins, return into a good conscience and serve the Gospel for the salvation of others: that afterwards he may rest with Christ, lest, while he too hastily wishes to be already with Christ, he neglect the mystery of preaching that is fitted to his brother's redemption.

[n: (HIER.)] The man that is healed is sent into his own house, that is, into the spiritual observance of the law: and he preaches, [0198B] etc., *as far as* because moral things are to be observed according to the letter.

[n: (BEDA.)] Matthew says that two, Mark and Luke that one, were healed of the demon, because one of them was of a more illustrious name, is of a more famous: and therefore his healing is the more famous. Allegorically: the two signify the nations from the two sons of Noe. For whereas Noe begot three sons, one was taken into the possession of God, two were given over to idols.

VERS. 22.-- *And there cometh a certain man,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] After these things Jairus the ruler of the synagogue comes, because when the fulness of the Gentiles shall have entered in, then all Israel, etc., *as far as* Abraham and Moses and Samuel pray for the dead people, and Jesus follows their prayers.

[n: (BEDA.)] Jairus, *enlightening* or *enlightened:* because [0198C] he enlightened the people with the words of life: and that he might be able to do this, he was enlightened by the Holy Ghost.

VERS. 23.-- *For my daughter. [n: (BEDA.)]* Luke: *For he had one only daughter, about twelve years old, and she was dying [n: (Luc. VIII)]*. The daughter of the ruler of the synagogue is the Synagogue, by the legal institution alone, etc., *as far as* suddenly laid low by the languor of errors, she despairingly forsook the spiritual ways.

VERS. 24.-- *And they thronged him.* While he goes to heal the girl, he is thronged by the crowd: because, offering to the Jews the warnings of salvation by which he might raise up their sick conscience, he was burdened by the harmful custom of carnal peoples.

VERS. 25.-- *And a woman,* etc. As the Lord goes on toward the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, the diseased woman of twelve years [0198D] snatches health before her, and afterwards the daughter was healed and was raised up: because the salvation of the human race was so dispensed that first some out of Israel, then the fulness of the Gentiles, and so all Israel shall be saved.

[n: (BEDA.)] It is to be noted that the girl was twelve years old and that the woman flowed with blood twelve years, etc., *as far as* while Christ was determining to heal Judaea, she forestalled with sure hope the salvation already prepared and promised to others.

VERS. 26.-- *Having suffered many things,* etc. [n: (ID.)] From false theologians, philosophers, teachers of the secular laws, who promised that they would give useful precepts of living. The demons too, etc., *as far as* they began both to hope for and to seek out a remedy for their languor.

[0199A]VERS. 27.-- *She came in the crowd. [n: (ID.)] If any man minister to me, let him follow me [n: (Joan. XII)]*. And elsewhere: *Thou shalt walk after the Lord God [n: (Ose. XI)]*. Or because she does not see God present in the flesh, but afterwards comes to the grace of the acknowledgment of faith, etc., *as far as* by the evangelical sacraments, as by his own garments, he cleanses the fountains of unclean blood.

VERS. 31.-- *Who touched me. [n: (BEDA.)]* The faithful woman alone touches the Lord, whom the crowd accompanying him on every side throngs. Because he, who is burdened by the diverse heresies of them that disorderly throng together, or by perverse manners, is touched only by the faithful heart of the Church; whence: *Touch me not: for I am not yet ascended to my Father [n: (Joan. XX)]*. Because to touch him truly is to believe him equal to the Father.

[0199B] [n: (HIER.)] By the seventh virtue the dead girl is raised, but on the way the woman with the issue of blood touches the hem from behind, who together with health her substance, etc., *as far as* the cymbal, confession; the pomegranate signifies the will of martyrdom and the whiteness of the virtues.

VERS. 33.-- *But the woman. [n: (BEDA.)]* Behold whither the questioning tended, namely, that the woman should confess the truth of her long infirmity, and of her sudden believing and healing: and that so she herself might be confirmed in faith and might afford an example to others.

VERS. 35.-- *While he was yet,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The woman being healed, straightway the girl is announced dead: because the Church of the Gentiles, cured of the stain of vices, and for the merit of her faith, etc., *as far as* but what is impossible to men is possible to God.

[0199C]VERS. 36.-- *To the ruler of the synagogue.* The ruler of the synagogue is the company of the teachers of the law, of whom: *Upon the chair of Moses have sitten the scribes and the Pharisees [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]*. And if they shall be willing to believe, the Synagogue also that is subject to them shall be saved.

VERS. 37.-- *And he admitted not,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The mysteries are not to be revealed to blasphemers and mockers, but to the faithful who may honour them. Whence elsewhere: *Wisdom is justified by all her children [n: (Matth. XI)]*.

VERS. 38.-- *Weeping and wailing. (ID)*. *Let all rejoice that hope in the Lord, and they shall exult for ever, and thou shalt dwell in them [n: (Psal. V)]*. This gladness the Synagogue lost by the desert of her unbelief: and therefore she lies dead among those that weep and howl. But Jesus does not suffer her to perish utterly.

[0199D]VERS. 39.-- *She is not dead.* To me, in whose disposing both the soul that has been received lives and the flesh that is to be raised rests. Hence it is a custom among Christians that the dead, who are not doubted to be about to rise again, are called sleepers; whence: *We will not have you ignorant, brethren, concerning them that sleep [n: (I Thess. IV)]*.

[n: (BEDA.)] Since the soul that shall have sinned dies, yet that soul which the Lord foresees unto eternal life may not unfittingly be said to have been dead indeed to us, but to have fallen asleep to him.

VERS. 40.-- *All being cast out. [n: (ID.)]* The crowd is cast out that the girl may be raised: because unless the multitude of secular cares be cast out from the heart, the soul that lies dead within is not raised. For while it scatters itself through all [0200A] its thoughts, it in no wise gathers itself to the consideration of itself.

VERS. 41.-- *Talitha cumi.* In the Syriac speech which the evangelist sets down it is no more than: Girl, arise. But perhaps to express the force of the Lord's command he added this: *I say to thee, arise,* and took care to convey the sense of the speaker rather than his words. So often the apostles and the evangelists, when they take up some testimony, set down the sense rather than the words.

[n: (HIER.)] To the girl it is said *Talitha cumi,* that is, Girl, arise. To the ruler of the synagogue, etc., *as far as* whence it is said: *Abraham believed God, and it was reputed to him unto justice [n: (Jac. II)]*.

[n: (BEDA.)] That Christ raised three dead persons signifies three kinds of souls. He raises the girl, etc., [0200B] *as far as* he heard of a fourth dead man when a disciple announced it: but because living men to pray for him were wanting: *Let,* he says, *the dead bury their dead [n: (Matth. VIII)]*.
