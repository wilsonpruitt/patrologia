## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 2.-- *Whence hath this man,* etc. Wonderful blindness. In his deeds and in his words they are able to know Christ, for the mere knowledge of his stock they despise him.

VERS. 3.-- *A craftsman. [n: (BEDA.)]* He who works with fire and with spirit; whence: *He shall baptize you in fire and in spirit [n: (Joan. I)]* [var: Vulg. Matt. III, 11 and Luc. III, 16 read *ipse vos baptizabit in Spiritu Sancto, et igni*, "in the Holy Ghost and fire", in the reverse order; Joan. I, 33 has only the Holy Ghost]. He is the craftsman who crafted the dawn and the sun.

VERS. 4.-- *A prophet is not without honour.* It is well-nigh natural that fellow-citizens should envy fellow-citizens, because they remember not his works but the childhood [0200C] which they saw.

[n: (ISID.)] Meanness often attends a man's origin. As, *Who is the son of Isai [n: (III Reg. XII)]*. But: *The Lord beholdeth the lowly, and the high he knoweth afar off [n: (Psal. CXXXVII)]*.

VERS. 5.-- *And he could not,* etc. Lest by doing many mighty works he should condemn the worse his unbelieving fellow-citizens. Or because he is despised in his own country, he did few mighty works and signs there, lest they should be wholly excusable. Greater signs he does daily among the Gentiles, not so much in the healing of bodies as of souls.

VERS. 6.-- *And he wondered. [n: (BEDA.)]* Not as at something unforeseen, but he shows it as a thing to be wondered at, because they believe neither the prophets, nor him whom they behold present.

VERS. 7.-- *And he called. [n: (HIER.)]* The twelve [0200D] apostles are sent, and there is given them power, precepts of teaching, that word and deed together may accompany them: and that mighty works may be mingled with invisible promises, and so, when they anoint the sick with oil, they strengthen the weakness of faith by a mighty work.

*And he gave them,* etc. The kind Lord and master does not grudge his own power to his disciples and servants. But he acts by his own power, they by their own weakness, if they do anything, and they confess the Lord's might, saying: *In the name of Jesus arise and walk [n: (Act. III)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *That they should take nothing,* etc. Let their confidence in the Lord be so great that they may know most certainly that nothing is wanting, lest, while they provide temporal things for themselves, they provide eternal things the less for others.

[0201A] [n: (BEDA.)] Matthew and Luke record that the Lord said to his disciples that they should not carry even a staff: Mark, that they should take nothing save a staff. But they take the staff really. Mark understands by the staff the power of receiving what is necessary from those subject to them.

VERS. 9.-- *Shod with sandals. [n: (ID.)]* That the foot be neither covered nor bare to the ground, that is, that neither the Gospel be hidden, nor lean upon earthly advantages.

*That they should not put on two coats. [n: (ID.)]* Which is, that they should walk in singleness, not in doubleness.

VERS. 11.-- *Shake off the dust from your feet. [n: (ID.)]* The dust is shaken off from the feet in testimony of their labour, that they have entered the city, that the preaching has reached even unto them. Or that they have received nothing from them [0201B] toward the food that was needful, who despised the Gospel.

VERS. 13.-- *And they anointed with oil. [n: (ID.)]* James says: *Is any man sick among you? let him bring in the priests of the Church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil [n: (Jac. V)]*, etc. Whence it is plain that this custom was handed down from the apostles, that the possessed and other sick persons should be anointed with oil consecrated by a bishop.

VERS. 14.-- *And the king heard. [n: (ID.)]* Luke [n: (Luc. IX)]: *Herod the tetrarch heard all things that were done,* etc. Whence it is to be understood that either, being confirmed by the words of others, he said it in belief, or that he was still hesitating, etc., *as far as* they chose rather to believe that he had not risen again, but had been carried off by stealth.

VERS. 17.-- *Of Philip his brother. [n: (BEDA.)]* An ancient [0201C] history relates that Philip was the son of the elder Herod, and the brother of that Herod under whom the Lord suffered, and that he married Herodias the daughter of Aretas, and that afterwards Aretas, quarrels having arisen, took away his own daughter, and joined her in marriage to Herod, the enemy of Philip, to Philip's own grief. Egesippus, however, or Josephus, far otherwise.

VERS. 18.-- *It is not lawful for thee to have,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He who had come in the spirit and power of Elias reproves Herod and Herodias, as, etc., *as far as* for man is born to labour, and the saints pass through death to rest.

VERS. 23.-- *And he swore. [n: (BEDA.)]* He is not excused from perjury by his oath. For perhaps he swore for this reason, that he might find an occasion of killing. And if she had asked the death of her father or [0201D] of her mother, Herod assuredly would not have granted it.

VERS. 26.-- *And being made sorrowful,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Made sorrowful after the accustomed manner of Scripture, not in fact, but in the opinion of many. As Joseph himself also, etc., *as far as* that under the occasion of piety he might become impious.

VERS. 27.-- *His head in a dish. [n: (ID.)]* The head of the law, which is Christ, is cut off from the body, etc., *as far as* but Christ, who is the light of the world, when the days begin to grow.

VERS. 29.-- *And they took. [n: (ID.)]* Josephus relates that John was bound, brought into the castle of Macherus and there beheaded. The Ecclesiastical history relates that it was in Sebaste, a city of Palestine, which was once called Samaria. [0202A] But in the time of Julian, etc., *as far as* the temple of Serapis being purged of its filth, he laid those same bones there, and consecrated a basilica in honour of Saint John.

VERS. 30.-- *And gathering together. [n: (HIER.)]* To the place whence the rivers go out they return. They always render thanks to God for those things which they have received.

*All things.* The apostles report to the Lord not only what they themselves had done and taught, but also what John suffered while they were teaching. Either his own disciples, or those of the same John, as Matthew writes.

VERS. 31.-- *Come apart. [n: (BEDA.)]* Not only for the sake of a seeking again, but mystically it signifies that, Judaea being left, which, etc., *as far as* he comes apart, he leads those whom he chooses, that among men living ill they may not attend to evil things, [0202B] as Lot in Sodom, and Job in the land of Hus, and Abdias in the house of Achab.

*Rest ye.* As the birds in the branches of the mustard. Little is the rest here for the saints, long the labour: but afterwards it is said to them that they should rest from their labours.

*For there were,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] In the ark of Noe the animals that were within were sent forth, and those that were without burst in. So in the Church Judas, etc., *as far as* then shall the wind and the storm cease, Jesus sitting and reigning in the ship, which is the universal Church.

VERS. 32.-- *They went away in the desert. [n: (BEDA.)]* By seeking solitude he tries the faith of the crowds, whether it follows him, and having tried it he rewards it with a worthy recompense. But the crowds, not with beasts of burden, not with carriages, but by the very labour [0202C] of their feet, take up the journey of the desert, and show their desire of salvation. Again, by receiving the weary, by teaching the ignorant, by healing the sick, by refreshing the fasting, he intimates how much he is delighted by the devotion of them that believe.

VERS. 33.-- *And on foot.* Note that they did not come to the other bank of the sea without a sailing of the Jordan. But, some strait or lake being passed over, they went to the nearest places of the same region, whither even the natives on foot were able to come.

VERS. 34.-- *And he had compassion. [n: (BEDA.)]* How he had compassion Matthew sets forth more fully in this manner: *And he had compassion on them, and healed their infirmities.* [var: Vulg. Matt. XIV, 14 reads *curavit languidos eorum*, "healed their sick"; the gloss immediately following expounds the healing of the sick, not of infirmities] For this is truly to have compassion on the poor and on them that have no shepherd, and to open the way of truth, and the languishing [0202D] to heal, and by refreshing the fasting to hearten them to the praise of the heavenly bounty; and the things that follow declare that he did this.

VERS. 35.-- *And when the hour was now.* The day being far spent, the Saviour refreshes the crowds, because either as the end of the age draws near, or when the sun of justice set for us, we were delivered from the long hunger of spiritual want.

[n: (BEDA.)] Christ, as he seeks the desert places of the Gentiles, many bands of the faithful follow, the walls of their former manner of life being left and the safeguard of divers doctrines neglected, and whereas God was first known in Judaea, then was he exalted above the heavens, and his glory above all the earth.

VERS. 37.-- *Give ye them.* He provokes the apostles to the breaking [0203A] of bread, that, when they testified that they had none, the greatness [sic: *magnitudino*] of the sign might become known. And intimating that daily through them fasting hearts are to be fed, when by their examples or by their words we are stirred up to love heavenly things. It is to be noted that John, about to write this miracle of the loaves, set before it that the Pasch was at hand. Matthew and Mark say that this was done immediately when John had been killed: whence it is gathered that John was beheaded when the Pasch was imminent, and that the Lord was crucified at the next following Pasch.

VERS. 38.-- *Five. [n: (BEDA.)]* The five loaves, the five books of the Mosaic law, in which is the knowledge of the divine eternity, the creation of the world, the course of the passing age, and the true religion of serving God, etc., *as far as* but at the last with a great gift he grants to the elect that they may eat and [0203B] drink at his table in his kingdom.

VERS. 39.-- *By companies. [n: (ID.)]* The diverse reclinings, the distinct orders of the churches which make one catholic Church.

VERS. 40.-- *By hundreds and by fifties.* By fifty, the rest of the jubilee. But the number fifty taken twice makes a hundred; and therefore it signifies the perfection of both rests, namely, of the body and of the mind. For he who rests from depraved acts has the rest of the body. But he who rests from perverse thoughts also has the rest of the mind.

And well do they that recline upon the green grass feed upon the nourishments of God, who by the pursuit of abstinence, the allurements of the flesh being trodden down, bestow their pains upon hearing and fulfilling the words of God.

[0203C]Therefore some are by fifties, others by hundreds, because rest is first had from evil work, that afterwards there may be rest in the mind also.

VERS. 41.-- *And having taken.* He creates no new viands, because, being incarnate, he preaches no other things than those which had been written: but he shows that the law and the prophets are big with mysteries.

*He broke.* And he distributed to his disciples the things to be set before the crowds, because he laid open the sacraments to the holy teachers, who should preach them throughout the whole world.

VERS. 43.-- *And they took up,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The more secret mysteries, which the untaught cannot grasp, the more perfect diligently search out. For by the baskets, the twelve apostles: and by the apostles the teachers that follow are figured, despised without, within heaped up with the leavings of the saving food [0203D] for the feeding of humble hearts. For in baskets servile works are carried: but he filled them with the bread of the fragments, who chose the weak things of the world, that he might break or confound.

VERS. 44.-- *Five thousand. [n: (ID.)]* Because of the five senses of the body. These, having followed the Lord, signify those who, being still set in a secular habit, know how to use well the outward things which they possess. Who are rightly fed with five loaves: because it is needful that such be still instructed by the precepts of the law. But they who wholly renounce the world are four thousand and are refreshed with seven loaves, that is, lofty in evangelical perfection, and inwardly taught by spiritual grace.

VERS. 45.-- *And immediately he constrained.* He gives us an example [0204A] that in all the good things which we do we should shun the recompense of human favour: lest the working of virtues bend us to the desire of temporal things. And the Lord himself, intimating this to us, when they who admired his mighty works wished to make him king, fled into the mountain to pray. He readily met his crucifiers, namely, that we may be ready to bear adversities, wary toward flatteries if they should smile upon us, and that, lest they deceive us by softening us if they abound, we should implore the Lord with frequent prayers.

*To Bethsaida. [n: (BEDA.)]* Bethsaida is a city in Galilee, of Andrew and of Peter and of Philip, near the lake of Genesareth. But Mark says, etc., *as far as* both of which are cities in Galilee beside the lake of Genesareth: which is also called of Tiberias, from the city of Tiberias [0204B] being so named.

VERS. 46.-- *He went into the mountain. [n: (ID.)]* He prays well who seeks God by praying, and, advancing from earthly things to the higher, ascends the summit of a loftier court. He who concerning riches, etc., *as far as* he can do all that he wills, because he is our advocate and our judge; the one the office of loving-kindness, the other the mark of power.
