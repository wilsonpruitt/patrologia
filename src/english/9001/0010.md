## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 1.-- *And they come together. [n: (ID.)]* It is plain how true is that confession of the Lord to the Father in which it is said: *Because thou hast hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones [n: (Matth. XI)]*. The unlearned come, they bring the sick, that they may touch even his hem, and therefore, having desired health, they suffer. But the Pharisees and the Scribes, who ought to have been the teachers of the people, run together not to [0205B] hear the word, not as to a physician, but to the battles of questionings.

VERS. 2.-- *With common hands. [n: (HIERON.)]* By unclean, or common, hands he signifies the communion of the Gentiles. The cleanness of the Pharisees is barren; the communion of the apostles does not wholly stretch out its branches as far as the sea. *Not washed.* They find fault over the unwashed hands of the body, though in their works nothing of uncleanness is found. They themselves, washed outwardly with water, are inwardly polluted with the rancour of conscience.

VERS. 3.-- *The Pharisees. [n: (BEDA.)]* The superstitious tradition of men, to wash the oftener, for the sake of eating and the like. But the necessary teaching of truth bids those who desire to eat the bread of life which came down from heaven to cleanse their works by the frequent lament of alms and of the other [0205C] fruits of justice.

VERS. 4.-- *Washings.* In vain do they keep the washings of vessels, who neglect the filth of their own hearts and bodies. For this was not said of material hands, but for the cleansing of minds, and the chastisement of works, and the holiness and salvation of souls.

VERS. 5.-- *Why do thy disciples,* etc. Wondrous folly! They accuse the Son of God because he does not keep the precepts of men. But the hands, that is, the works not of the body but of the soul, are to be washed, that the Word of God may be wrought in them.

VERS. 6.-- *Well did he prophesy. [n: (HIERON.)]* The fork of reason represses the barking of the Pharisees, that is, by the rebuke of Moses and of Isaias, that we too may be able to overcome heretics by the words [0205D] of Scripture.

VERS. 9.-- *Well do you make void.* He refutes the false calumny by true reasoning, as if, You, for the tradition of men, despise and neglect the precepts of God, why then do you accuse my disciples, who make little of the commands of the elders, that they may keep the commandments of God?

VERS. 11.-- *But you say. [n: (BEDA.)]* The Lord, taking counsel for the weakness whether of age or of the poverty of parents, commanded, etc., *as far as* to him who is the true Father let the oblation of the Lord be preferred. *It shall profit thee. [n: (ISID.)]* It is consumed for thy own uses, which is sacrilege. Or interrogatively. As if he said: Will it profit thee? It ought not: fearing this, parents were afflicted with hunger.

VERS. 15.-- *There is nothing outside,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] Nor [0206A] is it the meat offered to idols, in so far as it is food, in so far as it is a creature of God, but the invocation of demons, that makes this unclean. Whence: *You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons [n: (I Cor. X)]*.

The Jews boast themselves the portion of God, and call those foods common which all men use, such as oysters, hares, and animals of this kind, which do not divide the hoof, nor chew the cud, nor, among fishes, are scaly.

VERS. 18.-- *And you also are without understanding.* They are rebuked because they think those things mystical which are plain of themselves. From which we perceive him to be a faulty hearer who wishes to understand obscure things plainly, or things plainly said obscurely.

VERS. 19.-- *And into the draught. [n: (BEDA.)]* Hence certain heretics cavil that the Lord, ignorant of natural [0206B] philosophy, supposes all foods, etc., *as far as* it slips away through hidden passages (which the Greeks call poros) to the lower parts, and goes into the draught.

[n: (AUG.)] Some things so come to us that they both change and are changed, just as food itself, losing its own appearance, is turned into our body, and we, refreshed, are changed into strength.

VERS. 21.-- *From the heart of men. [n: (BEDA.)]* The chief seat of the soul is not, according to Plato, in the brain, but, according to Christ, in the heart. They are also reproved, etc., *as far as* so that, if he has often seen us gaze upon a beautiful woman, he understands that we love her.

VERS. 24.-- *And rising up from thence.* Leaving the Scribes and Pharisees, those calumniators, he withdrew into the parts of Tyre and Sidon, that he might heal the Tyrians and the Sidonians. *No man.* [0206C] [n: (BEDA.)] Although he could not be hid, yet what he willed was not done. But an example, etc., *as far as* that he might free the daughter of the Syrophoenician woman from the demon, and by the faith of a Gentile woman convict the perfidy of the Scribes and the Pharisees.

VERS. 25.-- *For a woman.* Mark says that the Lord was in the house when the woman came to him. Matthew says that *she crieth after us [n: (Matth. XV)]*. By which he hints that she poured out her prayers behind him as he walked. The woman therefore came to him into the house, but because Matthew says: *He answered her not a word,* he gave it to be understood that both were silent, and that they entered in silence. And so the rest is woven together, and in nothing do they disagree.

[n: (HIER.)] The woman is our Roman mother of the Church. The daughter is the demon-possessed barbarism of the West, whose [0206D] faith made a sheep out of a dog. She seeks the crumbs of understanding; she does not desire the unbroken bread of the letter.

[n: (BEDA.)] The woman, a Gentile, but coming to the Lord with faith, signifies the Church gathered out of the Gentiles, who for her daughter, etc., *as far as* who leave the ancient dwellings of their perfidy, and betake themselves with pious devotion into the house of God, that is, the Church.

VERS. 27.-- *Suffer first the children to be filled.* As if he said: It shall come to pass that you Gentiles also obtain salvation: but first the Jews, who by the ancient election are named the sons of God, must be refreshed with the heavenly bread, and so at last the food of life be ministered to the Gentiles. *Of the children. [n: (BEDA.)]* A wondrous turning about: for Israel was once the son, we the dogs. According to the diversity of faith the order of the names is changed. [0207A] Of them it is said: *Many dogs have compassed me [n: (Psal. XXI)]*. Of us: *Other sheep I have, that are not of this fold [n: (Joan. X)]*.

VERS. 28.-- *Of the crumbs.* I am not worthy to receive whole meats; I am content with the leavings of the whelps, that so I may come to the whole bread. *[n: (BEDA.)] Of the crumbs,* etc. The table is Scripture, whence: *Thou hast prepared a table before me,* etc. [n: (Psal. XXII)]. The crumbs of the children, etc., *as far as* that by the merit of humility they may raise themselves up to hope for the things above, the rewards which have been promised by the Lord in heaven.

VERS. 29.-- *For this saying. [n: (BEDA.)]* Here is given an example of catechizing and baptizing infants, because by the faith and confession of their parents little ones are freed from the demon in baptism, who cannot yet of themselves have understanding, or do anything of good or of evil.

[0207B]VERS. 31.-- *Of Tyre. [n: (HIERON.)]* Tyre, meaning straitness, signifies Judea, to which the Lord says: *The bed is straitened and the covering short: the one cannot cover both, so that he may betake himself to other nations [n: (Isai. XVIII)]*. *Sidon. [n: (HIERON.)]* Sidon, a hunting, an untamed wild beast, is our own region or nation. *Of Decapolis. [n: (BEDA.)]* The region of ten cities beyond the Jordan toward the east. But where it is said: *He came to the sea of Galilee, through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis,* this does not signify that he entered them; for neither is he said to have sailed across the sea, but to have come as far as the sea, and to a place which looked toward the midst of the coasts of Decapolis, set far beyond the sea.

[n: (HIERON.)] The Saviour is led by the apostles to the saving of the Gentiles, and teaches what he answered to the young man who questioned him: *Thou knowest the commandments; do these things, and thou shalt live [n: (Luc. XVIII)]*.

[0207C]VERS. 32.-- *One deaf and dumb. [n: (BEDA.)]* One, namely, who opens neither his ears to hear the words of God, nor his mouth to speak them. Such men they who have learned by long use to hear and to speak the word of God must needs offer to the Lord to be healed, that those whom human frailty cannot [heal], the right hand of grace may save.

[n: (HIERON.)] The human race, as one man consumed by divers plagues in the first-formed man, is blinded while it sees, is made deaf while it hears, its nose is wiped while it smells, it is struck dumb while it speaks, it is made maimed while it lifts up its hands, it is bowed down while it is raised up, it becomes dropsical while it lusts, lame while it goes forward, it is overspread with leprosy while it is cleansed, it is filled with a demon while it aspires to divinity, it dies the death while it boldly excuses itself. [0207D] *And they besought him.* The prophets and the patriarchs desired the incarnation of the Lord.

VERS. 33.-- *And taking him,* etc. He who deserves to be healed is led apart from turbulent thoughts, from disordered deeds, and from ill-composed discourses. The fingers which are put into the ears are the words of the Holy Spirit, of whom it is said: *This is the finger of God [n: (Exod. VIII)]*. And: *The heavens are the works of thy fingers [n: (Psal. VIII)]*. *He touched his tongue,* etc. The foam from the flesh of the Lord is the divine wisdom, which looses the ignorance of the human race, so that it may say: I believe in God the Father almighty, etc. *Apart. [n: (BEDA.)]* The first way of salvation is to be led out from the crowd. The Lord leads the sick man out from the crowd, when the mind, by sins, etc., *as far as* therefore, spitting, he touches [0208A] the tongue of the dumb man that it may be able to speak, when by the touch of his loving-kindness he forms mouths long brutish to utter the words of wisdom.

VERS. 34.-- *And looking up,* etc. *[n: (HIERON.)] He groaned.* He taught us to groan, and to lift up to heaven the treasure of our heart, which is purified by compunction of heart from the frivolous gladness of the flesh, as it is said: *I roared with the groaning of my heart; Lord, all my desire is before thee,* etc. *And he said to him: Ephpheta, which is, Be thou opened. With the heart we believe unto justice [n: (Rom. X)]*, etc. *And immediately they were opened,* etc. The ears are opened to hymns and canticles and psalms. The tongue is loosed, that it may utter forth a good word, which neither threats nor words can restrain or forbid. Whence Paul: *I am bound, but the word of God is not bound in me [n: (I Tim. II)]*.

[0208B]VERS. 36.-- *And he charged them that they should tell no man.* He taught that we must glory not in mighty works, but in the cross and in humiliation. *But the more he charged them,* etc. A city set on a mountain and looked upon from every side cannot be hid [n: (Matth. XV)]: humility always goes before glory.

*Be thou opened.* Properly, to the ears. For the ears were to be opened for hearing, and the tongue was to be loosed that it might be able to speak. Whence it follows: *Immediately they were opened,* etc.

VERS. 35.-- *And he spoke right. [n: (BEDA.)]* He alone speaks rightly, whether by confessing the Lord or by preaching to others, whose hearing divine grace brings back, that he may be able to hearken and be obedient to the heavenly commandments. Whose tongue the Lord instructs to speak by the touch of wisdom, which he himself is. Such a one can say: [0208C] *O Lord, thou wilt open my lips [n: (Psal. V)]*, etc.

VERS. 36.-- *But the more he charged them,* etc. He who knows all things before they come to pass knew that they would preach the more; and by so charging them he willed to show to the slothful how much more diligently and how much more frequently [ferventius] they ought to preach, whom he commands to preach: since those who were forbidden could not hold their peace.
