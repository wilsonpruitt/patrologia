## CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 1.-- *And after days,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] After the threatening of the cross the glory of the resurrection is shown, whence there follows:

*And after days,* etc. That they might not fear the reproaches of the cross, who had seen with their own eyes the glory of the resurrection to come.

[0212D] *[n: (BEDA.)] And after six days.* Luke: And it came to pass, about eight days after these words, etc., *as far as* lest in the eighth time of retribution we be seized upon by an angry judge. *Into a high mountain.* Into a mountain, that he might pray, etc., *as far as* then shall they deserve to rejoice in the eternal vision of him.

*Transfigured.* He did not lose the true substance of the flesh, but showed the glory of his own resurrection to come, or of ours. Such will he appear to all the elect after the judgment: at the judgment, in the form of a servant, to the good and to the wicked alike, so that the impious, whom they despised, whom the Jews denied, whom the soldiers crucified, whom Pilate and Herod judged, may not be able to recognize the judge [cj: *nequeant*; read *queant*, "may be able"].

[0213A]VERS. 2.-- *And his garments.* The garments of the Lord are the saints. Whence: *You who have been baptized in Christ have put on Christ [n: (Gal. III)]*. These, while the Lord remained in the world, seemed despised; but when he sought the mountain they shine again with a new whiteness. *Because now indeed we are the sons of God, but it hath not yet appeared what we shall be. When therefore he shall appear, we shall be like to him, because we shall see him as he is [n: (I Joan. III)]*. Whence it is subjoined.

VERS. 3.-- *And there appeared,* etc. Moses, a holy man dead, and Elias, caught up alive into heaven, seen in majesty with the Lord (as Luke says), signify the glory of all the saints to come in him: who at the time of judgment shall be found either alive in the flesh or dead.

*Elias with. [n: (BEDA.)]* The lawgiver and the chief of the prophets appear: they speak with the Lord living in the flesh, [0213B] showing that he is the one whom the law and the prophets promised; not in the lowest places, but on the mountain with him, because they who pass beyond earthly desires in mind perceive that the majesty of Holy Scripture, which has been fulfilled in the Lord, is unfolded.

VERS. 4.-- *Rabbi.* Although astonished, etc., *as far as* having one tabernacle, that is, the Church of God. *It is good.* Much better therefore is it to be among the choirs of the saints, and to enjoy the vision of the Godhead: if the humanity of Christ transfigured so delights, and the company of two shown for a moment.

VERS. 6.-- *A cloud overshadowing.* Whence, because he sought a material tabernacle, he received the shade of a cloud, that he might learn that in the resurrection the saints are to be protected, not by the covering of houses, but by the Holy Spirit. [0213C] *This is.* As if he said: This is truly he whom that Moses promised you, etc., *as far as* and it shall be enlightened by the same grace, and shall be protected for ever.

VERS. 7.-- *And immediately. [n: (BEDA.)]* When the voice came over the Son of man, he alone was found. Because when he shall have manifested himself to the elect, God will be all in all: nay rather, he himself with his own, one Christ throughout all, that is, the head with the body, shall shine forth; whence: *No man hath ascended into heaven, but he that descended from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven [n: (Joan. III)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *He charged them.* He wills not that the glory of the kingdom to come and of the triumph which he had shown on the mountain be publicly preached: lest both it should be past belief for its greatness, and, after so great a glory, the cross that followed should make a scandal among untaught minds.

[0213D]VERS. 9.-- *They kept the word. [n: (HIER.)]* That is, when death shall have been swallowed up in victory, the former things shall not be in memory, when the Lord shall have taken away the filth of the daughter of Sion, wiping away every tear from the eyes of the saints.

VERS. 10.-- *What then.* The scribes hand down, according to Malachy, that Elias shall come before the coming of the Saviour, and shall bring back the hearts of the fathers to their fathers, and shall restore all things to their ancient state. The disciples therefore judge this to be the glory of his coming which they had seen on the mountain, and they say: If thou hast already come in glory, why does thy forerunner not appear? especially because they see that Elias has withdrawn.

VERS. 11.-- *He shall restore,* etc. That is, *he shall turn the heart [0214A] of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to the fathers.* For he shall restore to death what he owes and what by living long he has withheld; whence he adds: *How it is written.* As if he said: As the prophets wrote in many ways of the passion of Christ, so also Elias is to suffer many things. He shall therefore restore all things. First, the hearts of the men of that time, to believe in Christ and to resist Antichrist. Then, he shall lay down his life for Christ.

VERS. 13.-- *A great multitude. [n: (HIER.)]* There is no rest under the sun: envy always slays the little ones, lightnings strike the great mountains; some learning with faith, others envying with pride, come to the Church. *Questioning. [n: (BEDA.)]* What they question about the Evangelist does not say, but it may be understood that a question had been raised about this: why the disciples could not free the demoniac [0214B] who was in their midst: for this can be gathered from what follows.

VERS. 15.-- *And he asked. [n: (ID.)]* Note that the places agree with the things. On the mountain he prays, is transformed, opens to the disciples the secrets of his majesty, in the low place he is received by the crowd, and is beaten upon by the weeping of the wretched. Above he unlocks to the disciples the mysteries of the kingdom, below he upbraids the crowds with the sins of unbelief. Above he lays open the voice of the Father to those who were able to hear, below he drives out evil spirits. Now, according to the quality of their merits, he ceases not to ascend for some and to descend for others. *And he asked. [n: (HIER.)]* He knows, and he asks, that confession may bring forth salvation, and that the murmur of our heart may first be loosed in words, as it is: Tell thy iniquities first, that thou mayest be justified.

VERS. 17.-- *And I spoke to the disciples.* Secretly he turns the blame [0214C] aside upon the disciples, whereas often the cure is hindered by the fault, not of those who heal, but of those to be healed.

VERS. 18.-- *O generation.* He is angry not with the man, but with the vice. He says this not out of weariness of feeling, but as a physician to a sick man who acts against his orders. And through one he convicts the Jews of unbelief.

VERS. 19.-- *Immediately the spirit.* Because while, after sin, we will to be converted to the Lord, we are the more assailed by the devil, that he may either shake out our virtue, or avenge his own expulsion.

VERS. 20.-- *From his infancy. [n: (HIER.)]* It signifies the Gentile people, in whom from birth the worship of idols grew, so that it foolishly sacrificed its own sons to devils; whence there follows:

VERS. 21.-- *And oftentimes he,* etc. For some [0214D] worshipped fire, others water. *Help. [n: (HIER.)]* Our belief, like a beaked tongue, is weak, unless it lean upon the help of God. Faith with tears obtains the prayers it desired, as it is: *Be it done to thee according to thy faith [n: (Matth. XV)]*.

VERS. 25.-- *Tearing him,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] The devil tears those who draw near to salvation, which is his beloved food, whom he desires to drag into his own belly by terrors and by losses, as in Job: *He was made,* etc. [n: (ISID., RAB.)] etc. For to the healed it is said: *For you are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God [n: (Col. III)]*. Whence the infirmity of Christians is not death, but the likeness of death.

VERS. 28.-- *This kind by nothing [sic: nonullo]. [n: (HIER.)]* Folly [0215A] belongs to the luxury of the flesh, and is healed by fasting. Anger and sloth are driven off by prayer. The medicine of each wound must be applied to it. What heals the heel does not heal the eye; by fasting the passions of the body, by prayer the plagues of the mind, are healed.

VERS. 30.-- *They shall kill him,* etc. He mingles sad things with prosperous, that when they come they may not be borne with unprepared minds. If it saddens them that he is to be killed, it ought to gladden them that on the third day he is to rise again.

VERS. 31.-- *They knew not.* Not so much from slowness of wit as from love: because they had known him to be true God, they could not believe that he would die. And because they were accustomed to speak to them in figures, shuddering at his death, they thought that in these things also he spoke figuratively.

VERS. 32.-- *Capharnaum. [n: (HIER.)]* The village of consolation: [0215B] it agrees with the foregoing sentence: *Being slain, on the third day he shall rise again [n: (Joan. XII)]*. The grain of wheat dies, that it may be gathered more manifoldly: if it does not die, it remains alone. *What in the way,* etc. In the way they were treating of the primacy: a treating suited to the place. For a princedom is entered by force, and so is forsaken, and while it is held it slips away: and it is uncertain in what lodging, that is, on what day, it shall be ended; whence he says: *He that will be first,* etc.

VERS. 33.-- *They had disputed among themselves. [n: (BEDA.)]* Hence the dispute seems to have arisen, because they had seen Peter and James and John led apart into the mountain, and that some secret had there been entrusted to them; and to Peter also, as Matthew says, the keys of the kingdom of heaven promised, and the Church to be built upon the rock of faith, from which he himself received his name. They thought therefore [0215C] either that those three were set before the rest, or that Peter was set before them all.

VERS. 34.-- *And sitting down,* etc. They as they went were disputing about the primacy, and he, sitting, teaches humility. For princes toil, the humble are at rest. He wills to heal the desire of glory by humility, and first admonishes by a simple command, then by the example of a child's innocence.

VERS. 35.-- *Having embraced him.* He signifies that the humble are worthy of his embrace, who can rightly glory and say: *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me [n: (Cant. II)]*.

VERS. 36.-- *Whosoever shall receive one.* Either he shows simply that the poor of Christ are to be received by those who would be greater, for [0215D] his honour. Or he persuades them to be themselves little ones in malice, that they may be simple as little ones. *And whosoever.* Because he was teaching that he is received in the children, as the head in the members, lest this only should be thought to be what appeared, he added: *And whosoever shall receive me,* etc.

VERS. 37.-- *He answered him.* Because he had said: *Whosoever shall receive one of such children, receiveth me,* [var: Vulg. Mark 9:36 reads *receperit in nomine meo*, "shall receive in my name"] John understands that they who do not walk sincerely do not receive them in his name; whence he says: *Master, we saw a certain man.* As if he said: This man ought not to be received in thy name. *And we forbade him.* He thought that one who does not use the office was to be shut out from the benefit; but he is taught that no one is to be barred from the good which he has in [0216A] part, but rather to be urged on to that which he has not: whence: *Forbid him not.*

VERS. 38.-- *Forbid him not. [n: (BEDA.)]* Hence the Apostle: *But whether by occasion or by truth Christ be preached,* etc., *as far as* we ought to detest and to forbid.

VERS. 41.-- *And whosoever shall scandalize.* Although this be general, yet according to the sequence of the discourse it can be said against the apostles, who were disputing about the primacy and could destroy by their own example those whom they were calling to the faith. [n: (BEDA.)] Note that in our good work the scandal of a neighbour is sometimes to be guarded against, sometimes to be counted as nothing: in so far as we can without sin, we ought to avoid the scandal of our neighbours. But if scandal is taken for the sake of truth, [0216B] it is more useful that it be suffered to arise than that the truth be forsaken.

*An ass's millstone. [n: (HIER.)]* According to the custom of the province, etc., *as far as* a more tolerable punishment of hell would in any case torment him.

VERS. 42.-- *And if it shall scandalize thee.* Because above he taught that we should not scandalize those who believe in him, he admonishes how much we ought to beware of those who scandalize. Σκάνδαλον in Greek is a stumbling-block, or a fall, or the striking of the foot. Or, as others say, a sharp stone. He therefore scandalizes his brother who by word or by deed affords him occasion of falling. *Thy hand.* A friend, of whose help and counsel we have need daily. But if he should be willing to wound us in the cause of the soul, he must be shut out from our fellowship, lest, if we will to have part with a lost man in this life, [0216C] we perish together with him.

*Than having two hands. [n: (HIER.)]* The two hands of princedom are humility and pride. Cut off pride; hold a humble princedom.
