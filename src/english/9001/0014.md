## CHAPTER IX. (cont.)

VERS. 43.-- *Where the worm. [n: (BEDA.)]* The stench of worms comes of the corruption of flesh and of blood; and therefore fresh meat is seasoned with salt, so that, the bloody humour being dried out, there can be no worm. Flesh therefore and blood breed worms, because carnal delight, which the seasoning of continence does not withstand, begets eternal punishment for the lustful. We ought therefore to chastise the body with the salt of continence, and the mind with the seasoning of wisdom, from the stain of error and of vices.

VERS. 48.-- *For every one shall be salted with fire.* Because for the third time he made mention of fire and of the worm, he shows how we may be able to avoid [0216D] both. *For every one with fire,* it is wondrously said: for what is salted with salt keeps off the rottenness of the worm. But what is salted with fire, that is, in fire, when salt has been sprinkled upon it, consumes the flesh as well. These things were done according to the law in the victims that were burned upon the altar, where it was commanded that salt be offered in every victim and sacrifice. *Every victim shall be salted with salt.* For he is truly a victim of the Lord who [offers] body and soul, etc., *as far as* to deny those dear and near to us. *Every victim shall be salted with salt. [n: (HIER.)]* The victim is the human race, which here is salted with the salt of wisdom or of reason, while the corruption of blood, the keeper of rottenness and the mother of worms, is here consumed, and afterwards shall be tried by the fire of purgatory.

[0217A]VERS. 49.-- *But if the salt,* etc. That is, if a man once refreshed with the seasonings of truth return to apostasy: by what other teacher is he corrected, who has spat out the wisdom he tasted, either terrified by adversities or enticed by prosperity? Whence: *Who shall heal a charmer struck by a serpent? [n: (Eccli. XII.)]* [var: Vulg. Eccli. 12:13 reads *miserebitur*, "shall have pity on"] In this it is believed that Judas and his fellows are signified, who, corrupted by love of money, did not hesitate both to lose his apostleship and to betray the Lord. But because there are many whom, while greater knowledge lifts them up, it severs from the fellowship of others, and the more they savour, the more they lose their savour of the virtue of concord. He subjoins: *Have salt in you, and peace. [n: (BEDA.)]* To have salt without peace is not the gift of virtue, but a proof of damnation. For the better a man savours, the worse he offends.
