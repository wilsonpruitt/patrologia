## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0217B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And rising up from thence.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus far the things which he did and taught in Galilee: from here he relates what he did in Judea, and taught, and suffered. ⟦s3⟧ And first the things beyond the Jordan toward the east: then those on this side of the Jordan, when he came to Jericho, and to Bethany, and to Jerusalem. ⟦s4⟧ For although the whole province of the Jews is generally called Judea, to distinguish it from the other nations, yet more particularly the southern tract is called Judea, to distinguish it from Galilee, from the Decapolis, and from the other regions in the same province.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The Pharisees asked.* ⟦s2⟧ Great is the distance between the crowds and the Pharisees. ⟦s3⟧ These come together that they may be taught, and that the sick may be healed, as Matthew [0217C] plainly says: those, that by tempting they may deceive. ⟦s4⟧ For the devotion of piety brings these: the goad of spite leads those. ⟦s5⟧ *Whether it is lawful for a man to put away his wife. [n: (BEDA.)]* He tempted him with a horned syllogism, etc., *as far as* but it was granted because of the necessity of those who sin.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For the hardness of heart. [n: (ID.)]* Is God contrary to himself, etc., *as far as* that the bond of wedlock should persevere through hatreds and murders.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And they shall be,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The reward of marriage is that of two there be made one flesh. ⟦s3⟧ Chastity joined to the spirit is made one spirit.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Let not man put asunder.* ⟦s2⟧ By the desire of a second wife. ⟦s3⟧ God separates: he who joins, when by the consent of both, for the service of God (in that the time is short), they so have wives as though not having them.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0217D]VERS. 10.-- *And in the house,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] It was not the disciples who asked first, etc., *as far as* that bread may strengthen the heart of man, as it is: *If any man will come after me [n: (Luc. IX)]*, etc.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Whosoever shall put away his wife. [n: (BEDA.)]* Matthew more fully: *Whosoever shall put away his wife, save for fornication, and shall marry another, committeth adultery [n: (Matth. V)]*. ⟦s2⟧ There is therefore one carnal cause only, fornication: one spiritual, the fear of God, that a wife be put away, as many are read to have done for religion's sake. ⟦s3⟧ But no cause is prescribed by the law of God, that another be married while she lives who has been left.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *But the disciples rebuked them that brought them.* ⟦s2⟧ Not because they were unwilling that these should be blessed by the Saviour's [0218A] hand and voice, but, not yet having full faith, they thought that after the manner of men he was wearied by the importunity of those who brought them.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *For of such is the kingdom of God.* ⟦s2⟧ He says "of such" with meaning, not "of these." ⟦s3⟧ As if he said: Manners reign, not age. ⟦s4⟧ The kingdom of heaven is promised to those who have the like simplicity and innocence, to whom that word to the apostles is fitting: *Be not made little ones in understanding, but in malice be little ones [n: (I Cor. XIV)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 14:20 *nolite pueri effici sensibus*, "be not children in understanding"].

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom.* ⟦s2⟧ He who does not persevere in anger, who when hurt does not remember it, who seeing a fair woman does not covet, who has not one thing in his mouth and another in his heart. ⟦s3⟧ As if he said: If you shall not be such, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven. ⟦s4⟧ *The kingdom of God.* ⟦s5⟧ That is, [0218B] we are bidden to receive the teaching of the Gospel as little ones do, that is, as a little one does not contradict his teachers, does not frame arguments against them, but faithfully receives, obeys with fear and is quiet: so we also, obeying simply and without any contradiction, ought to receive the words of the Lord.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *A certain man running up, on bended knee. [n: (BEDA.)]* He had heard, I believe, from the Lord that only those who wish to be like little ones are worthy of entrance into the heavenly kingdom, and therefore he desires to be more certain, not through parables but openly: by what merits of works he might be able to obtain eternal life.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Why callest thou me good. [n: (ID.)]* Because he had called him good master, etc., *as far as* but he testifies that apart from God there is no good.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0218C]VERS. 19.-- *Thou shalt not commit adultery. [n: (ID.)]* This is the chastity of childlike innocence, which is set before us to be imitated, if we would enter the kingdom of God. ⟦s2⟧ And it is to be well noted that the justice of the law, kept in its own time, conferred not only temporal goods but also eternal life.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Master. [n: (ID.)]* He is not to be thought [to have asked] with the intent of one tempting, etc., *as far as* whence he adds: *Go, sell whatsoever thou hast, and give to the poor [n: (Matth. V)]*, etc.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And come, follow me. [n: (ID.)]* As if he said: After riches have been despised, imitate the Saviour, that is, evils being left behind, do good things. ⟦s2⟧ A money-bag is more easily despised than concupiscence or pleasure.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *How hardly. [n: (ID.)]* They who apply themselves to the multiplying of riches, etc., *as far as* they may be able to enter [0218D] the kingdom of God.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *It is easier for a camel.* ⟦s2⟧ How then in the Gospel did Matthew and Zacchaeus, and Joseph, and in the Old Testament very many rich men enter in? ⟦s3⟧ Perhaps because they held riches for nothing, or learned to despise them altogether, whence David: *I am alone and poor [n: (Psal. XXIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And the same: *If riches abound, set not your heart upon them [n: (Ibid. LXI)]*. ⟦s5⟧ He does not say, Do not receive them.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *And who can be saved.* ⟦s2⟧ Since there are more poor than rich, they would not have said this unless they had understood in the number of the rich all who would wish to obtain riches.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *With God.* ⟦s2⟧ Who is able to turn men from the greed of earthly things [0219A] to the love of eternal things, and to make the proud humble.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Behold, we have left all.* ⟦s2⟧ Great confidence: he was a fisherman, he was not rich, he sought his living by hand and craft, and yet he says confidently: *Behold, we have forsaken all things. ⟦s3⟧ And we have followed.* ⟦s4⟧ Because it is not enough so to leave, he joins that which is perfect, *and we have followed thee.* ⟦s5⟧ As if he said: We have done what thou didst command: what reward therefore wilt thou give?

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *A hundredfold. [n: (BEDA.)]* On this occasion certain men lay down as doctrine the Jewish fable of the thousand years after the resurrection of the just, etc., *as far as* they enjoy the most true love of the heavenly fatherland, which is signified by the right hand, and of all the elect alike.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *But many.* ⟦s2⟧ Because many begin but do not finish, a terrible sentence is subjoined: [0219B] *But many,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The thief on the cross, a confessor on the same day on which he was crucified for his own sins, by the grace of faith rejoices with Christ in paradise. ⟦s4⟧ Daily also we see many laymen excel rather by the merits of their life, and others, fervent from their earliest age in spiritual study, grow torpid at the last in idleness.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *And they were astonished.* ⟦s2⟧ For this reason, namely, because they remembered that he had foretold that he should suffer many things from the chief priests and the scribes, and be slain. ⟦s3⟧ They feared therefore lest either they themselves should be slain with him, or he, in whose life and teaching they rejoiced, should be slain.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they shall take. [n: (BEDA.)]* Foreseeing that the minds of the disciples would be troubled by his passion, etc., *as far as* he approaches the place of death as one undaunted.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0219C]VERS. 35.-- *And they come.* ⟦s2⟧ Having heard of the resurrection, forgetful of the second coming, they think that he will reign after the manner of men. ⟦s3⟧ Matthew says that the mother made the request, but Mark says that they themselves did, wishing to lay open the desire of those by whose counsel the mother asked.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *Or with the baptism.* ⟦s2⟧ The same as the cup. ⟦s3⟧ Whence elsewhere he says of his passion: *I have a baptism wherewith to be baptized: and how am I straitened until it be accomplished.*

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *The cup indeed. [n: (BED.)]* James is beheaded by Herod, John is cast into a cauldron of boiling oil. ⟦s2⟧ Thence the athlete of Christ went forth to the crown of martyrdom, and was at once banished to Patmos. ⟦s3⟧ And so he both was not wanting to martyrdom in mind, and drank the cup of confession which the three children drank in the furnace, although the persecutor did not shed his blood.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0219D]VERS. 40.-- *But to sit.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say, Do you wish that I should grant this to you? ⟦s3⟧ But the kingdom of heaven is not the giver's but the receiver's. ⟦s4⟧ *For there is no respect of persons with God [n: (Act. X)]*, but whosoever shall be worthy shall receive that which is prepared not for the person but for the life. ⟦s5⟧ *To you.* ⟦s6⟧ Still proud, as if he said: If you wish to receive, be not what you are: it is prepared for others, and be you others, and it is prepared for you, which is, be humbled first, you who already wish to be exalted.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *They were indignant.* ⟦s2⟧ Not about the mother, but about the sons, who, not knowing their own measure, burned with immoderate greed.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *But Jesus calling them.* ⟦s2⟧ A humble master, and kind and gentle, he neither reproves the two for immoderate greed [0220A] nor rebukes the ten for indignation and spite, but teaches that he is the greater who shall have been the less, and lord who is servant. ⟦s3⟧ In vain therefore do either those sigh after greater things or these grieve over their desire, since not power but humility leads to the summit of the virtues. ⟦s4⟧ Lastly he sets before his own an example, that if they made light of words, they might blush at deeds. ⟦s5⟧ *For the Son of man also, etc.*

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 46.-- *And they come to Jericho. [n: (HIER.)]* Jericho is moon or anathema, which agrees with him drawing near to the passion. ⟦s2⟧ The failing of Christ's flesh is the preparation of the heavenly Jerusalem, whence, forsaking Jericho, they draw near to Jerusalem, that is, to the vision of peace. ⟦s3⟧ *And as he went forth. [n: (BEDA.)]* Because, the Lord ascending into the heavens and the faithful following, etc., *as far as* and he scattered them into the world for [0220B] the enlightening of all peoples. ⟦s4⟧ *Blind Bartimaeus. [n: (HIER.)]* The blindness of the Jews, which shall be enlightened at the end at the coming of Elias and Enoch.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *Who when he had heard.* ⟦s2⟧ The people of the Gentiles, having heard the fame of Christ, of whom it sought to be made partaker, many contradicted, first the Jews and afterwards the Gentiles also, lest the world, which was to be enlightened and healed, should call upon Christ, nor yet could the fury of those who assailed it hinder those foreordained to eternal life.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Son of David. [n: (HIER.)]* Through the merits of the patriarchs the Jewish people is enlightened, to whom God is present, merciful and compassionate, enlightening the blind and raising up them that are cast down.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 48.-- *But he much the more.* ⟦s2⟧ As the war of vices grows heavier, hands must be lifted up to the stone of help [0220C] with a cry, that is, to Jesus.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIER.)] A fitting order of salvation, etc., *as far as* beholding the apostles on high.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 49.-- *And they call the blind man. [n: (BEDA.)]* Allegorically, the Lord calls the blind man crying out, while to the people of the Gentiles, desiring the knowledge of the truth, he commits the word of faith by holy preachers. ⟦s2⟧ These, calling the blind man, bid him be of better courage and rise and come to him, when by preaching they command him to have hope of salvation, to rise from the torpor of vices, and to gird himself to those pursuits of the virtues by which he may deserve to be enlightened, as if he said: *Come ye to him and be enlightened [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *And, rise thou that sleepest [n: (Ephes. V)]*, etc.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 50.-- *Who, casting away his garment.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, [0220D] the cares of the world being cast off, with the step of his mind unhindered he hastens to the giver of light. ⟦s3⟧ *Leaping up.* ⟦s4⟧ He rewards a ready will with the fulfilling of its desire, whence: *Whatsoever you shall ask in prayer, believing, you shall receive.*

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 51.-- *Rabboni, and let me see.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not seek gold, but light. ⟦s3⟧ For a blind man can have riches, but he does not see what he has. ⟦s4⟧ By his example let us seek not false riches, but the light which we can see with the angels alone, to which the way is faith. ⟦s5⟧ Whence it is said to the blind man when enlightened: *Go, thy faith hath made thee whole.*

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 52.-- *He saw and followed. [n: (BEDA.)]* He sees and follows who does that which he well understands. ⟦s2⟧ To follow is to imitate. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *If any man minister to me, let him follow me [n: (Joan. XII)]*.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0221A] *In the way.* ⟦s2⟧ Let us consider by what way he walks, and let us follow through humility, through labours. ⟦s3⟧ The way which says: *I am the way, the truth and the life [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ This is the narrow way which leads to the steep places, of Jerusalem and of Bethany to the mount of Olives, which is the mount of light and of consolation.
