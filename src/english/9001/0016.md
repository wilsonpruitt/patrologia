## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- *Of Bethany. [n: (BEDA.)]* Bethany is a village or city on the side of Mount Olivet, etc., *as far as* two, for the knowledge of the truth and the cleanness of the work. *Two of the disciples. [n: (HIER.)]* Two by two they are called, two by two they are sent, etc., *as far as* they may know the Lord between the two cherubim, singing with the spirit, with the mind.

VERS. 2.-- *And straightway. [n: (BEDA.)]* The preachers, entering the world, found the people of the nations, etc., [0221B] *as far as* but that which is clean and holy belongs to God alone.

VERS. 4.-- *A colt tied. [n: (HIER.)]* The colt tied and untamed, which they loose and tame, is the Gentile people. Before the door of faith it stands with the bonds of its own sins where two ways meet: in the freedom of choice it wavers between death and life.

VERS. 6.-- *And they let them go. [n: (BEDA.)]* They who at the loosing spoke against it, etc., *as far as* they cover them with that whereby, with Christ as their rider, they may be made worthy.

VERS. 7.-- *And he sat. [n: (HIER.)]* He began to reign, that sin might not reign in mortal or in wanton flesh: but justice and peace and joy in the Holy Ghost.

VERS. 8.-- *But many spread their garments.* Because many martyrs, stripping themselves of the covering of their own flesh, prepare a way with their blood for the simpler sort, [0221C] that these may go unhindered to the city on high, to which Jesus leads.

*[n: (HIER.)] Many,* etc. The feet are the outermost members, which the Apostle appointed for the joining together, which, although they are not the back on which the Lord sat, yet are instructed along with the soldiers by John.

*And others. [n: (ID.)]* The just shall flourish as the palm tree, narrow in the roots, broad in flowers and fruits: since they are the good odour of Christ, and they strew the way of the commandments of God with good report. [n: (BEDA.)] And others cut down leaves or branches from the trees, who in the teaching of the truth cull out the words and sentences of the Fathers from their books. And these they lay down in the way of God, by humble preaching, before the mind of the hearer as he comes.

VERS. 9.-- *And they that went before.* The Jewish [0221D] people went before, the Gentile followed. And because all who are or have been faithful believed and believe in Christ, both they who go before and they who follow cry hosanna: which in Latin is rendered, Save us: for from him also all the former sought salvation.

VERS. 10.-- *Blessed is he that cometh.* Since there is one faith, one hope of all. They looked for him and believed that he would come, and we believe that he has come.

[n: (BEDA.)] By this which is joined to it: hosanna in the highest: which is salvation, it signifies that the coming of Christ is the salvation not of men only, but of the whole world, joining earthly things to heavenly, that *to him every knee should bow, of those that are in heaven, on earth and under the earth [n: (Philip. II)]*.

[n: (HIER.)] This is in accord with Gabriel: who says: *He shall be [0222A] great, and shall be called the Son of the Most High,* etc., *as far as* through the word itself of entreaty.

VERS. 11.-- *And he entered in. [n: (ID.)]* Now, the passion drawing near, he wills to be in the place appointed for the passion before the ages, etc., *as far as* he has already begun the sacrament of the blessed immolation.

*Into the temple.* He gave us an example, that wheresoever we should come, we should first turn aside to the house of prayer if there be one there: and when by the practice of prayer we have commended ourselves to God, we should withdraw to do those things for which we came. *And having looked about,* etc. He did this not once, but through all the five days. By day he taught in the temple, by night, going out, he abode on Mount Olivet, as Luke says. For by teaching he diligently bestowed on the unbelieving the office of correction, by abiding [0222B] among the faithful he graciously showed forth the grace of kindness.

*And when he had seen. [n: (BEDA.)]* As he speaks by parables, so he does, etc., *as far as* by working miracles, and he found none: therefore he condemned it.

VERS. 14.-- *Now no more. [n: (ID.)]* Thou also, if thou wilt not hear in the judgment from Christ, *Depart from me, ye evil ones, into everlasting fire, for I was hungry and you gave not to me, to eat [n: (Matth. XXV)]* [var: Vulg. Matt. 25:41 *maledicti*, "ye cursed"], beware to be a barren tree, but rather offer to Christ, poor and hungry, the fruit of piety which he lacks.

VERS. 15.-- *He began to cast out.* In the thing itself he shows what he did by a figure in the fig tree. For the fig tree did not sin if before the time it had no fruit, but the priests did.

*Them that sold. [n: (BEDA.)]* Those things, namely, which they who came from [0222C] afar bought from the natives to be offered, etc., *as far as* they are always deprived of the priesthood.

VERS. 16.-- *And he suffered not.* He sends before an example of the judgment to come, when he drives all the reprobate from the Church: and lest they enter any more to trouble her, he restrains them with an everlasting scourging.

*But you have made.* For to this end were they in the temple, that they might either bodily persecute those who did not give, or spiritually slay those who did give. The temple and house of God is the mind and conscience of the faithful, which in the hurting of a neighbour brings forth perverse thoughts, as robbers sit in a den, and slay those who walk in simplicity: and so the mind is now not the house of God, but a den of thieves.

VERS. 20.-- *Withered away. [n: (BEDA.)]* The fig tree was withered from [0222D] the roots, that it might be shown that the impious nation was not to be chastised for a time, or in part, by the inroads of foreigners, and to be set free through penance, as has often been done, but to be smitten with an entire and everlasting damnation. Otherwise. It was withered from the roots, that it might be shown to be utterly forsaken not only of human favour from without, but of divine favour within: for it lost both life in heaven and country on earth.

VERS. 21.-- *Peter remembering.* Peter acknowledges the tree withered, and the root cut off, to which succeeds the most beautiful fruitful olive, so called by the Lord. Whence there follows: *Amen, I say to you,* etc.

VERS. 23.-- *Whosoever shall say. [n: (BEDA.)]* Certain men are wont to say that our own had not yet full faith, [0223A] etc., *as far as* by how much he groans that he has been cast out from the hurting of the godly. [n: (HIER.)] But this was done: when the apostles said: *Worthily are we transferred to the Gentiles, because you judge yourselves unworthy [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. [n: (BEDA.)] The distinction of those who pray is to be noted, etc., *as far as* if yet they first forgive those who sin against them.

VERS. 25.-- *And when you shall stand. [n: (HIER.)]* Mark after his manner comprises the seven petitions of the Lord's Prayer in one prayer. For he to whom all things are forgiven, what more shall he ask, save that he persevere in that which he has obtained?

VERS. 28.-- *By what authority.* They doubt of the power of God, and wish it to be understood underneath that what he does is the devil's.

VERS. 29.-- *I will ask.* He could have confuted the calumny of the tempters by an open answer: but he prudently [0223B] asks, that they may be condemned either by their own silence or by their own sentence.

VERS. 31.-- *If we shall say. [n: (HIER.)]* By the lamp of the world they are darkened, whence he says: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ: his enemies I will clothe with confusion [n: (Psal. CXXXI)]*.

*Why then,* etc. As if he said: He whom you confess to have had his prophecy from heaven bore witness to me, and from him you have heard by what authority I do these things.

VERS. 32.-- *If we shall say.* Whichever of these they answer: they see that they will fall into a snare, they fear stoning: but more the confession of the truth.

VERS. 33.-- *Neither do I tell you. [n: (BED.)]* For two causes the knowledge of the truth is hidden from those who seek it, when either he to whom he seeks understands too little, or by hatred or by contempt of the truth he is unworthy that it should be opened to him. [0223C] For the one it is said: *I have yet many things to say to you, which you cannot bear now.* [var: Vulg. Jo. 16:12 *sed non potestis portare modo*, "but you cannot bear them now"] For the other, *Give not that which is holy to dogs.*
