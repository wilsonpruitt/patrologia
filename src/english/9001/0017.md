## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 1.-- *A vat,* that is, a winepress or an altar, or those winepresses by whose title certain psalms are inscribed. *Having gone into a far country.* Giving free choice of working, not by a change of place, because God is everywhere, and fills all things.

VERS. 6.-- *Because they will reverence.* He said this not in ignorance, because he knows all things, but God is always said to be in doubt, that free will may be reserved to man.

[n: (BEDA.)] We put the question to Arius and Eunomius, behold, the father is said to be ignorant, and he tempers his sentence, and, so far as is in us, he is said to have lied. Whatever [0223D] they shall answer for the father, let them understand the same for the Son, who says that he does not know the day of the consummation.

VERS. 7.-- *But the husbandmen. [n: (ID.)]* He proves that the princes of the Jews crucified Christ not through ignorance, etc., *as far as* and they strove to prefer their own justice which is of the law.

VERS. 8.-- *And they cast him out.* He marks their obstinacy: who, not even with the Lord crucified and raised again, were willing to believe at the preaching of the apostles, but cast him forth like a worthless corpse: because, so far as in them lay, shutting him out from their own borders they gave him to the Gentiles to be received.

[n: (BEDA.)] To whichever of the faithful the mystery of baptism, etc., *as far as* he will rejoice to crucify the Lord and to hold him up for a show.

[0224A]VERS. 10.-- *The stone which they rejected. [n: (HIER.)]* The rejected stone, which the corner bore, joining at the supper the lamb with the bread, ending the Old and beginning the New Testament, this shows forth wonders in our eyes.

VERS. 13.-- *And they send to him. [n: (BEDA.)]* They feared the crowd, and what they could not do of themselves, etc., *as far as* they would rather lose both the kingdom and all things than be tributaries.

VERS. 14.-- *Master, we know. [n: (ID.)]* The smooth and deceitful question provokes him who answers to this, that he should fear God rather than Caesar, and should say that tributes ought not to be paid, so that the Herodians, hearing it, might hold him the author of sedition against the Romans.

VERS. 15.-- *Who knowing their craftiness.* Let those who think that the Saviour's questioning is ignorance and not dispensation [0224B] learn from this place that he could know whose image it was. But he asks, that he may fittingly answer their speech. *A penny.* The denarius is a kind of coin which is reckoned for ten coins, and it has the image of Caesar.

VERS. 17.-- *Render therefore the things that are Caesar's.* This he himself did: paying tribute for himself and for Peter. *The things that are God's, to God.* This also he did, fulfilling the will of the Father. Otherwise: *Render the things that are Caesar's to Caesar.* The impress of his own image. *The things that are God's, to God.* The soul enlightened by the light of his countenance, Whence: *The light of thy countenance is signed upon us [n: (Psal. IV)]*, etc.

VERS. 18.-- *The Sadducees,* etc. There were two heresies among the Jews, that of the Pharisees and that of the Sadducees. The Pharisees set forth the justice of traditions and observances (which they [0224C] call δευτερώσεις): whence they are also called the divided, apart from the people. But the Sadducees, that is, the just, denied the resurrection both of the body and of the soul.

*The Sadducees,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] They who deny that there is a resurrection of bodies, or who do not believe it, judging that souls perish together with the bodies; rightly do they frame a fable of this kind, which may convict of raving those who assert the resurrection of bodies. But this could also have befallen at some time in their own nation.

VERS. 20.-- *Seven therefore,* etc. [n: (ID.)] They set against him the baseness of a fable, that they may deny the resurrection. But mystically, the seven brothers dead without children agree with all the reprobate: who through their whole life (which is rolled round in seven days) are barren of good work, and when these die one by one, at the last the very worldly [0224D] conversation dies also, that is, passes away, like a barren wife, whom they wore out without the fruit of a good work. HIER. The barren woman, leaving no seed from the seven brothers, etc., *as far as* because *the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom.*

VERS. 25.-- *Neither shall they marry, neither,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] The Latin usage does not answer to the Greek idiom: for it is properly of women that marrying is said: and of men, the taking of a wife. But let us understand it as simply said, *neither shall they marry*, the men, *neither shall they be married*, the women.

VERS. 26.-- *I am the God,* etc. [n: (BED.)] Although he could bring forward many plainer testimonies concerning the resurrection, etc., *as far as* the things which, whether good or evil, they wrought together with their souls.

[0225A]VERS. 28.-- *He asked him. [n: (HIER.)]* What does this question mean, since all who are skilled in the law know it? But the commandments are ordered differently in Exodus and in Leviticus and in Deuteronomy. For our infancy is nourished from these two breasts raised upon the bosom of the bride.

*The first of all,* etc. The greatest thing, which before all we ought to set in the heart as the one foundation of piety, is this, namely, the knowledge and confession of the divine unity together with the carrying out of the divine working, which is perfected in the love of God and of the neighbour: this is *the faith which worketh by love.*

VERS. 32.-- *Well, master. [n: (BEDA.)]* The scribe shows in this answer that a weighty question had long been debated among the scribes and the Pharisees, etc., *as far as* but no one without faith and love, in which judgment this [0225B] scribe declares himself to have been.

VERS. 34.-- *Thou art not far from the kingdom of God,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] Although he had come to tempt: for ignorance is farther off than knowledge, as it is said to the Sadducees: *You err, not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God [n: (Matth. XXII)]*. [n: (BEDA.)] Matthew says that he asked by way of tempting, etc., *as far as* according to that word: *He that believeth easily is light of heart, and shall be made less [n: (Eccl. XIX)]*.

*And no man.* Because they are confuted in words, they question him no more; but, having openly seized him, they deliver him to the Roman power, whence it appears that the poisons of envy can be overcome, but hardly be quiet.

VERS. 36.-- *Sit on my right hand.* For in this he shows the weakness [cj: *infirmitatem*; read *non infirmitatem*, "not the weakness"] of the Son, but that the one works in the other. For the Son both subjects his enemies to the Father, and glorifies God the Father [0225C] upon earth.

VERS. 38.-- *Beware. [n: (BEDA.)]* Note that he does not forbid those who are of this office, etc., *as far as* we be led astray by their pretence, or be inflamed by emulation to the same.

VERS. 40.-- *Who devour the houses of widows.* As men who are to be patrons in the judgment, they do not hesitate to take money from the weak and from those troubled by the conscience of their sins: whereas it is the hand stretched out to give, and not closed to receive, that commends prayer to God: to whom that word is fitting: *Let his prayer be turned to sin.*

VERS. 41.-- *The treasury. [n: (BEDA.)]* Φυλάττειν in Greek, to keep in Latin. Gaza, in Persian, riches, Gazophylacium the place in which riches are kept, and the chest, namely, in which the offerings of the people were gathered for the uses of the temple. Whence in the book of Kings: *Joiada the high priest took [0225D] one chest, and opened a hole in the top [n: (IV Reg. XII)]*, etc., and the porches in which they were kept. Whence: *These words Jesus spoke in the treasury, teaching in the temple.*

*He beheld how.* As he said that those who seek after primacy and vainglory are to be avoided, and foretold a more grievous judgment to those who pray in pretence, so also he discerns those who offer with a just examination, that he may render to each according to the heart and the works, which he does also today in the Church.

*The rich. [n: (BEDA.)]* The rich, the Jews puffed up over the justice of the law, etc., *as far as* they surpass all the works of the proud Jews.

VERS. 42.-- *A poor widow. [n: (HIER.)]* This poor little woman signifies me and such as me, who cast in what I can [0226A] and desire what I cannot to expound to you. God desires not how much, but out of how much. Every one can offer a farthing. This is the twist of bread in Leviticus, which is a ready will. A farthing, because it consists of three, in thought, in word and in deed.

*A farthing.* Reckoners call a farthing the fourth part of any thing, namely, of place, of time, of money. Perhaps therefore here it signifies the fourth part of a sicle, that is, five oboli.

VERS. 44.-- *For all. [n: (BEDA.)]* The Jew casts into the gifts out of that which abounds to him, etc., *as far as My God, his mercy shall prevent me [n: (Psal. LVIII)]*.
