## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And as he was going out. [n: (BEDA.)]* They shall withdraw from the temple, the Lord, etc., *as far as* it was to pay the penalty, and [0226B] the buildings to fall in ruin.

*Seest thou these,* etc. But it was divinely provided that, when the grace of the evangelical faith had been laid open throughout the world, the temple itself with its ceremonies should be taken away: lest perchance one still a little child, and a suckling in the faith, if he saw those things abide which were made by the prophets and instituted by the Lord, in admiring a worldly holy thing, should slip back little by little into Judaism. The shadow therefore is taken away, and the truth, declared throughout the world, holds the palm.

[n: (HIER.)] He foretells the calamity of the last time, that is, the destruction of the temple together with its people and its letter. Of which: *There shall not be left a stone upon a stone [n: (Luc. XXI)]*: that is, the testimonies of the prophets upon those against whom the Jews turned them back, as upon Esdras, upon Zorobabel, upon the Machabees.

[0226C]VERS. 3.-- *And when,* etc. [n: (BED.)] He abides quiet among the saints: while he detests the madness of the proud. For the Mount of Olives signifies the fruitful loftiness of the Church: that mountain brings forth not unfruitful trees, but olive groves, by which light is fed, rest is granted to the weary, weakness is loosed.

VERS. 5.-- *And answering. [n: (ID.)]* From the time of the Lord's passion, among the people of the Jews who chose the seditious robber and cast away Christ the Saviour, neither the wars of enemies nor the seditions of citizens have ceased; but the apostles are warned not to be terrified at the coming of these things, and not to forsake Jerusalem and Judea, *because the end is not at once.* For to the fortieth year was deferred the desolation of the province and the last destruction of the city and of the temple.

[0226D]VERS. 6.-- *For many shall come.* When the destruction was at hand, many came who would falsely claim that they were Christs, and that the time of liberty was already at hand. Many also in the Church, in the times of the apostles, threatened among other things that the day of the Lord was pressing on. Many antichrists came in the name of Christ, of whom the first was Simon Magus, *to whom all who were in Samaria gave ear from the least to the greatest, saying: This is the power of God which is called great: because for a long time he had bewitched them with magic arts.*

VERS. 8.-- *For there shall rise. [n: (BEDA.)]* All these things before the last and bitterest sorrows, etc., *as far as* who, fighting one against another, make the victory of the Church.

[0227A]VERS. 9.-- *But look to yourselves. [n: (BEDA.)]* Why all these things are brought in, he says *Look ye,* etc. For this alone is the greatest cause of the destruction, that after the slaying of the Lord they harassed with impious cruelty the heralds likewise of his name and of the faith, and the confessors like him.

VERS. 10.-- *And unto all nations. [n: (ID.)]* Because the Lord knew that the hearts of the disciples were to be saddened at the perdition of their own nation, he comforts them in this manner, that they may know that, the Jews being lost, they will have companions of the heavenly kingdom out of the whole world. For as the ecclesiastical histories testify, long before the destruction of Judea all the apostles were scattered through the whole world to preach the Gospel, save James the son of Zebedee, and James the brother of the Lord, who first in Judea shed their blood for the word of the Gospel.

[0227B]VERS. 14.-- *But when you shall see,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The abomination may be understood either as Antichrist, or as the image of Caesar which Pilate set in the temple, etc., *as far as* it was always obedient to the empire of the Romans.

VERS. 17.-- *But woe to them that are with child,* etc. Because, weighed down either by the burden of the womb or by the load of their children, they will not be able to flee. Whence in the book of Histories it is read that the wife of Jonathan, troubled in flight, took back her son, fallen from her bosom, lame for ever.

VERS. 21.-- *And then if any man. [n: (BED.)]* Some refer this to the Jewish captivity, when many, saying that they were Christs, drew the deceived people after them. But there was no faithful man there whom God might exhort not to follow perverse masters. All, both the besiegers and the besieged, being strangers to Christ, were hardened, [0227C] whence it is better to be taken of heretics, who, coming against the Church, falsely say that they are Christs: of whom the first was Simon Magus; the last, Antichrist.

VERS. 24.-- *But in those days,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The stars in the judgment shall be seen dark, etc., *as far as* then shall come to pass what the same prophet says elsewhere: *And the light of the moon shall be as the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, as the light of seven days [n: (Isa. III)]*.

VERS. 25.-- *Falling down.* From their light, when the seed of Abraham, to which the stars were likened, shall almost fail.

*And the powers which,* etc. [n: (BEDA.)] What wonder is it that men should tremble, when at the sight of the judgment the angelic powers tremble? Whence Job: *The pillars of heaven tremble, and dread at his beck [n: (Job. XXVI)]*. And what does [0227D] the little rod of the desert suffer, where the cedar of paradise is shaken? *They shall be moved. [n: (HIER.)]* To the wrath of vengeance, when they shall be sent by the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power, who before came down in humility like rain upon a fleece.

VERS. 27.-- *From the four winds.* From the east and the west, the south, the north, and not only from these; but from the utmost part of the earth to the utmost part of heaven, that is, from the farthest bounds of the earth straight on to its last limits, where to those who look from afar the circle of heaven seems to rest upon the earth.

VERS. 28.-- *But from the fig tree.* As if he said, as when there are tender shoots upon the fig tree, and the bud breaks out into flower, you understand the coming of summer and of spring [0228A] and of the west wind; so when you shall see these things which are written, think not that the end of the world is already at hand, but that they come as forerunners, to show that it is near.

[n: (BEDA.)] The fig tree is the Synagogue, which, because when the Lord came to her she had not the fruit of justice, etc., *as far as* and the summer of the true light and peace.

[n: (HIER.)] The parable of the fig tree, that is, the prophecy. The leaves that are born are the present words, but the summer near at hand is the day of judgment, in which every tree shall make manifest what it had within, whether dry, for the burning, or green, for the planting in Eden with the tree of life, whose leaves are unto the health of the nations, that is, the words in which it shall be said, *Come, ye blessed of my Father,* etc.

VERS. 31.-- *Heaven and earth shall pass away.* The airy heaven, from which are the birds of heaven and the clouds of heaven, whence Peter: [0228B] *But the heavens which now are, and the earth, by the same word are laid up in store and reserved unto fire [n: (II Pet. III)]*, plainly teaching that the heavens which are to perish by fire are other than those which were destroyed by water, that is, the empty and cloudy ones. For the water of the flood, which passed the tops of the mountains by fifteen cubits only, did not come beyond the borders of the air and of the ether. Ecclesiastes [n: (Cap. I)]: *One generation passeth away, and another generation cometh: but the earth standeth for ever.* So therefore heaven and earth pass away according to their image, they remain for ever according to their essence.

VERS. 32.-- *Neither the Son.* -- *For in him are all the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge hidden.* Therefore hidden, because it is not expedient for us to know, whence he says: *It is not for you to know the times or the moments which the Father [0228C] hath put in his own power [n: (Act. I)]*. In which he shows that he knows, but that it is not expedient for the apostles to know, so that, being always uncertain of the coming of the judge, they may live daily as though they were to be judged on the morrow. The Son therefore knows for himself, but not for us, that we may always be watchful, whence: *see ye, watch,* etc.

[n: (HILAR.)] The Son and the Holy Ghost, because they are not of themselves, do not know of themselves concerning that day: but the Father, because he is of himself, knows of himself.

VERS. 34.-- *And to the porter,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] To him, namely, who preaches. To whom it is said: *If thou declare not to the wicked man his iniquity, I will require his blood at thy hand [n: (Ezech. III)]*.

*Watch ye therefore.* The example of the father of the household being set before, why he keeps silence about the day of the consummation, he says: *watch ye therefore.* He who sleeps sees not bodies, but phantasms, [0228D] and when he awakes he has nothing of those things which he had seen. Such are they whom the love of the world snatches away in life, and forsakes after life.

VERS. 36.-- *And what I say to you.* Not only the rulers of the Church, but all of us are bidden to watch, keeping the doors of our hearts; lest the evil suggestion of the ancient enemy steal in, lest the Lord find us sleeping, whence Paul: *Watch, ye just, and sin not.* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 15:34 *Evigilate*, "Awake"]
