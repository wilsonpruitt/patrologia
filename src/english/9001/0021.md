## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- *And straightway,* etc. There follows: *Binding Jesus.* It was the custom of the Jews that whomsoever they had adjudged to death they should deliver bound to the judge. And it is to be noted that they had not bound him then for the first time, but as soon as he was seized by night in the garden, as John says: And thus *they led him away to Annas first [n: (Joan. XVIII)]*.

[n: (HIER.)] Here is Samson bound by Dalila. Samson [0235D] is interpreted, etc., *as far as* and he opens to us who thirst the fountain of everlasting life, that is, of his own body.

VERS. 2.-- *Art thou the king of the Jews?* When Pilate asks of no crime save whether he be the king of the Jews, the impiety of those men is convicted who found not even falsely anything to lay against him. *Thou sayest.* So he tempers his answer that he speaks the truth, and yet gives no occasion to calumny. And note that to Pilate, who bears the sentence unwillingly, he answered in some part: but to the priests and the chief men he would not answer, and judged them unworthy of his speech.

VERS. 4.-- *See,* etc. The heathen condemns Jesus, but refers the cause to the people of the Jews. *See in how many things,* etc.

[0236A]VERS. 11.-- *That rather Barabbas. [n: (BEDA.)]* Their own petition cleaves to the Jews unto this day, which with all their labour they obtained. For, the choice being given them, they chose a robber instead of Jesus, a murderer instead of the Saviour. Deservedly therefore they lost salvation and life, and gave themselves over to robberies and seditions so far that they lost their kingdom and their country, and never received again the liberty of body and of soul.

VERS. 14.-- *But Pilate.* Pilate gave many occasions of freeing the Saviour, first in comparing the robber with the just one, then in bringing forward: *What will you then that I do to the king of the Jews?* And when they had answered: *Let him be crucified,* he did not at once give in, but according to the suggestion of his wife, who had sent to him (as Matthew says): *Have thou nothing to do with that just man [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*, answering he said: [0236B] *Why, what evil hath he done?* And in saying this he acquitted Jesus. *But they the more,* etc. David: *Many dogs have encompassed me [n: (Psal. XXI)]*, etc. Isaias: *I looked that he should do judgment, and behold iniquity: and justice, and behold a cry [n: (Isa. V)]*. Jeremias: *My inheritance is become to me as a lion in the wood: it hath cried out against me [n: (Jer. XII)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *Barabbas, and delivered,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] These are the two goats, the one apompeius, that is, the emissary, is loosed and sent away with the sin of the people into the desert of hell. The other is slain as a lamb for the sins of those who are loosed. The Lord's portion is always sacrificed, the devil's portion, who is their master, is hurled unbridled into Tartarus.

*Jesus with scourges.* That he might free us from scourges; whence: [0236C] *The scourge shall not come nigh thy dwelling [n: (Psal. XC)]*: whereas before it was said: *Many are the scourges of the sinner [n: (Psal. XXXI)]*.

*[n: (BEDA.)] Jesus scourged with whips.* He was scourged by Pilate himself, whence John: *Pilate took Jesus and scourged him:* and afterwards he added: *And the soldiers platting a crown of thorns [n: (Joan. XIX)]*, etc. And he gave him over to the soldiers to be mocked. This he did for this end, that being sated with his punishments and reproaches they might not thirst further after his death.

VERS. 17.-- *And they clothe him. [n: (BEDA.)]* Matthew thus: *And stripping him, they put a scarlet cloak about him [n: (Marc. XXVII)]*. Which is the same thing. For it was given in place of the royal purple, etc., *as far as* she is clothed in purple or in scarlet, when in the triumph of the glorious martyrs [0236D] she glories.

VERS. 19.-- *And they struck,* etc. The head of Christ is God. They therefore strike the head of Christ who deny him to be God. And they who defend their error by the authority of Scripture (because writing is wont to be made with a reed) strike his head as it were with a reed. They spit in his face who spurn the presence of his grace with execrable words conceived out of the inward madness of a blind mind, and deny that he came in the flesh.

*They adored him.* As though he had falsely called himself God. But today (which is of a graver madness) certain men adore him with sure faith as true God, yet by their perverse deeds forthwith despise his words as though they were fables, [0237A] and set the promises of his kingdom after the enticements of things temporal.

*And they lead him out. [n: (HIER.)]* Here Abel is led out into the field by his brother to be slain. Here is Isaac, etc., *as far as* here is Jonas, cast out from the wood of the ship and into the sea and into the belly of the whale for three days.

VERS. 20.-- *That they might crucify him. [n: (HIER.)]* What is the very shape of the cross but the form of the four-square world? The east shines from the power, the right hand holds the north, the south stands at the left, the west is made firm beneath the feet. Whence the Apostle: *That we may know what is the breadth, the length, the height and the depth [n: (Ephes. III)]*. Birds fly to the upper air in the form of a cross, a man adores, and swims, in the form of a cross, a ship is driven through the seas by a yard-arm made like to a cross. The letter Tau shows the sign of salvation and of the cross [0237B].

VERS. 21.-- *And they forced. [n: (BEDA.)]* The Lord himself first bore his own cross, as John says: afterwards this Simon, of whom the others also speak, and this in an order of mystery altogether fitting. *For Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example that you should follow his steps [n: (Joan. XIX; I Petr. II)]*.

[n: (HIER.)] Simon, who bears the cross under compulsion, is he who labours for human praise. Men force him to this labour whom the fear or the love of God does not force.

*The father of Alexander.* This Simon seems to have been of great repute, since his sons also are marked out by name as being known to all.

[n: (HIER.)] Some are called to mind through the merits of their fathers, [0237C] others through the merits of their sons. But this Simon, etc., *as far as* whence: by Jeremias it is said to Judea: *A plentiful olive tree, fair, fruitful, goodly, the Lord called thy name.*

VERS. 22.-- *The place of Calvary. [n: (HIER.)]* The Jews hand down that in this place of the mountain the ram was offered for Isaac [n: (Gen. XXII)], that there Christ might be made bald, that is, might be severed from his own flesh, namely from carnal Judea.

[n: (BEDA.)] Outside the gate the heads of the condemned were struck off, and the places of the beheaded are called calvaries. Therefore the Lord was crucified there, that where was the threshing-floor of the condemned, there the standards of the martyrs might be raised up. And because for us was made the curse of the cross, and he was both scourged and crucified for the salvation of all, he willed to be crucified as one guilty among the guilty.

[0237D]VERS. 23.-- *And they gave. [n: (BEDA.)]* Matthew, *mingled with gall [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*, which is the same thing. For he put gall for bitterness. For wine mingled with myrrh is most bitter; although it may be that they made the wine most bitter with both gall and myrrh.

*[n: (HIER.)] And they gave,* etc. By this vinegar the juice of the deadly apple is wiped away. *And he took it not.* That for which he suffers. Whence of him: *That which I took not away, I then paid [n: (Psal. LXVIII)]*.

*And he took it not.* Matthew: *And when he had tasted, he would not drink.* Matthew shows that for us he tasted the bitterness of death, but on the third day rose again.

VERS. 24.-- *And crucifying him. [n: (HIER.)]* Here [0238A] salvation is fastened upon a tree. First death was fastened upon a tree. The first tree was of the knowledge of good and evil; the second is for us a tree of good only, and of life. The stretching out of the first hand to the tree laid hold on death; the stretching out of the second found the life that had perished. By this wood are we carried over the billowy sea to the land of the living.

*His garments. [n: (BEDA.)]* The garment of Christ, divided into four according to the number of the soldiers, signifies the Church distributed equally, that is, harmoniously, through the four quarters of the world.

[n: (HIER.)] The garments of the Lord are the commandments with which his body is covered, which is the Church, because they are divided among the soldiers of the Gentiles, so that there are four orders while the faith is one, that is, the married, the widowed, [0238B] the prelates, the set apart. They cast lots for the undivided tunic, which is peace and truth in all things, after the manner of a royal mantle or of a ring. *Casting lots. [n: (ID.)]* Upon the tunic, namely, which was without seam. Here the things allotted, etc., *as far as* and the lot comes or falls by the judgment of God alone.

VERS. 25.-- *Now it was the third hour. [n: (HIER.)]* This Mark has properly, for at the sixth hour darkness overspread the earth, so that none could move his head. For from the thirtieth, etc., *as far as* who are rather to be said to have crucified him, though they strive to dissemble it, than the soldiers, who according to their office obeyed their captain.

VERS. 26.-- *And there was a title. [n: (BEDA.)]* The title shows that not even by killing him could they bring it about that [0238C] he should not be their king; whence: *But I am appointed king by him,* etc. For he is fittingly at once king and priest, since he offered to the Father the excellent victim of his own flesh upon the altar of the cross, and held forth the dignity of a king by the title, that he might make known to all that through the gibbet of the cross he had not lost, but had confirmed, his empire.

VERS. 27.-- *And with him they crucify. [n: (BEDA.)]* As the Apostle says: *Our old man is crucified together with him, that the body of sin may be destroyed, that sin may no more have dominion over us.* [var: Vulg. Rom. 6:6 *vetus homo noster simul crucifixus est, ut destruatur corpus peccati, et ultra non serviamus peccato*, "and that we may serve sin no longer"] So long therefore as our works act, that the body of sin may be emptied out, so long, etc., *as far as* whence the Apostle: *That, being rooted and founded in charity, you may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth, the length, the height and the depth [n: (Ephes. III)]*. *Two thieves.* [0238D] [n: (ID.)] The thieves who were crucified with the Lord signify those who, by faith and confession of Christ, or by the struggle of their own martyrdom, undergo any strict continence whatsoever. And they who do this for eternal glory are signified by the merit and the faith of the thief on the right. But whoever does it for human glory or with any less worthy intention imitates the mind and the deeds of the thief on the left.

[n: (HIER.)] Truth, reckoned among the wicked, leaves the one on the left and takes up the other on the right; so will he do in the day of judgment, out of a like crime they obtain unlike ways. The one goes before Peter into paradise, the other before Judas into hell. A short confession purchases a long life, and a blasphemy that has an end is punished with an eternal penalty. Here the colt of Juda is bound to [0239A] the vine, ἀναβόλαιον, that is, the cloak is dyed in the blood of the grape, here the kids tear the vineyard.

VERS. 29.-- *Wagging their heads. [n: (HIER.)]* The death of the wicked is sent forth when the bonds of hell are stirred up even to the neck, which is the neck of the human race.

VERS. 31.-- *He saved others. [n: (HIER.)]* Even unwilling they confess that he made others safe, and they condemn themselves by their own sentence, because he made others safe and could have saved himself if he would.

VERS. 32.-- *Let him now come down.* It is more to rise from the sepulchre, and yet they did not believe. *These all have gone aside, they are become unprofitable together [n: (Psal. XIII)]*.

*And they that were with him. [n: (BEDA.)]* According to Luke [n: (Luc. XXIII)] one thief blasphemed, saying: *If thou be [0239B] Christ, save thyself and us.* The other, etc., *as far as* they who seek heavenly things without doubt attain to them, Christ mediating and having mercy.

VERS. 33.-- *And when the sixth hour was come. [n: (BEDA.)]* It is to be noted that at the sixth hour, that is, when the sun was about to withdraw from the centre of the world, he was crucified, and that at daybreak, at the rising of the sun, he rose again, etc., *as far as* and at what hour the first Adam by sinning brought death into the world, at that same hour the second Adam by dying should destroy death.

*Darkness. [n: (HIER.)]* Here is Noe made drunk and stripped, covered by heaven and earth with a drunkard's cloak, and mocked by man, here blood dripped from the wood.
