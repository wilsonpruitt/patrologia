## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *The book of the generation. [n: (HIER.)]* The Hebrews impose upon their volumes a name taken from their beginnings, as the book of Genesis, etc., *as far as* but this custom holds in many, chiefly in the prophets, as "the vision of Isaiah," where thou suppliest, "this is."

*Of the son of David.* The order is inverted, lest, Abraham being set first, the connected sequence of the generation should be broken. Of these [0065B] two he is specially called Son: because the one was first among the patriarchs, the other among the kings, to whom the promise concerning Christ was made, that he might disclose to the Jews that Christ was to come out of the law, in whom they should see the prophecies fulfilled.

*Of the son of David the son of Abraham.* He chose these two out of all, whose Son Christ is called, that to the Jews to whom he writes, who knew from the law that Christ was to come, it might appear that this is he who had long been promised, in whom all the prophecies should be seen to be fulfilled; for to these alone in the Old Testament is the promise of Abraham recorded as made, *and in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed [n: (Gen. XXII)]*.

Abraham and David are two pillars standing in the law as in a porch before the door which is Christ: [0065C] which a cord of twelve cubits encircles, that is, the faith of the apostles. He embraces these fathers in the genealogy, from the midst of whom Christ the door is opened to believers.

VERS. 2.-- *Abraham begot Isaac: and Isaac begot Jacob.* Fittingly let the beginning of the generation be from him to whom he was first promised, in whom is the blessing of all.

Matthew begins the generation from the beginning of the promise. And even to the end of the book this is pursued, that he who was born of this generation may be understood as God and man.

From the outset he begins from him to whom the promise of faith was first made, that in this father, to whom the promise was first made, they may learn who and what manner of men they ought to be who [0065D] wish to be blessed in his seed. Matthew begins, or reckons, the generation by descending: because he shows the humanity of Christ, through which God descended to men. Luke, relating it by ascending, discloses the form of the sacrament. For beginning from the baptism he ascends as far as God, showing that the baptized ascend to this, that they may be sons of God.

*Begot.* Matthew sets down, *begot:* he who numbers only the sons of nature. Luke: *Who was:* he who inserts some who are sons according to the law or by adoption. Matthew sets down "genuit," and not "generavit," perhaps that he might be recognized, shown forth at the end, of whom it is said: *This day have I begotten thee [n: (Psal. II)]*.

*Judas and his brethren.* Therefore Judas alone is named expressly, [0066A] that thou mightest recognize him who had been promised out of his stock.

He mentions the brethren of Judas, the brethren of Isaac and Jacob being passed over: because those were driven off from the people of God as strangers: these were written as heirs in the book of life, and are as it were the twelve patriarchs, in whom the number of the apostles also is signified. But they are not expressed by name, lest the generation be extended beyond the number of the sacrament.

In the seed of the aforesaid fathers Christ entered into Egypt, and the enemy, foreboding this, oppressed the people, and slew not the women, but the males, that he might extinguish him.

From Judas is Christ, whom all the Gentiles received: at whose coming Herod, a foreigner, was already ruling.

[0066B]VERS. 3.-- *And Judas.* Judas begot Phares and Zaram before he entered Egypt, into which both afterwards passed over with their father.

*And Phares begot Esrom.* In Egypt Phares begot Esrom, and Esrom Aram, and Aram Aminadab, and Aminadab Naasson: and then Moses led them out of Egypt. Naasson was leader under Moses in the tribe of Judah through the desert, in which he begot Salmon. This Salmon was prince in the tribe of Judah, who entered with Joshua into the land of promise. This Salmon in the land of promise begot Booz of Raab.

Christ is Phares, that is, division, because he will separate the sheep from the goats. He himself is Esrom, that is, an arrow or a court, because he pierces the hearts of his hearers. He has also [0066C] the breadth of charity, wherewith he loved even his enemies.

VERS. 4.-- *And Aram.* Aram is interpreted *Christ the elect* or *the exalted*: the Elect, of whom the prophet says: *Behold my servant whom I have chosen [n: (Ose. XI)]*. And elsewhere: *The Lord is high above all nations [n: (Psal. CXII)]*. He himself is Aminadab, that is, willing, who says: *I will freely sacrifice to thee [n: (Ibid. XXXIII)]*. The same is Naasson, that is, augury, who knows things past, present and to come. Or Naasson, serpentine, because Christ after the manner of a serpent guarded against all snares.

VERS. 5.-- *And Salmon begot Booz.* Christ is Salmon, that is, *perceiving,* because he perceives all things. He himself took Raab, that is, the Church of the Gentiles. Raab, *hunger,* or *blessedness,* or *onrush:* because the Church of the Gentiles hungers and thirsts after justice, and, spread abroad through [0066D] the world, converts philosophers and kings by the onrush of doctrine. Raab displayed the scarlet in the window, that is, in the mouth she declares the passion of Christ by confession. Raab the harlot, who received the messengers of Joshua, whom Salmon, prince of the tribe of Judah, joined to himself, signifies the Church of the Gentiles, which is joined to the prince out of Judah, that is, to Christ. And she alone with her own is saved, while Jericho, that is, the present world, walled about with seven walls of vices, is beset for the space of seven days with the trumpets of preachings, until *at the last trump* all the walls fall down, and the last death is destroyed.

*And Booz.* Booz is Christ, of whom it is said: *Behold the Lord God shall come with strength [n: (Isa. XL)]*. Note [0067A] that none of the holy women is taken into the genealogy of Christ, but those whom Scripture reproves. For he ought to be born of sinners, who had come for sinners, that his own might learn that the sins of the fathers do not stand in their way. Whence the lamb to be immolated at the Passover was commanded to be taken from the goats and the sheep, that is, to be begotten from the just and from sinners

*Of Ruth.* A Moabitess, that he may show the prophecy of Isaiah fulfilled: *Send forth the lamb, O Lord, the ruler of the earth [n: (Isa. XVI)]*. Through her the seed of Abraham is brought down to Christ. The Church of the Gentiles, signified by Ruth, before she came to Booz, that is, to Christ, was of her father the devil.

Ruth signifies the Church which is of Moab, that is, of the Gentiles, which [was] of her father the devil and of the rock of the desert [0067B] because of idolatry, out of which God raised up sons of Abraham: forgetful of her former nation she came to Booz, who is called strong, that is, to Christ, who raises up the seed of his brother, that is, of Moses, who died without children, *because the law brought nothing to perfection [n: (Hebr. VII)]*. Ruth, *seeing* or *hastening;* because the Church with a pure heart sees God, which hastens to the prize of the heavenly vision and determines to persevere in faith and love.

*And Jesse.* The father of David is more often named Isai, but he bore two names. Yet here he is called Jesse for this reason: that he might know that he had come; of whom Isaiah says: *There shall come forth a rod out of the root of Jesse [n: (Isa. XI)]*, etc. This is the flower which Isaac smells, saying: *Behold the smell of my son is as the smell of a full field [n: (Gen. XXVII)]*, because the Spirit filled him most abundantly.

[0067C] *King.* Not that he alone is king in this genealogy, but because he is the first king of Judah and the beginning of the kings, for whose sake the rest also are kept upon the throne of the kingdom: and through him the order of the generation runs down to Christ, who has the kingdom of David.

VERS. 6.-- *And David the king.* Mystically David, that is, Christ, who overcame Goliath, that is, the devil. He himself is strong of hand, or desirable in aspect. Of the same it is said: *The Lord strong and mighty [n: (Psal. XXIII)]*, etc. And again: *Beautiful in form above the sons of men [n: (Ibid. XLIV)]*.

*Of Urias.* Urias, my light, namely of God, that is, the devil, who said: *I will be like the Most High [n: (Isa. XIV)]*: whose wedded wife, the Church, Christ loved from the upper chamber of the Father's majesty, and, when she was made fair, joined her to himself [0067D] in marriage. Urias, who is a figure of the devil, is interpreted *my light is God*, who still transforms himself into an angel of light, and makes himself the light of God.

VERS. 7.-- *And Solomon.* Christ is Solomon, that is, *peaceable,* because he himself is our peace, who made both one. He himself is Roboam, that is, the onrush of a people, who swiftly converts peoples to the faith. *Roboam.* Of him it is said: *I will give to Roboam himself the tribe of Judah, that a lamp may remain to David all his days in Israel [n: (III Reg. XI)]*. [var: Vulg. 3 Reg. 11:36 reads *filio autem ejus dabo tribum unam, ut remaneat lucerna David servo meo cunctis diebus coram me in Jerusalem*, "to his son I will give one tribe, that a lamp may remain to David my servant all his days before me in Jerusalem"] Which cannot be taken according to the letter, because neither did Israel endure for all days, nor after Christ was there any leader of David's line in Israel. But Christ is an eternal lamp in the heavenly Israel, and therefore is kept always. But Christ is said [0068A] to sit upon the throne of David, because David's temporal kingdom prefigured the eternal kingdom of Christ. *Abias.* The Lord is Father, that is, Christ, who says: *I will be to him a Father, for as Lord he has done all things whatsoever he would [n: (Psal. CXIII)]*. Who is also Asa, that is, lifting up, because he himself took away the sins of the world.

VERS. 8.-- *Josaphat,* etc. *Judging* or *the judgment of God,* namely Christ, who judges the world in equity. *And Joram begot.* Christ is Joram, that is, exalted, who says: *No man ascends into heaven but he who descended from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven [n: (Joan. III)]*.

VERS. 9.-- *And Ozias.* Under Ozias, Joathan, Achaz and Ezechias, Isaiah prophesied concerning Christ. Because of the sins of the princes the mouths of the prophets were shut. [0068B] *Ezechias.* The Lord is strong, he it is who says to the apostles: *Fear not.* To Ezechias, when he was without children, it was said: *Set thy house in order, for thou shalt die and shalt not live [n: (Isa. XXXVIII)]*. Therefore he wept not for the sake of a longer life, since he knew that Solomon had for that very reason pleased God, because he had not asked for more years: but because he was in doubt whether the promise of God would be fulfilled, when he knew himself to be of the stock of David, through whom Christ must needs come: and he himself was without children, whence also he said [n: (Ibid. XXXVIII)]: *I shall not see the Lord God,* likewise, *I shall not look upon man any more,* etc. On the contrary he believed, because he who had promised is faithful. But God changes the sentence which he had given, for the testing of faith, and grants space for begetting.

VERS. 10.-- *Manasses.* Forgetful. This is he who [0068C] said, as it were to a converted sinner: *I will not remember all his iniquities [n: (Ezech. XVIII)]*. *Amon,* that is, *faithful* or *a fosterer,* signifying Christ, of whom it is said: *The Lord is faithful in all his ways, who calls his sons together, as a hen her chicks [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps. 144:13 reads *Fidelis Dominus in omnibus verbis suis*, "faithful in all his words"; *in omnibus viis suis* belongs to Ps. 144:17, *Justus Dominus in omnibus viis suis*, and the clause about the hen stands nowhere in the psalm (cf. Matt. XXIII)]

VERS. 11.-- *Josias,* that is, *the salvation of the Lord* or *incense.* He it is who says: *Let my prayer be directed as incense [n: (Psal. CXL)]*, etc.

Josias, a just king, which his sons whom he begot were not; not in the transmigration, because he never went into transmigration: but according to God's predestination to the transmigration, he sets Jechonias and his brethren together, that of those whose iniquity is common the misery also may be alike. Jechonias, the preparation of the Lord, because the Lord prepared both the father and the son for the transmigration.
