## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0068D] [n: (ISID.)] The truth of the history holds that there were two Jechoniases, so that the one, namely the father, stands at the end of the preceding fourteen, the other at the beginning of the following. ⟦s1⟧ But mystically, according to Augustine, one and the same is in the faith of the preceding and at the beginning of the following. ⟦s2⟧ But why has Joachim, the father of Jechonias, been passed over? ⟦s2⟧ Perhaps, that he might make Jechonias a type of Christ, he numbered him twice, and passed Joachim over lest the number should be increased.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ All who are numbered from David to the transmigration of Babylon were kings.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Jechonias.* ⟦s2⟧ The former Jechonias is called *the resurrection of the Lord,* the latter Jechonias *the preparation of the Lord.* ⟦s3⟧ Both [0069A] agree with Christ, who is *the resurrection and the life [n: (Joan. XI)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Salathiel.* ⟦s2⟧ My petition is God, it agrees with Christ, who says: *Holy Father, keep them whom thou hast given me, that they may be one as we also are [n: (Joan. XVII)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And Zorobabel begot Abiud.* ⟦s2⟧ The master of Babylon, that is, of confusion. ⟦s3⟧ It agrees with him who called the world back from the error of idolatry to the way of truth. ⟦s4⟧ There were zealous men from among the Jews called Heriles on account of the nearness of Christ's kindred, and they were Nazaraeans, who, retaining the order of Christ's genealogy partly by memory, partly from the books of Days, partly from grandfathers and great-grandfathers, wrote it down in order. ⟦s5⟧ *Abiud,* that is, *my father.* ⟦s6+7⟧ This agrees with Christ, of whom it is said: *He hath called upon me: thou art my father* [0069B] [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII)], etc. [var: Vulg. Ps. 88:27 reads *Ipse invocabit me*, "He shall call upon me": the future, where Migne prints the perfect *invocavit*] *Eliachim.* ⟦s8⟧ The Lord raising up. ⟦s9⟧ He who says: *Every one who sees the Son and believes in him shall have life everlasting, and I will raise him up [n: (Joan. VI)]*, etc.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Azor.* ⟦s2⟧ Helped. ⟦s3+4⟧ He it is who says: *Behold God hath helped me, the Lord is the upholder of my soul [n: (Psal. LIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps. 53:6 reads *Ecce enim Deus adjuvat me*, "Behold, for God helpeth me": the present, where Migne prints the perfect *adjuvit*, answering to his own etymology of the name, "Helped"] *Sadoch.* ⟦s5⟧ Just, that is, Christ, of whom it is said: *The Lord is just and hath loved justices [n: (Ibid. X)]*. ⟦s6⟧ *Achim,* that is, *this my brother.* ⟦s7⟧ Who else than he who willed to be made man, that he might have brethren, of whom he should say: *I will declare thy name to my brethren [n: (Ibid. XXI)]*. ⟦s8⟧ *Eliud,* that is, my God, it agrees with Christ, who says: *My God, why hast thou forsaken me [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*?

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Eleazar,* that is, *my God is my helper.* [0069C] He it is who said in the person of man: *My God, I will hope in him [n: (Psal. XC)]*. ⟦s2⟧ *Mathan.* ⟦s3⟧ Giving or given. ⟦s4⟧ He it is who gave good things to men, and of whom it is said: *So God loved the world, that he gave his Son [n: (Joan. III)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Through these fathers Christ [came] into the world, and bore in himself the office of them all. ⟦s2⟧ And it is fitting that we ascend to him by that same line of holiness by which he deigned to descend to us.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Joseph.* ⟦s2⟧ One who adds. ⟦s2⟧ Christ, who added the Gentiles to the Jews, who is also called the father of Christ, whom, born of his own wife, he received by adoption: but he is also called father by supposition. ⟦s3⟧ *Joseph* Matthew calls the son of Jacob, and Jacob the son of Mathan. ⟦s4⟧ Luke calls Joseph the son of Heli, and Heli the son of Mathat; but Mathan and [0069D] Mathat begot one son each of the same wife, Hesta by name. ⟦s5⟧ Mathan, who descended through Solomon, married her first, and, leaving one son, Jacob, died, and afterwards Mathat, who descended from David through Mathan, married the same woman and begot Heli, thus Jacob and Heli are brothers by the same mother. ⟦s6⟧ But Jacob, taking the wife of Heli his brother, who had died without children, to raise up his seed, begot Joseph, his own by nature, but by the law the son of Heli. ⟦s7⟧ That the son is said to be called by the name of him for whom he is raised up, is not true, since Booz called him whom he begot of Ruth not Elimelech, for whom he was raised up, but Obeth.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The husband of Mary.* ⟦s2⟧ What has the generation traced from David down to Joseph to do with Christ, since Christ is not of the seed [0070A] of Joseph? ⟦s3⟧ But it is not the custom of the Scriptures that the order of women should be woven into the generations; and therefore it is brought in not through Mary, but through Joseph, since Joseph and Mary were of one tribe. ⟦s4⟧ Whence also he was compelled to take her as a kinswoman, lest one tribe should be confounded with another: whence also they make their declaration together, as of one stock, returning to Bethlehem when each went to his own city.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ By the example of Mary it is clear to faithful married persons that, an equal consent being kept, continence can abide, and that it is called a marriage not by the mingling of bodily sex, but by the guarding of the mind's affection. ⟦s2⟧ Whence also Joseph is called the husband of Mary, because a marriage is true where the effect of love is preserved, and in Mary the fruit of the nuptials is found.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0070B]VERS. 17.-- *All therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ The order of the generation having at length been run through, the evangelist, concluding at the end, recapitulates under a mystical number.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Now the generation of Christ.* ⟦s2⟧ He sets it down without the addition, "Jesu," that thou mayest understand him of whom the prophet says: *Thy God hath anointed thee.* ⟦s3⟧ The name Jesus, which belongs to him substantially, he reserves for the angel, so that he who is afterwards to speak of the conception of the Holy Spirit may the more sublimely set forth his proper name from the office of salvation. ⟦s4⟧ *So it was.* ⟦s5⟧ As has been said, that from Joseph the origin of Mary, who was also of David, might be made clear: and Christ, not of his seed, is shown as God and man, as man through the genealogy, as God through this, that he was of a virgin by the Holy Spirit. ⟦s6⟧ So, I say, it was, as has been written above and will presently be said, as afterwards it is made manifest by signs and prodigies, [0070C] so that the whole book may demonstrate that God and man was born: which must be believed that it may be understood. ⟦s7⟧ For here human sense fails, where there is not nature, but power: he who does not believe does not understand that a virgin brought forth, that the word was made flesh.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *When she had been espoused. [n: (ORIG.)]* She was espoused for this reason, that she might signify the Church, which is virgin and spouse: and that through Joseph, etc., *as far as* the espousal was made some days before the constant cohabitation, and in the meantime she was a wife under the guardianship of her husband.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Before they came together.* ⟦s2⟧ Not that they came together afterwards: as if we should say, before he repented, he was overtaken by death. ⟦s3⟧ Not that they came together afterwards; but he shows the time next to the nuptials, in which the solemnities of the nuptials are celebrated.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0070D] *She was found with child.* ⟦s2⟧ Joseph found that she was with child, but did not know that it was of the Holy Spirit: since afterwards, as one in doubt, he deliberated to put her away; but the apostle adds this, lest in the meantime a suspicion should creep upon the reader. ⟦s3⟧ How this was done, and in what order, or in what city the conception of Christ was accomplished, this, being here passed over, is set forth by Luke.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Just.* ⟦s2⟧ By the faith whereby he believed that Christ was to be born of a virgin, and he willed to humble himself before so great a grace.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ That he was just, this is a testimony to the chastity of Mary, so that he who preserves the innocent may be called just: but merciful also in that he was unwilling to make it public, just from his knowledge of her chastity, merciful from fear. ⟦s2⟧ He knew that she was [0071A] blameless: but whence or what the matter was he did not know: and therefore he chose a middle way of escape, that he might neither betray the innocent, nor by consenting to a thing unknown make himself guilty before God. ⟦s3⟧ It is true virtue when there is neither mercy without justice, nor justice without mercy, which, separated from one another, fall away.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And would not have her.* ⟦s2⟧ Whom he had espoused, to lead into marriage, lest he should seem to conceal what he did not know. ⟦s3⟧ Or to expose her to a punishment in which he knew there was no infamy, because he knew that he had received her a virgin and had kept her untouched.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *But while he thought on these things.* ⟦s2⟧ Here we are taught that in uncertain matters we must deliberate long, lest we sin by the rashness of levity. ⟦s3⟧ *Behold the angel of the Lord.* ⟦s4⟧ Because he thinks thus mercifully, he deserves to be consoled, and his own counsel [0071B] is changed for a better counsel. ⟦s5⟧ *Son of David.* ⟦s6⟧ Acknowledge what was promised to the house of David, of which thou art, and Mary also, and see it fulfilled in her. ⟦s7⟧ *Fear not.* ⟦s8⟧ Although it be as great as thou believest, yet fear not on account of guilt, but embrace her with the regard of charity. ⟦s9⟧ *Thy wife.* ⟦s10⟧ She was a wife not by intercourse, but by affection, not by the joining of bodies, but by the coupling of souls.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Note that she is called wife from the first pledge of the espousal. ⟦s2⟧ It is therefore a matter of dispensation if any one is permitted to marry another after the sacrament of betrothal, and if he can never pay the debt.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Rightly is Joseph called husband, and Mary wife, since in him the affection of love is preserved, which is true marriage, and in her without intercourse there is the fruit of the nuptials.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is born in her.* ⟦s2⟧ To be born of her is to be brought forth into the light. [0071C] But to be born in her is to be conceived; or according to the presence of the angel which he has from God: to whom the future is as the past, it is called born. ⟦s3⟧ *It is of the Holy Spirit.* ⟦s4⟧ The Holy Spirit is called God, and the gift of God, and charity; and charity alone made him to be incarnate.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *She shall bring forth.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest Joseph should seem not to be necessary to her, he adds: *She shall bring forth.* ⟦s3⟧ And so thou shalt be necessary for her care. ⟦s4⟧ *And thou shalt call his name Jesus.* ⟦s5⟧ He says the same to this man which he had foretold to Mary, that from the utterance he may set before men as well as women that the promised Saviour has already come. ⟦s6⟧ *For he.* ⟦s7⟧ The name is interpreted.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Now this,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That she was espoused to a kinsman: that she was found with child: that a virgin brought forth: that he was called Jesus: that he saves. ⟦s3⟧ For the Evangelist says this when all things had already been [0071D] fulfilled.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That it might be fulfilled.* ⟦s2⟧ The prophecy of Isaiah is a sign of the foreknowledge of God, because what it says is certainly foreknown by God: and therefore it ought to be feared by man. ⟦s3⟧ But no sign effects what it designates: but only signifies what it shows, so prophecy does not make a necessity of those things which it foretells, but is a sign of the foreknowledge of God.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIER.)] There is no necessity of things from the utterance of the prophets, but only the showing forth of truth. ⟦s2⟧ Prophecy did not make a necessity of those things which it says, but is a sign of the foreknowledge of God.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ One prophecy is from the predestination of God, which must in all ways come to pass, so that it is fulfilled without our [0072A] choice: as is this of which we here treat. ⟦s2⟧ Another is from the foreknowledge of God, with which our choice is mingled. ⟦s3⟧ Another there is which is called a threatening, which is made for a sign of divine punishment, that the elect may flee from the face of the bow, and the incautious justly perish; and this not from foreknowledge: because a thing is known far otherwise than it is threatened as to come.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Behold a virgin.* ⟦s2⟧ The prophet Isaiah marvels, and as it were rousing all from sleep, says: *Behold a virgin shall be with child [n: (Isa. VII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ To the Holy Spirit that was present which in time was not yet. ⟦s4⟧ *Behold a virgin,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ The Jews say that this is not a name of integrity, but of age, a girl. ⟦s6⟧ But how then is it a sign? ⟦s7⟧ He follows the sense of Isaiah, not the words. ⟦s8⟧ In place of "she shall conceive" he sets down: *She shall be with child:* and "they shall call" for "thou shalt call," [0072B] or "he shall be called."

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Emmanuel.* ⟦s2⟧ It is proper to Christ, as is Jesus, because he abides always with those whom he saves, by helping them.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For three causes he supports from the Old Testament the things which he sets down in the Gospel: for testification, that he may have testimony from the law and the prophets: for the confirmation of faith, because we believe more easily when we see fulfilled the things which were foretold: for the joining of the New and the Old Testament.
