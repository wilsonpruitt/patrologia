## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And in all the borders thereof.* ⟦s2⟧ He was not content with the devastation of Bethlehem, but laid waste the neighbouring places; nor had he any mercy for age, from a son of one night to a son of two years, but that he slew them all.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From two years old.* ⟦s2⟧ Therefore from two years old, that as Herod had taken in beforehand the borders of the city, so also he might take in beforehand another year. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore also from two years old: because he feared lest the child, whom the stars served, should fashion for himself an appearance a little above or below his age.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0077A]VERS. 17.-- *Then was fulfilled.* ⟦s2⟧ It is called fulfilled: because before it came to pass it was but half-full to those who read it, since what sounded in the letter secretly promised something to come.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CHRYSOST.)] He says that the star appeared for a year, as in the destruction of the Romans signs appeared in heaven, when the voice of the angels was heard: Let us pass over from these seats.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *A voice in Rama.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, in a high place it was spread far and wide.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Allegorically. ⟦s2⟧ Or, the voice of the Church groaning over the slaughter of her members ascends to the throne of the judge on high. ⟦s3⟧ *A voice in Rama.* ⟦s4⟧ On account of the Levite's wife, destroyed by fornication and divided into twelve parts according to the number of the tribes, all Israel [0077B] destroyed the tribe of Benjamin save three hundred men: the weeping and wailing of those who survived was heard as far as Rama, which is a place near Gabaa, at the twelfth milestone from Bethlehem. ⟦s5⟧ *Was heard.* ⟦s6⟧ He heightens it, so that hyperbolically Rachel, already dead, is said with the feeling of one grieving to be already weeping for her sons. ⟦s7⟧ Whence by this the history concerning the Chaldeans is woven, that the devastation to come from them upon the people of the Jews would be like to the former disaster. ⟦s8⟧ But more truly, the Spirit revealing it through the evangelist, the prophecy is taken of the slaughter of the children. ⟦s9⟧ He says therefore that what was spoken was then completed, because although the word of the prophecy looks to the impending captivity, yet in it, as in a shadow, the future truth of the slaughter of the infants is proclaimed.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Rachel.* ⟦s2⟧ Because she was buried near Bethlehem, and from [0077C] the earthly lodging of her body received the name of mother. ⟦s3⟧ Or because many of Benjamin and Joseph were slain. ⟦s4⟧ Of Rachel was Benjamin born, to whose lot Bethlehem does not belong, but only to the tribe of Judah. ⟦s5⟧ But because Judah and Benjamin are neighbouring tribes in the borders of Bethlehem, many also of the tribe of Benjamin were slain.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Note that the figure of the sacrament is better intimated through Rachel (than if Bethlehem were said to have wept), because through her in all things the pattern of the Church is commended, which weeps not so much for those carried off by death as for those destroyed by torment. ⟦s2⟧ *Rachel.* ⟦s3⟧ That is, the Church, weeps for her tender lambs destroyed: neither will she be comforted in the present, because they are not: but she sends forward all her hope and consolation to the eternal life.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Church weeps, and will not be comforted here: because [0077D] her sons are not of this world. ⟦s2⟧ Or she weeps: because while she brings forth, she has justice [sic: justitiam], whence it is said: *You shall weep,* etc., but a consolation is added: *Your sorrow shall be turned into joy [n: (Joan. XVI)]*.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *But Herod being dead, behold,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because, the perfidy of the Jews being lulled to sleep, and Elias and Enoch preaching, Judaea shall receive the word of God.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The king being dead, his accomplices straightway perish, so, the persecution of the Church ceasing, she is invited to heavenly things.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Into the land.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not determine into what part, so that, Joseph doubting, the angel may return: and that by his frequent address Joseph may be made the more certain. ⟦s3⟧ Because he did not distinguish into what part, therefore Josephus understood Judaea, which was the worthier part of the kingdom. ⟦s4⟧ *They are dead.* [0078A] Some relate that Herod caused the nobler of the Jews to be slain over his own funeral, that he might so compel the Jews to weep for his death.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Who rising up.* ⟦s2⟧ Allegorically. ⟦s3⟧ Joseph bears the figure of the apostles, by whose office Christ is carried about. ⟦s4⟧ The apostles came first into the land of Israel: because they preached first to the Jews. ⟦s5⟧ But because of Herod, that is, the hereditary unbelief remaining, they fear and withdraw, being admonished to preach grace to the Gentiles, and to carry Christ over to them.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The land of Israel being named, it seemed to Joseph that such a child ought to dwell nowhere but in Jerusalem, where the temple was and the celebration of the prophets. ⟦s2⟧ Yet the angel wished Galilee to be understood, which the Jews also inhabited, which shows that it had thus been predestined, [0078B] because he shall be called a Nazarene.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *He feared to go thither.* ⟦s2⟧ If he feared Judaea on account of Archelaus, the heir of his father's cruelty, why not Galilee also, where another Herod, his brother, was reigning? ⟦s3⟧ Because Archelaus after his father obtained the sole rule of the whole kingdom: but growing insolent he was cast down by Tiberius Caesar into exile at Lyons, and then the kingdom was divided into four tetrarchies, one of which, that is Galilee, Herod his brother obtained.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is asked: why did Joseph not fear to go into Galilee as into Judaea, since Archelaus reigned there also? ⟦s2⟧ But he could better lie hid in Nazareth (which was remote) than in Jerusalem, where was the head of the kingdom and Archelaus constantly present.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He withdrew into the parts.* ⟦s2⟧ That Christ is not carried over thither [0078C] where Archelaus was reigning signifies that Christ shall be hidden from those in whom Antichrist shall reign.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Nazareth.* ⟦s2⟧ Nazareth is interpreted *a new shoot* or *the flower of the field*, from the root of which shoot the holy of holies is read to have ascended, to signify the substance of his eternity. ⟦s3⟧ As therefore he chose the time in which he should be born and the place, so also the city in which he should grow up: whose name is a token that he who is holiness itself, nurtured in holiness, is rightly called the holy of holies: whence also his disciples were first called Nazaraeans, but afterwards from Christ were called Christians.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Nazareth of Galilee, whither he is carried over, signifies that part of the same nation which was to receive the faith: whence it is interpreted *a flower*: because the more ardently the Church passes over from earthly things to heavenly, the more it abounds in the flower and shoot [0078D] of the virtues.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *By the prophets.* ⟦s2⟧ He says prophets in the plural, because we have no fixed instance from Scripture: he shows that he took the sense, not the words. ⟦s3⟧ Or in the same words in Isaiah according to the Hebrew verity: *A Nazarene shall ascend from his root [n: (Isa. XI)]*.
