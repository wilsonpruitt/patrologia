## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- *In those days.* He passes over many things, and crosses to the preaching of John. *In those days.* He takes in not the years of boyhood only, but all of them up to the preaching of John, that is, when he was nearly thirty years old. Luke expresses the times more exactly: In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar. *Preaching.* He who had before kept silence made himself known by baptizing, preparing the way [0079A] for the Lord. *In the desert of Judaea.* Because the Jews had been deserted by God, and for them repentance is necessary, that they may return.

VERS. 2.-- *Repentance.* The herald announces this, which afterwards the Truth announces through himself; repentance goes before, the promise of the kingdom follows: he who truly repents, in himself his past errors, then lifts up his mind to the heavenly kingdom. If remission is made freely in baptism, why is repentance preached? But the manner of repentance is threefold. The first, to renounce and condemn errors and all evils before baptism: which is necessary for those who are coming to Christ. The second, that those who have fallen after baptism be called back through repentance. The third, whereby the saints are daily cleansed of such sins as life is not lived without.

[0079B]To repent is to weep for things done before, and not to commit what must be wept for. The kingdom of heaven is said to be at hand for the penitent. Not earthly things, as in the old law: and this through the obedience of Christ. And John first preached this kingdom of heaven: *Do penance.* The first virtue is, through repentance to slay the old man, and to hate vices: and he who does not do this not only fails to lay hold of the virtues, but does not even escape punishments. The virtue of repentance is conceived by fear, which is the beginning of wisdom. Repentance is from punishing, whereby each man punishes what he has unlawfully committed.

To do penance is to make fruits worthy of repentance. *The kingdom of heaven.* That is, Christ, through whom the saints reign in the heavenly places. Or the Gospel, by which he invites to the kingdom.

[0079C] *For it hath drawn near,* etc. Unless it had drawn near, no one could return: because the weak and the blind were without the way (which is Christ): and John, preparing that way, sets it before the penitent.

VERS. 3.-- *The voice of one crying.* John is the voice, Christ the word, who cries in John: No word is heard without a voice, nor is a voice of any avail without the understanding of the word. The voice prepares the way for the word, that it may be received. Whence: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ [n: (Psal. CXXXI)]*.

*In the desert.* A pattern for the penitent: the place of his converse is not the throng of men, but the desert. His clothing is not soft to the point of looseness, but rough: his food scanty and unaccustomed.

Typically, the desert signifies the life of the saints, set apart from the enticements of the world.

[0079D] *The way of the Lord.* The way of the Lord is made straight to the heart, when his word is diligently heard.

VERS. 4.-- *And John himself.* He who preaches repentance displays the habit of repentance. In him the meanness of clothing and of food is praised, the use of which in the rich man is called to account.

And John himself instructs the penitent in his own person, and sets forth a pattern of life by dwelling in the desert, living on locusts, clothing himself in camel's hair.

The servant of God ought not to have clothing for adornment or for delight, but only for covering nakedness. Hence John was clothed in hair.

VERS. 5.-- *Then there went out to him,* etc. Because unless a man go out from evils, he is not washed. Whence it fitly follows: *They were baptized by him in the Jordan:* which is interpreted *a going down*, [0080A] because they were going down from the pride of the old man to the humility of confession and amendment. For already then an example was given to those about to be baptized, of confessing sins and promising better things.

VERS. 6.-- *In the Jordan.* Because in it the figure of baptism went before, and from its name it requires a going down from pride, which is fitting for the baptized. *Confessing.* The confession of sins is the testimony of a conscience that fears God. For he who fears the judgment of God is not ashamed to confess his sins. More perfectly, fear looses every shame. The confession of sin has shame in it, and that blushing is itself a heavy punishment. And therefore we are bidden to confess our sins, that we may suffer the blushing for a punishment. For this very thing is part of the divine judgment.

VERS. 7.-- *Many of the Pharisees,* etc. These are of the [0080B] heretics of the Jews, and the Pharisees set the righteousness of observances and traditions before all else. Whence also they are called Pharisees, that is, divided from the people by a private righteousness. The Sadducees, that is, the just: for they claim for themselves what they are not: these deny the resurrection, saying that the soul perishes with the body: they receive the five books of Moses, they reject the prophets. These, when they come to baptism, John rebukes: because they most of all had need of correction and repentance.

*Brood of vipers,* etc., that is, poisoned sons of the poisoned: because they envy the good and persecute them, they wound their neighbors as their fathers did.

The viper kills its own father, so the Jews killed the prophets who were their fathers.

VERS. 8.-- *Bring forth therefore fruits worthy. [n: (BEDA.)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 3:8 *fructum dignum*, singular; the plural *fructus dignos* is Lc 3:8's] [0080C] As if he were saying: Why do you not first lay aside your poisons, that so to baptism, etc., *as far as* it is grace that feels, and feeling seeks holiness.

VERS. 9.-- *For I say.* He draws them back from the flesh, that they may consider the works of God's predestination. The first are the rudiments of faith, to believe that God can do whatever he will, so that he who created all things out of nothing can form sons of Abraham from stones or from dust. *Of stones.* Pointing to those stones which Josue caused to be carried over out of the Jordan, toward which he says this as though stretching out his finger.

Josue caused twelve stones to be carried from the midst of the Jordan onto dry land, and others to be set in their place out of the land in the same channel: by which the blinding of the Jews and the transforming of the Gentiles to the light are foresignified.

[0080D]Or he calls the Gentiles stones: and the sense is: Do not glory in the seed of the flesh: because these only are the sons of God who have been enlightened by grace and inwardly raised up. He says "raise up" pointedly, because Christ received Sara, that is, the Church, and raises up sons of Abraham by grace, he having died without children, lest so great a patriarch should be deprived of the promises. In presage of this thing God once begot a son of Sara.

VERS. 10.-- *For now the axe.* But lest they should seem free of danger if they kept themselves apart from such sacraments, he threatens. *The axe.* Christ, who consists of haft and iron, that is, of the humanity by which he is held, and the divinity because he cuts. *Is laid:* because although he waits through patience, he yet sees what he is about to do. [0081A] *To the root.* That is, the end of the Jewish people, that he may take away from the land of the living those who do not believe in Christ. Or, the axe is the sentence of judgment, or the preaching of the Gospel. *To the root.* Note, not to the branches, but to the root. For when the sons of the wicked are taken away, what else is cut off but the branches of an unfruitful tree? When the whole offspring is taken away together with the parent, the tree is cut off at the root, that nothing may remain from which some shoot might spring up again. The roots are thoughts, by which men, being planted, are either lifted up on high to heaven, or sent down to the depths of hell. The tree is the human race: of this tree some branches are dry, that is, the pagans, fit for the burning. Others are green, but without fruit: as the hypocrites, who make a show of holiness, but are empty within: others are fruitful, but poisonous, that is, the heretics: who by preaching [0081B] bring forth fruit, but a deadly fruit. Others, that is, the catholics, who bear good fruit. *Every tree therefore,* as the Gentiles, the hypocrites, who bear none, and the heretics, who bear evil fruit.

VERS. 11.-- *I indeed.* Lest he should seem to threaten on his own authority, he begins to disclose of how great dignity he is: whose presence he holds out as a threat, or whose benefit he urges upon them: *I indeed baptize you in water.* I only wash bodies, because I cannot loose sins, so that as I run before in being born and in preaching, so I do in baptizing: that I may direct to the baptism of Christ the penitent, whom I mark off by this sign from the impenitent. *He who is to come after me.* And though I admonish you to bring forth fruits worthy of repentance, to cast away pride: yet I cannot loose from sins. *He is mightier than I.* Because I baptize unto [0081C] repentance, he unto remission. I have the Spirit, he gives it. I preach the kingdom of heaven, he gives it.

*Whose I am not,* etc. Others write: Whose I am not worthy to loose the latchet of his shoe. Now John could have said both, either in one continuous speech or at different times: so that one evangelist took up this, another that. Yet all of them told the truth. His aim in this is to show the excellence of Christ and his own humility.

Or according to the allegory. The shoe is the mystery of the incarnation; the latchet, the tying of the mystery. Therefore John is not able to loose the latchet, because he does not suffice to search out the mystery of the incarnation. Or, I am not worthy to loose the latchet of his shoe, [0081D] that is, I do not usurp for myself the name of bridegroom, nor do I wish to be believed or called the bridegroom, but the friend of the bridegroom. For it was the custom that if any man refused to take to wife her who fell to him, the man who should come forward as bridegroom to her by right of kinship would loose his shoe. John therefore, who is not the bridegroom, says: I am not worthy, etc. As if he were to say: I cannot uncover the footsteps of the Redeemer: because I do not usurp for myself the name of bridegroom.

Or he simply humbles himself, or he is not worthy to carry about the sacraments of the Gospel by preaching, which was not assigned to him but committed to the apostles. I am not worthy to unfold the nature of the Divinity to the humanity. Or, I am not worthy to bear [0082A] the preaching of the Gospel, or to usurp the name of bridegroom. John, as a mere man, could not give the Spirit, by whom guilt is remitted. But Christ gives it, because he is God: and he baptizes in the word, of which it is said: *And you are clean by reason of the word which I have spoken to you [n: (Joan. XV)]* [var: Vulg. Jo 15:3 *Jam vos mundi estis propter sermonem quem locutus sum vobis*]; and elsewhere: *Cleansing them by the laver of water in the word of sanctified life [n: (Ephes. V)]* [var: Vulg. Eph 5:26 *ut illam sanctificaret, mundans lavacro aquae in verbo vitae*, without *eos* and *sanctificatae*].

*In the Holy Spirit.* Either in the spirit of sanctification, and in fire, that is, in the proving of tribulation. Or in the spirit he washes in the present: afterwards, if any stain has crept in, he will burn it away to purity by the fire of purgatory: which is to be believed of the lighter sins. Whence in Leviticus, upon the altar a fire sent down from heaven burned continually, that is, the fire of divine love, which as it were rebaptizes all those in Christ (that is, those who are offered upon the altar) and burns away [0082B] the vices of their flesh.

VERS.12.-- *Whose fan.* The fan in his hand is the sifting of judgment by which are distinguished the light and those empty of the fruit of good work. *In his hand,* because the Father judges no man, but has given all judgment to the Son. *He shall thoroughly cleanse,* that is, he daily cleanses from divers temptations. Or, *He shall thoroughly cleanse,* when for manifest sins the perverse man is cast out of the Church, or after death is damned. *But the chaff.* The chaff springs from the same origin as the wheat, that is, from the seed, but the cockle from a different one. The chaff, therefore, are those who are steeped in the sacraments of the faith, but are not solid: the cockle are those who are severed from the elect both in work and in profession. Of these it is said: *He that believeth not is already judged,* and therefore no mention is made here of them. *Unquenchable.* Because it shall not [0082C] be quenched, neither shall it quench those it torments, but shall punish them eternally. And it is so called by way of distinction from that fire of which it is said: *Thou hast tried us by fire [n: (Psal. LXV)]*, etc.
