## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

*Casting a net.* Because those whom he sees often gaping after earthly gains he calls to heavenly desires, and through them, by the nets of the Gospel, he snatches others out of the deep of iniquity, whence he adds: *Come ye after me, and I will make you to be fishers of men [n: (Joan. I)]*.

VERS. 19.-- *Fishers.* He who is about to fish goes through the places of fishing.

Mystically. When being incarnate he took flesh, he saw by his divinity in the sea of the world spiritual fishers: and to others he showed what they were, whom he had foreseen from eternity.

VERS. 20.-- *Immediately.* Perfect obedience is to leave one's own things unfinished. Their nets being left: because even [0088A] if afterwards they take them up again, it is not out of covetousness and the love of ownership. In these a pattern is given to those who wish to follow. The intention is changed, not the fishing, the nets are changed into doctrine, covetousness into the love of souls: the sea becomes the world, the ship the Church, the fishes good and evil men.

VERS. 21.-- *He saw two others.* Behold, in these four the number of the evangelists is prefigured, who draw souls out of the world from the four quarters of the world.

They are called two and two, because charity is not save between two. A calling is spoken of: either according to the election of grace. Whence it is said: *Whom he called [n: (Rom. VIII)]*, etc. Or according to that word: *Many are called [n: (Matth. X)]*.

[n: (AUG.)] It is asked how Matthew and Mark say that Jesus called them two by two: first Peter and Andrew, etc., *as far as* but he sent unlettered fishermen [0088B] to preach for this reason, that faith might be reckoned to stand in the power of God, not in the eloquence of men.

*Of Zebedee, and John.* Zebedee, a fugitive, that is, the devil. James, a supplanter, who is helped by John, that is, by the grace of God. *And he called them. But they,* etc. He called others also, but it suffices to name these.

VERS. 23.-- *And Jesus went about all Galilee.* His fellow workers being called, he presses on with preaching, teaching that a teacher ought to be untiring. *Teaching in the synagogues.* Teaching about morals, those subject to the active life. Preaching about things to come, those perfect in the contemplative life. Teaching the natural righteousnesses, namely chastity and humility and the like, which man has by nature. Preaching the Gospel of the kingdom. To the Gospel belongs [0088C] the mystery of righteousness, a good beginning, the promise of beatitude, the remission of sins, adoption, resurrection, the heavenly inheritance, the fellowship of the angels.

*In the synagogues.* Nor let an idle teacher run about to no purpose, but let him preach where there are many.

*The Gospel.* Because righteousness is a trouble to one living in the flesh, the labor of righteousness is not undertaken unless the wage of the work be looked for. Therefore Christ preaches beatitude for good news, that he may stir men up to patience in good work.

*And healing.* To cure sickness and infirmity was no great thing, since they were to die afterwards. But it was done for this reason, that they might so be raised up to the kingdom. Morally instructing preachers to minister earthly supports also to their subjects, and so to draw them to the kingdom.

VERS. 24.-- *And his fame went abroad. [n: (BEDA.)]* The works of salvation [0088D] do not shine out enough to hearers without the fame of a good odor, nor does fame profit without the work: without which, etc., *as far as* he cures bodies, that they may the more readily hear invisible things. Otherwise it was a small thing to spend care upon men soon to die.

VERS. 25.-- *They followed. [n: (AUG.)]* Fourfold crowds, namely. Some, etc., *as far as* others, wishing out of envy to accuse him and to catch him in something.
