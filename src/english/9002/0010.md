## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- *And Jesus seeing. [n: (AUG.)]* With this Luke agrees, saying: *And it came to pass in those days, he went out into a mountain to pray [n: (Luc. VI)]*, etc. But Luke says, etc., *as far as* for not all men can do all things: [0089A] which is thought the truer, although the same day may be seen to have been made by both at once.

*He went up.* Teaching his own to go up and not to remain in the crowd: being about to teach a higher righteousness than that which is of the Scribes and the Pharisees. He himself, a mountain, went up into a mountain, that he might teach the higher summits of the virtues, and might show that the Church, above which he sits preaching, is to be raised more loftily by the Lord's precept, which the same teaching would more fully instruct unto the end of the world. He leads the apostles apart more eminently, that from the mountain the mountains may first receive peace for the people. *He was set down.* The sitting is of the humanity, by which he is known. Whence: *And the world knew him not,* before the incarnation. *They came.* That they may hear the nearer, who hasten in mind to fulfil.

[0089B]VERS. 2.-- *And opening.* The opening of the mouth signifies the depth of the sacrament. He opened as it were an abyss, as it were a treasure, as it were a river of paradise. On this mountain he writes the New Testament in the hearts of sons, beginning from beatitude, who on mount Sinai gave the Old Testament on stones to servants, beginning from terror.

[n: (AUG.)] The sermon which our Lord spoke on the mountain, as in the Gospel, etc., *as far as, Everyone who hears these my words and shall do them, I will liken him to a wise man [n: (Matth. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 7:24 *Omnis ergo qui audit verba mea haec, et facit ea, assimilabitur viro sapienti*]

VERS. 3.-- *Blessed. [n: (GREG. NYSS.)]* Beatitude, as my opinion carries it, is the comprehension of all those things which, etc., *as far as* for misery is a wretchedness in bitter and sorrowful chances [0089C] and calamities that do not fall out by our own will.

*Blessed are the poor.* The number of the sentences is to be diligently attended to. For beatitude begins from humility.

*[n: (HIER.)] Blessed are the poor in spirit.* Because it is not necessity but faith, and the devotion of poverty, that makes men blessed, etc., *as far as* as the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, so poverty is the beginning of beatitude.

[n: (CHRYSOST.)] The poor in spirit are the humble and contrite of heart. And by spirit he here means the soul and the will. But because many are humble not of their own accord, but constrained by necessity, he calls the first sort blessed.

VERS. 4.-- *Blessed are the meek.* The meek are those who master evil habits: perfect virtue is the possession of one's habits.

[0089D] [n: (THEOPH.)] The meek are not those who are not angry at all, etc., *as far as* so the doctrine of a man is discerned through patience.

*They shall possess the land. [n: (AUG.)]* That land I believe to be the one of which it is said in the Psalm: *Thou art my hope, my portion in the land of the living [n: (Psal. CLXI)]*.

VERS. 5.-- *Blessed are they that mourn.* For their own sins or for others', which mourning is from the lower watering, but that which comes from the upper watering is out of desire for the heavenly country. The one washes present filth, the other kindles more sharply the lovers of eternal things. But he who has laid down his sins, has corrected his habits by meekness, and has wept besides, can now hunger and thirst after righteousness, which before he could not. This [0090A] virtue is not fulfilled without the spirit of fortitude: because through him, by hungering, we shall one day come to fulness. *Blessed are they that mourn.* It remains, after the things aforesaid, to purge by mourning the foulness of our ruins. Mourning for sins or out of heavenly desire earns consolation, and no other does.

These are enlightened by the spirit of knowledge, that they may know in what evils they are wrapped.

[n: (HILAR.)] For those that mourn, not for those grieving over bereavements, or insults, or losses, but for those weeping over their old sins, and made wretched by the consciousness of the crimes with which we are befouled, this assiduous consolation is prepared in heaven.

VERS. 6.-- *Blessed are they that hunger.* Lovers of the good word, to whom it is not enough that they are just, but they always thirst for the work of righteousness.

[0090B]Righteousness is to render to each his own, to oneself, and to one's neighbors, and to God. This righteousness will not be fully fulfilled until God be all in all: therefore here we can hunger, not be filled. The light of righteousness is mercy, the strength of mercy is righteousness; this mercy has need of the spirit of counsel, without which no one is merciful with circumspection. The beginning is to have mercy on oneself, the end is to die for another. For the order of showing mercy is that each man begin from himself. Christ hints at the end, having died out of mercy for sinners.

VERS. 7.-- *Blessed are the merciful.* Mercy is born of the things that go before, because if true humility has gone before, and the mind grows meek, and weeps for its own falls and others', and hungers after righteousness, afterwards true mercy is born. For then it will make others' miseries its own [0090C] and will help according to its powers: and if the means of helping is wanting, compassion will not be wanting: *Be ye merciful as your Father also is merciful [n: (Luc. VI)]*, that is, let us have mercy unto that end unto which God also has mercy, that goodness may overflow upon all: mercy without righteousness is called remission and lukewarmness; righteousness without mercy is called severity and harshness.

VERS. 9.-- *Blessed are the peacemakers. [n: (RAB.)]* The peacemakers are those who compose all the motions of the mind and subject them to reason, because nothing resists those who are like the Father, etc., *as far as* but when God shall be all in all, then the beatitude of adoption will be given.

VERS. 10.-- *Blessed are they that suffer persecution.* In the seven aforesaid there is the showing and the proving of perfection. Those who have the aforesaid things can suffer persecution: and [0090D] he rightly holds the aforesaid things which he has received who is ready to die for them.

VERS. 11.-- *Blessed are you when they shall revile you.* Thus far he set down his sentences generally, as though to those absent, although they belonged to those present also, now he turns his words to them (although this belongs to others also), foretelling how great things they should suffer for his name: because he wished to make known to them the dignity of their rank, saying: *You are the salt of the earth, you are the light of the world;* that from the sad things they might take humility, from the glad things consolation.

*Shall revile.* The ancient translation has: *When men shall hate you, and shall say all that is evil, and shall cast you out.* Yet the sense is the same in ours, since revilings come from hatred of the heart. He who chooses the glory of heaven does not [0091A] fear reproach on earth; as much as each man rejoices at the praise of men, so much does he grieve at reproach. Him whom praise lifts up, blame casts down: where a man seeks to be praised only before God, he does not fear to be confounded in the sight of men. *Lying.* He adds this, lest he glory of whom evil things are truly said. He adds also: *For my sake,* because sometimes false things are said, which comes about through the rashness of men, yet they do not suffer this for Christ's sake, whom they do not follow.

VERS. 12.-- *For your reward is very great in heaven.* Not in rolling things, since an eternal reward ought not to be in rolling things, but in spiritual firmaments, where everlasting righteousness dwells. Already therefore they feel this reward who rejoice in spiritual goods. But it will be made perfect when this mortal shall put on [0091B] immortality. *For so they persecuted,* etc. He tempers the rigor of the passion by an example. Persecution is made in three ways: by hatred of the heart, by words, by deeds.

VERS. 13.-- *You are the salt of the earth.* Here now is shown the office of the apostles, namely that they are the salt of the earth and the light of the world. This belongs especially to the apostles, who ought to be adorned with the aforesaid virtues, namely poverty and the rest, that they may be the best salt, seasoning others by doctrine and by the example of their own life. Salt, health from corruption. Whence the prophet: *I have set thee for a light of the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation unto the ends of the earth.* [var: Vulg. Is 49:6 *dedi te in lucem gentium, ut sis salus mea usque ad extremum terrae*; Act 13:47 has *Posui te in lucem gentium, ut sis in salutem*]

*But if the salt,* etc. That is, if you, by whom others are to be seasoned, give way before adverse or prosperous things: by whom shall error be taken away from you, when God has chosen [0091C] to raise you up for others? It is good for nothing: because (as another evangelist says) it is useful neither to the earth, which by being cast upon it it hinders from sprouting, nor to the dunghill, which it does not suffer to grow fruitful. So he who goes backward neither bears fruit himself, nor is able to till others, but is cast out of the Church, and is laughed at in these words: *This man began to build, and was not able to finish. If the salt shall lose its savor [n: (Luc. IV)]*. That is, if through fear or through greed the teacher has let go the savor of wisdom, by whom shall his foolishness be amended? This befits the apostles alone: what follows befits all masters: *It is good for nothing any more,* etc. At the end he concludes thus concerning all: *Unless your righteousness abound,* etc.

*And be trodden upon by men.* The holy and true minister of Christ, even if he is laughed at by the wicked, cannot [0091D] be trodden upon, because fixed in mind he remains in heaven.

VERS. 14.-- *You are the light of the world.* Salt before light, because life before doctrine: life leads to the knowledge of truth. He who fears God does not lack knowledge.

*The light of the world.* There is another cause why they ought not to fail: because they are enlightened by Christ in word and in example: wherever they are carried among worldly men they ought to minister the light of faith and of knowledge. And as (because they are salt) they ought to drive away corruption: so also they ought to put darkness (because they are light) to flight.

*A city cannot,* etc. Because the apostles are foundations, they are fitly called a city. *Upon a mountain.* The place hints at the mountain of righteousness, which is lofty, whereby you are made public [0092A] so that you may not hide yourselves. For there is no power of slipping away or of doing anything in secret; therefore rule your subjects mercifully within by counsel, and without against adversaries defend them by fighting for righteousness: which belongs to the spirit of counsel and of fortitude.

VERS. 15.-- *Neither do they light a lamp.* By household examples he teaches them not to hide the light of the word committed to them. *They do not light,* that is, the doctrine of preaching was not given for this, that they should hide it and subject it to the servitude of the body, while they fear certain things. But let them subject their body to the ministry of God, that through the voice and the other offices of the body the light may appear more excellent than the rest. Whence it is said [n: (I Cor. IX)]: *I chastise my body,* etc. Or, Christ lit a lamp, when he filled the earthen shell of human nature with the flame of his divinity: [0092B] which he neither hid from believers, nor shut up under a bushel, that is, the measure of the law, or within the bounds of a single nation. But upon the candlestick, that is, the Church, he fastened the faith of his incarnation, where all can behold it who are in the house, that is, in the Church, or in the world. Whence he adds: *So let your light shine,* etc. *A candlestick.* As Christ is the lamp, the candlestick is the cross, upon which he was to be lifted up. The candlestick is the Church, which bears the word of the present life.

VERS. 16.-- *So let your light shine,* etc. And do all these things in such a way that you do not set the end of your good work in the praises of men. *That they may see,* etc. I require works, that they may be seen, and that so the doctrine may be confirmed: and this not to your own glory.

VERS. 17.-- *Do not think.* After he had exhorted them [0092C] to suffer all things for the truth, and not to hide it, and to work the glory of God, he begins to teach what they are to teach. As if he were saying: What is this that thou forbiddest to be hidden, for which thou biddest all things to be suffered? is there anything against the law? *I am not come to destroy the law, but to fulfil,* [var: Vulg. Mt 5:17 *non veni solvere, sed adimplere*, without *legem* in the second clause] either by doing what it says, or by supplying what was lacking and imperfect.

VERS. 18.-- *One jot. [n: (BEDA.)]* Here it is shown that the things which are thought least in the law are full of spiritual sacraments, and all things, etc., *as far as* because the jot signifies ten, that is, the decalogue, whose apex is the perfection of the Gospel.

*All things be fulfilled.* Let them be spiritually fulfilled, or let the imperfect be perfected, and let the things that signify be consummated, that is, by the fulfilment.
