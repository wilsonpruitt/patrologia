## CHAPTER V. (cont.)

[0092D]VERS. 19.-- *He therefore that shall break,* etc. The least things are the cross of Christ and his passion, and he who is ashamed of them is least; he who confesses them is great. Or he marks the Pharisees, who let go the commandments of God for their own traditions, which are worth nothing, while they pass over even the least thing that is in the law. But morally he breaks it who does not fulfil it, but holds it as these things were given to the weak. Or he who does not understand it spiritually. To break is: not to do what one rightly understands, or not to understand what one does not distort, or to lessen the wholeness of Christ's superadding.

*He shall be called the least,* etc. In the kingdom of heaven he shall be called least who will be unworthy in it, in which there is none least but all are great.

[0093A] *But he that shall do.* He who shall not break it, and so teaches, is not least, yet not great: but he does what he teaches. *For only the doers of the law are just before God [n: (Rom. II)]*. [var: Vulg. Rom 2:13 *Non enim auditores legis justi sunt apud Deum, sed factores legis justificabuntur*]

VERS. 20.-- *But I say to you.* [var: Vulg. Mt 5:20 *Dico enim vobis*] When he says, I say, he struck in a great force and a deep understanding for thinking: he shows how he superadds. And he does not undo the things he had commanded before. It is even more to love enemies than not to hate them: not to covet, than not to commit adultery: not to be angry, than not to kill.

*Unless,* etc. That is, you shall fulfil not only the least precepts of the law, but also those which I add: because while the things added are done unto perfection, these too are more fully performed which were set down before unto a beginning: [0093B] because he who does not look upon a woman to lust after her, neither does he commit fornication: nor does he kill who is not angry.

*Your righteousness.* He calls the righteousness of the Pharisees all those things actually commanded for the worship of God: or the mystical things of the figures, established only according to the letter.

VERS. 21.-- *You have heard,* etc. He sets forth what the righteousness of the Pharisees is, and he who fulfils it is not at once great, but has climbed some step.

VERS. 22.-- *But I,* etc. He begins to unfold the perfection which he came to fulfil, and how their righteousness ought to abound the more.

*Everyone who is angry.* Because they did not know that anything was murder except a killing: he discloses that every motion of the mind toward doing harm is reckoned as murder. The commandment of Christ is not contrary to the law, but more broadly [0093C] contains the law in itself. He who is not angry does not kill. But not conversely: the leave to be angry is the cause of murder. Take away anger, and murder is not committed.

VERS. 23.-- *If therefore thou offer thy gift.* He unfolds the fuller righteousness by showing that if it is not lawful to be angry with a brother, much less is it lawful to keep in the mind that which seeks to be turned into hatred. If we have anything of evil in the mind against a neighbor, we cannot offer to God in a way that pleases him. *If therefore thou offer.* According to the letter this is absurd. There is therefore in the inner temple an altar, that is, faith, upon which any gift whatsoever, that is, a psalm, a hymn, a prayer, and the like, unless it rest, will not be pleasing to God. *And there,* etc., that is, when in the heart (which is the inner temple) thou art about to offer such a gift, if it should come into thy mind [0093D] that thy brother has something against thee, whom thou hast wronged. For if he has wronged thee, it is not he, but thou who hast something against him: nor is it needful for thee then to seek pardon, but to give it, as thou wishest to be forgiven thyself.

VERS. 24.-- *Leave there,* etc. He does not say, Carry it back: because what is rightly offered may not be changed. But leave it, until thou mayest offer it more acceptably: for he does not drive back the gift, but seeks charity toward one's neighbor, without which no one comes to the charity of God. Whence: Let him first pay charity to his neighbor who desires his gift to please God. *Go.* Not with the feet, but in mind mayest thou humbly prostrate thyself before him in the sight of him to whom thou art about to offer. Or by seeking pardon if he is present. *Thou shalt offer.* This is from the spirit of piety, whereby a man becomes meek, and understands the divine word [0094A] piously, and by understanding obeys, and lifts up his heart to fulfil it.

VERS. 25.-- *Be at agreement.* As he has already commanded a man to be reconciled to his brother, and afterwards bids that enemies be loved, so here, while we run in the way of this world, he exhorts to peace and concord, as it is said: *If it be possible, as much as is in you, having peace with all men [n: (Rom. XII)]*. And in place of "consenting" the Greek has "kindly" or "well-disposed." *Quickly,* lest thou put it off till the morrow: *While thou art in the way,* while it is still lawful to work.

If thou hast wronged a brother, be reconciled to him. If an adversary has arisen, be well-disposed, that he may become a friend.

*In the way with him.* The divine word is with us in the way of Christ. The adversary is with us in the way of the world. [0094B] The divine word is to us the way in which we run. Whence: *I have run the way of thy commandments, when thou didst enlarge my heart [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*.

*Lest perhaps he deliver thee up.* He so tempered it, that it should not bring in a place of repentance: for it might be thought that if the adversary had died and one could not be well-disposed to him in the way, he would necessarily be delivered up to the judge. But he both left a place of repentance, and delivered the impenitent up to the judge, saying: Let the judge deliver thee to the officer. *Let him deliver thee up,* that is, let him be the cause why thou art delivered into the hands of Christ, to whom the Father has given all judgment. Christ to the officer, that is, to the angel, who shall gather the cockle.

VERS. 26.-- *The last farthing.* That is, small sins, because nothing will be unpunished. Or by the last farthing are signified earthly sins, [0094C] because the first part of the world is heaven; the second, air; the third water; the fourth, and last, earth. Therefore until thou repay the last farthing, that is, until thou pay for the sins which thou hast contracted from the earth.

VERS. 27.-- *You have heard. [n: (RAB.)]* Still he who said: *I am not come to destroy the law, but to fulfil [n: (Marc. XI)]*. He adds: how much it ought to abound, etc., *as far as* when the soul, having left the higher law, is corrupted, prostituted by a base will for lower things.

*Shall look upon a woman. [n: (BEDA.)]* A great superadding. Sight is put for every motion which is, etc., *as far as* because a man is accused or defended out of his conscience not according to what he was able to do and was not permitted, but according to what he willed and purposed.

*To lust after her.* That it may pass over into the affection of the heart, [0094D] so that he sets himself to do it, where the will is not wanting, but the occasion: which is called passion, which is death in the house. Pro-passion is a sudden affection of the mind, either of anger or of love. Passion is a form or deliberation of the mind out of consent. Whence a man is called wrathful or a lover.

[n: (ISID.)] If contemplation turns into weariness or into arrogance, let it go, and pass over to the active life: because it is better to be saved through the active than to perish through the contemplative.

VERS. 29.-- *Pluck it out.* According to the letter no member is commanded to be plucked out, but the inner sight, which is called the right, and the outer, which is called the left, if it hurts, is to be called back or removed by the zeal of dutiful labor: and [0095A] even to be put far away, so that it may not so much as be held in memory. The eye is plucked out when an evil habit is broken. It is cast away when it is wholly brought to nothing, nor is it suffered to enter.

VERS. 30.-- *Thy right hand.* Prosperous working, or some vigorous helper within the business of life: and if he is a scandal in the law of God, it is better to be saved with him cut off than to be damned along with him. *Cut it off,* etc. If anyone preaches this of the bodily members, he ought not to be listened to out of any affection of dutifulness. But that from thence the wickedness of habits and the crookedness of actions may be cut away, so that whatever in the eye of the mind overturns us from the way of righteousness and from uprightness of habits, and whatever beats without against the working of the virtues, may be driven far off. *Into hell.* The name of gehenna is not found among the ancients: but is first set down by the [0095B] Lord. The occasion of that name is this. The idol of Baal was beside Jerusalem at the foot of mount Moria, where Siloe was. This valley was well-watered and wooded and full of delights, and a place in it was consecrated to idols: but the people of Israel went so far in madness that, leaving the temple, they sacrificed there, and burned their own sons to demons. And that place was called Gehennon, that is, the valley of Ennon. And God threatens that he will fill that place with corpses, so that it may be called polyandrum, that is, a burial-mound of the dead: by the name of this place therefore the punishments to come are designated.

VERS. 31.-- *Let him give her. [n: (AUG.)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 5:31 *det ei libellum repudii*] Moses wrote in Deuteronomy that if a wife does not please her husband on account of some foulness, he should put her away. Which Christ expounds, etc., *as far as* as he loves an enemy, not in [0095C] this, that he is an enemy and evil, but that he is a man, so that he may be saved along with him.

[n: (CHRYSOST.)] Moses permitted a bill of divorce to be given, lest for hatred's sake blood should be shed. He permitted evils to be done, etc., *as far as* because we cannot forbid to the full the evil wills of men.

VERS. 32.-- *By reason of fornication.* Because in this the faith of chastity and of wedlock is broken: and he does not make her commit adultery, but she herself is the cause of her own guilt. But if he puts her away for fornication, he ought to be free from fornication himself, nor is it lawful to marry another while she lives. *By reason of fornication.* Of the woman, who has committed fornication, or of the man, lest he commit fornication, namely if the wife compels him to idolatry, or to avarice, or to other unlawful lusts. For fornication is not only of defilement, [0095D] but generally that which makes a man stray from the law of God. *And he that,* etc. [n: (CHRYSOST.)] Do not say that her husband has put her away, because even after she is put away, she remains the wife of him who put her away.

VERS. 33.-- *But thou shalt render.* If it should happen that thou swear: thou shalt swear by the Creator: not by a creature. For each man venerates that by which he swears: this he loves, this he fears. Therefore the law commands that no oath be sworn save by God.

*But I,* etc. He teaches that there is nothing so cheap among creatures that a man ought to forswear himself by it, since he forbids the oath from the highest to the lowest, that is, from heaven to a hair. He is convicted of forswearing himself against God, whose throne is heaven, and whose is whatever is.

[0096A] *By heaven.* They either supposed heaven to be something more, or, swearing by it, did not think themselves held by an oath: therefore he so tempers the sentence, that heaven may be believed to be a creature of God, and not something more: and that, because it is his throne, perjury may be feared. So also concerning the earth, because it is the footstool of his feet. He therefore who swears by a creature owes the oath to him who created and rules it, nor is it rightly thought to be paid to another.

Let us not swear by creatures so as to venerate them beyond what we ought by them, so as to esteem them cheap, namely so that when we swear by them we should think we swear by nothing.

*Nor by the earth.* Others swear by the safety of Pharao, who by the judgment of God is set among the lowest. *There is no power but from God [n: (Rom. XIII)]*: and in this thou swearest by God.

[0096B] *Nor,* etc. Because it is not thy work, but God's: and so in the least things, by swearing by God, thou canst incur perjury.

He who swears by his head owes it to him whose image and power abides in it.

VERS. 36.-- *For thou canst not,* etc. Since these things aforesaid are hard and difficult, no one keeps them without the spirit of fortitude. He drives men to perfection, while he takes away the occasion of swearing.

VERS. 37.-- *Yea, yea; no, no.* He says it twice for this reason, that what thou sayest with the mouth thou mayest prove by works. What thou deniest in words, thou mayest not approve or confirm by deeds.
