## CHAPTER V. (cont.)

He did not altogether forbid swearing, but the occasion of perjury. He taught what is more perfect, showing that what goes beyond is of evil. The Apostle swears, that he may persuade [0096C] to faith: the Church too, for the sake of a covenant of peace and of faith, grants her own to swear. But Christ taught what is more perfect, indulged what belonged to weakness, cut away what was superstitious.

The truth of the Gospel does not admit an oath, but another's weakness compels it, so long as he does not believe the truth.

*It is of evil.* Not indeed evil, because although not good, yet necessary, but it is of evil, not of thine, but of his by whom thou art compelled to swear, that is, of that weakness of his which is an evil.

VERS. 38.-- *You have heard. [n: (AUG.)]* The law appointed a measure of vengeance: an eye for an eye. But if it can be said, etc., *as far as* but out of love of justice, the just for the unjust, which is good for evil, which the judge also does.

[0096D]As to what he says, *an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth:* he does not wish to take away the one, but to preserve both, while he threatens retaliation. Which he also does with measure, that the vengeance may not exceed the injury: and this is the beginning of peace, and as it were the commencement of justice.

VERS. 39.-- *Not to resist.* The justice of the law instructs the untaught in the beginning of justice, that is, to repay no more than was inflicted. The justice of the Gospel (which leads men to the kingdom) is not to resist evil: *Not to resist:* as he himself likewise: *Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart [n: (Matth. XI)]*. And David: *If I have repaid them that repaid me evils [n: (Psal. VII)]*. Jerem. *And I have given my cheek to him that struck me [n: (Thren. III)]* [var: Vulg. Lam 3:30 *Dabit percutienti se maxillam*, "He shall give his cheek to him that striketh him"]. Behold, the same Lord of the law and of the Gospel.

[0097A] *Offer to him.* Not only shouldst thou not strike back, but if he wishes to strike the other cheek, bear it patiently: this concerning injury to the body. But as to necessary things, if thou hast lost one, allow the remainder also to be taken away. What then of things superfluous?

*The other. [n: (CHRYSOST.)]* First, it is an unworthy thing that a faithful man [sic: *nomo*] should stand in judgment before the sight of an unbelieving judge. For even if he be an unbeliever, etc., *as far as* If once thou hast entered into judgment, thou dost no longer desire that the truth of the cause should appear, but that in whatever way thou mayest come off victor.

VERS. 40.-- *And to him who,* etc. It is counsel, before thou contend. It is indeed lawful for the weak to demand back their own, but not to contend. Luke conversely: He that taketh away thy garment, forbid not thy coat also. Which is to be done not concerning these things alone, but concerning all temporal things. *Thy [0097B] coat.* Or, the coat is the soul, which if a man be unwilling to lose, let him give up his cloak, that is, his body to martyrdom. Spiritually, the teacher takes away the coat when he makes a man confess his inward vices, such as envy, pride, and the like. He adds: *And the cloak;* he who does not blush to confess the outward ones also, such as theft and fornication.

VERS. 41.-- *A thousand paces.* This is a sabbath day's journey, by which spiritual perfection is signified: because it is not lawful to do servile work on the sabbath. *Other two.* He goes other two also who, while he is reduced to servitude in body only, is ready to be reduced in spirit and soul, that is, in reason and in will.

VERS. 42.-- *Give to him.* Such things as can be given honourably and justly. If the means be wanting, give the affection by a word or by a service. [0097C] *Give to him.* So, namely, that it hurt neither thee nor another: for justice is to be weighed. For thus wilt thou give to everyone that asketh, and if not that which he asks, yet something better, when thou shalt have corrected him that asks unjustly. *And to him that would borrow.* He embraces two kinds of bestowing. For either we give of good will, or we lend to one who will repay. And sometimes he who gives is unwilling to lend, as though there were no reward in it when the debtor pays back the thing received. But truly he who commands even this, repays. He says therefore: *Turn not away,* that is, nor alienate thy will. *For God loveth a cheerful giver [n: (II Cor. IX)]*, and so the spiritual teacher ought to put out to his hearers the interest of the divine word, that he may be able to receive from them the recompense of a good work, and to hope for a reward from God.

VERS. 43.-- *You have heard.* He who came to fulfil the law leads at the [0097D] end to full charity, which is the fulfilling of the law.

*Thou shalt hate.* Perhaps it is to be understood according to what is above: *An eye for an eye,* etc. Or by withdrawing the offices of humanity.

VERS. 44.-- *Love.* The Church is fought against in three ways: by hatred, by words, by the torment of the body. The Church on the contrary loves, does good, prays. This is the new commandment, this makes, out of sons of wrath, sons of God; whence there follows: *That you may be sons.* The adoption of sons is acquired by charity alone.

*Do good.* By ministering food to the mind or to the body. Which he also does who says that such a one is not even to take food. For all these things look to this, that they may become brethren. [0098A] Single things are set against single, that all the parts of malice may be overcome by goodness. The imprecations of the prophets seem to be against this, which are not uttered by the wish of those desiring, but by the spirit of those foreseeing.

*Pray. [n: (AUG.)]* He does not say this for brethren, in whom there are sometimes sins graver than the persecution of enemies, etc., *as far as* although they confess that they have sinned, and vehemently repent of having done evil, yet they have such dispositions of mind that they do not humble themselves to ask pardon.

But it may move one, that John in his Epistle seems to admonish, concerning the love of God and of the neighbour, only that we should love the brethren, whereas the Lord says that prayer is to be made even for enemies. But by brethren are understood not only those who already believe, but those who are to believe, for whom [0098B] prayer must be made, that they may be joined to us by a brotherly covenant.

VERS. 45.-- *Sons.* Not degenerate. As if he should say: Having received the power, act by works that you may be sons by being born again through charity. *Of the Father.* He is already called Father. See that you be sons born again of God, who is charity. *Who maketh his sun to rise.* This visible one. For they say in the book of Solomon, *The sun of justice hath not shined upon us [n: (Sap. V)]* [var: Vulg. Sap 5:6 *justitiae lumen non luxit nobis*, "the light of justice hath not shined upon us"]. And of the rain of doctrine the prophet says: *Upon one city it rained, and not upon another. His sun [n: (Amos. IV)]* [var: Vulg. Am 4:7 *et plui super unam civitatem, et super alteram civitatem non plui*, "and I rained upon one city, and upon another city I rained not"], etc. As if he should say: Whom he made, whose properly are all the things which he created out of nothing. What then shall we do concerning those things which we have received of his gift?

VERS. 46.-- *The publicans.* The exactors of the Romans, whom the Jews so call, because they bore the charge of the commonwealth. They are also said to have drawn their name from Publius, a Roman [0098C] king, who first appointed them.

VERS. 47.-- *And if you salute.* If you shall have prayed for those only who are joined to you by some kinship, what more has your benefit than that of unbelievers? Brethren must be loved, and humbly saluted: but it is little.

VERS. 48.-- *Perfect.* In the love of God and of the neighbour. The crown of perfection is to love even enemies, and to pray for them, as Christ also did.
