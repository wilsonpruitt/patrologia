## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Take heed,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He who hitherto has given precept concerning mercy, now gives precept concerning cleanness of heart, by which God is seen. ⟦s3⟧ For if not with a simple intention but for the praise of men, the works [0098D] of mercy are not approved by God. ⟦s4⟧ The praise of men ought not to be sought, but to follow, that by example it may invite others. ⟦s5⟧ Thus far concerning the justice of the Gospel, which is above the justice of the Scribes and Pharisees. ⟦s6⟧ Now with what cleanness of heart that same justice ought to be fulfilled: which is observed in almsgiving and in prayer. ⟦s7⟧ For he does not here admonish either to pray or to give alms: but with what mind we ought to do it. ⟦s8⟧ For the matter here is of cleansing the heart. ⟦s9⟧ *With your Father.* ⟦s10⟧ Whose glory sons ought to seek, and to refer all good things to this, that they may please him.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *When therefore,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ From the whole to the parts, that he may set forth what good things are not to be done for the praise of men. ⟦s3⟧ *To sound a trumpet.* ⟦s4⟧ Perhaps they did this for the sake of calling men together: that all might go as if to a spectacle. [0099A] *Before thee.* ⟦s5⟧ He sounds a trumpet before himself who wishes to be praised before others: that he may appear greater than they. ⟦s6⟧ *They have received.* ⟦s7⟧ From God, the inspector of the heart, the punishment of their deceit, of whom it is said: *Depart from me, you crafty workers: for you have had my name: but you have not done my works.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Let it not know.* ⟦s2⟧ The left hand, vices: the right hand, virtues. ⟦s3⟧ What therefore virtue does, let not elation know, nor vainglory, nor any vice: but let the light of right working put to flight the darkness of sin.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *In secret.* ⟦s2⟧ That alms is done in secret which is mingled with no darkness: the light is within, the darkness without. ⟦s3⟧ *He seeth in secret.* ⟦s4⟧ To see, in God, is to approve. ⟦s5⟧ But he approves only those things which are hidden, the things which are without do not please him, neither does he approve them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0099B]VERS. 5.-- *And when you pray.* ⟦s2⟧ This sentence is joined to what goes before. ⟦s3⟧ He had said above: *Take heed that you do not your justice before men;* here he adds: *And when you pray.* ⟦s4⟧ He does not forbid to pray, but to be like the hypocrites in praying. ⟦s5⟧ *In the corners.* ⟦s6⟧ As it were hiders of prayers, and yet in the streets that they may be seen, and by this a fuller glory. ⟦s7⟧ Allegorically. ⟦s8⟧ He prays in a corner who feigns to pray in secret: and yet in the street, when he catches at fame. ⟦s9⟧ The corners of the streets are where a way is led through by the crossing of a way, and makes a crossroads. ⟦s10⟧ For it is not wrong to be seen, but to desire to be seen. ⟦s11⟧ *They have received.* ⟦s12⟧ In these men not only is the true reward emptied out by a depraved intention: but the vice of dissimulation and of mockery against God is increased.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *But thou.* ⟦s2⟧ This, simply understood, [0099C] teaches that the vainglory of the world is to be fled. ⟦s3⟧ But it seems rather to be enjoined that, the thought of the breast being enclosed and the lips pressed together, one should pray to God. ⟦s4⟧ Now the chamber is the secret place of the heart. ⟦s5⟧ The door, the carnal sense through which these outward things shamelessly thrust themselves in, and the crowds of phantasms make a din against those who pray. ⟦s6⟧ It is little therefore to enter the chamber, if the door stands open to importunate thoughts. ⟦s7⟧ But the carnal sense must be resisted, that spiritual prayer may be directed to God out of the inmost parts of the heart. ⟦s8⟧ *The door being shut.* ⟦s9⟧ The senses being shut, lest the crowds of outward phantasms should meet him. ⟦s10⟧ Or he calls the door the love or the fear of this world. ⟦s11⟧ For as every good thing is of the fear and love of God, so every evil is of the love and fear of the world. ⟦s12⟧ These being shut, that is, taken away, pray.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0099D] [n: (CHRYSOST.)] What then, sayest thou? ought one not to pray in the church? ⟦s2⟧ And very much indeed, but with this will, that thou look upon that one alone who has the power of granting what thou shalt have asked. ⟦s3⟧ For God everywhere beholds with what regard thou doest anything.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The Father.* ⟦s2⟧ Whom thou hast within by faith; whence: *With me is prayer to the God of my life [n: (Psal. XLI)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *But praying.* ⟦s2⟧ As it is of hypocrites to be shown and to please men, so it is of the Gentiles to use much speaking, from whom also it began. ⟦s3⟧ For these give more pains to exercising the tongue than to cleansing the mind: so that they transfer this kind of trifling office even to the bending of God by prayer, thinking that as an orator bends a judge, so they too can bend God.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0100A] [n: (CHRYSOST.)] If we must pray in few words, why did Luke set forth a parable that we must always pray upon the mountain, etc., *as far as* If indeed both Christ and Paul commanded that prayers be made short, but frequent, at short intervals.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CASS.)] They (the Fathers) judge it more useful that prayers be short indeed, but very frequent, etc., *as far as* let us avoid by a concise brevity.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For they think.* ⟦s2⟧ Thinking that God is soothed by persuasive words. ⟦s3⟧ Or that if he is absent he is called: and that what he does not know he is taught by the windings of words.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Be not therefore like to them.* ⟦s2⟧ For with God the business is not to be done with words, but with things, with the intention of the heart, with a simple affection. ⟦s3⟧ Which things when we say, we do not teach him, but we ourselves remember at the time of praying. [0100B] *For the Father knoweth.* ⟦s4⟧ Why then do we speak to one who knows? ⟦s5⟧ Not that we may make a declaration of our will, but that we may stir up the service of a devout affection.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9. ⟦s2⟧ *Our Father. [n: (BEDA.)]* Among the other saving and divine admonitions with which he takes counsel for believers, he set forth a form of praying, etc., *as far as* and these are the true adorers, who as Sons by grace adore the Father in spirit and in truth.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No prayer is more spiritual than that which proceeded from the mouth of the Son, who is Truth. ⟦s2⟧ *Our Father.* ⟦s3⟧ Whom we serve not by fear, but by love of the inheritance. ⟦s4⟧ While he is called Father, let us think of adoption, that we may repay to the Father a dutiful affection. ⟦s5⟧ While it is said *our,* he is admonished of brotherhood, since the adoption is common to all. ⟦s6⟧ Let no one say "mine," which belongs properly to the Son, [0100C] to whom he is Father by nature. ⟦s7⟧ Whence elsewhere. ⟦s8⟧ *I go to my Father [n: (Joan. VII)]*, specially by nature: and yours, joined by grace. ⟦s9⟧ *Our Father is said,* which is common to all, let no one say "mine": which belongs to Christ alone, who is Son by nature; to others is granted the common grace of adoption.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] The noble and the rich man is admonished not to be proud, etc., *as far as* What will he deny to sons, who has already given that he is Father?

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Nowhere is a precept found for the people of Israel, that it should say: *Our Father,* or should pray, etc., *as far as* if they did not stray from his commandments. ⟦s2⟧ As is that: *I have begotten sons and exalted them [n: (Isa. I)]* [var: Vulg. Is 1:2 *Filios enutrivi, et exaltavi*, "I have brought up children, and exalted them"], etc

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the heavens.* ⟦s2⟧ The spiritual ones, that we may learn to hasten to the spirituality in which [0100D] he dwells, and may seek heavenly things from him.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Hallowed be thy name.* ⟦s2⟧ The distinction of these seven petitions is to be observed. ⟦s3⟧ For since our life is carried on in time and eternal life is hoped for, the former are, etc., *as far as* for liberation makes free men, that is, sons, so that we may say in the spirit of adoption, Abba, Father.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Amen,* signifies that in all these petitions what is asked is granted by the Lord without doubt, if we do not neglect to keep the compact of the last condition. ⟦s2⟧ Whence he adds: *If you shall forgive,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *Hallowed be,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Here we pray for the spirit of wisdom, that we may not, first, in anything depart from the sanctification of the name: but that the name of the Father may be shown in the sons by manners and by life.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0101A]The name of the Father is in us, who are called sons: which we ask to be hallowed. ⟦s2⟧ Not that he himself may be holier, but that he may work in us an increase of his sanctification: for the sanctification of the sons is attributed to his name. ⟦s3⟧ So he is said to be hallowed as he is glorified and magnified in us: *Hallowed be,* that is, so as it is holy, let it be held by men: and so let God become known to them, that they may not think anything holier.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS.10.-- *Thy kingdom come.* ⟦s2⟧ Here the spirit of understanding is asked for, by which the heart is cleansed that God may be seen. ⟦s3⟧ The hardness and power of the devil is shut out, and in the future more perfectly, when he shall have made void all principality and power, and the kingdom shall be delivered to God the Father. ⟦s4⟧ It fittingly follows, that after the adoption of sons we should ask for the kingdom which is due to sons.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0101B]The kingdom of God always is: but let it come, that is, let it be made manifest to men by whom, as light by the blind, it is not seen, which no one will be able to be ignorant of, when the Son shall have come to judge. ⟦s2⟧ In which the saints also shall reign, as it is said, *Receive the kingdom which is prepared for you [n: (Matth. XXV)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 25:34 *possidete paratum vobis regnum*, "possess the kingdom prepared for you"], etc.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ By the spirit of understanding the future kingdom is declared to those entering in, and cleanness of heart is bestowed, through which God, reigning there, shall be seen as he is, where by the spirit of wisdom the seventh sanctification of his name shall be completed. ⟦s2⟧ In the perfection of the number six the coming of the kingdom is asked for, that we may all run into the perfect man: then, for those resting in him, may he himself be hallowed in us while we are hallowed in him. ⟦s3⟧ His rest we rightly ask to be hallowed in us, that being hallowed in him we may rest perpetually, in which sanctification the peace [0101C] of blessedness is made firm and full wisdom is given.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy will be done.* ⟦s2⟧ Here the spirit of counsel is asked for, by which the will of God is sought out, that it may be fulfilled by us.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The will of God on earth as in heaven is governed by the spirit of counsel. ⟦s2⟧ We too have need of the spirit of counsel: that the will of God may be directed in us, that we may be able to be merciful in deed. ⟦s3⟧ The will of God is always done: but we pray that the liberty of our choice may be joined by grace to his will, which is our sanctification and life everlasting. ⟦s4⟧ The will of God is the keeping of his commandments.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As in heaven.* ⟦s2⟧ As in the angels, so in men. ⟦s3⟧ Or as in the just, so in sinners converted. ⟦s4⟧ Or [0101D] as in Christ, so in the Church. ⟦s5⟧ Or as in mind I serve the law of God, so also in the flesh.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ According to the spirit we are heaven, according to the flesh we are earth. ⟦s2⟧ We pray therefore that as in heaven, that is, in the spirit, so on earth the will may be done: that flesh and spirit may be a spiritual man, of whom it is said: *My heart and my flesh have rejoiced in the living God.* ⟦s3⟧ Nor is it only prayed that we may will good things, but that the will may be done in us, that we may live to thee, not to ourselves. ⟦s4⟧ These three are had more perfectly in the future, but here meanwhile we pray in part, that afterwards they may be had more perfectly. ⟦s5⟧ The four following are the safeguards of the present life.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Bread.* ⟦s2⟧ The hunger of justice, that it may attain to fullness, is propped up by the spirit of fortitude. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore [0102A] we pray: Give us this day our daily bread: without which we should die of hunger.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Bread is the body of Christ, or the word of God, or God himself, of whom we daily have need, or that which is above all substances. ⟦s2⟧ *This is the living bread which came down from heaven [n: (Joan. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Jo 6:51 *Ego sum panis vivus, qui de caelo descendi*, "I am the living bread which came down from heaven"], the sublimity of whose nature is above all substances.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Our.* ⟦s2⟧ Because Christ has been made ours, and God the Father with Christ has given us all things. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore we pray that what is ours by gift may daily be given by effect.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Supersubstantial.* ⟦s2⟧ Or daily. ⟦s3⟧ Because *they that eat me shall yet hunger [n: (Eccl. XLIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Here daily, in the fatherland eternal. ⟦s5⟧ This bread ought continually to refresh us inwardly by faith and charity: because without it [0102B] no one can live. ⟦s6⟧ Or spiritual precepts are called bread, which must always be practised or kept.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And forgive,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ After food he asks pardon of sins, that he who is fed by God may live in God, and may come to eternal life.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ When we say: *Forgive us our debts:* we ask for the spirit of knowledge, by which we understand our offences, which without the spirit of knowledge are not understood. ⟦s2⟧ For knowledge belongs to the use of temporal things, which is a virtue in avoiding evils and seeking goods, that we may bewail our own contagions and those of others, and may desire good things.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As.* ⟦s2⟧ Behold with what caution we are bound, in which also he bends us mutually to mercy, and promises pardon to those who sin against him, and promises pardon to us and through [0102C] us to others.
