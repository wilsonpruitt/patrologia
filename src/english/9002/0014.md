## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ We ought not to deny pardon of a fault; whence: *When you pray, forgive if you have anything against any man: for otherwise your Father will not forgive you your sins.* [var: Vulg. Mc 11:25 *dimittite si quis habetis adversus aliquem : ut et Pater vester, qui in caelis est, dimittat vobis peccata vestra*, "forgive if you have anything against any man: that your Father also, who is in heaven, may forgive you your sins"]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is granted to demand money back from someone, but we never justly deny pardon to one asking it for the debts of sins.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And lead us not.* ⟦s2⟧ But enlighten us with the spirit of knowledge, that we may know by what evils we are entangled, and we ask that our tears may profit: *Forgive us our debts. ⟦s3⟧ And lead us not,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Not that he himself leads in, but because he deserts and permits us to go. ⟦s5⟧ Nor indeed does he pray not to be tempted, but not to be overcome even a little. ⟦s6⟧ For temptation is necessary for the crown. ⟦s7⟧ *And lead us not,* etc., deserting us [0102D] in temptation, do not suffer us to be overcome: even though thou suffer us to be tempted, without which no one is proved.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But deliver us from evil.* ⟦s2⟧ This one petition contains as much as all the foregoing.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ That by the spirit of the fear of God all hurtful things within and without may be driven away: and that under poverty we may live blessedly. ⟦s2⟧ *But deliver us.* ⟦s3⟧ Into that which is not yet, suffer us not to be led, and from that into which we have been led, deliver us: which being done, nothing is to be feared, nothing remains to be prayed for.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From evil,* from all evil, visible and invisible. ⟦s2⟧ This deliverance is daily necessary, since without evil of soul and body there is no living, but it shall be completed in the end, when we shall be immortal and impassible. ⟦s3⟧ From the lowest to the highest the ascent is thus made by degrees. ⟦s4⟧ From evil, through the spirit of fear, [0103A] we are delivered: thence being delivered we desire to be poor in spirit and in goods, while we say, Lead us not into temptation. ⟦s5⟧ We ask for the spirit of piety, that the inward man may grow gentle and become meek, lest he be moved by temptation.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *For if you shall forgive.* ⟦s2⟧ He has imposed a form of how we would be judged. ⟦s3⟧ Whence for those who pardon there answers before God a confidence of obtaining: but those who exact, the penalty of vengeance or of damnation bites again.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *But if you shall not forgive.* ⟦s2⟧ No excuse is left, because everyone shall be judged in the future according to this sentence. ⟦s3⟧ From this it is plain that everyone is condemned in his own unbelief.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *When you fast.* ⟦s2⟧ After he taught how [0103B] one must pray, he teaches concerning fasting, which again pertains to cleanness of heart, lest it be not simple toward God, while it is carried away to outward things. ⟦s3⟧ *Be not made.* ⟦s4⟧ He does not forbid the virtue, but the feigned appearance of virtue.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is good to be sorrowful by fasting for sins unto repentance, and to humble our soul; whence: *A contrite and humbled heart, O God, thou wilt not despise [n: (Psal. L)]*. ⟦s2⟧ But the sadness that is for praise, he forbids. ⟦s3⟧ Whence he does not say simply: Do not fast, but adds: like the hypocrites, sad.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They drive beyond bounds.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, they draw them beyond the bounds of the human condition. ⟦s3⟧ As there is boasting from the sheen of garments: so from excessive squalor and leanness. ⟦s4⟧ To demolish is as it were to destroy: and to reduce as it were to nothing the comeliness of the countenance. ⟦s5⟧ *Their faces.* ⟦s6⟧ With mournful filth in [0103C] which also there is boasting: and the more do they deceive under the appearance of religion.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Note that sheep ought not to lay aside their own skins, but to be clothed, if sometimes wolves cover themselves with them.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Anoint thy head.* ⟦s2⟧ Refresh thy mind with spiritual gladness on the days of abstinence: that the odour of love may be fragrant in works of mercy. ⟦s3⟧ Wash thy conscience with tears: that it may be clean before God. ⟦s4⟧ For the work of mercy does not suffice without a clean conscience: nor this without that. ⟦s5⟧ It was the custom among the Palestinians to anoint the head at feasts: therefore anoint thy head, that is, show thyself festive and cheerful: and refresh thy mind with spiritual oil: and seek not praise outwardly for thy fasting. ⟦s6⟧ But because thou art thus turned away from the world, rejoice before God: and so shalt thou cleanse thy face, that is, thy heart, from filth: which being removed, [0103D] it will be permitted to see God. ⟦s7⟧ *Anoint thy head.* ⟦s8⟧ Behold, not yet all things in the New Testament according to the letter. ⟦s9⟧ For they would be ridiculous who were smeared with oil in fasting: but of the spirit of his love, in whose sufferings we ought to share, ourselves being to be mortified, the mind ought to be anointed, and not with the oil of flattery: otherwise fastings are vain.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Christ was made an ointment for us: which being poured out through the passion of his death filled his Church with his odour. ⟦s2⟧ Therefore we ought not to fast for another's sake: but being poured over with his spirit, mortifying the flesh by abstinence, let us share in his passion. ⟦s3⟧ Let us then be anointed with him: that the works of mercy may be heaped up: and charity be poured abroad within: because without him the cup of our fasting is not made drunk with the affection of piety.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0104A]He fasts to God who mortifies himself for love of him and bestows on another what he withdraws from himself.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And thy Father.* ⟦s2⟧ The Father is within through faith: and rewards deeds done in secret. ⟦s3⟧ There therefore must one fast, where he himself may see. ⟦s4⟧ And it is necessary that he who fasts should so fast that he may please him whom he carries in his breast.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Lay not up treasures.* ⟦s2⟧ He commands that earthly things be paid out unto cleanness of heart.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS 20.-- *But to lay up treasures.* [var: Vulg. Mt 6:20 *Thesaurizate*, imperative, "lay up treasures"] As if: When you fast or do any good work: lay it up there where corruption cannot come.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In heaven.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, place all your hope in heavenly things: walking by faith, embracing charity. ⟦s3⟧ *In heaven,* that is, in the spiritual firmament: which does not pass away: [0104B] *Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away.* ⟦s4⟧ If therefore the heart is in heaven, it is clean, because heavenly things are clean. ⟦s5⟧ But if it is rolled about upon the earth, how is it clean? that is, if it does anything in order to obtain earthly things, how is it clean? ⟦s6⟧ For if those things are clean in their own kind, yet the worthier thing grows foul while it is mingled with the lower: as gold with silver, silver with lead.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *For where the treasure is.* ⟦s2⟧ If on earth, the heart is below; if in heavenly things, the heart is fixed in Christ.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The lamp.* ⟦s2⟧ He pursues cleanness of heart. ⟦s3⟧ As the eye directs the members to work, so do intention and the light of faith direct all the virtues. ⟦s4⟧ A lamp is a light in an earthen vessel, so is charity in faith. ⟦s5⟧ When faith shall cease, [0104C] charity alone will be the light. ⟦s6⟧ From the outward, he teaches the office of the inward man, by the intention of the mind and the light of faith all the virtues are illuminated, that they may render the body lightsome.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Likewise this chapter is of itself unto cleanness of heart, or by reason of the former works which appear openly.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *If the eye be,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If with a pure intention thou doest the good things thou canst, the works are of light, even if it does not so appear to men. ⟦s3⟧ If the intention is perverse, whatever is done is depraved, even though it seem right.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The eye is one and simple, namely charity, which now shines in faith as in an earthen vessel.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *But if the eye.* ⟦s2⟧ If the intention is not of faith, the whole inward man is dark. ⟦s3⟧ Because [0104D] what is not of faith is sin. ⟦s4⟧ *Shall be darksome.* ⟦s5⟧ Since indeed the intention that follows forms the work. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore if the good things which thou doest, because they are done with an evil intention, are darkness, what of those which are of themselves evil, and are done with an evil intention? ⟦s7⟧ *If then the light.* ⟦s8⟧ The intention with which we do whatever we do, which is known within us, is called light; but the outcome of the thing is uncertain, as when I give or do something to someone I know not whether it turn out to him for good or for evil: and therefore he calls it darkness. ⟦s9⟧ He says therefore: If the intention with which thou doest (which is known to thee) is soiled by an appetite for temporal things: how much more the deed itself, whose outcome is doubtful? because even if it turn out well for another, what thou doest not with a good intention will profit thee nothing: because it is imputed to thee how thou hast done it, not how it has turned out for him.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0105A]VERS. 24.-- *No man can,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: you will not be able to do good things before the eyes of men, and by these to please God. ⟦s3⟧ Not to lay up treasure at once in heaven and on earth, nor to serve both vices and virtues. ⟦s4⟧ Whatever thou doest, either of the love of God, or of the servitude of the devil.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Since the kingdom of God and of the devil are at variance among themselves, no one can serve as a soldier in both at once: whence they who are baptized renounce the devil and his works.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For either he will hate the one.* ⟦s2⟧ There is nothing between. ⟦s3⟧ For whatever thou doest, either of the love of God, or of the servitude of the devil.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] For the devil is always to be held in hatred: but God, etc., *as far as* but they despise, overcome by their own concupiscence and by diabolical persuasion.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Or he will sustain the one. [n: (ID.)]* Not indeed that he loves him: [0105B] for no one loves the devil, but being entangled by his own cupidity, etc., *as far as* even if he love not him whose handmaid he loves.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He will despise the other.* ⟦s2⟧ Not indeed that he will hate him: for no one's conscience can hate God, which the same Augustine retracted, saying: I do not see that it ought to have been said. ⟦s3⟧ For there are many of whom it is written: *The pride of them that hate thee [n: (Psal. III)]*, etc.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And mammon.* ⟦s2⟧ Mammon, in the Syrian tongue, riches, to serve which is to deny God. ⟦s3⟧ He does not say to have: which is lawful to be done so that one may dispense it well, but to serve, which belongs to the covetous. ⟦s4⟧ This is said to be the name of a demon who presides over riches, not because they are in his dominion: but because he uses these for deceiving, ensnaring men in the nets of riches.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0105C]VERS. 25.-- *Therefore I say to you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *Because you cannot serve God and mammon.* ⟦s3⟧ Be not solicitous, which is to serve riches. ⟦s4⟧ He has enjoined contempt of the world and confidence in things to come throughout the whole sermon, since he commanded both to bear evils and not to avenge, and to love enemies, and to flee human glory. ⟦s5⟧ Now he wishes to confirm it through faith in the hope of eternal things, because there are many whom the love of present things and despair of things to come make torpid.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ In the sweat of the face bread is got ready, but labour is to be exercised, solicitude is to be removed, which disturbs the mind: while it fears lest either the things possessed perish or the things laboured for do not come to hand.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Your soul.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, to the animal nature to which these things are necessary. ⟦s3⟧ Commit your whole hope to God, because you can acquire or retain nothing, [0105D] except what he has decreed.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Soul is here put for life, which is sustained by food, as there: *He that loveth his soul shall lose it [n: (Joan. XII)]*.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The animal nature is nourished by food: and is here put for life: it lives with the cattle. ⟦s2⟧ And he who loses it for Christ's sake shall find it spiritual unto life everlasting.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Is not the soul?* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not life for the sake of food, nor the body for the sake of raiment, but the contrary. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore let not the mind be solicitous, that it may receive that for whose sake it was created.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ As if he should say: He who gave the greater, that is, life and body, will give also the lesser, that is, food and clothing. [0106A] Let no one doubt in these promises of the truth: Let man be what he ought to be, and forthwith all things are added to him for whose sake they were made.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Behold.* ⟦s2⟧ This concerning food. ⟦s3⟧ The saints are deservedly compared to birds, because they seek heaven, and some are so far removed from the world that they now do nothing on earth, but by contemplation alone already dwell in heaven. ⟦s4⟧ Of whom it is said: *Who are these that fly as clouds? [n: (Isa. LX)]*, etc.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ These examples do not forbid providence, that it should not labour, but solicitude, that your whole confidence may be in God, since even the birds live without care.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And your Father.* ⟦s2⟧ He heightens and confirms the confidence of sons: because if the goodness of the Father extends itself even to those things, how much more to the sons of adoption?

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0106B]VERS. 27.-- *And which of you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ But leave to him the care of governing the body, who made it come to this measure.
