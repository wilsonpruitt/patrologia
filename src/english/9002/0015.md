## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)

VERS. 28.-- *And for raiment.* [var: Vulg. Mt 6:28 *Et de vestimento*, singular] Not that this, to labour and to provide, is not fitting, but all solicitude must be cast away, and hope must be had in God. *Consider the lilies of the field.* He confirms by a sufficiently fitting example the exhortation concerning clothing.

VERS. 29.-- *That not even Solomon,* etc. What purple of kings, what painting of weaving-women can be compared to flowers? The colour itself is called the covering of the flowers, as we say: a redness has covered that man.

VERS. 30.-- *For if the grass of the field.* [var: Vulg. Mt 6:30 *Si autem foenum agri*, "But if the grass of the field"] If those things which are for the use of time only, and quickly falling, shall be burnt with fire, God adorns them so beautifully.

[0106C]"Tomorrow" is put for the future in the Scriptures, Jacob saying: *My justice shall hear me tomorrow [n: (Gen. XXX)]* [var: Vulg. Gn 30:33 *Respondebitque mihi cras justitia mea*, "And my justice shall answer for me tomorrow"].

*Is cast into the oven.* Other books have: into the fire, or into a heap which has the appearance of an oven. *How much more you,* namely, will he clothe; and therefore all confidence is to be had in him; but if we are not confident even about these things, how do we hope for things invisible? Very small is the faith which is not certain even about the least things. Therefore he both presses upon us confidence about eternal things, and condemns unbelief. *Of little faith.* Whosoever doubts in any promise, is of little faith, and the more he doubts, the less he finds those things for which he was solicitous.

VERS. 31.-- *Be not therefore solicitous,* etc. He more fully inculcates [0106D] and turns over what he taught from the beginning of the sermon, that we should live without solicitude. *Be not,* etc. Here those seem to be reproved who, despising the common food or clothing, seek for themselves foods or garments more sumptuous or more austere than those among whom they live. *To be solicitous.* Solicitude chokes the word of God, that it may not bear fruit, and makes a man like a Gentile, that is, unbelieving.

VERS. 32.-- *For all these things.* What more then has he than a Gentile: whose unbelief makes the mind anxious, and wearies it with the cares of this life? *The Gentiles.* Who have no care about things to come, but it belongs to sons to seek the kingdom. *For he knoweth.* These things are provided by God, who knows that they are necessary for us on his way. *Your Father.* Thus he makes sons secure concerning [0107A] the goodness and providence of the Father, that is, without care.

VERS. 33.-- *Seek therefore.* Here he plainly shows that these things are not to be sought as our goods, although they are necessary. But the kingdom of God is to be sought, and in it our end is to be placed, for whose sake we should do all things: for example: Let us eat that we may preach the Gospel, we do not preach the Gospel that we may eat. When he says "first," he signifies that this is later, not in time, but in dignity. For that is to be sought, this is necessary for the sake of that: therefore seek first the kingdom of God, that is, set it before all other things.

*And all these things.* Because all things are the sons': and therefore all these things shall be added, even to those who do not seek them, from whom if they are withdrawn, it is for a trial; if [0107B] they are given, it is for thanksgiving; because all things work together unto good.

VERS. 34.-- *Solicitous.* By fear, by grief, and by the anxiety of cares, which disturb the mind.

*For the morrow. [n: (AUG.)]* For the future, concerning present things he grants it: whence the Apostle: *Night and day we worked, that we might not burden any man [n: (I Thes. II)]* [var: Vulg. 1Thes 2:9 *nocte ac die operantes, ne quem vestrum gravaremus*, "working night and day, that we might not burden any of you"]. Concerning things to come, which the divine ordering provides for, it is not fitting, etc., *as far as* because he who gave this precept, to whom angels ministered, in order that this example might be given had purses: whence he might supply the things necessary for their uses, of which Judas was the keeper.

*The day.* Not that which the Lord made, but that which Job and Jeremias cursed, namely that which the devil found. *It sufficeth.* And because minds are overwhelmed by present care, the malice of God [cj: *Dei*; read *diei*, "of the day"] ought not to be made to sprout.
