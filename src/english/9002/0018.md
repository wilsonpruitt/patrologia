## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And when he was come down.* ⟦s2⟧ After the Lord's descent from the mountain, etc., *as far as* he shows [0112C] after how great an interval of time. ⟦s3⟧ *And when he was come down.* ⟦s4⟧ According to Luke, and in a level place, he had delivered to him the discourse which perhaps he had already held above. ⟦s5⟧ *There followed him.* ⟦s6⟧ Some for charity's sake, some for doctrine's, some for wonder and for healing.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Adored him, saying: Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ Before he asked, he showed the worship due to divinity. ⟦s3⟧ *If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.* ⟦s4⟧ Concerning the will I am in doubt, not as one incredulous of his mercy; but, as one conscious of my own foulness, I do not presume.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Stretching forth.* ⟦s2⟧ After the discourse he works miracles also, by which the discourse is confirmed with the hearers. ⟦s3⟧ *He touched him.* ⟦s4⟧ Not because he could heal without touch, but that he might show himself not subject to the law, but the Lord of the law, [0112D] who fears no contagion. ⟦s5⟧ *He touched him.* ⟦s6⟧ For humanity's sake, that he might teach us to despise no one for any defilement of the body. ⟦s7⟧ It appears that grace, which heals him, is above the law (which shuts out the leper); as he says: *I am not come to destroy the law, but to fulfil,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ *I will.* ⟦s9⟧ He healed in that same manner in which he had been besought, then he commands: *Be thou made clean.* ⟦s10⟧ Behold his power.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *See, tell no man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He bade him keep silence, nor yet could he keep silence. ⟦s3⟧ Not that he willed something and could not do it, but he gives an example, that his own should wish to lie hidden in the great things they do: yet that they may profit others, they are made known against their will. ⟦s4⟧ *Tell no man [n: (Marc. I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Yet Mark, where he says that he preached this, [says] that he was as it were made an Evangelist. ⟦s6⟧ *Go.* ⟦s7⟧ He sends him to the priests for humility's sake, [0113A] that he may render honour to the priests, to whom the law bids the cleansed offer gifts, and that seeing they may believe him: or, if they do not believe, that they may be without excuse, and lest he should seem to break the law. ⟦s8⟧ *Show thyself to the priest.* ⟦s9⟧ That the priest may understand that he was healed not by the ordinance of the law, but by the grace of God above the law. ⟦s10⟧ *Offer,* etc. ⟦s11⟧ Because I do not destroy the law, because the true sacrifice has not yet begun. ⟦s12⟧ *For a testimony.* ⟦s13⟧ That is, let them believe the Lord, if the leprosy of impiety depart. ⟦s14⟧ *For a testimony.* ⟦s15⟧ Of the spiritual healing, the spiritual gift to be offered is the man himself.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *I will come and heal him. ⟦s2⟧ And the centurion, making answer, said,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Great humility, etc., *as far as* he might be thought to go bodily because of a weakness of strength.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *I am not worthy.* ⟦s2⟧ Because of the consciousness [0113B] of his Gentile life, he fears rather to be burdened by his presence than to be helped, for although he was endowed with faith, he was not yet steeped in the sacraments. ⟦s3⟧ But what our infirmity does not even presume, grace knows how to give.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *For I also,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If I, a man, etc., *as far as* as Cornelius is called *a just man and one that feareth God [n: (Act. X)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *From the east and the west they shall come and shall sit down.* ⟦s2⟧ Or the east means those who, being enlightened, straightway pass over; the west, those who bear sufferings even unto death. ⟦s3⟧ By these two quarters of the world, out of which the Gentiles were to be called, they are designated.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *But the children of the kingdom shall be cast out.* ⟦s2⟧ Because in this world they enjoy the benefits of God; according to that word: *He raineth upon the just and the unjust.* ⟦s3⟧ Now they are seen in [0113C] the face of the Lord, but then not at all. ⟦s4⟧ *Into the exterior darkness.* ⟦s5⟧ Because they shall be driven forth from the Lord, etc., *as far as* he repents of what he obstinately did amiss. ⟦s6⟧ *Weeping.* ⟦s7⟧ By this is shown the greatness of the torments. ⟦s8⟧ By the weeping, the torment of the soul; by the gnashing of teeth, that of the body.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *And when he was come,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ When this was done Matthew has not expressed, etc., *as far as* to suggest to his memory the things to be narrated. ⟦s3⟧ *And sick of a fever.* ⟦s4⟧ Fever is said in Greek, heat in Latin: and if at any time this affliction makes one tremble, it belongs to human nature.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And he touched,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Luke says that they besought him, etc., *as far as* as *Who can understand sins: from my secret sins cleanse me [n: (Psal. VIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *And she arose,* etc. [0113D] It is natural for the fevered to grow faint when health begins, etc., *as far as* she ministers in the spirit.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And when evening was come,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The setting of the sun is the death of Christ, through which he healed more demoniacs than, living before in the flesh, he had healed sick men.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And Jesus seeing.* ⟦s2⟧ It is manifest that it is another day, etc., *as far as* that we may pass over the bitter waves of the world.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And there came.* ⟦s2⟧ That Matthew says this was done when he bade them go across the strait: but Luke *as they walked in the way:* is not contrary: because they walked in the way that they might come to the strait. ⟦s3⟧ *Master.* ⟦s4⟧ As though he were one of many. ⟦s5⟧ And he himself was a man of letters, not a spiritual hearer. ⟦s6⟧ *Master.* ⟦s7⟧ He did not say [0114A] Lord, but Master: reckoning him one among many masters.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *The foxes have holes.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Why me, for the gains of the world, etc., *as far as* there is no place for the divinity of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ *But the Son of man hath not,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Although men call him the Son of God, he himself calls himself the Son of man, for humility's sake.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And another.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that this man was called by Jesus. ⟦s3⟧ *Lord.* ⟦s4⟧ He who thrust himself forward calls him Master, like the puffed-up Pharisee, the other, who was received, humbly calls him Lord, like the publican.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Their dead.* ⟦s2⟧ Those who went out in fault, as they themselves live with fault. ⟦s3⟧ To bury the dead is to cherish sinners in their sin.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0114B]VERS. 23.-- *And when he went up.* ⟦s2⟧ He who did great things on land passes over to the sea, that he may do them there also, and appear Lord of land and sea.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Behold a commotion.* ⟦s2⟧ The tempest did not arise of itself, but at his command: that the disciples might fear the more and entreat him: and that to those entreating he might show his power. ⟦s3⟧ *He was asleep.* ⟦s4⟧ The Lord sleeps in us, that is, faith in the Lord, when we act more negligently, and therefore comes the tempest. ⟦s5⟧ But if faith rises again, he commands the winds and the waves.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25. ⟦s2⟧ *They awaked him.* ⟦s3⟧ First, Jesus must be roused in tribulations by prayers, that afterwards, rising unto rebuke, he may command the winds and the sea, and there may be a great calm.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *O ye of little faith,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If you have known him powerful [0114C] on land, why do you not believe him so on the sea also? ⟦s3⟧ He who believes little is reproved: he who believes nothing is despised. ⟦s4⟧ *He commanded,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ That which is commanded feels him who commands. ⟦s6⟧ Note that every creature feels its Creator. ⟦s7⟧ The things which are insensible with us are to him things that feel.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *What manner of man is this,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] What manner of man is this, greater than Moses, etc., *as far as* they have no hearing, they obey.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28. ⟦s2⟧ *Into the country of the Gerazenes.* ⟦s3⟧ Geraza is a city of Arabia, etc., *as far as* visited by Christ through his preachers. ⟦s4⟧ *Two having demons.* ⟦s5⟧ Mark and Luke say one, etc., *as far as* they showed the foulness of their bodies. ⟦s6⟧ *Exceeding fierce.* ⟦s7⟧ Mark says that no one now [could bind] the demoniac, etc., *as far as* they assail any who would pass.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Jesus, Son of God. [n: (HILAR.)]* They are to be believed rather to have suspected [0114D] than to have known. ⟦s2⟧ *[n: (AMBR.)] Jesus, Son of God.* ⟦s3⟧ He became known to them just so far as he willed, etc., *as far as* what they saw in him, they did not believe. ⟦s4⟧ *Before the time.* ⟦s5⟧ For they knew that there would be for them at the judgment of God, etc., *as far as* for there were two thousand, as Mark says. ⟦s6⟧ *To torment us.* ⟦s7⟧ The presence of Christ is a torture to them; or, they are tormented while they are cast out of the bodies in whose torture they took delight, just as the presence of God is the refreshment of the angels.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *Send us.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that they can far less harm men by their own power, who cannot do so even to beasts. ⟦s3⟧ Unless a man live after the manner of a swine, the devil receives no power over him, save perhaps for his testing.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 32.-- *And behold with great violence.* [var: Vulg. Mt 8:32 *et ecce impetu abiit totus grex*, without *magno*] Man is worthier than beasts, [0115A] since for the salvation of two men two thousand swine perished. ⟦s3⟧ The people of the Gentiles being set free, unbelievers practise their evil rites in hidden places: sunk in blind and deep curiosity.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *And the keepers fled.* ⟦s2⟧ Because certain leaders even of the impious, though they flee the faith of Christ, yet do not cease to proclaim his power in wonder.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Went out to meet him. [n: (BED.)]* Those coming forth signify certain men bound by habit, who indeed admire and venerate the law of Christ, but are unwilling to suffer it: whence they beg him to pass on. ⟦s2⟧ *And they found the man* (as Luke says) *clothed and in his right mind, sitting at the feet of Jesus [n: (Luc. VIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *They besought him.* ⟦s4⟧ They beseech him not out of pride, out of humility judging themselves unworthy, as Peter did, [0115B] and the widow in the case of Elias. ⟦s5⟧ They feared lest the presence of his holiness should destroy them because of their sins, as it had destroyed the swine.
