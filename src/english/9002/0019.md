## CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 1.-- *Into the city.* Lest thou think this to be Nazareth, etc., *as far as* Capharnaum, as it were the metropolis.

VERS. 2.-- *They offered.* They are the teachers, who lift men up and offer them to Christ by admonishing and by interceding. *Faith.* How much a man's own faith avails with God, with whom another's so availed that he healed the man within and without. *Be of good heart, son, they are forgiven thee.* Luke says: Man, etc., *as far as* taking away the cause of the disease, he afterwards heals the body.

VERS. 5.-- *Which is.* Note that infirmities sometimes [0115C] come about on account of sins, etc., *as far as* whence: *The Lord shall crush them with a double crushing. [n: (BED.)] Which is easier,* [var: Vulg. Jer 17:18 *duplici contritione contere eos*, an imperative with no subject: "crush thou them"] but since you do not believe this spiritual thing, let it be proved by a visible sign, which is agreed to be of no less power, so that in the Son of man you may recognize the hidden power of majesty, which can forgive sins as God.

VERS. 6.-- *Then said he to the man sick of the palsy.* Behold, by the remission of the penalty understand the forgiveness of the fault. *Arise.* To arise is to draw the soul away from carnal desires. To take up the bed is to lift the flesh from earthly desires to the will of the spirit. To go home is to return to paradise, or to the inward keeping of oneself, lest one sin again. *Take up thy bed and go into thy house.* That what was the witness of infirmity [0115D] may be the proof of health.

VERS. 7.-- *And he arose.* Great is the power, where without delay healing accompanies the command. Whence deservedly those who were present, leaving their blasphemies and amazed, are turned to the praise of so great majesty.

VERS. 9.-- *He saw a man.* Here it seems more probable that he is recording things passed over, since he chose the twelve before the sermon. *In the custom-house.* Τέλος in Greek, in Latin a toll. Matthew in Hebrew is in Latin "given", who received so great a gift of grace: Levi, "added" or "taken up", because the divine election took him up and added him to the number of the apostles. *Matthew.* The other Evangelists, out of modesty, etc., *as far as* the aforesaid discourse was. *Rising up. [n: (BED.)]* Luke says more fully: [0116A] leaving all things, he followed him as much in body as in mind. Led not by levity, but drawn by the truth of the works and by the divine power working within, a perfect obedience and a perfect renunciation, he left his gains also, eager to follow Christ.

VERS. 10.-- *In the house.* He does not say whose, but Luke expresses it, etc., *as far as* is shown by the conversion of the penitent. *Behold many publicans.* A good omen: already by his example many are converted. Already he draws many after him, being about to be an Apostle and teacher of the Gentiles. *[n: (HIER.)] With publicans and sinners.* Tertullian says these were heathen, etc., *as far as* he commanded: *Go ye not into the way of the Gentiles. Sinners coming sat down.* Penitents, by Matthew's example, hoping for pardon: whence Mark says here: *that many followed [0116B] him [n: (Marc. II)]*.

VERS. 11.-- *Why with publicans.* Every question, etc., *as far as* and all that he did serves for salvation.

VERS. 12.-- *But Jesus hearing it, said.* He both strengthens further in faith the penitents he has received, and provokes the proud to the grace of humility and of mercy. *They that are in health need not a physician, but they that are ill.* Christ is the physician, etc., *as far as* they receive the word of God, they cannot hunger.

VERS. 13.-- *And going, say ye.* [var: Vulg. Mt 9:13 *discite*, "learn"] To those swelling with a false righteousness he gives counsel of correction, etc., *as far as* by the sheep, and the drachma, and the two sons. *For I am not come to call the just.* Were not Nathanael, and Peter, and Andrew, etc., *as far as* they no longer seem just to themselves.

[0116C]VERS. 14.-- *But thy disciples do not fast.* John, Mark and Luke, etc., *as far as* thou mayest understand the grace and the power.

VERS. 15.-- *Can they.* As if he should say: You are about to do so, that mourning, etc., *as far as* but from all earthly things. *To mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them.* By this word he expresses that fast, etc., *as far as* afterwards they shall fast, they shall not mourn. *And then they shall fast.* Grief for the bridegroom's absence was wanting neither in the fathers before the incarnation, nor after the passion, save for so long as he lived in the flesh with the disciples. *They shall fast.* From the love of the world, not only from foods. For since the spiritual fast is a part of the new life, it does not yet befit those who in other respects are given over to the old life.

VERS. 16.-- *And no man.* As if he should say: The carnal, [0116D] not yet made firm by faith in the passion and the resurrection, etc., *as far as* it does not suit that very oldness. *For it taketh away.* That is, the perfection of the new life, which is one, is divided, and from the old is taken away the faith which it has, to which the perfection of the new was owed.

VERS. 17.-- *Neither do they put.* He compares those same disciples to old bottles, etc., *as far as* we are inwardly renewed in newness of understanding.

VERS. 18.-- *Behold a certain ruler came up.* Mark and Luke call him a ruler of the synagogue, etc., *as far as* he began to hope for a remedy and to seek it.

VERS. 20.-- *A woman who.* The salvation prepared for the Jews the Gentile world snatched beforehand by a sure hope. *Twelve years.* For twelve years she had an issue, as that daughter also had grown [0117A] to twelve: because from the time when the Synagogue began to serve God, the Gentile world began to be defiled with idolatry and sin. *She came near.* She believed, she spoke, because by these three, by faith and by work, all salvation is gained.

VERS. 21.-- *If I shall touch.* The hem, not the garment. Because she did not see the Lord in the flesh, but received the word of the incarnation through the apostles. Blessed is he who touches by faith even the outermost part of the word.

VERS. 22.-- *Be of good heart, daughter.* She who asked did indeed believe, but he impresses upon her strength and perseverance in faith.

VERS. 23.-- *He had seen.* The minstrels are those singing a mournful song. These are the masters soothing their subjects by their own grief, and the crowd of the Jews kicking against the doctrine of Christ. *And the crowd.* Because the Synagogue [lost] the joy of the Lord's indwelling, etc., [0117B] *as far as* it was obedient in the receiving.

VERS. 24.-- *She is not.* To you she is dead. But to God, who can raise her up, she sleeps, as much in soul as in body.

VERS. 25.-- *And when the crowd was put forth.* That is, when the mockers had been rejected, he entered into the minds of the elect. *And he took her hand.* Unless first the hand of the Jews, etc., *as far as* can be blotted out by secret penance.

VERS. 27.-- *As he passed.* That is, from the ruler's house into thy house. The ruler's house is the Synagogue, his own house is the heavenly Jerusalem. *Two blind men,* etc. Two blind men, two ignorant peoples, etc., *as far as* the same deed, but another like it.

VERS. 32.-- *A man.* He signifies the Gentile people: or the whole race of men, which, being led by preaching [0117C] to Christ, and idolatry being taken away, confessed Christ. Κωφὸς is the more usual Greek word, but yet they say indifferently both dumb and deaf.

VERS. 34.-- *But the Pharisees.* In the Pharisees the unbelief of the Jews is signified.
