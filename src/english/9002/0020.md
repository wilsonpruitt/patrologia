## CHAPTER X.

VERS. 1.-- *And having called together.* From the healing of Peter's mother-in-law, etc., *as far as* the centurion's servant healed. *Twelve.* These are the labourers who were to be sent, and who were to call the quarters of the four-square world to faith in the Trinity. That their preaching might have the weight of authority, power is given them to heal every infirmity.

VERS. 2.-- *Peter and Andrew.* He joins the apostles in pairs and yokes, etc., *as far as* Christ, in whom he believed.

[0117D]VERS. 3.-- *Thomas.* The abyss: because by a sure faith he penetrated the depth of the divine power in the resurrection. Or, Thomas a twin, which in Greek is didymus, because of a doubting heart in believing.

VERS. 4.-- *And Judas Scariothis.* A great example of teaching for us, since he who knew all things did not hesitate to choose a betrayer for a disciple and to ordain him an apostle. For Christ has a witness conscious of his own cleanness, since he does not hesitate to choose his own betrayer.

VERS. 5.-- *Into the way.* After the resurrection he says: *Teach ye all nations.* But this beforehand: because the Jews must first be preached to, lest they have a cause for not receiving him, in that he sent the apostles to the Gentiles.

[0118A]VERS. 7.-- *The kingdom of heaven shall draw near.* [var: Vulg. Mt 10:7 *appropinquavit*, "hath drawn near"] Through faith the kingdom is near. For as one who sins is earth, so let the just man be heaven, in which God presides; whence it is said: *The kingdom of God is within you [n: (Luc. XVII)]*, etc. Behold what they ought to preach: The kingdom of heaven shall draw near.

VERS. 8.-- *Heal the sick.* The Church strengthens the sick by the standing of good work, raises the dead in sins, or without faith; cleanses the lepers sprinkled with errors, puts demons to flight by exorcisms. *Freely have you received.* Lest Judas, who had the purse, should wish to gather money out of the aforesaid power, the Lord suppresses his wickedness, saying: *Freely have you received,* etc. Here also he condemns the perfidy of the Simoniac heresy. Spiritual gifts are cheapened by a price.

[0118B]VERS. 9.-- *Gold nor silver.* Those who had these would seem to preach not for the sake of salvation, but of gain. *Nor money in your purses.* There are two kinds of necessary things: one whereby necessaries are bought, the other the necessaries themselves. By the money in the purses, that whereby they are bought: by the scrip, what is bought, what is stored there. Of this in the passion: *When I sent you without purse and scrip, did you want anything? But they said: No.* [var: Vulg. Lc 22:35 *At illi dixerunt : Nihil*, "Nothing"]

VERS. 10.-- *Nor shoes.* Plato too says that the two extremities of the body are not to be covered, and that softness of head and of feet ought not to be made habitual: But when these have firmness, the rest are stronger. *Nor a staff.* Matthew and Luke say *nor a staff;* Mark, *nothing but a staff:* by which [0118C] name, etc., *as far as* it may be shown by what way he ought to go upon the journey of preaching. *He is worthy.* Whence then necessaries? Take only so much as suffices for need, that thence being free from care you may better have leisure for eternal things.

VERS. 11.-- *Inquire who.* By the testimony of the neighbours the host is to be chosen, lest the preaching be injured by his ill fame. *Is worthy.* That he may know that he rather receives a favour than gives one. *There abide. [n: (AMBR.)]* He gives a general command of constancy, that they should keep the rights of the bond of hospitality: therefore a house is to be so chosen that no occasion be at hand for changing the lodging and for violating the bond.

VERS. 14.-- *Shake off the dust from your feet.* By the shaking off of the dust is understood the loss of the journey. *Shake off* the dust, for a testimony of the earthly labour [0118D] which they had undertaken for them in vain, etc., *as far as* they are said to have been washed by the Saviour himself.

VERS. 16.-- *Behold I send you.* Their punishment shall be kept for them in the time to come, etc., *as far as* he ought not to inflict evils, but to bear them. *As serpents.* The serpent's craft is, etc., *as far as* he puts off the old man.

VERS. 18.-- *For a testimony.* That thence the perverse may perish, etc., *as far as* the testimony of their innocence.

VERS. 19.-- *For it shall be given you in that hour what to speak.* Offer your will for Christ; but Christ will speak for himself.

VERS. 21.-- *And the brother shall deliver up,* etc. Less grief do those evils bring which are from strangers: greater [0119A] those which we suffer from those upon whose minds we had counted: because, along with the harm of the body, the evils of a charity lost torment us.

VERS. 22.-- *But he that,* etc. He teaches the virtue of long-suffering and of patience, etc., *as far as* we begin to possess this very thing which we are.

VERS. 23.-- *And when.* This belongs to that time of which it was said: *Go ye not into the way of the Gentiles,* namely that they ought not to fear persecutions, but to shun them: so that the occasion of tribulation may be a seed-plot of the Gospel.

VERS. 24.-- *The disciple is not,* etc. Do not disdain to bear what I bear, who am the Lord, doing what I will, and the master teaching what I know to be useful.

VERS. 25.-- *If the master of the house.* He shows his own calumnies, [0119B] that by his example they may bear injuries. Whence he said: *Sufficient for the day is the evil thereof. Beelzebub.* Beel is Bel himself, Zebub, a fly, etc., *as far as* the man of flies, or having flies.

VERS. 26.-- *Therefore fear them not.* Their savagery or their blasphemies, because the day will come when God shall judge the hidden things of men, where both their wickedness shall be uncovered, and your intention, now known to God alone, shall be known by all.

VERS. 27.-- *What I tell you.* That is, in what I wish you to be constant, etc., *as far as* the dwelling-place of the flesh being trodden underfoot, *preach ye.*

VERS. 29.-- *Upon.* He speaks according to the custom of Palestine, etc., *as far as* tell it in the whole world. *Are not two sparrows?* Luke: *Are not five sparrows sold for [0119C] two farthings [n: (Luc. XII)]*? Nor is there much difference, seeing that two go for an as, and five for a dipondius. For a dipondius is out of two asses. And what one is in numbers, that the as is in weight: and what two is, that is the dipondius. *And one of them.* How then does the Apostle say: *Doth God take care for oxen [n: (Luc. IX)]*? But care is one thing, knowledge another.

VERS. 30.-- *Numbered.* That is, by the knowledge of God, etc., *as far as* small and idle words lie hidden from him.

VERS. 32.-- *Whosoever shall confess.* Or a man confesses Jesus with that faith which works by love: he denies who does not obey the precepts of God.

VERS. 33.-- *But he that.* He is denied by the Son before the Father, to whom there is not manifested, in the power of the divinity, [0119D] the Son and the Father. He is confessed by the Son before the Father, who through the Son has access to the Father.

VERS. 34.-- *Peace.* A good war was sent, that an evil peace might be broken.

VERS. 35.-- *For I came.* Here Luke says: *There shall be five in one house divided, three against two and two against three shall be divided.* How then, etc., *as far as* circumcision and the other carnal observances. *To set apart.* That is, to extinguish the private affections of the flesh, by which men so love one another that they serve God the less. Or that they be utterly turned away, not that father and mother are not to be honoured, but after God; this is the order of charity.

VERS. 37.-- *He that loveth father.* After God they [0120A] are to be loved, but if both cannot be kept, hatred toward one's own is piety toward God.

VERS. 38.-- *And he that taketh not,* etc. The cross is borne in two ways, etc., *as far as* the love of Christ may be shown. *And followeth me.* For one man mortifies the flesh for empty glory, or has a carnal compassion for his neighbour so as to favour his fault: he seems to bear the cross, but does not follow the Lord.

VERS. 39.-- *He that findeth,* etc. By the name of the soul the present life is designated.

VERS. 40.-- *He that receiveth,* etc. When he sends them to preach, etc., *as far as* that they ought to receive their wages.

VERS. 42.-- *And whosoever.* He who by the testimony of the shaken-off dust denounced the peril of those who do not receive them, [0120B] commends beyond what was hoped the desert of those who do receive them. Note that God looks rather to the devout affection than to the quantity of the thing bestowed. *To one of these least ones.* The least are those who have absolutely nothing in this world, and will be judges with Christ.
