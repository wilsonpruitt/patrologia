## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 1.-- *At that time.* The preaching and the miracles of one year, done before John's question, having been related, he passes over to those things which were done in another year, namely, after John's death, when now in all things Christ is contradicted. *Being hungry,* etc. Because, as another [0124A] evangelist says, they had not even room to eat, for the crowds: on account of their exceeding importunity. The other Evangelists add: *that they rubbed them with their hands [n: (Luc. VI)]*. Whence also it is said to Peter: *Slaughter and eat [n: (Act. X)]* [var: Vulg. Act 10:13 *occide, et manduca*, "kill, and eat"].

VERS. 2.-- *It is not lawful.* According to the precept of the law. But because another scripture says: *I choose mercy rather than sacrifice [n: (Ose. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Os 6:6 *misericordiam volui, et non sacrificium*, "I have willed mercy, and not sacrifice"; the comparison is Migne's, and the gloss argues from it], it was better to free a man from the peril of famine than to offer sacrifice to God, since the victim that pleases is the salvation of men. Of the wasting of the crops they bring no charge, because by the law it is lawful to eat thereof.

VERS. 3.-- *But he said,* etc. In this deed we are taught nothing else, save that we should understand the sabbath not according to the letter, but spiritually. *Have ye not read.* If David and Abimelech are not reproved, each of whom [0124B] transgressed the commandment of the law under a probable necessity, and here the cause of hunger is like theirs, why do you not approve the same in the apostles?

VERS. 4.-- *The loaves of proposition.* Which signified that the priestly food would pass over into the use of the peoples. Or because we ought all to imitate priests. Or because we are all priests.

VERS. 5.-- *That on the sabbaths the priests.* Here he shows that the sabbath is violated without necessity by those very men who bring the charge. One example is given of the royal power, from David: another of the priestly, from those who by the ministry of the temple violate the sabbath, so that much less does the crime of the ears of corn plucked on the sabbath pertain to him who is the true king and priest and lord of the sabbath.

[0124C]VERS. 6.-- *But I say to you.* As if he should say: If the figurative temple could defend the priests that served it, why cannot the spiritual defend the disciples that believe in it?

VERS. 7.-- *But if.* And that he might show you that all things contain the figure of the time to come, he added: *But if you knew,* that is, that God has appointed to free the people by grace, and not by outward observances, you would not reprove mine, if they let the old things go.

VERS. 8.-- *For he is Lord.* If David when he was fed, and the priests who violate the sabbath by the ministry of the temple, are without crime, how much more the Son of man, who is the true king and priest and lord of the sabbath?

VERS. 10.-- *And behold a man.* This man is said [0124D] to have been a mason, because the hand to which the care of the spiritual building had been committed was withered until the time of Christ. *Behold a man having a hand,* etc. It signifies the human race dried up in the fruitfulness of good work, in requital for the hand stretched out to the apple, which hand the innocent hand stretched out upon the cross healed. Well was the hand withered in the Synagogue, because where the gift of knowledge is greater, there the transgressor lies under a greater fault.

*They asked.* Because he had excused the disciples concerning the violation of the sabbath by a probable example, they wish to accuse him himself, so that, if he should not heal, they may charge him with cruelty or with weakness: or if he should heal, with transgression.

*On the sabbaths,* etc. Chiefly on the sabbaths does Jesus teach and work, not only on account of the special sabbath, [0125A] but also on account of the more crowded gathering of the people, seeking the salvation of all.

VERS. 11.-- *What man shall there be.* By a fitting example he resolves the question, that he may show that they violate the sabbath in a work of covetousness who charge him with violating it in a work of charity. He reproves those who interpret the law ill, who say that on the sabbath one must keep holiday from good works. But one must keep holiday from evil ones only; whence it is said: *Ye shall not do any servile work therein [n: (Levit. XXIII)]*. That is, sin. So also in the eternal rest they shall keep holiday from evil things only, not from good.

VERS. 13.-- *Stretch forth.* Because nothing avails more for healing than largeness in almsgiving, whence it is said: *Let not thy hand be stretched out to receive, but closed to give [n: (Eccl. IV)]* [var: Vulg. Sir 4:36 joins the two members with *et*, not *sed*, so that the single negative governs both]. For in vain does he spread out [0125B] his hands to God when about to entreat for his sins, who does not stretch them out to the poor according to his power.

*As the other.* Man before the coming of Christ had his right hand feeble, because he was sluggish toward almsgiving: his left hand sound, because he was intent upon his own advantage: but at Christ's coming the right hand is healed like the left: because what he had gathered greedily, he now distributes in charity.

VERS. 15.-- *He departed thence.* Mark the example: *If they have persecuted you in one city, flee into another [n: (Matth. X)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 10:23 *Cum autem persequentur vos in civitate ista*, "But when they shall persecute you in this city"]. *They followed.* The learned, when they have seen his miracles, persecute; the unlearned, led by his repute, follow him in love, whence also they deserve to be healed.

VERS. 16.-- *And he charged.* Not out of fear, lest being betrayed he should be killed, but sparing the Jews, lest out of envy they should be slaughtered [0125C] in the face of his innocence and meekness. Here he instructs us mystically, that when we make much of anything, we should not seek praise abroad.

VERS. 18.-- *My soul.* By the soul every affection is shown.

VERS. 20.-- *The reed.* The Jews, yielding to every wind, who by disagreeing among themselves have been scattered, he will not straightway condemn, but will bear with them. *The bruised reed.* He who does not stretch out a hand to one that sins, nor bear his brother's burden, breaks the bruised reed. And he who despises the small spark of faith in the little ones, quenches the smoking flax: which Christ did not do, who came rather to seek that which had perished; for he kept patience, until the judgment that was being carried on in him should come through to victory, and, the prince of the world being cast out, [0125D] he should return to his kingdom, sitting at the right hand of the Father, until he put his enemies under his feet. *Smoking flax.* The Gentiles, whom, the ardour of the natural law being well-nigh quenched, the most bitter smoke of error, and hurtful of odour, wrapped about: whom he did not quench, but from a small spark he roused up into a great burning of the fire of God. *Until.* So long does his preaching, overcome by no snares, shine to the world, until he set judgment in the earth, and this be fulfilled: *Thy will be done, as in heaven and in earth.*

VERS. 22.-- *And he healed.* Three signs were wrought together, which are also wrought daily in the conversion of men, that, the demon being cast out, they may see the light of faith and praise God.

[0126A]VERS. 24.-- *In Beelzebub.* Beel, that is, Baal: of flies, on account of the filth of the sacrificial blood: he is the god of Accaron, by which name they call the prince of the demons.

VERS. 25.-- *Every kingdom.* He convicts them by a necessary dilemma. For either Christ casts out demons by the power of God, or in the prince of the demons. If by the power of God, they accuse him in vain. If in the prince of the demons, his kingdom is divided, and shall not stand: and therefore let them withdraw from his kingdom: which he hints that they have chosen for themselves, while they do not believe in him.

VERS. 26.-- *And every city.* The same reason holds of a city and of a house as of a kingdom. The city Jerusalem, ever glorious, after it blazed out against Christ and against the Apostles, division being made, fell in destruction.

[0126B]VERS. 27.-- *And if I in Beelzebub.* If the casting out of demons in your sons is ascribed to God and not to demons, why in me does the same work not have the same cause?

*They themselves,* etc. The contemptible things of the world, in which the holy simplicity of my power appears. Or rather thus: The sons, that is, the Apostles, who were well aware in themselves that they had learned nothing of evil art from him: therefore they themselves are the witnesses of my judgment, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.

VERS. 28.-- *But if I.* That member: *If I cast out demons in Beelzebub,* has been carried through to many absurdities; now the other member of the dilemma is handled. *But if I in the spirit of God.* Here Luke says: *in the finger [n: (Luc. XI)]*. The Holy Spirit is called a finger, on account of the parcelling out of gifts. By the kingdom of God, either he means himself. [0126C] Whence elsewhere: *There hath stood among you one whom you know not [n: (Luc. XI, Joan. I)]* [var: Vulg. Jo 1:26 *medius autem vestrum stetit*, "there hath stood in the midst of you"]. Or the kingdom which John and Jesus himself preached. *Do penance: for the kingdom of heaven shall draw near [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. There is also a third kingdom, that of Holy Scripture, which is taken away from the Jews and given to a nation bringing forth the fruit thereof. Or the kingdom, that is, all the parts of the world, which he distributed to the Apostles and their successors to be converted.

VERS. 29.-- *Or how can.* The Lord therefore shows by a plain parable that he is not in concord with the demons in a deceitful working, as they falsely charged, but that by the power of his divinity he was freeing men from the demon; and therefore they commit a great crime who cried that what they had recognized to be of God was of the devil. For neither can they be in concord: [0126D] they whose works are diverse. Whence he adds: *He that is not with me,* etc.

*His house.* Or the house, that is, all the parts of the world, which he distributed to the Apostles and their successors to be converted.

VERS. 30.-- *He that is not with me,* etc. That is, he who does works unlike mine is contrary to me: he draws to vices, I to virtues.

VERS. 31.-- *Every sin and blasphemy.* What is contracted by human frailty is remissible, if the congregation be not forsaken. But if by diabolical obstinacy there is never a return from dispersion to the congregation, the sin is judged indissoluble.

*[n: (HIER.)] Every sin.* Not all sins are remitted to all [0127A], but to these, etc., *as far as* asserting that all shall obtain pardon after the universal judgment, though many ages have passed.

*But the Spirit.* He who manifestly understands the works of God, since he cannot deny their power, if goaded: out of envy he accuses, and says that it is Beelzebub, to him it shall not be forgiven. Not that remission would be denied even to him if he should repent: but because this blasphemer, his own evil merits requiring it, as he will never come to remission, so neither will he come to repentance. As John the evangelist says of certain men blinded by the desert of their blasphemy: *Therefore they could not believe [n: (Joan. X)]*. Which Isaias said again: *He hath blinded their eyes [n: (Isa. VI)]*. They who believe that the Holy Spirit is not, or is less than the Father, and say so: because they do this not out of diabolical envy, [0127B] but out of human ignorance, they are not held by an irremissible crime. But they who, in the likeness of the devil, assail the majesty of God against their own conscience, have no remission for ever, as Mark plainly declares: who, this testimony of the Lord being set down, subjoined: *Because they said, he hath an unclean spirit [n: (Matth. III)]*.

VERS. 32.-- *And whosoever. [n: (AMBR.)]* Even he who, being scandalized at the infirmity of my flesh, shall have suspected me a mere man or a glutton, it shall be remissible.

*But he that.* He who, not acknowledging the grace of the Holy Spirit by which repentance is inspired and return is made to the congregation, shall have remained with an impenitent heart: since this now is not human but diabolical, it will be irremissible [0127C]. *Neither in this world.* For certain faults are loosed in this world: but certain (the small ones, namely) even in the world to come. These indeed weigh heavy after death, but they are also forgiven, if a man by good acts in this life has deserved that they be forgiven there. *Neither in the world to come.* Because there are certain light ones, which, if in this life they are not yet remitted, weigh heavy after death, but they are also remitted, if they are worthy.

VERS. 33. *Either make.* For there is nothing between, but that the works of a good author are good only, and those of an evil author evil. He hems them in on this side and on that with a dilemma, and presses them with both horns. If the devil is evil, he does not do good things; if they are good, they are not the devil's.

*Either make,* etc. Mystically the good tree is Christ; the fruit, the preaching of the Gospel; the healings, redemption. [0127D] The evil tree is the devil and his own, that is, the Scribes and Pharisees: and the other evil fruits are envy, detraction, blasphemy, heresy and the like.
