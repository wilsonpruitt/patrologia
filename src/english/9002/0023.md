## CHAPTER XII. (cont.)

VERS. 34.-- *Offspring of vipers,* etc. He shows that they are an evil tree: and that they bring forth the fruits of blasphemy. *Out of the abundance.* A man is often deceived who weighs the heart by the mouth and by the deed. But God judges the deed and the words out of the heart, which he knows. To him therefore the mouth speaks out of the abundance of the heart, who knows out of what intention it comes forth.

VERS. 35.-- *A good man.* The treasure of the heart is the intention, out of which God judges works, so that sometimes the lesser works have a greater reward and the greater a lesser. In this place he either shows out of what sort of treasure the Jews bring forth blasphemy. Or it hangs together with the things above [0128A], that as a good man cannot bring forth evil things, nor an evil man good, so neither can Christ do evil works, nor the devil good ones.

VERS. 36.-- *An idle word.* If a man shall render an account for an idle word in the day of judgment, how much more you who say that the works of the Holy Spirit are Beelzebub.

[n: (GREG.)] An idle word is that which lacks either the usefulness of rectitude or the ground of a just necessity, that namely, etc., *as far as* he shall here be held guilty not of an idle word but of a criminal one.

VERS. 37. *By thy words.* Death and life are in the hands of the tongue.

VERS. 38.-- *Then answered.* Since the Scribes and Pharisees could in no way disprove his words, they seek to tempt him in his works, seeking to see signs, [0128B] as though the things they had seen were not signs.

VERS. 39.-- *But the sign of Jonas.* He shows that the Jews are criminal after the likeness of the Ninivites, and, unless they repent, near to overthrow. But as to those men both the punishment is announced and the remedy is shown: so the Jews ought not to despair of pardon, if at least, after Christ has risen from the dead, they be admonished and do penance: because the death of Christ is the reconciliation of the world.

VERS. 40.-- *For as Jonas was.* Jonas, that is, one grieving, signifies him who bore our griefs. Or a dove: the fish that devoured him in the deep is the death which he suffered in the world. As he was cast out upon the dry land, so Christ rose again into glory.

*Three days.* After the manner of the ancients, who reckoned the day together with [0128C] the night preceding. Take the part of the day on which he was buried, together with the night gone before, for a night and a day, that is, for a whole day, by synecdoche. The night and the day of the sabbath entire. The Lord's night with the same day dawning, the part for the whole, and so thou hast three days and three nights. The first days are reckoned on account of man's coming fall from light into night. These on account of man's restoration, from darkness to light.

Mystically. *Three days,* three times, before the law, under the law, under grace. Three nights, the three dead whom the Lord raised up.

VERS. 41.-- *The men of Ninive.* And the sense is: Jonas in a few days and without signs preached to an unbelieving nation, and prevailed: I over a long time, and to the people [0128D] of God, and with very many signs, preach in vain. They are so much the more unworthy of pardon who are unfaithful though they have the law, by how much there is more faith in those who have not known the law. Ninive, *comely:* it signifies the Gentile world, which Christ loved when it was foul, that he might make it fair. In the Ninivites and the queen of the South the faith of the Church is signified, which is reconciled to God no less by repentance for folly committed than by diligence in learning wisdom. For there are two parts of the Church, that which knows not how to sin, and that which ceases from sinning, for repentance abolishes sin, wisdom guards against it.

VERS. 43.-- *But when,* etc. As if he should say: You are not like the Ninivites or the queen of the South: because when I preached repentance you did not repent, and when I [0129A] taught wisdom you did not learn. But you are like the man from whom the devil, being shut out, enters in again.

There is a difference between my works, who cleanse the things that are polluted, and Satan's, who more grievously defiles the things that have been cleansed.

*He walketh through places.* He explores the hearts of the faithful, cleansed from the softness of fluid thought, whether he can fix any steps there; and because he cannot, he returns to the damp places.

VERS. 44.-- *I will return into my house.* He sleeps under the shadow, in the covert of the reed, and in damp places. The dark shadow is the conscience: the reed, bright without and empty within, is the dissembler: the damp place is the wanton and soft mind.

VERS. 45.-- *Seven other spirits.* The seven vices, contrary to the seven spiritual virtues. *More wicked.* [0129B] Because he not only has vices, but also counterfeits good things. The more wicked, by so much the more crafty. Seven, because so many were the graces appointed with Christ.

Seven vices, seven demons contrary to the seven spirits, to the spirit of wisdom and of understanding, etc.

*So shall it be,* etc. What he said of one single case, this comes to pass in this whole nation. The unclean Spirit went out from the Jews when they received the law. He walked to the Gentiles, and when these afterwards believed the Lord, finding no place in them he said: I will return to the Jews, who stand empty of the protection of God and of the angels, cleansed by rebukes and chastisements, adorned with superfluous observances. Seven are added, because they that blaspheme Jesus are possessed by a far greater number of demons than they were possessed by in Egypt. Against the seven spirits [0129C] of the virtues is the number of the vices.

VERS. 46.-- *Behold his mother. [n: (RAB.)]* Mystically. The mother and the brethren are the people of the Jews, who, while Jesus teaches within, cannot enter in, because his words spiritually, etc., *as far as* The brethren of the Lord are his cousins, the sons of Mary his mother's sister: who is called the mother of James the less and of Joseph, and of Jude and of Simon.

VERS. 47.-- *And one said to him.* Insidiously, to try whether he would prefer the flesh to the spiritual work: and therefore he feigns not to know his mother and his kinsfolk by acquaintance, but says that they are near to him by the joining of the spirit.

VERS. 48.-- *Who is my mother?* He does not refuse the dutiful service of his mother, but prefers the ministries of the Father: he does not [0129D] despise the brethren, but prefers the spiritual work to kinship, that we too may prefer the spirit to the flesh. Whence: *He that loveth father or mother more than me, is not worthy of me [n: (Matth. X)]*. *Behold my mother,* etc. These are my mother, who beget me in the hearts of them that believe. These are the brethren, who do the works of my Father. Brethren and sister by believing, a mother by preaching.
