## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *On that day.* By this he indicates that this was done next in order, unless perhaps the day be put for the time, after the manner of Scripture. Mark keeps the same order. Luke passes to another matter, yet neither does he contradict this. [n: (RAB.)] Not only the deeds and the sayings of the Lord, but his [0130A] journeys also, etc., *as far as* to which the Jews did not enter in, fixed outside in the letter.

VERS. 2.-- *The crowd stood.* These are they who have lately come to hear, who are also set apart from the waves of the sea, and do not yet ascend with Christ, steeped in the heavenly mysteries which they desire.

VERS. 3.-- *He spoke many things.* Because the crowd is not of one mind but of diverse wills, he speaks to them in many parables, that according to their various pleasures they may receive diverse instructions. *He that soweth.* Christ himself, who sowed among men, by himself or by his own, the word of truth which he had seen with the Father. He who was speaking sacraments to the disciples went out to sow the word of God among the crowds.

VERS. 4.-- *By the way side.* The way is a mind trodden and dried up by the [0130B] busy passage of evil thoughts. There the seed perishes in two ways, because it is both trampled by the passing of thoughts, as Luke says, and snatched away by the birds. The demons are called birds, either because they are of a heavenly and spiritual nature, or because they fly through the air. *And they came.* According to Luke it was trodden down by men, because it was taken away by the crooked persuasion of the wicked. *And they ate it up.* Because they stole it away from the minds of men.

VERS. 5.-- *Upon stony ground.* Stony places seek to sprout quickly, but the root is not fixed, because the earth is not deep, that is, because there is little of saving desire within. In this exposition of the Lord we learn that things are not always set down in the same signification. For the rock here stands for hardness, the earth for softness of mind, the sun for the heat [0130C] of persecution. Whereas elsewhere the sun is in a good sense: *The just shall shine as the sun [n: (Matth. XIII)]*, and the rock in a foundation, for the strength of faith, and the earth for earthly thoughts. *The depth of earth.* The depth of earth is the uprightness of a mind schooled in heavenly disciplines and proved in temptations.

VERS. 8.-- *Sixtyfold.* Because in the number six the adornment of the world was completed, by this good working is signified.

VERS. 9.-- *He that hath ears to hear.* As often as this admonition is interposed in the Gospel or in the Apocalypse of John, it is intimated that what is said is mystical.

VERS. 10.-- *And the disciples coming,* etc. How do the disciples come to him, when the Lord was sitting in the boat? But perhaps it is to be understood that they had some while before gone up into the boat [0130D] with him: and standing there they ask.

VERS. 11.-- *To you it is given.* Mark that not only the words of the Lord, but his deeds also, are parables: that is, signs of mystical things, which many that are without did not understand.

VERS. 12.-- *For he that hath.* That is, to the apostles, who have faith, if they have anything less of the virtues, it is granted; from the Jews, who do not believe in the Son of God, even if they have anything of good by nature, it is taken away. For they can understand nothing wisely who have not the head of wisdom. *It shall be taken away from him.* He shall rejoice in no sweetness of true wisdom.

VERS. 13.-- *Therefore in parables.* It is said that they were blinded for this reason, and that the Lord's sentences were hidden from them by parables [0131A], that afterwards they might be more wholesomely converted. For because they did not understand the things obscurely spoken, therefore they did not believe in him, and so they crucified him, and so, after the resurrection, being terrified by miracles, they were pricked with the guilt of a greater crime. Whence, having received pardon, they burn with a fuller love. But John states this passage thus: *Therefore they could not believe, because Isaias said again: He hath blinded [sic: Excaevavit] their eyes, that they should not see [n: (Joan. XI)]*. Where he plainly says that the blinding was not brought about that, admonished by it and grieving that they did not understand, they might at some time be converted: for they could not do this unless they believed; but rather that they were blinded for this reason, that they should not believe, which they deserved by their other sins. *Seeing they see not.* As a covetous man has many riches and none, because they are useless to him.

[0131B]VERS. 14.-- *With hearing you shall hear.* These things were prophesied of the crowds, because they stand on the shore, and do not deserve to hear the discourse of the Lord.

VERS. 15.-- *Their eyes.* That is, they themselves were the cause to themselves that God should close their eyes.

*Lest at any time.* That is, therefore, because they would not open their eyes and ears, they did not deserve to be converted at any time: which nevertheless was done mercifully for them.

VERS. 16.-- *Blessed.* Truly blessed, because they deserved to see and hear even in the present that which none of the former men, of however great merit, deserved, however much he desired it.

VERS. 17.-- *And the just.* Luke calls the just kings, because they rule their own motions well

VERS. 18.-- *Hear you therefore.* He expounds the first parable [0131C], that they may attend to the same in the rest. He expounds it not according to the several words, but the sum of the meaning.

[n: (AUG. sup. Gen.)] Accordingly, what the evangelist related (was also done), namely that the Lord had spoken such things. But of his own, etc., *as far as* for that a narrator of things past wrote, this only a foreteller of things to come.

VERS. 20.-- *Upon stony ground.* They are sown upon the rock and among thorns who receive well: but either adversity by terrifying, or prosperity by flattering, holds them back from bringing to perfection. To riches is joined the anxiety of keeping them, which afflicts, and pleasure, which makes a man run to waste.

VERS. 22.-- *The deceitfulness of riches. [n: (CHRYSOST.)]* The things we have heard are choked by thorns, not indeed by the fault of the thorns themselves, but of him who them, etc., *as far as* [0131D] for there are in riches two vices opposed to one another, one which kindles anxiety and darkens with cares: the other which makes men softer with delights.

VERS. 23.-- *Some a hundredfold. [n: (RAB.)]* There are three differences in the good earth, as there were in the bad. For thirty is referred to marriage. Sixty to, etc., *as far as* the thirtyfold of the married, because this is the age of them that do battle, and to these the conflict is greater, lest they be overcome by lust.

VERS. 24.-- *Another parable.* As a rich householder refreshes those he has invited with diverse foods, that each one might take various nourishments according to the nature of his own stomach. Whence he also said another parable and not the other, that more may be understood to follow. This parable [0132A] is set forth, and, the crowds being dismissed, it is expounded at home to the disciples who ask. And here he teaches good will, and caution, and patience, and discretion, and long-suffering, and justice.

VERS. 25.-- *Men.* Those who are appointed over others in the Church for their protection and keeping: or when the apostles shall receive the sleep of death.

VERS. 28.-- *Wilt thou that we go.* When a spiritual man hears that this is from the devil, who avails nothing against the will of God, a will may arise in him to take such men out of the world, if he should have the power. But he consults the justice of God, whether he ought to do it, and whether God wills this to be the office of men. But the Truth answers: that a man does not know in this life what he will be who now errs, and what his error confers upon the good: and [0132B] that they are therefore not to be taken away, lest perhaps they be slain who perhaps are yet to be [good]: or lest it hurt the good, to whom they are profitable, etc. But that this is then done seasonably, when there is no longer a time for changing one's life and for profiting others: and then, he says, it is done not by men, but by angels.

VERS. 29.-- *And he said: No,* etc. Behold patience. For he did not blaze out into anger when the grievance was heard, nor did he wish to avenge himself at once; so let us also, when injured, bear it patiently.

*Lest perhaps.* Here place is given to repentance, and we are admonished not to cut off quickly: because he who errs today will perhaps tomorrow defend the truth. If therefore he were plucked up now, the wheat that was to be would be rooted out. Or it is necessary that the evil be mingled with the good, by whom they may be exercised. Or by comparison with whom they may strive toward what is better; and when these are taken away, [0132C] the height of charity withers, which is to be rooted out.

There the evil are to be borne patiently, where some are found by whom the good are helped.

[n: (AUG.)] A multitude is not to be excommunicated, nor the prince of the people.

VERS. 30.-- *Suffer.* This seems contrary to that word of the Apostle: *Put away the evil one from among you [n: (I Cor. V)]*. But as between the cockle and the wheat, so long as it is in the blade, there is little difference: so the Lord admonishes us not to judge concerning a doubtful thing, which the Lord in the day of judgment will condemn not by suspicion, but manifestly.

In suffering let us meditate justice: in judging let us not neglect justice.

*And in the time.* He who has borne long-sufferingly, by justice [0132D] repays what is deserved: for the virtues are distinct, but not contrary. *And bind ye.* In this life there is no binding, otherwise so many would not come to their senses out of error.

VERS. 31.-- *The kingdom is like,* etc. The kingdom of heaven is the preaching of the Gospel, concerning which elsewhere: *The kingdom of God shall be taken away from you, and shall be given to a nation bringing forth the fruit thereof [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. Or the knowledge of the Scriptures. *To a grain of mustard seed.* It is compared to a grain of mustard seed for the fervour of faith, because it is said to drive out poisons, that is, all the doctrines of depravity. In the grain of mustard seed the humility of the Lord's incarnation may be understood. Which being received, a man put it in his garden, because Joseph, receiving the body of the crucified Saviour, buried it in a garden. It spread out branches in which the birds of the air shall rest, [0133A] because he scattered preachers throughout the world, in whose words and consolations the faithful might take breath from the weariness of this life.

VERS. 32.-- *But when it is grown up.* In the whole world, or in the mind of each: because no one is perfect on a sudden. It grew and became a tree, because he rose again and ascended into heaven. Or it shall have grown, that is, it shall have become known to the minds of men how great its power is. The rest appear as herbs, which grow quickly and fall: not living, but withered: that becomes not an herb, but a tree, where there are leaves with fruit, and in its diverse meanings spiritual men rest.

*Than herbs, and becometh a tree.* That is, than the doctrines of the philosophers, which grow quickly and fall, promising nothing that has life.

[0133B]VERS. 33.-- *In three measures of meal.* That spirit, soul and body, brought into one, may not disagree among themselves. Or the three powers of the soul are brought into one, that in reason we may possess prudence, in anger the hatred of vices, in desire the longing for virtues. Or, the woman, the Church, mixes the faith of men in three measures of meal, in the belief of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. And when it has been leavened into one, it leads us not to a threefold God, but to the knowledge of one deity, because the nature is not diverse in the several persons.

*Until it was leavened.* So long ought charity, hidden in the mind, to grow, until it change the whole mind into its own perfection, so that it love or think upon nothing beside God, which is begun here, and perfected in the other life.

VERS. 34.-- *In parables to the crowds.* Because whatsoever [0133C] is there said of the army of Israel, and all the signs, are hidden sacraments.

VERS. 44.-- *To a treasure hidden in a field.* By the treasure may be understood the word of God, which is hidden in the flesh of Christ. Or the Scripture, in which the knowledge of the Saviour is laid up. Or the two Testaments in the Church, for the understanding of which all things are renounced, that they may be learned in leisure.

The kingdom of heaven is likened to earthly things, that through things known the mind may rise to things unknown, and love them.

*He hideth it, and for joy thereof.* That it may be kept safe from malignant spirits and from the praises of men. Let the work be so in public, that the intention remain hidden.
