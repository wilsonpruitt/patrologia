## CHAPTER XIII. (cont.)

VERS. 45.-- *To a merchant.* The blessedness of which we were stripped by the fault of the first man, we ought all to trade with, by understanding [0133D] rightly and working well. *Good pearls.* The good pearls are the law and the prophets. The one precious pearl is the knowledge of the Saviour, for which a man, like Paul, renounced all the mysteries and observances of the law.

VERS. 46.-- *But having found.* That is, Christ, who is over all men, or the spiritual precept of love. Or the Word understood, which was with God, the shell of the flesh being pierced through. Or the sweetness of the heavenly life, in comparison with which all things grow cheap.

VERS. 47.-- *To a net cast.* The prophecy of Jeremias is fulfilled: *Behold I send to you many fishers [n: (Jer. XVI)]* [var: Vulg. Jr 16:16 *Ecce ego mittam piscatores multos*, "Behold I will send many fishers"; the verb is future, and there is no "to you"]. After Peter and Andrew, James and John had heard: *Follow me, I will make you fishers of men:* [var: Vulg. Mt 4:19 *Venite post me, et faciam vos fieri piscatores hominum*, "Come ye after me, and I will make you to be fishers of men"] they wove themselves a net out of the Old [0134A] and the New Testament, and cast it into the sea of the world; which is drawn even to this day, and is stretched among the waves: taking whatsoever falls in, good and bad, whom it draws to the shore, that is, to the end of the world, that then the division may be made. Whence follows: *So shall it be at the consummation of the world,* etc. To a net is compared the Church, or the preaching of the Gospel, which draws from the waves of the world to heaven.

VERS. 48.-- *And by the shore.* The shore is the end of the world, in which the good shall be sent to heaven, the bad to darkness: for in the present they were mingled together.

VERS. 52.-- *Therefore every Scribe instructed,* etc. Because you understand the mysteries which I reveal to you: therefore I will make you preachers, and you shall be like me, who out of the treasure of my knowledge bring forth new things and old, that you [0134B] may do likewise.

The apostles are the scribes and notaries of Christ, marking his words upon the tablets of the heart, and they are strong in the riches of a householder, bringing forth out of the treasure of their doctrines new things and old, because whatsoever is preached in the Gospel they confirm by the voices of the law and of the prophets.

VERS. 54.-- *So that they wondered.* A great blindness, him whom by words and deeds they recognize to be Christ, they despise on account of their acquaintance with his family.

VERS. 55.-- *The carpenter's son.* Not without cause did he will to be called the carpenter's son, not of him whom they suppose, but of the maker of all things. But the Jews, ignorant of this sacrament, despise the works of the divine power by looking upon his fleshly lineage. For they do not consider his present works, but remember his frail infancy.

[0134C]VERS. 57.-- *A prophet.* Christ is called a prophet. Whence Moses says: *The Lord shall raise up to you a prophet out of your seed [n: (Deut. XVIII)]* [var: Vulg. Dt 18:15 *Prophetam de gente tua et de fratribus tuis, sicut me, suscitabit tibi Dominus Deus tuus*, "A prophet of thy nation and of thy brethren, like unto me, shall the Lord thy God raise up unto thee"]. *In his own country.* That is, among the people of the Jews: but he does greater things among the Gentiles.

VERS. 58.-- *And he did not.* He did some miracles, but not many, lest he should condemn the unbelieving. Or, being despised, he does few, lest they be wholly excusable, but he does greater things among the Gentiles.
