## CHAPTER XIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *At that time Herod heard.* ⟦s2⟧ But Luke thus: *And Herod the tetrarch heard [n: (Luc. IX)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ What therefore he says: *This is John [n: (Joan. IV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Either he said it after his hesitation, being confirmed by the opinion of certain men, or being still in doubt: for these words admit of either.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0134D]VERS. 2.-- *And he said.* ⟦s2⟧ Mark the envy of the Jews. ⟦s3⟧ Here a foreigner says that John has risen again, who wrought no sign. ⟦s4⟧ They contend that Jesus, approved by so many miracles, has not risen again. ⟦s5⟧ *And therefore.* ⟦s6⟧ For they perceived that the saints, rising again from the dead, are to be of greater power than when weighed down with frail flesh.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *For Herod.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he spoke of the opinion of the resurrection when he had said nothing of the death, he turns back and relates how and why he died. ⟦s3⟧ This Herod and Philip were brothers, sons of that Herod under whom the Lord fled into Egypt. ⟦s4⟧ *Herodias his brother's wife.* ⟦s5⟧ Herodias was the daughter of king Aretas, whom her own father took away from Philip, and, to Philip's grief, gave to Herod his enemy.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *It is not lawful.* ⟦s2⟧ Herod perhaps held the law of the Jews [0135A], and therefore John forbade him the adultery.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He feared the people.* ⟦s2⟧ He feared the people, but love prevailed, for which he even set God behind. ⟦s3⟧ The fear of God corrects; the fear of men puts off, but does not take away the will: whence it also makes the more eager for the crime those whom for a time it holds back from the crime.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *With an oath.* ⟦s2⟧ Perhaps he swore to this end, that he might prepare an occasion by which he should seem to do unwillingly what he was doing of his own accord. ⟦s3⟧ For he would not have fulfilled this concerning anyone he loved, which he fulfilled concerning the prophet: which he would have done even before, if he could have done it excusably.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] If it should happen that we have perhaps sworn something incautiously, etc., *as far as* nor did he grieve that he had contracted any fault to himself by such perjury.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0135B] [n: (ISID.)] An oath is not to be kept whereby an evil is incautiously promised, as if, etc., *as far as* for it is more tolerable not to fulfil an oath than to remain in defilement.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Being made sorrowful [sic: Constristatus].* ⟦s2⟧ As it seemed to those that sat at meat. ⟦s3⟧ For such was the sorrow of Herod as was, namely, the repentance of Pharaoh.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And he beheaded.* ⟦s2⟧ John was made less by the head, Christ was lifted up on the cross: because the fame of the one decreased, that of the other increased; whence: *He must increase, but I must decrease [n: (Joan. III)]*. ⟦s3⟧ In the severed head of John it is shown that the Jews had lost Christ (who is the head of all the prophets).

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Morally. ⟦s2⟧ The death of John signifies the end of the law, which being ended, one deservedly passes over to Christ, who is the end [0135C] of the law unto justice to everyone that believeth.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And when Jesus had heard it.* ⟦s2⟧ After among the Jews the law and the prophet lose their head and their voice: Jesus passes over to the desert place of the Church, which before had no husband. ⟦s3⟧ *He withdrew.* ⟦s4⟧ He did not flee out of fear, but he withdrew, sparing his enemies, lest they should join murder to murder, giving an example of rashness to be shunned. ⟦s5⟧ Or *he withdrew,* that he might prove the faith of them that believe; whence: *And when the crowds had heard,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ *On foot.* ⟦s7⟧ By their own labour, that they might show the ardour of their mind. ⟦s8⟧ They leave the cities, that is, their former ways of life. ⟦s9⟧ They have the will, but not the strength to arrive. ⟦s10⟧ Therefore Jesus goes out to meet them, and having pity heals them, that a full faith may have its reward at once. ⟦s11⟧ Behold how greatly he is delighted with the devotion of them that believe.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And when it was evening,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Jesus withdraws [0135D] from Judea, goes into the desert, the crowds follow, whom he meets and heals. ⟦s3⟧ This not in the morning or at midday, but in the evening, when the sun of justice had set and the end of the world had drawn near.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *His disciples.* ⟦s2⟧ Carnal, and ignorant of the coming miracle of the refreshment, to whom it had been said: *Go ye not into the way of the Gentiles [n: (Matth. X)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically. ⟦s2⟧ The Jews disdain that the Gentiles should be received, and they murmur: whose voice Isaias uses [n: (Isa. XL)]: *Behold the nations are as a drop of a bucket.* ⟦s3+4⟧ Again: *All nations are as though they were not, but they are before him.* [var: Vulg. Is 40:17 *sic sunt coram eo*, "so are they before him"] But the Lord determined to refresh them by the apostles; whence he subjoins: *But Jesus,* etc.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Send away the crowds.* ⟦s2⟧ It is long that she has followed idols, and therefore she is not to be fed by us with the bread of the word of God.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0136A]VERS. 16.-- *Give ye them.* ⟦s2⟧ He provokes them to give, that, when they testify that they have nothing, the greatness of the sign may be the better known; wherein he also hints that by them the hungry hearts are to be fed.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Five loaves and two fishes.* ⟦s2⟧ By the five loaves and the two fishes the whole Old Testament is signified. ⟦s3⟧ By the five loaves, the five books of Moses, which are well of barley, which is the food of beasts of burden, for to the untaught things rough and coarse must be given. ⟦s4⟧ *For the sensual man perceiveth not the things that are of the spirit of God [n: (I Cor. I)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 2:14 *Animalis autem homo*, "But the sensual man"]. ⟦s5⟧ Within, nevertheless, lies hidden the marrow of a most sweet meaning. ⟦s6⟧ By the two fishes, the prophets and the psalms: whence also after the resurrection he says: *The things that are written in the law and in very many prophets concerning me [n: (Luc. XXIV)]* [var: Vulg. Lc 24:44 *in lege Moysi, et prophetis, et Psalmis de me*, "in the law of Moses, and the prophets, and the psalms concerning me"; the gloss's own point is the psalms].

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Having taken the five,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He took five [0136B] loaves, he did not create new ones, because coming in the flesh he preached no other things than those which had been foretold, and fulfilled them in deed. ⟦s3⟧ *He blessed.* ⟦s4⟧ When, opening their understanding that they might understand the Scriptures, he multiplied them by the gift of an inward sweetness. ⟦s5⟧ He broke, when he brought forth the mysteries of the law into the midst.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *The fragments.* ⟦s2⟧ The more secret things, which cannot be grasped by the untaught, are not to be held negligently, but are to be diligently sought out by the twelve apostles, who are signified by the twelve baskets, and by their successors. ⟦s3⟧ With baskets servile works are carried on. ⟦s4⟧ *And the weak things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the strong [n: (I Cor. I)]*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Besides women and children.* ⟦s2⟧ The frail sex and the lesser age are unworthy of the number. ⟦s3⟧ It signifies those weak in faith, not yet fit for battle.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22. ⟦s2⟧ *And immediately he compelled,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Who, [0136C] as John says, *were about to come to make him king [n: (Joan. VI)]*, who had seen the miracle of the loaves, etc., *as far as* let us pray for them, lest they soften us.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *He went up.* ⟦s2⟧ He fed the crowd as God, he went up to pray as man. ⟦s3⟧ Not as one not having all things in his power, but that he might fulfil the form of a man. ⟦s4⟧ In this there is a duty of piety; there follows a notable miracle of power. ⟦s5⟧ *And when it was evening.* ⟦s6⟧ Rightly is this storm said to be in the evening, because while the Church is in tribulation, she is said to be as it were in the night, and the sun of justice seems to have hidden himself.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *But the boat.* ⟦s2⟧ The ship in the midst of the sea, Jesus alone on the land: because sometimes the Church is so, not only afflicted but also defiled, that she seems utterly forsaken by God: but God does not [0136D] forsake her. ⟦s3⟧ Whence Mark says: *He saw them labouring in rowing [n: (Marc. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Mc 6:48 *videns eos laborantes in remigando*, "seeing them labouring in rowing"]. ⟦s4⟧ Though not present, and though he defers, yet he sees and strengthens.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 25.-- *And in the fourth.* [var: Vulg. Mt 14:25 *Quarta enim vigilia noctis*, "For in the fourth watch of the night"] They are in peril the whole night, because when a man considers himself, he finds nothing but the darkness of straits and the surgings of enemies.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He came to them.* ⟦s2⟧ Mark says: *Willing to pass by them,* for whose deliverance nevertheless he came. ⟦s3⟧ Which happens so that, being troubled for an hour but soon delivered, they may wonder the more, and give thanks; which same thing often happens in the passions of the saints.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *And seeing him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because it often happens that when some sudden abatement of a long tribulation arises, it is not straightway believed to be a true deliverance. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore, the former dread remaining intense [0137A], prayer is directed toward deliverance.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *It is I, fear ye not.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not add who, because from the voice known to them they could recognize him. ⟦s3⟧ Or because he himself is he who said: *He who is hath sent me to you [n: (Exod. III)]*.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Lord, if it be thou.* ⟦s2⟧ If thou art that deliverer, bid me come to the imitation of thee by overcoming pressures.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *And going down.* ⟦s2⟧ Despairing of human help, he treads down pressures with his mind, that he may be conformed to Jesus. ⟦s3⟧ *He walked,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Behold, a man walks upon the waters. ⟦s5⟧ Doubt not therefore that Christ is a true body, because he walked upon the waters.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He made him walk upon the sea, that he might show the divine [0137B] power. ⟦s2⟧ He makes him sink, lest he forget his infirmity, lest he think himself equal to God, and lest he grow proud.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *And when he had begun,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He is left a little to temptation, that faith may be increased; for as he believed that by his command he could be upheld upon the waves, so, while he sinks, that he could be saved by his power.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *The wind ceased.* ⟦s2⟧ In whomsoever God is present by grace, straightway all wars grow quiet.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *And they adored him,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The sign being seen, they confess him the Son of God: why then should Arius bring it about that he is a creature?

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Into the land of Genesar.* ⟦s2⟧ Allegorically. ⟦s3⟧ Genesar is interpreted *the garden of princes.* ⟦s4⟧ By the type therefore of the apostles and of the ship, it is to be understood that the Church, delivered from persecutions, is led by the Lord [0137C] to the shore, that is, to rest, where the inheritance of paradise and the gladness of the first robe shall be restored.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *The hem of his garment,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, the least commandment, or the flesh by which we come to the word of God, and he who touches him by believing is healed of every disease of impiety.
