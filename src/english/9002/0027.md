## CHAPTER XV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Then they drew near.* ⟦s2⟧ The men of Genesar, being less learned, believe, but those who seem wise come for a fight.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CHRYSOST.)] Then, he says, that is, when he had wrought countless signs, when, etc., *as far as* that he might show how great their malice was.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For they wash not.* ⟦s2⟧ Within they reprove nothing, [0137D] who within are polluted with spite. ⟦s3⟧ In vain indeed do they keep their washings, who neglect to wash away the filth of their own hearts and bodies.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Why do you also transgress.* ⟦s2⟧ If you despise the commandments of God for the sake of the traditions of men, why do you reprove the disciples, who let go the commandments of men that they may keep the decrees of God?

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Honour father. [n: (HIER.)]* Honour, in the Scriptures, is not so much in salutations and in the paying of courtesies, as, etc., *as far as* that sons should honour their parents also in the ministering of the necessaries of life.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *But you say,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ But your tradition is contrary. ⟦s3⟧ You compel sons to say this to their parents: [0138A] What I was to have offered to God I spend upon food for thee, and it profits thee, O father, that he should be afraid to eat what has been consecrated. ⟦s4⟧ Or: what I offer to God profits thee thyself. ⟦s5⟧ *Whosoever shall say.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, he will be worthy of eternal life. ⟦s7⟧ Or *shall say,* that is, to say it will profit. ⟦s8⟧ *It shall profit.* ⟦s9⟧ As if he said: No, nay, thou wouldst be sacrilegious if thou didst take it, because I have vowed it to God, *Or it shall profit thee:* if I shall have given it to God.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Well hath he prophesied,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the spirit, the dissembling of the Jews, that in guile they would fight against the Gospel.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *He honoureth me with the lips. ⟦s2⟧ Master, we know that thou art true [n: (Marc. XII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Or, by commending outward cleanness — the inward, and that which is true, is not in them.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0138B]VERS. 11.-- *Not that which entereth in.* ⟦s2⟧ There is nothing outside a man that defiles him within, nor meat offered to idols in this, that it is God's creature; by one word every superstition of observances is struck down, when religion is thought to lie in distinguishing foods.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Then the disciples, drawing near.* ⟦s2⟧ Great is the Pharisees' religion in the matter of foods, who on hearing this word were scandalized, that is, were indignant. ⟦s3⟧ *They were scandalized.* ⟦s4⟧ Σκάνδαλον in Greek, we can say a stumbling-block, or a ruin, or the striking of the foot. ⟦s5⟧ Whence: He that shall scandalize anyone, that is, shall have given occasion of ruin.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Let them alone,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Leave them to their own judgment, because they are past recall. ⟦s3⟧ Whence Paul: *A man that is a heretic, after the first and second correction, avoid* [0138C] [n: (Tit. III)]. [var: Vulg. Tit 3:10 *post unam et secundam correptionem*, "after one and a second rebuke"]

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Expound.* ⟦s2⟧ What was said plainly, the apostles think was said in a parable. ⟦s3⟧ He is a faulty hearer who wishes to understand either obscure things plainly, or things plainly said obscurely.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Out of the heart,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For they are not sent in by the devil, but are born of a man's own will. ⟦s3⟧ For the devil is an inciter, not an author, since neither does he know inward things save through outward bearing and gesture.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *With unwashen hands.* ⟦s2⟧ The spiritual sayings of the prophets, concerning the chastening of the heart and of the body, such as these: *Be washed, be clean [n: (Isa. I)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The Pharisees took them carnally, of the body alone, which it is vain to do.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And going out,* etc. ⟦s2+3⟧ The slanderers being left behind, [0138D] within the borders of Tyre and Sidon, that he might set the daughter free, and by the faith of a Gentile woman convict the perfidy of the Scribes. "The slanderers being left behind" signifies that after the resurrection he would leave behind the perfidy of the Jews, and would go by his preaching to the Gentiles.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *She cried out,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By faith she cries out, whence, she knows how to call him Son of David, confessing the incarnation of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ She prays for her daughter, that is, for her own people not yet believing, that they may be loosed from that very deceit of the devil. ⟦s4⟧ Or, the daughter is the soul or the conscience of anyone within the Church that has been made over to the devil, for which mother Church prays, or the man himself for his defiled conscience.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Who answered not.* ⟦s2⟧ Not out of pride, but lest he should be contrary to his own saying: *Into the way of the Gentiles [0139A] go ye not [n: (Matth. X)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Whence he avoids the slander of the Jews, but the salvation of the Gentiles is reserved for the time of the passion and the resurrection.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *I was not sent.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he said: First I must fulfil the sacrament of my coming among the Jews, and so bring in other sheep, and this by deferring, in the matter of the sign, the petition of this woman.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *The children's bread.* ⟦s2⟧ The word of salvation, which is owed first to the Jews, is not good to give to dogs, or to the Gentiles, that always bark with a dog's madness.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Yea, Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ Under the person of the woman, the marvellous faith of the Church, and her patience, and her humility are proclaimed. ⟦s3⟧ Faith, because she believes that her daughter can be healed. ⟦s4⟧ Patience, because so often despised she persists in her prayers. [0139B] Humility, because she compares herself not to dogs, but to whelps.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For even the whelps.* ⟦s2⟧ The table is Holy Scripture; the children's crumbs are the inward mysteries with which the humble are refreshed under the table: because, being humbly subject to the Scriptures, the whelps are the humble Gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ The little ones eat not loaves, not crusts, that is, not the surface of the letter, but the marrow.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Great is the faith.* ⟦s2⟧ So above, of the centurion: *I have not found so great faith in Israel [n: (Matth. VIII)]*, who likewise (as this Canaanite woman does) prefigured the faith of the Gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ Great is the faith of the Gentiles, who at the hearing of the ear obey: and in God she obtains salvation for herself and for her own. ⟦s4⟧ That he heals the centurion's servant and the Canaanite woman's daughter without coming to them signifies that the Gentiles, [0139C] to whom he did not come by his presence, were to be saved by his word.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And she was healed.* ⟦s2⟧ Here an example is given of catechizing and baptizing infants, who can neither understand nor do anything, but are set free from the devil through the faith and confession of their parents.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *And when he had passed over.* ⟦s2⟧ When in the daughter of the Canaanite woman he had prefigured the salvation of the Gentiles, he returns into Judaea, because when the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in, then all Israel shall be saved. ⟦s3⟧ *Into a mountain.* ⟦s4⟧ The Lord on the mountain, the Word, sits on high: because in his own majesty he could not be understood.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *The blind.* ⟦s2⟧ The blind are those who do not understand, even if they obey those who command. ⟦s3⟧ The deaf, those who do not obey, even if they understand.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0139D]VERS. 32.-- *But Jesus.* ⟦s2⟧ He calls the disciples together, that he may teach masters to share their counsels with their inferiors. ⟦s3⟧ Or that from his speech they may understand the greatness of the sign. ⟦s4⟧ *I have compassion on the crowd.* ⟦s5⟧ The crowd endures with the Lord for three days, because faithful penitents turn themselves to the Lord in deed and in word and in thought, who would faint on the way of the present life if they were left in their own conscience without the food of doctrine. ⟦s6⟧ In the earlier sign the kinsfolk are called to mind, and the neighbours of the five senses, and the Lord does not remember them, but the disciples do, at evening, the sun being now declined. ⟦s7⟧ Here the Lord says that he has compassion, and why: because they have now continued with me three days. ⟦s8⟧ In the whole world the third is the time in which grace is given: the first before the law, the second under the law, [0140A] the third under grace, the fourth is in the world to come, where there will be day without night, toward which, as he presses on, a man is refreshed on the way.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Loaves in the desert.* ⟦s2⟧ The loaves are the preachers, the desert, themselves, that is, they call themselves unfruitful. ⟦s3⟧ As if he said: Whence have we such eloquence that we can suffice for hearers so greedy?

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *And taking.* ⟦s2⟧ That he may show that he rejoices together with us over the salvation of men, and he forms us to give thanks even for temporal things. ⟦s3⟧ *Seven loaves.* ⟦s4⟧ He feeds with the sevenfold Spirit, with apostolic authority joined to it, as with the savour of a few fishes, who refresh us by the example of their life or of their death. ⟦s5⟧ Seven loaves: the Scripture of the New Testament, in which the grace of the Holy Spirit is both revealed and given. ⟦s6⟧ Nor are they of barley, [0140B] as above, because here the life-giving food of the soul is not (as in the law) covered over with figures, like a most clinging husk. ⟦s7⟧ Here there are not two fishes (as in the law two were anointed, the king and the priest), but a few, that is, the saints of the New Testament, who have been snatched from the waves of the world, and endure the troubled sea, and refresh us by their example, lest we faint on the way.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] There upon the grass, that the desires of the flesh may be pressed down, here upon the ground, where the world itself also, etc., *as far as* both refreshments were celebrated on a mountain, because the Scripture of both Testaments enjoins the height of the heavenly precepts and of the rewards: both proclaim the height of Christ.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And that which was left over.* ⟦s2⟧ The higher mysteries which the common crowd does not grasp, the apostles take up and fill [0140C] seven baskets, that is, the hearts of the perfect, enlightened for understanding by the sevenfold grace of the Spirit. ⟦s3⟧ *Seven baskets.* ⟦s4⟧ Baskets are wont to be woven of rush and of palm leaves, and they signify the saints, who set the root of the heart in the very fountain of life, lest they wither, as the rush in the water: and they keep the palm of the eternal reward in their heart.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *And having sent away the crowd.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he teaches that preachers, when the word has been ministered to the crowd, ought themselves to be refreshed within the chamber of the heart with the fruits of the virtues. ⟦s3⟧ *Into the coasts.* ⟦s4⟧ Magedan, which by another name is called Dalmanutha, which is the region about Gerasa. ⟦s5⟧ And it is interpreted *fruits* or tidings, and it signifies the garden of which it is said: *A garden enclosed, a fountain sealed,* where the fruits of the virtues grow. ⟦s6⟧ And there the name of the Lord is announced.
