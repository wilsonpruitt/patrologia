## CHAPTER XVI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0140D]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And they came,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *That he would show a sign from heaven,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ That he who fed many with few things should rain down manna wherewith to refresh all. ⟦s4⟧ Whence John, after the eating of the loaves: *What sign dost thou do, that we may see and believe thee? what dost thou work? ⟦s5⟧ Our fathers did eat manna in the desert [n: (Joan. VI)]*. ⟦s6⟧ They asked by way of tempting, that after the manner of Elias he should send fire from on high, or in the likeness of Samuel should make the thunders roar in the summer season, as though they could not have slandered those things too, and said that they had come to pass from the hidden and various affections of the air.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *When it is evening.* ⟦s2⟧ He rebukes them, because from the order of the elements they distinguish the days, but the signs of the times [0141A] they do not know, since the whole law and the prophets signify the tokens of his coming out of the wonder of the works that he was to do.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *For it is red.* ⟦s2⟧ The apostles suffer after the resurrection, by whom you can know that I shall judge in time to come, because when I do not spare my own good ones but that they suffer, I shall not spare others in time to come.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Signs.* ⟦s2⟧ Things belonging to the time of grace, foretold by the prophets, cannot make you believe concerning my coming. ⟦s3⟧ *But the signs.* ⟦s4⟧ He spoke of the signs of the times concerning his own coming or his passion, to which a rosy sky at evening is like. ⟦s5⟧ And likewise concerning the tribulation before the coming that is to be, to which a rosy sky with gloom is like. ⟦s6⟧ *It shall not be given him.* ⟦s7⟧ But it is given to the faithful from heaven, when, the apostles looking on, he ascended into heaven, [0141B] and sent the Holy Spirit.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *They forgot to take bread.* ⟦s2⟧ How do they not have loaves, who but now, the baskets being filled, went up into the boat, and as they sail hear that they are to beware of the leaven of the Pharisees. ⟦s3⟧ But nevertheless the Scripture says that they forgot to take loaves with them, being caught by the sweetness of the one true and inward bread (who was with them), judging that they cared little about the care of the body and about necessaries, since even natural refreshment itself had passed from their mind.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Of the leaven.* ⟦s2⟧ Leaven is perverse doctrine, which even in small quantity corrupts the whole mass, and draws it into its own savour.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Why do you think.* ⟦s2⟧ Why do you think that I spoke of earthly loaves, about which you need not be in doubt, since out of so few I made so many [0141C] leavings abound?

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees.* ⟦s2⟧ Of superstitious doctrine, which after the manner of leaven feigns that it has the substance of true bread. ⟦s3⟧ By this he hints what the five loaves or the seven signify. ⟦s4⟧ For if by leaven he means heretical teachings, the loaves are true doctrine.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Into the parts of Caesarea Philippi.* ⟦s2⟧ This is the place where the Jordan rises at the foot of Lebanon, out of Jor and Dan, two springs. ⟦s3⟧ *That men say he is.* ⟦s4⟧ They are men who speak of the Son of man: for they are gods who understand the Godhead. ⟦s5⟧ *The Son of man?* ⟦s6⟧ The Lord professes of himself the lowliness of his humanity, while the disciples understand his Godhead.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Some, John the Baptist.* ⟦s2⟧ So they could [0141D] err about Elias or about the rest of the others, as Herod erred about John when he said: *John, whom I beheaded, is risen from the dead: and therefore mighty works are wrought in him [n: (Marc. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Mc 6:16 *Quem ego decollavi Joannem, hic a mortuis resurrexit*, "John, whom I beheaded, he is risen again from the dead", and the clause about the mighty works belongs to Mc 6:14, *et propterea virtutes operantur in illo*]. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore he wills that their errors be set forth, so that, these being driven off, the disciples may bring forth the truth, not out of the opinion of the common people, but out of the Lord's revelation.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Simon Peter answering.* ⟦s2⟧ Therefore one answers, and to one answer is made on behalf of all, that unity may be kept in all. ⟦s3⟧ *Of the living God.* ⟦s4⟧ For a distinction from the false gods, which the Gentiles set up for themselves either out of dead men or out of sensible matter.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Barjona.* ⟦s2⟧ Well is he the son of the dove, because he followed the Lord with a devout simplicity, or because he was full of spiritual grace. ⟦s3⟧ He is called the son of the Holy Spirit, [0142A] who was shown in the dove, by him whom he had called the Son of the living God, and to the son of the dove the Father reveals, because the operation of the Father and of the Son with the Holy Spirit is one. ⟦s4⟧ He reveals, I say, the mystery of faith, because flesh and blood could not reveal it, that is, men puffed up with carnal wisdom, who are not sons of the dove, and therefore strangers to the wisdom of the Spirit; of whom above: *Others John the Baptist, and others Elias,* etc.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Thou art.* I do not say, thou shalt be called, but thou art Peter, for the strength of thy faith and the constancy of thy confession. ⟦s3⟧ *Peter.* ⟦s4⟧ From me, the Rock, yet so that I keep for myself the dignity of the foundation: thou shalt set clean stones upon me, and shalt cast away the leprous.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Just as light and the other names, as: *You are the salt of the earth,* were allotted to the apostles by the Lord, so to Simon he gave from [0142B] himself the name of Peter.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the gates.* ⟦s2⟧ The gates of hell are sins, threats, blandishments, heresies, by which the weak rush into death, who are to be believed to have built the house of their profession not truly upon the rock, but upon the sand, that is, not to follow Christ with a simple and true intention, but to have put forward the habit for some earthly cause. ⟦s3⟧ For he who receives the faith of Christ in the inmost love of the heart easily overcomes whatever assails him from without.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And whatsoever thou shalt bind.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, whomsoever thou shalt have adjudged to eternal punishments as persisting in his sins, or whomsoever thou shalt have absolved as humble and truly penitent, so shall it be also in heaven.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth. [n: (HIER.)]* The other apostles have [0142C] indeed the same judicial power, to whom after the resurrection he says: *Receive ye the Holy Spirit [n: (Joan. XX)]*, etc., *as far as* whence in Leviticus the lepers are bidden to show themselves to the priests, whom the priests do not make lepers, but discern who are clean or unclean, so also here.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *That they should tell no man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Above he commands them to preach his coming, here that they should not say that he is Jesus. ⟦s3⟧ But it is one thing to preach Christ, which is a common name of dignity: another to preach Jesus, which is the Saviour's own name. ⟦s4⟧ As if he said: It does not profit that my majesty be publicly preached to the peoples, who are to see me suffer many things, which will be lawful only then, when the resurrection shall have shown the power of the Godhead. ⟦s5⟧ *Jesus Christ.* ⟦s6⟧ The Saviour: lest they should be scandalized in him whom a little [0142D] after they are to see spat upon and crucified.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *From that time,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Behold the reason why the preaching was forbidden, as if he said: Preach me then, when I shall have suffered these things, because before that it does not profit.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Far be it.* ⟦s2⟧ Peter does not wish his own confession to be destroyed, nor does he think that the Son of God can be slain.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Go.* ⟦s2⟧ Above: all those glorious things were promised for the future, not given in the present. ⟦s3⟧ *I will build my Church, and to thee I will give the keys,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Which if he had given at once, error would never afterwards have found place in him. ⟦s5⟧ *Because thou savourest not.* ⟦s6⟧ It does not please thee that I should redeem the world by my death, which is the will of the Father: but thou savourest these things carnally.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0143A]VERS. 24.-- *If any man will.* ⟦s2⟧ The passion and the resurrection having been foretold to the apostles, he exhorts both them and the crowds to follow. ⟦s3⟧ *Let him deny himself.* ⟦s4⟧ That which is by oldness, that he may take hold of that which is beyond himself. ⟦s5⟧ Vices being removed, let virtue follow: let him take up his cross, and follow me. ⟦s6+7⟧ That which was by oldness, he had denied who said: *But I myself live, now not I.* [var: Vulg. Gal 2:20 *Vivo autem, jam non ego*, without the emphatic *ego* Migne adds after *Vivo autem*] But that which was beyond himself he had begun: *yet Christ liveth in me [n: (Gal. II)]*. ⟦s8⟧ *Let him take up his cross.* ⟦s9⟧ That is: the cross is taken up either when the body is afflicted through abstinence, or when the mind is afflicted through compassion for one's neighbour. ⟦s10⟧ Paul had the cross of abstinence when he said [n: (II Cor. XI)]: *I chastise my body and bring it into subjection.* ⟦s11⟧ And the cross of compassion too, when he says: *Who is weak, and I am not weak?*

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0143B]VERS. 26.-- *For what doth it profit.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church has a time of persecution: it has also a time of peace. ⟦s3⟧ These times he designates in his precepts. ⟦s4⟧ For in the time of persecution the soul must be laid down, and in the time of peace earthly desires must be broken.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Or what,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Let neither gains, nor fear, nor anything else draw a man back from salvation: because *what exchange shall a man give,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *I will take the chalice of salvation, and I will call upon the name of the Lord [n: (Psal. CXV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Or: a man will be able to give nothing earthly for a soul that is lost.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *For the Son.* ⟦s2⟧ Nor let it seem hard to suffer this: *Because the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, that shall be revealed in us [n: (Rom. VIII)]*.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *There are some.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the apostles were untaught, there is promised them even in the present life some [0143C] consolation, that they may believe the more surely concerning the life to come. ⟦s3⟧ *In the kingdom.* ⟦s4⟧ In the kingdom of eternal blessedness, or by showing his power in the Church that now is.
