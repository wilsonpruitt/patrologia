## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 1.-- *And after days,* etc. Mark says the same, but Luke eight. These therefore set down the middle days, Luke adds, etc., *as far as* and in the world to come they shall be parted in every way, when he shall hide them in the hiding of his face from the disturbance of men.

*Peter.* The names of these men are fitting. Each ought to be Peter, that is, firm in faith. Then James, that is, the supplanter of vices. Then John, that is, one ascribing all things to divine grace against the pride [0143D] of life, which is wont to arise out of the virtues.

VERS. 2.-- *He was transfigured.* The form which he had by nature he showed, not losing the flesh which he had assumed by will. But that body which he had by nature he gave to his disciples at the supper, not mortal, not corruptible. *His face did shine.* As a pattern of the blessedness and brightness to come, which the just shall see, the wicked being first taken away. *And his garments,* etc. They were made white, which they were not before, because it hath not yet appeared what we shall be, but when it shall appear, we shall be like to him. Mark says, such as a fuller upon earth cannot make. That is, God cannot in this life make them so bright, because no one is without sin. No cleanser of himself upon earth will do what God does in heaven, [0144A] cleansing his garment from every defilement. The fuller is a teacher of souls, or one who cleanses his own body.

VERS. 3.-- *And behold.* Of what sort they were, or what they spoke, Luke says: *Seen in majesty, and they spoke of his decease which he was to accomplish in Jerusalem [n: (Luc. IX)]*, that is, his passion and resurrection. They signify the oracles, etc., *as far as* that if Peter, seeing the humanity glorified, is affected with so great joy that he would never wish to be parted from the sight of it, what is to be thought of those who have deserved to see the Godhead.

VERS. 5.-- *As he was yet speaking.* That he who sought a tabernacle may learn that in the resurrection they are to be sheltered not by the roof of houses, but by the grace of the Holy Spirit. *Behold a bright cloud,* etc. As when the Lord was baptized, [0144B] so also when he was glorified the mystery of the holy Trinity is shown: because his glory, which in baptism we confess by believing, in the resurrection we shall praise by seeing. The Holy Spirit there in a dove, here in a bright cloud. Because he who now keeps the faith with a simple heart will then contemplate in open vision what he has believed, and will be sheltered by that grace by which he will be enlightened. *And behold a voice,* etc. Because he asked imprudently, he did not deserve an answer from the Lord: but the voice of the Father bears witness concerning the Son, and teaches Peter, and in him the rest, the truth. *This is the Son.* Moses and Elias are servants, and together with us they ought to prepare in their hearts a tabernacle for the Lord. *Hear ye him.* This the Father now enjoins, which Moses, giving testimony to the incarnation, foretold: *He shall raise up a prophet [n: (Deut. XVIII)]*, etc. As if he said: [0144C] This is he whom Moses promised.

VERS. 6.-- *And the disciples hearing fell upon their face.* For they understood, higher than their sense, the one Godhead of both. They feared, either because they knew that they had erred, or because the cloud covered them, or because they heard the voice of the Father.

VERS. 7.-- *Arise,* etc. Fear being driven off, doctrine is given, which while fear remained would not be heard.

VERS. 8.-- *And lifting up their eyes,* etc. The cloud being taken away, they see Jesus, and that Moses and Elias had vanished: because after the shadow of the law and of the prophets departed, which had covered the apostles with its veil, the true light is found in the Gospel. *But only Jesus.* For if Moses and Elias had remained with Jesus, the voice of the Father would seem uncertain, to which of them it had rather given testimony.

[0144D]VERS. 9.-- *Tell it to no man unless,* etc. [var: Vulg. Mt 17:9 *Nemini dixeritis visionem*, "Tell the vision to no man", and Migne prints *nisi* where the received text has *visionem*] Lest the glory of the kingdom to come, being shown beforehand, should be past belief, and lest among the untaught the cross that followed should make a scandal.

VERS. 10.-- *That Elias must,* etc. It is the tradition of the Pharisees, according to Malachi the prophet, that Elias will come before the coming of the Saviour, and will turn back the hearts of the fathers to the sons and of the sons to the fathers, and will restore all things to their ancient state. The apostles therefore judge that this transformation of glory which they had seen is the last, and they say: If thou hast already come in glory, how does thy forerunner not appear, especially because they had seen Elias too depart.

VERS. 13.-- *Then they understood,* etc. From the token [0145A] of his own passion, which he had often spoken of, and of his forerunner's, which they saw fulfilled.

VERS. 14.-- *And when he was come.* The places agree with the things, etc., *as far as* he comforts the weak, teaches, chastens. *For he is a lunatic.* Mark calls him deaf and dumb, etc., *as far as* that through the creature he may defame the creator.

VERS. 15.-- *And I brought him,* etc. He secretly accuses the disciples, whereas often the impossibility of healing comes not from the fault of those who heal, but of those who are to be healed.

VERS. 16.-- *Answering.* Not overcome by weariness does he who is meek break out into words, but as a physician does with a sick man who acts against his own prescriptions.

VERS. 17.-- *And he rebuked.* Because he was angry not with the man, but with the vice: and through one man he convicts the Jews [0145B] of unbelief. Whence he adds: *Bring him hither to me. And the demon went out of him.* Mark says, etc., *as far as* than before, when it possessed him quietly.

VERS. 19.-- *Because of unbelief.* Behold, when we are not heard, it is no impossibility on the part of him who grants, but the fault of him who prays. For on the contrary, if your faith were as fervent as it is troubled, it would grow warm more and more. *As a grain of mustard seed.* He compares perfect faith to a grain of mustard seed, which is lowly in appearance and fervent in the breast: mean to those who see it, and appearing of no strength, but when bruised by pressures it shows what it has within.

VERS. 20.-- *But this kind.* He shows how the insolence of the devil is overcome, that we may know that all the graver things are to be overcome by fastings and prayers, and that the anger [0145C] of God against us can be appeased by this remedy. *But by prayer.* This changeableness of carnal pleasures is not changed, etc., *as far as* whence it is said: *Pray without ceasing [n: (I Thess. V)]*.

VERS. 21.-- *The Son of man.* He always mixes sad things with glad, that when they have come they may be borne less grievously, as things foreseen.

VERS. 22.-- *And they were sorrowful.* This came not from unbelief, but from love: because they cannot bear to hear anything lowly of him.

VERS. 23.-- *And when they were come to Capharnaum.* Through Augustus Caesar Judaea was made tributary, etc., *as far as* whether he pays the tribute, or gainsays Caesar.

VERS. 24.-- *What is thy opinion.* Before Peter [0145D] can suggest it, the Lord asks, lest the disciples be scandalized at the demand of the tribute, since they see that he knows the things that were done in his absence. *The kings of the earth.* Our Lord was, according to the flesh also, a king's son, born of David, and the Word of the Almighty: therefore, as a son of kings, he did not owe tribute, but for the lowliness of the flesh it becomes him to fulfil all justice.

VERS. 25.-- *Then the sons are free,* etc. The sons of a kingdom are free in every kingdom, then much more are the sons of that kingdom free under which all kingdoms are, in any earthly kingdom whatever.
