## CHAPTER XVIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Who, thinkest thou,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ From the equality of the price [0146A] by which, in the paying of the tribute, he had been reckoned with the Lord, they judged that Peter was set above all the apostles. ⟦s3⟧ *In the kingdom of heaven.* ⟦s4⟧ Or the kingdom of God is the teaching of the Gospel, which we ought to obey simply and without any drawing back, as a child does not contradict his teachers.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And calling to him.* ⟦s2⟧ According to Mark, Jesus embraces the child, signifying that the humble are worthy of his embrace and of his love. ⟦s3⟧ *A little one,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ A little one in age, that he might show a likeness. ⟦s5⟧ Or he set himself in the midst, who came not to be ministered unto, but to minister. ⟦s6⟧ Or *a little one,* that is, he set the Holy Spirit in their hearts, that by humility they might shun pride.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Whosoever therefore shall humble himself,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord invites [0146B] his disciples, and all others, that what a child has by nature, that is, innocence and humility, this the disciples and all others may have by virtue. ⟦s3⟧ *As this little child.* ⟦s4⟧ He will be innocent as this little child, who does not persist in anger: Jesus [cj: *Jesus*; read *laesus*, "when hurt"] does not remember: having seen a woman he does not desire her.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *But he that shall scandalize.* ⟦s2⟧ Although this is said generally against all, etc., *as far as* we ought to take counsel, it is expedient, etc. ⟦s3⟧ *It is expedient.* ⟦s4⟧ Because it is much better to undergo a short punishment for a fault than to be kept for eternal torments. ⟦s5⟧ *For God will not judge twice for the same thing.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Woe to the man.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he is not judged according to the profit that comes of it, but according to the outcome that he intends.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0146C]VERS. 8.-- *And if thy hand.* ⟦s2⟧ He taught above that we should not scandalize believers, etc., *as far as* that under the occasion of piety thou shouldst lie open to scandals. ⟦s3⟧ *It is good for thee,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ It is better to lead a solitary life than for the necessaries of the present life to lose life eternal.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *See that you despise not.* ⟦s2+3⟧ Thus I enjoin upon you the severity of putting away all friendships, etc., *as far as* the salvation of the weak as far as you can. *For their angels.* ⟦s4⟧ Why are they not to be despised? because for them angels are daily sent. [n: (HIER.)] Great is the dignity of souls, that each one should have, from the beginning of its birth, an angel deputed to guard it. ⟦s5⟧ *They always see,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ They enjoy the manifest vision of God, etc., *as far as* they run within him who is everywhere.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *If a man have a hundred,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord [0146D] had a hundred sheep, etc., *as far as* to whom, for perfection, unity is wanting. ⟦s3⟧ *Doth he not leave,* etc. [n: (GREG.)] Because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant,* etc., *as far as* whereas it is only so much, if the unjust man condemns what he has done amiss.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 13.-- *He shall rejoice,* etc. [var: Vulg. Mt 18:13 *gaudet super eam magis*, "he rejoiceth for it more": the present, where Migne prints the future *Gaudebit* — the gloss itself reverts to the present *gaudet* in its next words] The Lord rejoices more over our restoration (because he makes us rejoice the more) than over the unchangeableness of the angels. ⟦s3⟧ For the power and the humility of God are known to be greater in our restoration than in our creation.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *But if he shall offend against thee,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For if against God, it is not in our judgment. ⟦s3⟧ In this order we ought to shun scandals, that we hurt no one: and that we rebuke the sinner [0147A] with a zeal for justice, and open the bowels of mercy to him that repents. ⟦s4⟧ *Rebuke him,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ He does not bid us forgive the sinner indiscriminately; but him that repents. ⟦s6⟧ He sins thus, who seeing his brother sin keeps silent: as does he who does not pardon the penitent. ⟦s7⟧ For what he said, "if he repents, forgive," he prefaced by "if he shall have sinned, rebuke." ⟦s8⟧ Let pardon be neither hard, nor indulgence slack.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Tell the Church.* ⟦s2⟧ That it may hold him in detestation, etc., *as far as* they are condemned, that a human sentence is confirmed by the divine sentence. ⟦s3⟧ *As the heathen.* ⟦s4⟧ More detestable is he who acts under the name of a believer, etc., *as far as* through thefts and through perjuries.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Amen I say,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ So far is your sentence confirmed by the divine, whether for receiving the penitent, or for casting off the obstinate.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0147B]VERS. 20.-- *If two.* ⟦s2⟧ Typically. ⟦s3⟧ Where the spirit, the soul and the body have agreed, so that there is no war between them, the flesh lusting against the spirit and the spirit against the flesh, whatsoever they ask shall be done. ⟦s4⟧ Because if the body wills what the spirit wills, the asking is only for good things. ⟦s5+6⟧ *There am I in me.* [var: Vulg. Mt 18:20 *ibi sum in medio eorum*, "there am I in the midst of them" — the words the gloss goes on to quote, *ut dicat Deum in medio esse eorum*] That is, every discourse calls to concord and to the peace which he so far commends that he says God is in the midst of them, because peace and charity set up their seat in the peaceable, and he affirms that by the merit of unity all things that are to be asked are to be obtained.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Therefore is likened.* ⟦s2⟧ That you may know that one must forgive one's fellow servants, I will show by a likeness, that if that king and lord forgave his servant, much more must a man forgive his fellow servants.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *And his wife.* ⟦s2⟧ Him, not only the people, [0147C] but also the prelates and the subjects, together with all their household or substance, he delivered over to foreign nations for the greatness of their offences: that what they would not pay by vow, they might pay by torment. ⟦s3⟧ He commanded them to pay the penalties for their covetousness and their works, as for a wife and children.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *And moved with pity.* ⟦s2⟧ The merciful Lord not only loosed the Jewish captivity, but also promised that at the coming of Christ he would give pardon of all sins, if they persisted in what they had begun. ⟦s3⟧ *He forgave him.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, he loosed him from the weight of carnal observances, and forgave the debt, that is, his former transgressions.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *He found one.* ⟦s2⟧ The Gentile people he wearied, as though it were his debtor: saying that the grace of Christ was not unto salvation for them, not only by persuasions, but even by torments [0147D] he exacted carnal observances from them.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *Thou wicked servant.* ⟦s2⟧ Often have I forgiven thee thy debts, when thou didst ask in thy straits; but being set free thou hadst no compassion, nay rather thou persecutest the prophets and the apostles, and, ungrateful and presuming upon justice, thou despisest the Gentiles, judging them unworthy of thy fellowship in Christ.
