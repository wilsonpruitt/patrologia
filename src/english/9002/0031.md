## CHAPTER XIX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *And they came.* ⟦s2⟧ As though he might be caught in a dilemma. ⟦s3⟧ If he should say: She is to be put away for any cause whatever, and another is to be married, the preacher of chastity acts against himself. ⟦s4⟧ If he says she is not to be put away, he acts against the teaching of Moses and of God. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore he tempers his answer, setting the natural law and the first sentence of God over against the second, [0148A] which was granted not by the will of God, but by the necessity of those who sin.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And female.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not say females, that there may be one marriage and not many, which was being sought by the putting away of former wives.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He shall cleave to a wife.* ⟦s2⟧ If a wife be evil in her ways, if wanton, or of any such sort whatever, willing or unwilling we must bear with her. ⟦s3⟧ For when we were free, we willingly subjected ourselves to servitude. ⟦s4⟧ If a bondwoman is put under a free man, or a bondman under a free woman, it is not reckoned a marriage after it has been found out: so also concerning kindred. ⟦s5⟧ *And they shall be two in flesh.* ⟦s6⟧ The reward of marriage is one flesh out of two: chastity joined to the spirit is made one spirit. ⟦s7⟧ It was done by the wholesome counsel of God that a man should embrace a portion of his own body [0148B] in the woman, lest that should be thought different from himself which he knew to have been fashioned out of himself.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Let not man separate.* ⟦s2⟧ Man separates, when out of desire for a second he puts away the first: God separates (who also joins) when by consent, for the worship of God, because the time is short, we so have wives as though not having them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Why then did Moses,* etc. [n: (CHRYS.)] Doctrine displeases sinners, etc., *as far as* that the interpretation of the truth be passed over in silence.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *And shall marry another, committeth adultery. ⟦s2⟧ And he that shall marry her that is put away.* ⟦s3⟧ Because it could happen that someone should bring a false charge against an innocent woman, and for the sake of a second fasten a crime upon his first wife: so he bids him put away the first in such a way that while she lives he may not have a second. ⟦s4⟧ And lest a wife [0148C] should do the same to her husband, the same caution is enjoined, that she should not have a second husband. ⟦s5⟧ And because an adulteress does not fear reproach, it is said to the husband that if he marries such a one he is under the charge of adultery, the Scripture says: *He that keepeth an adulteress is foolish and wicked [n: (Prov. XVIII)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *It is not expedient.* ⟦s2⟧ They saw the heavy burden of wives, that they are wrathful, given to drink and the like, who nevertheless must be borne with, because, when we were free, we subjected ourselves to servitude.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *For there are.* ⟦s2⟧ There are three kinds of eunuchs, two carnal: of those who are so born, and of those who have been cut by captivity and by the pleasures of their mistresses. ⟦s3⟧ The third is spiritual: those who, though they could be husbands, are continent for Christ's sake. ⟦s4⟧ *Who were made so.* ⟦s5⟧ Among these are reckoned also those who under the appearance of religion feign [0148D] chastity. ⟦s6+7⟧ *He that can.* It is the voice of one exhorting, not of one commanding, as though each should weigh his own strength, whether he is able to fulfil the precepts of virginity and of chastity.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Suffer the little children.* ⟦s2⟧ If they are to be holy, why do you forbid them to come to the Father? ⟦s3⟧ If evil, why do you pronounce sentence before the fault. ⟦s4⟧ *Of such.* ⟦s5⟧ For as to what sort they now are, that indeed is mine, in that you honour them: as to what sort they are afterwards to be, that is their own, in that you have pity on them. ⟦s6⟧ He does not say, of these, but, of such: that they may show that it is not age that reigns, but character: and to those who have a like innocence and simplicity he promises the reward.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *And when he had laid on.* ⟦s2⟧ He lays hands upon little ones, when he bestows the help of grace upon the humble.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And behold one. [n: (BED.)]* He had heard [0149A] that those like a little child are worthy of the kingdom, and he wishes it to be set forth more certainly, not by parables but openly, by what merits he might obtain life. [n: (HIER.)] This young man was both rich and proud. ⟦s2⟧ And according to another evangelist he questions him not with the intent of one learning but of one tempting.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Why askest thou me concerning good?* ⟦s2⟧ Because he called him a good master, and did not confess him God, or the Son of God: he says that any holy man whatever is not good in comparison with God. ⟦s3⟧ But Christ does not refuse the testimony of goodness, but shuts out the error of a master apart from God. ⟦s4⟧ He does not deny that he is a good master, but he says that no master apart from God is good. ⟦s5⟧ *Keep the commandments.* ⟦s6⟧ This man had asked, what shall I do? in return he does not say, learn: because God, the maker of the rational creature, had taught him: showing [0149B] that he is not good who asks about the good, but he who does what is good.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *The young man saith to him. [n: (BED.)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 19:20 *Dicit illi adolescens*, and Migne prints *ei* where the received text has *illi*] This man is not to be thought (as some say) either to have tempted the Lord, or to have lied about his own life, but he confessed simply as he had lived: otherwise (as another Evangelist says) Jesus, looking into the secrets of his heart, would not be said to love him. ⟦s2⟧ For he loves those who keep the commandments of the law, but what is lacking there he adds for those who wish to be perfect; whence there follows: *If thou wilt be perfect. ⟦s3⟧ All these. [n: (HIER.)]* He lies. ⟦s4⟧ For if he loved his neighbour as himself, he would have fulfilled it in deed, and would not have been sad when he was bidden to give his own goods to the poor.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *A rich man hardly.* ⟦s2⟧ Not the rich who remain such, but those who have ceased to be such, shall enter. ⟦s3⟧ Of all things it is safer [0149C] neither to have nor to love riches.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Than for a rich man. [n: (ISID.)]* No rich man shall enter, that is, no one trusting in riches, as Jesus expounds it according to Mark.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *They wondered.* ⟦s2⟧ Because they counted in the number of the rich all who love riches, even if they do not have them. ⟦s3⟧ For otherwise, since the crowd of the poor is greater, they would not say: *Who shall be able to be saved?*

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *God.* ⟦s2⟧ Who turns them from the love of earthly things to the charity of eternal things: not that the covetous and the proud enter.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *In the regeneration.* ⟦s2⟧ There are two regenerations, the first of water and of the Holy Spirit, the second in the resurrection. ⟦s3⟧ So there are two adoptions and two resurrections. ⟦s4⟧ *The Son of man.* ⟦s5⟧ That is, when the form of a servant [0149D] which was judged shall have exercised judicial power, you also shall be judges with me. ⟦s6⟧ *Upon twelve seats.* ⟦s7⟧ By the twelve seats, the whole company of those who judge. ⟦s8⟧ By the twelve tribes is understood the whole company of those who are to be judged. ⟦s9⟧ Those who left all things and followed the Lord, these shall be judges: those who, having lawful goods, used them rightly, shall be judged, to whom it shall be said: *Come, ye blessed of my Father: For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat.* ⟦s10⟧ For there are two orders of the good.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *A hundredfold.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, he who shall have left carnal things for the Lord shall receive spiritual things, which in comparison and by their own worth will be such as if a hundred were set against a small number. ⟦s3⟧ Mark says: *A hundred times as much now in this time, with persecutions, brethren and [0150A] sisters, and in the world to come life everlasting [n: (Marc. X)]*, that is, in this life full of persecutions they taste beforehand the joy of the kingdom, and enjoy the sincere love of all the elect. ⟦s4⟧ But because many grow lukewarm or fall back, he adds: *But many shall be first [n: (Luc. XV)]*, etc.
