## CHAPTER XX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The kingdom is like.* ⟦s2⟧ He shows by a parable that the first shall be last, and the last first. ⟦s3⟧ This father of the household is our Creator, who has a vineyard, the universal Church, which from Abel to the last of the elect who is to be born at the end has sent forth as many branches as it has brought forth saints. ⟦s4+5⟧ This father went out to dress his vineyard in the morning, at the third hour, the sixth, the ninth, the eleventh he hired workmen, from the beginning of this world to its end: he does not cease to send preachers to school the people of the faithful. [0150B] *The kingdom is like.* ⟦s6⟧ Not to the man only, but to the whole business carried on by the man, and so likewise in like cases. ⟦s7⟧ *Early in the morning.* ⟦s8⟧ Plainly he began from the first just man to invite preachers, with an everlasting wage, to plant the Church.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *By agreement.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, by a confirmed promise that they should be reformed to the image of God, if by building up the Church they should press on in the commandments of the decalogue.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For a penny. [n: (HIER.)]* The penny bears the image of the king, etc., *as far as* or in your last days come to your senses.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From the last even unto,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because those who at the Lord's coming have been called to the kingdom arrive without any lateness, equal to those who began from the beginning of the world, all indeed have the same heaven, but star differs from star in brightness. ⟦s3⟧ He began from the last, when he brought the thief into paradise [0150C] before Peter. ⟦s4⟧ Because those who come late are sometimes rewarded first, because they go forth out of the body before those who were called in boyhood.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Or is it not lawful.* ⟦s2⟧ A foolish question against the goodness of God. ⟦s3⟧ The thing to be asked would be, not whether he gave what he did not owe: but whether he did not give what he did owe. ⟦s4⟧ *Is thy eye,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Truly the Jews had a crooked intent, who forbade the apostles to speak to the Gentiles, that they might be saved.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *So the last shall be first. [n: (AUG.)]* That is, the Jews shall be turned from the head into the tail: and the Gentiles from the tail into the head. ⟦s2⟧ Whence Moses in the book of Deuteronomy: *The stranger that sojourneth with thee shall go up above thee: he shall be for the head, and thou shalt be for the tail.* ⟦s3⟧ But yet [0150D] neither all the first nor all the last belong to the number of the elect.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Behold we go up,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] This he had often told them, etc., *as far as* let them not doubt that he would rise again.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *To be mocked,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Therefore the Lord would not prosper in the world but suffer heavy things, that he might show us who fell through delight with what bitterness we ought to return.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Then came to him.* ⟦s2⟧ Having heard of the resurrection she thought that he would reign on the spot (forgetful of the second coming), and with a woman's greed she desires things present, forgetful of things to come.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Say that,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ A good desire, but [0151A] an unconsidered petition. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore not the will, not the purpose is blamed, but the ignorance: and if simplicity ought not to obtain what it asks, it does not deserve to be put to confusion.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *You know not.* ⟦s2+3⟧ You who ask or seek the seat of glory which you have not yet deserved, the summit of honour delights those whom the way of labour has not yet exercised. ⟦s4⟧ *You can.* And this he knew, that they were able to imitate his passion; but he says this so that, with him questioning and them answering, we may all hear: that no one can reign with Christ unless he has imitated his passion.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *The chalice.* ⟦s2⟧ As James was beheaded by Herod, and John was sent into a cauldron of boiling oil and banished to Patmos, martyrdom was not wanting to his mind. ⟦s3⟧ *It is not mine to give.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, the kingdom of heaven is not the giver's but the receiver's. ⟦s5⟧ *For there is no respect of persons [0151B] with God:* but whosoever is worthy receives that which is prepared not for the person, but for the life. ⟦s6⟧ If therefore you are such as may obtain it, you shall receive what the Father has prepared for the victors. ⟦s7⟧ Or, it is not mine to give to the proud, which you still are, but to others for whom it is prepared. ⟦s8⟧ Therefore be not you either what you are, but be others, that is, be humbled, you who already wish to be exalted, and it is prepared for you.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *And the ten hearing it.* ⟦s2⟧ As those asked carnally to be above all, so these too were carnally grieved that anyone should be before them.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *And behold two blind men.* ⟦s2⟧ Of these Mark commemorates one, because he had been most famous in that city: who, cast out from great prosperity, was of a most notorious misery. ⟦s3⟧ Blind indeed and a beggar, whence he records both his name [0151C] and the name of his father, Bartimeus the son of Timeus, which does not easily occur in the others, save in Jairus, who was noble in that place too. ⟦s4⟧ But the notoriety of the man enlightened won great fame for the miracle. ⟦s5⟧ Coming to Jericho he enlightens one, which Luke tells, because drawing near to death he preached the light of the Gospel to the Jews, going out, two, because after the resurrection to the Jews and to the Gentiles. ⟦s6⟧ Of whom Mark sets down one, who writes to the Gentiles.
