## CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 1.-- *And when they drew nigh.* Here Matthew passes over the things that were done before he entered Jerusalem, which the others treat at greater length. *And when they drew nigh.* The Lord drew nigh to Jerusalem [0151D] spiritually when he was made flesh, he visited the Church, in which is the vision of peace, and reconciled her to himself by confession. For Bethphage is called the house of the mouth. *Bethphage.* Bethphage was a hamlet of priests on mount Olivet, on the side of which mountain is the town of Bethany. Bethphage, the house of the mouth. Bethany, the house of obedience. These the Lord, journeying to Jerusalem, exalts by his presence, because before his passion he filled many by his teaching with the gifts of devout confession and of obedience. And those towns are set on the mount of Olives, that is, in Christ the Lord, from whom anointing and light come to all; whence: *A city cannot be hid, set upon a mountain.--To mount Olivet: then Jesus,* etc. Looking to the loftiness of mercy, [0152A] ἔλεος is interpreted *mercy.* The nature of oil is to be the servant of light, and the solace of labours and of pains, and it excels the other liquids. *He sent.* Because he does not wish the lamp which he received to be hidden under a bushel. *Two disciples.* Two, as being about to preach the knowledge of truth and cleanness of work, and the sacrament of the twofold love. Some say that these two are Peter and Philip, who first brought Gentiles to Christ, Philip, Samaria, which they interpret as the *she-ass*, Peter, Cornelius, as it were the colt of Samaria.

VERS. 2.-- *And the colt.* The colt is the people of the Gentiles, on which none of the teachers had put the bridle of correction, free and wanton, which itself also was ensnared in the bonds of sins. *For all have sinned and need the grace of God [n: (Rom. III)]* [var: Vulg. Rom 3:23 *egent gloria Dei*, "need the glory of God"]. Others, of the colt only: Matthew, [0152B] who writes to the Hebrews, speaks also of the she-ass, teaching that salvation is not to be despaired of for the Hebrews if they repent. Mark says that the colt was found before the gate where two ways met. The gate is Christ, outside which the people of the Gentiles stood bound: not in the one way of faith, but in divers errors.

VERS. 5.-- *Tell ye the daughter of Sion.* The daughter of Sion is Jerusalem, set on mount Sion. Mystically. The Church belonging to the Jerusalem above, which is the mother of us all, of which no small part was then in the people of Israel. *King.* Lest you say: *We have no king but Caesar [n: (Joan. XIX)]*. *To thee.* If thou believest; against thee if thou believest not. *Upon a she-ass,* etc. He did not sit upon both according to the letter, but in the hearts of both peoples. The Lord could go up upon both, coming down from the one, and going up upon [0152C] the other.

VERS. 7.-- *And they brought.* So the apostles also loosed the world by the power of Christ from the power of the devil, and laid their garments upon it. But the rest of the crowd, that is, the Jews, strew their garments, that is, the commandments of the law, because they were to be trodden underfoot by Christians, in the way, that is, in Christ. *Upon them.* The garments of the apostles are the divine precepts and spiritual grace, by which the foulness of our flesh is covered. Naked of these before, but now adorned by the apostles, the peoples have Christ for their rider. The lesser crowd does not put its garments upon the she-ass, but in the way, and not where he might stumble: because the holy martyrs, putting off the flesh, prepare a way for the simpler sort by the example of their own blood.

VERS. 8.-- *Branches from the trees,* etc. They cut branches or [0152D] leafy boughs, because they culled words and sentences, or examples, from the books of the Fathers.

VERS. 9.-- *Osanna. [n: (HIER.)]* Osi, that is, save, make safe. Anna is the interjection of one entreating, as though, save, I beseech. Osanna is wholly Hebrew, and it signifies that the coming of Christ is the salvation of the world: whence there follows: *Blessed. Osanna,* that is, save. *In the highest.* Because the coming of Christ is the salvation not of men only but of the whole world, joining earthly things to heavenly, *that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, of things on earth, and of things under the earth [n: (Phil. II)]*. *In the highest.* Because it is the salvation of the angels also, whose number he fills up.

VERS. 10.-- *When he had entered.* He entered the city of Jerusalem often, but not with these praises: [0153A] he is not called king, which he had always fled, save only now when he goes up to suffer. And this was done for this reason, that he might the more stir up their envy against himself, because the time of the passion was now at hand.

VERS. 12.-- *And he cast out. [n: (AUG.)]* He had cast them out at another time before, which John alone tells: but of this last casting out the others also speak. *The tables of the money-changers.* Because they came to the temple from all quarters, the priests sold all the animals to those who had them not, even the doves which the poor were to offer, so that afterwards, when these had been sold, they might get back the offerings. But because some had nothing wherewith to buy, they set up money-changers who might make loans: and because the law forbids usury, in its place they took various kinds of goods, that is, apples, grapes and the like; these he casts out. So too mystically he casts them daily out of [0153B] the Church. And he holds guilty of one and the same crime both those who sell and those who buy ecclesiastical offices. *And the chairs of them that sold.* Which belong to the masters, that is, he destroys the priesthood of those who take a price for the laying on of the hand, by which the Holy Spirit is given. Hence it is that the heresy of Simon is condemned. But according to the letter it is absurd to say that dealers in doves sat in chairs.

VERS. 13.-- *Of thieves.* He is a thief who follows after gain, who both persecutes bodily those who do not give, and slays spiritually those who do give, and while he plots the hurt of his neighbour, sits like a thief in a den.

VERS. 14.-- *And there came.* Mystically. He daily heals simple sinners coming to him by penance, enlightening them first when they are blind [0153C] in knowledge and lame in work, and directing them into the life of uprightness.

VERS. 15.-- *The wonderful things which,* etc. That by the scourge of one man, and he of low account, so many should be cast out and the tables overturned, than which he did nothing more wonderful. Something fiery and starlike shines out from his eyes, the majesty of the godhead gleams in his face.

VERS. 16.-- *He said to them.* He does not lie open to a false charge, he does not say, they do well or ill, or, spare their age, but he holds the mean, and, himself keeping silence, confirms the words of the children by the testimony of Scripture. *Of infants and sucklings.* Witnesses of the miracles. They are called Christ's infants, not by age, but by simplicity. Sucklings, as delighted, so to speak, by milk, that is, by the sweetness of the miracles, which are called milk, whereas bread is perfect doctrine.

VERS. 17.-- *He went forth abroad.* Because he was poor and flattered [0153D] no man, he found no host in so great a city, but was received in the house of Lazarus.

VERS. 19.-- *The fig tree.* As he said many things in parables, so he also did them. For why should he seek fruit on a fig tree whose season anyone would know was not yet come, and for that condemn it to everlasting barrenness, unless it were that he here terrified by a deed the people whom he was teaching by word; lest anyone having leaves, that is, words of justice without the fruit of works, should deserve to be cut down. *Leaves.* The traditions of the Pharisees without the fruit of truth, whence another evangelist: the time of the salvation of Israel was not yet, because the people of the Gentiles had not yet come in, or because the time of faith has passed by, when, being despised, he went over to the Gentiles. *Never fruit from thee.* The Creator does no wrong [0154A] to the possessor if he uses his own creature at his own will, and for the profit of others.

VERS. 20.-- *And seeing it.* Since the Lord was to suffer and to bear the scandal of the cross, he had to mark the minds of the disciples by the anticipation of a sign, whence they wondered too that the fig tree had withered: he could by the same power have dried up his enemies, had he not by his patience awaited their salvation.

VERS. 21.-- *Faith.* He has perfect faith who works by love: he can remove mountains. Others obtain pardon of sin if they themselves forgive others.

VERS. 22.-- *You shall receive.* Even if it be deferred to a fitting time, and even if not to those for whom it is asked, yet to those who ask worthily.

[0154B]VERS. 24.-- *I will ask,* etc. He could have refuted them openly: but he asks for this reason, that they may be condemned either by their own silence or by their own sentence, and may know of themselves by what power he does this, according to that saying: *God shall break their teeth in their mouth.*

VERS. 27.-- *Neither do I tell you.* For two causes the truth is hidden, either because the hearers are not capable, whence: *I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now;* or because they are despisers, whence *Give not that which is holy to dogs.*

VERS. 28.-- *A certain man had two sons, and coming,* etc. When he had made a wary answer to the priests, he at once brings in a parable, both to convict them of impiety and to teach that the kingdom of God is to be carried over to the Gentiles.

[0154C]VERS. 31.-- *They say to him.* In certain codices there stands, *instead of* the first, the last. For they understand the truth, but by shifting about they are unwilling to say what they think, as in the case of John's baptism.

VERS. 32.-- *John in the way of justice.* The Jews, despising John, neither grasped the way of justice, nor did penance for their evil works. *But you. [n: (GREG.)]* Because they were unwilling to answer wisdom openly, they are caught in parables.

VERS. 33.-- *A vineyard.* Of which Isaias [n: (cap. IV)]. *The vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth,* etc. *He dug in it a winepress.* By the digging fear is marked, because they kept those commandments of the law not out of love but out of fear.

VERS. 34.-- *When the time of the fruits drew nigh,* [0154D] etc. Rightly the time of the fruits, not of the fruit: because there was none in that stubborn people.

VERS. 37.-- *Last of all,* etc. See the patience of the father of the household, who waits for repentance. *They will reverence.* As though, I shall do what is mine, let them see to what is theirs.

VERS. 38.-- *This is the heir.* It is plain that the chief priests killed Jesus not through ignorance but through envy, since they knew him to be the one to whom it is said: *Ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thine inheritance [n: (Psal. II)]*; whence also, taking counsel for themselves, they said: *If we let him alone so [n: (Joan. II)]*, etc. *We shall have his inheritance.* The inheritance of Christ, which he possessed in rising again, is the Church, which the Jews were striving to snatch away: [0155A] while, his faith being put out, they endeavoured to persuade the Gentiles of the justice that is of the law.

VERS. 39.-- *They cast him forth out of the vineyard.* Mark changes this order, saying: *They killed him and cast him forth out of the vineyard [n: (Luc. XX)]*, as a worthless corpse. In which he marks their obstinacy, because when the Lord had been killed and raised again, they shut him out from their own borders and sent him to the Gentiles.

VERS. 42.-- *The builders.* Who seemed to be building a house for God, as it were, out of the people subject to them.

*And he that shall fall,* etc. He who believes and yet sins: the stone falls upon him and he is not ground to powder, but he is bound tight, because through patience he is kept for salvation; but he upon whom the stone rushes down, that is, he who denies, is utterly ground to powder, so that not even a shard remains; or it falls upon [0155B] him who now despises and does injuries, and he has not yet utterly perished, but is shaken so that he does not walk upright. The stone falls upon him when, coming in judgment, it crushes him, when he says: *Go into everlasting fire.* [var: Vulg. Mt 25:41 *Discedite a me maledicti in ignem aeternum*, "Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire"]
