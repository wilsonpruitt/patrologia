## CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 2.-- *It was made like.* Again he convicts them by parables: he is not silent because they are exasperated, but by another parable he tells them that a worse anger of God is to come. *A marriage feast.* The bridal chamber of the marriage is the virgin's womb. Of those invited to the marriage Matthew alone tells. Luke tells another likeness in part, of a supper, that is, of the eternal banquet, from which no one who has entered is driven out, as he does of the present Church.

[0155C]VERS. 4.-- *The oxen.* Because first the prophets, and afterwards the apostles, suffered from unbelievers: who are now to you for an example of what you must believe. Come, by faith, by agreeing with the rest. The oxen are the fathers of the Old Testament, to whom leave was granted to strike their enemies as with a horn. The fatlings, from feeding, that is, the heralds of the New Testament, fat with inward charity, seeking things on high on the wing of contemplation. Slain, either from what they had been, or already set in rest through the death of the flesh.

VERS. 5.-- *Another to his farm.* To go to a farm is to lie immoderately upon earthly labour. But to trade is to gape after earthly gains.

VERS. 7.-- *And the king.* Above, he was a man while he was inviting and doing works of clemency, now in taking vengeance he is king only [0155D]. *And having sent,* etc. Having sent his armies, that is, the angels by whom he will exercise judgment, he destroys the persecutors: and the city, that is, the flesh in which they dwelt, he will burn with the soul in hell.

VERS. 8.-- *The marriage indeed is ready.* The whole sacrament of the incarnation is accomplished, the nativity and the passion.

*To the outgoings,* etc. Actions are the ways: the outgoings are the failing of actions: because for the most part those are easily converted whom no prosperity attends in earthly doings.

VERS. 11.-- *That he might see the guests.* [var: Vulg. Mt 22:11 *ut viderent discumbentes*, plural, "that they might see"] That is, that he may make the merits of those to be judged be seen, and may discern them. *Not clothed with a wedding garment.* Now, the Pharisees having been refuted, he adds [0156A] that not all who are in the Church are worthy of the kingdom, lest his own grow slothful.

VERS. 12.-- *Friend, how camest thou in hither not having a wedding garment?* A friend, because invited to the marriage. *How,* he convicts him of shamelessness.

VERS. 13.-- *His hands and feet being bound.* They are bound by punishment who here would not be bound from evil, that is, restrained. The hands which were not stretched out to almsgiving. The feet slow to good. There each several member shall be subjected to punishments, which here served each several vice. *Outer,* etc. Because here they have the inward darkness of the heart.

VERS. 14.-- *But many are called, but few chosen.* [var: Vulg. Mt 22:14 *Multi enim sunt vocati*, "for many are called"] One being cast out, in whom the whole body of the wicked is set forth, a general sentence is brought in. *But many [0156B] are called.* As though he should say: Even if I have spoken of one, it is to be understood of many.

VERS. 16.-- *With the Herodians, saying.* As though to men unknown, that they might the more easily deceive, because they feared the crowds. What they dare not by themselves, they attempt by the governor's hands. *With the Herodians.* That is, with the soldiers of Herod. Under Caesar Augustus, when the enrolment was made, Judea was made tributary. Over these tributes was set Herod the son of Antipater, an alien and a proselyte, appointed king for the Jews by Augustus. There was therefore sedition among the people, some saying that tributes ought to be given, for security and quiet, since the Romans bear arms for all. The Pharisees striving against it, that the people of God, which pays tithes and the rest that the law prescribes, ought not to lie under human laws. The Pharisees therefore send [0156C] to see whether they might deceive him.

*Thou hast no care,* etc. To this they provoke him, that he should fear God more than Caesar: and therefore should say that tributes ought not to be paid, so that the Herodians might hold him as the author of sedition.

VERS. 18.-- *Hypocrites.* Because they are one thing and feign another.

VERS. 21.-- *Render therefore.* Or, as Caesar exacts the stamping of his own image, so also does God the soul signed with the light of his countenance.

VERS. 23.-- *Sadducees,* etc. There were two heresies among the Jews. That of the Pharisees, who set forward the justice of traditions and observances: and that of the Sadducees, who also claimed for themselves what they were not yet, that is, [0156D] just men. For Sadducees is interpreted *just men*, they do not believe the resurrection of bodies, and think that souls perish with the body, they invent the foulness of a fable to convict of raving those who assert the resurrection. But it may be that this once happened among their nation.

VERS. 29.-- *You err.* For this reason, because you know not the Scriptures, Whence there follows, that they know not the power of God.

VERS. 30.-- *In the resurrection.* Bodies shall indeed rise which could marry and be given in marriage: which is said of those who have the members of generation: but for another reason they shall not marry.

VERS. 31.-- *Have you not read.* In Moses, whom you receive, otherwise he could have given plainer testimonies of the resurrection [0157A] from the prophets: but the Sadducees do not receive the prophets.

VERS. 32.-- *The God of Abraham,* etc. Who were already dead when he said these things, therefore by this he proves that souls remain after death: for he would not be called the God of those that do not exist. And thus the resurrection of bodies also is brought in, which together with the souls did good or evil: *The God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob.* By this he proves the eternity of souls, and at once brings in: *He is not the God of the dead,* etc., so that when he had proved that souls remain after death, the resurrection of bodies also, which together with the souls did good or evil, might consequently be brought in.

VERS. 34.-- *Silence.* Note that the poisons of envy can be overcome, but with difficulty are laid to rest. *They came together.* [0157B] That by their multitude they might overcome him whom they could not by reason. In tempting him those come together who in doctrine are contrary to one another. They had already been refuted in the showing of the penny, they had already seen the Sadducees too struck dumb, but yet ill-will and spite nourish shamelessness.

VERS. 35.-- *Tempting him.* Mark says that the Lord said to him, when he had answered wisely: *Thou art not far from the kingdom of God.* For it could come to pass that he who came tempting was converted by the Lord's answer. Or the temptation is not an evil one, as of a man wishing to deceive, but by reason of a man wishing to try out further what he does not know.

VERS. 37.-- *Thou shalt love the Lord thy God.* With his first answer the Lord strikes the feigned conscience of the questioner. *Thy God is one. Thou shalt not take the name of thy God in vain. Observe the day of the sabbath.* These three belong to the love [0157C] of God: That thou shouldst think upon his unity, his truth, his will. *With thy whole heart.* The integrity of faith is expressed. In the whole soul, perfect obedience is required. In the whole mind, perseverance is shown.

VERS. 42.-- *Of Christ.* Whether he has come, or whether you believe he is to come, whom you assert to be mere man.

VERS. 44.-- *He said.* To say that is to beget a Son equal to himself. *To my Lord.* Not according to that whereby he was born of him, but according to that whereby he always was of the Father.

VERS. 45.-- *How.* These things are contradictory according to your understanding.

VERS. 46.-- *And no man could,* etc. Note that the poisons of envy can be overcome, but with difficulty are laid to rest.
