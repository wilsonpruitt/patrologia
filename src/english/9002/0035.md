## CHAPTER XXIII.

[0157D]VERS. 1.-- *Then he spoke.* Those being refuted who are crafty, he instructs those who are fit, none the less exhorting them to be subject to those men for the sake of the priesthood and the dignity of the name: considering not their works, but their teaching.

VERS. 4.-- *Heavy burdens.* The commandments of the law, with their own traditions mixed in, persuading men to live according to these: and not to come to the easy and delightful grace of Christ; whence above: *Come to me, you that labour and are burdened.*

VERS. 5.-- *But all things.* He said that such teachers are to be heard, not imitated: now he shows the cause for which they cannot believe Christ, that is, because they do all things to be seen of men. For he who [0158A] desires earthly glory cannot believe Christ preaching heavenly things.

VERS. 8.-- *But you.* For a twofold reason he bids us take heed of them, lest we be led astray by their dissembling, or lest we be inflamed with emulation.

VERS. 9.-- *One is.* Who enlightens man, which no other man does, but only trains him, by teaching he does not bestow understanding. *Your Father.* God is called Father and Master by nature, man by indulgence. Father, that honour may be paid to age, not that he be held the author of life. Master, from fellowship with the true Master, as his messenger: and he is honoured out of reverence for him by whom he is sent.

VERS. 13.-- *You shut.* Either by evil example, or by taking away the mysteries of the Scriptures. For by the example and [0158B] teaching of those who are masters, others indeed do not receive Christ.

VERS. 15.-- *You go about.* In the whole world you disparage the Gospel: and if anyone cleaves to you, he is the son of a twofold punishment: because he neither receives the remission of sins, and he falls into the fellowship of those who have persecuted Christ. Everyone is called the son of him whose works he does. *a proselyte.* Proselytes are those received from among the Gentiles into the Synagogue, whose rarity is signified by the one, because scarcely any after Christ.

VERS. 16.-- *Whosoever.* The Pharisees, who did all things for gain, used to say that the offerings were even holier than the temple or the altar: so that men might be readier to offer gifts than to pour out prayers in the temple or to do works of justice. In which [0158C] they were looking not to the fear of God, but to their own greed. Therefore the Lord convicts them of folly and of fraud.

VERS. 22.-- *By heaven.* By the creature: where you do not think there is any perjury, he swears by heaven, that is, he swears by the creature that is subject, and by the divinity that presides over the creature.

VERS. 23.-- *Woe to you.* Here too he convicts them of avarice: because they pass over nothing of the things that are for their own advantage. *You that tithe.* Because God had commanded the tithes of all things to be offered for the sustenance of the priests. Note that by almsgiving no impunity is bought for those who remain in iniquity.

[0158D] *These things ye ought.* Lest he should seem to spurn alms, the fruit of the earth, he added these words.

VERS. 24.-- *Blind guides.* Under the opinion of religion you show diligence in the least things which have gain in them: but the great commandments concerning judgment, faith and mercy you devour and neglect. *Straining out.* Mystically. By the name of the gnat the seditious murderer Barabbas is signified: because this creature disquiets by its buzzing and delights in blood. By the name of the camel, for its lowliness in taking up burdens, the Lord is understood. The Jews therefore strain the gnat, that is [sic: *in est*], they observe the least things, because they let Barabbas go, who had not broken the sabbath, and they swallow the camel, that is, they despise the greater things: because they killed Christ, who was setting forth the sabbath spiritually through judgment and [0159A] mercy and faith (which things they despise).

VERS. 25.-- *Woe to you.* By divers likenesses he convicts the pharisees of dissembling and of lying, who show one thing to men and do another at home. *You that make clean.* In vain do you hold out holiness outwardly, as if a man should cleanse a cup on the outside, but leave the filth within: whereas the use of it is within. Luke thus. *Fool, he that made that which is without made also that which is within [n: (Luc. XI)]* [var: Vulg. Lc 11:40 *Stulti nonne qui fecit quod deforis est, etiam id quod deintus est fecit?*, a question addressed to many: "Ye fools, did not he that made that which is without make also that which is within?"], that is, he who made both natures of man willed both to be cleansed. Let no one therefore think that fornication and bodily vices of this kind are alone grievous, and that avarice, and pride, and wrath, and the other spiritual vices are light.

VERS. 26.-- *Of the cup.* By way of likeness, not that [0159B] their superstition is in the cup and the dish.

VERS. 29.-- *You that build.* You raise up a memorial of the slain for the favour of the common people, that you may seem just, you even accuse the crimes of your fathers.

VERS. 31.-- *Wherefore.* Because you detest the crimes of your fathers under an appearance indeed of holiness; while by persecuting Christ and the apostles you do the same things, you prove by the deed that you are their sons not only in flesh, but in wicked imitation: and it is plain that they sin knowingly. That is, you speak against yourselves while you condemn your fathers: for you do the like.

VERS. 32.-- *And you.* When it has been proved that they are the sons of murderers, he sets down that to which he was tending: *And fill you up,* fill up what was lacking to them, they slew the prophets, do you slay the Lord of the prophets. He does not command, but shows what [0159C] they are about to do.

VERS. 33.-- *How will you flee. [n: (HIER.)]* By building the sepulchres of the saints, or rather by cleansing your hearts from malice. Will the saints, whose sepulchres you adorn, set you free? Certainly not. The saints are not friends to the enemies of God: in vain do they honour the saints who despise holiness.

VERS. 34.-- *Wise men.* Who know what discourse they utter. *Scribes.* Men skilled in the law.

VERS. 35.-- *That upon you may come.* He speaks not of these present only, but of the whole generation of the wicked, going before and to come: because all are one city, one body of the devil. *Of just Abel.* The justice of Abel was approved by the acceptance of his gifts. *Even to the blood.* Why only thus far, since many were slain after him [0159D] before the birth of Christ, and, when Christ was born, the children were straightway destroyed by this generation? But because Abel was a shepherd of sheep and was slain in the field: Zacharias a priest was slain in the court of the temple: he willed to signify under their names the martyrs of both ranks, both laymen and those bound to the office of the altar. Zacharias, who was slain by king Joas between the temple and the altar, as the history of the Kings relates, was the son of Joiada, and in the Gospel which the Nazaraeans use, instead of the son of Barachias there is read the son of Joiada. But that the justice of Joiada the priest may be proclaimed, he himself is called Barachias, that is, blessed of the Lord. This Zacharias was one of the twelve prophets, as the father of John was one of the prophets.

[0160A]VERS. 36.-- *Upon the generation.* Which is reckoned from the first parricide to the last son of perdition: as though, they shall be alike in punishment as they are also in guilt.

VERS. 37.-- *Jerusalem.* As though he should say: You are guilty of blood, and deservedly, because many times I sent to gather you, and you would not. *How often.* As if he should say: I would, and thou wouldst not, and as many as I gathered, I did by my own will, which is always effectual, while thou wouldst not, because thou wast always ungrateful. *A hen.* This creature has great affection toward her young, so that, touched by their weakness, she grows weak, and, covering them with her wings, fights against the kite. So the wisdom of God, made weak through the flesh, protects us and defends us from the devil.

VERS. 38.-- *It shall be left.* The city, stripped of the help of God, [0160B] shall be left to its own dominion, and it is well left desolate, when it is forsaken by God.

VERS. 39.-- *Blessed is he that cometh.* This the crowds said to the Lord as he came to Jerusalem. But that the Lord says this here, when these things are past, compels it to be understood mystically of the coming of his charity, where they shall confess the Son whom they crucified.
