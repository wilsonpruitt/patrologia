## CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 2.-- *There shall not be left here a stone upon a stone.* Mystically. When the Lord withdrew from the temple, all the buildings of the law and that framing of the commandments was destroyed, so that nothing can be fulfilled by the Jews. And it was divinely provided that, as the grace of the Gospel increased, that temple with its ceremonies should be taken away, lest [0160C] anyone weak in faith should, while those things stood, have occasion of Judaism. The same who threatened by word showed it by a sign, when he goes out from them after the threat has been made.

VERS. 3.-- *And as he sat.* Where Mark says, *over against the temple.* He sits over against the temple when he discourses of its ruin and of the destruction of the nation, so that the posture of his body also may agree with his words. Mystically. He sits on mount Olivet, that is, remaining at rest in the Church he detests the arrogance of the proud. *Tell us.* Because he had said that all things were to be destroyed, they ask the time and the signs of the destruction. They ask three things: at what time is Jerusalem to be destroyed? when is Christ to come? when is the consummation of the world to come?

VERS. 4.-- *Take heed.* When these things shall come to pass, let us not think [0160D] that the day of the Lord is at hand, the sign of which is plainly set down in what follows. He forearms them lest they be troubled or fail through deception, as through Simon Magus, or through tribulation and persecution, as through Nero. Note that these things which come before the destruction of Jerusalem are like, and almost the same as, those which shall come to pass at the consummation of the world, and he speaks of the one as though of the other.

VERS. 5.-- *many.* When the destruction of Jerusalem was at hand there were many leaders who said that they were Christs, and that the time of liberty was near. Many in the Church in the times of the apostles, who besides other falsehoods said that the day of the Lord was at hand.

VERS. 6.-- *You shall hear.* These things abounded in Judea from the passion of the Lord [0161A] onward: and lest the apostles, terrified by them, should forsake Jerusalem and Judea, they are admonished that the end is not at once, but after forty-two years.

VERS. 8.-- *And all these things.* But all these things are beginnings, not the consummation, being as it were the heralds of Antichrist, or of the destruction of the city. These belong partly to the overthrow of the Jews, partly to the day of judgment, because the apostles asked in that way about both.

VERS. 9.-- *Then shall they deliver you up.* He adds by what desert these things befall the Jews, who after the killing of Christ persecuted the heralds of his name and of his faith.

VERS. 11.-- *And many false prophets.* Some refer this to the overthrow of the Jews. But then among them, because there was no believer to whom the exhortation might be made; this is better taken of the heretics, of whom the [0161B] first was Simon Magus. The last, greater than all, is Antichrist. It is to be diligently considered which of the signs is to be referred to the destruction of the city, and which to the day of the Lord. For the Lord answered concerning the things that were to follow from that time, whether of the destruction of Jerusalem or of his own coming.

VERS. 14.-- *And it shall be preached.* Because the Lord knew that the hearts of the disciples were to be saddened over the destruction and ruin of their own nation, he lifts them up with this consolation, that they might know that far more companions of eternal joy were to be gathered out of the whole world. Hold moreover this sign, that neither the day of the Lord nor the desolation of Jerusalem is at hand before the apostles have been scattered through the whole world, except the two Jameses, who were killed first in Judea. Scattered, I say, so that if they should grieve over the ruin of their own nation, they might have as a solace [0161C] more companions gathered out of every nation.

VERS. 15.-- *The abomination,* etc. Or if it be said of the destruction of the city, the abomination, that is, an idol. For Pilate set the image of Caesar in the desolated temple. Or this is said of the equestrian statue of Hadrian, which stood a long time in the very place of the holy things.

VERS. 16.-- *Then they that are in Judea.* These things were done according to the letter, when, as the extermination of the Jews drew near, all the Christians, warned by an oracle, fled into the kingdom of Agrippa, who was obedient to the Romans.

VERS. 22.-- *And unless.* What hope then is there for the elect, save the grace of God, which gives patience to the godly, that it may the sooner take away power from the ungodly? For by how much this tribulation shall be the heavier, by so much shall it be the more moderate in its shortness. Or by the name [0161D] of days the evils themselves are signified. *Had been shortened.* Not in measure, but in number, lest by the delay of the times the faith of believers be shaken. For these evils shall last three years and a half only. *Behold, here is Christ.* Each several heresy has also its own fixed parts of the world, whence it is said: *Lo here, and lo there.* Or in hidden or in obscure conventicles it deceives the curiosity of men, whence it is said: *Behold, in the chambers, behold, in the desert.* There shall be many who will not believe that Christ is the common salvation of all, but of some only, whence it is said: *here or there.*

VERS. 24.-- *If it could be done.* [var: Vulg. Mt 24:24 *(si fieri potest)*, present indicative, "if it can be done"] That is, if in any way it could come to pass that God's predetermination were changed, and therefore it cannot be understood of the true elect. *The elect.* Not that the divine [0162A] election is made void, but those who by human judgment seem elect.

VERS. 26.-- *Behold, in the desert.* Many also believe that he does not come into the open, but into the desert. Many believe that Christ gave the more secret precepts of the faith to some, not to all, whence it is said, *in the inner chambers. Do not believe,* that Christ is in the desert or in the inner chambers, but that his faith shines clear and manifest from the East to the West among catholics. Let no credit therefore be given to schismatics. By the name of East and West he designates the whole world.

VERS. 31.-- *From the utmost parts of the heavens.* Lest anyone should think that the elect were to be gathered from the four quarters of the earth, and not from all its bounds, and from the inland regions besides, it is added: *From the utmost part of the earth to [0162B] the utmost part of heaven,* that is, from the farthest bounds of the earth straight on to its uttermost limits: where to those who look from far off the circle of heaven seems to rest upon the earth.

VERS. 32.-- *From the tree.* Mystically. From the people of Israel, in whom blindness has happened in part: and it was the fig tree that was condemned. But when out of the dry wood the branch of faith and charity shall have grown green in him, and the leaves, that is, the words of preaching, shall have sprung forth, the summer which is looked for is near, that is, eternal calm and the longed-for newness of those born again. For when at last, unbelief being taken away, all Israel shall be saved, it must not be doubted that the day of judgment is near.

VERS. 35.-- *Heaven and earth.* They shall be made new, the former shape laid aside, but the substance remaining, [0162C] whence it is said: *The earth shall stand for ever.* [var: Vulg. Ecl 1:4 *terra autem in aeternum stat*, present, "the earth standeth for ever"]

VERS. 36.-- *Of the day.* Lest perhaps, when the signs had been heard, they should seek also the fixed limit of that day, he says: Of the last day, after how many years or days it is to come, be not solicitous, it has been defined to no one whether the judgment shall be by day or by night, but the day is spoken of for manifestation, or the night for concealment. *No one knows,* etc. In certain codices there is added, *neither the Son.* Who indeed knows all things with the Father, but does not make men know, as the Apostle says: *That in him are hidden the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge [n: (II Col. II)]*. He does not make it known, because it would be known to no profit. But let us always be uncertain of the coming of the judge, that we may so live every day as though we were to be judged on the next. Nor have the elect angels made anyone know it. Or [0162D] God has granted the knowledge of this day to none of the holy angels, nor to an adopted son, that is, nor to any of holy men.

VERS. 37.-- *But as.* He affirms the suddenness of the day of his coming by very many examples: for he both compared it to the lightning that flies swiftly over all things, and likens it to the days of Noe, or of Lot according to another evangelist.

VERS. 38.-- *Eating and drinking, marrying,* etc. Not foods and marriages, but the immoderate use of lawful things is reproved. After wars, famines, earthquakes, etc., then a short peace shall follow, that the believer may be tried, whether, when the evils are past, he hopes that the judge will come, whence it is said: *For when they shall say peace and security, then shall sudden destruction come upon them [n: (I Thess. V)]*.

[0163A]VERS. 40.-- *Then,* etc. Luke, *in that night,* that is, in that tribulation in which they are tried, in which some stand fast, others fall. *Two shall be.* There are two sorts of the aforesaid in the Church, of those labouring as it were in a field, of those who sincerely announce Christ, and of those who adulterate the word of God.

VERS. 41.-- *Two grinding.* Two sorts of those who follow the round of changeable things, who are designated by the feminine gender, because women are governed by men, by the counsel of their betters. *Two in a bed.* Those who choose leisure and rest, which are signified by the name of the bed, occupied neither with secular nor with ecclesiastical business. *One shall be taken.* He who for God's sake strives to be continent, that living without solicitude he may think on the things that are God's. But he who has chosen the monastic [0163B] life for human praise or through some corruption of vices, shall be left by God. There are no other kinds of men in the Church than these three. Whence Ezechiel saw three men delivered, Noe, Daniel and Job, that is, rulers like Noe, the continent like Daniel, the married like Job. And according to these three kinds of men he sets down three parables. In the first he treats of those labouring in the field, which is where he says: *Watch ye therefore.* In the second he treats of those who are in the bed, which is where he says: *The kingdom of heaven is like to ten virgins.* In the third he treats of those grinding at the mill, which is where he says: *For even as a man going into a far country,* etc.

VERS. 42.-- *Watch ye therefore. [n: (HIER.)]* He did not say, because we know not, but, because you know not, that he might show that he himself is not ignorant of the day of judgment.

[0163C]VERS. 43.-- *If he knew,* etc. The thief breaks through the house while the father of the household knows it not, because while the spirit sleeps from its own guarding, unforeseen death bursts into the dwelling of the flesh, and drags it away to punishment. But he would withstand the thief if he watched, because, guarding beforehand against the coming of the judge who comes secretly, he would meet him by doing penance.

VERS. 45.-- *Who, thinkest thou, is the faithful servant.* By three likenesses, of the lightning, of the thief, and of the drowning of the Pentapolis, he brought in the terror of judgment to all in common, that they might watch and be ready. Now he speaks especially to prelates, who ought to be humble, prudent, not seeking the good for the sake of temporal gain, but to feed the sheep by word and by example and by work. *Who, thinkest thou,* as though he should say: You are to be admonished. For how rare is he that serves the Lord for the Lord's sake, feeding the sheep [0163D] of Christ not for gain, but out of love of Christ. *Prudent.* Providing for himself in what is to come. *Whom he hath appointed.* How rare is he who is called by God as Aaron was and has not rather thrust himself forward, and who does not feed himself rather than the sheep.

VERS. 46.-- *Blessed is that servant.* But he is to be glorified with eternal blessedness who, when the Lord comes to judgment, shall be found faithfully dispensing the allowance of the word and watching in the care of the flock. Rarely is one found faithful or prudent, but he who is found such is blessed.

VERS. 47.-- *Over.* That is, over all the joys of the heavenly kingdom he will make the rest, that is, the good hearers, sit down, and passing by he will minister to them.

VERS. 49.-- *To strike his fellow servants.* To do them injuries, [0164A] or to corrupt the consciences of the weak by a crooked word or example. Whence Luke says *menservants or maidservants [n: (Luc. XII)]*, whose fall is by so much the easier as their age or sex is the weaker.

VERS. 51.-- *With the hypocrites,* etc. With those, namely, who were in the field and who were grinding, and were nevertheless left behind. Who are called hypocrites both in the field and at the mill, that is, who seemed to do good things, but the outcome of a different will appeared. *Weeping* By a likeness of the members the greatness of the torments is shown.
