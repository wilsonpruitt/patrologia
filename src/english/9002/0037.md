## CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 1.-- *Ten virgins.* The virgins are all who refrain themselves from the luxury of the world. *And the bride.* All together are the bride, who has now been espoused a virgin away from the corruption of the world. She shall be wedded, when the mortal shall be joined to the [0164B] immortal.

VERS. 2.-- *Five.* Because continence in carnal enticements is fivefold, namely that in the five senses they should refrain themselves from pleasures.

VERS. 5.-- *And the bridegroom tarrying.* Because there is no little time between the first and the second coming of Christ. *They all slumbered and slept.* Because they are afterwards to be raised up.

VERS. 6.-- *And at midnight there was a cry made.* That is, with no one knowing or hoping for it. Whence *The day of the Lord shall come as a thief in the night [n: (I Thess. III)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *Give us of your oil,* etc. Those whose deeds are propped up by what is another's fail when that same praise is withdrawn, and out of habit they always seek that whence the mind is wont to rejoice. And so they wish to have before God, who is the searcher of the heart, the testimony of men, who do not [0164C] see hearts.

*They are going out.* That is, at the coming of the judge they are darkened within, and they have no reward from God, because they have received their praise.

VERS. 9.-- *Lest perhaps.* Because each shall render an account for himself, nor is anyone helped before God by another's testimony, since to him secret things lie open, and each scarcely suffices for himself, that his own conscience may bear him witness. *Lest perhaps* each one shall receive a reward for his own works, nor in the day of judgment can the virtues of some relieve the vices of others. *To them that sell.* They that sell are flatterers, because they give praise and receive some reward.

VERS. 10.-- *But whilst.* While they bend themselves toward [0164D] the things that are without, and seek their accustomed joys, since they have not known the things within, he comes who judges. *It was shut.* After the judgment great is the severity of him whose mercy before it is unspeakable.

VERS. 11.-- *But last of all.* By true penance, by fruitless tears, those left outside knock. It is not said that they bought oil: because, no joy from the praises of others now remaining, in their straits they return to the imploring of God.

VERS. 13.-- *Watch ye.* Namely, that we may prepare for ourselves the testimony of a good conscience, lest with the foolish virgins we remain outside. *You know not the day nor the hour.* Not only the day of judgment, but the day of each man's death, or the hour in which he who is found ready [0165A] shall then also be found ready. And because he desires to find us ready, he adds another parable on the same matter.

VERS. 14.-- *Going into a far country he called his servants,* etc. The place of the flesh is the earth, therefore he led the flesh of Christ, our own, unto foreign parts, when he ascended into heaven.

VERS. 15.-- *And immediately he took his journey.* Not changing place, but allowing them a free power of working.

VERS. 16.-- *He traded with them.* By keeping himself from visible things, by exhorting others as he was able, that they might pass over from visible things to invisible. Or by preaching to both sexes, and by forming them in good morals, he doubled the talents he had received.

[0165B]VERS. 19.-- *But he reckoned.* He began to examine the deeds of each, their conscience accusing or excusing every one.

VERS. 20.-- *Behold other five.* As many as he converted from error, so many he offered to the Lord, his conscience bearing him witness that by the grace given him he not only profited himself, but also gained those whom he could.

VERS. 21.-- *Over a few things.* The good things of the present life are few, although they seem many, because they are not without an admixture of some trouble. But the eternal good things are many, which are without any trouble of corruption. *Enter into the joy of thy Lord.* Mingled with the choirs of angels: so rejoice within over the gift, that there may now be nothing without over which thou shouldst grieve for corruption.

[0165C]VERS. 22.-- *He that had received two talents.* This man by his own powers doubled in the Gospel whatever he had learned in the law of God. Or he understood the conscience and the works of the present life as figures of the blessedness to come.

VERS. 24.-- *Thou art a hard man.* Because the way that leads to life is steep, and because the higher the step, the heavier the fall.

VERS. 25.-- *And being afraid I went away,* etc. And fearing to attempt the step of a higher life, lest, seeking another's salvation, I myself should be endangered. *I hid thy talent.* I did not preach what I understood by thy gift.

VERS. 26.-- *Thou knewest that,* etc. Why did that thought not strike fear into thee, so that thou shouldst know that I would seek my own more diligently? What he said by way of excuse is turned into blame.

[0165D]VERS. 27.-- *To the bankers.* Or to the other teachers, as to the apostles and bishops. Or, to all believers who are able to double the money.

VERS. 28.-- *And give it to him,* etc. We see this for the most part done in the Church, because very many, while they minister well the outward things which they receive, are led on by an added grace to the mystical understanding also.

VERS. 29.-- *For to everyone,* etc. For many, though wise and sharp by nature, if they are slothful, lose the good of nature, and the reward that had been promised them seems to pass over to others, who acquire by industry what they have less of by nature. Or he who has faith and a good will, even if he has less in work, it shall be given to him by the good judge. But he who has it not in faith [0166A] loses even the virtues which he has naturally, without which [faith] there are no virtues. Or he who has love of the word, to him is given the sense of understanding. But he who has it not, even if he is skilled by natural study, fails. Or he who has not charity loses every good that he has. Note that whatever is given to each in worldly or in spiritual things is reckoned for a talent of which he is to render an account.

VERS. 31.-- *And when.* Being about to be delivered to the cross very soon, he set before them the glory of him who triumphs, that he might make up by this promise for the scandals that were to follow. After the parables about the end of the world, the Lord now sets forth the manner of the judgment to come.

VERS. 32.-- *The sheep from,* etc. There shall be two orders in the judgment, namely of the elect and of the reprobate: but of the elect [0166B] some shall judge, to whom it is said: *You also shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the tribes of Israel.* Others shall be judged, to whom it is said: *I was hungry and you gave me to eat.* Likewise of the reprobate, some who are outside the Church shall not be judged: others who shall be judged, to whom it is said: *I was hungry, and you gave me not to eat,* etc.

VERS. 33.-- *The goats.* The he-goat is a butting and rank-smelling animal, which was offered for sins in the law, and it signifies sinners: nor does he say she-goats, which can have young.

VERS. 34.-- *Blessed of the Father.* Advanced by the benefit of my Father to the obtaining of glory, in which you may reign without corruption, to which you have been predestined from the foundation of the world.

[0166C]VERS. 37.-- *Then shall they answer.* Not distrusting the words of the Lord, but either amazed at the greatness of the king's majesty; or because every good thing that they did seems little to them, whence it is said: *The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come [n: (Rom. VIII)]*, etc.

VERS. 40.-- *Of these brethren.* My brethren and my mother are they who do the will of my Father.

VERS. 42.-- *You gave me not,* etc. What does he deserve who has seized what belongs to another, if he is eternally damned who has not given anything of his own?

VERS. 44.-- *Lord, when,* etc. They busy themselves to excuse themselves, as though they who were deceiving men could deceive God. But they have no excuse who would not be converted from evils and do alms; whence he adds: [0166D] *As long as you did it not to one,* etc.

VERS. 46.-- *Into everlasting punishment.* In vain therefore does Origen promise deliverance after many years to evil men and to the demons themselves. *Into everlasting punishment. [n: (AUG.)]* In which matter Origen was assuredly the more merciful, who believed that the devil himself too and his angels, after punishments heavier according to their deserts and of longer duration, were to be rescued out of those torments and joined to the holy angels. But the Church has not undeservedly condemned him, both for this and for sundry other things, and chiefly for the blessednesses and miseries alternating without ceasing, and for the endless goings and returnings from those to these at fixed intervals of ages.
