## CHAPTER XXVI.

[0167A]

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass when he had finished.* Concerning the consummation of the world and the separation of the judgment: or, from the beginning of the Gospel until the passion he had accomplished all things by doing and by preaching. After the discourse in which he foretold that he would come in brightness, he showed that he would suffer, that he might give warning that the sacrament of the cross is mingled with the glory of eternity.

VERS. 6.-- *And when.* The counsel having been held, the order of the narrative passes over to that place, *then went one of the twelve,* so that the deed of Judas may be joined to the counsel of the priests. But he returns to that day which was before the six days of the Pasch, saying: *And when,* etc. *Of Simon.* The former name remains, that the power of him who heals may appear. By that house understand that part of the people which believed [0167B] the Lord and was healed. *Simon,* that is, obedient.

VERS. 7.-- *There came near. [n: (HIER.)]* Observe diligently what is said of the two women concerning the head. For indeed the sinful woman, upon the feet, and she who is said not to have been a sinner, upon the head. *A woman having.* Mary Magdalen, the sister of Lazarus, now just and of his household, who once came a sinner and washed his feet with tears, now anointed his head and his feet, the feet now, as John says. *Of precious ointment.* Mark: of spikenard, that is, made of the spikes and the leaves, which is more precious. John, of pistic nard, that is, faithful, not adulterated with other herbs: πίστις in Greek, faith in Latin. *Mystically.* This devotion of Mary signifies the faith of the Church, which, while it preaches the Godhead of Christ, anoints the head: while it preaches his humanity, the feet. Of this ointment in the house of Simon the leper in [0167C] Bethany both Matthew and Mark and John speak. But Luke tells of another thing like to this, done before in the house of another Simon, a Pharisee, not a leper, nor in Bethany, but concerning the same woman who then came a sinner.

VERS. 8.-- *The disciples had indignation.* John says that Judas spoke these things, and this for the sake of covetousness: the others could agree with his words, or even say the same, but out of care for the poor.

VERS. 11.-- *But me,* etc. In bodily presence, otherwise he says elsewhere: *Behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world.*

VERS. 12.-- *For she in pouring.* It is an office of burial, which you think to be waste. Nor is it a wonder if she gives me the odour of faith, for which I am soon to pour out my blood.

[0167D]VERS. 15.-- *What will you give.* Betraying him as though a worthless chattel, he puts it in the power of the buyers how much they will give.

VERS. 17.-- *Where wilt thou,* etc. There was no fixed house for them: wherein the building of superfluous things is condemned.

VERS. 18.-- *To a certain man.* He signifies the same man whom Mark and Luke call the master of the house. He therefore who says to a certain man, as though in his own person (and not the Lord's), for the sake of brevity indicated him in a summary way. *To a certain man.* Not to anyone whatever, but to some certain man, whom, speaking in his own person, he would rightly have set down. But now, when he has set down the words of the Lord, *Go ye into the city:* he himself puts in, *to a certain man:* not [0168A] because the Lord said this, but that he himself might indicate to us, the proper name being left unspoken, that there was there a certain man to whom they were sent.

VERS. 19.-- *And the disciples did even as.* In another Gospel it is written: *They found a large upper room furnished* [var: Vulg. Mc 14:15 *Et ipse vobis demonstrabit coenaculum grande, stratum*, "and he will show you a large upper room furnished"]. The upper room is the spiritual law, which, going forth from the straits of the letter, receives the Saviour in a lofty place. For he who follows the letter keeps the Pasch in the lowest place. But he who follows the water-bearer, that is, the herald of grace, into the house of the Church, he, transcending the letter by the spirit, prepares a refreshment for Christ in the height of the mind.

VERS. 21.-- *That one of you.* He who foretold concerning the passion foretells concerning the betrayer, that seeing himself not to be hidden he might repent of the deed. He casts the charge upon the number, that the guilty one might do [0168B] penance, yet he does not point him out particularly, lest he should become more shameless.

VERS. 22.-- *They began every one.* Because they believe the master more than themselves: and fearing also their own frailty, they ask concerning a sin of which they have no knowledge in themselves.

VERS. 23.-- *He that dippeth.* While the others were made sorrowful and were drawing back their hand from the food, with that shamelessness with which he betrayed him he puts his hand in with the master, that by boldness he might feign a good conscience. Behold the humility and the patience with which he admits the betrayer to the table. *In the dish.* Mark: *in the bowl.* A paropsis is a four-cornered vessel for food, so called from equal apsides, that is, from equal sides: a catinum is an earthen vessel fit for pouring in liquid, and it could happen that the table held a vessel both earthen [0168C] and four-cornered. The penalty is foretold, that the punishments declared might correct him whom shame had not overcome.

VERS. 24.-- *It were better for him.* It would be much better not to be at all than to be in evil case, he says this in the customary and simple way. For it cannot be well with him who is not. Or does he say to the devil, not to be born unto sin: or was it also good for him that he should not be born to Christ by calling, lest he should be an apostate by betrayal? Or we are wont to say simply, it would be better not to be than to be in evil case.

*Rabbi.* The others say, Lord, this man, Rabbi. As though it were a lesser sin to betray a master than the Lord, or else by way of flattery.

VERS. 25.-- *Thou hast said it.* Nor is it openly said here whether [0168D] it was he: for it can be understood as though he were saying, I did not say it.

VERS. 26.-- *And whilst they were at supper,* etc. The solemn joys of the old Pasch being ended, which were kept in memory of the deliverance of the people of God out of Egypt, he passes over to the new, which he willed the Church to frequent in memory of himself. Namely, that in place of the flesh and blood of the lamb he might substitute the sacrament of his own body and blood, and might show that he is the one to whom *the Lord hath sworn, and he will not repent: thou art a priest [n: (Psal. CIX)]*, etc. *He took. [n: (HIER.)]* After the typical Pasch had been fulfilled, and he had eaten the flesh of the lamb with the apostles, he takes bread. *And blessed and broke.* Because he so subjected the man he had assumed to death, that he showed the power of immortality [0169A] to be in him, and that therefore he would rise again quickly. *He broke,* that he might show that the breaking of his body would not be without his own will. He blessed first, because the nature which he assumed he filled, together with the Father and the Holy Spirit, with the grace of divine power. Therefore he gave his body after supper, because it was necessary that the typical Pasch should first be completed, and that so the sacraments should afterwards be substituted for the old Pasch. But we, for reverence of so great a sacrament, are refreshed with the sacraments of the Lord's passion before bodily food.

VERS. 27.-- *He gave thanks.* He shows what each one ought to do under the scourge of his own fault, since he himself bore with an even mind the scourge of another's: because bread strengthens the body, wine produces blood.

VERS. 29.-- *I will not drink.* I will not take delight in the ceremonies [0169B] of this people, among which the rites of the paschal lamb are chief. After the passion I will not take up flesh, until I take it up new, which is now mortal.

*New in the kingdom of the Father.* Calling that *new,* and this old, he shows the body: namely, that which is of the old Adam, which shall be changed by immortality. *With you,* by this immortality is promised to his own also, although not at the same time. *Of this fruit,* because the same bodies are to rise again. But the day of my resurrection will be when, exalted in the kingdom of God, that is, in the glory of immortality, I shall be filled with a new joy together with you over the salvation of that same people regenerated by spiritual grace.

VERS. 30.-- *And a hymn.* This is what the psalm says: *The poor shall eat and shall be filled [n: (Psal. XXI)]*, etc. Or, according to John, he sang a hymn giving thanks to the Father, [0169C] in which he prayed for himself and for the disciples and for those who should believe. *They went out unto the mount,* etc. That through the receiving of the sacraments and through his own intercession he may teach us to ascend to higher gifts, with which we are anointed in heart: where is the refreshment of labour, the solace of sorrow.

VERS. 31.-- *That you all suffer scandal* [var: Vulg. Mt 26:31 *Omnes vos scandalum patiemini,* "you shall all be scandalized"]. He foretells to them what they are to suffer, that afterwards they may not then despair, but be set free by penance.

VERS. 32.-- *After.* As he foretold the place of the passion, so he foretells the place of the resurrection, that as they are sorrowful from the passion, so they may rejoice, glorified by the resurrection or by the vision. *I will go before you,* etc. That he might console their weakness by the promise of his own return.

[0169D]VERS. 33.-- *And if all.* It is not a lie nor rashness, but faith and burning love, to such a degree that he did not look to his own weakness nor to the trustworthiness of the words of God.

VERS. 36.-- *And pray.* Death being close at hand, he fitly prays in the valley of fatness, because through humility and the fatness of charity he underwent death for us.

VERS. 37.-- *He began to grow sorrowful.* Not from fear of the passion, since he had come for this, but on account of the scandal of the apostles and the perdition of the ungodly. *[n: (HIER.)] He began to grow sorrowful,* that he might prove the truth of the man he had assumed. He was truly made sorrowful, but the passion does not rule over his mind: rather it is a propassion, whence he says: *He began [0170A] to grow sorrowful.* But he is made sorrowful not from fear of suffering, since he had come for this, but on account of the unhappy Judas and the scandal of the apostles and the casting out of the Jews and the overthrow of Jerusalem.

VERS. 38.-- *Sorrowful.* It is the soul that fears and is made sorrowful. Peter, the lesser, does not fear, saying: *I will lay down my life for thee:* because, as a man, he does not know the force of death. Christ fears, because, as God set within a body, he sets forth the frailty of the flesh: he who took a body was bound to undergo all things that belong to a body.

*Even unto death. [n: (HIL.)]* Not because of death, but until he should free the apostles by his own passion. It is not death, but the time of death, that is in fear, because after the power of the resurrection the faith of believers is to be strengthened.

VERS. 39.-- *If it be possible.* This not out of fear, [0170B] but out of mercy, lest the Jews, who have not the excuse of ignorance, should not kill him [cj: *non occidant*; read *occidant*, "should kill him"]; whence he says: *this,* that is, the cup of the people of the Jews, not simply a cup: *If it be possible* that the Gentiles may believe without the destruction of the Jews, I refuse the passion. But if they are to be blinded that the Gentiles may come in, let not my will, but thine be done. *Not as I will. [n: (AUG.)]* He transfigures his own into himself, who wills nothing other than the Father, as in that place: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat. And why persecutest thou me?* But he teaches them to correct their own private will and to direct it according to the divine. [n: (AMBR.)] The man whom he showed forth by the truth of a body, he made equal in affection, so as to say: *But not as I will, but as thou* [var: Vulg. Mt 26:39 *verumtamen non sicut ego volo, sed sicut tu*]. He took up my will, he took up my sorrow. [0170C] Confidently do I name sorrow, I who preach the cross.

VERS. 41.-- *But the flesh.* He says this assuredly not of himself, but of those who said that they would never deny him.

VERS. 42.-- *Again a second time.* He prays a second time, that is, if Nineveh cannot be saved otherwise, unless the gourd wither, that is, the people of the Jews, who before were his tabernacle, so that it may kill me.

VERS. 44.-- *He prayed the third time.* He prayed three times over, that we too may pray for pardon from past sins, and for protection from the evils we repent of, and for wariness against future perils, and that we may direct every prayer to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Likewise, as there is a threefold temptation of desire, so also of fear. For there is the concupiscence of the flesh, the concupiscence of the eyes and the ambition of the world. There is also the fear of death, and the fear of advantage, [0170D] and the fear of pain, against all which he teaches that we ought to be fortified by prayer. Whence also, on account of the threefold temptation of the passion, it can be understood that the Lord prayed three times.

VERS. 47.-- *With swords and clubs.* The deed agrees with the changed mind. For now he comes with clubs who, so long as he was at peace, bore himself more quietly.
