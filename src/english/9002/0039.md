## CHAPTER XXVI. (cont.)

VERS. 48.-- *He gave them a sign* [var: Vulg. Mt 26:48 *dedit illis signum*]. He thought that the signs which he had seen were done not by divine power, but by magic: and him whom he had heard was transfigured upon the mountain he feared lest in some such way he should now slip out of their hands: and therefore he gave a sign. *Whomsoever I shall kiss.* He still had something of the modesty of a disciple, in that he did not betray him openly to persecutions, but by the sign of a kiss.

[0171A]VERS. 49.-- *And he kissed him.* The Lord received the kiss, not that he might teach us to dissemble, but lest he should seem to flee the betrayal, whence: *With them that hated peace I was peaceable [n: (Psal. CXIX)]*.

VERS. 52.-- *They shall perish with the sword.* With that sword which, being of fire, is turned before paradise, that is, of divine vengeance, and with the sword of the spirit, that is, of the word of God.

VERS. 53.-- *Thinkest thou that,* etc. As if he should say, I have no need of the help of the twelve apostles, I who can have twelve legions of angels, in which are seventy-two thousand, by which the tongue of all nations is expressed. But he intimates that it is not to be feared if all nations rise up against Jesus, since he himself has far stronger armies of angels. Otherwise: This number signifies the whole race of men [0171B] that was to contend with the Roman empire against the Jews. These are the angels of God, who in the forty-second year after the resurrection, together with Titus and Vespasian, destroyed those murderers and burned their city.

VERS. 55.-- *You are come out with swords.* It is foolish to seek with swords and clubs him who gives himself up of his own accord, and to track down by night through a betrayer, as though he were in hiding, one who taught daily in the temple. But you gather together in the darkness, because your power is in the darkness.

VERS. 57.-- *To Caiphas.* The name agrees with the deed. Caiphas, that is, a searcher out, or shrewd to fulfil, namely, the wickedness of his own guile. Or, one vomiting with the mouth, because he was shameless in uttering a lie and in demanding a murder.

[0171C]VERS. 58.-- *Afar off.* Rightly afar off, he who was about to deny him close at hand, he signifies the Church, which would imitate the passion of Christ far differently, because the Church suffers for itself, but Christ for the Church.

VERS. 64.-- *Thou hast said it.* Mark: *I am:* which is the same: And there is a like answer against Pilate and against Caiphas, that they may be condemned out of their own sentence. *The Son of man.* Me, exalted in the form which you judge, and having the name which is above every name.

VERS. 65.-- *He rent his garments.* The fury which made him rise from his seat makes him also rend his garment. By a great mystery: since the garment of the Lord could not be rent even by the soldiers. For it signifies that their priesthood, the pontificate, was for their crimes to be utterly dissolved: but the solidity [0171D] of the Church (which is called the garment of God) will remain unbroken to the end of the world. It is the custom of the Jews, when they hear anything of blasphemy, they rend their garments.

Which Paul and Barnabas did in Lycaonia, when they were called Jupiter and Apollo.

VERS. 67.-- *Then they spit.* As he was then spit upon with the spittle of unbelievers, so now he is dishonoured with the reproaches of false believers, and is beaten with buffets, that is, with the blasphemies of the same men.

VERS. 68.-- *Prophesy.* They speak an insult to him who willed to be held a prophet by the peoples. For they veiled his face, according to Mark, not that he might not see their crimes, but that they might hide from themselves the grace [0172A] of the knowledge of him. As there was a veil upon the face of Moses, which remains to this day.

VERS. 69.-- *One maidservant.* Why does a maidservant betray him first, when the men were better able to recognize him, unless that this sex might be shown to have sinned unto the death of the Lord: and redeemed through the passion. And therefore a woman first received the mystery of the resurrection: and kept the commands, that she might abolish the old error of the transgression. As through a woman the ancient enemy seduced the first parent, so through a woman he drives the prince of the Church to deny the master whom he had confessed to be Christ the Son of God.

VERS. 72.-- *I know not the man.* How harmful are the conversations of the wicked, which compel Peter to deny Christ, or that he knew the man whom he confessed [0172B] among the disciples to be the Son of God.

VERS. 73.-- *They that stood by.* John and Luke speak of one, a kinsman of Malchus. But either it is a plural for a singular. Or there was one who had seen him and was speaking chiefly, whom the rest, following him, accused in like manner.

VERS. 74.-- *And immediately the cock crew.* After the third denial the cock's crowing follows. The sacraments of things are signified by the state of the times. At midnight he denies, at the cock's crowing he repents, after the resurrection, in the light, he professed three times that he loved him whom he had three times denied: because what he had erred in the darkness of forgetfulness, he corrected by the remembrance of the light already hoped for: and by the presence of that same true light he fully raised up whatever he had changed.

VERS. 75.-- *He remembered.* In another Gospel it is said, [0172C] *The Lord looked upon Peter [n: (Luc. XXII)]*, and by his look drew him to tears, as though bringing back to his mind how often he had denied, and what he had foretold to him, nor could he remain in darkness upon whom the light of the world had looked. *And going forth.* Shut off from the counsel of the ungodly, he washes away his fault, which he did not do within. For this cause he was permitted to waver, that in the prince of the Church a remedy of penance might be established, and that no one should dare to trust in his own strength.
