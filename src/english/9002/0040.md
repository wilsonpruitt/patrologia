## CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *And in the morning.* Matthew and Mark have woven together the narrative of the things that were done with the Lord up to the morning, but afterwards they go back to tell the denial of Peter: which being ended, they return [0172D] again to the morning, that from there they might weave together the rest.

VERS. 4. *I have sinned in betraying,* etc. As though it were in his own power to change the sentence of the persecutors: but although he changed his will, yet he did not change the outcome of his first will. *What is that to us.* What does it concern us about this? *look thou to it,* do thou know what thou hast done. Or, what dost thou see as pertaining to us. What does the price received concern us: thou hadst approved of that, do thou with it what thou wilt. They who sin unto death have so great ignorance and blindness that they are neither troubled in their crimes, nor tormented with the pain of repentance.

VERS. 5.-- *And casting down.* As his repentance was fruitless, so also this offering in the temple is execrable. *And going away. [n: (HIER.)]* It profited Judas nothing to have done penance, by which he could not [0173A] correct his crime. But when a brother so sins against a brother that he is able to amend what he has sinned, it can be forgiven him. But if the works remain, penance is taken up with the voice in vain. This is what is said of him: *And let his prayer be turned to sin [n: (Psal. CVIII)]*: so that not only was he unable to amend the wickedness of the betrayal, but he added the crime of his own murder, whence he was the more wicked.

VERS. 6.-- *It is not lawful for them.* Truly straining out the gnat and swallowing the camel: for if for this reason they do not put the money into the corbona, that is, into the treasury, that is, among the gifts of God, because it is the price of blood, why then is the blood itself poured out? And what reward is theirs, if from it a field is bought for the burial of the poor?

VERS. 9.-- *By Jeremias. [n: (HIER.)]* This is read not in Jeremias, but in Zacharias: yet in other words. [0173B] I lately read an apocryphon of Jeremias, in which I found these words written. But nevertheless it seems to me rather that this was taken from Zacharias, after the manner of the apostles, who, the order of the words being passed over, bring forward only the meanings out of the Old Testament.

*Of the children of Israel,* etc. From whom it was less fitting, who ought to have magnified him.

VERS. 11.-- *Thou art the king of the Jews.* According to Luke they had brought two charges, namely that he forbade tribute to be given to Caesar, and that he said he was a king. Concerning the kingdom, therefore, Pilate asks, because he does not know it. Concerning the tributes: because he had heard that he had answered: *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's [n: (Zac. II)]*: he disregards, as it were, the open lie of the envious. Whence Mark: *And their testimonies were not agreeing [n: (Marc. XXIV)]* [var: Vulg. Mc 14:56 *et convenientia testimonia non erant*, without *eorum*]. In [0173C] this, that Pilate asks of no other crime except whether he is the king of the Jews: the Jews are convicted of impiety, who could not even falsely find what they might lay against the Saviour.

VERS. 16.-- *Who was called Barabbas.* Barabbas, in the Gospel according to the Hebrews, is interpreted *son of the father* or *their master.* He himself is Antichrist, whom they prefer to Christ, to whom it is said: *You are of your father the devil [n: (Joan. VIII)]*. Pilate, who is interpreted *the mouth of the hammerer,* signifies the devil, who is the hammer of the whole earth, by whom the Lord permits his own vessels to be tried: and at the last he grinds him down, when in the final damnation he shall break him in pieces.

VERS. 17.-- *Whom will you.* Wishing to satisfy the people, he gives the choice, although he does not doubt that Christ [0173D] is to be chosen, knowing that he had been handed over through envy. *Who is called Christ.* The same as what Mark says: the king of the Jews, whose king Christ was called.

VERS. 19.-- *For the judgment seat.* The tribunal is the seat of judges, as the throne is of kings, the chair of teachers. *His wife.* The wife of a Gentile man understands in a vision that which the Jews, waking, would not understand. *Have thou nothing to do with that just man.* Now at last the devil, understanding that through Christ he was to lose his spoils, just as at the first he brought in death through a woman; so now through a woman he wishes to free Christ out of the hands of the Jews, lest through his death he lose the empire of death.

VERS. 21.-- *Barabbas,* etc. They chose the son of the devil instead of the Son of God, that they might destroy the Saviour together with their own salvation, [0174A] and set free a betrayer, which petition of theirs cleaves to them even to this day, their nation and their place and their liberty being lost: because they willed to destroy the giver of liberty.

VERS. 22.-- *What therefore shall I do with Jesus.* He sought many occasions to set him free with the goodwill of the people. First, comparing a robber with a just man. Then: *What then shall I do with Jesus, who is called Christ?* [var: Vulg. Mt 27:22 *Quid igitur faciam de Jesu, qui dicitur Christus?*; Migne's own lemma above prints *igitur*] And when they said: *let him be crucified,* he did not at once yield, but according to his wife's suggestion he says: *Why, what evil hath he done?* By which saying Pilate absolved Christ.

VERS. 24.-- *He washed his hands.* Signifying the Gentile people, a stranger to the impiety of the Jews, who cried out: *Crucify him, crucify him.*

VERS. 25.-- *His blood.* The imprecation persists to this day, and the blood of Christ is not taken away [0174B] from them.

VERS. 26.-- *Then he released to them Barabbas.* Barabbas, a seditious robber and the author of murders, was released to the people of the Jews. [n: (ISID.)] The devil, who long since had for his pride been driven out of the fatherland of light, and had been sent into the prison of darkness. And therefore the Jews cannot have peace, because they chose rather the prince of seditions. Or, Barabbas bore the type of Antichrist.

*But Jesus having been scourged* [var: Vulg. Mt 27:26 *Jesum autem flagellatum*]. Therefore it is to be believed that Pilate scourged Jesus and gave him to the soldiers to be mocked, that the Jews, sated with his punishments and his reproaches, might thirst no further for his death. *But Jesus.* Serving the Roman laws, which command that those who are to be crucified be first scourged.

[0174C]VERS. 28.-- *A scarlet cloak. [n: (HIER.)]* In the scarlet cloak he bears up the bloody works of the Gentiles. In the crown of thorns he looses the ancient curse. In the reed he kills the venomous beasts. Or he holds the reed in his hand, that he might write the sacrilege of the Jews. He is not mocked in his own garments, but in those which he bore for our sins. When he was crucified he received his own adornment at once: because the elements are troubled, and the creature gives testimony to the Creator.

VERS. 32.-- *A man of Cyrene.* Behold, not a Hebrew, but a foreigner is put under the reproach of Christ, that the fulness of the sacraments might be shown to pass over from circumcision to uncircumcision.

VERS. 33.-- *Golgotha.* It is Syriac, and is interpreted *of Calvary,* not because of the baldness of Adam, whom they falsely say was buried [0174D] there, but because of the beheading of the condemned. Adam is read in the book of Josue to have been buried near Hebron.

VERS. 34.-- *Mingled with gall.* Another evangelist says *myrrhed,* that is, most bitter. And there could have been a mingling of both, of gall and of myrrh. *And when he had tasted, he would not.* This Mark passes over in silence: *he would not drink.* This is what Mark says: *he took it not,* namely, that he should drink it.

VERS. 35.-- *They divided.* This John more distinctly: the garments into four parts, that the Church may be signified in the four parts of the world. The coat allotted signifies the unity of all the parts by the bond of charity, which comes about by lot, that is, by the grace of God, not by human choice.

VERS. 37.-- *The cause.* For which the Jews handed him over [0175A] to Pilate. The King through the cross did not lose, but confirmed, his empire, whether the Jews will or will not.

VERS. 38.-- *One on the right hand.* One on the right hand, by whom is signified the mortification of the flesh for the sake of heavenly glory: one on the left hand, by whom again is signified the mortification of the flesh for the sake of some cause of the world.

VERS. 39.-- *They blasphemed.* It had been foretold in the book of Wisdom [n: (Cap. II)]: *Let us condemn him to a most shameful death. For there shall be respect in his words. For if he be truly the Son of God, he will take him up and will deliver him from the hand of his adversaries* [var: Vulg. Sap 2:20 *erit enim ei respectus ex sermonibus illius*, and Sap 2:18 *Si enim est verus filius Dei, suscipiet illum, et liberabit eum de manibus contrariorum*]. Because therefore he was being crucified and was not delivered, they believed that he was not the Son of God: therefore they insult him as he hangs upon the tree, and shake their heads:

VERS. 40.-- *If thou be the Son of God.* If, yielding to those who insulted him, [0175B] he had come down from the cross, he would not have shown the power of patience.

VERS. 42.-- *And we will believe him* [var: Vulg. Mt 27:42 *et credimus ei*, the present tense]. A fraudulent promise: he does a greater thing in rising from the tomb, and you do not believe: if therefore he should come down from the cross, you would not believe. But the demons put this in; for they felt at once the power of the cross, and their own strength broken, and they do this that he may come down from the cross. But the Lord, knowing this, remains, that he may destroy the devil.

VERS. 44.-- *And the selfsame thing the robbers also.* At first both blasphemed, then, the signs being seen, one believed. So both peoples first blasphemed God: but the one, terrified by the signs, repented, and now convicts the Jews.

VERS. 45.-- *And from the sixth hour.* Note that the Lord is crucified when the sun is about to withdraw from the centre of the world, because [0175C] he dies for sins, and Adam, when he had sinned, heard the voice of the Lord after midday. And it is the order of reason that at the time at which he shut the gate of paradise against the transgressing Adam, at that time he should unlock it to the penitent robber. That Mark says the Lord was crucified at the third hour is for this reason, that he may show that the Jews, who excuse themselves, crucified him rather with their tongues than the soldiers by whose hands it was done: who at the third hour cried out that he should be crucified. For when he was crucified, it was about the sixth hour, that is, not yet fully the sixth.

VERS. 46.-- *And about the ninth hour.* When the day had declined toward evening and the sun had grown cool from its heat, he completes the passion, showing that he dies for our sins, by which we have fallen from the divine light and love into this night. In the morning the Lord rose again, [0175D] teaching that he would lead forth those raised up in soul into the light of eternal happiness. *Eli, Eli, lamma.* Whose nature he takes upon him, of the same men he bewails the misery. Wherein he shows how much they themselves ought to weep who sin. Marvel not at the lowliness of the words and at the complaints of one forsaken, since, knowing the form of a servant, thou seest the scandal of the cross.

VERS. 47.-- *And certain.* Not all, perhaps Romans, not knowing the property of the Hebrew speech. Or some Jews, reckoning Christ less than Elias, so that he should beg his help.

VERS. 49.-- *Let be, let us see.* Because they understood the voice of the Lord amiss, they waited in vain for the coming of Elias.

[0176A]VERS. 51.-- *And behold the veil of the temple.* That the ark of the testament and all the sacraments of the law, which were being covered, might appear and might pass over to the Gentiles. Josephus says that the angelic powers, once the guardians of the temple, then cried out together: let us pass over from these seats. The veil which was called the outer one: because now we see in part; but when that which is perfect shall come, then the inner veil shall be rent asunder. *The earth was moved.* All the elements show forth their Lord crucified. The earth is moved, not enduring to bear its Lord as he hangs. *The rocks.* That is, the hearts of the Gentiles, or the prophecies of the prophets, who are called rocks from the rock Christ, that whatever in them was shut up under the hard veil of the law might, being rent, lie open to the Gentiles.

VERS. 52.-- *And the monuments.* And yet, although the monuments [0176B] were opened, they did not rise before the Lord rose again, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren. *The bodies of the saints.* These are to be believed to have ascended in like manner with the body when the Lord ascended.

VERS. 53.-- *Into the holy city.* Jerusalem, which is called holy because of the temple, and the Holy of holies, which no other city has.

VERS. 54.-- *And the centurion.* These men confess God in the scandal of the cross. Arius in the Church preaches a creature, whom the blind Jews do not believe even for so many miracles. In the centurion is signified the faith of the Church, which, the veil of the heavenly mysteries being unlocked by the death of the Lord, affirms Jesus to be the Son of God while the Synagogue is silent.
