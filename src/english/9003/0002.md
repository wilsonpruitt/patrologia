## ON PSALM II.

VERS. 1.-- *Why have the gentiles raged, and the peoples meditated vain things,* etc. It is a question of rebuking. For the prophet chides the peoples. *Why have they raged* against the Lord the Saviour, when they had no causes of anger? But he says that they meditated *vain things,* because they did not accomplish what they wished, namely that Christ should be extinguished.

[0756A]VERS. 2.-- *The kings stood up,* etc. This is said of the persecutors of the Lord, who are also commemorated in the Acts of the Apostles. But *they stood up* signifies not presence, but will.

VERS. 3.-- *Let us break asunder their bonds,* etc. Although it can be taken otherwise also, yet it is more fitly taken from the person of those whom he said had meditated vain things, so that this is *let us break asunder their bonds,* let us take pains that they bind us not, nor that the Christian religion be laid upon us. But *theirs,* either of God the Father and of his Christ, or of Christ and of the apostles.

VERS. 4.-- *He that dwelleth in the heavens,* etc. The sentence is repeated. For in place of that which is, *He that dwelleth in the heavens,* there is set afterwards: *the Lord.* In place of that which is, *shall laugh at them,* there is set afterwards, *shall deride them.* [0756B] None of these things ought to be understood carnally, as though God laughed with cheeks or derided with the nose; but that force is to be taken which he gives to his saints, that, discerning things to come, that is, the name of Christ and his dominion about to spread abroad among those who come after and to lay hold of all nations, they may understand that those men meditated vain things. For this force, by which those things were foreknown, is the laughter of God and the derision. But by the *heavens* we take holy souls, through whom, since they indeed foreknow what shall come to pass, God will deride and mock.

VERS. 5.-- *Then shall he speak,* etc. The *anger and fury* of God must be understood not as a perturbation of his; but as the force by which he most justly takes vengeance, all creation being subject to him for his ministry. Although the *anger of God* may rightly be understood also as that very darkening of the mind which [0756C] follows upon those who transgress the law of God.

VERS. 6.-- *But I am appointed,* etc. Some interpret Sion as *watching,* which we ought to take as the Church, where daily the intention of watching upon the charity of God is raised up. Which is also called a *mountain* because of its eminence and its firmness.

VERS. 7.-- *The Lord said to me,* etc. That day may seem to be spoken of in the prophecy on which Jesus Christ was born according to the man. Yet because *to-day* signifies a present, and in eternity there is neither anything past, as though it had ceased to be, nor future, as though it were not yet, *to-day* may also be understood, by a most sincere and Catholic faith, of the everlasting generation of the power and the wisdom of God, who is the only-begotten Son.

[0756D]VERS. 8.-- *Ask of me,* etc. Namely, that the nations may be joined to the Christian name, and so be redeemed from death, that they may be possessed by God.

VERS. 9.-- *Thou shalt rule them with a rod,* etc. *With a rod of iron,* in inflexible justice, crushing in them earthly desires and the miry businesses of the old man.

VERS. 10.-- *And now, ye kings,* etc. *And now,* that is, now made new: for they are signified as spiritual who *judge the earth:* for whatever we judge is beneath us: but whatever is beneath the spiritual man is deservedly named *earth.*

VERS. 11.-- *Serve ye the Lord,* etc. Most excellently is *rejoice* subjoined, lest that which was said, *serve,* should seem to avail unto misery. But again, lest that same thing should incline [0757A] into an outpouring of rashness, there is added, *with trembling,* that it might avail unto a circumspect wariness.

VERS.12.-- *Embrace ye discipline,* etc. To understand and to be instructed, this is *to embrace discipline.* But in that which is said, *embrace ye,* there is signified sufficiently that there is a certain defence and fortification against all the things which could hurt, unless it were embraced with so great care. But *to perish* from *the just way* is a great punishment, which is dreaded by those who have perceived something of the sweetness of justice.

VERS.13.-- *When his wrath shall be kindled in a short time,* etc. That is, when the vengeance shall come which is prepared for the ungodly and for sinners, it shall not only not touch those who *trust in the Lord,* but shall even profit them unto that blessedness where is the sum and the heaping up of all good things. But that [0757B] which is set down: *in a short time,* I judge to signify this, that it will be something sudden, when sinners reckon it remote and far off in time to come.

## ON PSALM III.

VERS. 1.-- *A psalm of David, when he fled from the face of his son Absalom.*

*David,* who is interpreted *strong of hand* or *desirable,* signifies our Lord Jesus Christ, whose voice is brought in in this psalm speaking of his passion and his resurrection. But *Absalom* signifies Judas the betrayer, or the persecuting people. For Judas also was still among the apostles when the Lord said: *The children of the bridegroom cannot fast, so long as the bridegroom is with them.* [var: Vulg. Mt 9:15 *lugere*, "to mourn"; "fast" is Mark's word at Mc 2:19] And of the people he says through Moses [0757C]: *A firstborn son among Israel.* [var: Vulg. Ex 4:22 *Filius meus primogenitus Israel*, "Israel is my firstborn son"] The Truth therefore fled the mind of Judas, or of the persecuting people, while it did not cease to enlighten them. Or, as the Gospel narrates: *These things Jesus spoke, and he went away and hid himself from them.* But whereas Absalom is interpreted *the Father's peace,* it is well understood that Christ showed peace to his betrayer, although that man was laid waste by the inward war of so wicked a thought: and toward the persecuting people he stood so appeased that he besought the Father for them, saying: *Father, pardon them, for they know not what they do.* [var: Vulg. Lc 23:34 *dimitte*, "forgive"]

VERS. 2.-- *Lord, why are they multiplied,* etc. So greatly, namely, *are they multiplied,* that even out of the number of the disciples there was not wanting one who joined the number of the persecutors.

VERS. 3.-- *Many say,* etc. It is manifest [0757D] that unless they had despaired of his rising again, they would certainly not have killed him. Therefore neither would Judas have betrayed him, unless he had been of the number of those who despised Christ, saying: *There is no salvation for him in his God.*

VERS. 4.-- *But thou, O Lord, art my protector,* etc. He too calls the Lord his *glory,* whom the Word of God so took up that together with him he became God. But *head* I think is here to be taken as the human mind itself, which so cleaved to, and in a manner grew together with, the excellent supereminence of the Word taking up the man, that in so great a lowliness of the passion it was not laid down.

These things can also be taken from the person of the Church. For in that man the Church also was taken up by the Word, which was made flesh and dwelt among us. Let therefore [0758A] the Church speak together with her head, set amid the storms of persecutions throughout the whole round of the earth: *Lord, why are they multiplied,* etc., her head being exalted, who as the firstborn from the dead ascended into heaven.

VERS. 5.-- *With my voice I have cried to the Lord,* etc. Not with the voice of the body, which is put forth with the noise of beaten air, but with the voice of the heart, which is silent to men, but sounds to God as a cry, when the soul alone, drawing nothing of the flesh and nothing of fleshly intentions into the prayer, speaks to the Lord where he alone hears. And this too is called a cry because of the force of the intention itself.

But he says that the Father heard him *from the mountain,* that is, from his own justice. For it was just that he should raise up [0758B] from the dead one slain though innocent, and to whom evils were repaid for good things, and should repay worthy things to his persecutors: For we read: *Thy justice is as the mountains of God.* But the Church also is heard from that same mountain, which is likewise her head, of which the Prophet says: *The mountain of the house of the Lord shall be prepared on the top of mountains.*

VERS. 6.-- *I have slept,* etc. Not unfittingly may *I* be noted, to signify that of his own will he underwent death, according to that: *Therefore doth the Father love me, because I lay down my life, and I shall take it again: no man shall take it away from myself.* And it is well taken: *I have slept and have taken my rest:* I gave myself over to the passion, and death followed. To the people of the Church also it is said: *Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall touch thee,* [var: Vulg. Eph 5:14 *illuminabit te Christus*, "Christ shall enlighten thee"] because he was taken up from among sinners. *I will not fear [0758C] thousands,* etc. It is not to a God sleeping, not to a God lying down, that it is said, *arise:* but it is the manner of the divine Scriptures to attribute to the person of God what he works in us, not indeed everywhere, but where it can fittingly be said: or that he himself be said to speak, when by his gift the several messengers of truth speak.

VERS. 7.-- *For thou hast struck all them,* etc. They are broken, that is, brought to nothing, the words of sinners tearing and cursing the Son of God. The *teeth of sinners* can also be taken as the princes of sinners, by whose authority a man is now cut off from the fellowship of those living rightly, and as it were incorporated with those living ill.

VERS. 8.-- *O Lord, there is salvation,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ps 3:9 *Domini est salus*, "salvation is the Lord's"] Let no man therefore presume of himself, since it is the Lord's to save from [0758D] the death of sin. But do thou, O Lord, bless thy people, that hopeth in thee for salvation.
