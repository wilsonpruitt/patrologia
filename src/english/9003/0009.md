## ON PSALM IX. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Whose mouth is full of cursing,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For it is a great cursing to seek after heaven by arts so wicked, and to procure such merits for the seizing of an everlasting seat. ⟦s3⟧ But with this his *cursing* his *mouth is full.* ⟦s4⟧ For this covetousness shall not have its effect, but beneath all his own it will avail only to his own undoing, because he dared to promise this to himself. ⟦s5⟧ *With bitterness and deceit,* that is, with anger and with treacheries, whereby he will turn the multitude to his own part. ⟦s6⟧ But what follows: *Under his tongue are labour and sorrow,* is referred to that which he said in his heart: *I shall not be moved,* and therefore *under his tongue,* not upon his tongue, since he will think these things silently, but will speak such things to men that he may [0772C] seem good and just and the Son of God.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *He sitteth in ambush,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ With what rich men, save with those whom he will heap up with the gifts of this world? ⟦s3⟧ And therefore *in ambush* with these he is said *to sit,* since he will make a show of their false happiness for the casting down of men. ⟦s4⟧ But *in secret* I think it was said, where it is not easily understood what is to be sought after and what to be shunned. ⟦s5⟧ But *the innocent* indeed *to kill* is to make a guilty man out of an innocent one.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *His eyes look upon the poor man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For he will chiefly persecute the just, of whom it is said: *Blessed are the poor in spirit. ⟦s3⟧ A lion* moreover *in his den* he calls him, in whom both violence and guile will be at work. ⟦s4⟧ For the first persecution of the Church was violent, [0772D] when by proscriptions, torments and slaughters Christians were compelled to sacrifice. ⟦s5⟧ The second persecution is fraudulent, which is now carried on through heretics of this kind and false brethren. ⟦s6⟧ A third remains, to come through Antichrist, than which nothing is more perilous, since it will be both violent and fraudulent. ⟦s7⟧ It will have violence in its rule, guile in its miracles. ⟦s8⟧ To violence is referred what is said, *A lion:* to guiles, what is said: *in his den.* ⟦s9⟧ And again these same things are repeated in reversed order: *He lieth in wait that he may seize,* etc. ⟦s10⟧ For *ambushes* belong to guile, *to seize* is reckoned to violence. ⟦s11⟧ Likewise the things that follow are the same:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 31.-- *In his net he hath humbled,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ps 9:31 *In laqueo suo humiliabit eum*, "in his net he shall humble him"] For *a net* well signifies ambushes: but dominion [0773A] most fitly intimates terror: whence he says: *he will bow down and fall,* etc.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *For he hath said in his heart,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This is the bowing down and the most wretched fall, while the human mind as it were prospers in its own iniquities, and thinks itself spared, when it is blinded, that it may be kept for the last and seasonable vengeance, of which he now already speaks, saying:

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Arise, O Lord God, let it be exalted,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, let thy power be made manifest. ⟦s3⟧ For the Church labouring in those times, like a ship among great waves and storms, rouses the Lord as though he slept, that he may command the winds, and calm return. ⟦s4⟧ And so now, the judgment being manifest, they that understand and exult say:

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0773B]VERS. 34.-- *Wherefore hath the ungodly provoked God,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, what did it profit to do so great evils? ⟦s3⟧ Then follows:

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *Thou seest, for thou considerest labour,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This sense demands a right delivery, in which, if there be an error, it is darkened. ⟦s3⟧ For thus the ungodly man said in his heart, *God will not require it,* as though God considered labour and sorrow, *that he may deliver them into his own hands,* [var: Vulg. Ps 9:35 *ut tradas eos in manus tuas*, "that thou mayest deliver them into thy hands"] that is, as though he feared to labour and to grieve, and therefore spared them, lest their punishment be burdensome to him, or lest he be troubled by the storm of anger: as men for the most part do, dissembling their vengeance lest they labour or grieve.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To thee is he left,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For therefore is he poor, that is, he has despised all the temporal goods of this world, [0773C] that thou alone mayest be his hope. ⟦s3⟧ But he called him *an orphan* whose father, this world, dies to him, by which he was carnally begotten, but who can now say: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world.* ⟦s4⟧ For to such orphans God becomes a father, to whom he says: *Call not to yourselves a father upon earth.* [var: Vulg. Mt 23:9 *Et patrem nolite vocare vobis super terram*, "and call none your father upon earth"]

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *Break thou the arm of the sinner,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Of him, namely, of whom it was said above: *he shall have dominion over all his enemies. ⟦s3⟧ The arm* therefore he called his power. ⟦s4⟧ *It shall be sought* moreover, *his sin,* that is, he shall be judged concerning his own sin: *and it shall not be found,* because he himself shall perish for his own sin.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *The Lord shall reign for ever,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By *the nations* he set down sinners and the ungodly.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *The desire of the poor,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That desire [0773D] wherewith they burned, when in the straits and tribulations of this world they longed for the day of the Lord.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But *the preparation* of *the heart* is that of which it is sung in another psalm: *My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready:* and of which the Apostle also speaks: *but if we hope for that which we see not, we wait for it with patience.* ⟦s2⟧ But now we ought by rule to take *the ear of God* to be not a bodily member, but the power whereby he hears. ⟦s3⟧ For it is not lawful to think it bodily, since the Lord God heard not a sounding voice, but the preparation of the heart.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *To judge for the fatherless and the humble,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is one thing *to judge the fatherless,* another *to judge for the fatherless. ⟦s3⟧ He judgeth [0774A] the fatherless* even who condemns him: but *he judgeth* moreover *for the fatherless* who brings forth sentence on behalf of the fatherless. ⟦s4⟧ But *men* are not only those of whom it was said: *Appoint, O Lord, a lawgiver over them, that the nations may know that they are men;* but he also who in the same place is understood to be set over them will be *a man,* of whom it is now said: *that man may no more presume to magnify himself upon the earth,* when, namely, the Son of man shall have come to judge for the fatherless, who put off the old man, and in this way as it were carried his father out to burial.
