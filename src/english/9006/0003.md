## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *If thou wilt return. [n: (HIER.)]* The Seventy: *If Israel shall be converted, saith the Lord, let him be converted to me.* ⟦s2⟧ Another reading, converteris, that is, if thou desirest salvation, and sayest that thou hast sinned, be fully converted, that is, believe him whom thou hast denied. ⟦s3⟧ *If thou wilt take away.* ⟦s4⟧ It is plain, because when we are moved and say: *But my feet were almost moved, my steps had well nigh slipped [n: (Psal. LXXII)]*. [0014D] Not out of the weakness of nature do we suffer this, but because we set our stumbling-blocks and our idols against God.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And thou shalt swear, the Lord liveth,* etc. [n: (ID.)] For a reproach, namely, of the dead gods, by whom every idolater swears.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Break up anew. [n: (ID.)]* By rooting out the briers and thorns, etc., *as far as* a mind full of the troubles of the world cannot receive the seed of God and bring forth fruit. ⟦s2⟧ *Be ye circumcised. [n: (ID.)]* It is commanded to the man of Juda and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem that they forsake the letter that killeth, and follow the spirit that quickeneth. ⟦s3⟧ *Lest perhaps. [n: (ID.)]* He warns and foretells, lest he be compelled to do that which we approve in the Ninivites, to whom the sentence was foretold, that by repentance they might turn aside the impending wrath.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0015A]VERS. 7.-- *The lion is come up. [n: (HIER.)]* If any man is a favourer or an author of perverse teachings, of him it can be said, *the lion is come up out of his den,* etc.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the robber.* ⟦s2⟧ Of whom it is said, *He shall have dominion over all his enemies [n: (Psal. IX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Who glories, saying: *I have gone round about the earth, and walked through it [n: (Job II)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For who is there whom the poisons of the devil touch not, save he who says: *For the prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not any thing? [n: (Job. XIV.)] Out of his own place.* ⟦s5⟧ Out of the abyss, in which he is to be bound, into which he entreats that he be not sent.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For this.* ⟦s2⟧ Without repentance we cannot escape the most savage beast, and unless we be converted to God not only in mind, but also in body. ⟦s3⟧ For while the Church is laid waste, the wrath of God is open.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0015B]VERS. 9.-- *Of the princes. [n: (HIER.)]* Who ought to have taught the law, and to have defended their subjects from the lion.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The priests.* ⟦s2⟧ Who are thought to be wise: *For God hath made foolish the wisdom of this world,* because through it they knew not God. ⟦s3⟧ *The prophets shall be amazed,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Aquila, *they shall be out of their mind.* ⟦s5⟧ For who would not go mad, who would not lose heart, when he shall have beheld his princes and kings and priests, once his own, under the lion.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And I said: Alas, alas, alas, Lord God.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he said above, *in that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the Lord,* etc., and now he says, *the heart of the king shall perish,* etc., the prophet is troubled, and thinks that God has lied to him, and does not understand that the one is to be long after, this in the time next at hand.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *A burning wind. [n: (HIER.)]* When, namely, [0015C] the sword shall have reached unto the soul, etc., *as far as* by no means to the people, but to me shall it come, that my wheat may be scattered.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *A full spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ Certain men so expound this passage, that after the threshing-floor has been cleansed, the remnant may be saved. ⟦s3⟧ Whence it is written: *The spirit of fulness shall come to me;* [var: Vulg. Jer. 4:12 *Spiritus plenus ex his veniet mihi*, "a full wind out of these shall come to me"] whence the evangelist, *Of his fulness we all have received [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s4+5⟧ *And now I.* An aposiopesis, as Virgil says [n: (AENEID.)]: Whom I [sic: *egos*]; but it is better to settle the troubled waves.
⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The prophet, being about to speak prosperous things, holds himself back, and has joined sad things to sad.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Behold as,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He beholds things to come as though present; and he describes the army of the Babylonians, [0015D] the din of whose chariots and wheels is compared to a most savage tempest, and the swiftness of whose horses to eagles. ⟦s3⟧ The army of the devil, that is, the chariots and horsemen of Pharao, which the churchman understanding says, *Woe to us, because we are laid waste.* ⟦s4⟧ Believing that saying. ⟦s5⟧ When thou shalt have been converted and shalt have groaned, thou shalt be saved. ⟦s6⟧ *Woe to us,* because the prophet has pointed it out as with a finger, the people groans. ⟦s7⟧ And it perceives them not as things to come, but as already done, saying: *Woe to us.*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Wash from wickedness.* ⟦s2⟧ The prophet answers, nay rather the Lord in the prophet, *Wash.* ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *Wash yourselves, be clean [n: (Isa. I)]*, that is, with the water of baptism, with the water of saving repentance.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *For a voice of one declaring.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks according to the situation [0016A] of Judaea. ⟦s3⟧ For the tribe of Dan, beside Mount Libanus and the city which is now called Paneas, looks toward the north, whence Nabuchodonosor was to come. ⟦s4⟧ *From the mountain.* ⟦s5⟧ For after the tribe of Dan follows the land of Ephraim, through which one comes into Jerusalem. ⟦s6⟧ Allegorically. [n: (HIER.)] The judgment of the Lord comes upon the land that offends against the Lord, with all abundance of punishment.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Watchers.* ⟦s2⟧ Adversaries, namely, who so diligently beset it, and shut in the city with fortifications, that thou wouldst think them keepers of vineyards and fields rather than adversaries. ⟦s3⟧ The Lord wills that all the nations round about know his sentence, and that, Jerusalem being scourged, all men receive discipline.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Thy ways. [n: (HIER.)]* Whatever of evil befalls us, etc., *as far as* how much less against men [0016B] once of the city of God?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *My bowels. [n: (ID.)]* The Lord speaks this, when he sees sedition and discord in the Church, and the partridge crying in her conventicles, and the rest of God turned into wars. ⟦s2⟧ Whence there follows:

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *How long shall I see.* ⟦s2⟧ The voice of the prophet, and through the prophet of God, grieving over the breaking of his people, whose bowels are torn after the manner of a man, the Saviour wept at the death of Lazarus, and over Jerusalem. ⟦s3⟧ Nor does he hide his grief in silence, and the blare of the trumpet and the din of battles disturbs his affection, while evils are heaped upon evils, and the whole land of the two tribes is laid waste.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *For my people is foolish. [n: (HIER.)]* Because [0016C] the princes of my people have not known, etc., *as far as* whom he ever desired to see? ⟦s2⟧ *They are wise,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Malicious wise men, etc., *as far as* it is called not wisdom, but craft and cunning.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *I beheld the earth.* ⟦s2⟧ The prophet discerns in spirit the things which are to come, that the people, hearing, may be terrified and do penance, that it may not suffer what it dreads. ⟦s3⟧ Whatever is said according to the history concerning Judaea and Jerusalem, let us refer to the Church of God: which, when it shall have offended God, and been laid waste by vices and by persecution, where once was the company of virtues and gladness, there may a multitude of sins and mourning be found.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *For thus saith. [n: (HIER.)]* The clemency of the Lord is mingled with wrath: the land is forsaken, but it is not consumed, that there may remain those who understand his clemency [0016D].

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *I have thought and have not repented,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That I might take away the sentence foretold, and that the wrath of him that rages might not come to its end; the Lord threatened through Jonas, but the greatness of tears and groanings overcame the impending sword.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *At the voice of the horseman.* ⟦s2⟧ He describes the army of the raging ones, for fear of whom the people left the city and climbed the steep places: but yet it could not turn aside the wrath of God. ⟦s3⟧ All this can be referred to the Church, when it shall have offended God, and been delivered over to adversaries in the time of persecution, or to vices and sins.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *But thou being laid waste. [n: (HIER.)]* This same thing is to be said against those who have lost the affections of wedlock and [0017A] the chastity of the true faith. ⟦s2⟧ *When thou shalt clothe thyself.* ⟦s3⟧ For these things thou preparest even for thy lovers, and a narrow bed cannot hold them both. ⟦s4⟧ Nor does God receive the ornaments with which before thou didst please thy lovers.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *For a voice as of,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] Two examples are set down, both of a woman in travail and of one mourning, that whatever a woman suffers, either in her offspring or in the death of her children, Jerusalem may suffer in her peoples.
