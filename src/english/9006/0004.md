## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Go about the ways. [n: (HIER.)]* Great is the love of justice, that it is not according to the questioning of Abraham and the answer of God, that for ten just men God should set free the city, and that already on the point of perishing; but if he shall [0017B] find one who doeth judgment and seeketh faith.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *O Lord, thine eyes. [n: (ID.)]* The works of the Jews (in which they exulted according to the ceremonies of the law) are condemned through the faith of Christians, through which by grace we have been saved. ⟦s2⟧ *Thou hast struck them. [n: (GREG.)]* Every divine striking, etc., *as far as* and there is one scourge which begins in time, and is consummated in eternal punishments.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they have not grieved.* ⟦s2⟧ Punishments are brought in that vices may be corrected, but Jerusalem is not amended, not even through torments.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Perhaps they are poor. [n: (HIER.)]* As that, *I will send my son, perhaps they will reverence him [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. [var: Vulg. Matt. 21:37 *Verebuntur filium meum*, without "perhaps"; the word stands at Luc. 20:13] God uses the speech of one doubting, that out of the ambiguity of the sentence and the suspending of the words [0017C] the free will of man may be shown.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Behold rather,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ According to the tropology. ⟦s3⟧ They who are thought great in the Church, because they loose the yoke and break the bonds, are delivered over to the shame of the passions, that they may do the things which are not fitting. ⟦s4⟧ *Bonds. [n: (HIER.)]* Of the precepts of God, not of the Pharisees, of which it is said: *Let us break their bonds asunder, and let us cast away their yoke from us [n: (Psal. I)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *A leopard watching.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies the onset of Alexander, who, when divers nations had been subdued to him, took up arms against the Medes and Persians like a spotted leopard. ⟦s3⟧ But because he weaves his history not of the future, but of the past, or of things now on the very point of coming, therefore he is silent concerning the Roman empire.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0017D]VERS. 7.-- *I fed them to the full. [n: (HIER.)]* Let him hear this who, riches having been received, serve luxury.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Lovers of horses. [n: (ID.)]* To horses, when they do not see a beast of burden, etc., *as far as He was likened to senseless beasts, and was made like to them [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Psal. 48:13 *Comparatus est jumentis insipientibus*, "he was compared to senseless beasts"]

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And scatter. [n: (ID.)]* Let the Church hear how quickly the walls, etc., *as far as* because of the clemency of the judge, not because of the merit of the sinners.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *They have denied the Lord,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Let the Church, negligent and rejecting the providence of God, hear this: that she shall suffer both the sword and famine, unless she shall have believed the things to come which are spoken.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Behold I give,* etc. [n: (ID.)] That the unbelieving [0018A] may be burned up by thy word, etc., *as far as* if we shall have built upon the foundation of Christ.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Thou shalt not know the tongue,* etc. [n: (ID.)] In the Hebrew: *Thou shalt not understand what he speaketh.* ⟦s2⟧ For it is a solace in evils, if thou hast enemies whom thou art able to entreat, who understand thy prayers.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *And he shall eat up the corn,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He describes the waste of the land, the slaying of many, the driving off of the cattle, the overthrow of cities and of walls, in that all things are taken by the sword of the enemy.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Nevertheless,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] The remnant, namely, shall be saved, either of those who were led into Babylon, or of those left for the tillage of the land of Judaea; or of those who after the heat of persecution, whether by flight or by confession, kept faith with God.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0018B]VERS. 19.-- *But if you shall say. [n: (ID.)]* Tropologically. ⟦s2⟧ This can be taken of the heretics, etc., *as far as* dwell, nay rather serve those whose gods you worship.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Hear, O foolish people.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks properly to Juda and to the house of Jacob. ⟦s3⟧ For Israel had now for a long time been in exile among the Assyrians. ⟦s4⟧ *Who hast no heart.* ⟦s4⟧ He gives to understand that without a commandment we ought naturally to understand the things which are right. ⟦s5⟧ *Who having,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ To which is like: *they have eyes, and shall not see,* etc., *Let them that make them become like unto them, and all such as trust in them [n: (Psal. CXIII)]*.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Your iniquities,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] If ever the sea pass over its bounds, etc., *as far as* to which labourers in the vineyard one and the same reward of eternity is promised.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0018C]VERS. 27.-- *As a snare.* ⟦s2⟧ While they hunt one another to the death, and fill their own houses with the losses and expenses of others, according to that saying of the philosophers, *Every rich man is either unjust, or the heir of one unjust.* ⟦s3⟧ And would that these things were done only by those who are without, and not by us, and that of those who come to us they should look not upon the hands, but upon the faces.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *They are grown fat,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ To which is like: *He grew fat, he grew thick, he grew broad, he forsook God his maker [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And: *The beloved grew fat and kicked [n: (Ibid.)]*. ⟦s4⟧ *And they are grown gross,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Trusting in divine things: as though he should say: *Soul, thou hast many good things laid up for very many years: take thy rest, eat and drink [n: (Luc. XII)]*.
