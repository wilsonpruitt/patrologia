## CHAPTER LI.

VERS. 5.-- *For it was not,* etc. Because the Lord sent them for the vengeance of his people, whom he chastised not by correcting, but crushed by raging: whence, *Woe to Assur, the rod of my fury [n: (Isa. X)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *Flee ye out of the midst,* etc. After the plagues of Babylon he turns his exhortation [0059C] to the Jews historically, to the faithful mystically, that they may flee out of the midst of Babylon, that is, of worldly confusion.

[n: (ORIG.)] Not by degrees, that is to say, not step by step, but swiftly go ye out, whosoever have a confounded soul, etc., *as far as* Likewise, when the sins are lesser, God uses ministers: but when they are graver, then he himself hastens to the retribution.

VERS. 7.-- *A golden cup,* etc. The golden cup of Babylon is not always even in the hand of the Lord; for when it shall have come for vengeance and shall have been placed in the hand of the Lord, there shall be brought about the earth which was made in Job. Nor is it continually held in the hand of the Lord; but then only, when the Lord shall restore to her what she deserves.

*[n: (ORIG.)] A golden cup.* Nabuchodonosor, wishing to deceive men by the cup of Babylon, mingled it not [0059D] in an earthen vessel, etc., *as far as* often have I seen a golden cup in the fair adornment of speech, and, considering the poisons of its doctrines, I have detected the cup of Babylon.

*Making drunk,* etc. [n: (ID.)] See all men drunken; we are made drunk with anger and with sadness: we are made drunk, etc., *as far as* by him are we all made drunk so long as we are earth.

*Of the wine. [n: (ID.)]* As in those who drink bodily wine beyond measure we see the motion of a drunken body, etc., *as far as* the nations drank, therefore are they moved.

VERS. 8.-- *Suddenly is Babylon fallen.* It signifies that the consummation of the world shall be sudden, which shall come to pass not by parts but on a sudden, just as Jericho, falling, perished quickly or suddenly. But if thou come to the advent of Christ, and [0060A] see his great work, how he overthrew all the doctrines of the Gentiles, that he might take believers out from the yoke of error, thou shalt see that in his passion Babylon suddenly fell and was crushed; let each man see that Babylon has fallen in his own breast. For in whose heart she has not fallen, to him Christ has not yet come: Babylon falls, Jerusalem the holy city of God is built.

*Howl ye. [n: (HIER.)]* Because no soul is incurable with God, he gives counsel to those who are able, etc., *as far as* but because she would not be healed, the good physicians do their part and say:

VERS. 9.-- *We have healed. [n: (ORIG.)]* See lest at any time God command the angels to prepare medicines for thy languishing soul, etc., *as far as* God brings forth his judgment to humble that which is exalted.

[0060B]VERS. 16.-- *Who lifteth up the clouds,* that is, preachers and prophets, who defend us from the heat of the judgment to come by the shower of their eloquence, when they set forth the things to come. *Lightnings.* The preachers of the prophets are lightnings, when they strike the enemies: rains, when they wash the devout from sins, and pour in the shower of salvation: whence spiritual fruits may germinate, and grow together into a ripe harvest. *And he bringeth forth the winds out of the treasures.* [var: Vulg. Jer. 51:16 *et produxit ventum de thesauris suis*, "he brought forth the wind out of his treasures"] The winds are the apostles, whose preaching runs through the world like a most swift wind: of whom it is said elsewhere: *He hath made his arrows for them that burn [n: (Psal. VII)]*. For to those same winds wings are attributed because of the swiftness of their coursing; whence: *He flew upon the wings of the winds [n: (Ibid. XI)]*.

VERS. 17.-- *Every man is become a fool.* Rightly after the overthrow of Babylon does he rebuke the makers of idols, [0060C] because through the folly of the human mind works vain and worthy of laughter have been made: because there is neither spirit in them, nor voice, nor any efficacy: whence they can deliver neither themselves nor their worshippers in the day of visitation.

*Every founder is confounded. [n: (RAB.)]* The overthrow of Babylon being shown, the prophet returns to the author of so great a judgment: because there is no power but from God, who rules in the kingdom of men, and will give it to whomsoever he will.

VERS. 25.-- *Behold, I,* etc. The destroying mountain, Nabuchodonosor, and the kingdom of the Chaldeans; or the devil, who elsewhere is called a dark mountain, because of the darkness of error. *And I will make thee.* To this mountain belong the Jews, heretics, blasphemers, and all the impious, who persevere in malice, and are not converted that they may become living stones in the building of God.

[0060D]VERS. 30.-- *They have ceased,* etc. When Cyrus and Darius came, the strength of Babylon and of her princes ceased.

VERS. 33.-- *The daughter of Babylon,* etc. There was a people of Babylon, when through the power of her kingdom and the abundance of her riches she threshed and ground down the nations: but the time of her harvest came, when she is compelled to receive what she has done, of whom it is consequently said in the person of the Jewish people: *He hath eaten me, and devoured me, Nabuchodonosor, king of Babylon.*

*And I will make the sea a desert,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Mystically the sea signifies the turbulence of this world, which the Lord sets as a desert. For he shall make its riot to cease, and the wicked shall be scattered, who were slaves to the confusion of errors and of vices.

[0061A]VERS. 41.-- *How is Sesach taken. [n: (RAB.)]* Sesach is interpreted *the fine linen of sackcloth,* or *the hand of sackcloth:* as Babylon, confusion, etc., *as far as* that they may go out from their fellowship, lest they perish with them.

VERS. 45.-- *Go ye out of the midst of her. [n: (ID.)]* He who wishes to make his soul safe, etc., *as far as* but strenuous in the meditation of his law and in the exercise of the virtues.

VERS. 52.-- *Therefore,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He enumerates the plagues of Babylon that shall come upon her, and upon her nobles, her leaders and magistrates, and all her people, etc., *as far as Go out from her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues [n: (Apoc. XVIII)]*, etc.

VERS. 64.-- *So shall Babylon be sunk.* For according to [0061B] the history Babylon was sunk [cj: *mersa*; read *non mersa*, "was not sunk"] in the Euphrates, although reduced to a wilderness. But he foretells the everlasting destruction of the spiritual Babylon, which shall be plunged into the depth of hell with the devil her prince. Whence John: *A strong angel took up a stone, as it were a great millstone, and cast it into the sea, saying: With this violence shall Babylon be cast [n: (Apoc. XVIII)]*.
