## CHAPTER LII.

VERS. 1.-- *The son of twenty.* The history which relates that Sedecias was taken, and Jerusalem laid waste by the Chaldeans, is contained entire and in full in the book of Kings.

VERS. 4.-- *And it came to pass.* The number nine, less than ten, signifies the imperfection of the law, etc., *as far as* hedged about by the darkness of sins they flee, and, seeing the wolf coming, they forsake the sheep.

[0061C]VERS. 7.-- *By the way of the gate.* Covertly he reproves the sloth of the teachers, who, set between the walls of the two Testaments, seek not to wage war but to escape, and rather to abound in delights (which the garden signifies) than to withstand the enemy with the shield of faith.

VERS. 8.-- *He pursued,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In the book of Kings it is thus read: *Sedecias fled by the way,* etc. When malignant spirits surround the people, the ruler does not flee, etc., *as far as* Having laid hold of the king therefore, they led him to the king of Babylon into Reblatha, etc.

VERS. 11.-- *And the king brought him,* etc. Because the soul, bound in the confusion of sins by the fetters of vices, is led by malignant spirits into the house of the prison unto the day of death, and is shut up in the straitness of mind, [0061D] that it may await the sentence of death.

VERS. 12.-- *And in the fifth month,* etc. The order of the time agrees with the reason of the vengeance: in the fifth month is the city laid waste, which despised the Pentateuch, on the tenth day of the month, because it did things contrary to the decalogue of the law, flattering itself with security on account of its dwelling in the holy places; whence above: *Trust ye not,* etc.

[n: (GREG.)] While the Scripture [sic: *Scripturae*] relates the captivity of Sedecias, [0062A] it announces the order of the inward captivity, etc., *as far as* For sometimes even the light of the whole reason is closed up, because it is weighed down by evil use and by the multitude of its own iniquity [sic: *iniquitates*].

*Nabuzardan came,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] By the just judgment of God came Nabuzardan, prince of the army of the king of Babylon, upon Jerusalem, and set it on fire, etc., *as far as* he burns every house with fire, that is, the conscience of each man with the flame of unlawful love.

*Of the poor,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Those who could be useful by word and by example, etc., *as far as* but thorns and thistles of vices.

VERS. 20.-- *The pillars also,* etc. By the divers vessels are signified divers offices in the Church, which the malignant spirits take away from the service of God, and turn to their own [0062B] use.

VERS. 21.-- *Of the pillars,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In that he describes the fashioning of the pillars with the capitals, and the nets and the pomegranates, he shows, etc., *as far as* let him read our work upon the book of Kings, where what the holy Fathers thought concerning these things has been explained.

VERS. 24.-- *And the master took,* etc. The devil deceives and takes captive even the strong in the Church; whence: *His meats are choice [n: (Habac. I)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. Habac. 1:16 *cibus ejus electus*, "his food is choice"]

VERS. 25.-- *He took out of the city,* etc. The continent, that is to say, not having in their breast the oil of grace and of mercy (like the foolish virgins), who are set in vain over men of war, since they take no forethought to shun arrogance.

VERS. 26.-- *Seven men,* etc. By the sevenfold Spirit, [0062C] that is to say, sanctified. But because they lost grace, being delivered over to seven other spirits more wicked, they look not upon the commandments of the heavenly king, but obey the will of the king of hell. *And the scribe,* etc. Whom the book of Kings calls Sophar, who is interpreted *scattering* or *dividing,* and he signifies those who scatter by depraved examples the untaught in the Church, whom they ought to have nourished unto the warfare of Christ, and who sequester them from the assembly of the faithful through error.

*And sixty men,* etc. Fools, that is to say, fellow workers, who are deservedly called by the name of the common crowd, who neglected to discern and to fulfil the profit of the divine counsel.

VERS. 27.-- *And the king of Babylon struck them,* etc. This the king of Babylon compasses, that those whom he plucks out of the Church he may bring through to the slaying of everlasting death.

[0062D]VERS. 28.-- *This is the people,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Three times did Nabuchodonosor lay waste Judaea. First, with Joachim, etc., *as far as* and he thrusts them back into the prison of hell, where the damned groan for ever.

VERS. 31.-- *And it came to pass. [n: (ID.)]* It signifies the wondrous power of God through good and evil alike, his own, etc., *as far as* him corrected he mercifully regarded, and, freeing him from punishments, restored him to rest and to honour.
