## §1 · CONVENTIONS — INHERITED, NOT RE-DERIVED

Ruth (8968) `cruces.md` §1 governs and was not reopened: `VERS. n.--` passes through verbatim;
the lemma is Englished; a fragmentary lemma is rendered as the phrase it stands for; the
attribution sigla arrive as ordinary `[n: …]` notes with contents unanglicized. `CAPUT VI.` →
`CHAPTER VI.`, the Roman numeral kept.

**`*usque ad*` → `*as far as*`**, in its own italic span, per 8978 and Tobit (8970) §1(b),
**and no verb is ever supplied to complete the abridgment.** Sites where the resumption tail
lands mid-clause and reads broken are listed in §4 and are the formula working, not defects.

⚠ **`usque ad` LOOK-ALIKES — decided per occurrence (brief addendum D).** Six occurrences in
this batch are **not** the abridgment formula and are rendered as ordinary words:

| column | printed | reading taken |
|---|---|---|
| 0019A | `VERS. 9.-- *Usque ad racemum.*` | the **lemma's own words**, Jr 6:9 — "Even to the cluster." |
| 0022A | `*a minimo usque ad maximum omnes avaritiae student*` | **inside a scripture quotation** — "from the least even to the greatest" |
| 0024B | `Hic error usque ad nos pervenit` | ordinary prose — "has reached even to us" |
| 0025D | `*Vobiscum sum usque ad consummationem saeculi*` | **inside Matt 28:20** — "even to the consummation" |
| 0023C | `qui possunt in sublime ascendere usque ad simplices` | ordinary prose inside a resumption tail — "even to the simple" |
| 0027D | `cum usque hodie sint leones in sylva` | ordinary prose — "even to this day" |

The first and the fourth are the dangerous shape (a formula-looking pair standing inside a
lemma or a proof-text); the Sap 8:1 trap named in addendum D is exactly this class.

## §2 · THE 7a″ DIVERGENCE LIST — every lemma in bands 0018D–0031A

Collated against `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` verse by verse (Jr 6–14 pulled entire,
plus every non-Jeremiah proof-text). ⛔ **A `[var:]` is a public claim about Migne's printed
page and our Latin is not that page** (CLAUDE.md 8a; `data/plate-reads.json` records **no read
on any column of 9006**). The policy adopted, and applied uniformly:

> **Fire `[var:]` only where the divergence is (a) multi-word or a whole distinct word — so
> that no one-letter digitization slip could have manufactured it — AND (b) changes what the
> sentence asserts. Everything else is logged here and fires nothing.**

That is the ship test applied to Pattern 14 rather than to `[sic:]`, and it is why six markers
stand where twenty-odd divergences are recorded.

### 2a · THE SEVEN `[var:]` FIRED

1. **0019D · Isa 40:9** — Migne prints *In montem excelsum ascende tu qui evangelizas Sion,
   exalta **sicut tuba** vocem tuam, qui **annuntias** Jerusalem.* The Clementine has *Super
   montem excelsum ascende, tu qui evangelizas Sion ; exalta **in fortitudine** vocem tuam, qui
   **evangelizas** Jerusalem.* Three separate substitutions, and the trumpet is drawn in from
   Isa 58:1 (*quasi tuba exalta vocem tuam*). **Material to the gloss**, which is expounding
   *Vocem tubae* — the proof-text was chosen for a word the Clementine does not have there.
2. **0022B · Jr 6:13 / Jr 8:10** — Migne quotes *a minimo usque ad maximum omnes **avaritiae
   student***, citing Jer. VI. Jr 6:13 reads *a minore quippe usque ad majorem omnes avaritiae
   student*; Jr 8:10 — the verse actually under gloss — reads *a minimo usque ad maximum omnes
   **avaritiam sequuntur***. The quotation is a **cross-contamination of the two near-identical
   Jeremiah verses**, taking the opening of one and the predicate of the other. Both halves
   multi-word.
3. **0024D · Isa 1:2** — Migne prints ***Audi, coelum,** et auribus percipe, terra, **quia**
   Dominus locutus est.* The Clementine has ***Audite, caeli,** … **quoniam** Dominus locutus
   est.* Singular addressee for plural, two words changed. Fired because the addressee's number
   is the thing the sentence does.
4. **0026A · Gn 49:8** — Migne prints *manus **tuae super dorsum** inimicorum tuorum*; the
   Clementine has *manus **tua in cervicibus** inimicorum tuorum.* A different image (hands on
   the back vs. hand on the necks), three words.
5. **0027A · Mt 23:8–9** — Migne prints one sentence, *Nolite vocari magistri **super terram**,
   unus quippe est **magister** vester **qui in coelis est***, which exists in neither verse:
   23:8 gives *nolite vocari Rabbi : unus est enim magister vester*, and *super terram* +
   *qui in caelis est* belong to 23:9, which is about **fathers**, not masters. A conflation
   that makes a sentence of its own.
6. **0029B · Dn 12:2** — Migne prints ***Isti** resurgent in vitam aeternam, et **illi** in
   opprobrium et **confusionem sempiternam***; the Clementine has *alii in vitam aeternam, et
   alii in opprobrium **ut videant semper***. The closing clause is different matter, not a
   different word.
7. **0030D · Ps 88:45** — Migne prints ***Thronum** ejus in terra **allisisti***; the Clementine
   has ***sedem** ejus in terram **collisisti***. Two content words, and *Thronum* is the word
   the lemma *Solii gloriae* is being matched to.

(0024D) · 0008 ×2 (0026A, 0027A) · 0009 ×2 (0029B, 0030D)** — seven markers, one per site

### 2d · TWO FRAGMENT-RENDERING RULINGS

**(i) The `Nunquid` fragments are NOT expanded — negation fidelity outranks convention 3.**
Migne prints `VERS. 9.-- *Nunquid super his*` (Jr 9:9) and `*Nunquid carnes sanctae.*`
(Jr 11:15). Convention 3 would render each as the phrase it opens — but Jr 9:9 continues
***non** visitabo*, so "Shall I **not** visit for these things" would put on the page a `non`
Migne does not print. **The rule that every negative in the English answers to one on the page
is not suspended by a lemma convention.** Both are therefore rendered from the printed words
alone and identically as a class: **"Shall it be for these things"** and **"Shall the holy
flesh."** The stumpiness is the plate's.

**(ii) Where convention 3 DID expand, and why each is safe.** *Sicut frigidam.* → "As a cistern
maketh her water cold" (Jr 6:7, and the gloss is entirely about coldness) · *Ut quid mihi thus.*
→ "To what purpose do ye bring me frankincense" · *Nemo quod bonum,* → "No man speaketh that
which is good" · *Quia ascendit.* → "For death is come up" (Jr 9:21) · *Si mutare potest,* →
"If the Ethiopian can change" · *Nunquid projiciens,* → "Hast thou utterly cast off" ·
*Nunquid avis discolor?* → "Is my inheritance a speckled bird?", where the Septuagint reading
Migne prints beside it carries the same full clause and the contrast is unreadable without it.
**None of these imports a negative, and none imports a word the verse's own Clementine text
does not have.** Complete one-word lemmata (*Speciosae.* · *Pastores.* · *Nescierunt.* ·
*Gladium.* · *Lignum.* · *Grex.* · *Tabernaculum.* · *Vicinos.* · *Nudavi.* · *Agricolae.*)
are rendered as they stand — a fragment and a complete one-word lemma are **different classes**
and are not to be contrasted.

⚠ *Tabernaculum.* (Jr 10:20 *Tabernaculum meum*) is rendered **"The tabernacle,"** not "My
tabernacle": same reasoning as *Tonde capillum* above.

## §4 · THIS BATCH'S CRUCES — sense, subject, and negation

### 4a · ⛔ THE ONE SITE WHERE THE PRINTED NEGATIVE MAKES THE SENTENCE CONTRADICT ITSELF

**0028C · Jr 13:5, resumption tail:** *…usque ad si **non** adhaerens Domino, uno spiritu fuerit
in Christo.*

Englished as printed: **"if, not cleaving to the Lord, he shall have been one spirit in
Christ."** The allusion is 1 Cor 6:17, *qui adhaeret Domino unus spiritus est* — so the clause
as printed says the opposite of the doctrine it is built on. The *non* attaches to *adhaerens*
and to nothing else (it stands immediately before the participle, and *fuerit* has its own
predicate in *uno spiritu*). **It is carried.** Per Pattern 7a and the standing rule, a word is
never translated as its opposite to rescue a sentence, and the tail is a resumption whose head
the abridgment removed — the missing head may well be a negative of its own (*nemo …*, *nisi
…*), in which case the sentence is sound and only looks broken. **No conjecture is offered and
no marker fires**, because both candidates (a lost governing negative; a plate slip for
*adhaerens*) require the head of Jerome's sentence, which is exactly what `usque ad` deleted.

### 4b · UNEXPRESSED SUBJECTS — every "he/she/it/they" I supplied, and what fixed it

- **0021C · *offerre debuisset*** → "ought **he** to have offered it": the subject is the
  **master**, lifted from *primum occurrisset **domino*** in the same clause. The rival (the
  animal) is impossible — an animal does not offer.
- **0021C · *Quando recessit… duxit Sedeciam*** → "When **he** departed from fealty to his
  lord": **Sedecias**, named four clauses later as the object of *duxit*. The *dominus* he
  departs from is Nabuchodonosor, which is why *Nabuchodonosor illum fugavit* follows.
- **0022A · *Unde ipse:*** before Ps 11:2 → "Whence **he himself**." ⚠ **Two readings both
  construe** and I have not chosen silently: (a) the Psalmist, since the preceding quotation is
  Ps 13:3 and this is another psalm; (b) the Saviour, since the sentence opens *In tempore
  Salvatoris.* The English "he himself" is deliberately as underdetermined as the Latin.
- **0024C · *dum coeli sibi colorem ostendunt*** → "while **they** display upon themselves the
  colour of heaven": the heretics, from *falsitas involvitur* and the *haeretici* of VERS. 7 two
  paragraphs above. The abridgment removed the nearer antecedent.
- **0025A · *Quod ordine suo fingit*** → "That which **he** fashions in his own order": the
  **artifex** of Jr 10:14 (*confusus est artifex omnis in sculptili*), the verse being glossed.
- **0026C · *in Septuaginta non habentur*** → "**they** are not found in the Septuagint": the
  words of the verse from *Quia contestans* down to *ut facerent haec*, which is what the
  sentence has just delimited.
- **0027B · *sermo Salvatoris in eam descendit*** → "descends into **it**": feminine, and the
  antecedent (the bitter water of Mara, Ex 15:25, with the *lignum* thrown into it) lies in the
  part of the sentence `usque ad` removed. Rendered neutrally rather than named.
- **0030D · *Tunc alliditur… quia culpa perditur… sustentatur*** → "Then is **it** dashed
  down… because **it** is lost by fault, **it** is upheld": the subject throughout is the
  *solium* / *templum* of the lemma, which is what *in quo… Deus offenditur* requires.
- **0026D · *Dicamus hoc principibus… putant se Dei mereri clementiam*** → "who… **think** that
  they merit": the princes and the rich, from the dative just before; *putant* is indicative
  against the subjunctives of the *cum* clause and so belongs to the relative, not to *dicamus*.

### 4c · NEGATION HOSTS — the sites in brief-test 2a's trigger population

Every printed negative in the batch was checked, and its host named from **structure** rather
than from what reads well. The ones with a quantifier, correlative, or pivot beside a verb:

- **0019D · *non solum non cavere, sed nec intelligere velle peccata, et nullam habere
  distantiam*** — three negatives, three hosts: `non` on *solum*; `non` on *cavere*; `nec` on
  *velle*; `nullam` on *distantiam*. The English carries each on its own word, and the object
  *peccata* is carried through to both infinitives ("not to beware of sins… not even to be
  willing to understand them") — the fourth question the brief names, which 7a⁗'s three do not

- **0019A · *non temporis esse victoriam, sed virtutum*** — host is `temporis`, proved by the

- **0025B · *Non enim Deus, sicut prius, longe post futura minatus, sed statim futura*** —
  ⭐ the sharpest case in the batch. The `Non` stands before the subject and the participle, and
