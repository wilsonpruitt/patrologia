## §2 · CRUCES — the printed text, where it is odd

### 2.1 · 0047A · *sedet* for *sed et* — the one `[sic:]`, and why it fired

Migne prints *Non solum verba, **sedet** opera prophetarum, sunt nobis in exempla virtutum.*
*sedet* ("he sits") is a real word with **no grammatical slot** in a clause whose verb is *sunt*
and whose skeleton is the correlative *non solum … sed et*. That is Pattern 12's first test
(11632's four markers on real words were ruled to keep it), and *sedet* for *sed et* is one of
the fused runs Pattern 10 itself names.

⚑ **7a⁗ applied:** the marker does **not** carry the force. The English reads *Not only the
words, but also [sic: *sedet*] the works…* — the correlative's second member is in plain
English beside the marker, so stripping every marker leaves a readable sentence.

⚠ **Provenance is the open question and I am not asserting it.** Addendum A's ten-for-ten split
concerns *non*-words; a **fused** run is a well-formed-looking token that no mechanical test
sees (7a⁗-b), and the withdrawn-`[sic:]` class of Addendum B was digitization damage blamed on
Migne. **Worth a plate read** (PL 114 col. 47, first line of the CAPUT XXXII gloss). If the
plate prints *sed et*, this marker comes out and the fix is a `data/tei-patches/9006.json`
entry.

✅ **READ, AND THE MARKER STANDS.** Plate **p. 29**, 2026-08-29: Migne prints *sedet* fused, and at
**6×** the flanking word-spaces (*verba* | *sedet* | *opera*) are **visibly wider than any
inter-letter gap inside the run** — which is the measurement that decides a fusion, and the reason
the open question above is now closed rather than merely unrefuted. `data/plate-reads.json`
`works.9006` records it as the finding of the p. 29 read. ⚠ **The same page then produced three
further `[sic:]` that nobody had suspected** (§3): reading the whole line, not the token, is what
turned them up.

### 2.2 · 0046D · *et sic vestimentum veterascent* — will not construe. `[cj:]` **DECLINED, THEN FIRED**

*Si coeli peribunt, et sic vestimentum veterascent…* The subject of *veterascent* is *coeli*
(plural), so *vestimentum* (neuter singular) has no slot; the sentence wants *sicut vestimentum*
(cf. Ps 101:27 *omnes sicut vestimentum veterascent*), which is what the argument requires.

Rendered literally — "and so shall grow old a garment" — and **no `[cj:]`**: the two conjectures
weighed were *sicut* (near-certain, from the psalm the clause echoes) and reading *vestimentum*
as a stranded nominative (which fails on number). *sicut* wins on evidence, but Pattern 18a's
bar is that the printed word makes the English **assert something false**, and an incoherent
clause was ruled to pass that bar only for a negation or a correlative *whose answering member
is printed*. Nothing answers *sic* here. **Declined, with the conjecture recorded.**

⛔ **REVERSED, 2026-08-29 — and this heading said DECLINED while the marker was in the file.**
`0015.md` reads: *"If the heavens shall perish, and shall grow old so, a garment `[cj: *sic*; read
*sicut*, "as a garment"]`, how can the seed of Israel be everlasting?"* The `[cj:]` **is fired**;
the count in the header is corrected with it.

**Why the decline was reversed.** The reading above declined on a reading of 18a's bar as
*negation-or-answered-correlative only*. That is too narrow for this site. *sic* here is not an
unanswered correlative — it is a **word doing a job it cannot do**: the English forced to render it
literally must write *"shall grow old **so**, a garment,"* which asserts an apposition Migne's
sentence does not make and leaves *vestimentum* dangling as though it were a second subject of
*veterascent*. That is 18a's actual test — **the printed word makes the English assert something
false** — and it is met. The conjecture is not mine either: *sicut vestimentum veterascent* is the
clause of Ps 101:27 that this sentence is quoting, so the correction is the psalm's own words.

renders *sic* as printed ("so"), the dangling *a garment* is still visibly dangling, and the

### 2.3 · 0054C · *resecandum gladium verbi Dei* — a stranded accusative. `[cj:]` **DECLINED, THEN FIRED**

*fundendum est quidquid in membris nostris carnale et terrenum inolevit et resecandum gladium
verbi Dei.* Both gerundives agree with *quidquid*; *gladium* is accusative where the sense
demands the ablative of means *gladio* ("cut away **by** the sword of the word of God"), which
the preceding sentence supplies in so many words (*Maledictus ergo qui prohibet gladium… a
sanguine*) and Gregory's note repeats.

Rendered as printed and left visibly broken: "must be poured out and cut away the sword of the
word of God." **Declined** for the same reason as 2.2: a case slip is not a negation or a
correlative, and 18a's ruling is expressly scoped. Writing "by the sword" would have been the
silent repair Pattern 7a exists to forbid.

⛔ **REVERSED, 2026-08-29 — and this heading said DECLINED while the marker was in the file.**
`0019.md` reads: *"…must be poured out and cut away the sword `[cj: *gladium*; read *gladio*, "with
the sword"]` of the word of God."* The `[cj:]` **is fired**; the count in the header is corrected

### 2.8 · 0050C · *Nequaquam pro se orare postulavit* — two readings, and neither was chosen silently

Jer 37:3 has Sedecias sending to Jeremias *Ora pro nobis*, so he plainly did ask. The negation's
host is therefore genuinely undecided between:
(a) *pro se* — "he by no means asked [him to pray] **for himself**", i.e. he asked for the
people, not for himself; and
(b) *postulavit* — "he by no means asked **to pray** for himself", i.e. he sought another's
prayer because he would not pray himself, which is what *quia divinis mandatis obtemperare
noluit* and the Prov 28:9 proof-text about a man whose own prayer is abominable both support.
**Both construe.** The English keeps Migne's word order and the fronted *Nequaquam* so that the
scope stays where the Latin leaves it: "By no means did he ask to pray for himself, because he
would not obey the divine commandments." Reading (b) is the better-supported one and is the one
an English reader will take; (a) is recorded here rather than adjudicated in the text.
