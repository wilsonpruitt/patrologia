## §2 · THE NUMBERS AND MEASURES OF CAPUT LII — audited one by one, **none conformed**

The launch flagged this as the class that gets silently squared with a familiar English Bible.
Every number that reaches our English, and the one place a number does not:

- **0061B `VERS. 1.-- *Filius viginti.*`** — the Clementine's Jr 52:1 reads *Filius viginti **et
  unius anni** erat Sedecias*. Migne's lemma is **clipped in the middle of the number**, and I have
  rendered it clipped: **"The son of twenty."** ⛔ This is a deliberate exception to convention 3's
  expansion of fragments, and the boundary is worth stating because I applied expansion four times
  elsewhere in the batch (§3): **a fragment is expanded from the verse it opens, except where the
  elided material is a NUMBER or a MEASURE.** Supplying "and one years" would put a figure on our
  page that Migne's plate does not carry, in exactly the class the launch named. The full reading is
  here; the Latin stands opposite.
- **0061B VERS. 4** — *Novenarius minor denario* — nine and ten, the gloss's own arithmetic on Jr
  52:4's *anno nono … mense decimo*. Rendered as printed.
- **0061C VERS. 12** — Migne's prose reads *decima **die** mensis*; Jr 52:12 has *decima mensis*.
  This is the glossator's own sentence, not a lemma; rendered as printed, no marker.
- **0062A VERS. 21** — Migne's abridgment (*Quod facturam columnarum describit … usque ad*) cuts
  **before** the measurements of Jr 52:21–23 (eighteen cubits, a line of twelve cubits, four
  fingers' thickness, five-cubit capitals). **No measurement reaches our English at all** — there
  was nothing here to conform, and nothing was supplied to fill the gap.
- **0062A VERS. 26 *Septem viros*** — Jeremiah has **septem**; the parallel at 4 Rg 25:19 has
  ***quinque***. Migne follows Jeremiah, and the gloss is built on the seven (*Septiformi Spiritu …
  septem spiritibus nequioribus*). Checked and left; the Kings variant is **not** a divergence in

- **0062C *Et sexaginta viros*** — sixty in both Jr 52:25 and 4 Rg 25:19.
- **0062D VERS. 28 *Tertio vastavit Nabuchodonosor Judaeam*** — a third time; agrees with the three

### (a) 0056A · ἄρκος, where the sense requires Ἄρης

> *Metropolis Moabitarum, quae nunc Ariopolis dicitur, composito nomine ex Hebraeo et Graeco, quod
> **ἄρκος**, id est, Martis civitas vocetur.*

The etymology is Hebrew *Ar* + Greek *πόλις*, and the gloss glosses the Greek element as *Martis

that reads oddly is Migne's until a plate says otherwise), it is the **only Greek in the entire
work** — `grep` returns one hit in all of `src/latin/` — so the corpus offers no control whatever,
and a `[cj:]` here would be a public claim about a Greek accidental in a Latin plate on the strength
of an argument from sense alone. **Requested: a plate read of col. 0056A.** If the plate reads
Ἄρης, the fix is a `data/tei-patches/9006.json` entry, not an emendation of the English.

> *…et habitant per solitudinem totam, de quibus poeta dicit: **Lateque vagantes Agi.***

## §6 · READINGS, PARSES AND UNEXPRESSED SUBJECTS

### 6a. Two readings that both construe — named rather than chosen silently

**0059C · *Non est semper calix aureus Babylonis vel in manu Domini.*** Two parses:
(i) *semper … in manu Domini* is one predicate and *vel* is "even" — "The golden cup of Babylon is
not always **even** in the hand of the Lord"; (ii) two predicates — "It is not always a golden cup
of Babylon, **or** in the hand of the Lord." **(i) was taken**, and the reason is the sentence's own
structure rather than what reads well: the two clauses that follow are both, and only, about the
Lord's hand — *cum … in manu Domini posita fuerit* and *Nec jugiter in manu Domini continetur; sed
tunc tantum*. **(ii) loses** because nothing in the paragraph ever questions that Babylon is a
golden cup. The `sed tunc tantum` correlative also fixes the host of both negatives on the temporal
adverbs (*semper*, *jugiter*), not on *est*/*continetur* — the 2a trigger fired here and the
structure, not the reading, settled it.

**0056B · *quae consurgit adversus spiritum, ut non faciamus ea quae sunt spiritus, et animam, etc.***
*et animam* can hang either on *adversus* ("rises up against the spirit … and the soul") or inside
the *ut* clause. The intervening purpose clause makes both possible and the abridgment's `etc.` cuts
before it is resolved. **Rendered in the printed order, preserving the ambiguity** — "which rises up
against the spirit, that we may not do the things which are of the spirit, and the soul, etc."
Neither reading was adopted in the English, because the Latin does not adopt one either.

### 6b. A construction that will not construe, and was not repaired

**0056C** — *Sed Dominus e contra omnia secreta eorum, et patriarcharum Esau (qui primas scilicet
haereses invenerunt) **viros ecclesiasticos et doctores** profert in medium.* Two accusative groups
stand asyndetically before one verb. Rendered with the asyndeton intact ("…brings forth into the
midst all their secret things, and those of the patriarchs of Esau (…), churchmen and teachers"),
which is what the printed words allow. The obvious conjecture is ***per** viros ecclesiasticos et
doctores* — the Lord publishing the heretics' secrets *through* churchmen and teachers — and it is
**not adopted**: nothing is broken here, there is no `[cj:]` case because no single printed word is
misleading, and supplying a preposition would be exactly the silent smoothing Pattern 7 forbids.

### 6c. A printed negative kept although the argument seems to want the opposite

**0061C · *Malignis spiritibus populum circumdantibus, **rector non fugit**, etc., usque ad…*** The
surrounding gloss censures shepherds who run (0061C's own *non bellum gerere, sed effugere quaerunt*;
0061B's *videntes lupum venientem oves dimittunt*), so the sentence reads against its neighbours.
**The `non` is carried** — "the ruler does not flee" — with no repair and no marker. Either Migne's
sentence states the good ruler's contrasting case and the abridgment has cut the contrast away, or
the plate has lost a word; **neither can be settled from this file**, and translating *fugit* as its
opposite to rescue the paragraph is the precise failure 7a exists to forbid.

  place I could not name the subject. Rom 8:7's own subject is *prudentia carnis*; the run resumes

- **0062D** *correctum misericorditer respexit* → "**him** corrected **he** mercifully regarded" =

### 6e. Ellipsis where English cannot leave the verb out

**0062C** — ***Quem liber Regum Sophar***, *qui interpretatur dissipans vel dividens, et significat…*
The naming verb is elided in the Latin (a gapping English has no way to reproduce), and *et
significat* takes *Sophar* as its subject. Rendered "Whom the book of Kings **calls** Sophar."
Ordinary gapping, not an emendation — but recorded, because a reader comparing the columns will find
no Latin word answering to "calls".

⚠ In the same sentence Migne prints ***Sophar*** where the Clementine's 4 Rg 25:19 has ***Sopher***.
This is a name in the glossator's own prose, not a quoted lemma, and the two are the same Hebrew name;
**no `[var:]`**, logged here.

### 6f. Passages read closely and left exactly as printed

- **0059C** *efficietur terra quae facta est in Job* — obscure, and no candidate in Job is close
  enough to name. Rendered literally ("there shall be brought about the earth which was made in
  Job"). Real words throughout; no marker.
- **0060B** *Praedicatores **prophetarum** fulgura sunt* — the genitive is odd where the parallel
  wants *praedicatores* and *prophetae* in apposition (as at 0060B's own *praedicatores et

- **0062D** *per bonos et malos aeque **sua**, etc.* — *sua* is left without its noun by the

### 11c · `0061B` (chunk 0022) · `[cj: *mersa*; read *non mersa*]` — Pattern 18a's own case

> *Sic submergetur Babylon.* Nam secundum [0061B] historiam Babylon in Euphraten **mersa** est,
> quamvis in solitudinem redacta.

**English:** *"For according to the history Babylon was sunk `[cj: *mersa*; read *non mersa*, "was
not sunk"]` in the Euphrates, although reduced to a wilderness."*

⛔ **This marker is fired and had no entry anywhere in this file until now; the header's `0 [cj:]`
is corrected with it.**

**Why it fires where §6b and §6c decline.** Pattern 18a's bar is that the printed word makes the
English **assert something false**, and here it does, twice over:

1. **Migne's own sentence refutes it.** *quamvis in solitudinem redacta* — "**although** reduced to
   a wilderness" — is a concessive, and a concessive needs something to concede *against*. If
   Babylon was drowned in the Euphrates, being reduced to a wilderness concedes nothing; the
   sentence only works if the literal city was **not** sunk and was merely laid waste.
2. **The argument the gloss is making requires it.** The whole point is the contrast with the next
   sentence, *Sed spiritualis Babylonis aeternum interitum praedicit, quae in profundum inferni …
   demergetur* — the **spiritual** Babylon is the one that is sunk. The *Sed* is the pivot, and it
   pivots on a negative that the printed clause has lost.

⚠ **Distinguish this from §6c, which declines on the same page-worth of text.** At 0061C a printed
*non* reads against its neighbours and **is carried with no marker**, because there the abridgment
has plainly cut the contrast away and the missing material is exactly what would settle it. Here
nothing is abridged: the concessive and the answering *Sed* are both **printed, in full, in the
same two sentences**, so the answering member Pattern 18a asks for is on the page. That is the
difference, and it is why one site takes a marker and the other does not.

⚑ **The `[cj:]` proposes; it does not repair.** The English still reads *"Babylon was sunk"*, as
printed. Nothing was silently emended, and the plate gate does not read `[cj:]` — a conjecture is a
claim about sense, not about type, and makes no assertion about what the plate prints.
