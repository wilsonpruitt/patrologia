## HERE BEGINS THE BOOK (cont.)

Before, O imperial ornament, I stretch out my hand to write to the pipes of the spiritual harmony — that is, to the concord of the allegories — the Holy Spirit breathing; and before, by the office of the pen, I touch the depths of contemplation by spreading the sails of the history, and unbar as with a key the hidden things of the house, whereby the inner mysteries may more easily be revealed: I have determined to show, in the argumentation [0555B] of the faith, why this book is placed third among the little works of Solomon, and what significance it bears, or of what life it is asserted to hold the type; and likewise by what names holy Church is inscribed in this same book; why too the same volume is called not simply the Song, but the Song of Songs; and finally by what names Solomon is marked out in Holy Scripture — so that, all things being tasted beforehand, when they have been known in advance, the exposition may afterward be grasped more easily and more eagerly. For the Scriptures teach most plainly that Solomon was called by three names. *Solomon,* that is, *peaceable,* because he obtained the kingdom in peace and instructed the people by parables, as a signification [0555C] of the Savior, who says to his disciples: « Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you [n: (Joan. XIV)]. » And he himself taught the crowds by parables, of whom also it is written: « His empire shall be multiplied, and of peace there shall be no end [n: (Isa. IX)] » and the rest. And *Idida,* that is, *beloved of the Lord,* namely because he was first beloved by the Lord, likewise as a type of the Lord who is beloved by the Father, as he himself says: « This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased [n: (Matth. III)]. » And *Coheleth,* that is, *Ecclesiastes.* Now he is called Ecclesiastes in the Greek tongue, and by that, because he gathers the assembly, that is, the Church — whom we may name *the preacher,* because he speaks to the people, and his discourse is directed not specially to one, but generally to all; who as with hand outstretched quiets the tumults of all [0555D] and calls them back to one judgment. So the same Solomon is shown to have done in this book, when at the end of the book he says: « Let us all alike hear the end of speaking: Fear God, and keep his commandments; for this is all man [n: (Eccli. XIII)], » prefiguring him who not only brought in peace and stood forth beloved of God the Father, but who says to all: « Do penance: for the kingdom of heaven will draw near [n: (Matth. III)] » [var: Vulg. *appropinquavit*, "hath drawn near"] — who, « the middle wall being destroyed, » and emptying out the enmities in his own flesh, made both one, and who commands us to fear God and to keep his commandments. Finally, as Solomon was called by the three aforesaid names, so according to the number of the appellations he published three volumes: namely *Proverbs,* which we name *Parables,* which the Hebrews call *Masloth;* [0556A] *Ecclesiastes,* which they also, as we have said, name *Coheleth;* and the *Song of Songs,* which they entitle *Schir aschirim.* And these he distinguished in a reasonable order. For in Proverbs he teaches the little one, and instructs him as it were concerning duties by maxims; whence also his discourse is often found addressed to a son. But in Ecclesiastes [0556B] he instructs a man of ripe age, that he by no means believe anything among the things of the world to be perpetual, but that all things we behold are falling and brief. Lastly, in the Song of Songs he joins in embraces the man now made perfect and prepared, the world being trodden underfoot. For unless we have first left our vices, and, renouncing the pomps of the world, have made ourselves unencumbered for the coming of Christ, we shall not be able to sing the Song of Songs, and to say: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Or he published the volumes thus, in which he set forth the disciplines of three kinds, by which one arrives at the knowledge of things. For in the first the moral, in the second the natural, and in the last of all the contemplative — which the Greeks have named thus, *ethnicam, physicam,* and *theoriam.* Whence it is not repugnant to the understanding that [0556C] this book is placed in the third place among the little works of Solomon. So then in Proverbs the moral life is expressed, where it is said: « Hear, my son, my wisdom, and incline thine ear to my prudence [n: (Prov. I)]. » [var: Vulg. Prov. V, 1 *Fili mi, attende ad sapientiam meam*, "My son, attend to my wisdom"] But in Ecclesiastes, the natural. For there it is considered that all things tend toward faith, when it is said: « Vanity of vanities, and all is vanity [n: (Eccli. I)]. » But in the Song of Songs the contemplative life is expressed, while in it the coming and the sight of the Lord himself is desired, when it is said with the voice of the bride: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » These orders also the lives of the three patriarchs signify, namely *Abraham, Isaac,* and *Jacob.* For Abraham held morality by obeying, when he went out from his own land, and did not delay [0556D] to sacrifice his son. But Isaac figured the natural life by digging wells; for indeed to dig wells is, by natural consideration, to search out by scrutiny all the things that are below. But Jacob held the contemplative life, who saw the angels ascending and descending. But because natural consideration is not brought to perfection unless morality be first held, rightly is Ecclesiastes placed after Proverbs. And because the heavenly contemplation is not beheld unless these slipping things below be first despised, rightly is the Song of Songs placed after Ecclesiastes. For first is to order one's morals; then, to consider all things that are present as though they were not present. But in the third place, with a clean keenness of heart, to behold the things above and the things within. By these degrees of the books, therefore, as the treater of the sum of moral [0557A] books, Pope Gregory, sets forth, he made a certain ladder and engine unto the contemplation of God: so that, while first in the world honorable things are well performed, afterward even the honorable things of the world are despised, and at the last even the inmost and secret things of God are contemplated. Whence not undeservedly is this book named not the Song, but the Song of Songs, where the conjunction of the heavenly Word of God and of the soul is shown. Which song is so understood to excel all songs, as our Lord, the man assumed, excels the apostles, the patriarchs, or the prophets, or whatsoever those heavenly powers are: so that, as Christ is King of kings and Lord of lords, so also the Song of Songs is entitled above all the songs that were sung by the prophets. And as in the Old Testament [0557B] some things are holy places and others the Holy of holies, and some are sabbaths and others sabbaths of sabbaths, so also some are songs and others the Song of Songs. The holy places were in the tabernacle, and the things that were done outwardly; the sabbaths were those which were celebrated out of each several week; but the Holy of holies was received with a certain more secret veneration, and the sabbaths of sabbaths were not celebrated except in their own festivals. In the Song of Songs there is a certain secret and an inward solemnity, which secret is penetrated in hidden understandings; for if it is attended to in outward words, it is not a secret. Whereof we have already disputed above in the preface. It is also to be known that in Holy Scripture some songs are songs of victory, others songs of exhortation and attestation, others songs of exultation, others songs of help, [0557C] others songs of conjunction with God. The song of victory is that which Miriam sang when the Red Sea had been crossed, saying: « Let us sing to the Lord, for he has been gloriously honored: horse and rider he has cast into the sea [n: (Exod. XV)]. » [var: Vulg. Exod. XV, 1 *gloriose enim magnificatus est … deiecit in mare*, "gloriously magnified … cast down"] The song of exhortation and attestation is that which Moses spoke to the Israelites as they drew near to the land of promise. « Give heed, O heaven, and I will speak; and let the earth hear the words out of my mouth [n: (Isa. XXVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Deut. XXXII, 1 *Audite, caeli, quae loquor; audiat terra verba oris mei*] The song of exultation is that which Anna sang, not only for Samuel, but having foreseen in herself the fruitfulness of the Church, saying: « My heart hath exulted in the Lord [n: (I Reg. II)]. » Where through her own self the truth expressed the fruitfulness of the ecclesiastical offspring, when she says: « The barren hath borne very many, and she that had many sons is made weak [n: (Ibid.)] » The song of help David sang after [0557D] the battle, saying: « I will love thee, O Lord, my might [n: (Psal. XVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Psal. XVII, 2 *fortitudo mea*, "my strength"] But the song of conjunction with God is that song which is sung at nuptials as an epithalamium of bridegroom and bride, that is, the Song of Songs, which is by so much more solemn than all songs, as it is offered at nuptials of a more sublime solemnity. By those songs vices are shunned; but by these each one is enriched with virtues. By those the enemy is guarded against; by these the Lord is embraced with familiar love. Other songs too were sung by divers prophets, for divers persons or causes: as Habakkuk also noted his beforehand for the ignorant. Deborah too sang concerning the victory over Sisera. The three children also sang in the furnace, in which the marvels of Almighty [0558A] God are recounted. Jeremiah too sang a song of lamentation concerning the conversion of Jerusalem and of the sanctuary and of his whole people; and many others. But this is called the Song of Songs for this reason: because it is plainly proved to belong to the proper conjunction of Christ the King and the Church, where the Church prays to the Father of the Bridegroom, who is the Word of God, that now not through go-betweens — who are the angels and the prophets, who bore from the Son of God to the Church tidings of the reprobation to come — but that he show him himself at last in person through the mystery of the incarnation; that now she may deserve to embrace his form, beautiful above the sons of men, and to receive from him most sweet kisses, and to handle his most welcome breasts. For she who thus desires is called by the Greek word ἐκκλησία; in Latin she is interpreted *congregatio,* or better *convocatio;* in Hebrew [0558B] *Synagoga.* According to place or time she is proved to have been called by divers names by the Word of God. Concerning whose appellations we made promise above. For sometimes Jerusalem, sometimes *Sion,* very often *friend,* but often *beloved.* Sometimes *sister,* or beautiful *bride, dove, or spotless,* or perfect — so is she known to be called in the person of Christ by the mouth of the prophets and in this book. She is called Jerusalem indeed, because after the wrath of Almighty God she received the peace sent from heaven by the Father, in that she is signified to have some peace. Which peace and conjunction she is said to entreat when she beseeches him: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » But *Sion,* because out of the valley of the tears of idolatry, where she had fallen in Adam in body, and not in heart, she ascends again to the mount of paradise, [0558C] and by offering herself as a mirror to the undivided Trinity for cleanness of heart, she is made conspicuous and lofty. In which Almighty God may delight to walk as upon a fertile mountain, and to scatter the blessed seed of his doctrine; on account of which *watchtower* or *seed* is interpreted in her. But she is called *friend,* because by repudiating the devil she called God back to friendship. But she is named *beloved,* because after the most bitter hatreds of unbelief she is made beloved of God by the gifts of belief. She is named *sister,* namely, through the mystery of the assumed flesh, when he is born to the lands out of the same matter of seeing out of which the Church too is begotten. She is pronounced *beautiful,* namely, being wiped clean through baptism from all the stains of sins, after much Ethiopian and shadowy custom of crimes, [0558D] her skin being cleansed and she called back to her native beauty. But she was made *bride* by joining his body and blood to her own body. By which sacrament she pledged that besides him she loves no other lover with her whole heart, preserving with great zeal his precious ring of faith. For she is proclaimed *shapely,* by reforming in her soul the form of that image in which she was created. But she is named *dove:* since all the rapacity which she had been wont to perpetrate with hooked hands as of vultures has been laid aside, now, joined in fellowship with the Holy Spirit, she shines with the whiteness of the simplicity and bounty of doves. And she who in evil teachers had been wont to snatch away from other souls the foods of eternal life, now in good teachers imparts them again from her own most sweet [0559A] throat to those who hear, as is the nature of doves. But she is praised as *spotless:* the old and ancient custom of crimes being laid aside, and the robe of most holy baptism having once been put on [sic: *in luta*], by living spotlessly she has not stained herself further. And having been made the imitator of her Lord Christ, by dying for his name she grows bright with the file of martyrdom and of mortification. She could find no other iron tool stronger, by which the ancient dross of the soul might be filed away, as Apponius says, save the file of martyrdom; by which, in the furnace of torments, the soul — however great the mass of the filth of sins by which it is surrounded — may be renewed in its own blood; by which she may be a perfect dove, and spotless in Christ. Whence indeed she is named *perfect* also, as one who is to receive all judgment from the Father, namely, in [0559B] Christ. This Church, therefore, in whom the multitude of all who believe in Christ consists — she who before, namely before the coming of the Bridegroom, lay ignominiously and defiled in her own blood, according to the prophet; nourished amid great cares, and advanced even to the years of puberty, and adorned with his doctrine as with necklaces, but by his conjunction exalted even to the kingdom of heaven. But how ignominiously and trodden down she lay is rebuked through the Prophet. « Thy root and thy generation is of the land of Chanaan. Thy father an Amorrhite, and thy mother a Cethite, in the day wherein thou wast born. In the day of thy birth thy navel was not cut; thou wast not washed with water unto health, nor salted with salt, nor swaddled in cloths. My eye did not spare over thee, to do one of these things for thee, out of pity for thee; but [0559C] thou wast lying naked and full of ignominy, trodden down in thy blood. Thy breasts swelled, and thy hair grew, and behold, thy time of lovers; and thou wast lying naked, full of confusion [n: (Ezech. XVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Ezech. XVI, 5 *Non pepercit super te oculus* (without *meus*), and for the clause following, *sed proiecta es super faciem terrae in abiectione animae tuae*] And in what manner, having pity on her, he willed through the coming of his conjunction to do one of these things, he subjoined: « And I sent my mantle, and covered thy ignominy. I washed thee with water and cleansed thy blood from thee, and anointed thee with oil, and clothed thee with a many-colored garment. With fine flour and honey I nourished thee. I girded thee with fine linen, and shod thee with hyacinth, and set a crown of comeliness upon thy head, and rings in thy ears; and I swore to thee, and I entered into a covenant with thee, and thou becamest mine. saith the Lord God [n: (Ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Ezech. XVI, 8–10 *expandi amictum meum super te*; *vestivi te discoloribus*; *calceavi te ianthino*] Which, although the people of the Jews is accused [0559D] of stubbornness, he addresses in her person — every soul that is born through the laver of regeneration, by his will, which soul she had formerly begotten through the sin of the Amorrhite, who is interpreted *bitter,* that is, of the devil, and through the nefarious custom which in the present place is called Cethite, which is interpreted *excess of mind,* should have forgotten to be reproved — it is proved by the rebuke with which our merciful Lord Jesus Christ, through his holy appearing, nourished her, gathered though unknowingly out of the aforesaid filth, and adorned her with precious garments and necklaces, and deigned to make her the consort of his kingdom through the wave of most holy baptism, where the glorious coupling of Christ the Son of God and of the Church is celebrated. In which sacred mystery she is washed with the water of ablution; the salt of wisdom being received, she is salted with the faith of the undivided [0560A] and coeternal Trinity by the handing over of the brothel; being stripped naked, she is covered with garments by the charism of various virtues. She is filled with the fatness of the Holy Spirit, with the unction of the charism. Where she is nourished with fine flour in the body of Christ, and with honey in his eloquences; where her ears are adorned with the rings of divine understanding, the jewelled crown of martyrdom shining upon her head; she is girded with fine linen, which is the token of purity, that is, with the love of virginity and chastity, which keeps her from all dissolution and, having called her back, binds her fast. They are shod with hyacinth, which in Hebrew is called *preparing color,* that the feet may be prepared to run together always to the precepts of the Gospel of peace. With these ornaments, namely, the Church desired to be adorned; but because she could not be adorned save through his appearing, therefore she [0560B] desired his visitation. For she had heard, as a bride, through the prophet, in the person of the Bridegroom, that is, of the Word of God the Father, saying: « I will espouse thee to me for ever, I will espouse thee to me in justice, I will espouse thee to me in judgment, I will espouse thee to me in mercy, I will espouse thee to me in faith [n: (Ose. II)]. » [var: Vulg. Ose. II, 19–20 *sponsabo* three times, and includes *et in miserationibus*] And through Isaiah likewise the voice of the Church herself says: « He has set a mitre upon me as upon a bride, and has clothed me with the garments of salvation; as a bridegroom decked with a crown, and as a bride adorned with her jewels, he has encompassed me [n: (Isa. LXI)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. LXI, 10 lacks *Posuit mihi mitram sicut sponsae* and reads *et indumento iustitiae circumdedit me*] And Zacharias, in announcing glory of this kind to the trembling Church, says: « Fear not, daughter of Sion; behold, thy King cometh to thee, meek and gentle, sitting upon the colt of an ass; he himself is Savior and Redeemer [n: (Zach. IX)]. » [var: Vulg. Zach. IX, 9 *Exsulta satis, filia Sion … ecce rex tuus veniet tibi iustus et salvator; ipse pauper, et ascendens super asinam et super pullum filium asinae*] All these patriarchs and prophets, therefore, [0560C] sang of the future, ineffable coupling of Christ and the Church. But the Apostle set it forth as already celebrated, saying: « Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the Church, who delivered himself up for her, that he might present to himself a spotless Church, not having spot or wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V)]. » [var: Vulg. Ephes. V, 27 *gloriosam Ecclesiam*, "a glorious Church"] And again to the Corinthians: « For I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you a chaste virgin to Christ [n: (II Cor. XI)]. » And in another place he thus exhorts the faithful: « You are the body of Christ, and flesh of his flesh [n: (I Cor. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. I Cor. XII, 27 *Vos autem estis corpus Christi, et membra de membro*, "and members of a member"] To her, namely, who is to be confirmed by so great and such witnesses, all the offices of the nuptials are to be applied. Where there are at hand also the friends of the Bridegroom, the companions too of the bride, and the manners as well of the singers, who with sweet modulation may sing the power of the Bridegroom or praise the beauty of the bride, by which she obtained the bounty [0560D] of the Bridegroom: how the exalted one humbled himself from that height of deity to the mire of the deep, that he might join to his own majesty the humble and cast-down matter of flesh, lifted up to the summit of the heavens. And it must be noted that sometimes Holy Scripture calls him Lord, sometimes father, sometimes bridegroom. For when he wills to be feared, he names himself Lord; when he wills to be honored, father; when he wills to be loved, bridegroom. He himself says through the prophet: « If I am Lord, where is my fear? If I am Father, where is my honor? [n: (Mal. I)] » [var: Vulg. Mal. I, 6 *Si ergo pater ego sum, ubi est honor meus? et si Dominus ego sum, ubi est timor meus?* — the two clauses in the reverse order] And again he says: « I have espoused thee to me in justice and in faith; or assuredly, I have remembered the day of thy espousal in the desert [n: (Ose. II)]. » [var: Vulg. Ose. II, 20 *sponsabo te mihi in fide*; the second clause answers rather to Jerem. II, 2 *recordatus sum tui … et caritatem desponsationis tuae*] And what is there with God of "when," and when [0561A] it is not. But because he wills first to be feared, that he may be honored, and first to be honored, that one may come to his love, he both names himself Lord on account of fear, and father on account of honor, and bridegroom on account of love — that through fear one may come to honor, and through honor one may arrive at his love. By how much, then, honor is worthier than fear, by so much the more does God rejoice to be called father than Lord. And by how much, then, love is dearer than honor, by so much the more does God rejoice to be bridegroom than father. In this book, therefore, the Lord and the Church are named not lord and handmaid, but bridegroom and bride, so that service may be rendered not to fear alone, not to reverence alone, but also to love, and that in outward words the inward affection may be stirred up. When he names himself Lord, he indicates that we are created. When he names himself father, he indicates [0561B] that we are joined. But it is more to be joined to God than to be created and adopted. But thus generally, in the voice of the Church, the coming of the Lord is awaited in this work, so that also specially each single soul may receive the entrance of God into her heart as the approach of the bridegroom into the bridal chamber. And it must be known that in this book four speaking persons are introduced: namely the bridegroom and the bride, and the maidens with the bride, and the flocks of companions with the bridegroom. For the bride herself is the perfect Church, the bridegroom the Lord, as has often been said. But the maidens with the bride are the souls that are beginning, and coming to maturity through a new zeal. But the companions of the bridegroom are either the angels, who often appeared to men, coming to them, or assuredly all the perfect men in the Church, who have known how to announce the truth [0561C] to men; but they who singly are maidens or companions are, all together, the bride, who all together are the Church — although these names can also be so taken with respect to each single believer. For he who already loves the Lord perfectly is the bride; he who preaches the bridegroom is a companion; he who as yet, being new, follows the way of the good is a maiden. Let us imitate, therefore, that we may be the bride. If we are not yet able for this, let us be companions. If neither have we attained this, let us at least come together to this bridal chamber as maidens. But it must be known, before we come to the peace of conjunction, that the most wise Solomon showed in figure and in riddles whatsoever, from the beginning of the world unto the end, the Word of God has done or shall do in mysteries toward the Church. In which song [0561D] all the things that are narrated, covered over with mysteries, are taught to have been revealed and fulfilled in the incarnation of the Word — where the human race, dashed down, is raised up; fettered, it is loosed; corrupted, it is reformed to the integrity of virginity; driven out of paradise, it is restored: out of a captive, free; out of a stranger, a citizen; out of a handmaid, a mistress; out of the most vile, a queen — and it is shown to have been made, by the kindness of its Creator the Word of God Christ, a bride worthy to receive his kisses, and, made one spirit together with him, to reign exalted in the heavenly places. Which sublimity is known to have been celebrated by the humbling of the Son of God, as the teacher of the Gentiles teaches, saying of Christ: « He humbled himself, that he might exalt us [n: (Phil. II)]. » [var: Vulg. Phil. II, 8 *humiliavit semetipsum, factus oboediens usque ad mortem*; *ut nos exaltaret* is not in the received text] For it is not reckoned an injury to a craftsman, when a precious ring has fallen into a pit of the body, [0562A] to lay aside his robe for a little while and go down to seek it, and, having found it, to restore it again to his right hand. For whatsoever the foolish and impious heathendom — whose mist of unbelief has blinded the eyes of the heart — reckons humble, whatsoever most vile, whatsoever despised and unworthy: this is proved to be glorious, this marvellous, this, I say, magnificent, in the sight of the divine clemency and of all the heavenly powers. For what immense power, what sweetness of goodness, what surpassing fragrance of compassion out of all the works of the paternal excellence, admired by the angels and by the choirs of all the saints, is offered with lofty voices and perpetual praises, unless that the Lord should couple to himself a truly poor little handmaid — as an outsider, a mortal one — the Lord Jesus Christ, through the assumption of flesh, the Church, that is, the Word of God [0562B] the soul, under the title of conjunction? Since therefore we have rather often spoken of bridegroom and bride, of the Lord and the Church, and rightly, according to the poet, we lighten the way with speech: Both hope and faith accompany my step into steep places,
More kindly at last we come through to the highest citadel.
