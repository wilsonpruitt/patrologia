## CHAPTER ONE.

« Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » The Lord had sent angels to her, [0562C]and to her the patriarchs and the prophets, bearing spiritual gifts; but she sought to receive those gifts themselves not through the servants of the bridegroom, but now through the bridegroom himself. Let us set before our eyes the whole human race from the beginning of the world unto the end of the world — namely, that the whole Church is one person: who had received earnests by a spiritual gift, but nevertheless sought the presence of her bridegroom, who says. « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth, » For holy Church, sighing for the coming of the Mediator of God and men, for the coming of her Redeemer, makes the words of her prayer to the Father, that he may send the Son, and that he may enlighten her with his own presence; that he may make speech to that same Church no longer through the mouths of the prophets, but with his own mouth — as if she said more openly: Let him touch me with the sweetness of his presence, [0562D] which I have rather often heard promised by the prophets. Whence also concerning the same bridegroom it is written in the Gospel [n: (Matth. V)], when he was sitting on the mountain and was speaking the words of the sublime precepts: « This Jesus, opening his mouth, said. » [var: Vulg. Matth. V, 2 *et aperiens os suum, docebat eos dicens*, "and opening his mouth he taught them, saying"] As if it were said: Then he opened his own mouth, who before, for the exhortation of the Church, opened the mouths of the prophets. Behold gladly, Caesar Augustus, how from the beginning of the world — namely from Adam, nay rather from Abel the just — unto John the Baptist the Church always sought Christ in those who were of higher understanding, and through all the patriarchs and prophets cried with tearful voice to the Almighty Father that his kisses be brought near to her; whom, both in the baptism of Jordan the voice of the Father from the cloud, and John with his finger, pointed out, saying: [0563A] « Behold the Lamb of God who taketh away the sin of the world [n: (Joann. I)]. » And the Father himself from the cloud: « This is my Son, he says, the beloved, in whom I am well pleased [n: (Matth. III)]. » But understand John to be one of the many friends of the bridegroom preparing the bride, by whose presence the Church received the holy kisses of Christ, he himself testifying concerning Christ. « He that hath the bride is the bridegroom. But the friend of the bridegroom rejoiceth with joy at the voice of the bridegroom [n: (Joan. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. III, 29 *amicus autem sponsi, qui stat et audit eum, gaudio gaudet propter vocem sponsi*] John marvels, namely, when he saw him coming to the baptism for the sanctification of the waters, who says: « I ought to be baptized by thee, and comest thou to me? » [n: (Matth. III.)] But he rejoices with joy, hearing the voice of him saying: « Suffer it now, for so it behooves us to fulfil all justice [n: (Ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Matth. III, 15 *sic enim decet nos*, "for so it becometh us"] Wherein were fulfilled the prayers of the Church, [0563B] promised long since, to God the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, saying: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » But while therefore holy Church desires the bridegroom yet absent, who is to be incarnate, suddenly she beholds him present. For the grace of our Redeemer has this: that when we speak of him as those who seek him, we enjoy his presence — thus it was concerning the two, namely Cleophas and the other, as is written in the Gospel. Whence she subjoins: « For thy breasts are better than wine, » « and the odor of thy ointments above all aromatical spices [n: (Cant. III)]. » The wine was the knowledge of the law, the knowledge of the prophets; but the Lord, coming, because he willed his wisdom to be preached through the flesh, made it as it were to grow milky into the breasts of the flesh. And that which we were by no means able to grasp in his divinity, we might come to know by his incarnation: [0563C] whence not undeservedly are his breasts praised, because the condescension of his preaching wrought that in our hearts which the doctrine of the law was by no means able to work. For the preaching of the incarnation nourished us more than the doctrine of the law. « And the odor of thy ointments above all aromatical spices. » The ointments of the Lord are the virtues. The ointment of the Lord was the Holy Spirit, concerning whom it is said to him through the Prophet: « God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows [n: (Ps. XLIV)]. » With this oil he was anointed then, when he was incarnate. For he did not first exist as man and afterward receive the Holy Spirit; but because he was incarnate by the mediation of the Holy Spirit, he was anointed with this same oil then, when he was created man. The odor, therefore, of his ointments is the fragrance of the Holy Spirit, who, proceeding from him, remained in him. [0563D] The odor of his ointments is the fragrance of the virtues which he wrought. This Church had aromatical spices, because she had many gifts of the spirit, which in the house of God, that is, in the congregation of the saints, would give forth the odor of good repute. But the odor of thy ointments above all aromatical spices, because the fragrance of the bridegroom's virtues, which was made through his incarnation, conquered the preachings of the law, which had been bestowed beforehand as earnests by the bridegroom. Those aromatical spices of the law were ministered through angels; this ointment was given through the presence of the bridegroom, For by so much the more does the Church believe unto understanding, as she also deserved to be enlightened by the grace of a fuller vision. But this which we have said generally of the whole Church, let us now understand specially [0564A] of each single soul. Let us set before our eyes that there is a certain soul clinging to the pursuits of the gifts, receiving understanding from another's preaching, who desires that she too be enlightened by divine grace, so that at some time she may understand even of herself; who considers that she understands nothing except through the words of preachers, and who says: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » As if she said: Let it touch me within, that I may know understanding, and that I may enjoy no longer the voices of preachers, but the touch of his inward grace. The Lord kissed Moses as with the kiss of his mouth, when through the confidence of familiar grace he extended understanding to him. Whence it is written. « If there be a prophet, in a dream I speak to him, and not as to my servant Moses. For I speak to him mouth to mouth [n: (Num. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. Num. XII, 6–8 *Si quis fuerit inter vos propheta Domini, in visione apparebo ei, vel per somnium loquar ad illum. At non talis servus meus Moyses*] For to speak mouth [0564B] to mouth is, as it were, to kiss, and to touch the mind with inward understanding. There follows: « Thy breasts are better than wine. » The breasts of God are, as we said before, the most humble preaching of his incarnation; but the wisdom of the world is as it were a kind of wine. For it inebriates the mind, because it renders it a stranger to the understanding of humility. The philosophers are inebriated as with a kind of wine, while through worldly wisdom they pass beyond the manner of the common people. Let holy Church despise that wisdom; let her seek after the most humble incarnation of the Lord's preaching. Let that savor more to her whereby she is nourished through the infirmity of his flesh, than that whereby this world is exalted through the elation of false prudence; and let her say: « For thy breasts are better than wine, » that is, the most humble preaching of thy incarnation surpasses the puffed-up [0564C] wisdom of the world. Whence it is written: « That which is weak of God is stronger than men. And that which is foolish of God is wiser than men [n: (I Cor. I)]. » [var: Vulg. I Cor. I, 25 *quod stultum est Dei, sapientius est hominibus: et quod infirmum est Dei, fortius est hominibus* — the two clauses in the reverse order] But because the wise men of this world themselves sometimes seem to pursue certain virtues — for we see very many have charity, keep gentleness, practice outward honorableness in all things. Which virtues, however, they display not that they may please God, but that they may please men. Which for that reason are not virtues, because they do not seek to please God. Yet they smell sweet in human nostrils, while by human judgment they render a good repute. But let these be compared with the true odor of our Redeemer, let them be compared with true virtues, and let it be said: « And the odor of thy ointments above all aromatical spices, » that is, the fragrance of thy [0564D] virtues surpasses every appearance of the virtues of the wise men of the world, because, namely, it transcends their feigned images out of truth. Since we have said that in the second place this which has been said is to be understood of each single soul, let us still pursue the same sense more subtly, if we can, with God helping. Every soul that fears God is already under his yoke. But still far off, because it fears. For each one becomes near to God only when he loses the punishment of fear and receives from him the grace of charity. Let us set before our eyes the soul of any elect person, which is kindled by continual desire into the love of the vision of the bridegroom; because what it is not able perfectly to receive in this life, it contemplates his loftiness and is pricked with compunction out of that very love. For that very [0565A] compunction which comes about through eagerness, because it is also kindled by desire, is as it were a kind of kiss. For as often as the soul is pricked with compunction in his love, so often does it kiss God. For there are many who already indeed fear God, already receive him in good working, but do not yet kiss him, because they are by no means pricked with compunction by his love. Which was well signified in the banquet of the Pharisee. Who, when he had received the Lord, and when in his heart he disparaged the woman kissing his feet, heard. « I entered into thy house, thou gavest me no kiss; but she, from the time she came in, hath not ceased to kiss my feet [n: (Luc. VII)]. » Everyone who already gives alms, who is already zealous in good works, receives Christ as it were into a banquet, feeds Christ, because he does not cease to sustain him in his members. [0565B] But if he is not yet pricked with compunction through love, he does not yet kiss his footsteps. But it is well said, « she hath not ceased to kiss his feet [n: (Ibid.)] » [var: Vulg. Luc. VII, 45 *pedes meos*, "my feet," as also in the quotation printed just above] For it is not enough to be pricked with compunction once in the love of God and to rest, but the compunction ought both to be and to grow frequent. Whence the woman is praised in this, that she did not cease to kiss, that is, she by no means ceased to be pricked with compunction. Whence also it is said through the Prophet: « Appoint a solemn day in frequentings, even to the horn of the altar [n: (Psal. CXVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Psal. CXVII, 27 *in condensis*, "in thick boughs"] The compunction of our heart is a solemn day to the Lord. But then is a solemn day appointed in frequenting, when one is assiduously moved to tears for love of the Lord. Wherefore, or if we should say, how long are we to do these things, how long are we afflicted with tribulations? at once he subjoined the limit up to which it ought to be done, [0565C] saying: « Even to the horn of the altar. » For the horn of the altar is the exaltation of the inward sacrifice. When we have now arrived there, it is by no means necessary that we make a solemnity to the Lord out of our lamentation. Let the soul, therefore, which already desires to be pricked with compunction through love, which already seeks to contemplate in the vision of her bridegroom, say: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Or assuredly the kiss of his mouth is that very perfection of inward peace, at which when we have arrived nothing further will remain that we should seek. Whence also it is aptly subjoined. « For thy breasts are better than wine. » The wine is the knowledge of God, which, placed in this life, we receive. But we embrace the breasts of the bridegroom then, when in the eternal fatherland we contemplate him, now present, through embrace. Let her say therefore: « Thy breasts are better than [0565D] wine. » As if she said: Great indeed is the knowledge which thou hast conferred upon me concerning thyself in this life; great is the wine of the knowledge of thee wherewith thou inebriatest me; but thy breasts are better than wine, because then, through sight and through the sublimity of contemplation, whatever is now known of thee through faith is transcended « And the odor of thy ointments above all aromatical spices. » Here holy Church has aromatical spices, while she is strong in the virtue of knowledge, the virtue of chastity, the virtue of mercy, the virtue of humility, the virtue of charity. If the life of the saints did not have the odor of aromatical spices out of the virtues, Paul would not say: « We are the good odor of Christ unto God in every place [n: (I Cor. II)]. » But far more excellent is that unction of the contemplation of God, to which we are one day to be led; [0566A] far more excellent is the odor of the ointments of God than the aromatical spices of our virtues. And if the things we have received are already great, yet far more powerful are those which we are to receive from the contemplation of our Creator. Whence let the soul pant and say: « The odor of thy ointments above all aromatical spices, » that is, those good things which thou preparest through contemplation of thee transcend all these gifts of the virtues which thou hast bestowed in this life. Let us say hence to the Church, let us say hence to the soul thus out of her senses, thus burning with love for her bridegroom, whence nevertheless she conceived her desire, whence she laid hold of the knowledge of his divinity. But behold, she herself expresses it and says:
