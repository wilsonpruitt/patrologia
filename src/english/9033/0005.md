## CHAPTER THE FIRST. (cont.)

« Ointment poured out is thy name. » [var: Vulg. *oleum effusum nomen tuum*, "oil poured out"] The ointment poured out is the divinity made flesh. For if ointment be [0566B] in a little vessel, it gives back less of its scent outwardly; but if it is poured out, the scent of the poured-out ointment is spread abroad. The name of God, therefore, is ointment poured out, because from the immensity of his divinity he poured himself out outwardly unto our nature, and from that which is invisible he rendered himself visible. For if he had not poured himself out, he would by no means have become known to us. The ointment pours itself out when he both kept himself Lord and showed himself man. Of which pouring out Paul says: « Who, when he was in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant [n: (Phil. II)]. » What Paul said, « emptied, » this Solomon said, « poured out. » Since therefore the Lord became known to the human race through the humility of the incarnation, let it be said to him: Ointment poured out is thy name. But [0566C] because in the Hebrew verity something is otherwise held, we are compelled to make an apostrophe to the things above, and to insert some things otherwise. Finally, when she had said: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth, » and had subjoined: « For thy breasts are better than wine, » where the exceeding joy of love fulfilled is shown by the handling of the breasts, straightway she added, according to the Hebrew: fragrant with the best ointments — which things, although they have been set forth mellifluously above by the highest expositor in shining discourse, we can nevertheless repeat again according to the opinions of certain men. The wine, they say, was the word of the messengers, brought down by angels, who spoke to the prophets or in the prophets, as Zacharias the prophet says [n: (Cap. II)]. And he said to the Angel « who spoke in me, » [var: Vulg. Zach. I, 9 *dixit ad me angelus*, "the angel said to me"] which gladdened the heart of the Church, [0566D] hearing of the coming of him whom she desired. But when with mouth and hands she handled his body and blood, she knew breasts better — flowing with the milk of the apostolic doctrine and of the Gospels, where is all the divine perfection which is now ministered — than the wine of the Old Testament and of the law, which according to the Apostle brought no one through to perfection. For although perfect apostolic men, who are teachers to the Christian people appointed beforehand, are understood to be the breasts of Christ, whom Christ nourishes as little ones, yet it will not be unfitting: he understands properly the two breasts of Christ to be the Baptist and the Evangelist, the two Johns, who after the kisses of the aforesaid sacrament were proffered to the Church as fullest words, since the one shows him to the Church as alone taking away the sin [0567A] of the world, and as the lamb, and the other as, in the beginning, the Word, God, abiding with God the Father — who through an unspotted life so clung in his love as breasts to the bosom, while the one shows to the Church, still little and suckling, the true man under the name of the unspotted lamb, and the other, from the effect of the creating of all things, shows him the maker, in the beginning the Word, God, through whom all things were created. No taste of sweetness of this kind, therefore, had until this time come to the Church, that in the meekness of the lamb, the king being terrible, the troops of the wolves, the demons, should be put to flight — whose presence strikes terror into all earthly and ethereal powers, who clothes others with the covering of the Holy Spirit and himself has need of none, who lead the Church, lifted up [0567B] from earthly things, to the fellowship of the Father through the assumption of flesh, with which he was made one in mind and in matter, sitting at the right hand of the Father, with whom in the beginning was God the Word. By the milk, therefore, of his doctrine conferring eternal life — the aforesaid breasts of Christ our Lord flowing to the beloved Church, from whose milky bosom eternal life, which Adam had lost, was poured into the apostles through the whiteness of doctrine — which life, if the Church has received it through the breasts in believers, she holds out daily also to her own posterity, as butter made from milk, through the interpretation of the word. For first it contains a mystery through the nature of milk, that is, the liquid of what is chaste; and when the liquid has been worked by art it yields butter: so also the milk of the apostolic doctrine, when it has found a master, declares [0567C] that it contains within itself the undivided Trinity. Unless the soul shall have used these foods unceasingly, it will not be able to live for ever. Which food the Church received from the aforesaid breasts in the sacrament of baptism, where by the threefold confession of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit she knows the scent of that knowledge, since it has often been said to her that the milk of doctrine has conferred the threefold marrow of redemption: namely, the most white liquid of baptism, and the sweet juice of the body of Christ, and the richest butter, the oil of the sacred chrism, through which the Holy Spirit is poured in. Of which fatness the Church, in another Prophet, prays that the marrow of her soul be filled, saying: « Let my soul be filled as with marrow and fatness [n: (Psal. LXII)] » Whose scent raises up again souls wounded by sins; [0567D] by the power of whose fatness the fragrance hidden in the aforesaid breasts, which after the kisses received she is taught to praise in the following little verse, saying: « Fragrant with the best ointments. » That ointment in the Old Testament, compounded with God for master, was indeed of good odor, that is, of merit; with it kings, prophets, and priests were anointed. But by as much as a living man differs from a picture smeared over with the dyes of colors, by so much is there a difference between what the Church receives by being born again in the Spirit and that whose figure the Synagogue was receiving in the body — whose things were so great that scarcely in one nation, Judea, and to a few, did it grant the primacy. But this ointment of the Church bears in itself so much power, of fragrance and of medicine at once, that it renders all believers most sound, [0568A] appoints them kings and priests, and the scent of the knowledge of it has filled the whole world from the rising of the sun even to its setting. What other fragrance can it be understood to contain within itself, save the name of Christ? Of which it follows: « Ointment poured out is thy name. » For as men shut in amid a multitude of putrid corpses are endangered by disease, so it had befallen the inhabitants of this world through Adam, the devil bringing in a plural number of gods by the mouth of the serpent, saying: « You shall be as gods [n: (Gen. II)]. » But when the name of the one true God had been brought in, the little vessel having been laid up in a body through the mystery of the incarnation — which vessel, being broken by the blows of the nails and of the lance, by the scent of the knowledge of him every stench of the devil's doctrine was wiped away from the whole world. And that great name which in the people of Israel alone was known [0568B] through the anointing of the chrism — now, as when the vessel of ointment is poured out in a house the whole house is filled with the scent, so also from the time of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ the whole world is proved to be filled with the knowledge of the name of the one true God, Christ, so that through all the nations of the Gentiles the fragrance of the virtues runs back daily like the scent of ointment, « while the dead rise at the invocation of his name, the blind see, the lame walk, the dumb speak, » demons are put to flight, every sickness falls away, languor is driven off, in the power of this name invoked. Behold with what odors the love and affection of Christ kindles the young maidens' souls — of whom, namely, he now says: « Therefore the young maidens have loved thee. » These things having been tasted beforehand, let us return to the sense above of the most sweet doctor. For it follows: « Therefore the young maidens [0568C] have loved thee. » What do we take the young maidens to be in this place, save the souls of the elect renewed through baptism? For the life of the sinner belongs to the old man, the life of the just man to the new. Since therefore the ointment poured itself outward, it made young maidens burn in its love, because it showed souls renewed and aflame with desire for it. The age of boyhood is not yet suited to love. He is a boy who has not yet begun the zeal of an ardent life. He is an old man who had indeed begun, but has left off. Since therefore neither do these burn in the Lord who have not yet begun, nor these who have already begun the faith but have grown cold, the boyish and the aged life being set aside. The young maidens are said to run, that is, those souls which are in the very fervor of love. Yet this we can also understand otherwise. For adolescence can be referred to weakness. [0568D] For the youthful ages are the orders of the angels, who have been conquered by no debility, overcome by no infirmity. Let it therefore be said: « Ointment poured out is thy name, therefore the young maidens have loved thee, » that is: through thy incarnation thou hast poured out the knowledge of thee outwardly; therefore weak souls have strength to love human nature. For those highest Powers, like the youthful ages, love thee even there where thou art not poured out, because there too they see thee, where thou containest thyself in the state of thy divinity. Thou therefore who by those highest orders, as by youthful ages, art seen even when not poured out, art poured out outwardly for the sake of men, that thou mayest be loved also by young maidens, that is, by weak minds. It follows.
