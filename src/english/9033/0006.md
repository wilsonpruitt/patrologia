## CHAPTER THE FIRST. (cont.)

[0569A] « Draw me. » Everyone who is drawn is led forth unwilling, either as one not able or as one not willing. But he who says, draw me, has something which he is able to do, has something which he is not able to do. Human nature wills to follow the Lord, but, overcome by the habit of infirmity, it has not the strength to follow as it ought. It sees, therefore, that there is one thing in itself whither it strives, another thing in itself which it is not able to do, and rightly it says, draw me. Paul had seen himself as willing and yet not able, when he said: « With the mind I serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin [n: (Rom. VII)]; » and: « I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind. [n: (Ibid.)] » Since therefore there is something which incites us, and another which weighs us down, let us say: « Draw me; we will run after thee in the odor of thy ointments. » [var: Vulg. *in odorem*, accusative, "to the odor of thy ointments"] We run in the odor of God's ointments when, [0569B] breathed upon by his spiritual gifts, we gape after the love of the vision of him. But it must be known that, in the fact that men follow God, sometimes they walk, sometimes they run, sometimes they run strongly, as it were after God. He walks who follows lukewarmly. He runs who follows fervently. He runs perfectly who follows perseveringly. For the heart was immovable for the following of God, and was unwilling to walk after him, when the coming of the Lord appeared in the world and moved human minds from their insensible standing. Whence it is written: « His feet stood, and the earth was moved [n: (Psal. CVI)]. » But to these not motion but running is spoken of, because it is not enough that we follow, unless we also run by desiring. But because neither does it suffice to run, unless it be run perfectly, Paul says: « So run that [0569C] you may obtain [n: (Rom. IX)]. » But some, while they run too much, slip away into indiscretion. For they are wise more than is needful, and they set behind them the judgments of him whom they were following. Whence well, when it was said *we will run,* there was set before it, *after thee.* For they run after God who consider his judgments, who prefer his will to their own, and who strive to come to him under worthy work with discretion. Hence the Prophet, considering the will of God and following it, says: « My soul hath cleaved after thee [n: (Ps. LXXII)]. » Here it is said to Peter as he gives counsel: « Go back behind me, Satan. For thou savorest not the things that are of God, but the things that are of men [n: (Matth. XVI)]. » [var: Vulg. *Vade post me, Satana*, "Go behind me, Satan"] Since therefore perfect souls behold the judgments of God with the utmost caution, and presume to run before them neither through torpor nor through indiscreet fervor, well [0569D] is it said: « We will run after thee in the odor of thy ointments. » For then do we run after God, when we both follow by loving and, by fearing, anticipate the divine judgments. It follows: « The king hath brought me into his bedchamber; we will exult and rejoice in thee. » [var: Vulg. *in cellaria sua*, "into his storerooms"] The Church of God is like a certain house of the king, and that house has a gate, has an ascent, has dining-halls, has bedchambers. Everyone who within the Church has faith has also entered the gate of that house, because as the gate opens the rest of the house, so faith is the door of the remaining virtues. Everyone who within the Church has hope has already come to the ascent of the house. For hope lifts up the heart, that it may seek high things and forsake the lowest. Everyone set in that house [0570A] who has charity walks about, as it were, in the dining-halls. For charity is broad, which is stretched out even to the love of enemies. Everyone set in the Church who now searches out the high secrets, who now considers the hidden judgments, will enter as it were into the bedchamber. Of the gate of that house a certain one said: « Open ye to me the gates of justice; having entered into them, I will confess to the Lord [n: (Ps. CXVII)]. » Of the ascent of hope he said: « He hath disposed ascents in his heart [n: (Ps. LXXXIII)]. » Of the broad dining-halls of that house it is said: « Thy commandment is exceeding broad [n: (Ps. CXVIII)]. » In the broad commandment charity is specially designated. Of the bedchamber of the king he was speaking who said: « My secret to myself [n: (Isa. XXIV)]. » And elsewhere: « I heard secret words which it is not lawful for a man to speak [n: (II Cor. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. *audivit*, "he heard"] The first approach of that house, [0570B] therefore, is the gate of faith; the second advance, the ascent of hope; the third, the breadth of charity; the fourth is now the perfection of charity unto the knowledge of the secrets of God. Since therefore holy Church, in her perfect members, in her holy teachers, in those who are now full and rooted in the mysteries of God, has come as it were to the high secrets, and, still set in this life, already penetrates those things, she says: « The king hath brought me into his bedchamber. » For through the prophets, through the apostles, through the teachers, who, set in this life, already penetrated the high secrets of that life, the Church had entered into the bedchamber of that king. And it is to be observed carefully that she does not say, into the bedchamber of the bridegroom, but into the bedchamber of the king. For by naming the king she wishes to show the reverence of the secrets, because the more open [0570C] the bedchamber is, the greater is the reverence to be shown to those things into which one enters: lest therefore, while each one knows the secrets of God, while he searches out the hidden judgments, while he is raised up to the heights of contemplation, he be lifted up and slip away into pride, it is said to be the king's bedchamber that he enters. To whom the greater reverence is to be shown, the more the soul is led to the knowing of his secrets, so that each one who advances, who now, exalted by grace, has come to the high secrets, may attend to himself and by that very advance be the more humbled. Whence Ezekiel also, as often as he is led to the contemplating of high things, is called Son of man. As if it were said to him: But attend to what thou art, and be not lifted up because of those things unto which thou art raised. But it belongs to few in the Church to search out and [0570D] comprehend these high and hidden judgments of God: nevertheless, while we see that strong men are able to come to so great a wisdom that they contemplate the secrets of God in their hearts, let us little ones also have confidence that at some time we shall come to pardon, at some time to his grace. Whence also, from the words of the young maidens, it is subjoined. « We will exult and rejoice in thee. » While the Church, in those who are perfect, enters the bedchamber of the king, the young maidens promise themselves the hope of exultation. Because while the strong come to the contemplating of high things, the weak take hope of pardon for their sins, therefore she says: « The king hath brought me into his bedchamber; we will exult and rejoice in thee. » -- « Mindful of thy breasts above wine. The upright love thee. » This bridegroom has [0571A] breasts; he who is also called king for reverence' sake has breasts — holy men cleaving to him with the heart. The breasts are fixed in the narrow part of the bosom, and they draw nourishment for what is without, unto those whom he has nourished abroad. Holy men, therefore, are the breasts of the bridegroom, because they draw from the inmost things and nourish outwardly. His breasts are the apostles and all the preachers of the Church: the wine, as we also said above, was in the prophets, the wine was in the law. But because fuller commandments were given through the apostles than had been given through the prophets, she says: « Mindful of thy breasts above wine; » Because they who can fulfill those things which are commanded in the New Testament, they without doubt transcend the knowledge of the law. Yet this we can also understand otherwise: « Mindful of thy breasts above wine; » there are many who indeed have the wine [0571B] of wisdom, but have not the knowledge of humility. This knowledge puffs up, because charity does not build up. [var: Vulg. *scientia inflat, caritas vero aedificat*, "knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth"] But there are many who so have the wine of knowledge that they know how to consider the gifts of doctrine. For the gifts of spiritual grace are like certain teats in the bosom, which subtly minister to spiritual men by hidden passages and nourish them. Therefore she says: « Mindful of thy breasts above wine. » Because these, who know how to remember the gifts of thy grace, so as not to ascribe to themselves what they are wise in, and are not lifted up, on account of the same wisdom which they have received, above those who are lifted up and puffed up on account of their own wisdom. For it is more to be wise humbly than to be wise proudly. For neither is it truly to be wise, not to be wise humbly; she says: « Mindful therefore of thy breasts above wine. » Because those who know how to consider [0571C] the gifts of spiritual grace transcend those who indeed have knowledge, but have not in memory the knowing of the gifts. Openly, therefore, is it to say: « Mindful therefore of thy breasts above wine, » because humility is stronger than knowledge. Wine is the knowledge which inebriates the memory of the breasts, which inebriates him whom it calls back to the knowing of the gifts. Let her say: « Mindful therefore of thy breasts above wine, » because humility conquers the abundance of knowledge. The upright love thee. No one, he says, loves thee save the upright. And no one is upright, save he who loves thee. For everyone who does good works through fear, even if he is perfect in the work, is not upright in desire. For he would wish that there were not that which he fears, so that he might not do good works. But he who does good works through love is upright both in work and in desire. [0571D] But the sweetness of love is hidden from those who fear. Whence it is written: « How great is the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee, and hast perfected them for them that hope in thee [n: (Ps. XXX)]. » [var: Vulg. *perfecisti eis qui sperant in te*, "thou hast perfected [it] for them that hope in thee"] For sweetness is unknown to those who fear God; to those who love, it becomes known. He therefore who has striven through love to be upright, his love is perfect, so that he does not fear the judge when he comes, so that whatever he has heard of eternal punishments, he does not dread. Whence Paul also, while he awaited the coming of the judge, while he sought the rewards of eternal life, said: « Which God hath prepared not only for me, but for all who love his coming [n: (II Tim. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. II Tim. IV, 8 *non solum autem mihi, sed et his qui diligunt adventum eius*, "and not only to me, but to them also that love his coming"; the opening *Quae praeparavit Deus* is not in that verse] For eternal rewards are prepared for those who love the judge, because everyone who knows that he does [0572A] evil works fears the judge when he comes. But he who presumes upon his own works seeks the coming of the judge. Rewards therefore are prepared for those who await the coming of God and love his coming, because they do not love the coming of the judge save as presuming upon their own cause. But all certitude of uprightness is in love, and therefore it is rightly said: The upright love thee. It follows:
