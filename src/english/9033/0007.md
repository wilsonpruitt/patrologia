## CHAPTER THE FIRST. (cont.)

« I am black, but comely, O daughter of Jerusalem, as the tabernacles of Cedar, as the skins of Solomon. Do not consider me that, » etc. [var: Vulg. *filiae Hierusalem*, plural, "O ye daughters of Jerusalem"] We know that in the first beginnings of the Church, when the grace of our Redeemer had been preached, some out of Judea believed, others did not believe. But those who believed were despised by the unbelieving and suffered persecution, and were judged to have departed, as it were, into the way [0572B] of the Gentiles. Whence the Church cries out in those same ones against those who were not converted. « I am black, but comely, O daughter of Jerusalem. » Black indeed by a true judgment, but comely through the illumination of grace. How black? « As the tabernacles of Cedar. » For Cedar was the second of the stock of Ishmael, and the tabernacles of Cedar were Esau's. How then black? As the tabernacles of Cedar. Because in your sight I have been judged after the likeness of the Gentiles, that is, after the likeness of sinners. How then comely? « As the skins of Solomon. » It is reported that Solomon, when he built the temple, covered all the vessels of the temple with skins. But doubtless the skins of Solomon could be comely, which were fitted to the tabernacle. Which, if they were in his tabernacle, could not but be comely [0572C] for the service of the king. But because Solomon is interpreted *peaceable,* we understand him to be the true Solomon himself, because all souls cleaving to God are the skins of Solomon, mortifying themselves and bringing themselves back into the service of the king of peace. But in your judgment I am as the tabernacles of Cedar, because I am judged to have departed, as it were, into the way of the Gentiles; but according to the truth I am as the skins of Solomon, because I cleave unto the service of the king. Do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discolored me. That part which had not believed regarded as a sinner that part which had believed in Christ. But let this one say:

« Do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discolored me. » The sun is the Lord himself; he himself, coming, discolored me; by his precepts he showed that I was not fair [0572D] in the precepts of the law. Whom the sun touches more closely, him it has discolored: so also the Lord, coming, discolored him whom through his grace he touched more strictly, because the more we draw near to grace, the more we know ourselves to be sinners. Let us see Paul, coming out of Judea, discolored in the sun. But if, wishing to be justified in Christ, we ourselves also have been found sinners. He who finds himself a sinner in Christ is found discolored in the sun. But behold, that part which believed out of Judea suffered persecution from the unbelieving Jews, afflicted with many tribulations and pressed. Whence it follows: « The sons of my mother have fought against me. » Because the sons of the synagogue who remained in unbelief waged a war of persecution against the faithful [0573A] of the synagogue. But while that part which came out of Judea to the faith suffers persecution, it departed to the preaching of the Gentiles. Whence it follows: « They have set me keeper for the vineyards. My vineyard I have not kept. » Because, while those who are in Judea persecute me, they have made me a keeper in the churches. « My vineyard I have not kept, » because I forsook Judea. Whence Paul also says: « To you the word of God had been sent, but because you have judged yourselves unworthy, behold we turn to the Gentiles [n: (Act. XIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Act. XIII, 46 *vobis oportebat primum loqui verbum Dei; sed quoniam repellitis illud et indignos vos iudicastis aeternae vitae*, "to you it behoved us first to speak the word of God: but because you reject it, and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life"] As if he should say: We wished to keep our own vineyard, but because you yourselves have rejected us, you send us over to the keeping of other men's vineyards. These things which we have said of the Synagogue converted to the faith, let us now say of the Church called out of the Gentiles to the faith. « I am black, but comely, O daughter of Jerusalem. » [0573B] The Church, coming out of the Gentiles, considers the souls of the faithful which she found, which she also calls daughters of Jerusalem. For Jerusalem is called *the vision of peace.* She considers what she was, considers what she has become, and confesses her past faults, that she be not proud. She confesses her present life, that she be not ungrateful, and says: « I am black, but comely. » Black through merit, comely through grace. Black through her past life, comely through the manner of life that follows. How black? As the tabernacles of Cedar. For the tabernacles of Cedar were the tabernacles of the Gentiles, they were the tabernacles of darkness. And to the Gentiles it was said: « You were heretofore darkness, but now light in the Lord [n: (Eph. V)]. » How comely? As the skins of Solomon. We have been macerated through penance; and being mortified through penance, [0573C] as a skin it is led into the service of the king. All who afflict themselves through penance make themselves members of Christ. The members of Christ, therefore, afflicted through penance, are the skins of Solomon, because they become mortified flesh. But behold, there were faithful in Judea who disdained to come to the faith of the Gentiles. Whence they rebuke Peter also, that he had received Cornelius. Whence here too the Church of the Gentiles subjoins: « Do not consider me that I am brown. » Do not despise the heathenism of my unbelief, do not despise my former sins, do not regard what I was. Why? « because the sun hath discolored me. » For the sun discolors him upon whom it presses itself more closely and more strictly. God, when he holds strict judgment, when he shows forth his strictness more fully, also discolors, while he shines [0573D] more fully, because while he exercises his strictness more subtly, he strictly adjudges. For the sun as it were holds back its rays when it mercifully desires our works. As it were strictly it shows forth its power when it strictly weighs our works. Let the Church therefore say: Thence am I brown, thence a sinner, because the sun has discolored me, because when my Creator forsook me, I fell through sorrow. But thou, O afflicted one so forsaken, what hast thou merited?

What hast thou obtained from the gift? The sons of my mother have fought against me. The sons of the mother are the apostles. For the mother of men is the Jerusalem above. They fought against the Church, when out of unbelief, in the faith of their preaching, they pierced her through as with certain lances. Whence Paul also, as a certain fighter, says [0574A] [n: (II Thess. II)]. « Destroying the counsels of thoughts, and every height that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God. » [var: Vulg. II Cor. X, 5 *consilia destruentes*, without *cogitationum*] He who destroys height is assuredly a fighter; these fighters, therefore, these sons of the mother Jerusalem, warred down the Church from her error, that they might found her unto justice: « The sons of my mother have fought against me. » And what did they do in fighting? « They have set me keeper in the vineyards. » The vineyards of the Church are the virtues which bear fruit, because while the vices fight it out within me, they storm as it were my own evils within me. They gave me fruitfulness and the pursuits of the virtues; they made me keeper in the vineyards, that I might bring forth fruitfulness. After the storming, let her say specially: « My vineyard I have not kept. » The vineyard of the Church is the ancient habit of error. Which, while one is set as keeper unto the virtues, he forsakes — [0574B] that vineyard, that ancient habit of his error. We have spoken of the heathen world converted; let us now speak generally of the whole Church together, and specially of what is to be thought concerning each single soul. Depraved hearers are wont to consider their teachers not as what they are, but as what they were. But sound teachers both confess what they were and bring forward what they are, so that they neither hide that they were sinners, nor again, like ungrateful men, deny the gifts. Let the Church therefore say in these. « I am black, but comely. » Black through myself, comely through the gift. Black from the past; comely, from that I have been made so in things to come. How black? how comely? Black « as the tabernacles of Cedar, comely as the skins of Solomon. » And it is not just that anyone be weighed from his past life, and that there be not more regarded what he was, but what he is. Whence [0574C] she subjoins: « Do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discolored me. » Sometimes in Holy Scripture the sun is put for the excessive heat of earthly desires. Whence then brown? Because the sun hath discolored me. Thus it is understood: Do not consider that I was black before through unbelief, because the sun, Christ — of whom it is written: « But unto you that fear my name the sun of justice shall arise [n: (Matth. IV)], » — has discolored me through faith. « The sons of my mother have fought against me. » In all creation two rational creatures were founded, the human and the angelic. The angel fell; he persuaded man. For the mother of every creature is the kindness and the power of God. We therefore and the angels, from the fact that we were founded rational, have as it were a certain fellowship of brotherhood, because the angels were founded by the same power by which we also [0574D] were. Yet because the falling angels daily wage war against us, she says: « The sons of my mother have fought against me. » Behold, while these spirits, reasons, fight — these spirits, the sons of the mother, while they fight against the soul, make it lean upon earthly things, be at leisure for the actions of the world, seek transitory things. Whence she subjoins: « They have set me keeper in the vineyards: the vineyard I have not kept. » For the vineyards are earthly actions. As if she should say: In earthly actions they have set me as keeper. And what? My vineyard, that is, my soul, my life, my mind, I have neglected to keep; because, while outwardly I was wrapped up in the action of earthly things, I fell away from the inward keeping. Very many consider themselves from that which is beside them, [0575A] not from that which they are. Beside them are dignities, beside them are the inner ministries. And while they keep what they have beside them, they neglect to keep themselves. Let her say therefore: « They have set me keeper in the vineyards, my vineyard I have not kept, » that is: while by an outward keeping I serve in secular actions, I have lost the solicitude of the inward keeping.
