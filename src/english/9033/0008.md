## CHAPTER THE FIRST. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday. » At midday the sun is more fervent; everyone who is fervent in faith is fervent in love. ⟦s2⟧ This bridegroom, who below is called a fawn, feeds in their heart with the greenness of virtues; she says: « Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday. » [0575B] Why she thus asks where he feeds, where he lies, she has given the cause of her inquiry. ⟦s3⟧ « Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » God's companions are his familiar friends, such as are all who live well. ⟦s4⟧ But many appear to be companions, and are not. ⟦s5⟧ For many teachers, while a perverse doctrine was persuading, seemed indeed companions, but proved enemies. ⟦s6⟧ While Arius, Sabellius, Montanus was a teacher, they seemed as it were companions; but when they were strictly examined, they appeared enemies. ⟦s7⟧ Let her say therefore: « Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » As if she should say: Show me in whose hearts thou truly restest, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of those who seem to be thy companions, that is, who are believed to be thy familiars, and are not. ⟦s8⟧ All [0575C] priests, all teachers are companions as to appearance; but as to life, many are not companions, but are adversaries. ⟦s9⟧ For many little ones, faithful within the Church, desire to live well and wish to hold to a life of uprightness; they consider the life of the priests who are set over them, and while those priests themselves do not live well, those who follow slip away into error. ⟦s10⟧ Whence the Church, as it were in those same little ones and faithful, says: « Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday, » that is: show me the life of those who truly serve thee, that I may know where thou feedest with the greenness of virtues, that I may know where thou liest in the midday, that is, restest in the fervor of charity, lest, while I look upon the flocks of thy companions, I myself begin to wander, not knowing whose words I ought to believe, [0575D] whose teaching I ought to use. ⟦s11⟧ Behold, the words of the bridegroom are returned to the bride.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women, go forth, and go after the steps of thy flocks, and feed thy kids beside the tabernacles of the shepherds. » Every soul ought to care for nothing more than that it know itself. ⟦s2⟧ For he who knows himself has known that he was made to the image of God. ⟦s3⟧ If he was made to the image of God, he ought not to follow the likeness of beasts, nor to be rolled down either in luxury or in present appetite. ⟦s4⟧ Of which ignorance it is said elsewhere: « Man, when he was in honor, did not understand; he was compared to senseless beasts, and was made like to them [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]. » The steps of the flocks are the actions of the peoples. [0576A] Which, by how much they are many, by so much are they hindered, by so much perverse. ⟦s5⟧ Let it therefore be said to the Church: « If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women, go forth and go after the steps of the flocks and feed thy kids beside the tabernacles of the shepherds. » O thou who wast foul through ignorance, through faith thou art made fair among the souls of others. ⟦s6⟧ Which is evidently said to the Church of the elect. ⟦s7⟧ If thou know not thyself, that is, if thou art ignorant of this very thing, that thou wast made to my image, go forth, go outside. ⟦s8⟧ But if thou knowest not, thou hast been made something, go forth and go after the steps of the flocks. ⟦s9⟧ Follow not my examples, but the examples of the peoples. ⟦s10⟧ « And feed thy kids beside the tabernacles of the shepherds. » Our kids are carnal motions; the kids are our unlawful temptations. ⟦s11⟧ « Go after the steps of the flocks, » that is, depart after [0576B] the examples of the peoples. ⟦s12⟧ « And feed thy kids, » that is, nourish carnal motions — no longer spiritual senses, but carnal motions. ⟦s13⟧ Thou wouldst go beside the tabernacles of the shepherds, or to feed lambs, that is, in the doctrines of masters, in the doctrines of the apostles, in the doctrines of the prophets. ⟦s14⟧ Or if thou feedest kids, feed them beside the tabernacles of the shepherds, that thou mayest be called Christian by faith and not by works, because thou art seen to be within through faith, and not within through works. ⟦s15⟧ Behold, thou hast rebuked; behold, thou hast reproved. ⟦s16⟧ For what dost thou not say. ⟦s17⟧ What hast thou kindly wrought in her? ⟦s18⟧ Say it plainly. ⟦s19⟧ For it follows:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « To my cavalry among the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, O my love. » All who serve luxury, who serve pride, who serve avarice, who serve envy, who serve deceit, are still under the chariot of Pharaoh, that is, [0576C] under the rule of the devil, who are as it were under the chariot of Pharaoh. ⟦s2⟧ But everyone who is fervent in humility, in chastity, in doctrine, in charity has already been made a horse of our Creator, is already set in the chariot of God, already has God for his rider. ⟦s3⟧ Whence to a certain one over whom the Lord presided it is said: « It is hard for thee to kick against the goad [n: (Act. IX)]. » As if he should say: Thou art my horse; now thou canst not fling thy heels against me; now I preside over thee. ⟦s4⟧ Of these horses it is said elsewhere: « Thou hast sent thy horses into the sea, troubling many waters [n: (Habac. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Habac. III, 15 *viam fecisti in mari equis tuis, in luto aquarum multarum*, "thou madest a way in the sea for thy horses, in the mud of many waters"] God therefore has a chariot, because he presides over holy souls and runs about everywhere through holy souls. ⟦s5⟧ Whence it is written: « The chariot of God is ten thousand [n: (Psal. LXVII)], » and the rest. ⟦s6⟧ Pharaoh nevertheless has chariots, which chariots nevertheless were sunk in [0576D] the Red Sea. ⟦s7⟧ The bridegroom therefore says: « To my cavalry among the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee. » While thou wast still serving devilish works, I likened thee to my cavalry, because I regarded what I had done in thee through predestination, and compared thee to my horses. ⟦s8⟧ Where the hidden judgments of God are to be considered: how many seem, through preaching, through wisdom, through chastity, through liberality, through long-suffering, to be horses of God, and yet by God's hidden judgment are likened to the horses of Pharaoh. ⟦s9⟧ And many seem, through avarice, through pride, through envy, through luxury, to be horses of Pharaoh, and yet by God's hidden judgment are likened to the horses of God, because he sees the former turned from good to evils, and sees the latter brought back [0577A] from evil to good. ⟦s10⟧ For as through predestination many who seem to be horses of God are horses of Pharaoh through the reprobate life which follows them, so also through piety many who seem to be horses of Pharaoh are, through the holy life which they will keep at their end, likened to the horses of God. ⟦s11⟧ Whence he speaks flatteringly and says: « To my cavalry among the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, O my love, » that is: thou wast still serving the vices among the chariots of Pharaoh, thou wast running under the vices, but I regarded what I have done concerning thee through predestination. ⟦s12⟧ To my cavalry have I likened thee, that is, I regarded thee as like to my elect. ⟦s13⟧ Behold, at last thou perceivest, magnificent Caesar, what we said beforehand in the introduction: that nothing is found historically in this volume, but all things are redolent with the allusions of allegories, and are fragrant with the runnings-through of dramas [0577B] and with the sweetnesses of moral senses. ⟦s14⟧ Wherefore I greatly desire to persuade, nay rather to suggest, though rashly, to the summit of your excellence, that, since you have obtained a great wisdom of skill and have attained the defense and protection of holy Church, you should not only, set in readiness for wars by the aid of arms, fenced about with wedges of nobles and thronged with troops of soldiers, protect her from the incursions of the foreign nations of adversaries, but also persist devoted and diligent in sacred doctrine and in the understanding of the divine Scriptures, so that, fortified by the Gospels, girded with the swords of the divine Scriptures, adorned with the darts of sentences, you may be able to defend that same holy Church among your subjects from the shields, that is to say, of the inward malignant spirits: who always seek to extinguish not the bodies, [0577C] but the souls of the faithful. ⟦s15⟧ Whence Peter exhorts, saying: « Watch, because your adversary the devil goeth about as a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour; whom resist ye, strong in faith [n: (I Petr. V)]. » And that your nobility may learnedly be able both to beware of the snares of the ancient enemy and to preach them to others, be busy to study the expositions of this volume and of other books at fitting hours after the ordering of the empire, so that she may deservedly and with rejoicing be able to say what is read here: « The king brought me into the wine cellar, he set charity in order in me. » And that: « Show me where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions, » so that the bridegroom himself, namely the Lord who below is called *a fawn,* may deign, through the keeping of thyself and the vigilance of others [0577D] and the understanding of the sacred Scriptures, in some manner to feed in you with the greenness of virtues, and to lie down in the fervor of charity. ⟦s16⟧ It follows:
