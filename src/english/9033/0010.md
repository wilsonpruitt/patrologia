## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

« A cluster of cypress is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi. » Well does he say: « A cluster of cypress is my beloved to me, » as if he cried out openly: He who was a bundle of myrrh in the bitterness and sadness of the passion, the same is a cluster of cypress in the sweetness of the resurrection; myrrh signifies sadness, wine gladness: because, namely, Christ, begotten of the Jews according to the flesh, and hung by them upon the wood of the cross like a cluster, yet on the third day after the winepress of death showed the gladness of the resurrection to his disciples. And well « in the vineyards of Engaddi, » on account of the gifts of graces, which after the resurrection he lavished in baptism upon those who share in them. For Engaddi is called *fount of the kid,* or [0581B] *eye of the kid,* signifying, namely, baptism, where out of kids lambs are made. But in that it is interpreted *eye* [sic: *haeai*], it hints at Christ himself, who is the light of all sinners who come to him. Behold, for brevity's sake these things have been said concisely, that they may the more quickly be impressed upon my reader's mind. But because holy Church desires in her members to imitate the mystery of Christ's passion, that she may come with Christ to the glory of the resurrection, and may deserve to be set apart not with the kids on the left, but with the sheep on the right, let us speak more fully. Finally, when she had said, « a bundle of myrrh is my beloved, » she added to me: she understood that he had suffered for her salvation, and had risen from the dead for her glory. For she says: « A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me; he shall abide between my breasts. » [0581C] For myrrh is interpreted *bitter,* which signifies in itself the saving discipline which the Church received in Christ; and as she, for his name, endures in her diverse members the diverse bitternesses of torments, Christ is made for her a bundle of myrrh. The tree of myrrh, therefore, from which is begotten a juice that will profit many infirmities of the body, is like the willow; and it is asserted by those who have described the natures of trees that it puts forth little branches with very fine and twisted twigs after the manner of little bundles. This aforesaid Church, therefore, or any holy soul whatever, when through the hearing of eternal life she receives the sweetness of taste at the coming of the bridegroom, does not feel the manifold and bitter sufferings brought upon her — that is, prisons, exiles, proscriptions, the stripping of present goods, hunger, [0581D] thirst, chains, unjust slaughters, reproaches, perils, shipwrecks, showers of stones, the headlong casting into the deep, and the stings of various punishments, and many other things. When she has undergone these for the death of her Redeemer, with great gladness she says: « A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me. » When therefore, for the healing of the many infirmities inflicted by the devil, she has patiently borne many chastisements from God, or has with joy sustained the diverse sufferings brought upon her for his name, as was said above, Christ is made her bundle of myrrh, for the driving out of all the diseases of the vices from her breast. Then, now clean and made ready with prayers, she is a divine lodging, so that now with such great confidence she invites Christ himself into the [0582A] recess of her heart, as though within her breasts, saying in the Gospel: « I and the Father will come, and will make our abode with him [n: (Joan. XIV.)] » [var: Vulg. *ad eum veniemus*, "we will come to him" — the received text does not name the speakers here] Or also the Church rejoices that he abides between her breasts, among those men without doubt who, according to their age or according to their capacity, nourish little ones with the spiritual milk of their teaching. Of whom the Lord himself says: « If two or three shall have agreed upon earth, whatsoever they shall ask in my name shall be done for them. » [var: Vulg. Matt. XVIII, 19 *si duo ex vobis consenserint super terram de omni re quamcumque petierint fiet illis a Patre meo*, "if two of you shall consent upon earth, concerning anything whatsoever they shall ask, it shall be done to them by my Father"] And: « Where two or three shall have been gathered together in my name, I am in the midst of them [n: (Matth. XVIII)]. » One of whom said out of his own breasts to his hearers: « I gave you milk to drink, not meat [n: (I Cor. III)]. » There can also, if it please anyone, be understood as the two breasts of the Church Moses and Elias, in whose midst Jesus, transfigured on the mountain, the beloved [0582B] of the Church, is proved to have abode unto the future glory of that same Church: of whom the one showed him promised, the other proclaimed that he had already come, in signification of the two Testaments. Whence it is said: In the midst of two living creatures thou shalt be known. [var: Vet. Lat. reading of Hab. III, 2; Vulg. *in medio annorum vivifica illud*, "in the midst of the years bring it to life"] Which two Testaments are the breasts of the Church. In whose midst Christ abides. And because Moses the lawgiver, and Elias the chief of the prophets, were the ones speaking of the departure of his passion, therefore there follows: « A cluster of Cyprus is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi. » Before all lands, as it is reported, the island of Cyprus is attested to beget clusters of grapes of wondrous size. Of that size was the cluster which is read to have been carried by two young men out of the land of promise into the desert, and which in every way marked the figure of the assumed [0582C] manhood of Christ our Redeemer: which indeed, according to place, or time, or occasion, has been made the food and drink of the Church through the sacrament of his body and blood. But Cyprus is interpreted *sadness* or *mourning.* Engaddi, as has been said, *fount of the kid.* He is therefore a cluster of Cyprus to his friend the Church, while he sows in the minds of sinners the sadness, or the mourning, of penance, that he may lift them up to that blessedness of which he himself said in the Gospel: « Blessed are they that mourn now, for they shall be comforted [n: (Matth. V)]. » [var: Vulg. *Beati qui lugent, quoniam ipsi consolabuntur*, without *nunc*, "now"] Who, like that cluster of which we spoke, was lifted up by two peoples — the Jew, namely, as persecutor, and the Roman soldier in command — by the phalanx onto the gibbet of the cross. Who to this day, through sadness and mourning, has begotten for the Church a life conferring eternity, as has been said before, in [0582D] those who, after countless heaps of crimes, are gathered into the bosom of the Church. But the aforesaid cluster daily supplies spiritual food and drink to those who believe in him, to those at least who are gathered in the vineyards of Engaddi, where is the fount of the kid, which is the fount of the most holy baptism, where the kids go down who were to be assigned to the left hand before the tribunal of the judge, to be handed over to eternal fire, and there ascend spotless lambs, to be gathered at the judge's right hand, to be granted the eternal kingdom. It is certain therefore that, just as the one Jewish nation is named the vineyard of the Lord through the knowledge of the divine law, so also the diverse nations brought to the faith of Christ through the husbandry of the apostles and the teaching of the doctors are called many vineyards. In the midst of which, as has been said, [0583A] is the fount of the water of holy baptism, to which the kids come, that they may be made lambs. Which vineyards of Engaddi beget not wine, but balsams. Engaddi is a very great village in the land of Judaea, next to the Dead Sea. Which land holds the figure of the land of the living, where are the vineyards of balsam, which of old were only within the bounds of twenty iugera. After the Romans got possession of that same region, it was propagated also over the broadest hills — in stock like the vine, in leaves like rue, but whiter and always remaining. But the tree is balsam; the wood is called *chirobalsamum,* that is, its little branch; the fruit or seed, *carpobalsamum*; the juice, *opobalsamum*. Which is therefore signified with an addition, because the bark of the wood, struck with iron claws, distils through its caverns a drop of surpassing fragrance. For a cavern [0583B] in the Greek tongue is called ὀρὴ. Its drop they adulterate by mixing in oil of cyprus or honey. But balsams, if they be pure, have such force that, if the sun grow hot, they cannot be held in the hand. From which vineyards many kings have transplanted little balsam trees into other places or lands — which the Romans too are read to have done — and they were utterly unable to take root or to live. Whence it came about, as we hinted above, that in that same place, or within those same bounds, great zeal was applied by kings in planting vineyards that beget balsams. And what the cause may be, that in no other soil do the vineyards come forth save in Engaddi alone, is worth the labor of understanding. I think, as a certain one of the doctors says, that this juice of these vineyards is to be understood to hold the type of the confession of the one faith, in which the hope [0583C] of the faithful stands, in which each good medicinal fragrance of Christ, restoring life to dead souls, is proved to germinate. From which confession, whatsoever nation or soul shall have been plucked away or transplanted by evil husbandmen or by heretics, withers. Which is proved to have befallen many souls, which, torn away from the one most true aforesaid confession of faith and dried up, offered themselves as fodder for gehenna. For just as the body cannot subsist without food and drink, so it is declared by the voice of the Church that without the cluster which is Christ, the eternal life coming down from heaven, it cannot live; because in no other land of promise, in no other vineyard save in the vineyards of Engaddi is he found by those who hunger and thirst after justice — that is, in no congregation of heretics, in no sect of philosophers, in no [0583D] flattery of schismatics, where, if anyone be transplanted, deceived by the report of his own perversity, he perishes withered. Understanding these things indeed, the Church says: « A cluster of Cyprus is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi. » There follows:

« Behold thou art fair, my friend, behold thou art fair: thy eyes are of doves. » After the comeliness of the cheeks and of the neck, by rendering the bride her turn, the beauty of the eyes is praised in the simplicity of her doctors, and by a doubled proclamation kept hidden. Both the beauty of the whole body, and the eyes, are compared to the eyes of doves. And they are compared to that creature which, in gentleness, in fruitfulness, in keenness of sight, in swiftness of wings, is found more excellent [0584A] than all winged living things. The eyes therefore are proved to be the guides of the whole body, without which either the body cannot advance, or it advances only scarcely, by stumbling. In them are understood the holy doctors, contemplating heavenly things with spiritual sight, who for their simplicity and innocence are compared to doves. There can also be understood, in right order, the priests, to whom the most holy mysteries have been committed, in whose faith the Church is adorned, in whose unspotted life she is exalted with frequent praises. For she is fair in exhibiting to God the purest knowledge; fair in showing to enemies and to those estranged from the faith of Christ a blameless manner of life. A third praise of beauty is also brought in, in the keenness of the eyes. For the nature of doves, beyond those things we have said, is proved to have this peculiar thing also, [0584B] that it watches for the coming of the enemy behind it from afar, before it in the water — the coming, namely, of the hawk — and considers lest he be able to snatch away her young. So without doubt the holy doctors, endowed with simplicity and innocence; so the priests of the Church, contemplate heavenly things with the keenness of their contemplation. Meditating, they sit upon the streams of the Scriptures, and they busy themselves to declare to their hearers, by the admonition of their words, the snares of the devil, who hastens to snatch away the souls of believers, that they may beware; and therefore in such the beauty of the Church is praised by the bridegroom.
