## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

« Behold thou art fair, my beloved, and comely; our little bed is flowering. » The voice of the Church to Christ. [0584C] As if she said: Thou alone art fair; I am fair from thee. And because she understands that she was justified not by her own merits, and that she received the beauty of the virtues from him, being kindled in his love, she praises his beauty. No one can praise the comeliness of anyone whom he loves not at all, nor can he love him with whose ways he does not agree, as the Apostle teaches, saying: « No man, he says, speaking in the Spirit of God, saith anathema to Jesus. And no one saith the Lord Jesus, but in the Holy Spirit [n: (I Cor. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. *nemo potest dicere Dominus Iesus*, "no man can say the Lord Jesus"] He therefore can both see the beauty and the form of the beloved Son of God, and extol it with praise, who by holy works has kept his Spirit in his own mind; and therefore in the present place, the Church, full of the Spirit of truth, as the bridegroom himself showed above, saying: « Behold thou art fair, [0584D] my friend, » — he who in the likeness of a dove came down from heaven upon the assumed man in the Jordan — and recognizing the beauty of his body without sin, and the comeliness of his true soul without lie and guile, utters in reciprocal and doubled praise, saying: « Behold thou art fair, my beloved, and comely; our little bed is flowering [n: (Ephes. V)]. » Although, namely, a comely face or a costly garment may show the bride her costly bridegroom set at a distance, yet until the joining of the little bed she does not know the comeliness of the rest of the members. But when the lawful joining has been celebrated, and the two have been made one flesh according to the sentence of the first man — which sacrament master Paul asserted was carried through in Christ and in the Church — so also [0585A] the aforesaid friend, up to the day of the burial, loved him indeed by desiring his coming, and praised him seen and kissed; but nevertheless all the love, and all the form of his comeliness, is proved to have reached, from the time of the resurrection up to the appointed term, as far as the shedding of blood for his name. For the flowering bed, that is, one strewn with flowers, where she recognized his beauty and comeliness in all his members, is understood by the Church, spoken in riddles, as the sepulchre of the Lord, where the body which the Word of the Father had assumed is proved to have lain. And the bed is with just reason proclaimed flowering, in which the spices, or the aloes, which are composed by the perfumer's art out of the juices or flowers of many kinds of herbs, were brought in with the body of Christ, and with which, when he rose again, the sepulchre [0585B] was besprinkled. In whose teaching the Church, that is, the throng of the faithful, found liberty; in death, life; in burial, silence; in hard labors, rest — which labors, driven by the scourges of the demons, she was daily discharging; where the true man is known to have dwelt, by the enclosing of his body, under the seal of the impious, and the true God to have gone forth by rising again from the sepulchre that had been marked. Where, under the name of the little bed, the joining of the Son of God and of the Church was celebrated; where with him the Church deserved to find the delightful sleep of the passion and the joy of the eternal waking, while the Apostle cries out to her members: « If you be dead with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is at the right hand of God the Father [n: (Colos. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Col. III, 1 *si consurrexistis cum Christo*, "if you be risen with Christ", and *in dextera Dei sedens*, "sitting at the right hand of God"] For his resurrection both the minds of men are daily prepared for the Holy Spirit to dwell in, and [0585C] the roofs of the congregations of believers; and even visibly, how innumerable houses of God and churches are enlarged without ceasing at every time, and of what things or from what materials they are built, or how durable they are, or how comely, he taught by saying:

« The beams of our houses are of cedar, the ceilings of cypress. » But it is to be known that certain of the doctors understand by *the flowering bed* the peace of holy Church, in which, secure from the tumult of the vices and from the dominion of the malignant spirits, she rests in Christ, and flowers with holy virtues, and is multiplied with spiritual flower. But let us, as we began, follow out the former sense. By the little bed we understand the sepulchre of the Lord, where his flesh, through the slumber of death, rested as though in a little bed, [0585D] which truly flowered through the glory of the resurrection. For the divine word says: « The beams of our houses are of cedar, our ceilings of cypress. » The beams and the ceilings are, it may be, the doctors and the hearers, or the hearts of the elect, on account of their strength and comeliness; of cedar and of cypress he says, on account of their surpassing virtues, the fragrance of a good life and of incorruptions, in which Christ rests as under a roof. And, that it may lie open more plainly, let us speak by an example: The nature of the cedars is said always to grow, nor is it ever suffered to pass into old age, according to that saying of the Psalmist: « The just shall flourish like the palm tree, and shall be multiplied like the cedar of Libanus [n: (Psal. XCI)]. » The juice of which trees, according to the opinions of the physicians, is of such virtue that it heals the diverse [0586A] members of bodies, kills the worms of ulcers, and, when bodies are anointed with it, defends them from the stings of flies. With the wood of which tree, together with living water and a bundle of hyssop bound with scarlet in an earthen vessel, the cleansing of sins by sprinkling is celebrated in the Old Testament. Which is now taught to be celebrated in truth through the priest, through baptism, and through the blood of Christ, who, defending the multitudes of the one faith, encompassed by that bundle, from the assault of the devil, is taught to be celebrated in the eternal and in the temporal remission of sins. The houses, namely, of the Son of God and of the Church — the little assemblies of the Christian people, where the aforesaid redemption of souls is celebrated — are known to be in the whole world. Which houses are proved to be built out of the doctors, nay rather by the doctors, that is, by the hewn iron tools of their teaching, in whose minds the Lord deigns to dwell. The very incorruptible cedar timbers [0586B] of the aforesaid houses are understood to be those who do not perish from their strength of holiness by the most foolish suggestions of wicked thoughts, and, always forgetting the things that are past, grow daily without intermission, like cedars, toward the good works that are before them — that is, they are multiplied by the fruits of the saints unto the help of gains for holy souls; the juice of whose speech or of whose consolation defends souls from the stings of the demons, and heals the wounds of the habits of filthy misdeeds long since inflicted, and destroys the worms of the most wicked thoughts. In which, worthily, for the delight of those dwelling there, the cypress ceilings, fixed for the faithful, rightly hang down. For the wood of the cypress, planed by the craftsman's hand, affords a comely [0586C] appearance, and gives back a most pleasant odor to him; it is proved to contain in itself the virtue of medicine for many sufferings of the body. Which things are understood to express chastity, modesty, obedience, humility, charity and mercy — the pattern of the hearers who dwell there, the honor of the priests who venerate — who hang unceasingly upon the word of the doctors, who, after the manner of a vault, by solacing out of their just labors the doctors of the present time, deck the aforesaid ceilings joined to the beams of the houses and cover their nakedness. For the glory of the doctors is the people, and likewise the glory of the people is the firm establishing of the doctors: while the one impart spiritual gifts to the people, and the people carnal offerings to them, glorious houses are prepared for Christ; and the flowers of heavenly wisdom, and lilies of integrity, through their [0586D] penance, the Lord himself bestows upon the aforesaid houses unto the great delightfulness of honor.
