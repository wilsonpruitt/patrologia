## CHAPTER II.

« I am the flower of the field, and the lily of the valleys. » As if it were said openly: I am the glory of the world and the glory of the humble. Although hell too may be understood, by way of surplus, and the place where Christ deigned to descend to rescue the patriarchs, as certain men understand it. But let us, these things having been tasted beforehand, lest we be charged with having been ignorant of the meaning of certain men, speak more richly, setting down the words of blessed Ambrose. Before his incarnation, he says, the Savior, speaking in the person of an angel to the patriarchs and to the prophets, alone, to whom none is like among the angels, the thrones and the dominations, to be marveled at by all, is proved to be the singular flower glittering in the whole field of the heavens. [0587A] Which portion of the fragrance of his knowledge the patriarchs or the prophets bore as they were able, and the world itself always admonished mortals by teaching. All knowledge, through the prophets, through the powers of signs — by slaying the impious through the waters of the deluge, or the Sodomites through fire — whereby the unwise might understand wisdom, and the foolish proud might at some time have sense that a loftier judge abides above them, paying out just vengeance. But when through the sacrament of the incarnation he came down hither into the valley of tears, among the thorns and the fellowships of sinners, he testifies that he was made a lily. In which valley what else was rioting but the forests of thorns — the foulest religion of idolatry, of envy, of thefts and murders, [0587B] of soothsaying and auguries, or of fornications, or of magic arts? In the midst of which Christ, shown forth in the fragrance of his teaching and in the whiteness of his example, daily sets free the throng of believers. Three things therefore delightful in themselves and exceedingly pleasing to the eyes the nature of the lily is proved to contain, that is, whiteness, fragrance, and a medicine when burnt with fire. Which three wonderful things the Lord is known to have brought by like reason into this valley of the world, that is, the abolishing of sin, the wiping away of the lie, the tempering refreshment of the generative fires, from which is born the love of preserving virginity. And whosoever in either sex restrain themselves from such things are kindled by the examples of the glorious assumed manhood and of the blessed Virgin Mary. And thus he is called the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys [0587C] through the assumption of an immaculate body. Which sacrament was for the freeing of his people, whereby he might heal us in his diverse members from our diverse languors; and the wounds which had been inflicted by the demons with the fiery darts of fornications might be healed by his teaching; and those which with the keenness of wickedness were wounding his friend in the prophets and the doctors might be turned into the pleasantness of lilies, from which the Church, born again, shines out — and she is pricked with the most bitter goads, as he himself says in what follows:

« As the lily among thorns, so is my friend among the daughters. » These friends, therefore, among whom the Church stands as a lily among thorns, although it is not said with distinctness whose daughters they are, yet from the fact that they are compared to thorns we can perceive that they are called the offspring, not of God, but [0587D] of the devil, through most wicked teaching. For among so many venomous thorns the Church dwells — the world, namely, of the Gentiles or of the heretics, who assail her; and lest she grow numb with idleness, she is permitted to be armed against enemies and to be assailed, so that after the fight her strength may appear. Of such the Lord says: « Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees [n: (Matth. XVI)], » which is the teaching of the heretics. [var: Vulg. *a fermento Pharisaeorum et Sadducaeorum*, "of the Pharisees and Sadducees"] And the Apostle [n: (I Cor. VI)]: « What, he says, fellowship hath light with darkness? » Or what meaning hath the temple of God with idols? Or what communication hath Christ with Belial? [var: Vulg. II Cor. VI, 16 *qui consensus templo Dei cum idolis*, "what agreement hath the temple of God with idols"] And nearly in like manner Isaias says: « Go out, he says, from the midst of them, and be ye clean, you that carry the vessels of the Lord [n: (Isa. LII)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. LII, 11 *exite de medio eius … mundamini qui fertis vasa Domini*, "go out from the midst of her"; *de medio eorum*, "from the midst of them", is the reading of II Cor. VI, 17] But in that he says: « As the lily among thorns, so is my friend [0588A] among the daughters, » he shows not the reproach of the just, who are the members of the Church, but their praise, that he might teach us that it is exceedingly praiseworthy to live piously among the impious, and to be in no way drawn along by the thorny converse of the perverse, among whom the just man is proved to shine like a light in the darkness. As if it were said to the Church: Thou seekest rest, but remember the praise of the little bed, that thou art made whiter by the goads of tribulations, and shalt acquire greater fruits from preaching than from quiet. And well does he say: « As the lily among thorns, so is my friend among the daughters, » that is, as the lily among thorns keeps its own fragrance and whiteness, so the Church of the elect among depraved Christians and heretics, if she yield not to them, is fragrant with virtues. There follows:

[0588B] « As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat down under his shadow, whom I desired, and his fruit was sweet to my throat. » As therefore before the mystery of the incarnation, « in many parts and in many ways, » according to the Apostle, « the Son of God spoke to our fathers [n: (Hebr. I)], » or was shown forth in the prophets, so after the aforesaid mystery he kept festival, bestowing many good things in diverse ways upon human nature; and he is compared to diverse things and to diverse persons, according to time and place, or occasion. [var: Vulg. Hebr. I, 1 *multifariam et multis modis olim Deus loquens patribus in prophetis*, "God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spoke in times past to the fathers by the prophets"] For according to David the prophet [n: (Psal. LXVII)] he is the mountain of God, for that the fullness of the divinity was to dwell in him. He is also the young calf putting forth horns, through the two Testaments. And hoofs, through the eight beatitudes, put forth from the feet of the four evangelists. [0588C] And the lamb taking away the sin of the world, through the imparting of the gentleness of the Holy Spirit, so that, while clothing others, he himself always abounds. He is also the beloved bundle of myrrh, and the cluster of cypress in the vineyards of Engaddi; and so, according to the place, the Church calls him the apple tree or the pomegranate, for that he is the tree of life, offering himself with a different savor to different persons. He is without doubt the pomegranate tree, which shelters and defends under the shadow of his protection the souls afflicted with the emptiness of hunger, wearied under the burning sun of wickedness, and with the fruit of the lips of his teaching refreshes by his sweetness those who hunger and thirst. From the expulsion from paradise up to the childbearing of the Virgin, the human offspring was afflicted with many labors and with intolerable assaults of the demons — which offspring is here, under the person of the Church, [0588D] called the friend; and at the coming of him who said: « Come to me, all you that labor and are burdened, and I will make you rest [n: (Matth. XI)], » [var: Vulg. *et ego reficiam vos*, "and I will refresh you"] resting under the covering of his protection, believing in one God, she says with gladness: « I sat down under his shadow, whom I desired. » Which sweetness of the kingdom of the heavens — although present protection may be understood, and she names the rest of the blessed — or else, tasting his body and his blood, which confer eternal life, she says with exultation: « And his fruit was sweet to my throat. » It may be that it was uttered so that the sweetness of the divine Scriptures should be understood in the holy preachers, who are called the throats of the Lord. Of which sweetness the Prophet says: « Taste, he says, and see that the Lord is sweet. » [0589A] « Blessed is the man, etc. [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]. » And that: « How sweet are thy words to my palate! O Lord, more than honey to my mouth [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]. » From the fruit of this tree, as one languishing in the last infirmity of despair, the throng of believers was restored at his coming by the juice of the pomegranate; which fruit is shown to be poured daily into the throat of the Church through those who act in Christ's stead; and thus through the several degrees of advancement she is led in to the hidden understanding of the divine law, that she may be able to recognize all those things which were done in the Old Testament. For she says: « As the apple among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. » As if it were said: As the apple goes before all the trees of the wood in sight, in fragrance and in taste, so Christ goes before all the saints who are called the sons of God. Of whom it is said: [0589B] « Then shall all the trees of the woods rejoice before the face of the Lord, because he cometh [n: (Psal. XCV)]. » Whence they are called sons, not by malice, as the daughters are, who are compared to thorns. There is indeed great exultation to the trees of the woods, while they behold that such a tree has risen up in their midst, whose fruit has adorned the whole forest, has taken away all the languors of souls, and has sweetened all the fruits of bitterness. Under whose shadow, all the heat of sadness and all the weight of sin being cast off, the Church tells of the rest long desired and now found, and, fattened with the sweetest fruit, of the joys obtained for growing souls. « Under the shadow which I desired, she says, I sat down, and his fruit was sweet to my throat. » As if she said: With him protecting me, whom I always sought to have present, I remain quiet and secure, [0589C] and by his grace he has made me a partaker of heavenly sweetness: and thus she testifies that she is led in, in due order, to the understanding of higher mysteries, saying:
