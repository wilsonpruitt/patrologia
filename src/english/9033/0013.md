## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

« The king brought me into the wine cellar, he set in order charity in me. » [var: Vulg. *introduxit me in cellam vinariam*, without *rex*, "the king"] The wine cellar ought to be understood as the Church, as not a few suppose, in whose unity alone the Holy Spirit is wont to be given and received. Whose grace is designated in this place by the name of wine. Wine — since, when the apostles had received the Holy Spirit, the Jews said: « These men are full of new wine [n: (Act. II)]. » In which cellar charity has been set in order, that each may love God with his whole heart more than himself, and his neighbor as himself. Although the wine cellar may, by way of surplus, be understood as the mystery of Christ's passion, into [0589D] which the Church has been led, when through faith she deserved to share in his sacraments. But because she testifies that she has been led in, in due order, to the mysteries to be understood by growing souls, of necessity she wishes the wine cellar to be understood as the knowledge and the sweetness of the divine law. To which sweetness she invites the young maidens, and by praising it urges them to hasten — the sweetness which Christ taught by giving it to drink, when he said: « Labor for the food which perisheth not, but which endureth unto eternity [n: (Joan VI)]. » [var: Vulg. *operamini non cibum qui perit sed qui permanet in vitam aeternam*, "labor not for the meat which perisheth, but for that which endureth unto life everlasting" — the received text sets the negation on *cibum*] Those set in this work the Prophet calls blessed, saying: « Blessed are they that search his testimonies, that seek him with their whole heart [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]. » For there are testimonies of the law in the Old Testament which prove that Christ, the Redeemer of the human race, was to come, has come, [0590A] and will come again in glory. Let us therefore set down the law as a most spacious palace, in which Christ the king dwells, the Word of the almighty Father, where the souls of believers are led in to the eternal warfare; where she says that she has been led in — into that same understanding of the law, namely — to consider the marvels which God wrought through the several patriarchs or prophets up to the childbearing of the Virgin, which she has now figuratively named the wine cellar; where, being led in, she received into herself the order of charity. In which order of charity, what else is to be believed that she is first imbued with, save that she should believe and acknowledge that first the bread ought to be named, in whom the Son always is, that he may invoke him as the Word; second the Son, in whom the Father always is; third the Holy [sic: *Spitum*], who by true reason is proved to proceed, as from voice and word, from [0590B] the Father and the Son? Who through all is the one God to be loved, according to that saying at the beginning of the Decalogue: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart. » The second order is, « with thy whole soul. » The third order is, « with thy whole strength [n: (Exod. XX)]. » Then already in the several precepts of the law charity sets in order how, or with what affection, the neighbor is to be loved, or the several persons in the Church. For by one order is reciprocal charity to be weighed out to God, who according to the Apostle [n: (I Joan. IV)] first loved us, who is proved to have conferred upon us the whole sum of charity through Christ. By another order to a faithful friend, by another order to a dearest son, by another order it is to be paid to parents, through whom we are who were not; by another order to a full brother, by another order to a spouse; by another order the servant [0590C] to his master, by another order the master to his servant, by another to a stranger, by another to an acquaintance, by another to a priest, by another to kinsmen and neighbors. For to bestow necessary things upon a needy sick man, or upon a stranger, not for the display of human praise but for the sake of eternal recompense, is the token of ordered charity. To console the needy and those oppressed by unjust judgment, to visit the sick or those consigned to prison, is shown to be ordered charity. In no business to stand in awe of a man's person against the truth, mercifully to admonish the unwise, most truthfully to rebuke the proud, is the manifest order of charity. But whoever loves father, or mother, wife, sons, or brothers, or the riches of the present time, more than God, there is indeed charity in him, but not ordered, since above all things we ought to love him [0590D] and our neighbor as ourselves. This is the order of charity. The Son of God therefore has set charity in order in his Church, that every son of the Church may know in what order of charity he is to render his duty to each, saying in the Gospel: « Render the things that are Caesar's to Caesar, and the things that are God's to God [n: (Matth. XXII)]. » And through the Apostle he says: « Render to each one his dues: to whom honor, honor; to whom fear, fear; to whom tribute, tribute; to whom custom, custom [n: (Rom. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. Rom. XIII, 7 *reddite omnibus debita*, "render to all their dues", and the received order runs *tributum … vectigal … timorem … honorem*] Not all who are called by the Christian name are led in through this order of charity into the aforesaid understanding which she named the wine cellar, but he who shall have imitated, however little, Paul, who said [n: (II Cor. XI)]: I have labored more than [0591A] all, in watchings, in fastings, and the rest: « and I have counted all things, he says, as dung, that I may gain Christ [n: (Phil. III)]. » At the head of this canticle the Church, led into the bedchamber or the storeroom of the king, gives praise, where, having entered, she says, « We will be glad and rejoice in thee, » having recognized, namely, his bodily presence, who is the wisdom of the Father, who lay hidden as in an inner chamber amid the heralds of the prophets, in whom are all exultations and gladnesses and treasures. But here she says that she is led in to the understanding of the divine law and to the learning of the order of charity, and that she does not rejoice and exult, but languishes, for that, according to Solomon [n: (Eccle. I)]: « He that addeth knowledge, addeth sorrow. » [var: Vulg. Eccle. I, 18 *qui addit scientiam addit et laborem*, "he that addeth knowledge, addeth also labour"] And whoever by pursuing has begun to be made nearer to wisdom, by so much does he scarcely behold her, drawn far off. [0591B] From which sorrow is born the blessed love of Christ, and from love a more glorious soundness, the languor of the soul, which withdraws from her all her powers for sinning. And though she has attained the hidden mysteries of the manifold wisdom of God, though she holds the citadel of perfection, yet she does not believe that the sum of the aforesaid sentence or understanding alone suffices for her, unless she be compassed with apples and stayed up with diverse flowers. Whence she straightway subjoined:

« Stay me up with flowers, compass me about with apples, because I languish with love. » She seeks the bodily presence of the wisdom of God the Father, that is, Christ, in the cellar of the knowledge of the law; but because she is not yet permitted to see him whom she desires as he is, she demands the examples of others for her consolation, whereby she may be able meanwhile to temper her languor. For she says: [0591C] « Stay me up with flowers, compass me about with apples, » that is, console me with the examples either of those beginning a good work, or of those bringing it to perfection: because as yet, in the weariness of my pilgrimage, I languish with the love of the vision above. Whence not undeservedly by the flowers are understood the souls already beginning a good work and giving out the fragrance of heavenly desire; by the apples from the flowers, the minds already perfect in good things, which come to the fruit of a good work from the beginning of a holy purpose. She therefore who languishes with love seeks to be stayed up with flowers, to be compassed about with apples, because she is still not permitted to see none whom she desires; it is a great consolation if she rejoice at the advances of others, so that the soul which is not yet able to contemplate the face of God may rest in the good work of her neighbor. Let us see what the mind of Paul was, who said: « To me to live [0591D] is Christ, and to die is gain [n: (Phil. I)]. » Hence it is that again he says: « I have a desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ, a thing by much the better [n: (Ibid.)]. » But behold, thou who desirest to be dissolved, let us see with what love thou languishest. Because meanwhile thou seest thyself put off, dost thou not seek, I pray, to be stayed up with flowers? Plainly thou dost seek it: for there follows: « But to abide in the flesh, I judge necessary for you [n: (Ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Phil. I, 24 *permanere autem in carne magis necessarium est propter vos*, "but to abide in the flesh is more needful for you"] And to his advancing disciples he says: « For what is our hope or joy, or crown of glory? Are not you, before our Lord Jesus Christ? » [n: (I Thes. II.)] The soul therefore, joined to the Word of God, led into the understanding of the law, running through the several points of Scripture, as has often been said, and tasting the savors of the several vessels of the wine cellar [0592A] of the divine books, as though inebriated with the wine of the gladness of the beatitude always to come, although she does not discern the bridegroom in the present, says that she languishes with love, because she comes to him but slowly. With that love, without doubt, of which the Prophet says: « My soul hath thirsted after God, the living fount; when shall I come and appear before the face of the Lord? » [n: (Psal. XLI.)] [var: Vulg. *ad Deum fortem vivum*, "after the strong living God", and *ante faciem Dei*, "before the face of God"] Through which love of knowledge is germinated the love of eternal life, and from the love of eternal life the endurance of persecution. Savoring which love, as though placed upon a little bed, she besought that she might be embraced by the left hand of promise and the right hand of Christ's help, saying:

« His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. » For what is designated by the left hand but the present life, or the temporal gifts [0592B] of God almighty? And what by the right hand, but everlasting life is figured? For the left hand of God — the Church, namely — has placed the prosperity of the present life and its temporal gifts as it were under her head, which she presses down with the intention of the highest love. But the right hand of God embraces her, because she is held with whole devotion beneath his eternal blessedness. Since here the bridegroom strengthens the mind through hope, and there glorifies it through recompense. Or by the left hand the Old Testament, and by the right hand the New Testament, is intimated. For it can be so understood, that the left hand of Christ sustains the soul on the left, lest by the hooks of the incentives of the vices she be led astray on the left, but that, being always displayed with hidden compunctions, she be lifted up to the kingdom. And lest, uplifted into pride, she make a fall on the right, in which the devil fell, he embraces her with his right hand by permitting tribulations [0592C] and manifold restraints, and by helping her when she is wearied. It is also of force, by way of surplus, to understand it thus: that the left hand under the head is the shield of faith, which the fighter holds in his left hand, and that the embrace of the right hand is understood as the sword of prayer, with which he is always armed on the right, so that by the one kind of arms the adversary may be repelled, and by the other laid low.
