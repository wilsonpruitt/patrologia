## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

« I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, till she please. » After many labors, namely of the Church, and many runnings to and fro, through which she is rendered anxious and, wearied within the holy wine cellar, is given over to slumber, the daughters [0592D] of Jerusalem are adjured not to stir her up, lest, awakened from sleep, she be too quickly given back to her former labors; and by this they are adjured, that she may be loved with firm charity. By the roes, namely, and the harts of the fields, which are given to be understood as souls of no small merit or labor, and by which the daughters of Jerusalem are adjured. For among the Christian people souls are compared to diverse animals according to the quality of their merits. Our Lord Jesus Christ therefore has a palace, which is understood to contain within itself many storerooms, and a wine cellar, and the spaces of the fields. Whose flowers blessed Paul, having entered, praises in wonder: « O the depth, he says, of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! [n: (Rom. XI.)] » In which palace, among many riches, that is, the multitude of the saints, he is understood to have also diverse souls, [0593A] just as in a great house, according to the Apostle [n: (II Thes. II)], there are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of earth and of wood. Whence not undeservedly the daughters of Jerusalem are the souls of the elect, contemplating the vision of peace of the country above. But the spiritual roes or harts he calls the loaves of the Old and the New Testament, for which the multitude of the sacred Scriptures is the pasture. Or else certain men will have those souls understood as roes which, by the keenness of dialectic, by the sense of understanding, have been accustomed to range about through philosophy and with the mind — which souls, now enclosed within the aforesaid palace by the net of the hunters' art, that is, of the apostles and of the other doctors, taken by their hand, their religion being changed for the better, range over the fields of the divine Scriptures, or, interpreting whatever is steep and obscure in wisdom, range over the [0593B] mountains; and the worship of the gods is daily turned into the praise of the Creator. And they show the swiftness of their course in faith, and with keenness of sense, after the manner of roes, they look out from afar upon the ambushes of their adversaries. It is the nature of roes to have the front part of the body proved higher, the hinder part lower. And for this cause, always ascending upward, it is found swifter in its course upon the mountain, by which route the adversary is the lesser in pursuing. But harts appear to be understood as those who, in the very swiftness of their philosophy of teaching, confessed not many gods, but one God invisible, immense, inestimable, the Creator of all things, filling the whole world everywhere — who themselves also were taken by the holy doctors with the net of preaching into the bosom of the Church. Who, dwelling in the steep places of life, [0593C] after the manner of harts seek the fount of waters, destroy serpents, and, like clean animals, chew the cud. These therefore are without doubt the harts of the fields and the roes, that is, the souls of faithful philosophers, converted from the unbelief of superstitions, by whom the bridegroom conjures the daughters of Jerusalem to suffer the bride to rest a little in the sleep of rest — souls which delight to run together with the Church in those works in which they themselves were exalted to heavenly glory, that through them, the bodily burden being laid aside, they may feast with those souls in the heavens. Whom the Lord Christ bids to rest in the body, for the gain or the consolation of other souls, till she herself please, until the bridegroom himself, looking to her advantage, and she herself, may know her own strength: because without him she can do nothing. [0593D] But it can be understood, according to the former understanding, where we said that the roes and harts signify the Fathers of the Old and the New Testament, in this way: I call to witness the peaceable — as if it were said, the souls of the faithful — by their several virtues, which were signified by the clean and cud-chewing animals, that they hinder not by any importunity the pious pursuits of the brethren, but that each so rejoice at the advancement of his neighbors as at his own. Otherwise Christ admonishes that no one of the doctors frighten off, by gifts, namely, or by threats, those who are about to believe, but that they preach only the things that are holy, and lead those about to believe, or those converted, willingly to the faith or to religion, and disturb them not in their own tranquillity, until, driven by divine inspiration, they gird themselves to the pursuits of a loftier life. But the perfect [0594A] soul of the elect, and, so to say, holy Church, in the very slumber of rest always hears him by fearing him, always sees him by loving him. Whence now she says — it is to be supplied — this is he whom I heard adjuring the daughters of Jerusalem, that they should not stir me up as I rested in his embrace. Or else his voice is the evangelical preaching. And what this voice of the beloved of the Church may say, he declares in the Gospel itself with his own mouth. « See, he says, that you be not seduced by false prophets. For many shall come in my name, saying that I am Christ, and shall seduce many, and shall give signs, so that even, if it be possible, the elect be led into error [n: (Matth. XXIV)]. » [var: Vulg. Matt. XXIV, 4 *videte ne quis vos seducat*, "take heed that no man seduce you"; *a pseudoprophetis*, "by false prophets", is not in the received text] And: « See that your hearts be not made heavy with surfeiting or drunkenness [n: (Luc. XXI)]. » And: « Be like to servants who wait for their lord, when [0594B] he shall return from the wedding [n: (Luc. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. Luc. XII, 36 *similes hominibus expectantibus dominum suum*, "like to men who wait for their lord"] Lest the Church, resting in the embraces of the bridegroom, be deceived under the appearance of doctors. And lest she grow numb with idleness after her labors, or be unstrung by delights, she is thus admonished in the Gospel. But in the very rest of her security let her be solicitous always to lend the ear of her heart to the voice of Christ — that is, in the divine Scriptures — and let her study to give her labor to assiduous prayer. Those therefore who are set over the Christian people are admonished by these things to announce to the people without intermission what this voice of the beloved of the Church enjoins: how, namely, he came from the bosom of the Father into human fellowship, that the Word might be made flesh; how, according to Habacuc the prophet [n: (Cap. III)], humbling the pride of the demons, he broke the mountains of violence, and melted the everlasting hills, treading down under the feet of believers [0594C] Satan, leaping across the aforesaid mountains. And weighing this, the Church says:

« Behold he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills. » Wilt thou hear what leaps he made? Such leaps, I say, did my beloved make. From heaven he came into the womb, from the womb into the manger, from the manger he came to the cross, from the cross he came into the sepulchre, from the sepulchre he returned into heaven: he it is who was raised up also above all mountains and hills, that is, the Fathers of the New and of the Old Testament, who by the merit of their virtues and by the summit of their faith are more eminent than the rest. In these the Lord is said not only to dwell, but to leap across, because he appeared incomparable not only in divinity, but in humanity. Or else through the apostles, as we began to say above, he melted all the pride of the wisdom of the world lifting itself up [0594D] against the Creator, and, brought to nothing, it is humbled. In whom shone not rhetorical arguments, but the power of the Holy Spirit; and, the throng of demons laid low under the feet of believers, he was shown to the Church to have come into the world leaping upon the mountains and skipping over the hills, and he conversed among men. Whence also there is presently subjoined:

« My beloved is like a roe and a young hart of the harts. » We understand Christ compared to a roe not by any diminishing of the divinity, who is always the same, but through the mystery of the incarnation, namely because he appeared in the likeness of sinful flesh, in which the nature of the flesh, received into heaven, obtained increase. For some think he was likened to a roe for this reason also, [0595A] that the illumination of our salvation, taking away the blindness of the heart, showed itself by coming in the flesh in the latter part of the age — which illumination he had shown to Moses when he asked for it on Mount Sinai. Of which mystery David says: « The wings of the dove are silvered over, unto the hinder parts of his back [n: (Psal. LXVII)]. » Just as the hinder part of the body of roes shows whiteness; and because many, not only of the Jews but also of the philosophers of the Gentiles, believed through the humility of his incarnation — by whom are understood those wild souls, once cleaner than the rest of those mentioned above, which through his teaching were gathered into the fellowship of the one true Israel. To such, according to the opinion of some, he is proclaimed like, in the voice of the Church, who for the innocent and for the multitude of believers is compared to a young hart of the harts. [0595B] He is called a young hart of the harts for this reason, that he was begotten of the stock of the patriarchs, who are designated by the name of harts; or else a young hart of the harts on account of the form of his humility, the horns of his power not yet being lifted up, with which at the second coming he will plead against the world, of which it was said in the psalm: Then « the horns of the just shall be exulted [n: (Psal. LXXIV)]. » [var: Vulg. *cornua iusti exaltabuntur*, "the horns of the just shall be exalted"] For the young hart of the harts is without horns, and shows in itself the love of its own aspect rather than terribleness, as our Lord Christ is known to have done at his first coming. Of whose first coming there is presently subjoined: « Behold he standeth behind our wall, looking back through the windows, looking forth through the lattices. » [var: Vulg. *despiciens per fenestras*, "looking down through the windows"] Clothed with the wall of our mortality, he lay hidden as to the substance of his divinity. But looking forth toward us [0595C] through the lattices and the windows while he wrought miracles, so that he might appear from his miracles who lay hidden from his sufferings. For he who showed to human eyes that which he assumed of mortal nature, and in his own self, who remained invisible, stood as it were behind a wall for those seeking to see him in the open, because he did not offer himself to be seen with majesty made manifest. For he stood as it were behind a wall, who showed the nature of the humanity which he assumed, and hid the nature of the divinity from human eyes. For whoever looks back through windows or lattices is neither wholly seen nor wholly not seen. For our Redeemer, because he both did divine things and endured human things, came to men as it were through lattices and windows: that as God he might appear from his miracles, as has been said, and lie hidden from his sufferings, and be discerned as man from his sufferings. [0595D] Rightly is it said: « Behold he standeth behind our wall, » and the rest. It can, if it please anyone, be understood thus: Behold he himself who was crucified, who with the right hand of his divinity held the Church languishing, that is, mourning in the apostles, in the embrace of consolation, with whom he was buried in the flesh — with her indeed he stands, rising again, behind the wall of our unbelief, which we had built with muddy works by sinning. Of which he says through Isaias the prophet: « Is not my hand strong to deliver? But your sins have made a wall between you and God [n: (Isa. L)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. L, 2 *numquid adbreviata et parvula facta est manus mea ut non possim redimere*, "is my hand shortened and become little, that I cannot redeem?"; the second clause conflates Isa. LIX, 2 *iniquitates vestrae diviserunt inter vos et Deum vestrum*, "your iniquities have divided between you and your God"] Standing behind which, the Lord Christ waits to be called upon by the impious, and calls the sinful soul to penance, waits to be believed by the unbelieving. Behind which wall he [0596A] stands, until the time of baptism or of penance. And though we do not deserve to look upon him, yet by this, that we renounce idolatry, we make windows in the aforesaid wall, through which Christ looks back upon us, defending us from unclean spirits. And by bending the knees of the heart to him alone we make lattices, through which he may look forth upon our compunction as we turn to him. But when we have come to the true conversion of the aforesaid baptism, or of penance, he takes it away out of the midst, pardon of sins being granted, and speaks to us, saying: « Come to me, all you that labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you [n: (Matth. XI)]. » And: « Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world [n: (Matth. XXV)]. » Even as the master of the Gentiles, Paul, taught that it was done at his coming. [0596B] « Who took away, he says, the wall of the fence from the midst, and reconciled us to God the Father through his own blood [n: (Ephes. II)]. » [var: Vulg. Ephes. II, 14-16 *medium parietem maceriae solvens … ut reconciliet ambos in uno corpore Deo per crucem*, "breaking down the middle wall of partition … that he might reconcile both to God in one body by the cross"] Whence, when the Church was in a manner shut in within the enclosures of walls for fear of persecution, after his resurrection, while the Apostles were afraid and cast down with exceeding terror, he entered, the doors being shut, and brought great confidence and great joy, saying: « Peace be to you. It is I, fear not [n: (Luc. XXIV)]. » Then was fulfilled that which follows:
