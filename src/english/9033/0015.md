## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

« My beloved speaks to me: Arise, make haste, my love, my dove, my beautiful one, and come. » He speaks to her, that is, by calling her to the perfection of life, by telling her no longer to fear the author of death, the devil, nor to remain any longer within the [0596C] barriers of unbelief and of torpor. « Make haste, » he says, « and come, » because I by dying have given back the life that was lost. Now be no longer terrified by death, whose empire I have destroyed. For he was standing behind our wall, because the sun of justice stood in our way with his own shadow, of whom the prophet had promised, saying: « The sun of justice shall rise for you that fear God, and health in his wings [n: (Mal. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. *timentibus nomen meum*, "that fear my name"] He speaks to the Church in the apostles when, rising from the dead, he consoles them, saying: « Be of good cheer, for I have overcome the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]. » And: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]. » And: « Fear not them that kill the body, but cannot kill the soul [n: (Matth. XI)]. » Whence by this saying she is summoned to the office of preaching, as she herself says: « my beloved speaks [0596D] to me, » that is, he exhorts me to preach, saying: « Arise, make haste, my love, and come, » that is, arise from the couch of repose, on which you seek to take care of yourself alone; make haste, and come, to fulfil also the care of your neighbour's salvation; spend your zeal on the office of diligent preaching. For he also grants confidence by affable words when he adds:

« For winter is now past, the rain is gone and departed. The flowers have appeared in our land, the time of pruning is come. » He teaches her, that is, no longer to fear at all the savagery of wicked men, nor to dread the terrors of the demons, because he has both reconciled God the Father to the Church by the offering of the flesh he assumed, and has hamstrung the army of the demons by the cross, [0597A] and after the wintry and icy harshness has shown the flowery time of his springtime coming, and — as to how great and of what sort would be the rewards of joys that he was to display to her by his presence — has told what was still to come as though it were already done. Whence it is also said to her: « Rise up, make haste, my love, my beautiful one, and come. » For she becomes his love by acknowledging God; beautiful, by keeping the humility of Christ. And she is made a dove by requiring nothing of earthly desires beyond the meanest food and the clothing of the body, but, always holding the simplicity of doves, she joins herself to the Holy Spirit. Or she is called love through charity; beautiful or fair through the comeliness of the virtues and the laver of regeneration; a dove through the pouring-in of the Holy Spirit. As therefore when spring comes on, the forerunner of summer, which puts winter to flight, [0597B] when every creature rejoices restored to life, and all animals after their kind are embodied for offspring, and being made pregnant build their lairs, and the birds put together their nests, and call one another with their distinct voices and movements; when now the ground prepares banquets for creeping things, lest food be lacking to the feathered creatures; when the tuneful voice of the flying things resounds sweetly in melody and pipes, and the bee, armed with wings, goes forth to plunder the flowers: so our Lord Jesus Christ, after the rough winter of idolatry and philosophic teaching, adorned with the flower of springtime — which is our Pasch, the passing from death to life — through his passion the face of the world of martyrs, or of all holy works, and the beginnings of the faith of believers. At that time he calls the Church from the couch of repose to the office [0597C] of preaching, nay rather from the valley of tears to the mountain of God's paradise, by the example of his own death. In short, when he had said, « Arise, make haste, my love, my beautiful one, and come, » he presently added, « for winter is now past, » that is, the power of darkness, which made the world sorrowful, passes from this world to hell, now that Christ the sun of justice is come. Now the cold of unbelief [sic: *infidelitatiis*] and the rain of iniquity have departed, which covered the whole globe until the time of the Lord's incarnation and resurrection. « The flowers have appeared in our land, » that is, the beginnings of faith and of justice flowered in the world as the Church grew. « The time of pruning is come, » that is, when the useless shoots of vain religion have been cut away, the hearts of men are prepared for the faith to come; for the summer time is come, [0597D] when out of one stock of the body the good are separated from the wicked, lest by the nearness of the wicked the good perish, lest by the treachery of unbelievers believers be endangered. This, surely, was the time of pruning announced as coming, of which the Lord says in the Gospel: « For I came to separate the son from the father, and the daughter-in-law from her mother-in-law, and the daughter from her mother [n: (Matth. X)], » and the rest. [var: Vulg. Matt. 10:35 reads *hominem adversus patrem suum*, "a man against his father," *filiam adversus matrem suam*, *nurum adversus socrum suam* — "against," not "from," and "a man," not "the son"] Or, winter is past, because the tribulation of the present life has withdrawn. The rain too has gone, because when we are led to contemplate almighty God in his own substance, the drops of words will no longer be necessary, so that the rain of preaching should have to be poured out. For what a man can here hear less of, there he shall see more fully: « Because now we see through a glass in a dark manner, but then face to face, [0598A] and we shall see him as he is [n: (I Cor. XIII)]. » [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 13:12 stops at "face to face"; the final clause is drawn from 1 John 3:2] Then the flowers appear in the land, because when the soul has begun to taste beforehand certain first sweetnesses of the life of eternal blessedness, it is as though already going out it smells among the flowers what, after it has gone forth, it shall have more abundantly in the fruit; whence there also is subjoined: « The time of pruning is come. » For in pruning the barren shoots are cut back, so that those which prevail may bear fruit more abundantly. And so the time of our pruning shall come when we forsake the unfruitful and hurtful flesh of corruption. Which fruit shall be for us most abundant: the vision of the one God. As is the time of pruning, so is the calling out of the world of the soul of the heavenly ones. There follows: « The voice of the turtledove is heard in our land. » Let us state the opinions of various persons, lest perhaps we seem to plead ignorance. The voice [0598B] of the turtledove, on account of the comeliness of chastity, they would have understood as the blessed Mary, whose voice was first heard in our land when she said to the angel: « How shall this be done, because I know not man? » [n: (Luc. I.)] And: « Behold the handmaid of the Lord, be it done to me according to thy word [n: (Ibid.)] » . And: « From henceforth all generations shall call me blessed [n: (Ibid.)] » . To whom the angel answered: « The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee. » And: « That which shall be born of thee, holy, shall be called the Son of God. » And she is worthy indeed, whom alone all generations call blessed, whom, glorious among all women, not only the nations of divers peoples but even the powers of the heavens praise in wonder; for through her the entrance of life, death put to flight, the world reconciled, are our joy. But some assert the blessed John [0598C] the Baptist, whose voice was heard saying: « I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, prepare ye the way of the Lord [n: (Matth. V)]. » And: « Bring forth fruits worthy of penance [n: (Luc. III)]. » For so long as the winter of transgression, a sorrowful time and bound fast from every good shoot, had covered the face of the world, and the devil held the earth under his dominion, this most sweet voice of the turtledove, whether through the blessed Mary or through John, was not heard in the land. But when that great sun of justice, who rose for us bringing by his indulgence the richness of summer, then by the desire of preserving chastity the voice of the turtledove began to be heard in our land. For with just reason virginity is compared to the turtledove, that most chaste bird — virginity which in John or in Mary holds the first place. [0598D] For she, once joined to the Word of God or to the Holy Spirit, never thinks of another mate, nor is there any other joining of the desires of her love save to him to whom she is proved to have been once cemented — like the turtledove, which loves the dwelling-places of the beloved wilderness, and calling its mate resounds with a sweet voice, and when he is lost seeks not another, but always sends forth to the other the voice of its promise and of its mind, to whom it promised to keep the affection of love. When he is dead, it is never said to be joined to another; but all the days of its life it seeks with longing him to whom it was once joined. But it must be known that, although the voice of the turtledove which was heard in our land be such, it is that of the Lord and Saviour, who says in the Gospel: « Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven will draw near [n: (Matth. III)]. » [var: Vulg. *appropinquavit*, "hath drawn near," perfect] Or, by the turtledove most rightly [0599A] is the holy Church understood, adorned as with feathers by the variety of the virtues.

« The fig tree hath put forth her green figs. » Since therefore the harvest of human redemption and the coming of the glorious Redeemer were drawing near, the fig tree, that is, the Synagogue, bound fast by the winter and the frost of impiety, is understood to have had in itself nothing but the most bitter juice of a teaching which it falsely gives out as food of souls. But at the Lord's coming, after the voice of the turtledove was heard saying, « Do penance, » and the rest, the fig tree put forth her green figs, that is, the precepts of the law fell, and she herself through the apostles put forth her green figs, who, begotten of her, were made the most sweet food of teaching for souls. But because she would not believe them, she withered with an everlasting barrenness, and, stripped of all comeliness, was kept as nothing in herself but fuel for the eternal fire. [0599B] The people, freed from the shadow of her perverse oppression, began — the evil teachers, the Pharisees, being removed — to flower by believing in Christ, and by their own example to summon the nations of other peoples to the flower of belief. Of whom it is said in the following verse: « The flowering vines have given their odour. » For by believing in one almighty God the vines flowered, that is, the evangelical people. Or, the flowering vines, holy ways of life, gave their odour far and wide throughout the world. For as in the winter season the vines are without leaves and flowers and without comeliness, so it is most certainly proved that the nations throughout the world, which he has now called vines, were without the comeliness of justice and the ornament of holiness.
