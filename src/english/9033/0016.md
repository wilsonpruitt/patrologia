## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

« Arise, make haste, my love, my fair one, and [0599C] come, my dove, in the holes of the rock, in the hollow of the wall. » The Church, then, called from very far off, was lying in a place lowly enough and hidden, troubled and oppressed by the storm of the demons — she who now is proved to reign in the whole world. But when the sacraments of Christ's passions were celebrated through the teaching of the apostles, in whom Christ speaks, she is summoned to arise and to come to the bridegroom walking among the vines, to rejoice with him, and to put herself forth unto the yield of the fruit of justice of the aforesaid nations. As if it were said to her: O bride and love, to whom I have offered such great goods and for whom I have borne such great things, arise and come, gird thyself for the contest, whence thou mayest receive eternal rest « in the holes of the rock, in the hollow of the wall. » What the rock is, the Apostle sets forth: « The rock, he says, was Christ *(I Cor. [0599D] X)*. » Whoever therefore desires to be snatched from the enemies of the power of the air, and to escape unharmed, let him always enter, by keeping faith, into the holes of this rock — that is, let him consider the wounds of the nails and of the lance which the bridegroom took upon himself for the safety of the bride — and into the hollow of the wall, that is, the keeping of the heavenly virtues. For this rock has many holes, that is, approaches, through which entrance is made to the Father who prepares the kingdom for believers: namely, meekness, humility, patience, contempt of riches, the accepting of no persons, passing the night in prayer, the mastery of mercy, unmoved chastity, the form of kindness, a singular mirror, the acknowledgment of the undivided Trinity, in which is our redemption. These are beyond doubt the [0600A] holes in which the soul is taught to come to the joys of the kingdom of heaven. The hole of the rock may be understood as the protection of that same Lord Jesus Christ, to which the Church takes refuge, as a dove to the hole of the rock. Hence Isaias says: « Enter into the rock, hide thyself in the trench in the earth [n: (Isa. II)]. » The hollow of the wall may be taken to signify the defence of the Holy Spirit, or the congregation of the faithful — although the holes of the rock may be understood as the affirmations of the four Gospels, which bear witness that the body of that same rock was pierced through by the blows aforesaid. But the hollow of the wall [is] the teachings of the apostles, out of which God is proved to have built for the people of believers, as for a vineyard, a most firm wall against the assault of the malignant spirits. For as they [came to know] the divine mysteries through Christ, so we too, coming to know them [0600B] through the apostles, are held within the sight of that faith which they themselves confessed. And because « faith without works is idle [n: (Jac. II)], » it is rightly subjoined: « Show me thy face. Let thy voice sound in my ears. For thy voice is sweet, and thy face comely. » As if it were openly said: Show me thy face, I pray; go forth into the public view of action; do not rest under the torpor of repose, and show thy faith by works. And let thy voice sound — that is, the voice of preaching, of praise and of confession — in my ears. For his voice is sweet to God who knows how sweetly and pleasantly, out of the sweetness of the kingdom, to declare the words of the Lord to his neighbours, and to resound praises to the Lord himself. And that faith is comely which, adorned with works, does not fear to suffer adversities. There follows:

« Catch us the little foxes that destroy [0600C] the vines; for our vineyard hath flowered. » The storm of the demons being now over, which above he called winter, the bride, already joined to the king's body through the sacraments of baptism, is invited into the vineyard, that is, into the people of believers gathered to her from the Gentiles, and she finds it putting forth flowers through the good of nature, made ready to trust in the God of heaven. But [it being] made ready for pulling down and devouring by the craft and the ambushes of the foxes that await the ripeness of the fruit — which are the most biting dogmas of the heresy of perverse souls — Jesus says to his teachers: « Catch us the little foxes that [sic: *demo liuntur*] the vines, » that is, convict the heretics and schismatics, depraved in faith and deceitful in word, who are wont to tear with the tooth of depraved teaching the untrained minds of the faithful. For our vineyard hath flowered, that is, the holy souls of the elect [0600D] bring forth the fruit of faith and of good works far and wide. But what he says, "our," is understood in a twofold way, that is, whether he has called it his own and the Father's, or whether he calls the Church joined to himself a sharer. And when he had seen both the Church from the Jews and, in greatest measure, that from the Gentiles put forth flowers, he prepares hunters or guards to catch the foxes before the flowers bring forth ripe fruits. Of whose number is one who said: "Reproving every man, we bring into captivity every knowledge that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God." [var: Vulg. 2 Cor. 10:5 brings into captivity "every understanding unto the obedience of Christ" and speaks of "every height," not "every man" and "every knowledge"] For whether the foxes be understood as demons, or as heretics and perverse teachers through whom the demons speak blasphemies, it is manifest that both agree — men through whom the minds [0601A] of men, in which the right faith of the Church dwells, are broken apart. Whose destruction, or whose hidden ambushes, we ought carefully to guard against, and to love God with our whole heart that we may deserve to be loved by him; and therefore the Church most rightly subjoins:

« My beloved to me, and I to him. » The Church therefore being joined to Christ, or the soul to the Word of God, so that he may heap upon her a more abundant joy in all things — both those in which he himself is fed with gladness, and what it is that delights him in her, he shows; and what she ought to answer for such great benefits is taught. The bridegroom, that is, desires in her a comely face, as he intimated above: one stained with no blackness of sin, not hidden by base words and by lying, nor dreadful of voice with the hoarseness of blasphemies, but she renders in turn a sweetness resounding [0601B] in her praises; and that same Church says: « My beloved to me, and I to him. » Christ and she, head and body, are as one man; and as the members [follow] the head, so by loving she follows him, and for that reason she speaks in this manner. For there is no beloved and helper of the Church save Christ, and no beloved of Christ save the Church: because no other rightly loves Christ save the one only Church, and no other is loved by him save she. « Who feedeth among the lilies, till the day break, and the shadows be bowed down. » That is, who is fed with the cleanness of faith and the whiteness of the virtues, until the true day of that judgment of the world to come arrives, and light arises, and the shadows — that is, the errors of the present world, which hinder us — pass away before the sun of justice, and are bowed down together with their lovers into the chasm of hell.

[0601C] « Return: be like, my beloved, to a roe and to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel. » [var: Vulg. *Bether*] This is the voice of the aforesaid Church to Christ: I beseech thee, my beloved, that thou wouldst return to me more often by the coming of thy visitation, because the flesh which thou tookest from the stock of the patriarchs, rising from the dead, thou hast borne up above every one of the heavenly mountains, that thou mayest lighten the labour of my pilgrimage by eternal beholding. The roe in this place, as we have tasted beforehand above, they say is the flesh of Christ, on account of the likeness of the flesh of sin. He himself is called the young hart, because begotten of the stock of the patriarchs, who after his resurrection returning to the heavens was exalted above the mountains of Bethel, which is called *the house of God*, that is, the heavenly fatherland of the angels. Whence the Psalmist, speaking to him, [0601D] says: « O Lord, our Lord, how admirable is thy name in the whole earth! How exalted is thy magnificence above the heavens! » [n: (Psal. VIII.)] [var: Vulg. reads *Quoniam*, "For," where Migne prints "How"] Nor indeed is the moral sense to be admitted. For every one of those who believe in God has made himself either a mountain of God by holy conversation, or a mountain of the devil by living perversely. For Bethel is interpreted *the house of God*, as we have said before. Whoever therefore has so displayed his own life that the Holy Spirit delights to dwell in him, is made a mountain of God, which is the Church of God. Above these things therefore, if at any time, being offended by the sins of the people, he has withdrawn afar, he returns; he is made like in course or in habit to the roe and to the young hart, whose nature it is, when a voice sent forth behind their back resounds before them in the mountains, while they suppose it to be running to meet them, [0602A] to return with swifter course to the place from which they had departed. And this is the reason why, being provoked by enemies, it will not forsake its accustomed seat. So too Christ our Lord, however provoked, however exasperated by the sins of men, being called back by fastings and prayers, returns to the accustomed seat of his mercy. And if at any time he seems to be provoked, so that zeal in seeking him may be deservedly increased, no one ought to lose trust nor to cease from seeking. Whence there follows:
