## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

« The pillars thereof he made of silver. » The silver pillars are the teachers shining with the light of eloquence. « The seat of gold. » The golden seat is the hope of perpetual rest promised to the faithful, or the splendour of that inmost brightness in which the souls of the elect recline unto rest. And the golden seat rests upon silver pillars, [0605B] because through this, that he is said to shine from the holy preachers, the minds of the hearers find the splendour of the brightness in which they may recline. For through this, that they hear clearly and openly, they rest in that which grows bright in the heart. Silver pillars therefore and a golden seat have been made, because through the light of speech there is found in the mind the brightness of rest. For he already shines who inwardly irradiates the mind, so that by intention it may rest there where the grace of preaching is not sought. But further, as to what is shown bright within, he adds of what sort the ascent is, when he immediately subjoins concerning the same litter: « The going up of purple. » What is the purple going up, save the blood of the martyrs and the passion of our Redeemer? For there is no going up to the banquets of life save through the mystery [0605C] of Christ's passion. And well is the purple going up subjoined. For true purple, because it is dyed from blood, is not undeservedly seen in the colour of blood. And because the greatest multitude of the faithful, in the beginning of the Church's birth, came to the kingdom through the blood of martyrdom. Our King made a purple going up in the litter, because to that bright thing which is beheld within, one comes through the tribulation of blood. What then shall we do, wretched and destitute of all fortitude? Behold, we cannot be pillars in this litter, because in us neither the fortitude of work nor the light of preaching shines forth. We have no seat, because we do not yet, as we ought, behold through spiritual understanding the rest of eternal charity. We are not the purple going up, because we are not strong enough to pour out our blood for our [0605D] Redeemer. What then is to be done concerning us? what hope will there be, if none comes to the kingdom save he who has been endowed with the highest virtues? But our consolation too is at hand: let us love God as much as we can, let us love our neighbour also, and we too belong at the same time to God's litter, because, as it is written, he covered it with charity in the midst. For he has charity, and there without doubt thou dost come beforehand, where both the silver pillar is raised and the purple going up is held: for that this is said on account of our weakness is openly shown when it is subjoined there: « He covered it with charity in the midst, because of the daughters of Jerusalem. » They say daughters, not sons, that is, the weak: by the sons of Jerusalem he signifies the minds [0606A] of those who are weak, for whom, among the holy preachers and martyrs, he has appointed a place in the heavenly things through the precepts of charity; because those also who are weak in the virtues, if they do not neglect to do with charity the very good things which they can, are not strangers to the building of God. For everyone who has the charity of God and of his neighbour comes with gladness to this rest and to these banquets. All these ornaments Christ bestowed upon the Church, out of the exceeding charity wherewith he loved us, and delivered himself up for us.

« Go forth and see, ye daughters of Jerusalem, king Solomon. » [var: Vulg. Song 3:11 reads "daughters of Sion"] To this gladness therefore, and to the contemplation of the secrets of this mystery, not the weak but the perfect souls are called together, they who understand dominion in king Solomon, to whom it is said: Go forth [0606B] in mind and in deed from the turbulent converse of the world, that you may be able to see the king. « In the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him. » See the Lord Christ in the humanity which, taken from his virgin mother, he set at the right hand of the Father's majesty. « In the day of his espousals. » That is, in the time of his incarnation, in which he came forth from the virginal womb to join to himself the Church as bride. « And in the day of the joy of his heart. » That is, of the redemption of the human race, which was a day of joy to Christ. Otherwise: he admonishes that holy souls should always have before the eyes of the heart king Solomon, that is, our peaceable one, who is Christ. In the diadem, that is, in that crown of thorns wherewith his mother crowned him, namely the Synagogue of the Jews, of which according to the flesh [0606C] he was begotten, in the day of his espousals, in which he espoused to himself the Church. What greater gladness could there be to Christ, than when through his passion the redemption of the human race was accomplished?
