## CHAPTER IV.

« How beautiful thou art, my love, how beautiful thou art! » He praises, that is, the twofold beauty of the Church, scraped clean of every habit of the vices of the flesh, and turned from the worship of many foul gods to the one true God. He called the Church beautiful, and he repeats "beautiful," because he saw her to be praiseworthy both in preaching and in action. « Thy eyes are of doves. » Thy senses, he says, are excellent in the contemplation of spiritual things, and modest. Or by the eyes the holy [0606D] teachers are understood, the glory of the people. « Besides that which lieth hid within. » Great indeed is the glory of open work, but far more undesirable is that of the eternal recompense, which cannot yet be seen. « Thy hair is as flocks of goats, which have come up from mount Galaad. » By the hairs the faithful peoples may be understood, who by their multitude furnish the greatest adornment to the Church. Galaad is interpreted *a heap of testimony*, which well suits the united multitude of the saints. By the hairs there may be designated in particular those very men who believed from the circumcision; these came up from mount Galaad, because through the testimonies of the Holy Scriptures they came to the faith.

« Thy teeth are as flocks of the shorn, which have come up [0607A] from the washing. » In the hairs the more frail are designated, but to those seeing, all the more perfect, and the expounders of the holy Scriptures, and those fit for governing the Church, who do not need the milk of doctrine, and who by chewing impart solid food to others. Which have come up from the washing, that is, from the font of holy baptism — who are both shorn and washed, that is, stripped of their goods, renouncing the world, and cleansed by the washing of life. « All with twin offspring. » That is, fruitful with a twofold charity, or with faith and work. « And there is none barren among them: » there is none who does not bring forth the offspring of good work.

« Thy lips are as a scarlet fillet, and thy speech is sweet. » Scarlet is the color of blood; the scarlet fillet (it is called myrrh) designates the gore of the blessed martyrs. Or the scarlet fillet is understood as the doctrine of truth. [0607B] The lips of the bride, that is, of the Church, are likened to scarlet, because she does not cease to preach the price of the Lord's blood by which she was redeemed, and in preaching she blazes with the ardor of holy charity, and makes her hearers burning in the love of God. « As a piece of a pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, besides that which lieth hid within. » In the cheeks modesty is expressed, in the pomegranate the passion of Christ. The bride therefore has in her cheeks the redness of a pomegranate, when she confesses in words the mystery of the Lord's cross, and proves it by deeds. Otherwise: For those make the face of the Church comely who, with modesty accompanying, keep chastity. Or assuredly the cheeks of the Church are the holy martyrs, who, imitating the passion of Christ, have been suffused with the rosy comeliness of their own blood, like a piece of a pomegranate.

[0607C] « Thy neck is as the tower of David, which is built with its own bulwarks. » The tower of David is holy Church, or the perfection or doctrine of the saints, which is not from itself, but from Christ. The neck is the preachers themselves, by whose constancy that same city is both firm and on every side unconquerable. But the bulwarks of that same city are the defenses of the holy Scriptures, or the examples of the preceding fathers. « A thousand bucklers hang upon it. » Because as many as are the precepts in the divine books, so many are the defenses of our breast, by which we are defended against all snares. « All the armor of valiant men. » It is every equipping, whether of work or of heavenly doctrine, through which we not only escape but even overcome. Let us speak as Pope Gregory, after his manner, wonderfully thunders. [0607D] For in the neck is the throat, in the throat the voice. What is designated by the neck of holy Church, if not the sacred utterances? On which a thousand bucklers are recorded to hang: by this perfect number the universal number is shown, because our whole fortification is contained in sacred utterance. For there are the precepts of God, there the examples of the just. For if the soul grows sluggish from the desire of its Creator, let it hear what is said: « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God [n: (Exod. XX)], » and the rest. Perhaps it slips into hatred of its neighbor: let it hear what is said: « Thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself [n: (Matth. XXII)]. » It covets the goods of others: let it hear what is written there: « Thou shalt not covet the property of thy neighbor [n: (Exod. XX)]. » [var: Vulg. *domum proximi tui*, "the house of thy neighbor"] The mind is kindled to anger over an injury inflicted by a neighbor's mouth or deed: [0608A] let it hear what is said: « Seek not revenge, nor shalt thou be mindful of the injury of thy fellow citizens [n: (Levit. XIX)]. » The mind, ill-wounded, is kindled in the concupiscence of the flesh; lest the eye follow the mind, let it hear what was said: « He that shall look on a woman to lust after her, hath already committed adultery with her in his heart [n: (Matth. V)]. » Perhaps someone purposes to let his soul loose in hatred against an enemy: let him hear what is written there: « Love your enemies [n: (Ibid.)], » and the rest. But he who now seizes not the goods of others, yet perhaps still holds them in a disordered way: let him hear what is said there: « Sell what you possess, and give alms [n: (Luc. XII)]. » The soul of one growing weak, who desires to enjoy God and the world at once: let it hear what is written there: « No man [0608B] can serve two masters [n: (Matth. VI)]. » Another keeps his possessions not for the necessity of a livelihood, but for the will of desire: let him hear what is said there: « He that shall not renounce all that he possesses, cannot be my disciple [n: (Luc. XIV)]. » On the neck of the Church, that is, in the preaching of sacred utterance — which for its fortification and its height is called like the tower of David — a thousand bucklers hang, because as many as are the precepts there, so many also are the defenses of our breast, as has been said: And so, for the keeping of innocence, even when wronged by a neighbor we hasten to persevere in humility. Let Abel come before our eyes, who is both said to have been slain by his brother, and is not read to have resisted. So is it to be judged of the other examples of the saints. Because therefore in the voice of sacred utterance, if we seek the defenses [0608C] of any virtue whatever, we find them. A thousand bucklers hang upon it, all the armor of valiant men. For behold, if we hasten to stand strong against the powers of the air, in this tower we find the armor of our mind, so that from it we may take the precepts of the Creator, from it the examples of the preceding fathers, by which we may be armed unconquerably against our adversaries. And it is to be noted that it is said to be built with its own bulwarks. For here the bulwarks do what the bucklers do, since both fortify the fighter. But this differs between the two, that we move bucklers wherever we wish for our defense, whereas by a bulwark we can be defended, but we cannot move it. The buckler is in the hand: for the bulwark is not held. What then differs between bulwarks and bucklers, save that in sacred [0608D] utterance we both read the miracles of the preceding fathers, which we are not able to work, and hear the virtues of good works, by which in working we can be fortified?

« Thy two breasts are like two twin fawns of a roe, that feed among the lilies, till the day break and the shadows incline. » The two breasts of the Church are the two Testaments. The two fawns are the two peoples, coming from the circumcision and from heathendom, because from thence they are nourished. And these through humility understand themselves to be little ones and sinners, and running with charity they pass over all the obstacles of the world. Which feed among the lilies, that is, among the most white examples or doctrines of the saints. Of which we read that it was discussed above, as it were from the armor of valiant men. Till the eternal day break, and the shadows of the present life pass away. [0609A] And of this we have already discussed above. « I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense. » By myrrh the mortification of the flesh is expressed, by frankincense the devotion of prayer. As if the Bridegroom were to say: I will frequent them, and, propitious, I will glorify with a holy illumination those whom I find to be sublime in virtues through sufferings or prayers. Otherwise: It is the voice of the imperfect, who ascend to the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense when they desire to be imitators of those who were pleasing through the mortification of the flesh and most pure prayers.

« Thou art all fair, my love, and there is no spot in thee. » This is what the Apostle says [n: (Eph. V)]: « That he might present to himself a glorious Church, not having spot » of evil working, « nor wrinkle » of a doubled [0609B] faith.

« Come from Libanus, my bride, come from Libanus, come. » Libanus is interpreted *whiteness*, that is, the soul made white by good acts, whom the Bridegroom exhorts a third time to come. First, coming in the flesh through good works; second, released from the flesh to receive eternal life; third, the body having been taken back, to enjoy the perfect joys after the resurrection. « Thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana, from the top of Sanir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards. » Lions on account of pride, leopards on account of cruelty, designate the malign spirits; but by the name of mountains are designated the proud hearts of unbelievers, where unclean spirits have their seat. While holy preachers convert such men to the life of salvation, they are crowned from the head and from the top [0609C] of the mountains, that is, from the princes of the wicked: because from the labor of the contest the crown of glory grows. Otherwise: Libanus, as has been said, is interpreted *a whitening*. For unless a man has received the remission of sins through the whiteness of holy baptism, he cannot come to Christ. For Sanir is interpreted *a lamp*; Amana, *iniquity*; but Hermon, *consecration*. All which are referred to Christ, who consecrates those whom he enlightens after iniquity, and crowns them also. They come from the dens of lions who run to the faith from the houses of the kings and princes of this world. From the mountains, he says, of leopards, they who, having left behind the elation of pride or the tradition of divers philosophers, go simple and humble to Christ.

« Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my bride, thou hast wounded [0609D] my heart. » In the wounding of the heart the greatness of Christ's love in the Church is understood. « With one of thy eyes, and with one hair of thy neck. » That is, in the holy unity of the teachers; and with one hair of thy neck, that is, in the devout unity of the subject people, that is, of the hairs. Again: With that eye, therefore, the Church was pleasing to Christ, with which she looked not on temporal things, but on eternal. Whence the Psalmist: « One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life » [n: (Psal. XXVI)]. « And with one hair of the neck, » which sought to be adorned not with visible things, but with eternal. And this Church is alike the sister of Christ and his bride. Bride, that is, through faith; sister, through love.

[0610A] « How beautiful are thy breasts, my sister, my bride. » By the name of breasts the holy teachers are expressed, on account of the consolation of the weak and the sustaining of little ones. The sweetness of grace is more beautiful than the austerity of the law. « And the odor of thy ointments above all spices. » The most sweet report of the faith, spread through the whole world, is broader than that of the old law and of the fathers in it, which are confined within Judea alone. What is meant by spices, save the gifts of the Old Testament, which are compared to the odor of the Church, that is, to the grace of the Holy Spirit, or to her most beautiful virtues. The Bridegroom continues always to render like for like to the Church, just as the Church had praised him above. Therefore he says: « Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, » and the rest.
