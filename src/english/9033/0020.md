## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

« Thy lips are a dropping honeycomb, O bride. » What is meant by [0610B] the wax, if not the letter? and by the honey, if not the spiritual sense? The honey in the wax is the spiritual sense of the divine utterances within the letter. The honey drips from the wax, because almost every single sentence brings forth manifold senses. The lips of the bride are the teachers, who lay open the manifold senses that lie within the sacred letters. « Honey and milk are under thy tongue. » In the milk the instruction of little ones, in the honey the stronger doctrine of the perfect is signified. Under the tongue, that is, in the meditation of the heart. There is no need to subjoin what Paul the Apostle says thereupon: « And the odor of thy garments is as the odor of frankincense. » The garments of the Church are her good works, which are compared to the odor of frankincense: because all the holy things that the Church does for the Lord render in return the office of prayers on her behalf. For the garments of the Church are understood as her preachers, [0610C] sending up pure and clean prayers to the Lord.

« My sister, my bride, is a garden enclosed, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed up. » Above, in the preface, we determined to discuss this succinctly. The garden enclosed is the Church: enclosed, because, fortified by the Lord's protection, she stands firm in the holy faith; a garden, because she brings forth manifold seeds of spiritual works, or she is so called on account of the sons who are spiritual through baptism. She is a fountain, because she overflows with saving doctrine. She is called sealed up by the word of faith.

« Thy shoots are a paradise of pomegranates with the fruits of the orchard. » Through the watering of holy baptism, holy Church sends forth a paradise out of herself, because the men whom she begets through holy baptism [0610D] she desires to direct to the paradise of heaven. The pomegranates are called the holy martyrs; by the fruits of the orchard the sweetnesses of good works are understood. But because not only the command but also the coming is at hand, we have resolved from this point to pluck the sentences of the book more hastily than we had begun to draw them out above, lest we should seem to exceed the measure of the little book, as we have confessed.

« Cypress with spikenard, spikenard with saffron, sweet cane and cinnamon. » [var: Vulg. *nardus et crocus*, "spikenard and saffron"] The cypress is an aromatic tree, signifying the blessing of heavenly grace. Spikenard holds the type of the Lord's passion. Saffron expresses the fervor of charity. The cypress is joined to the spikenard when divine grace strengthens [sic: *coufortat*] us to suffer with Christ. Again, the spikenard is joined with the saffron when through charity [0611A] we willingly undergo the death of Christ. Sweet cane, which is also called *cassa*, is an aromatic tree, but a small one, and therefore it designates the humble in spirit. Again cinnamon signifies those who deceive themselves, and it too is a low tree, but fragrant and sweet. So humility has great praise and sweetness with him. « With all the trees of Libanus. » As sweet cane and cinnamon designate the humble thoughts of the saints, so also the trees of Libanus designate their lofty actions: « because a good tree cannot bring forth evil fruits [n: (Matth. VII)]. » « Myrrh and aloes with all the chief ointments. » Myrrh and aloes are aromatic trees, which express the mortifications of the vices and the penance of the flesh. With all the chief ointments, that is, with the more excellent free gifts of the virtues. And [0611B] beautiful is the joining of these trees with the ointments, because while we bridle the flesh from wantonness, it follows that we receive the greater gifts of the Spirit.

« The fountain of gardens, the well of living waters, which flow with force from Libanus. » The Church is both: she is the fountain of gardens, because she begets spiritual fruits, and the well of living waters, on account of the hidden mysteries which are laid open to the saints alone through the revelation of the Holy Spirit. Of living waters, on account of the divine utterances which proceed from the invisible treasures of divine grace. Which flow with force from Libanus, that is, from the Church herself, who is both white through the cleanness of faith, and lofty through the glory of the virtues. Otherwise: By the fountain of gardens Christ is understood, watering his Church. By the well of waters the Holy [0611C] Spirit is understood, from whom divers gifts are said to proceed: which are called living, because those whom they fill they make to live. Which flow from Libanus, because the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Son. By the fountain, which runs openly, Christ can be understood according to his humanity; by the well, which has hidden and most deep water, the same according to his divinity. From whom the waters flow with force, as he himself bears witness in the Gospel: « He that believeth in me, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water [n: (Joan. VII)]. »

« Arise, O north wind, and come, O south wind, blow through my garden, and the aromatical spices thereof shall flow. » This is the voice of the Bridegroom: for in the north wind the adversities of the world are designated, in the south wind its blandishments, because by a twofold assault the Church is proved. Arise: this is the voice of one permitting, [0611D] not of one commanding. In the aromatical spices are understood the virtues of the conscience, set in order with a marvelous odor. Otherwise: By the north wind, which is said to be cold and to freeze the earth, lest it bring forth fruit, the devil is understood. He is bidden to withdraw from the garden, that is, from the Church. But by the south wind, which is a warm wind, and is said to loose the earth from the frosts, the Holy Spirit is intimated. Who by blowing, that is, by enlightening his holy Church with his gifts, makes her to bud it forth in divers virtues. Well does he say: « Arise, O north wind, and come, O south wind, » and the rest. Because assuredly, when the Spirit of truth has filled holy Church with the virtues of his gifts, he scatters from her far and wide the odors of good work.
