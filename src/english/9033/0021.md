## CHAPTER V.

[0612A]

« Let my beloved come into his garden, and eat the fruit of his apple trees. » That is, let Christ come into his Church, and himself keep her everywhere unspotted, and make her fruitful with the fruit of faith, and gladly look upon and thankfully receive the works of his saints. « I am come into my garden, my sister, my bride. » This is not the voice of one commanding, but of one declaring a past time. As if he were to say: I have come, he says, most often into my Church, and I come to correct, to help and to confirm, even as thou hast besought. « I have gathered my myrrh with my aromatical spices. » By myrrh, suffering or mortification; by the aromatical spices, all the virtues are expressed. He reaps the myrrh with the aromatical spices, since he leads the martyrs, with the rest of the elect, to the ripeness [0612B] of their rewards. « I have eaten the honeycomb with my honey, and I have drunk my wine with my milk. » In the honeycomb and the wine the preachers are understood, in the honey and the milk the hearers, and over both is the inward judge who proves and rewards. « Eat, O friends, and drink, and be inebriated, my dearly beloved. I sleep, and my heart watcheth. » It is the voice of Christ to the Church. As if he were to say openly: Friends, by doing the things I command. Dearly beloved, by embracing with whole charity. I beseech you therefore, fill your inward parts with the deeds of the saints, as with choice banquets. To whom the Church answered: « I sleep, and my heart watcheth, » that is: by the giving of God's grace, I worship him in the peace of the present life; and the more tranquilly I am free from inward assaults, the more loftily I see how good God is. Morally, a man sleeps with a watching heart [0612C] who, by the fact that he advances inwardly in contemplating, rests from restless work without. But among these things it is to be known that, so long as one lives in this mortal flesh, no man so advances in the virtue of contemplation that he fixes the eyes of his mind upon that ray of light which is now uncircumscribed. For neither is almighty God yet beheld in his own brightness, but the soul discerns something beneath that brightness, whereby, being refreshed, it may advance, and afterward attain to the glory of his vision. This voice can also be understood as addressed to the apostles: « Eat and drink. » As if he were to say: Let there be joy and refreshment to us from the conversion of the peoples, who pass over into your unity.

« The voice of my beloved knocking: Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled. » The Lord knocks when he teaches through [0612D] holy doctrine the things that are good. Or he knocks at the door of our heart when he rouses us to the increase of the virtues. For the Church opens when she lays bare to him the inmost parts of her heart. She is called sister, because she is co-heir through the blood of Christ. Love, because she is privy to his secrets, or through love, because she keeps the things that are commanded. Dove, because she is enlightened by the gift of the Holy Spirit. Undefiled, because she alone is worthy of the sight of God. « For my head is full of dew, and the locks of my head of the drops of the nights. » The head of Christ is God. The locks are the gatherings of the faithful. In the dew and the drops of the nights is shown the charity growing cold in many, which they ought to have had toward God and toward their neighbors. Therefore of necessity the Lord rouses each of the faithful to the zeal [0613A] of preaching. And to him, calling her to the labor of teaching, the Church answered:

« I have put off my garment, how shall I put it on? » As if she were to say openly: I have forsaken worldly business for thy sake, how shall I seek it again? « I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them? » That is: I have already washed away earthly thoughts with the tears of secret compunction, how shall I be polluted again with the filth of the world? Because the office of preaching can scarcely be without worldly occupation. Otherwise: The priests in the Church, who in the very congregation of the faithful minister the visible sacraments, are described as certain locks in her adornment. But because for the most part they are darkened by the village of this mortality, or by works of darkness, weighed down as with the drops of the nights, they seem as it were to cleave to Christ. [0613B] But because they do not sincerely announce that same Christ, therefore, groaning for such men, there follows: « I have stripped off my garment, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them? » That is: coming to the faith, I have already laid aside the old man according to my former manner of life, and have put on the new; and if any defilement came upon my feet while I was walking among earthly things, it has been washed away by Christ's washing; and now I behold the ministers of the sacraments, by whom I ought to be ordained, infected with carnal vices. Is it right that I should suffer myself again to be defiled by my former evils, by living ill after their examples?

« My beloved put his hand through the key hole. » My beloved indeed puts his hand through the key hole [0613C] when the Lord invisibly kindles us by hidden compunction to the work of the virtues, and teaches us to pass through the strait and narrow way to the kingdoms of heaven, and calls back to our memory how and how greatly he suffered for us, coming down from the bosom of the Father. « And my belly trembled at his touch. » The bride, recalling these things in her inmost conscience, wholly terrified, accusing the sloth of her rest, hastens to the labor of preaching. Morally: The beloved puts his hand through the key hole when by his power the Lord knocks at our mind through a subtle understanding. And the belly trembles at his touch, because our weakness, by the very fact that it is touched by an understanding of heavenly joy, is troubled by its own exultation; and there comes to be in the mind a dread within the gladness, because it now feels what it loves of heavenly [0613D] joy, and still fears that it may not obtain what it scarcely and faintly feels. What therefore remains, save what follows:

« I arose up to open to my beloved: my hands dropped with myrrh, and my fingers were full of the choicest myrrh. » It is the voice of the bride. To arise is to awaken in heart to Christ. To open is to preach the word of God, or to bring forth pure confusion. In the hands works are signified, in the fingers discretion, or else the martyrs. In the myrrh, the mortification of the flesh, or sufferings. Which is then the choicest, when either continence is practiced, or sufferings are undergone, solely for the charity of God and of one's neighbor

« I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved. » The Church [0614A] opened the bolt to the Bridegroom, because she makes the temple of her breast worthy of the divine visitation and indwelling. « But he had turned aside and was gone. » Because to no one in this life is the full vision of God granted, as it is in the life to come, therefore he is said to pass by, because in the life to come he shows himself more fully to be seen and enjoyed. « My soul melted, as the beloved spoke. » The Church continues in the wonder of her amazement. The more sweetly, she says, I received the voice and the nearness of my beloved, the more loftily did whatever was cold in me grow warm with charity, and whatever was rigid melted away. « I sought him and found him not. » Because the gift of compunction and of inward sweetness is not in the choice of him who wills, but in the mercy of him who gives. Therefore they do not always have it in equal measure, because God does not so offer himself [0614B] to those laboring in exile as to those reigning in the fatherland.

« The keepers that go about the city found me: they struck me and wounded me: the keepers of the walls took away my cloak from me. » The keepers of the city, that is, of the Church, are the holy teachers, who by diligent preaching go about the hearts of individuals, and wound them with the darts of heavenly love, and set them aflame that they may burn the more, and take from them the covering of their old manner of life. By the keepers of the city there can also be understood the powers of this world, who are known to rule the commonwealth as a kind of city. For these, seizing the elect Church in her first beginnings as she believed in Christ, laid her waste with blows and torments, and, slaying her priests and ministers, [0614C] took away, as it were, a certain cloak of her adornment.

« I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, that you tell him that I languish with love. » Who the daughters of Jerusalem are has often been said: rightly does the bride languish when, struck with the sword of the word of God, she puts off the mantle of earthly action. And she adjures the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, the souls worthy of God, to carry the greatness of her love to God, and to ask for themselves heavenly help toward seeing his glory.

« What manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, O thou most beautiful among women? what manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, that thou hast so adjured us? » It is the voice of the holy preachers [0614D] questioning her and inquiring after the faith of the Church from her answer; that is: I beseech thee, since thou hast so adjured me, that thou show me the love by which thou sayest thou languishest, and make manifest what manner of one thy beloved is of the beloved, that is, the Son of the Father, God of God, and in what part he may be loved and not feared. since « perfect charity casteth out fear. »

« My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands. » The beloved of the Church, Christ, is white, because without sin. Otherwise: He is called white according to that word, « in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. » Ruddy, because « the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us. » Or white in virginity, ruddy [0615A] in the blood of his passion. Chosen out of thousands, because he alone is the mediator of God and men.

« His head is as the finest gold. » The head of Christ is God. As among metals nothing is better than gold, so neither among creatures shall anything be compared to the Creator, because he alone is good and best. « His locks are as branches of palm trees, black as a raven. » The locks of Christ are the companies of the saints, which cleave to God in faithful service. Branches of palm, according to that word of the Apostle: « Our conversation is in heaven [n: (Phil. III)]: » Black, on account of their contempt among men, or because they testify that they are sinners. They can also be called branches on account of victory, and black on account of afflictions: The Bridegroom praised the beauty of the bride, and therefore the bride renders like for like and praises him.

[0615B] « His eyes are as doves upon brooks of waters. » That is, the holy teachers, through whom the Church sees the things that are right: who are well compared to doves on account of simplicity and innocence, and to brooks of waters on account of the divine free gifts. « Which are washed with milk, and sit beside the plentiful streams. » He calls them washed with milk, that is, cleansed with most sweet grace, or purified through baptism. The plentiful streams are the abundance of all spiritual gifts. In the brooks there can be designated the instruction of the old law, and in the streams the fullest perfection of evangelical doctrine, because the holy preachers bring forth from their treasures new things and old [n: (Matth. XIII)].
