## CHAPTER V. (cont.)

« His cheeks are as beds of aromatical spices, set by the perfumers. » In the cheeks of our Saviour his modest [0615C] mercy, and at the same time his severity, is expressed. By the beds of aromatical spices his virtues and his sweetness and the report of his glory are designated. Set by the perfumers, that is, by the prophets and the apostles, who tell the secrets of his incarnation to come, and his deeds. Otherwise: As in the eyes the apostles, so in his cheeks are understood those Churches which in the first beginnings believed through the apostles, and out of a holy manner of life brought forth from themselves, as it were a bed of aromatical spices, unto the gift of the virtues; which beds were set, that is, instructed, by devout preachers, that is to say, « His lips are lilies dropping choice myrrh. » His lips are the words of doctrine. Lips, because by their brightness they promise the heavenly kingdom. Dropping choice myrrh, because they preach that it is to be attained through the contempt of present pleasures. Otherwise: The lips [0615D] of Christ are his teachers, virgins in mind or even in flesh. And in this they preach his passion and death, that for him who died for us we should not refuse to undergo death.

« His hands are turned and golden, » etc. His hands are his works, because what he taught in words he fulfilled in deeds. Turned and golden, because they hold in themselves the whole rule of justice, as he himself says: « For so it becometh us to fulfil all justice [n: (Matth. III)]. » They are called golden, because all that he did in man, he filled with the glory of his divinity. Full of hyacinths, because he rouses us to the hope and the love of heavenly things, since the hyacinth is a gem of an ethereal color. There can also be reckoned among the hands of Christ those who do not cease to work the things that are good, [0616A] because for the hope of the kingdom of heaven they always persist in every good work, and are ready to bring it to perfection.

« His belly is of ivory, set with sapphires. » The belly, then, of the beloved designates the frailty of his humanity. The ivory, the comeliness of chastity; the sapphire, the loftiness of the heavenly virtues. Set with sapphires, because partly his human frailty is understood in hunger, temptation, weariness, death; partly his divine loftiness in miracles, in the resurrection and in the glory of the ascension. By the belly there can be understood those in the body of Christ who, believing, bring forth in faith a good work. The belly of ivory is also understood as the font of holy baptism, or of ivory on account of the perseverance of chastity, out of which all believers are born in faith and persevere in the chastity of faith and of mind.

[0616B] « His legs are as pillars of marble, that are set upon bases of gold. » By the word "legs" the journeys of Christ's incarnation are intimated, which are compared to pillars of marble on account of their firmness and straightness. Which are set upon bases of gold, because whatsoever was done by him or concerning him, all of it was ordained before the ages of the world by the counsel of the divine promise. Otherwise: The legs, which bear the whole body, are they who uphold the gift of the Church, as it were the gift of the pillars. In fine, both in the tabernacle and in the temple pillars of silver were wrought, in signification of the teachers who uphold the fabric of the Church. Set upon bases of gold, that is, upon the preaching of the preceding fathers, and strengthened through the books of the law and of the Gospel. « His stature is as that of Libanus. » [0616C] As Libanus excels the other mountains in loftiness and in the grace of its trees, so assuredly the Lord Christ excels all the saints in the loftiness of merits and in the grace of virtues. « Chosen as the cedar. » [var: Vulg. *ut cedri*, "as the cedars"] As the cedar surpasses in its own dignity the other trees that grow on Libanus, so Christ transcends by his own glory all who are born to life in the Church.

« His throat is most sweet, and he is all lovely. » In the throat the inward sweetness of his words is recorded, and he who tastes it does not hunger. In the throat there can also be understood those who expound the mysteries of Christ, and daily meditate on his law, who bring forth from their mouth the word of preaching. And he is all lovely: O man, what more dost thou seek? He is all lovely, because he is wholly God and wholly man, on whom [0616D] the angels desire to look. God in the majesty of the Father, man in the virginity of his mother: in the one the Creator, in the other the Saviour. « Such is my beloved, and he is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem. » The Church speaks exulting with thanksgiving, on account of her reconciliation and the known mysteries of her Bridegroom, and, as it were rejoicing and made more familiar, she says: The more devoutly a man loves God, the more familiarly he holds him as a friend; and such, she says, must thou understand him to be, if thou wilt have him for thy friend.

« Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou most beautiful among women? whither is thy beloved turned aside? and we will seek him with thee. » This is the voice of the young maidens who [0617A] speak to the bride. For the persons of the speakers are varied for the comeliness of the song, but nevertheless this most beautiful among women designates the Church of Christ, which is expressed by the word "bride." Beautiful are the several churches of the saints, but most beautiful is the whole body of the entire holy Church throughout the whole world. Otherwise: For rightly is holy Church called most beautiful among women, because she is a virgin and uncorrupted, to whom the Apostle, speaking, says: « For I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ [n: (II Cor. XI)]. » And so the peoples of the heretics, of the Jews and of the hypocrites are called women, but corrupted in faith and in work. But the Church alone is most beautiful not only in the font of baptism and in the variety of the virtues, but in faith and in work, not having spot or wrinkle. For [0617B] the young maidens say: Whither is thy beloved turned aside? we will seek him with thee; that is: tell us whither he has turned aside who once dwelt on earth in bodily form, that we may follow him with thee.
