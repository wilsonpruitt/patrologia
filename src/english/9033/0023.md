## CHAPTER VI.

« My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the little bed of spices, » and the rest. As though he had said: Thither he ascended, whence he came down. Into the garden, that is, into his Church, that he might water her with the fountain of his grace, as it were a little bed of spices, that she might bud forth with the flowers of the virtues. Otherwise: The beloved of the Church, Christ, came down into his garden when he visited his former people. By the little bed of spice he means holy Mary the Virgin herself, of whom he was born [0617C] according to the flesh: « That he may feed in the gardens, and gather lilies. » That is, that he may reign among the nations, or take his delight in the Churches, that out of them he may take to himself virgins and saints. Otherwise: « That he may feed in the gardens and gather lilies. » He feeds in the gardens while he takes delight in the holy labors of the saints. He gathers lilies while he leads to the heavenly kingdoms those who come to the whiteness of perfect merits.

« I to my beloved, and my beloved to me, who feedeth among the lilies. » These things holy Church speaks out of the confidence of her love. I prepare for my beloved a place of dwelling in myself, and he for me with himself. I am his kingdom, and he my king. I am his body, and he my head: who is ever fed, that is [0617D] delighted, among the holy desires of chaste minds or bodies. Thus far it is the voice of holy Church seeking and praising; now what he who is sought may answer is subjoined.

« Thou art beautiful, O my love, sweet and comely as Jerusalem, terrible as an army set in array. » Thou art beautiful, holy Church, through justice: my love, because ever peaceable; for Jerusalem is interpreted *the vision of peace*, by whose name the heavenly fatherland is expressed. Holy Church is called sweet and comely as Jerusalem, because her life and her desire are already compared to the vision of inmost peace, so that in this — that she loves her author, that she longs to see his beauty, of whom it is written [n: (I Petr. I)]: « Upon whom the angels desire to look, » — she may already, [0618A] by the very desires of her love, be called like to the angels. And the more she is made lovable to God, the more she brings it about that she becomes terrible to the malignant spirits. But in what manner she is terrible is shown by the comparison subjoined, that is, as an army set in array. We know, and it is agreed, that an army is then terrible to its enemies, when it has been so packed close and thickened that in no place does it seem broken. For if it is so disposed that an empty place is left through which one might enter, assuredly it is no longer terrible to its enemies. We too therefore, when we set the line of spiritual combat against the malignant spirits, it is above all needful that we be found always united and bound together through charity, and never broken through discord: for whatever good works there shall have been in us, if charity be wanting? Through the evil [0618B] of discord a place is opened in the line, whence the enemy may be able to enter to strike us. But the ancient enemy does not fear chastity in us if it be without charity, because he himself is not pressed by flesh, that he should be dissolved into its wantonness. He does not fear abstinence, because he himself uses no food, being one who is not urged by the necessity of the body. He does not fear the distributions of earthly goods, if to those same the works of charity be wanting, because neither does he himself need the supports of riches. This he greatly fears in us — true charity, that is the humble love which we bestow upon one another in turn. And he exceedingly envies our concord, because we fear this on earth which he himself, unwilling to hold it, lost in heaven. Well therefore is she called terrible, and the rest: because the malignant spirits are the more affrighted at the multitude of the elect, in that they behold them, united through the concord of charity, [0618C] massed together against themselves.

« Turn away thy eyes from me, for they have made me flee away. » That is: with the eyes of thy mind do not seek to know me perfectly on the journey of thy pilgrimage, which cannot be done, because the more intently thou seekest to know me, the more certainly thou understandest me to be incomprehensible. And do not seek on the way the reward which is reserved for thee in the fatherland. But it is understood otherwise, as if he said: Wouldst thou not always seek him to be beheld in the body, whom thou always discernest better through faith? For this cause indeed I ascended into heaven, that I might always appear to thee as bound to a place. « Thy hair is as flocks of goats, which have come up from mount Galaad. » [var: Vulg. at Cant. 6:4 *grex caprarum quae apparuerunt de Galaad*, "a flock of goats that have appeared from Galaad"; the wording printed here is that of Cant. 4:1] These little verses have been expounded before, but repetition is a token [0618D] of steadfastness. In the hairs are understood the peoples, in the teeth the doctors, in the twin young the precepts of charity.

« As the rind of a pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, besides thy eyes. » [var: Vulg. *absque occultis tuis*, "besides that which is hidden within thee"] The cheeks of the Church are the spiritual fathers, as we have said above, who are wonderful in virtues and venerable in character, and not ashamed to glory in the cross of Christ. [sic: *Et.haec*] which are seen in her are exceedingly great, but far greater are those which are not seen, and are reserved for the time to come. Whence the Prophet: « All the glory of the king's daughter is within. [n: (Psal. XLIV)]. »

« There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and of young maidens there is no number » The queens are they who, out of love of the bridegroom and with a view to the heavenly reward, beget spiritual offspring for the eternal king through the preaching of truth and of faith, [0619A] and through the font of holy baptism. The number six pertains to the perfection of the present life, on account of the six days in which it is lawful to work good things. But the concubines signify the simple in the Church, who, having received the seed of the word, are wise not spiritually but carnally; or those who teach Christ, in a merely carnal manner as it were, by preaching or by baptizing. The young maidens are souls newly born again in Christ, whose sum, because of the multitude of the heavenly citizens, transcends number. And so by the young maidens may be understood those who always desire to hear the things that are holy, yet do not busy themselves to fulfil in works the things they have heard.

« One is my dove, my perfect one; she is one to her mother, the chosen of her that bore her. » [var: Vulg. *una est matris suae, electa genetrici suae*, "she is the one of her mother, the chosen one to her that bore her" — the cases are inverted here] Holy [0619B] Church, namely, the universal Church throughout the whole world, is one to her mother, that is to the heavenly Jerusalem, which is the mother of us all, whence the grace of the Holy Spirit descends to us, through which we are born again to God. And she is called one because, when all the reprobate have been cast out, she alone shall be joined to Christ. « The daughters of Sion saw her, and declared her most blessed; the queens and the concubines praised her. » [var: Vulg. *Viderunt eam filiae*, without *Sion*] Those whom he before called young maidens he now names daughters. The queens and the concubines are the same as before, and all of them praise the Catholic Church.

« Who is she that cometh up as the morning rising, fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an army set in array? » [var: Vulg. *quae progreditur*, "that goeth forward" — the word the gloss below itself uses] This is the voice of the Synagogue admiring the beauty of the Church out of the nations, [0619C] in that, without the judgments of the law and the instruction of the prophets, she had grown up at the preaching of the Gospel so suddenly and with so great a perfection of virtues, even as she had already marvelled above. For she says.: « Who is she that cometh up as the morning rising, » etc. The Church goes forward as the dawn, because now the sun of the true light is shown to have risen in her upon the world after the darkness of long ignorance; or when, leaving behind her the works of darkness, she lives chastely and soberly, and tends toward the perfect day: « Fair as the moon, » because, illumined by the sun of justice, she lights up the night of the world. « Chosen as the sun, » because she bears the image of her Creator in all justice, holiness, and truth. Otherwise: « Fair as the moon, » through virtue and comeliness in the present world. « Chosen as the sun, » in the blessedness to come. Or « fair [0619D] as the moon, » in the married; « chosen as the sun, » in virgins: terrible to the powers of the air, and through the power of binding and loosing. Set in array in the unity of faith, of hope, and of charity, and in the disposition of the mysteries.

« I went down into the garden of nuts, to see the fruits of the valleys. » For the garden of nuts is the present Church, where we in no wise see one another's consciences; but when the shell of the flesh or of the body has been broken through temptation, the taste of the inward sweetness will appear. The fruits of the valley are the fruit of humility: for holy Church goes down through the holy doctors, that she may see who advance to the fruits of good things, and who as yet need the watering of doctrine. The garden of nuts may also be taken to designate the canon of the New and the Old Testament: in which, [0620A] under the covering of the letter, as under a rind, the spiritual sense lies hid. « That I might see if the vines had flourished. » That is, if the faithful people had flourished in the pursuit of the virtues. « And if the pomegranates had budded. » If any had been made ready, after the example of the Lord's passion, to shed their own blood.

« I knew not: my soul troubled me for the chariots of Amminadab. » The voice of the Synagogue: I knew not that the gifts of spiritual grace were in thee, O bride; but my soul troubled me, because of the bringing in of the New Testament and of the evangelical chariot. For Amminadab is interpreted *the willing one of my people*, that is Christ, who is borne through the whole world. To whom the Church, comforting her, forthwith answered:

« Return, return, O Sunamitess; return, return, [0620B] that we may behold thee. » [var: Vulg. *Sulamitis*] That is: Be not troubled, but return to the knowledge of thy Redeemer, who was rather promised to thee in the prophets and in the law. Return by purity of faith, return by perfection of works. O Sunamitess, that is, captive, return now from the bond of unbelief to thy Redeemer, that thou mayest be saved!
