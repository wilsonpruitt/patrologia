## CHAPTER VII.

« What shalt thou see in the Sunamitess, but the companies of the camps? » [var: Vulg. *in Sulamite, nisi choros castrorum*] That is: Thou grievest that the Synagogue is hardened; the time is at hand when thou shalt see in her the companies of those who war against the malignant spirits and who praise God. *I knew not* may also be understood otherwise. My soul troubled me, because of the chariots of Amminadab. What did Christ not know, save sin? He knew not [0620C] those also to whom he will say in the judgment: « Amen, I say to you, I know you not [n: (Matth. XXV)]. » By the chariots of Amminadab he means the evangelists. For Amminadab is interpreted *the willing one of the people*, that is, the faithful people of the Church. This Sunamitess signifies the Church, whose figure that Sunamitess in the book of Kings bore. Which for this cause, which is adorned with the blood of Christ, sounds in the Latin *scarlet*; to whom it is frequently said by the holy preachers, « Return: » that even if through adversities she has fallen away, she may not sink down into despair, but may come to her senses out of the habit of sinning, with hope of pardon. « How beautiful are thy steps in shoes, O prince's daughter! » Now the praises of the Church are set forth by the bridegroom himself, and first the constancy of her works and the mortification of pleasures are praised in her, whom also he names the daughter [0620D] of the prince, that is of Christ, on account of the grace of baptism and the nobility of her virtues. « The joints of thy thighs are like jewels, that are wrought by the hand of a craftsman. » The concord of the two peoples, made fruitful with a special offspring, is designated in the joint of the thighs, which are as jewels wrought by the hand of a craftsman, that is by the unspeakable bounty of our Creator: in the jewels good works are expressed.

« Thy navel is like a turned bowl, never wanting cups. » Thy navel is the frailty of our infirmity. The bowl is made turned when, admonished by the consciousness of our mortality, we busy ourselves to hand round with a ready mind the cup of the word of salvation. Likewise in the navel are understood those who dwell not at the edge but in the midst [0621A] of the body of the Church. In these therefore are found the things that are useful vessels: in this it is turned, because they are always ready for obedience and for every good work. In this it never wants cups, inasmuch as they are wholly filled with the grace of the Holy Spirit. « Thy belly is like a heap of wheat, set about with lilies. » For those who always receive the nourishments of the Holy Scriptures, and gather within themselves the things that are salutary, and cease not to preach, dealing out their teaching to others like wheat, are reckoned in the likeness of the belly. It is a heap of wheat set about with lilies when the abundance of good work is gathered by the hope of perpetual light alone. In the belly memory is expressed, in the wheat the multiplication of good work, in the lilies the chastity of hope.

[0621B] « Thy two breasts are like two twin fawns of a roe. » Of this verse it was spoken above, but repetition is a token of steadfastness. The two breasts are the doctors of either people. They are twin fawns because of their one concord: likewise the two breasts of the Church are the two Testaments, fitted to the two peoples that come from the propagation of sin. For these are born again in Christ as fawns, and sucking from these two Testaments the milk of wisdom.

« Thy neck is as a tower of ivory. » In the neck the doctors are designated, who are compared to a tower of ivory because of their firmness and their beauty, since they afford strength and comeliness to the city of God. In the neck may also be designated the knowledge of the Holy Scriptures, which is therefore called a tower of ivory, because those who apply themselves to the word of God, [0621C] by daily progress advance to loftier things through the brightness of justice. « Thy eyes like the fishpools in Hesebon, which are in the gate of the daughter of the multitude. » The eyes of the Church are the doctors, because of their foresight, as the neck is because of nourishment. And rightly are they compared to fishpools, because they cease not to offer the streams of doctrine to their hearers. Hesebon is interpreted *the girdle of sorrow*, because the saints, in place of the vain gladness of the flesh, are bound with the girdle of abstinence. And well, in the gates of the daughter of the multitude, because of the abundance of the peoples running together through the gate of faith into the Church. By the gate Christ also may be understood. « Thy nose is as the tower of Libanus. » In the nose are designated the dispensers of the word of God, by reason of discernment, who, perceiving the odor of the justice which is in Christ, hold it out to their hearers, and by the understanding [0621D] of discernment distinguish from on high between the odor of the virtues and the stenches of the vices. They are likened to a tower, who through wisdom hold the most eminent place in the Church. « Which looketh toward Damascus, » that is, toward the bloody and the impious, for Damascus is interpreted *drinking blood*, and signifies the carnal and the cruel, against whom the holy doctors keep watch always in the tower of Libanus, that is in the firmness of the Church.

« Thy head is like Carmel, and the hairs of thy head as the purple of the king bound in the channels. » In the head the mind is designated, in the hairs the thoughts: and as the members are ruled by the head, so are the thoughts by the mind, that they may be occupied in Carmel, that is in the loftiness of the virtues and in the passion of Christ, which is expressed by the name of purple. [0622A] The channels are the inward parts of the saints, in which such thoughts are bound. Otherwise: The head of the Church is Christ, than whom nothing is more lofty. The hair of the head is all the more eminent saints, namely the blessed martyrs, made like to purple, joined to royal ornaments and to saving doctrines.

« How beautiful art thou, and comely, my dearest, in delights. » The Church is called beautiful, in faith and in work. Comely, by the ornaments of the virtues. Dearest, through love. « In delights, » that is, in spiritual virtues.

« Thy stature is likened to a palm tree, and thy breasts to clusters of grapes. » That is, the uprightness of good working ever tends to victory. To the breasts are compared the doctors of the Church, because of the milk of first instruction, and they are matched with clusters, because of the mysteries of most sweet eternity.

[0622B] « I said: I will go up into the palm tree, I will take hold of the fruits thereof. » For fittingly is the most victorious cross compared to a palm, into which Christ, going up, took hold of its fruit. For they go up into the palm who advance in the cross of Christ. Whereof the Apostle says: « For they that are Christ's have crucified their own flesh with the vices and concupiscences [n: (Gal. V)]. » -- « And thy breasts shall be as the clusters of the vine, » that is, the gifts which he bestowed upon holy Church, which from that time budded forth the clusters of the vine, that is the holy doctors, who after the cross and resurrection of the Savior abounded in greater knowledge and grace. By the breasts may also be understood the preachers of the Church, who nourish those that are born again in the faith. « And the odor of thy mouth like the odor of apples. [0622C] Thy throat like the best wine to drink. » In the throat is designated the voice of teaching present, in the odor the fame of it absent. And therefore the one is compared to wine, because of the fragrance of the virtues, the other to apples, because of the sweetness of absent fame: « Worthy for my beloved. » For the bride snatched the word out of the mouth of the bridegroom, because he had compared her to the best wine, and she subjoined it. Worthy for my beloved, that is: so great is the loftiness of the evangelical preaching, that my beloved himself, first appearing through the incarnation, opened the heavenly way to the world: in the throat may be understood those who through evangelical preaching inebriate the hearts of the hearers: whence Christ is drunk, when out of her the Christians, who are his members, are filled with incomparable sweetness. « And for his lips [0622D] and teeth to chew upon, » that is, for the apostles and the preachers, to whom above all he gave it to meditate upon.

« I to my beloved, » and to no other, upon whom I bestow the whole care of my service and of my love. « And his turning is toward me, » that is, he loves me alone, and helps me, that I fail not in the way. To which beloved it is said by the Psalmist in the voice of the Church: « My soul hath cleaved after thee: thy right hand hath received me [n: (Psal. LXII)]. »

« Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field, let us abide in the villages; let us get up early to the vineyards, let us see if the vineyard hath flourished, if the flowers bring forth fruits, if the pomegranates have flourished. » Since the Church is in no wise sufficient either to go forth and advance to the working of good, or to abide by persisting in the exercise of good working, [0623A] or even to rise up to the purpose of acting well, or to discern how far the minds of her hearers have advanced, unless she be helped by the grace of God, who said: « Behold I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the world [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]. » Christ goes forth with the Church into the field, when through the holy doctors he visits the world. He abides in the villages, namely through faith, among the peoples believing out of the nations, spread abroad on every side. He rises early to the vineyards, that the souls which through faith have already deserved to be gathered may know that Christ rose again in the morning hours, and may no longer doubt of the resurrection of their own body. Let us see if the vineyard had flourished, if the Church also brings forth flowers by faith, if the flowers bring forth fruits, that is, if the holy faithful pronounce with the mouth [0623B] what they have believed with the heart. If the pomegranates have flourished, if they who have believed in Christ so advance in love that they even delight to be slain for him. For in each one of these the bride seeks the penitence of her beloved. « There will I give thee my breasts, » that is, the tutors of my little ones. Because in all these things the holy doctors advance, in this holy confession my doctors shall be joined to thee. And because they are ready to suffer for thee, they shall reign with thee.

« The mandrakes have given their odor in our gates. » [var: Vulg. *Mandragorae dederunt odorem*, without *suum*; there *in portis nostris* opens the clause that follows] Of mandrakes there are two kinds, the male and the female. The female is red, the male white: in both there is one power for medicines. They are called mandrakes because they have sweet-smelling apples, of the size of an apple, whence the Latins also call it the apple of the earth: this [0623C] the poets call ἀνθροπόμορφον, because it has a root resembling the form of a man; which is given in wine to drink to those whose body must be cut for the sake of treatment, that being lulled asleep they may not feel the pain. Mandrakes, because of their manifold kinds of medicines, are compared to the virtues of the saints, which, after the manner of physicians, are shown to be healed from the infirmities of the vices unto the health of souls. The gates of the Church are the holy doctors, and in gates of this sort the mandrakes give their odor, when all spiritual men spread abroad from themselves far and wide the palm of the virtues. « All the fruits, new and old, my beloved, I have kept for thee. » The new and old fruits are the precepts or the promises of the New and of the Old Testament, all of which the Church refers to his grace; or the Fathers of the New and of the Old Testament may [0623D] be understood, because the Church is one and the same.
