## CHAPTER VIII.

« Who giveth me my brother, sucking my breasts? » [var: Vulg. *Quis mihi det te fratrem meum sugentem ubera matris meae*, "Who shall give me thee for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother"] This is the voice of the men of old, desiring the coming of Christ in the flesh, and that he should be born and nourished in the Synagogue, according to the nature of the human condition. « That I may find thee without, and kiss thee. » Within was the beloved, while « in the beginning was the Word »; without, while « the Word was made flesh. » « And kiss thee »: that I may see face to face, and speak mouth to mouth. Of which kiss I think enough has been said at the opening of the book. « And now let no man despise me. » Before the coming of Christ the Church was only within the narrow bounds of Judaea; after [0624A] the ascension she was spread abroad in the whole world, and made venerable. Before it had been said, « God is known in Judaea »; but now it is said: « Be thou exalted, O God, above the heavens, and thy glory above all the earth. » It may also be understood otherwise. Christ sucks the breasts of his mother when the Christian people is refreshed with the histories of the ancient law. For they call the Synagogue the mother of the Church, that is of the body of Christ, to whom in the beginning the divine oracles were entrusted; and what in the Old Testament was covered under a figure is found in truth through the Gospel in the New.

« I will take hold of thee, » that is, I will receive thee coming with ready and faithful devotion. « And I will lead thee into my mother's house. » The dispensation of the flesh accomplished, I will lead thee, returning, with joyful eyes into my mother's house, [0624B] that is of the heavenly Jerusalem, which is our mother. « There thou shalt teach me, » that is, thou shalt make me hope for better gifts than I had had in the law. « And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, » that is, a fervent love made fragrant or adorned with the various pigments of the virtues. « And the new wine of my pomegranates, » that is, the triumph of the glorious holy martyrs, who with most fervent charity do not hesitate to pass over to thee through sword and flames. Otherwise: The Church leads Christ into the house of her mother, the heavenly Jerusalem, when together with all the good things which she has obtained through his grace she also deserves herself to be led thither. And there she is taught, « that she may now see, not in a riddle through mirrors, but face to face, » when martyrdom shall have brought forth its best and sweetest fruits, as it were the new wine of [0624C] pomegranates.

« His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. » In the left hand are expressed the gifts of Christ's incarnation; in the right, the joys to come of the saints with Christ. Or in the left hand the present life, which the saints place under the head of the mind, because they despise it. In the right hand, the beatitude which the saints, out of exceeding desire, shall embrace. Of this verse there has been fuller and more abundant discussion above.

« Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights? » But of this too we have already discoursed; yet because repetition of verses is, as we have often said, a token of steadfastness, let us now touch on it briefly. It is the voice of the Synagogue, or of the prophet, marvelling how the Church had come up so suddenly out of the desert of heathenism into the embraces of the bridegroom: [0624D] to whom it is said by the prophet: « For that thou wast forsaken and hated [n: (Isa. LX)], » etc. And in the psalm: « They wandered in the wilderness, in a place without water [n: (Psalm. CVI)]. » Flowing with delights, that is, with the beauty of all gifts. « Leaning upon my beloved. » [var: Vulg. *innixa super dilectum suum*, "upon her beloved"] Because all that she has she refers to his grace, being established in him by faith, hope, and charity — whom they believed was to be committed to me alone. « I raised thee up under the apple tree. » The bride answered on behalf of the Synagogue. I, the bridegroom himself, under the tree of the cross freed thee from perpetual death, as I freed the apostles and the rest of the elect out of Judaea. « There was thy mother corrupted, there was she defiled that bore thee. » That is, the greater part of the people, by denying Christ and choosing Barabbas, was reprobated at the cross.

[0625A] « Put me as a seal upon thy heart, and as a seal upon thy arm. » A seal we bind upon the arm or the finger for the remembrance of some matter. By « heart » thought is designated, and by « arm » working. That is: If thou wilt have me as bridegroom, let there be within charity with faith unfeigned, and without devout working; and so shalt thou receive my faith, that thou mayest keep it unspotted. And because through my cross thou hast received a seal, fear not that the treasure of justice which I have conferred upon thee be carried off by any thief, so that both in thought and also in working thou mayest run back to that sign; and as I have so loved thee that I was willing to die for thee, so, on the other hand, let thy saints not refuse death for me. « For love is strong as death, jealousy is hard as hell. » Strong [0625B] even unto death is my love toward thee, O Synagogue; but thy jealousy toward me was hard as hell: yet turn thy jealousy into love, and thou shalt be to me bride, and sister, and love. « The lamps thereof are lamps of fire and of flames. » The lamps of love are the hearts of the faithful, in which charity dwells, ever bright and clean with the brightness of charity, and burning with the fire of the Holy Spirit, and shining forth with the orb of the flames of good working.

« Many waters cannot quench charity, neither shall the floods drown it. » By many waters and floods he means the onsets of temptations, which visibly or invisibly assail the souls of the faithful, to which charity does not yield. « If a man should give all the substance of his house for love, they shall despise it as [0625C] nothing. » But even as those giving the riches of their whole substance for the greatness of the love of Christ despised it as nothing, that is, the world. But in another manner the sense of the sentence hangs upon what goes before. For when he had said, « many waters » or floods « could not quench charity, » he subjoined: « If he shall give all the substance of his house, » etc., so that there may be understood what the Apostle says: « And if I should distribute all my goods to feed the poor, and if I should deliver my body so that I burn, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing [n: (I Cor. XI)]. »

« My sister is very little, and she hath no breasts. What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? » [var: Vulg. *Soror nostra parva*, "Our sister is little"] It designates the first times of the Church newly born out of the nations, since as yet she was both small in number [0625D] and less fit to preach the word, as the Apostle says: « I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal; as unto little ones in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not meat [n: (I Cor. III)]. » As it were the voice of the bridegroom, or of the apostles, had said of her to the Synagogue: « What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? » As if he plainly said: The Church of the nations is indeed small, nor is she sufficient to undergo the mystery of the word of God. What then seems to thee, O Synagogue, must be done concerning our sister, when she is to be spoken to, that is, to be led into the faith through the mystery of the word? The Synagogue keeping silence, the bridegroom himself, or the apostles, answered what ought to be done.

« If she be a wall, let us build upon her bulwarks [0626A] of silver. » As if he said: If she has in her any strong in faith, or notable in talent, or instructed in philosophy, let us add to them bulwarks of silver, that is the knowledge of the divine Scriptures, that they may be able the more easily to protect the weak and the unlearned. « If she be a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar. » That is, if they be simple, yet gaping with zeal for being taught, and, like a door which is opened and shut, understand « a time to keep silence or to speak »: let us set before them the examples of the just of former times, whereby they may the more surely and effectually fulfil the office of teaching. The cedars designate the virtues of the saints, and the boards the breadth of charity. And that they may be able to say with the Apostle: « But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom [0626B] the world is crucified to me, and I to the world [n: (Gal. VI)]. » For to these things the Church herself answered:

« I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower, since I am become in thy presence as one finding peace. » [var: Vulg. *coram eo*, "in his presence"] I am compacted of living stones, and made one by the glue of charity, and built upon an immovable foundation; and my doctors are most strong, as a tower, who both know how to nourish little ones and to drive out all evil things; and it has befallen me, from that time in which Christ reconciled me, that peace is found through him.

« The peaceable had a vineyard in that which hath the people; he delivered it to keepers. » [var: Vulg. *quae habet populos*, "which hath peoples"] Whereof the Apostle says: « He is our peace, who hath made both one [n: (Ephes. II)]. » And whereof the prophet: « His empire shall be multiplied, [0626C] and of peace there shall be no end [n: (Isa. IX)]. » The vineyard, that is the Catholic Church, abounding in the fruit of faith, belonged to the peaceable, that is to Christ, who pacified all things in heaven and on earth, in that congregation wherein he possesses many peoples out of the whole world, not in Judaea alone; he delivered it to keepers, but also to prophets or apostles, or to evangelical teachings, or to angelic dignities. « A man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver. » That is, Christ weighing out the kingdom of heaven for its holy labors or alms. It may also be understood otherwise: « A man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver, » that is, for an eternal recompense. The man of this vineyard brings a thousand pieces of silver, that is, for the acquiring of the heavenly kingdom, which is the fruit of the violet, all the things which he left to the worlds. The number of a thousand is taken for the perfection of him who left all things. Whence also the pieces of silver are taken for all [0626D] money.

« My vineyard is before me. » It is the voice of the bridegroom, because after the resurrection to come all the saints shall be always with the Lord, as he himself says: « I will, Father, that where I am, they also may be with me [n: (Joan. I)]. » Otherwise, as if the bridegroom said: Truly, even though I commend thee to other keepers, yet I have thee always in my presence, seeing and rewarding in all things the devotion of thy labor. « A thousand are thine, O peaceable one. » « A thousand are thine » — pieces of silver is to be understood. As though he had said: Let him who for love of me lets go all his own know that he has them kept in peace; signifying the simpler sort in the Church, who lose for the charity of God all their substance, marked with the number of a thousand: « And two hundred to them they keep the fruits [0627A] thereof. » [var: Vulg. *et ducenti his qui custodiunt fructus ejus*, "and two hundred to them that keep the fruits thereof"; the printed lemma lacks *qui*] That is. the pieces of silver to those who keep the fruits thereof, who are the holy doctors, who let go all the things of the world and cease not to labor in the word of preaching; these are granted with me a twofold reward, which is designated by the number of a hundred. It may also be understood otherwise: For twelve, if they be multiplied by the number of a hundred, make a thousand two hundred. Which number surely befits the apostles or apostolic men.
