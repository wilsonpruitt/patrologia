## CHAPTER VIII. (cont.)

« Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken to thee, make me hear thy voice. » For in the gardens dwells the Church, who is already filled with the greenness of hope and of good works. As if he plainly said: Since the discourse must be ended, holding this last farewell heard from me, dwell thou ever [0627B] in the gates of the virtues, and know that the friends, that is the angelic spirits and the separated souls of the saints, are considering thee, and rejoice at thy progress. Likewise the friends hearken, because all the elect, that they may come alive again unto the heavenly fatherland, desire to hear the words of life: « Make me hear thy voice, » that is, let the word of preaching and the voice of praise be always heard from thee by me, as far as thou art able. To these things the bride answered:

« Flee away, my beloved, and be like to the roe and the young hart upon the mountain of spices. » [var: Vulg. *super montes aromatum*, "upon the mountains of spices" — the plural, which the gloss below itself prints] As if she plainly said: Since, appearing in the flesh and bringing to me the precepts and the gifts of life, now, these being accomplished, return into the bosom of the Father: O my beloved, « be like to the roe and the young hart upon the mountains of spices, » that is: and let this be my solace, that since by continual vision [0627C] I cannot behold thee, remember at least to console me by frequent visitation. Christ is like the roe, because of the likeness of sinful flesh, who was as it were outwardly begotten of the stock of the patriarchs. He is called a young hart because of the variety of the virtues, who, with the flesh wherewith he ascended into the heavens, with that same dwelt upon earth. By the mountains of spices are understood the angels, above whom the man assumed has been exalted, as the Psalmist says: « God is ascended with jubilation, and the Lord with the sound of the trumpet [n: (Psal. XLVI)]. » [var: Vulg. *in jubilo*] Hence he says truly: « Christ ascending on high led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men [n: (Ephes. IV)]. » Hence Luke says: « And the Lord Jesus, after he had spoken to them, ascended into heaven, and sitteth on the right hand of God [n: (Marc. XVI)]. » [var: Vulg. Mark 16:19 *assumptus est in caelum*, "was taken up into heaven"]

For now at length, God favoring us, the book demands its [0627D] end — the book which, down to the very heel, by his own support, with an investigation upright, as we believe, and true according to the mind of the doctors and to the frailty of our own understanding, we have carried through; and, O Caesar Augustus, we have striven, though fearfully, to obey the imperial and Augustan decrees. And, a suppliant, by supplicating I supplicate it of the summit of your excellency, that it may not irk your nobility to survey it with subtle investigation, and that your skill may with the utmost zeal study to read through likewise the little works of the holy doctors. For if the emperor Theodosius, born and reared in empire, stood forth in such excellence that he obtained the sole monarchy of the whole world, and took care to write out the book of Priscian the grammarian, the ornament of Roman eloquence, [0628A] most eloquent with wondrous eloquence, down to the very heel, with tender little fingers and with his own joints, and out of its price and the trafficking of its sale was ever wont to live, despising imperial banquets, and flourished with such zeal that by continual meditation and by his own copying he wrote out the Roman law by divine gift, and, as it were with the sword's point of the law, corrected the distorted morals of the Romans to the rule of rectitude, and restrained them from their hardness — so much so that, when the rest was finished, an angelic trumpet thundered upon him from heaven in a nocturnal vision, and through the vision of a dream, in congratulation, lifted up its voice, saying: Let the book, he says, of the law not depart from thy mouth; meditate therein day and night — how much more, O most gentle prince, [must you] with your own studies run through the authority of the Holy Scriptures with continual meditation, [0628B] and seek the height of the divine law as it were a mountain, that, instructed in spiritual doctrine, you may be able both sagaciously to advance yourself to the higher things of the virtues, and to summon others to the path of rectitude. Which assuredly you will not be able more fully to penetrate, and in a manner to climb the mountain of Holy Scripture, save by diligent zeal and purity of heart and chastity of body. For Holy Scripture, as the aforesaid expositor often says, is a mountain from which the Lord comes into our hearts. Whereof the prophet says: « God will come from Libanus, and the Holy One from the shady and thick mountain. » [var: Vet. Lat./LXX at Hab. 3:3; Vulg. *Deus ab austro veniet, et Sanctus de monte Pharan*, "God will come from the south, and the Holy One from mount Pharan"] That mountain is thick through its sentences, and shady through its allegories. But it must be known that, when the voice sounds upon the mountain, we are commanded to wash our garments, and to cleanse them from every defilement of the flesh, if we would hasten to approach the mountain. [0628C] For it is written: « That if a beast touch the mountain, it shall be stoned [n: (Hebr. XII)]. » A beast touches the mountain when anyone given over to wantonness or to irrational motions draws near to the loftiness of Holy Scripture, and understands it not according to what it ought to be, but irrationally bends it to the understanding of his own pleasure. But such a one, absurd or sluggish of sense, if he be seen about this mountain, is slain by most terrible sentences, as it were by stones. For this mountain burns, because Holy Scripture kindles with the fire of love him whom it spiritually fills. Whence it is said through Moses: « In his right hand a fiery law [n: (Deut. XXXII)]. » Because in the minds of the elect, who are to be set at the right hand of the judge, the divine precepts burn, and are kindled with the ardor of charity: therefore, O ornament of Augustus and glory [0628D] of the Frankish nation, study with reasonable minds and with chastities of body to climb this mountain, and, kindled with the fervor of charity, to penetrate it by reading; that, according to the merit of your understanding, and of the others whom your sagacious prudence shall have striven to correct, you may indeed deserve at length, the Lord favoring you, to approach the mountains of spices, that is the heights of the angels, and by the suffrages of their prayers to be placed at the right hand of the judge with the holy emperors and consuls, and likewise to hear from the Lord: « Well done, good servant, faithful, because thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will set thee over many; enter into the joy of thy Lord [n: (Matth. XXV)]. » Amen.
