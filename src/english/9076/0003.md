## Commentary on the Song of Songs (cont.)

[0306B] « The flowers have appeared in our land, the time of pruning is come. » The winter and the rain being ended, the flowers have appeared, namely the comeliness of the virtues and of chastity, and, the harshness of unbelief passing away, the spring mildness of faith has succeeded. « The time of pruning is come, » that is, of the purging of the saints. For the pruning of the vineyards of the saints signifies the purging which is in Christ and through Christ; concerning whom he himself says: « I am the vine, you the branches. » « The voice of the turtle is heard in our land. » This is the voice of the preaching apostles. For the turtledove is a bird; and since it is always wont to tarry and nest in lofty trees, it signifies the apostles or the other teachers, who can say: « Our conversation is in heaven. » And because it has a groaning for its song, it signifies the weeping of the saints, who [0306C] exhort their own to weeping and lamentation, saying: « Be miserable and mourn, » or, « your mourning shall be turned into joy. » The voice of the turtle is heard in our land; that is, the voice of the preaching apostles, in our land, that is, in the Church. Concerning which the Psalmist says: « The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof. »

« The fig tree hath put forth her green figs. » The fig tree signifies the Synagogue, and the green figs are the early and unripe figs, unfit for eating, which fall easily at the buffeting of the wind. When the voice of the turtle sounded, the fig tree put forth her green figs, because, the apostles preaching, there came forth many from the Synagogue of the Jews, who believed in Christ, and yet wished to observe the law carnally, and were more delighted by the authority of the law than by the sweetness of the Gospel, wishing to observe circumcision, [0306D] the Sabbath, and the other precepts of the law according to the letter. Concerning whom the Apostle says: They are zealous for you not well, but they would exclude you, that you might be zealous for them; that they may glory in your flesh. « The vineyards in flower yield their sweet smell. » After the voice of the turtle was heard, after the fig tree put forth her green figs, the vineyards in flower yielded their odor; because the multitude of churches was diffused throughout the breadth of the world, which should spread abroad far and wide from themselves the flowers of the virtues and the odor of good repute. « Arise, make haste, my love, my fair one, and come. » Be unwilling (he says), my bride, whom I have made my love through faith and affection, to grow sluggish in idle time, but arise and come, and show outwardly to thy neighbors the examples of the virtues.

[0307A] « My dove, in the clefts of the rock. » If the rock is Christ, according to what the Apostle says, « And the rock was Christ, » the clefts of the rock are the wounds which he received on the cross for our salvation, namely the piercings of the nails and the wound of the lance. In these clefts, therefore, the dove, that is, the Church, tarries, because she has placed all the hope of her salvation in the passion of her Redeemer. There, safe from the snares of the malignant spirits as from the seizure of a hawk, she lies hidden; there she nests, that is, she gathers the offspring of good works. « In the hollow of the wall. » Walls are wont to be made of dry stones for the fortification of vineyards, to drive off foxes or other harmful little beasts. By the wall, therefore, is signified the guardianship of the heavenly virtues, namely the angelic protection, as Isaiah says of the Lord's [0307B] vineyard: « And he made a wall about it, and dug round about the vineyard. » In the hollow of the wall, therefore, she tarries, because she is on every side surrounded by the protection of the angels, and guarded from the temptations of the devil. « Show me thy face, let thy voice sound in my ears, for thy voice is sweet, and thy face comely. » Thou (he says), my beloved, who restest most sweetly in the bed of contemplation, come and show me thy face; that is, go forth from the secret of quiet to public action, and show forth the beauty of thy good works to others for an example. « Show me, » he says: for the Church shows as it were her face to God, when to her neighbors, for their profit and advancement, she demonstrates by the works of the virtues how great a beauty she has within. This is what the Lord says in the Gospel, « Let them see [0307C] your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven. » « Let thy voice sound in my ears. » The voice, namely, of preaching or of divine praise, by which thou mayest make others to profit. For the voice of the Church sounds in the ears of the Lord, when through preaching God is delighted by the conversion of many. And well he says: « Show me thy face, » and thy voice, not another's; namely, show me thy face, which I gave thee in baptism, when I cleansed thee from all sin. « Let thy voice, » he says, « sound in my ears, » that it may proceed from a sincere intention of my love, that thou preach for no other reason than for my sake.

« Catch us the little foxes, that destroy the vines, for our vineyard hath flourished. » It is the voice of the bridegroom commanding [0307D] his bride and the daughters of Jerusalem, namely the holy teachers or the faithful souls. « Catch us (he says) the little foxes. » The fox is a crafty and fraudulent animal, dwelling in dens and caves, and going not by a straight but by a winding path: and therefore it designates the heretics or schismatics, who destroy the vines, that is, the Church of God, namely the people of the faithful, and cease not to tear and deceive them. He commands, therefore, that they catch the little foxes, that is, that they cease not to war down and destroy heretical depravity in its very beginning, lest, become strong, it assail more mightily. « For our vineyard hath flourished, » that is, the Church throughout the whole world has put forth the flowers of the virtues. And note that whereas above he said "vines" in the plural, [0308A] now he says in the singular, « our vineyard hath flourished, » because out of many churches there is one elect Church.

« My beloved to me, and I to him, who feedeth among the lilies, till the day break, and the shadows incline. » The voice of the bride, « my beloved to me, » supply: let him be joined by the bond of charity and love, and I to him let me be conjoined and associated by the alternation of mutual love. And « my beloved, » supply: let him give the words of exhortation and of divine promise, « and I, » supply: will show him my face. Or also: may my beloved deign to be joined to me by the glue of charity, and I to him let me be joined by the bond of worthy obedience, obeying the precepts of him who feedeth among the lilies, that is, is delighted and gladdened among the white and fragrant virtues of the saints. And it is to be noted that above « lily » is said [0308B] in the singular, but here in the plural « lilies, » because many are the virtues of the saints, yet there is one faith, and one charity, with which God is especially delighted. Or also, « he feedeth among the lilies, » that is, he is most sweetly delighted among the virginal choirs. For the whiteness of the lilies is rightly compared to virginal purity. « Till the day break, » that is, till the clouds of the present life pass away, and the day appear, that is, till the brightness of everlasting blessedness come. And the meaning is: My beloved bestows on me his aid, and I offer him worthy obedience, who is delighted with the virtues of the saints, until, the night of the present life being run through, in which he is discerned through a glass and in a dark manner, the day of the heavenly brightness appear, that he may be seen face to face.

« Return, be like, my beloved, to a roe or [0308C] to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel. » The voice of the Church desiring the sight of her bridegroom. O my beloved, who commandest me to arise from my bed, and exhortest me to show thee my face, return, that is, permit me to behold the brightness of thy divinity, and this which thou perfectly promisest me in the other life, namely thy blessedness, permit me at least to have in this pilgrimage still in a figure and in a dark manner. « Be like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel. » It has already been said above that the roes look down on the plains, and strive toward the heights of the mountains. Christ, therefore, is compared to roes, because, rising according to the flesh from the dead, he sought the heights of the heavens; but he is called a young hart, that is, the son of the ancient fathers. For the harts are the patriarchs and prophets, [0308D] from whom Christ drew the origin of his flesh. The young hart is said to have this nature, that in the burning of the sun it seeks shady and dark places, in which it may be protected from the heat; so too Christ rests in the minds of those who by the dew of the Holy Spirit are tempered from the heats of carnal pleasures. As if he said: O beloved, return, that is, gladden me often by illumining me with thy visitation, thou who through the flesh which thou tookest on hast now penetrated the heights of the heavens. But the mountains of Bethel signify the minds of the saints, raised up from earthly things by heavenly desires. For Bethel, "the rising house," or "the house of vigils," signifies the watchful minds of the saints, which rise up to desire the heavenly things, and keep watch to receive them, [0309A] having God as their indweller within them, who says through the Prophet: « I will dwell in them, and walk among them. » Upon the mountains of Bethel, therefore, the beloved is like a roe or a young hart, because he deigns to visit those minds which strive to be the house of God.

« In my little bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, and found him not. » The voice of the Church gathered from the Gentiles. In my little bed (she says), while I was still placed in the darkness of unbelief and ignorance, I sought him whom my soul loveth. For many of the philosophers, being ignorant of God, yet with the highest zeal sought him, wishing to know the Creator through the creature; as Plato, who in the Timaeus disputed much concerning the soul, and as Aristotle, Socrates, [0309B] and the rest, who spent all the time of their life in the studies of searching out the truth. « I sought him, and found him not. » For God could not be known through worldly wisdom.

« I will rise, and will go about the city: in the streets and the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth. I sought him, and found him not. » I will rise up from there, from the couch of the body and of carnal delight, and I will go about the city of this world, traversing seas and lands. « In the streets and the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth, » that is, I will look upon those who walk in the wide ways of this world, and are given over to their pleasures, whether perchance in them I may look upon the traces of my beloved. Although this may be said of many, yet it was especially fulfilled in that eunuch [0309C] who came from the ends of the earth to Jerusalem to adore the God of heaven, whom Philip found, and showed him Christ in the prophecy of Isaiah. This too was fulfilled in Cornelius, who, still a pagan and ignorant of God, gave alms, and was constant in prayers, whom Peter baptized with his household. And there was still a difficulty of finding, and she says: « I sought, and found him not. »

« The watchmen who keep the city found me. » The watchmen are the holy ones and the other teachers of the Church, who keep the city, that is, the holy Church, and defend it from the snares of the unfaithful enemies. Whom she asks, « Have you seen him whom my soul loveth? » The Church asks as it were the watchmen, when with attentive ear she receives their preaching.
