## Commentary on the Song of Songs (cont.)

[0309D] « When I had a little passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth. » To pass by the watchmen is to search out their sayings and teaching diligently. For we are wont to say: I have run through a book in reading it, or I have passed through it. When (she says) I had passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth. Because when with careful meditation we seek out the sayings or writings of the saints, at once we find the beloved, because we discover the Lord in their doctrine. It can also be understood thus: When I had passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth, that is, when I had understood that Christ surpasses all loftiness and grace, then I found him whom my soul loveth, that is, then I truly understood how far he stands apart from the merits of the other saints. « I held [0310A] him, nor will I let him go, till I bring him into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her that bore me; » that is, with a most ardent faith and devotion I cleaved to him. « Nor will I let him go, » but I will persevere in his love and faith, « till I bring him into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her that bore me; » that is, till I preach him to the Synagogue, which is spiritually my mother, and lead her too to his faith. This shall come to pass in the day of judgment, when (as the Apostle says) « the fullness of the Gentiles shall have come in, then all Israel shall be saved. » So greatly, therefore, does the Church promise herself concerning the charity of her bridegroom, that she believes she can even convert the Synagogue. But that which she says, « Till I bring him, » it is not to be thought that she will then leave him, when she shall have brought him into the chamber of her mother, [0310B] but *Till* is put for the everlasting, that is, I will always cleave to him, I will always persevere in his faith and love. Such is the passage in the psalm: So « our eyes are unto the Lord our God, until he have mercy on us. » Shall it be that after he has had mercy on us, our eyes will not be unto him? Surely they will.

« I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she please. » This little verse is repeated twice: for it has already been said above, but above it pertains to the primitive Church gathered from the Jews, whereas here to the Church gathered from the Gentiles, and coupled to Christ by the bond of love. For one and the same is God's care concerning the saints, who pleased him either in the circumcision, or also in the time of baptism, when the [0310C] grace of the Gospel shone forth. But because this little verse has been expounded above, it is now to be seen why he brings in the Church as it were sleeping, whence too he adjures the daughters of Jerusalem not to rouse her, when a little above he brings her in not sleeping or resting, but applying herself to the zeal of a most laborious search, by which she sought the bridegroom. If, then, she was seeking the bridegroom, how was she resting? But it is a most sweet and most pleasant sleep to seek Christ. The Church, therefore, sleeps, and seeks Christ; she sleeps, namely, from earthly desires, from the acts of the world, but she watches and seeks the bridegroom, because she cleaves to his contemplation, she desires him alone, she strives to attain to him. The bridegroom, therefore, adjures the daughters of Jerusalem, namely the souls cleaving to the desires of the heavenly fatherland, [0310D] that they rouse her not from this sleep of most sweet quiet and contemplation until she herself please. But sleep is deservedly called death, because as one sleeping closes his eyes to visible things, and contemplates the invisible, so he who applies himself to divine contemplation is as it were a stranger, and sleeps to outward things, and sees only the spiritual and heavenly things. Nor is it a wonder if love is called sleep, since in what follows love is called death: « Love is strong as death. » But when he says: « Until she please, » he shows that the Church of the Gentiles will willingly prepare herself even to bear labors and persecutions for Christ.

« Who is she that goeth up by the desert, as a little rod of smoke of the aromatical spices of myrrh and frankincense, and of all the powders [0311A] of the perfumer? » The voice of the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, of the saints of the primitive Church, marveling at the beauty of the Church gathered from the Gentiles. « Who is she (they say)? » Who, that is, of what sort. Of how great praise and admiration worthy, who, not cleansed by circumcision, nor yet sanctified by baptism, already runs to God? « She goeth up (they say) by the desert. » This is said in imitation of the ancient Jewish people, which, freed from the Egyptian servitude, through the desert, the Red Sea being crossed, came to the land of promise. So too the Church went up through the desert of the nations, now freed from Egypt (that is, from the confusion of idolatry), and, Pharaoh, that is, the devil, being drowned, that she might be fed with the heavenly manna, and enter the true land of promise. « She goeth up (they say) » to the mountains [0311B] of the virtues, or also to him himself, raised up by the virtues, who says in the psalm: « I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, » etc., and elsewhere: « To thee have I lifted up my eyes, who dwellest in the heavens. » The mountains themselves are the patriarchs, prophets, apostles, and the other saints of God, who are deservedly called mountains of the virtues. For first the Church, or the faithful soul, lifts up her eyes to the mountains, that is, to the saints, because she considers their teaching and acts, and rejoices to be fortified by their example and intercession. Afterward, advancing, she dares to direct the gaze of her heart to him too who made the mountains, and says: « To thee have I lifted up my eyes, who dwellest in the heavens, » that is, who restest graciously in the saints. « As (she says) a little rod of smoke of the aromatical spices of myrrh and frankincense. » Smoke is born of fire, and soon seeks the higher parts of the air, until [0311C] it is withdrawn from the sight of those beholding. Smoke, therefore, signifies holy desire suffused with the fire of divine love. And not absurdly is the smoke compared to a little rod, because the holy Church, or the soul loving God, is slender and delicate, made thin by the disciplines of the Holy Spirit, not having the thickness of carnal desires. Or the little rods of smoke are compared to unscattered smoke, on account of the unity of the Church, which in Christ is one. And whence that smoke came, she shows when she adds: « Of myrrh and frankincense, and of all the powders of the perfumer. » With myrrh the bodies of the dead are embalmed, but frankincense is burnt in the sacrifice of God: and therefore by the myrrh the mortification of the flesh is designated, by the frankincense the purity of prayers is expressed. To little rods of smoke, therefore, of myrrh and frankincense the Church is likened, because the saints, by the mortification of their flesh [0311D] and by prayers suffused with a pure and simple heart, strive to please God. « And of all (she says) the powders of the perfumer, » that is, of the heap of all the virtues. But the aromatical spices are said not to have been whole, but reduced to powder, because the actions of the saints are to be considered with great discretion, and to be winnowed as it were with the finest sieve of consideration, lest perchance anything unfitting exist in them, or the thousand-formed adversary steal in through the very virtues, who, being crafty, is wont to do harm even through good things.

« Behold the little bed of Solomon, threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround it, all holding swords, and most expert in war. Every man's sword upon his thigh. » Whither this beloved had gone up is shown [0312A] when it is added: « Behold the little bed of Solomon, » etc. The bed of Solomon, although the heavenly rest of that celestial blessedness may be understood, in which God rests with his saints, yet is more probably understood as the present rest, in which the saints of God, the tumults of the vices being lulled to sleep, are delighted with the embrace of Solomon, that is, of the true Peaceable One. Whence too the Psalmist says of Christ: « His place is made in peace, and his abode in Zion. » The little bed of Solomon, therefore, that is, the Church of Christ, threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround. By the threescore valiant ones is understood the order of the elect, who either by preaching fortify the Church, or by contemplating desire to attain to that heavenly blessedness. Now the number sixty consists of six and ten. For six times ten, or ten times six, [0312B] make sixty. And six signifies the perfection of work, because in six days God perfected his works: but ten signifies the recompense and reward, which at the end shall be given to the elect. Whence too those who came into the vineyard are read to have received a penny. The threescore valiant ones, therefore, signify (as has been said) the holy teachers, valiant and constant in mind. And it is added: « Of the most valiant of Israel. » All who believe in Christ, and love Christ, are called Israel (that is, "a man seeing God"). But the most valiant of Israel are those who know how to protect the Church by preaching, and to defend it from the assaults of demons or the attack of heretics. These, therefore, surround the little bed of Solomon, because to them the Church is committed, and by them it is to be guarded with watchful care. « All holding swords, and most expert in war; » [0312C] for they hold the spiritual sword of the word of God, of which the Apostle says: « And take (he says) the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God. » And they are most expert in war, because it is necessary that those be instructed in the art of spiritual battle who wage wars against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places. For that enemy is most cunning, and of so great subtlety of spiritual nature, being also by the long span of time most expert, against whom the saints take up their wars. « Every man's sword upon his thigh because of fears in the night. » By the thigh is received the propagation of offspring. Upon the thigh, therefore, they have their swords, because by the fear of God all are restrained, and they curb the motions of carnal pleasure. And this « because of [0312D] fears in the night, » that is, because of the hidden snares of the malignant enemy, who in the night of this world especially lies in wait for the saints who are preeminent in the Church, that, they being deceived, he may defile the little bed of Solomon, that is, the rest of the saints. And it is to be noted that above they are said to hold swords, but here to have the sword upon the thigh. For they hold swords, that they may resist the adversaries, and pierce through the heretics and all who contradict the sound faith. Upon the thigh, therefore, they have their swords, that they may take care of themselves, that they may repress the flux of carnal pleasures, lest, preaching to others, they themselves become reprobate.

« King Solomon made himself a litter of the wood of Lebanon. » What above was designated by the bed of Solomon, this [0313A] now is designated by the litter, namely the holy Church, in which the saints of God rest as in a bed, and as in a bed recline at the banquets of eternal satiety. Of the wood of Lebanon Solomon made a litter. Lebanon is a mountain of Phoenicia, whose trees are preeminent above the rest in height and beauty and durability, and therefore they signify the saints shining with the hope of the virtues and hastening to the eternal things. For Lebanon is interpreted "whitening," or "whitening out." The wood of Lebanon, therefore, are the saints made white and whitened in baptism, and adorned with all the beauty of the virtues.
