## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

« My beloved to me, and I to him, who feedeth among the lilies, till the day break, and the shadows incline. » The voice of the bride, « my beloved to me, » supply: let him be joined by the bond of charity and love, and I to him let me be conjoined and associated by the alternation of mutual love. And « my beloved, » supply: let him give the words of exhortation and of divine promise, « and I, » supply: will show him my face. Or also: may my beloved deign to be joined to me by the glue of charity, and I to him let me be joined by the bond of worthy obedience, obeying the precepts of him who feedeth among the lilies, that is, is delighted and gladdened among the white and fragrant virtues of the saints. And it is to be noted that above « lily » is said [0308B] in the singular, but here in the plural « lilies, » because many are the virtues of the saints, yet there is one faith, and one charity, with which God is especially delighted. Or also, « he feedeth among the lilies, » that is, he is most sweetly delighted among the virginal choirs. For the whiteness of the lilies is rightly compared to virginal purity. « Till the day break, » that is, till the clouds of the present life pass away, and the day appear, that is, till the brightness of everlasting blessedness come. And the meaning is: My beloved bestows on me his aid, and I offer him worthy obedience, who is delighted with the virtues of the saints, until, the night of the present life being run through, in which he is discerned through a glass and in a dark manner, the day of the heavenly brightness appear, that he may be seen face to face.

« Return, be like, my beloved, to a roe or [0308C] to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel. » The voice of the Church desiring the sight of her bridegroom. O my beloved, who commandest me to arise from my bed, and exhortest me to show thee my face, return, that is, permit me to behold the brightness of thy divinity, and this which thou perfectly promisest me in the other life, namely thy blessedness, permit me at least to have in this pilgrimage still in a figure and in a dark manner. « Be like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel. » It has already been said above that the roes look down on the plains, and strive toward the heights of the mountains. Christ, therefore, is compared to roes, because, rising according to the flesh from the dead, he sought the heights of the heavens; but he is called a young hart, that is, the son of the ancient fathers. For the harts are the patriarchs and prophets, [0308D] from whom Christ drew the origin of his flesh. The young hart is said to have this nature, that in the burning of the sun it seeks shady and dark places, in which it may be protected from the heat; so too Christ rests in the minds of those who by the dew of the Holy Spirit are tempered from the heats of carnal pleasures. As if he said: O beloved, return, that is, gladden me often by illumining me with thy visitation, thou who through the flesh which thou tookest on hast now penetrated the heights of the heavens. But the mountains of Bethel signify the minds of the saints, raised up from earthly things by heavenly desires. For Bethel, "the rising house," or "the house of vigils," signifies the watchful minds of the saints, which rise up to desire the heavenly things, and keep watch to receive them, [0309A] having God as their indweller within them, who says through the Prophet: « I will dwell in them, and walk among them. » Upon the mountains of Bethel, therefore, the beloved is like a roe or a young hart, because he deigns to visit those minds which strive to be the house of God.
