## CHAPTER IV.

« How beautiful art thou, my love, how beautiful art thou and comely. » The voice of Christ. He repeats twice, « how beautiful art thou, » namely in work and in preaching. In work, because thou doest nothing foul, nothing unworthy of my eyes; in preaching, because thou ceasest not to invite even thy companions of Jerusalem to consider the mystery of my incarnation. « Thy eyes are dove's eyes. » By the eyes is understood the keenness of the spiritual senses, [0315A] with which the Church contemplates the divine and everlasting things. But the eyes of the Church are compared to a dove on account of spiritual grace, because the Holy Spirit appeared in the form of a dove. There follows: « Besides that which is hid within; » that is, besides the glory of that heavenly recompense which thou art to receive at the end of the world, which, although in the present thou contemplate it through faith, yet lies hidden within: for it cannot be seen in the present, but shall be perfected in the future. « Thy hair is as flocks of goats, which come up from Mount Galaad. » By the hair of the Church are received the subtle thoughts of the Church, by which temporal things are for the most part disposed. And they are compared to flocks of goats, because these animals are clean, and feed on their backs among the crags or in lofty places; because the thoughts of the saints, [0315B] although for bodily necessity they are engaged in temporal things, yet do not depart from the intention of the eternal, and even in that very care which they bestow on bodily necessity, they as it were always gaze upon heaven. We can also by the eyes receive the teachers and preachers of the Church, who in the body, whose head is Christ, hold the highest place, and see heavenly and spiritual things beyond the other members. But by the hair, the innumerable multitude of the simple faithful, established in the lay order, who, although they see spiritual things less, yet by their multitude and number furnish a great adornment to the Church. And well are they compared to flocks of goats. The goat suits sinners, by which are understood the peoples of the faithful given over to the actions of the world, which cannot be done without sin. Well [0315C] does it follow: « Which come up from Mount Galaad. » Galaad is interpreted "heap of testimony" according to the letter, for this reason, that when Jacob was returning from Mesopotamia, Laban pursued him, and on the third day found him, and there in testimony of the mutual covenant they raised a heap of stones. Mystically, the heap of testimony is Christ, in whom stands the multitude of all the saints, who are living stones, adhering to that true stone of which Peter says: « Unto whom coming, a living stone, you also as living stones are built up. » The flocks of goats, therefore, came up from Mount Galaad, because the multitudes of the saints ascend to the heights of the virtues, and strive to follow in all things the teaching and precepts of Christ.

« Thy teeth as flocks of sheep that are shorn, which [0315D] come up from the washing. » As by the hair of the Church the innumerable multitude of the simple faithful is understood, so also by the teeth the teachers of the Church are understood: for with the teeth we grind our food. And the holy teachers are well conformed to teeth, because the spiritual foods which the simple cannot grasp, they in a certain manner break up small by expounding. Peter especially was the teeth of the Church, to whom it was said in the vision: « Arise, kill, and eat. » As if it were said: Those whom thou convertest to the faith, kill from that which they are, that they may cease to be sinners; and eat, that is, transform them into the body of the Church. And well are these teeth compared to sheep shorn and washed in the washing. For sheep are wont after shearing to be washed in running water, [0316A] lest the unwashed sweat cling to the body, and generate the scab. For shorn are the holy teachers and masters of the Church, who are both washed in the washing of baptism; and have lost their fleeces, that is, their faculties and substances, for Christ. To all the faithful indeed suits that which he says: « Which come up from the washing, » for no one can be faithful without the washing of baptism. But shearing does not suit all, because not all are so perfect that they are able to lose their goods for Christ, but this especially befits those who, according to the word of the Lord, sell all their goods, and give to the poor, and naked and unencumbered follow Christ. Such, therefore, are the teeth of the holy Church, that is, its preachers and masters, sheep washed in baptism through innocence, rejoicing in voluntary poverty [0316B] and in the surrender of their goods. There follows: « All with twins, and there is none barren among them. » Because they are preeminent in the twin charity and love of God and of the neighbor, and teach that this be observed, or also because they preach faith and work. And there is none barren among them, namely no one estranged from good works.

« Thy lips are as a scarlet fillet, and thy speech is sweet. » By the lips of the Church the holy preachers are received, who are also figured by the teeth. But scarlet has the appearance of blood or of fire. The lips of the Church, therefore (that is, the holy preachers), are compared to a scarlet fillet, because they assiduously preach the passion of Christ, who for our redemption poured out his blood; or they are likened to a scarlet fillet, because the preachers burn with love, loving those [0316C] to whom they impart words, and also those from whom they suffer evils. To this too it pertains that the Holy Spirit appeared in fiery tongues, because he made the apostles both speaking and burning, burning with love, speaking with preaching. And well is it called a scarlet fillet. For a fillet binds and constrains the hair, and the holy teachers by their preaching bind together the multitude of the faithful in the Church unto the unity of faith and love, and reduce them into one comeliness. By the hair can also be received the subtle thoughts of the mind. With a scarlet fillet, therefore, we bind the hairs, when we constrain and curb our thoughts with the discipline of the fear of God. « And thy speech is sweet. » Sweet is the speech of the Church, when the holy teachers either recall to memory the passion of their Redeemer, [0316D] and preach how greatly God loved man, or also when they announce the heavenly rewards to their hearers. « Thy cheeks are as a piece of a pomegranate, besides that which lieth hid within. » By the cheeks we said above that the Church is figured. For when we are ashamed, the redness of blood is poured out in the cheeks. But the pomegranate has a reddish rind, while within it is full of a multitude of seeds: whence too it is called the seeded apple. By the piece of a pomegranate, therefore, is received the memory of that same Lord's passion, which is also figured above in the scarlet. For one thing is wont to be expressed by diverse figures. The cheeks of the Church, therefore, are compared to a piece of a pomegranate, because the holy teachers reverently keep the memory of the passion [0317A] of their Redeemer, nor are they ashamed of his cross, nay, they glory, saying with the Apostle: « But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ; » and elsewhere the Apostle, writing to his disciple: « Be not thou (he says) ashamed of the cross of Christ, nor of me his prisoner. » And well are the cheeks compared not to a whole pomegranate, but to a piece. For the pomegranate, when it is broken, shows redness without, whiteness within. So too the holy preachers, when they announce the passion of Christ, are as it were red without; but when they demonstrate how great a profit of our redemption has come thence, and that man through the passion of Christ has merited not only to be justified from sins, but even to be admitted to the divine fellowship, it is as it were white which lies hidden within. There can [0317B] also by the cheeks of the Church be figured the martyrs, who are reddened by the shedding of their blood, like a pomegranate. But the pomegranate being broken, whiteness appears within, because after death they shine with miracles.

« Thy neck is as the tower of David, which is built with bulwarks. » The neck of the holy Church is its holy preachers and teachers. For the same are here designated by the neck who above were designated by the eyes or by the teeth; namely the holy preachers, who, like necks, transmit to the body, for its nourishing, the food of the word received, and utter forth the voice of divine preaching to their hearers. Or also the holy teachers are marked by the neck, because as through the neck the head and the other members are joined to the body, so the holy teachers are as it were mediators between Christ and the rest [0317C] of the faithful whom they instruct, and as it were join Christ to his body, that is, to the Church. And this neck is compared to the tower of David. David, who is interpreted "strong of hand" or "desirable in appearance," signifies Christ. And the whole Church indeed is the city of David, that is, of the great king Christ, who is strong of hand, since he conquered the devil, according to what the Psalmist says: « The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle. » He is also desirable in appearance, because (as the Apostle says) the angels desire to look upon him. But the tower of this city are those who are preeminent above the rest either in knowledge or in the perfection of work. « Which is built with bulwarks. » The bulwarks are the divine mysteries of the holy Scriptures, from which, as it were darts, proceed the shafts by which the adversary [0317D] powers are driven back. « A thousand bucklers hang upon it, all the armor of valiant men. » By the thousand bucklers are understood the innumerable defenses of the divine protection, with which the holy Church is walled about and defended. « All the armor of valiant men, » that is, the equipment either of holy work or of divine preaching. And well is the neck of the Church, that is, its preachers and teachers, compared to the tower of David, because they are always as it were in war, fighting for the defense of the holy Church.

« Thy two breasts are like two young roes that are twins. » The two breasts of the Church signify the same holy teachers. Nor is it a wonder that one and the same thing is figured in diverse ways. For the holy teachers are eyes, because the ways of life which they ought to enter upon they [0318A] demonstrate to those subject to them; they are teeth, because they grind and break up small the foods of the divine Scriptures, that they may be chewed; they are the neck, because they join the head and the members, that is, Christ and the Church. These same teachers, therefore, are also the breasts of the Church, because they nourish the little ones and the simple faithful with the milk of the word of God, according to what the Apostle, speaking to the more simple, says: « I could not speak to you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal, and as unto little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not meat. » And well he says not simply "breasts," but with an addition, « two breasts, » since a woman has neither more nor less than two breasts, because assuredly the holy teachers nourish the little ones from either people, namely the Jewish and the Gentile, with the milk of the word of God. For Paul too says of himself: « He who wrought in Peter [0318B] to the apostleship of the circumcision, wrought in me also among the Gentiles. » And fitly are the two breasts said to be as two young twin roes, because namely they are the offspring and the sons of our Savior Jesus Christ, who above was designated by the roes: for these animals both see most keenly, and with exceeding swiftness ascend the higher places. So too the teachers of Christ both themselves see what is to be done, and show it to others, and, despising earthly things, strive toward the heavenly. « Who feed among the lilies, » that is, they are delighted with the purest and brightest senses of the Scriptures.
