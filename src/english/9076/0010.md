## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

« Thy lips are a dripping honeycomb, O bride. » A honeycomb is honey in the wax. Now honey in the wax is spiritual understanding within the letter. The lips of the Church, therefore, are called a dripping honeycomb, because the holy teachers, who are designated by the lips, bring forth spiritual instructions for the faithful who are to be taught. And not only a honeycomb, but a dripping one are the lips of the Church called. For the honeycomb drips, when [0321C] the abundance of honey overflowing can no longer be contained in the waxen cells. Rightly, therefore, is Holy Scripture called a dripping honeycomb, because it is understood in manifold ways and expounded in various senses, now according to the letter, now according to allegory, now according to morality, now according to anagogy, that is, the higher sense. « Honey and milk are under thy tongue. » Milk befits little ones, and therefore by milk is designated the simple doctrine with which the beginning of believers is imbued; but honey, which is believed to be made from the dew of heaven, signifies the heavenly and spiritual doctrine which befits the perfect and the instructed. Under the tongue of the Church, then, are honey and milk, because sometimes they announce the heavenly mysteries to the perfect, sometimes plain and simple things to the untrained. « And the odor of thy garments is like the odor of frankincense. » The garments of the Church are the ornaments of good [0321D] works, according to what John says in the Apocalypse: « For the fine linen is the justifications of the saints. » And in Job it is said: « I was clothed with justice, and I clothed myself as with a robe and a diadem, with my judgment. » But by frankincense we said the purity of holy prayers is designated. The garments of the Church, then, are compared to frankincense, because all the works of the Church are as it were prayers. For in all that she does she always entreats the Lord, and in such manner is fulfilled that which the Lord says: « We ought always to pray. » Nor can this be fulfilled before the Lord in any other way, unless our whole life and conversation be such that it may be reckoned as prayer in the sight of God. Frankincense is a tree of Arabia, in bark and leaf like the laurel, sending forth a juice after the manner of the almond, [0322A] which is gathered twice in the year, in autumn and in spring. In the autumn gathering the trees are prepared, the bark being cut in the most burning heat of summer, and thus, the rich foam leaping forth, which when hardened is thickened, where the nature of the place demands, a palm mat catching it. What has clung to the tree is scraped off with iron, and therefore what is of the bark seems to be the purest, that is, the white frankincense. The second vintage is in spring, the bark being cut for it in winter; this comes out red, and not to be compared with the former. That of the young tree is believed to be whiter, but that of the old more fragrant. What has hung from it in the roundness of a drop, we call male; the drop struck off by a blow, we call manna.

« An enclosed garden is my sister, my bride, an enclosed garden, a sealed fountain. » His sister and bride he calls [0322B] the Church, whom from a handmaid he established to be his sister, and, pledged with the dowry of the Holy Spirit, he made his bride. This Church, therefore, is a garden, because she brings forth the shoots of spiritual virtues, which are designated in what follows by the names of aromatics. And this garden is enclosed, because the holy Church is fortified by the help of her Redeemer and Lord, and walled about with the protection of the angelic powers, and lies open to no snares of the malign spirits. This same Church is a sealed fountain. A fountain, because it flows with the streams of heavenly doctrine, whereby it washes all who believe in Christ from their sins, and gives them the knowledge of truth to drink. And this fountain is sealed, because it is fortified with the seal of Gospel truth, so that neither heretics nor malign spirits are able to violate or break into the catholic faith. [0322C] This garden, or this fountain (that is, the Church), was first planted or sprung up in a small little place of Judaea, and then, preaching being disseminated throughout the whole world, sent forth its beds or its little streams. Whence follows:

« Thy shoots are a paradise of pomegranates with the fruits of the orchard. » The shoots of the Church (as has been said) are the increases of faith and the dissemination of preaching. And these shoots are a paradise. Paradise in Greek is garden in Latin, because that primitive Church, which in Judaea was as it were a garden, sent forth throughout the whole world many gardens, that is, Churches. But the pomegranates, which have a ruddy bark, signify those who were not only [0322D] sanctified by the general baptism, but also, baptized in their own blood in martyrdom, were made martyrs. But the fruits of the orchard signify the works of the virtues, or those who exercise the virtues themselves in deed. The shoots of the Church, then, were a paradise of pomegranates, because after the apostles the martyrs obtained the first place in the Church, who strove to imitate the very passion of the Lord by suffering; although generally the whole multitude may be taken as a paradise of pomegranates, who are pomegranates because they always retain in memory the passion of their Redeemer. And just as the pomegranate contains within one bark a multitude of seeds, so in the Church the multitude of the faithful is contained within the faith of the Lord's passion. Hence [0323A] the Apostle says: « As many of you as have been baptized in Christ Jesus, have been baptized in his death. »

« Cypress with spikenard, spikenard and saffron, sweet cane and cinnamon with all the trees of Lebanon. » Cypress is an aromatic tree, having a seed like coriander, that is, white and somewhat translucent, which is boiled in oil, and thence is pressed out that which is called cyprus, from which a royal ointment is prepared. We read in Exodus that the manna was like the white seed of coriander. Cypress, therefore, and manna have one signification. For they signify heavenly grace, coming, as it were, like manna from the heavenly places. Cypress, then, grows in the garden of the Church, when all the faithful are taught to prefer heavenly grace above all things, and to hope for salvation through it. For no one can be saved by his own virtues, but only by the grace [0323B] of God. Spikenard is a type of the Lord's passion. Whence also Mary anointed the head and feet of Jesus with genuine spikenard. Spikenard, then, is in the garden of the Church, when all the saints venerate the memory of the Lord's passion, and give him thanks that he so loved them that he took upon himself death for their salvation and deliverance. « Spikenard and saffron. » Saffron has a flower of golden color, and therefore signifies charity. For as gold is more precious among all metals, so charity holds the primacy among all the virtues, the Apostle saying: « But now there remain faith, hope, charity; but the greater of these is charity. » With spikenard, then, saffron grows in the garden of the Church, when the members of Christ, that is, all the faithful, strive effectually to hold the love of God and of neighbor. Now saffron [0323C] is said to cool fiery fevers; so true charity cools the ardor of worldly concupiscence, and kindles in the mind the love of God and of neighbor. » Sweet cane and cinnamon. » The sweet cane is a short shrub, which is also called casia, with a purple bark. It signifies the saints in the Church who are eminent in humility and patience, namely the poor in spirit, whose is the kingdom of heaven. And the purple bark signifies the likeness of the passion of Christ, which those who are grounded in true humility, ever retaining it in their heart, strive to imitate. Cinnamon too is itself a short shrub, but of wondrous virtue and odor, and therefore they designate the progress of humility, which is designated also by the sweet cane. The sweet cane, then, and cinnamon are in the Church, when the saints of God truly hold humility and patience [0323D] in their heart, and outwardly show them to others. But that the sweet cane has a ruddy bark, while cinnamon has an ashen color, signifies that the saints of God, the more they are mindful of the passion of their Redeemer, the more base and contemptible they are in their own eyes, saying with Abraham: « I will speak to my Lord, though I am dust and ashes; » and with Job: « Therefore I reprove myself, and do penance in ember and ash. » « With all the trees of Lebanon. » The trees of Lebanon are eminent in beauty and height and strength, and therefore signify the teachers and all the perfect in the Church, of whom above the house of the Church is read to be made, the same Church saying: The beams of our houses are of cedar, [0324A] our ceilings of cypress, » because, namely, the holy teachers fortify the Church by their preaching. With the sweet cane, then, and cinnamon all the trees of Lebanon are mentioned in the garden of the Church, because whether the humble and patient, or all the perfect teachers, have the same faith in the Church, and await the same beatitude. « Myrrh and aloe with all the chief ointments. » Myrrh is a tree whose juice is called stacte, of such virtue that whatever is touched by it is kept unharmed from all rot and worms. Aloe is a tree of most sweet odor, so much so that it is burned upon the altars in place of incense. But it has a most bitter juice, resisting rot and worms. Hence also in the passion of the Lord Nicodemus is said to have brought a mixture of myrrh and aloe about a hundred [0324B] pounds. By these trees, then, is designated the continence and chastity of the flesh. For rot is wont to designate luxury, according to what the Prophet says of certain men: « The beasts have rotted in their dung. » In the garden of the Church, then, are myrrh and aloe, that is, heavenly minds free from all corruption, hearing from the Apostle: « Follow peace with all men, and chastity, without which no one shall see God. » « With all the chief ointments. » By the chief ointments what better is understood than charity? Of which the Apostle says: « I show you yet a more excellent way, » and then: « If I speak (he says) with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not charity, it profits me nothing. » After myrrh and aloe the chief ointments are well placed, [0324C] because after the continence of the flesh there succeeds true charity, which is God, because God is loved above all things. For neither can those who are held by worldly love, that is, who still delight in pleasures and enticements, be partakers of this charity.

« Fountain of the gardens, well of living waters, which flow with force from Lebanon. » The fountain of the gardens is that same primitive Church, which with the knowledge of truth waters the gardens, that is, the Churches disseminated throughout the whole world; she is also the well of living waters, because the knowledge of truth, which is the Church, is in some things as it were a fountain where it lies open, in some as it were a well where it lies hidden, and beyond reach where it cannot be perceived. For between a fountain and a well this is the difference: that a fountain is said to be both in the depth and on the surface of the earth, [0324D] but a well is always in the depth. The Church, then, is a fountain and a well, because the mysteries of God in some things are easily grasped as though from a fountain, but in some are drawn out with difficulty as though waters from a well. By the living waters are designated the very mysteries of the Scriptures, because they have in themselves a heavenly power. But over against them, cisterns are named, that is, gatherings of waters, the documents of the heretics, of which the Lord says through the Prophet: « They have forsaken me, the fountain of living water, and have dug for themselves cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no waters. » These flowing waters flow with force from Lebanon, that is, they issue from the Church, which is washed and made white by baptism and by good works, which is what Lebanon is interpreted to mean. From this Lebanon [0325A] (that is, from the Church) flow (that is, issue) the waters of heavenly doctrines, and this with force, that is, with a certain power, by which all the fictions of the heretics are destroyed and overturned. Hence also the Psalmist: « The force of the river maketh glad the city of God, » that is, the power of the divine utterances.
