## CHAPTER VII. (cont.)

« And the hairs of thy head are like the king's purple bound in the channels. » If by the head of the Church the mind is understood, by the hairs of the head we ought to understand the thoughts of the mind, which continually issue from it. But the purple color, which has the appearance of blood, signifies the mystery of the Lord's passion. According to the letter, when he says « the king's purple bound in the channels, » it signifies wool already dyed with the purple color and not yet [0345C] drawn out into threads, but still set in the channels, which the Greeks call *batia*, in which the blood of the shellfish is accustomed to flow down and to turn the wool into a purple color. Mystically these channels signify the humility of the saints, in which the purple of the eternal king is dyed, when all the saints, by imitating the humility of their Redeemer, strive to become conformed to his sufferings, so that they may be turned into the dignity of purple; that is, laboring for Christ, they may deserve to reign with Christ. And this purple is said to be bound, because the hearts of the saints are fixed and made steadfast in the fear and love of their Redeemer, nor can they ever be separated from his charity.

« How beautiful art thou, and how comely, dearest, in delights. » How beautiful art thou, namely in faith, and [0345D] comely in operation, dearest in delights. Above it was said of the Church: « Fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an army set in array; » but here it is said, « Beautiful and comely, dearest in delights; » which seem to be as it were contrary. For to be set in array like an army, and to be in delights, is repugnant, because delights are accustomed to enervate the strength of soldiers. But the holy Church does both. She is set in array like an army, because she resists the unbelieving heretics, and by her holiness and perfection terrifies the malignant spirits. She is also in delights, because she continually tastes the sweetness of the heavenly sweetness with the palate of the mind, and amid the pressures of this world desires to attain those delights and continually to be satisfied with them. Whence deservedly [0346A] she who is such is said to be dearest to her spouse Christ.

« Thy stature is likened to a palm tree. » The stature of the Church is her perfect uprightness. For she is not bent toward vices, but stands upright and unmoved. Whence the Psalmist said, in the voice of those who wait: « I am bowed down and humbled exceedingly. » And the prophet, under the figure of Jerusalem, says to the sinning soul concerning the malignant spirits: « Because they have humbled thee, and have said to thy soul: Bow down, that we may pass over. » But on the contrary the holy Church stands upright, and never consents to be bent, hearing from the Apostle: « Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, do manfully and be strengthened, and let all your things be done in charity. » And the Lord speaks to the sons of Israel: « I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of [0346B] the prison-house of the Egyptians, that you might walk upright. » The stature of the Church, therefore, is likened to a palm tree, which in all that it does ever keeps in memory the heavenly recompense. For the palm adorns the victorious hand, and therefore signifies that reward which is to be given to the victorious saints in the heavens. « And thy breasts to clusters of grapes. » The breasts of the Church (as was said above) are the holy teachers, who nourish those regenerated in Christ with the milk of simple doctrine. But these breasts are likened to clusters, when these same teachers announce more perfect things to the perfect, just as the Apostle, speaking to the simpler, said: « I could not speak to you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal; as unto little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not meat. » Behold breasts full of milk. Which breasts [0346C] were turned into clusters, when he said: « We preach Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God, and we speak wisdom among the perfect. »

« I said, I will go up into the palm tree, and will take hold of the fruit thereof. » That it might be shown that all this excellence and greatness of the gifts of the Church comes from the grace of God, the same spouse is introduced saying: « I will go up into the palm tree. » As if he said: Thence hast thou breasts to nourish the little ones, whence thou hast clusters, by which thou mayest offer to the more perfect the more perfect doctrine, because I by my grace have gone up into thee, and have granted thee this perfection. « I will go up (he says) into the palm tree. » The palm signifies the Church, or also the soul of any of the faithful, who is mindful of the Lord his God who says: Have confidence, I have overcome the world. She too strives to be the victor over all [0346D] vices and iniquity. And well is he said here to have gone up into the palm, when above he is read to have gone down into the garden. For the Church said: « My beloved is gone down into his garden. » The garden is the same as the palm, namely one and the same Church. But into her the Lord goes down, and to her he goes up. He goes down into her, sending her an abundance of his grace from the heavens; he goes up to her, increasing his gifts in her, and raising her up by certain advances, as by certain steps, to higher things. But this too concerning the nature of the palm is to be known, that in the lower parts it is rough, in the higher parts most beautiful with the grace of leaves and the beauty of fruits. So also the holy Church in the lower parts [0347A] endures the roughness of labors and sufferings for Christ; but in the higher parts, that is, in the heavens, she awaits the beauty and sweetness of rewards. By the palm also can be expressed the tree of the Lord's cross, into which our Redeemer ascended for the redemption of the human race, and on which he overcame the enemy of the human race. He says, therefore: « I said, I will go up into the palm; » that is, through my prophets I have spoken, and have promised that I will come to the passion, and will redeem my Church. « I will take hold (he says) of the fruit thereof; » that is, of the fruit of the good works and labors, in which the Church sweats, by recompensing them; or also according to my meaning, by which the cross is understood by the palm. « I will take hold of the fruit, » that is, the triumph of the passion accomplished, I will come to the glory of the resurrection, [0347B] and will grant spiritual gifts to my apostles. « And thy breasts shall be like the clusters of the vine. » This has already been expounded above: for the breasts of the Church are the teachers, when they nourish the little ones with simple doctrine; but they become clusters of the vine, when they announce sublime things to the more perfect. For the blessed Paul at one and the same time had breasts and clusters, when, speaking of Christ, he said: « Whose fathers, and of whom is Christ. » Behold breasts, that is, the simple preaching concerning Christ. But these breasts were turned into most sweet and most strong wine, when he added: Who is God blessed forever.

And the odor of thy mouth like that of apples. Thy throat like the best wine. » The mouth and the odor of the Church are her present preaching, which by report or by writing reaches [0347C] to those absent from her. But the throat is the very voice of the preacher, which is heard in the present. The odor, therefore, of the mouth of the Church is compared to the odors of apples, because the very report of her preaching is full of sweetness and grace. But the throat is likened to the best wine, because the very voice of those preaching showed in itself great power to the hearers. And that the odor of apples is in newness, but that of wine in age, signifies that the preaching of the Church has all sweetness and grace both in its newness and also in its perfection. « Worthy for my beloved to drink, and to be chewed by his lips and teeth. » It is the voice of the Church. Who, hearing herself praised by the spouse, joyfully answers: « Worthy for my beloved to drink. » As if she said: My spouse has compared my throat to the best wine, [0347D] and that wine is worthy to be drunk by my beloved himself, that is, the evangelical preaching which he has placed in my mouth is to be announced in the world through him, and not through another. For it was not fitting that the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven be preached in the world through any other than through the mediator of God and men. « And to be chewed (he says) by his lips and teeth. » The lips and teeth of the spouse, as was said above, are the holy teachers. That best wine, therefore, is to be chewed by the lips and teeth of the spouse, because the evangelical preaching is to be handled and expounded by the teachers. Nor is it absurd that, though wine be drunk and not chewed, here nevertheless it is said to be chewed by the lips and teeth, which belongs rather to food [0348A] than to drink. For the holy teachers chew the spiritual wine, when they diligently search out the evangelical precepts, and investigate them by frequent meditation and confer them one with another.

« I am my beloved's, and his turning is toward me. » « I am my beloved's, » understand: I render the obedience of justice and devotion: to him, and to no other, I promise my faith and my service. « And his turning is toward me, » that he may protect me in the present amid the labors and troubles of this life, and in the future lead me to the joys of the heavenly life. This can also be the voice of the Synagogue, in the person of the holy Fathers, desiring the coming of Christ. As if she said: I know (she says) that he is always present to me by his spiritual presence, and therefore I desire to serve my beloved with all devotion, but [0348B] at length « his turning is toward me, » because I wish that at length, as he promised, he may appear to me in my own substance. For the spouse had said above: « I will go up into the palm; » that is, he had promised that he would come in the flesh, and would suffer for the human race, and thence she joyfully said, « I am my beloved's, and his turning is made toward me; » that is, I believe that he will come as man, and, in my substance, be manifested as man to the world.

« Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field, let us abide in the villages. » It is the voice of the same Synagogue, « Come, my beloved, » that is, appear through the man thou hast assumed. Such also is that of the Psalmist, « Stir up thy might and come; » and of Isaiah, « O that thou wouldst rend the heavens, and come down. » « Let us go forth into the field. » [0348C] The field is this world. « Let us go forth (she says) into the field, » that is, let us preach to the world. « Let us abide in the villages, » that is, let us announce thy faith also to the very pagans. No one is ignorant that the pagans are named from the village, because *pagos* in Greek is "village" in Latin. Thence they are called pagans, because they are far from the city of God. It can also be the voice of the Church, « Come, my beloved. » As if she said: Thou hast now, by the mystery of the ascension, raised the man thou didst assume up to the heavens, but come, by visiting me more often with the presence of thy divine majesty. « Let us go forth into the field. » In the same sense as above, Let us preach the faith of thy incarnation to the world. « Let us abide in the villages; » that is, let us announce thy faith even to the pagans who know thee not, and not in passing, but let us abide there, until we make them Catholics out of pagans, thine own [0348D] out of strangers.
