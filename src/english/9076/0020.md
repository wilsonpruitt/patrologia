## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother? » From these words it is understood that this whole song is divine and spiritual, and has in it nothing that could be understood according to the letter. ⟦s2⟧ For [0350B] what lover ever wished this, that he whom she loves should be made a little child, and her brother, and, turned into an infant, should suck the breasts of his mother? ⟦s3⟧ It is, therefore, the voice of that Church which preceded the coming of Christ, whom we call the Synagogue (that is, the congregation). ⟦s4⟧ « Who (she says) shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother? » And the sense is: Thou who now art in the bosom of the Father, who will grant that thou be made man, and, existing as a partaker and sharer of my nature, be called my brother? ⟦s5⟧ For he himself deigned to call himself the brother of the apostles, saying through the Psalmist: « I will declare thy name to my brethren. » And after the resurrection to the women, « Go, he said, tell my brethren. » « Sucking the breasts of my mother, » he says, that is, fulfilling all things that belong to human nature, [0350C] sin excepted. ⟦s6⟧ For the mother of the Synagogue is understood in this place to be human nature itself, from which the Synagogue itself sprang. ⟦s7⟧ « Sucking (he says) the breasts of my mother, » that is, enduring all things that pertain to human frailty. ⟦s8⟧ « That I may find thee without. » That is, that I may see thee, made man, in the open. ⟦s9⟧ For God was, as it were, within, when he yet lay hidden in the bosom of the paternal majesty, but he was found without, when, made man, he offered himself as lowly offspring to men, and, being incomprehensible, willed to be comprehended. ⟦s10⟧ « And I may kiss thee. » And I may kiss thee, he says, that is, I may behold with manifest vision him whom now I hold only in the kiss of faith — which she desired at the beginning of this song, saying: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth, » that is, let him now come through himself, that he may appear to me. [0350D] For the beloved, as it were, kissed the Church through the prophets, when he promised his coming through them. ⟦s11⟧ She desires, therefore, that, coming through himself, he may kiss her, that is, may display his presence in his own flesh. ⟦s12+13⟧ « And now let no one despise me. » For the Synagogue was, as it were, despised before Christ came, because, given up now to the observances of the law, she preached the coming of Christ under a type and figure, and not in truth. [0351A] But after Jesus appeared, and by the grace of his coming brightened the world, now no one despised her; nay rather she was terrible to her enemies as an army set in array, because throughout the whole world she preached Christ, and subjected the worldly empire to herself. ⟦s14⟧ Nor ought it to seem a wonder if we now call the Church the Synagogue. ⟦s15⟧ For there is one Church in the preceding and in the following Fathers.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I will take hold of thee, and lead thee into the house of my mother. » O beloved, by cleaving to thee through faith and charity, I will lead thee into the house of my mother. ⟦s2⟧ The mother of the Church, in this place, is understood as the heavenly Jerusalem, of which Paul says: But that Jerusalem which is above is free, which is our mother. ⟦s3⟧ « I will lead thee (she says) into the house of my mother, » that is, I will follow after [0351B] thee returning into heaven, after the act of thy incarnation and the mystery of thy passion. ⟦s4⟧ And "I will lead thee," she says, for that which is "I will conduct and follow after," because the apostles conducted with their eyes the Lord ascending into heaven, and with devout gaze followed after him until the cloud received him, until the angels standing by said: « Ye men of Galilee, why stand you looking up to heaven? ⟦s5⟧ This Jesus who is taken up from you into heaven shall so come as you have seen him going into heaven. » « There thou shalt teach me. » This is referred to the words above, where it is said: « I will find thee without. » When (she says) I have found thee without, that is, when I have beheld thee in the assumed flesh, there thou shalt teach me, because to all that I shall wish to ask, thou wilt answer. ⟦s6⟧ For often the Lord anticipated even the apostles wishing to ask, [0351C] and instructed them. ⟦s7⟧ It can also be joined to this place, where she says: « I will lead thee into the house of my mother, there thou shalt teach me. » When (she says) thou hast been received into heaven, and hast taken me also with thee into heaven, there thou shalt teach me, and lead me into all truth, which is impossible for man in the present life to be able to attain; hence it is that the Lord promises to the apostles, saying: « The hour cometh, when I will no more speak to you in proverbs, but will show you plainly of the Father. » Therefore there thou shalt teach me, that is, thou wilt reveal to me, in open vision, thee and the Father, that is, thou wilt show me thy divinity face to face. ⟦s8⟧ « And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine. » By the wine is understood the fervor of charity and love. ⟦s9⟧ I will give (she says) thee wine, that is, I will show with how [0351D] most fervent charity I love thee. ⟦s10⟧ And this wine will not be simple, but spiced, namely with good works and virtues. ⟦s11⟧ For the love of God, as blessed Gregory says, is never idle; it works great things if it be present. ⟦s12⟧ For if it refuses to work, it is not love. ⟦s13⟧ For spiced wine is love with operation. ⟦s14⟧ Whence also the Lord in the Gospel: « If any one love me, he will keep my word. » The proof of love is the showing of the work. ⟦s15⟧ « And the new wine of my pomegranates. » The pomegranate is the same as the Punic apple. ⟦s16⟧ And it is called a pomegranate (*malum granatum*), because it is full of a multitude of seeds (*grana*). ⟦s17⟧ It is a Punic apple, because it abounds most greatly in Africa. ⟦s18⟧ For Punica is Africa itself. ⟦s19⟧ And it has [0352A] a rind of a blood color, and therefore (as has often been said) holds the figure of the Lord's passion. ⟦s20⟧ For the new wine of pomegranates is the love of the holy martyrs, who so loved God that they did not hesitate to die for him. ⟦s21⟧ And well is it called new wine, and no other cup whatever, because new wine is most fervent, so that in its exceeding fervor it boils off and purges from itself all filth. ⟦s22⟧ The Church, therefore, will give the beloved the new wine of her pomegranates, that is, she will show in the sufferings of the holy martyrs with how great charity Christ is loved.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. » By the left (as blessed Gregory says) are understood all the prosperous things of the present life; but by the right the future blessed life in [0352B] the heavens is understood. ⟦s2⟧ The Church, therefore, has the left hand of the spouse under her head, and his right hand embraces her, because whatever prosperous things of the present world flatter her, she counts as nothing, and despises all that smile upon her temporally; and while she strives toward the vision of the heavenly blessedness alone, she desires to embrace that alone.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, till she please. » The Church has within her certain persons who are reborn by the baptism of Christ, and marked with the name of Christianity, yet in their morals and manner of life serve the world, and strive to disquiet the Church, lulled in most sweet rest, and to rouse her to endure tumults. ⟦s2⟧ These, therefore, on account of the grace of the mysteries of Christ which [0352C] they have received, are called daughters of Jerusalem in this place, although in their morals they do contrary things. ⟦s3⟧ And the spouse adjures them that they stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, that is, that they rouse not her — set in the sleep of most sweet rest, that is, in the contemplation of her Maker — by their disquiet, and compel her to awake to the solicitudes and cares of the world. ⟦s4⟧ « Till she please; » that is, until, the office of due service accomplished, by which in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs she serves God, she be admonished again by the care of bodily frailty to return to the use of the world.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Who is she that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon my beloved? » It is the voice of the Synagogue, marveling at the Church gathered from the nations. [0352D] Who is she, she says, that is, of what kind is she, of what merit, who cometh up from the desert? ⟦s2⟧ For she had been forsaken by God through idolatry and ignorance of the truth. ⟦s3⟧ She was coming up, therefore, by the steps of the virtues from the desert, that is, from the error of unbelief, in which for a time she had been forsaken. ⟦s4⟧ « Flowing with delights, » that is, abounding in virtues. ⟦s5⟧ « Leaning (she says) upon my beloved, » that is, acting confidently in Christ, and referring all her virtues and all her good things to his grace, she knew that she could accomplish no good by her own strength, and therefore said with the Apostle: « By the grace of God I am what I am. » And it is to be noted that she says, Upon my beloved. ⟦s6⟧ For the Synagogue thought that she alone had the knowledge of God, the other nations being left in [0353A] ignorance. ⟦s7⟧ « For God is known, » as the Psalmist says, « in Judea; in Israel his name is great. » But to the Synagogue thus marveling, her own spouse and beloved answers that which follows.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Under the apple tree I raised thee up. » By the apple tree we ought to understand the Lord's cross, from which tree the Synagogue was raised up, because she was redeemed from the transgression of original sin and the power of the devil through the cross of Christ; there was she raised up who was dead in sins. ⟦s2⟧ And well does he say the apple tree. ⟦s3⟧ For, as was said above, to this tree he compared Christ, saying: « As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. » « There was thy mother corrupted, there was she deflowered that bore thee. » By the mother and her that bore the Synagogue he means the greater and more principal [0353B] fathers of the Jewish people, such as were the Scribes and Pharisees, who delivered Christ to Pilate, who ought to have ruled and instructed the rest of the people as a mother and one who bears, and such as were those who, at the urging of those princes, wickedly cried out: « Crucify, crucify him. » This mother of the Synagogue, therefore, was corrupted and deflowered under the apple tree, when she bound herself and her posterity with a cruel curse, saying: « His blood be upon us and upon our children; » for these too were under the cross of Christ, though not by faith and devotion, yet by the cruel imprecation of vengeance upon themselves and their posterity.
