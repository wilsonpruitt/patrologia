## CHAPTER VIII. (cont.)

« Set me as a seal upon thy heart, as a seal upon thy arm. » Because (he says) under the apple tree I raised thee up, that is, because by my passion [0353C] on the cross I redeemed thee, set me as a seal upon thy heart, that is, hold me always in memory, and forget not me, how much I endured for thy salvation. Set me (he says) as a seal upon thy heart through faith, set me as a seal upon thy heart through worthy operation. For we too, when we wish to hold something in memory, are accustomed to set a seal upon the finger or the arm, so that, admonished by it, we may remember that thing for which we have care. And we ought to set Christ as a seal upon our heart and arm, so that we may always have his memory, and, rightly believing in him, may adorn that very faith with good works. And if we do this, he too will take us up as a seal, and will remember us, according to what he promises to Zerubbabel the leader of Judea: [0353D] « And I will set thee as a seal in my sight. » For if we have forgotten his benefits, and have not rendered him worthy thanksgivings, he too will forget us, as is said of the impious king Jeconiah the son of Joachim: « If he were a ring on my right hand, thence would I pluck him off. » « For love is strong as death, jealousy hard as hell. » The love of Christ is strong as death. For just as death separates the soul from the body, and now nothing is it permitted to man to desire, nothing to strive after in the present life, so the love of Christ, which has truly pervaded anyone, mortifies him wholly to this world, and renders him as it were insensible, and, living for Christ alone, he is dead to the world. The same is the sense when he says: « Jealousy hard as [0354A] hell. » For just as hell never restores those whom it once receives, but ever retains them, so the true love of Christ, which it has once received, it never lets go; whence the Apostle says: « Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation? or distress? or persecution? or famine? or nakedness? or danger? or the sword? » But also the love of Christ can be understood in this place, whose love was strong as death, because he so loved us that, being immortal, he took death for us. His jealousy was hard as hell, because hell knows not to have mercy or to be softened by the punishments of the wretched, so also the love of Christ could not cease from mercy, or by any wickednesses of persecutors be removed from the love of the human race. Some refer this that is said, « Jealousy is hard as hell, » [0354B] properly to the Synagogue, which was jealous of the nations, and envied their salvation. Christ therefore admonishes by saying this, that she cease from envy, and rejoice together at the salvation of the Church. « The lamps thereof are lamps of fire and of flames. » That is, the lamps of love are lamps of fire and of flames. The lamps of love are the hearts of the saints, in which, as in vessels, love dwells. These lamps, therefore, are indeed lamps of fire, because they burn in the heart through love; but they are lamps of flames, because outwardly they shine through operation.

« Many waters cannot quench charity, neither shall the floods overwhelm it. » The waters and the floods are the violent threats of persecutors, or also the enticements, by which they strive to separate the saints from the love of God. [0354C] Of which the Lord says in the Gospel, concerning the man who built his house upon a rock: « The floods came, the winds blew, and beat upon that house, and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock. » « Many waters, therefore, cannot quench charity, » because no tribulation, no distress, will be able to separate the saints from the love of God. « If a man should give all the substance of his house for love, he shall despise it as nothing. » This little verse needs no exposition. For no substance of the world, no riches, are anything in comparison with love. We read indeed that the saints left their goods for the love of Christ, but yet they seemed to themselves to leave nothing in comparison with the charity of Christ, whom they loved. Whence the Apostle, when he left not only earthly substance, but [0354D] also the paternal traditions for Christ, said: « All these things I count and esteem as dung, that I may gain Christ, » that is, that I may acquire his love.

« Our sister is little, and hath no breasts. What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? » It is the voice of the beloved, speaking to the Synagogue on behalf of the Church of the nations. « Our sister, » he says, that is, the Church of the nations, « is little, and hath no breasts; » and with great condescension, the name being shared with the Synagogue, he says, « Our sister: » because he assumed flesh from the Jews, therefore he tempers himself, as it were, to the Synagogue and makes himself its equal. « Our sister (he says) is little. » Just as the age of some man is described, [0355A] so also the age of the Church is described. For the Church, as yet gathered from the nations, was little in the very times of the Lord and the apostles, because both by the smallness of believers she was little; and she had not yet breasts, that is, she was by no means able, by preaching, to beget spiritual sons to God. He says, therefore, as if seeking the counsel of the Synagogue, « What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? » The speaking to the Church is when God admonishes the soul of each of the faithful, either inwardly by hidden inspiration, or outwardly by open preaching. And the sense is: When through my apostles I shall speak to the Church gathered from the nations, what shall we do? how shall we commit to her the heavenly precepts? Namely whether we shall commit to her the least precepts, or, that she may be made the greater, [0355B] shall we hand over to her the more perfect mysteries of the heavenly secrets? For God almighty tempers the word of preaching according to the measure and capacity of the human mind, considering the strength and capacity of the hearer. But while the Synagogue was silent, the spouse himself answered for her:

« If she be a wall, let us build upon it bulwarks of silver; if she be a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar. » If (he says) our sister be a wall, let us build upon it bulwarks of silver. As if he should say: When my apostles shall have begun to preach to the Church gathered from the nations, if the hearers shall be such as by the merit of perfection can be called a wall — that is, when, being either endowed with natural genius, or excelling in philosophical learning, they can as it were [0355C] defend and fortify others in the stead of a wall — let us build upon such a wall bulwarks of silver, that is, let us add to them the eloquences of the divine Scriptures. For, as the Psalmist says: « The words of the Lord are chaste words, silver tried by the fire. » But if the hearers shall be such as can by no means resist the enemy, nor repel the darts coming against them, but shall be a door, as, announcing simple things, they can bring some into the Church and to the faith — such as are priests of a lesser grade, who, by baptizing in the Church, and initiating men into the divine mysteries, introduce them into the Church — let us join that door together with boards of cedar; that is, let us set before them the examples of the fathers who went before, who, like cedars — namely incorruptible wood, that is, imperishable works [0355D] — have works, and, like boards, are spread out with breadth of knowledge and charity. For the breadth of the boards signifies the breadth of the heart or of knowledge, the Psalmist saying: « I have run the way of thy commandments, when thou didst enlarge my heart. » With these boards of cedar, then (that is, with the examples of the saints), let us join that door together, so that, namely, their examples, to be imitated, may open to others, as a door, an entrance for entering the Church.

« I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower. » It is the voice of the Church. Who, when she had heard her beloved taking counsel for her salvation, herself joyfully answers: « I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower. » I (she says) am a wall, I am founded upon the firm rock, and made solid with the glue of divine charity. Or I am a wall, [0356A] because I am built of living and chosen stones (that is, of the saints); « and my breasts are as a tower, » because I contain within me such as, in the stead of breasts, can nourish others with spiritual doctrine, and, in the stead of a tower, can defend and fortify others, and who, as towers stand out in the wall, so by the merit of doctrine and manner of life excel my other members. And this I have not by my own merits, nor by the liberty of my own will, but by the gift and grace of my spouse. « Since I am become in his presence as one finding peace. » That is, since he shed his blood for me, and, propitiating God the Father toward me, dissolved the enmities which were between God and man, and made heavenly things peaceful for me. Since, then, I have found this peace, I have deserved to be a wall, and to have breasts in the stead of towers. [0356B]

« The peaceable had a vineyard, » that is, my beloved, who is the true peaceable one, because through him all things were made peaceful that are in the heavens and on the earth. Whence also by the Prophet the Father of the world to come, the prince of peace, is he called, in whose type — the true peaceable one — Solomon the peaceable, the author of this song, went before. To this one, then, there was a vineyard, that is, the holy Church. « In that which hath peoples. » This he says to show that he sings of the Church of the nations, not of the Synagogue. For the Synagogue was of one nation only. But the Church has peoples, because she takes and receives all the nations within her most capacious bosom. « He delivered it to keepers. » The keepers of this vineyard (that is, of the holy Church) are the prophets, the apostles, and the successors of the apostles. [0356C] Or also the keepers of this world are the angelic powers, which keep the Church from the assaults of temptations and demons. « A man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver. » By men he means the strong and the perfect, for *vir* (man) is named from *virtus* (strength). The fruit of the Church is the recompense of the heavenly life, for which the holy Church ceases not to labor in the present. And the thousand pieces of silver, which the man brings for the fruit, signify all the things that can be possessed in this world. For the number of a thousand is set for the highest perfection and fullness. The man, therefore, brings for the fruit of this vineyard a thousand pieces of silver, because the saints leave all their goods, and dispense them to the poor, that they may obtain the fruit of this vineyard (that is, the heavenly reward). Such is that which the Lord says in the Gospel: [0356D] « The kingdom of heaven is like to a treasure hidden in a field, which a man having found, hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field. »
