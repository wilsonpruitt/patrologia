## Standing decisions — the prayer vocabulary (1 Tim 2:1 and its two Latin enumerations)

The passage turns on four sets of names, so each Latin word keeps one English word throughout:
*oratio* "prayer" · *obsecratio* "supplication" · *postulatio* "request" · *gratiarum actio* "thanksgiving" ·
*precatio* "precation" · *interpellatio* "interpellation". *Precation* and *interpellation* are deliberately
Latinate: Augustine's list (*Ep.* 149) is being contrasted word by word with Ambrose's and with "our codices".
*subponere* → "place under" (three times), since Eriugena is assigning each kind of request to a heading.

- **No `[var:]`.** Ambrose's order *orationes, obsecrationes, postulationes, gratiarum actiones* differs from the
  Clementine 1 Tim 2:1 (*obsecrationes, orationes, postulationes, gratiarum actiones*), but it is reported as
  Ambrose's enumeration, not quoted as scripture, and the passage itself is about what different codices read.
  Logged, not fired.

## 1243B — *ut vestri codices habent* "as your codices have"

*vestri* is plural → "your" (Pattern 17). Floss's note ¹: codex *uri*, *f. nostri, ut paulo post?* (i.e. *nostri*, as
*nostri codices* a few lines on). The English follows Floss's printed *vestri*.

## 1243B — *quas omnes sive paene omnes frequentat ecclesia in solempnitate sacramentorum*

Italic as printed (Augustine quoted); "all of which, or nearly all, the church frequents in the solemnity of the
sacraments". Floss notes both *omnes* as cod. *omnis*.

## 1244B — *quaudo populus benedicitur* (E12)

*quaudo* is a one-letter non-word (broken n) printed on the plate AND in our twin; the only word it can be is
*quando*. Rendered "when", no marker, crux here.
