## FRAGMENT I. CHAP. I, 11-29 (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I am the voice of one crying in the desert.* ⟦s2⟧ If you ask, he says, what I put forth concerning myself, know that I am the voice. ⟦s3⟧ He did not say, « that I am a man » , [0304A] or « I am John the son of Zachary » , but he said: « I am the voice » . ⟦s4⟧ For he did not consider in himself human substance, nor human generation, since indeed the forerunner of the Word was exalted beyond all these things. ⟦s5⟧ He forsook all things which are contained within the world, he ascended on high, he was made the voice of the Word, so that he confessed no substance in himself, except that which he received beyond every creature out of the abundance of grace, namely to be the voice of the Word. ⟦s6⟧ Now this name was imposed on him not by himself, but long before by Isaias the prophet, nay rather by the Holy Spirit through Isaias. ⟦s7⟧ Therefore I am the voice, not my voice, but the voice of one crying; for voice is said relatively. ⟦s8⟧ Of the crying Word, therefore, that is, of the Word preaching through the flesh, John is the voice. ⟦s9⟧ And where does the Word cry, whose voice [0304B] John is? ⟦s10⟧ *In the desert.* ⟦s11⟧ Many wish this desert to be understood of Judaea, and of all Israel. ⟦s12⟧ For Judaea was reduced to a desert, emptied of all divine worship, and defiled with the filth of idolatry, following only the legal letter, emptied of all spiritual sense, polluted with diverse superstitions; and she who before was called confession, for Judaea is interpreted *confession* [nt: Jerome: Juda, praise, confession. For (Hebrew) is said from the verb (Hebrew), Hiph. (Hebrew), that is, to confess.], was turned into an utter denial of the truth. ⟦s13⟧ In this solitude, therefore, both the voice of the Word and the Word itself first preached. ⟦s14⟧ But why the forerunner of the Word is signified by the name of voice is not without reason asked. ⟦s15⟧ The voice, then, is the interpreter of the mind. ⟦s16⟧ For everything which the mind first both thinks and orders invisibly within itself, it brings forth sensibly through the voice into the senses of the hearers. ⟦s17⟧ The mind, therefore, that [0304C] is, the intellect of all things, is the Son of God. ⟦s18⟧ For he is, as saint Augustine says, the intellect of all things, nay rather all things. ⟦s19⟧ His forerunner is beautifully named by the word voice, because through him he was first shown to the world, when he said « Behold the lamb of God » and the rest. ⟦s20⟧ But by a higher *theoria* the desert is understood as the ineffable height of the divine nature, removed from all things. ⟦s21⟧ For it is deserted by every creature, because it surpasses every intellect, while it deserts no intellect. ⟦s22⟧ And this is signified more clearly than light by the Greek name, which is ἔρημος . ⟦s23⟧ For Ἐρημία is interpreted removal and loftiness, which altogether befits the divine nature. ⟦s24⟧ In this very desert of the divine loftiness, therefore, the Word cries, through which all things were made. ⟦s25⟧ Hear Moses saying in Genesis: « God said: Be light made » , God said [0304D]: « A firmament » ; likewise in all the works of the six days there goes before: « God said » . ⟦s26⟧ Where by the name of God we understand the Father, but « said » signifies the Word of God. ⟦s27⟧ The Word of God therefore cries in the most remote solitude of the divine goodness. ⟦s28⟧ His cry is the founding of all natures. ⟦s29⟧ For he calls those things which are, as those which are not [var: Vulg. *vocat ea quae non sunt, tamquam ea quae sunt*, "calleth those things that are not, as those that are"], because through him God the Father cried, that is, created all things which he willed to be made. ⟦s30⟧ He cried invisibly, before the world was made, that the world be made. ⟦s31⟧ He cried, coming into the world, [0305A] visibly that the world be saved. ⟦s32⟧ First he cried eternally through his divinity alone before the incarnation; he cried afterwards through his flesh. ⟦s33⟧ And what does that voice of his, that is, his forerunner, cry aloud? ⟦s34⟧ Hear. ⟦s35⟧ *Make straight the way of the Lord.* ⟦s36⟧ What is « Make straight the way of the Lord » ? ⟦s37⟧ Is it not what follows, « make the paths straight » ? ⟦s38⟧ Make straight therefore the way of the Lord, that is, rightly preach and believe the Lord, who is the way, as he himself says: « I am the way and the truth and the life » . ⟦s39⟧ It can also be understood thus: « Make straight the way of the Lord » , that is, rightly believe in the Lord. ⟦s40⟧ For the Lord enters the hearts of men by no other way than through faith, which is the way of the Lord. ⟦s41⟧ *As said Isaias the prophet.* ⟦s42⟧ The evangelist, seeing the sentence of Isaias about the forerunner of the Lord most fully [0305B] written in the Gospel according to Luke, wished to leave off recapitulating the same sentence, lest it should seem superfluous. ⟦s43⟧ In this place it is not unfitting, as I think, to insert a few things about the mystical names of the forerunner. ⟦s44⟧ He is called voice because, just as the voice precedes the conception of the mind, so John preceded the Word of God. ⟦s45⟧ He is called Πρόδρομος, that is, forerunner, because John preceded the Lord not only in conception and nativity, but also in the mystery of baptism and of the preaching of penance. ⟦s45⟧ He is called φωσφόρος, that is, light-bearing, because he first brought the light of the world to the knowledge of the world, and carried it in himself. ⟦s46⟧ John is called the morning star, which the Greeks call ἄστρον πρωϊνὸν, because, just as that star which the astrologers call lucifer precedes the rising of the sun, so the forerunner of the Lord first [0305C] appeared to the world, whom the sun of justice followed. ⟦s47⟧ Likewise by the Greeks he is called ἀκριδομελιτροφὸς, on account of his abstinence from the common foods of men. ⟦s48⟧ For Ἀκρὶς is interpreted *locust,* μελὶ *honey,* τροφὴ *food;* ἀκριδομελιτροφὸς therefore is interpreted eating locusts and wild honey.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they who were sent were of the Pharisees*. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked: why were Pharisees sent to John? ⟦s3⟧ But to this it must be said: The Pharisees especially, for this reason, hearing that the preaching of John had persuaded the people of the general resurrection of all from the dead, and most of all in that place where he says, « offspring of vipers, who will deliver you from the wrath to come upon you [var: Vulg. *quis ostendit vobis fugere a ventura ira*, "who hath shewed you to flee from the wrath to come"] » , namely in the judgement after the resurrection, desired to hear John; for they too most firmly [0305D] believed and preached the resurrection of the dead, and through this they are reported to have agreed with Christ in many things, and also frequently to have helped Paul the apostle.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Why then dost thou baptize, if thou art not the Christ, nor Elias, nor a prophet?* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked why the Pharisees attributed the authority of baptism to Elias, or to any of the prophets; for they had heard in the prophets that Christ would come into the world and would baptize. ⟦s3⟧ Knowing therefore that the Jordan bore the figure of baptism, and that Elias and Eliseus crossed it with dry feet, they did not doubt that the figure of baptism had gone before in Elias and Eliseus, or that those very men had risen [0306A] and baptized. ⟦s4⟧ And through this they did not ask: « Art thou one of the prophets » ? but only: « Art thou a prophet » ? namely he who prefigured baptism. ⟦s5⟧ He answered them:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I baptize in water;* as if he said: I dip in water only for the cleansing of bodies, prefiguring the sanctification of body and soul in him who truly baptizes. ⟦s2⟧ *But he standeth in the midst of you,* [var: Vulg. *stetit*, "hath stood"] that is, he appears present to you, *whom you know not,* because you do not believe in him, nor know who he is.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He it is who is to come after me.* ⟦s2⟧ This is he who in the divine disputation and predication, before the world was made, was defined, that he would come after me into the world, so as to follow me his forerunner. ⟦s3⟧ *Who was made before me;* that is, who was made present before [0306B] the eyes of my mind and body. ⟦s4⟧ For I see him, and know him in the midst of you, but you know him not. ⟦s5⟧ There is also another understanding in these words, as pleases Maximus. ⟦s6⟧ John bears the figure of penance, since he preached penance; but Christ the figure of justice, not only because he judges the world, but also because he himself is eternal justice. ⟦s7⟧ Penance cannot come to be except after the transgression of justice; human nature therefore, which transgressed the divine laws in paradise, under the person of John preaches and does penance, and manifestly declares the substantial justice of the Word of God, which goes before it through divinity and is to come after it through the dispensation of the flesh, so that it might recall it, human nature I mean, living according to the divine laws which [0306C] by pride it had spurned, to the pristine state of eternal justice. ⟦s8⟧ *Whose I am not worthy,* and the rest. ⟦s9⟧ The shoe of the Word is his flesh, which he had assumed from the virgin, which is intimated through the figure of the shoe. ⟦s10⟧ For as a shoe is made from the hide of dead animals, so the flesh of Christ was made mortal for our sake, that by his death our death might be altogether destroyed. ⟦s11⟧ For the flesh of Christ was not made mortal by the desert of its own sin, as our flesh is, but it was passible by condescension to our nature after sin. ⟦s12⟧ For he did not refuse to undergo death for our sake, but the cause of death, sin I mean, he did not receive. ⟦s13⟧ And therefore death could not hold him, because he was not a debtor of death. ⟦s14⟧ Death could not hold as captive him whom it had not found guilty. [0306D] From the first man before sin, therefore, he received to be without sin; from the same after sin he received to be able to die; by which two he restored our whole nature in himself. ⟦s15⟧ For he altogether destroyed our death in himself, and restored eternal life. ⟦s16⟧ But in that he lacked sin, he manifested in himself our nature, before it sinned. ⟦s17⟧ If therefore the shoe of the Word is the flesh of the Word, not unfittingly understand the latchet of his shoe as the subtlety and unsearchable perplexity of the mysteries of the incarnation. ⟦s18⟧ The height of which mystery the forerunner judges himself unworthy to loose. [0307A] It is to be noted, however, that he did not say, « the latchet of whose shoe I will not loose » , but he said: « I am not worthy that I should loose the latchet of the shoe » . ⟦s19⟧ For he did loose the mysteries of the incarnation of Christ, when he most openly manifested him to the world, and disclosed many things about his divinity and humanity. ⟦s20⟧ Yet he rates himself unworthy to do this. ⟦s21⟧ By the shoe of Christ can also be signified the visible creature and Holy Scripture; for in these he fixes his footprints as it were his feet. ⟦s22⟧ For the visible creature is the garment of the Word, which openly proclaims him, manifesting its beauty to us. ⟦s23⟧ Scripture also was made his garment, which contains his mysteries, the latchet of all of which, that is, of the creature and of the letter, that is, the subtlety, the forerunner deems himself unworthy to loose. [0307B] There are two feet of the Word, of which one is the natural reason of the visible creature, the other the spiritual understanding of Holy Scripture. ⟦s24⟧ The one is covered by the sensible forms of the sensible world, the other by the surface of the divine letters, that is, of the Scriptures. ⟦s25⟧ For in two ways the expositors of the divine law intimate the incarnation of the Word of God. ⟦s26⟧ Of which one is that which teaches his incarnation from the virgin, by which he coupled human nature to himself in the unity of substance. ⟦s27⟧ The other is that which asserts the Word itself as it were incarnate, that is, thickened in letters and in the forms and orders of visible things. ⟦s28⟧ The latchet of this twofold shoe is the diligent inquiry after truth in all these things and the very perplexed tracing of it, of whose loosing the forerunner judges himself unworthy.
