## FRAGMENT II. CHAP. III, 1--IV, 28.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *There was a man of the Pharisees, Nicodemus by name, a ruler of the Jews.* ⟦s2⟧ Pharisee is interpreted *divided*; Pharisees, *divided ones* [nt: St Jerome: *Pherez(?)i*, *separating, or scattered, or bearing fruit.* For that name is derived from the root (Hebrew), *to separate,* that is, *to divide.*]. ⟦s3⟧ For their sect was divided from the rest of the Jews, that is, from the Scribes and Sadducees. ⟦s4⟧ For the Pharisees alone believed in the resurrection of the dead, the rest contending against it. ⟦s5⟧ Nicodemus [0314B] is interpreted *victory of the people,* or *victor people,* bearing the figure of all who from the Jewish people believed in Christ. ⟦s6⟧ And everyone who believes in Christ conquers the world. ⟦s7⟧ *A ruler of the Jews,* that is, one of the rulers of the Jews, learned in the letter of the law, a master of Israel.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *This man came to Jesus by night.* ⟦s2⟧ According to Augustine, Nicodemus is set in the figure of the catechumens. ⟦s3⟧ For catechumens, that is, those instructed in the faith, yet not yet baptized, nor imbued with the sacrament of the body and blood of the Lord, having faith alone, come to Christ; still ignorant of the power of baptism, which they do not yet receive, they approach as in a kind of night of ignorance of the sacraments. ⟦s4⟧ But according to Gregory the Theologian, Nicodemus is taken as a kind of [0314C] night disciple, conforming those who believe most perfectly in Christ, so that nothing of the integrity of the catholic faith is hidden from them, yet they lack the light of perfect works; fearing the assault and envy of their carnal thoughts and actions, as of the faithless Jews, they enjoy converse with Christ by faith alone, not having the confidence of good works. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore the Evangelist says: « This man came to him by night » . ⟦s6⟧ *And said to him: Rabbi, we know that thou art come a master from God; for no man can do these signs which thou dost, unless God be with him.* ⟦s7⟧ From these words, with which Nicodemus is seen to have addressed Christ, it is given to understand that he himself, Nicodemus I mean, was one of those who had believed a little before, on the day of the Pasch, of whom it was said: « Now when he was [0314D] at Jerusalem at the Pasch, upon the festival day, many believed in his name, seeing his signs which he did » . ⟦s8⟧ For to this he looks back when he says: « No man can do these signs which thou dost, unless God be with him »

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Jesus answered, and said to him: Amen, amen I say to thee, unless a man be born anew [var: Vulg. *renatus*, "born again"], he cannot see the kingdom* [0315A] *of God.* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked: why did Jesus answer Nicodemus about the second nativity, which is according to the spirit, when he himself seems to have asked nothing about it? ⟦s3⟧ But it must be answered: since the Lord saw that he had believed, as the rest also believed who saw his signs, and had supposed that faith alone sufficed them, without the power and sacraments of spiritual generation, he at once began to teach him about the nativity which is according to the spirit. ⟦s4⟧ As if he openly said to him: It does not suffice thee only to have believed in me, unless thou receive the sacraments of baptism and understand the power of spiritual generation. ⟦s5⟧ It is to be noted that in the codices of the Greeks ἄνωθεν is read, where in the Latin codices *denuo*, "anew," is found, so that the sense is: unless a man be born ἄνωθεν , that is, from above, [0315B] so that we say "from above" for "anew"; which is both more easily understood, and more fitting to the two nativities, namely the earthly and the heavenly. ⟦s6⟧ For the Apostle says: « The first man of the earth, earthly; the second man from heaven, heavenly » . ⟦s7⟧ For there are two nativities, as Augustine says; of which one is from the earth, the other from heaven, that is, from above; one from parents, according to the flesh, the other from Christ and the Church, according to the spirit; one in the likeness of beasts of burden, through sin, unto death, the other in the likeness of the heavenly powers, through grace, unto life; one against nature through offence, the other according to nature through the remedy of offences. ⟦s8⟧ But Gregory the Theologian establishes four nativities, which our Lord Jesus Christ also deigned to undergo for the salvation [0315C] of human nature. ⟦s9⟧ Of which the first is that nativity in which the whole human race was born together from nothing; of which it is written: « And God made man to his image and likeness » . ⟦s10⟧ The second, which, following upon the offence of man, is from both sexes, after the likeness of the other animals, of which the same Scripture says: « Male and female he made them » ; through which the whole human race is multiplied without limit by earthly succession. ⟦s11⟧ The third, which is according to the spirit, of which the Lord now says: « Unless a man be born anew » ; in which nativity human nature begins to return to its former seat, from which it fell. ⟦s12⟧ The fourth will be in the resurrection of all, when our whole nature will be born together, [0315D] death having been slain, into life eternal. ⟦s13⟧ The first therefore is natural, the second on account of sin, the third through the grace of the Redeemer, the fourth according to nature and grace together. ⟦s14⟧ For the power of resurrection is naturally in human nature, since eternal death is altogether contrary to it. ⟦s15⟧ But what nature contributes to the resurrection, and what grace, is another place for discussion. ⟦s16⟧ *He cannot see the kingdom of God.* ⟦s17⟧ Of the kingdom of God the understanding is manifold. ⟦s18⟧ The kingdom of God is the church of the faithful, of which it is written: « The kingdom of God is among you » . ⟦s19⟧ The kingdom of God is the universe of corporeal and spiritual creation. ⟦s20⟧ The kingdom of God is the heavenly powers and the souls [0316A] of the just. ⟦s21⟧ The kingdom of God is the Son of God, who « reacheth from end to end mightily and ordereth all things sweetly » . ⟦s22⟧ The kingdom of God is the vision of truth. ⟦s23⟧ What therefore he says, *he cannot see the kingdom of God,* is not incongruously understood: he cannot know me, who am the kingdom of the Father. ⟦s24⟧ For I am not only the king of all, but also that kingdom and all things. ⟦s25⟧ Rightly therefore the vision of truth is both called and understood the kingdom of God.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Nicodemus saith to him: How can a man be born when he is old? ⟦s2⟧ Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb, and be [re]born*? ⟦s3⟧ He understood carnally, who as yet had been born only according to the flesh, and therefore he answered carnally, since he had not been born of the sacrament of baptism and of the spirit. ⟦s4⟧ Yet he said truly that no one can be born twice [0316B] according to the flesh, just as no one can be born twice according to the spirit. ⟦s5⟧ For as a man is born once in the flesh from the flesh, so a man is born once from the spirit in the spirit. ⟦s6⟧ For the sacrament of baptism, once received, no one can repeat.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Jesus answered: Amen, amen I say to thee, unless a man be born of water and the spirit [var: Vulg. *renatus … ex aqua et Spiritu sancto*, "born again of water and the Holy Spirit"], he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.* ⟦s2⟧ Where it is given to understand that it is not one thing to see the kingdom of God and another to enter into the kingdom of God, but the very same. ⟦s3⟧ For he who sees the kingdom of God, he enters; and he who enters, he sees. ⟦s4⟧ For to see is to enter, and to enter is to see, that is, to know the truth. ⟦s5⟧ *Unless a man be born of water and the spirit,* that is, of the visible sacrament and the invisible understanding. ⟦s6⟧ As if he had openly said: Unless a man [0316C] receive the symbol of baptism visibly, and do not perceive the spirit, that is, the understanding of that symbol, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God. ⟦s7⟧ It can also be understood thus: of water, that is, of the visible sacrament, and of the Holy Spirit. ⟦s8⟧ It is asked for what reason the Lord established that the visible sacrament of baptism be done in water, since spiritual teaching alone through faith seems to suffice for the nativity of man from the spirit. ⟦s9⟧ But this also must be said: since man is constituted of a visible body and an invisible soul, a visible sacrament was necessary for the purification of the visible body, just as the invisible teaching of faith is necessary for the sanctification of the invisible soul. ⟦s10⟧ For although the visible sacrament of baptism seems to confer nothing as yet on the body in this life, yet in the future resurrection [0316D] it will make of the mortal immortal, of the corruptible incorruptible, and the other things which will pertain to the glory of the future resurrection. ⟦s11⟧ Nevertheless, even in the present life the power of the sacrament sanctifies the bodies of the faithful, since they are made the temple of God, as the Apostle says: « Know you not that your bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit » ? ⟦s12⟧ But if anyone desires to know fully the power of baptism, let him read saint Dionysius the Areopagite in the book *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There follows: *That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the spirit is spirit.* ⟦s2⟧ He repeats the two nativities, [0317A] namely from the flesh and from the spirit. ⟦s3⟧ That which is born of the flesh, that is, from the first man through the succession of the flesh, is flesh, that is, is carnal. ⟦s4⟧ It is to be noted that in this place by the name of flesh he signified not the flesh only, but the whole carnal man. ⟦s5⟧ For often the carnal soul is called by the name of flesh, whence the Apostle: « The flesh lusteth against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh » . ⟦s6⟧ It is also to be known that, just as the whole carnal man, in soul and body, is called flesh, so also the whole spiritual man, in soul and body, is called spirit, since men who are born in Christ through the Holy Spirit are made one with that Spirit according to body and soul. ⟦s7⟧ Where it is given to understand that the sacrament of baptism prefigures that the change of flesh into [0317B] spirit is to come.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Wonder not that I said to thee: You must be born anew.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he had said: let it not be a wonder to thee and incredible, that a man be born again from the spirit.
