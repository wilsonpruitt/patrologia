## FRAGMENT II. CHAP. III, 1--IV, 28. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0323B] *For John had not [yet] been cast into prison.* ⟦s2⟧ For after he was cast into prison, he immediately ceased to baptize, the baptism of Christ beginning. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: what did the baptism of John profit those who were baptized by him? ⟦s4⟧ For in no way is it to be believed that it conferred the remission not only of original sin, but also of personal offences. ⟦s5⟧ What then did it confer? ⟦s6⟧ Something great indeed. ⟦s7⟧ For as much as the teaching of the faith profits catechumens not yet baptized, so much did the baptism of John profit those baptized in it, before they underwent the baptism of Christ. ⟦s8⟧ For John preached penance, and foretold the baptism of Christ, and drew them into the knowledge of the truth which appeared to the world. ⟦s9⟧ Do not the ministers of the Church do likewise daily? ⟦s10⟧ They instruct first those coming to the faith; [0323C] then they reprove their sins; then they promise the remission of all sins in baptism; and thus they draw them to the knowledge and love of the truth. ⟦s11⟧ It is added to the heap of praise and usefulness which the baptism of John accomplished. ⟦s12⟧ For the forerunner ought in all things to fulfil the effect of his forerunning, so that, just as he preceded Christ in nativity and in preaching, so also in the likeness of baptism. ⟦s13⟧ For as the dawn precedes the rising of the sun, so the sacraments of John preceded the works of Christ, and just as the dawn in a certain manner to shake off the shadows, so the teaching and mystery of the forerunner began to shake off the darkness of the ignorance of the whole world. ⟦s14⟧ And so, lest the true baptism of Christ should appear suddenly to the world, a figured baptism went before, that the truth might shine forth more highly and [0323D] more clearly, if the figure had first smiled upon those sitting in darkness. ⟦s15⟧ Likewise it is asked why Christ was baptized by his own servant. ⟦s16⟧ To which it must be said: Christ deigned to be baptized by a servant for this reason, that he might both honour the office of his forerunner, and make it known to all the Jews and to the whole world that it had been true. ⟦s17⟧ But if this was the sole cause of the Lord's being baptized by his servant, why was not Christ alone baptized by John? ⟦s18⟧ For the humility of the Lord sufficed for the praise and testimony of the servant's baptism. ⟦s19⟧ But to [0324A] this it must be said that, if Christ alone were baptized by John, the baptism of John would seem to confer more on the baptized than that of Christ. ⟦s20⟧ For the single baptism by which the Lord was baptized could be thought much better than the baptism by which many were baptized by the Lord himself and his disciples. ⟦s21⟧ But lest this be thought, many were baptized by John. ⟦s22⟧ But if it is asked whether it profited the Lord himself in anything that he was baptized by his forerunner, some answer, not absurdly, that Christ effected a certain sanctification of John. ⟦s23⟧ But that baptism conferred nothing on Christ. ⟦s24⟧ We know, however, that the authors of the Greeks confidently assert that the sanctification of the humanity of Christ was increased through the sacrament of the baptism of John. ⟦s25⟧ This they argue chiefly from the descent [0324B] of the Holy Spirit in the form of a dove upon him when he had been baptized. ⟦s26+27⟧ Nor is it a wonder, since the evangelist says: « But Jesus advanced in wisdom and age and grace with God and men. » What wonder, if Christ received a certain increase of his humanity through the sacrament of his forerunner's baptism, for example by the merit of his humility, by which he did not disdain to undergo the baptism of his servant? ⟦s28⟧ He received grace, that by the power of his baptism all who believe in him might receive the remission of all offences, both original and personal. ⟦s29⟧ Augustine also adds another cause of the Lord's being baptized by his servant: lest catechumens, living chastely and piously after receiving the faith, should grow proud, supposing that the mystery of baptism could add nothing to them, since they live according to [0324C] that faith with which they have been imbued. ⟦s30⟧ But lest they should err in this, and labour in vain, the Lord willed to give them an example from his own baptism, so that none of them should dare to be puffed up, seeing that their Lord had undergone the baptism of his servant. ⟦s31⟧ For if the Lord would not despise the baptism of his servant, by what reasoning would they be unwilling to undergo the baptism of their Lord?

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *There arose therefore a question on the part of the disciples of John with the Jews concerning purification.* ⟦s2⟧ The disciples of John, seeing that many of the Jews had believed in Christ, and had been baptized by his disciples, moved by envy, questioned the Jews themselves concerning purification, that is, concerning baptism. ⟦s3⟧ For they wondered that others could baptize besides John and his disciples, supposing John to be greater than Christ, since they had seen not long before [0324D] Christ himself baptized by John in the Jordan.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they came to John,* wishing to accuse Christ and his disciples, as presuming to baptize to his injury, *and said to him: Rabbi, he that was with thee beyond the Jordan,* who seemed to be thy disciple at the time when he was baptized by thee beyond the Jordan, *to whom thou gavest testimony,* by saying: « Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who taketh away the sin of the world » , *behold he baptizeth,* now in Judaea, at which we greatly wonder, *and all come to him.* ⟦s2⟧ We wish therefore to ask thee [0325A] what seems good to thee concerning this presumption.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *John answered and said: A man cannot receive anything, unless it be given him from heaven.* ⟦s2⟧ In certain codices of the Greeks it is read: *unless it be given him from above from heaven.* ⟦s3⟧ Humbly did the servant answer those accusing his Lord. ⟦s4⟧ *A man cannot,* he says, *receive anything.* ⟦s5⟧ As if he said: You think me greater than him whose servant and forerunner I am; you err, not believing in him, and not knowing what he is and whence he came, and you think me to be what I am not. ⟦s6⟧ But that you may know what he is, and what I am, hear my answer: *A man cannot receive anything.* ⟦s7⟧ I am a man and not God, but he is both God and man. ⟦s8+9⟧ He it is of whom I bore testimony, saying: « Of his fullness we all [0325B] have received. » I am therefore a man; from myself I have received nothing, who have nothing to receive from myself. ⟦s10⟧ Whatever I have received, I have received from him. ⟦s11⟧ If I have received the grace of a forerunner, I have received it from him. ⟦s12⟧ You suppose that I am beyond human nature. ⟦s13⟧ Because you have heard that I was born not without a miracle of divine power, namely of parents of advanced age, and was born as it were beyond the custom of human nature, on this account you think that I am beyond man. ⟦s14⟧ Not so; I am only a man. ⟦s15⟧ If you had known what I am, you would not exalt me beyond what I am; and if you knew what he is, in no way would you compare me to him. ⟦s16⟧ For I am man, he God; whatever I have received, I have received from him. ⟦s17⟧ The most wise forerunner of Christ separates nature and grace in himself, attributing to himself that he is man, that is, that he is [0325C] mortal, that he is a sinner, that from the first earthly man he was made earthly, and the other things which he considered in himself and in other men concerning the infirmity of human nature and sin and the penalty of sin, setting apart from himself the grace which he had received not from merits, but from the abundance of the fullness of him whose forerunner he was. ⟦s18⟧ Two things are considered in man: namely the given and the gift. ⟦s19⟧ The given is referred to nature, the gift is referred to grace. ⟦s20⟧ And although none of these is except from God, as the Apostle says: « O man, what hast thou that thou hast not received » ? yet Scripture is accustomed to ascribe the given of nature as it were to man himself, but the gift to none but God. ⟦s21⟧ For to this end God gave nature, that he might adorn it through grace. ⟦s22⟧ And this is what he says: *A man cannot,* that is, human [0325D] nature, *receive anything* of grace, *unless it be given him from heaven.* ⟦s23⟧ John has put given in this place for gift, which is very often found in Holy Scripture. ⟦s24⟧ For given can be taken for gift, and gift for given. ⟦s25⟧ For the givens of nature and the gifts of grace are so joined that they seem to become inseparable. ⟦s26⟧ For there is no rational nature which is wholly without the gift of grace. ⟦s27⟧ *Unless it be given from heaven.* ⟦s28⟧ From what heaven? ⟦s29⟧ From the Father, namely. ⟦s30+31⟧ Hear James the apostle saying: « Every best given and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights. » With which words what John says wholly agrees: [0326A] *A man cannot receive anything, unless it be given him from heaven,* that is, from the Father, who is the principle of all good things; and, if from the Father, surely first through the Son, of whose fullness we all have received.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *You yourselves bear me witness.* ⟦s2⟧ This alone in your words agrees with me, you who not only do not believe in him who sent me, but even think his baptism void, that you bring testimony for me. ⟦s3⟧ *That I said: I am not the Christ, but that I am sent before him.* ⟦s4⟧ You heard these words from me when I was speaking of him beyond the Jordan; you heard them surely, since you say: « He that was with thee beyond the Jordan, to whom thou gavest testimony » ; and if you heard, why do you accuse to me him of whom I bore such testimony, as if [0326B] to my injury he had baptized many? ⟦s5⟧ I said: I am not the Christ, but a simple man; I am not anointed by the Father with the fullness of the spirit, but he is anointed; and of his fullness I have received. ⟦s6⟧ But that I am sent before him, I was sent into the world first, by being born before him. ⟦s7⟧ He, before all ages, God with God the Father; I, a man sent in the flesh at the end of the age, before he was born in the flesh.
