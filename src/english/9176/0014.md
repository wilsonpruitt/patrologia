## FRAGMENT III. CHAP. VI, 5-14. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *The men therefore, seeing that Jesus had done a sign, said: That he is truly the prophet, who cometh into the world.* [var: Vulg. *qui venturus est*, "who is to come"] Again the imperfect crowd is reproved in this, that it was on account of the visible sign, not on account of the intelligible power of the sign, that they said: *that he is truly the prophet, who cometh into the world.* ⟦s3⟧ In this miracle of the fivefold quantity of the barley loaves it is not without cause asked what it means that the fragments of the five loaves only, and not of the two fishes, are gathered, from which twelve baskets are filled, since the Gospel relates that five thousand men were filled from five loaves and two fishes. ⟦s4⟧ The depth of this mystery seems to me obscure, so that it can scarcely be searched out. ⟦s5⟧ For Augustine in his exposition [0344C] on John treats nothing of it, but passed it over altogether. ⟦s6⟧ Although to many it may seem sufficient, what is read in Mark: « they took up of fragments twelve baskets full, and of the fishes », so that it be understood: and of the fishes they took up fragments, either other ones or the same. ⟦s7⟧ But if other ones, there were not XII, but XXIIII baskets; if the same, it had been sufficient to say: they took up twelve baskets of fragments. ⟦s8⟧ It seems not to be void of mystery that, as it were after the fragments of bread were gathered, he says « and of the fishes », absolutely, adding nothing. ⟦s9⟧ For Matthew and Luke mention the fragments without distinction, leaving it in doubt whether they were of the loaves only, as John has it, or of the loaves and the fishes together. ⟦s10⟧ Let us try therefore, as far as the light of minds shall give, to inquire, not prejudging the senses of others. ⟦s11⟧ For we have said before [0344D] that the five senses of the body, or the five Mosaic books, are intimated by the fivefold number of the loaves, and by the two fishes the symbols of each Testament. ⟦s12⟧ Where it must first be asked what difference there is between the mysteries of each law, namely of the letter and of grace, and the symbols. ⟦s13⟧ Mysteries, then, are properly those which are handed down according to the allegory both of deed and of word, that is, they were both done according to things done and said, because they are narrated. ⟦s14⟧ For example, the Mosaic tabernacle was both constructed according to the thing done, and said and narrated in the text of Holy Scripture. ⟦s15⟧ Likewise the sacraments of the legal victims were both done according to history, [0345A] and are said according to narration. ⟦s16⟧ Circumcision likewise was both done in the flesh, and narrated in the letter. ⟦s17⟧ In the New Testament also the mysteries of baptism, and also of the Lord's body and blood, and also of the holy chrism, are performed according to things done, and are handed down and said in letters. ⟦s18⟧ And this form of sacraments is reasonably called by the holy Fathers the allegory of deed and word. ⟦s19⟧ The other form is that which has properly received the name of symbol, and is called the allegory of word, but not of deed, since it is constituted only in the words of spiritual doctrine, but not in sensible deeds. ⟦s20⟧ Mysteries, then, are those which in either Testament were both done according to history, and narrated according to the letter; but symbols are those which, only not done, but as it were done, [0345B] are said by doctrine alone. ⟦s21⟧ For example, in the old law it is written: « Thou shalt not boil a kid in the milk of its mother ». ⟦s22⟧ That, indeed, is never read to have been done according to history, as Augustine witnesses; yet it was said and written, and is handed down as though done, though the truth of divine history is not found to narrate that it was done. ⟦s23⟧ Again in the Psalms: « The mountains skipped like rams, and the hills like the lambs of the sheep ». ⟦s24⟧ For in these places of Holy Scripture and in many like them the allegory of word alone, and not of deed and word, is understood. ⟦s25⟧ In the New Testament many things are narrated which according to the faith of history were not done, but only said, and as though done; which examples are found chiefly in the allegory of the Lord's parables, for example, the parable of the rich man and of Lazarus the poor man in the bosom of Abraham, [0345C] and of the chasm set between, of the flame, of the tongue, of the finger, all of which no authority hands down to have been according to things done, but they were said altogether figuratively. ⟦s26⟧ Which form is recognized in almost all the parables, and is properly named symbolic; although it is the usage of Holy Scripture to put symbols for mysteries, and mysteries for symbols, by a certain nearness and likeness. ⟦s27⟧ Let us set down, then, a certain likeness, by which we may be able to persuade what we wish to assert. ⟦s28⟧ The people of the carnal faithful went before both in the old law and in the new. ⟦s29⟧ Now there is one who thinks that nothing exists outside the letter and the bodily senses, since he can[not] ascend higher beyond the letter of Scripture, and the things which are received through the senses. ⟦s30⟧ Likewise in the old law the perfect and wise in action [0345D] and in knowledge of spiritual understandings, and in the New Testament, are narrated to have been, who, like the disciples, ascend with Christ into the height of contemplation, as into a certain mountain, the carnal being left behind, lying down under the letter and sense in the grass of temporal things. ⟦s31⟧ « For all flesh is grass, and all the glory thereof the flower of grass ». ⟦s32⟧ Let us therefore think in our mind of a certain multitude, as it were, of simple faithful, sitting on the sides of the mountain, or on the plain, that is, below the height of divine understandings. ⟦s33+34⟧ For from the text of the Gospel it is given to understand that the Lord fed the crowd not on the summit of the mountain, but on a certain plain, where there had been much grass. [0346A] Where he says, « having returned into the mountain himself alone » [var: Vulg. *fugit iterum in montem*, "fled again into the mountain"]; so that according to things done we should take it that the Lord alone with his disciples first ascended into the mountain, and then again descended to the lower parts, and, the miracle being performed, returned alone again into the mountain. ⟦s35⟧ Before this multitude, hungering for the faith of Christ, are set five loaves and two fishes, that is, the sacraments, which were both done and written, and the symbols, which were only said, but not done. ⟦s36⟧ Christ, taking the five loaves and two fishes, and giving thanks, namely to the Father, who willed to feed his faithful with symbols and sacraments, delivers them to his disciples, his masters, his ministers; and they divide them to the crowd. ⟦s37⟧ The barley loaves are broken by the disciples when the mysteries of each law are divided by them [0346B] into the things done and into their spiritual understandings. ⟦s38⟧ The carnal are fed with simple history; the spiritual gather the divine understandings of that history as certain fragments. ⟦s39⟧ The fragments which the carnal eat and are filled are the things done; the fragments which the spiritual eat are the divine understandings of the things done. ⟦s40⟧ For example, let the book of Genesis be read, at that place where the passage of carnal Israel through the Red Sea is narrated. ⟦s41⟧ The simple Christian, still lying as it were in the grass of temporal and carnal things, is fed with the history alone. ⟦s42⟧ For whatever was done there was known to the five bodily senses. ⟦s43⟧ For both the Israelites were seen to have crossed the sea with dry feet, and the Egyptians to have been killed. ⟦s44⟧ This also was heard by the neighbouring nations, the sea wave [0346C] was tasted, smelled and touched. ⟦s45⟧ All which things the soul of the faithful, still simple, handles and ponders with itself, and thence is filled, as with certain fragments of divine history. ⟦s46⟧ But the understanding of the letter itself it cannot take, and therefore it is gathered by those who savour spiritual things, lest it be lost, but that it may profit those who are able to understand. ⟦s47⟧ Behold the five barley loaves: namely the mysteries of things done, divided into letter and spirit, and, in so far as it is letter, they suit the fivefold bodily senses. . . . . . .. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Abraham », he says, « had two sons, one indeed of the bondwoman, the other of the free woman ». ⟦s2⟧ Behold the first fragments of history, sufficient for the simple. ⟦s3⟧ Consequently he subjoins the spiritual fragments, saying: [0346D] « But these are the two Testaments ». ⟦s4⟧ Let the two fishes be set before the same multitude, that is, the spiritual doctrine alone of what was allegorically said, but not done according to history. ⟦s5⟧ Which is contained as it were in the number two, since it is perceived by two senses only, namely by sight and hearing, but cannot be perceived by the other senses. ⟦s6⟧ For it is read with the eyes, heard with the ears, but it is removed from smell or taste or touch. ⟦s7⟧ For example, to use the same examples, let that be read which is written in Exodus: « Thou shalt not boil a kid in the milk of its mother ». ⟦s8⟧ This symbol is both read through the eyes, and heard through the ears; but it [0347A] cannot be broken, because it is not received according to the faith of things done, but was only said allegorically. ⟦s9⟧ And since it is not divided, it is commended whole to the memory of the carnal faithful, that they may believe that a spiritual sense is in these words, although they do not understand it. ⟦s10⟧ It is gathered whole by those who spiritually know spiritual things. ⟦s11⟧ In symbols, then, that is, in the sayings of spiritual doctrine, which the allegory of deed alone, but not of word, [cj: *facti, non autem dicti*; read *dicti, non autem facti*, "of word, but not of deed"] hands down, no fragments are gathered, since it is not divided into history and understanding. ⟦s12⟧ For only an understanding is thought of in it, but no deed. ⟦s13⟧ Likewise in the New Testament, that we may take an example from there too, the body and blood of our Lord is both performed sensibly as a mystery according to things done, and according to spiritual understanding [0347B] its brain (?) is searched out. ⟦s14⟧ What is outwardly sensed and perceived by carnal men, subject to the fivefold bodily sense, is barley bread, because they cannot ascend the height of spiritual understanding; and it is as it were a certain fragment, by which their carnal thought is filled. [0348A] It is a spiritual fragment for those who are able to know the height of the divine understandings of that mystery, and therefore it is gathered by them, lest it be lost. ⟦s15⟧ For the mystery, made up of letter and spirit, partly perishes, partly remains eternally. ⟦s16⟧ That perishes which is seen, because it is sensible and temporal; that remains which is not seen, because it is spiritual and eternal. ⟦s17⟧ Let an example of a symbol be given: « In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God and God was the Word ». ⟦s18⟧ This is only said; in it no thing done is known, and therefore let it be received whole simply by the carnal, and whole uniformly by the spiritual. ⟦s19⟧ Nor can any fragments be made there, since there is nothing there which may be understood according to history, but the whole is referred to theology, which surpasses every sense and intellect [0348B]. ⟦s20⟧ It is read by the eyes of those who read, it is sensed by the ears of those who hear, and through this, like certain two fishes, the one and the same theology of the evangelist is received, suiting two senses. .. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .
