# Cruces — 9176, chunks 0001–0003 (cols 0300D–0309D)

Stint cruces, to be merged into `cruces.md` under its standing decisions. Every column of this
range was read at the plate (archive n186–n191, corners first; Princeton `YkVOAAAAYAAJ` as second
witness), recorded in `data/briefs/9176-PLATE-READS-0001.json`. TEI divergences are filed in
`data/briefs/9176-PENDING-TEI-PATCHES-0001.md`, and **the English renders the plate** at each.

Conventions followed from the anchor: *Helias* → Elias, *Esaias* → Isaias, *Elisaeus* → Eliseus;
*corrigia* → "latchet"; *praecursor* → "forerunner"; *theoria* italic; thou/you by number (the
Pharisees' *es / dic* to John are "thou", John's *quaeritis / cognoscite* and *vos nescitis* are
"you", Eriugena's *intellige / si quaeris / cognosce* to the reader are singular).

## Col 0300D (chunk 0001) — Matt 11:27 printed with *vidit* for *novit*: `[var:]` fired

*Nemo vidit Filium nisi Pater, neque Patrem nisi Filius.* The Vulgate has *nemo novit Filium … neque
Patrem quis novit*. The whole passage is about whether anyone has SEEN God, and the verb the
quotation carries is the one the argument needs; rendered "hath seen" with `[var: Vulg. *novit*,
"knoweth"]`. Plate-read (n186), Princeton not needed (word-level, clean).

## Col 0301A (chunk 0001) — Eph 1:10 loosely quoted, not marked

*In quo restaurata sunt omnia, quae in caelo et in terra* against the Vulgate's *instaurare omnia in
Christo, quae in caelis et quae in terra sunt*. A paraphrase with no change of sense
(*restaurare/instaurare* both "restore"); no marker.

## Col 0301C (chunk 0001) — Floss's bracket `[dicatur de]`

The plate prints the brackets (Floss's supplement to the codex). Carried as "[is said of]", the
brackets kept round the English of the supplied words, since they are his and not ours.

## Col 0301D (chunk 0001) — *nemo* / *nullus*: shown in Latin

Eriugena's point (the Latin codex's *nemo* against a Greek οὐδείς he renders *nullus*) collapses in
English, where both are "no one". Rendered « No man (*nemo*) hath seen God » / « None (*nullus*)
hath seen God », on the model of the anchor's *homo*/*vir* at 0297C. No marker.

## Col 0302A (chunk 0001) — 1 John 3:2 missing *similes ei erimus*: `[var:]` fired

Printed *Scimus autem quia, cum ipse apparuerit, videbimus eum, sicuti est*; the Vulgate reads
*cum apparuerit, similes ei erimus: quoniam videbimus eum*. The clause about likeness is absent and
the quotation is adduced for seeing alone. Marker on "we shall see". (The opening *Scimus quia filii
Dei sumus* for *nunc filii Dei sumus* is noted, not marked.)

## Col 0302B (chunk 0001) — plate *intellectuum*, TEI *intellectum*

Plate and Princeton both print *virtute purgatissimarum animarum et intellectuum theophaniae sunt*.
Rendered from the plate, "by the virtue of the most purified souls and intellects". Patch filed.

## Col 0302C (chunk 0001) — the plate prints *in sinu⎵Patris* where the argument needs *in sinum* ⚑

*vel ut in Graeco scribitur: qui est in sinu⎵Patris, vel in sinibus Patris.* The TEI has *sinum*,
the accusative that renders the Greek εἰς τὸν κόλπον, and that is plainly what Eriugena is pointing
at: he sets "as is written in the Greek" against the lemma's *in sinu*. **Both copies print *sinu*
followed by a blank exactly one letter wide**, so the plate's type is not the photograph's defect.
Under `PL122-WITNESSES.md` no `[sic:]` may stand on a single letter in this tome, and Pattern 18's
bar (the English asserts what the author argues against) is not met by a repetition, so no marker.
The English renders the plate, "who is in the bosom of the Father", which makes the Greek variant
read as a repeat of the lemma; that is the defect, shown. The TEI's *sinum* is filed as a patch
*toward* the plate reading. ⚑ **A human should decide** whether a blank where a sort failed to
print is a reading to patch toward, or whether the TEI's *sinum* stands and this crux is rewritten.

## Col 0302D (chunk 0001) — *unusquisque … possidebunt*

Singular subject, plural verb, confirmed on the plate. A normal medieval *constructio ad sensum*
(Pattern 9), and English "shall possess" has no number to show; no marker.

## Col 0302D (chunk 0001) — *Sinus … insinuavit*

The etymological play (the Son is the *sinus* of the Father because he *insinuavit* the Father to the
world) is lost in English; both Latin words shown in parentheses, "intimated" for *insinuavit* as
the anchor renders *insinuans* at 0300B.

## Col 0304B (chunk 0002) — *Judaea enim confessio interpretatur*: Floss's NOTAE key ª

The plate sets a raised ª after *interpretatur* and a note under a second rule headed **NOTAE**
(not *Variae lectiones*): *Hieronymus: Juda, laudatio, confessio. Nam* [Hebrew] *dicitur a verbo*
[Hebrew] *Hiph.* [Hebrew]*, id est, confiteri.* Nothing injected (the brief forbids it); recorded in
the plate-reads JSON under `notae`. Same layer at 0307C (Bethania) and 0308A (Jerusalem). ⚑ See

## Col 0304D (chunk 0002) — Rom 4:17 REVERSED: `[var:]` fired

*Ipse enim vocat ea, quae sunt, tanquam quae non sunt* — "he calls the things that are as those
that are not". The Vulgate reads *vocat ea quae non sunt, tamquam ea quae sunt*. Plate-read on the
archive and the whole line re-read on Princeton PA303: printed as in the TEI. The inversion is
sense-reversing, and Eriugena goes on to gloss the call as creation (*creavit cuncta, quae fieri
voluit*), which fits the Vulgate's order, not his. Rendered as printed with
`[var: Vulg. *vocat ea quae non sunt, tamquam ea quae sunt*, …]`. Not argued further.

## Col 0305A (chunk 0002) — *rectas facite semitas*

Eriugena says the phrase "follows" *Dirigite viam Domini*; it does not in John 1:23, it is Isaiah
40:3 / Matt 3:3. Not a divergence in a lemma; noted only.

## Col 0305B (chunk 0002) — *vox praecedit mentis conceptum*

"the voice precedes the conception of the mind": logically the conception comes first, and the
parallel (John preceded the Word) needs the voice to come first *in manifestation*. Rendered
literally, no marker.

## Col 0305B (chunk 0002) — plate *φωσφόρος*, TEI *Φόσφορος*

Both copies print lower-case φωσφόρος with ω. The English carries the plate's Greek. Letter-level,
patch filed with that flag.

## Col 0305C (chunk 0002) — Luke 3:7 / Matt 3:7 paraphrased: `[var:]` fired

*genimina viperarum, quis vos liberabit a superventura ira*: "who will deliver you from the wrath
coming upon you". The Vulgate (Luke 3:7) has *quis ostendit vobis fugere a ventura ira*, "who hath
shewed you to flee". A different verb and a different agent; Eriugena's gloss (*in judicio videlicet
post resurrectionem*) builds on the deliverance. Marker fired.

## Col 0306A (chunk 0002) — *Medius autem vestrum stat*: `[var:]` fired

The Clementine prints *stetit*. Eriugena's gloss *hoc est, praesens vobis apparet* rests on the
present tense, so the tense is material; `[var: Vulg. *stetit*, "hath stood"]`, placed after the
closing italic of the lemma so the marker's own italics do not nest.

## Col 0306A (chunk 0002) — *in divina disputatione et praedicatione*

Confirmed on the plate. "disputation" is odd for God's decree before the world; a *dispositione* or
*praedestinatione* may lie behind it, but the printed words are real and the sentence says nothing
false. Rendered literally, "in the divine disputation and predication". No marker.

## Col 0306D (chunk 0002) — the plate DROPS A LINE: *Si itaque calceamentum Verbi caro Verbi subtilitatem* ⚑⚑

Our TEI reads *Si itaque calceamentum Verbi caro Verbi est, non incongrue corrigiam calceamenti ejus
subtilitatem, et investigabilem perplexionem mysteriorum incarnationis intellige*: "If then the shoe
of the Word is the flesh of the Word, not unfittingly understand the latchet of his shoe as the
subtlety and unsearchable perplexity of the mysteries of the incarnation." **Both independent copies
of the plate omit the whole line *est, non incongrue corrigiam calceamenti ejus*** — the TEI's line
breaks match Migne's, and that line is simply not on these pages. The printed sentence has no verb
for *Si* and no object for *subtilitatem* to be the predicate of. Rendered from the plate, as closely
as the printed words allow: "If therefore the shoe of the Word the flesh of the Word understand
subtlety, and the unsearchable perplexity of the mysteries of the incarnation." No `[ed:]` (nothing
is lost from the digitization; the digitization has MORE than the plate) and no `[sic:]` (a missing
line is not carried type).

⚑⚑ **For a human, and it reaches past this site.** Corpus Corporum's TEI follows this plate line for
line, yet it carries a line these two printed copies lack, and at every other divergence in this
range it has the *better* reading (*sinum*, *remote*, a normalized *Φόσφορος*). That is the pattern of
a later corrected printing with the same lineation as copy-text, like PL 114's two printings. If so,
"the plate" is not one thing for PL 122, and the choice of which printing the English follows is a
ruling, not a stint's call. This stint followed the rule as written (the printing it read).

*investigabilem* is rendered "unsearchable", its Vulgate sense (Rom 11:33 *investigabiles viae
ejus*), not "investigable".

## Col 0307A (chunk 0002) — John 1:27 quoted without *ejus*

*Non sum dignus, ut solvam corrigiam calceamenti* for the Vulgate's *ut solvam ejus corrigiam
calceamenti*. No change of sense; noted, not marked.

## Col 0308A (chunk 0003) — *ad integram plenissimamque divinae speculationis*: adjectives with no noun

Confirmed on both copies. The feminine adjectives most naturally take *civitatem* from the sentence
before (*caelestem civitatem significat*): "into the entire and most full [city] of divine
speculation". Rendered "into the entire and most full one of divine speculation", the neutral "one"
standing for the ellipsis without naming a noun the plate does not print.

## Col 0308B, 0309D (chunk 0003) — Luke 16:16 as *et ab eo regnum caelorum*: `[var:]` fired twice

Printed *Lex et prophetae usque ad Joannem, et ab eo regnum caelorum*, and again at 0309D *Et ab eo
regnum caelorum*, which Eriugena then glosses: *manifestatio gratiae, quam regnum caelorum vocat*.
The Clementine has *ex eo regnum Dei evangelizatur*: "kingdom of God", and a verb. Both sites marked,
because the second is where the gloss turns on the printed words.

## Col 0308C (chunk 0003) — *tres τελεταί … tres mysticae hostiae*

*hostia* rendered "victims" (its proper sense, and the sense that makes three "rites" into three
*offerings*); the Greek τελεταί and συνάξεως carried as printed. Floss's VL at συνάξεως reads *Cod.
synaxios.*

## Col 0309C (chunk 0003) — plate *remota*, TEI *remote*

*omnique carnalis conversationis contagione remota*: both copies. Rendered as an ablative absolute,
"with all contagion of carnal conversation removed". Patch filed (single letter, flagged).
