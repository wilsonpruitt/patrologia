in `cruces.md` apply. **All six leaves of this range (n191–n196) were read at the plate**, corners

renders the plate. ⚠ The archive image clips the right edge of cols 312, 316 and 320; those were

## Col 0310D (chunk 0004) — Floss's NOTAE layer, not our apparatus

The foot of n191–n193 carries a lettered **NOTAE** layer (prose notes, not *Variae lectiones*),
keyed at 0310D (*poena generalis peccati est a*: "J. Scotus pessime omnino docet…"), 0311B
(*mente, non dente, comedimus b*: on Eucharistic doctrine, citing Berengarius's formula), 0313A
(*nasci non possunt a*: that the whole passage on original sin "cum veritate Christiana non satis
convenire") and 0314A (*Pharisaei, divisi b*: Jerome's etymology of *Pharisaei*). Neither the notes
nor their keys are in the TEI. Nothing injected; recorded in the plate-reads file for a ruling.
They are sentences, so under Pattern 15/19 they would want an English `[nt:]` if recovered.

## Col 0312B–C (chunk 0004) — *theoria*, *theoricae scientiae*, *theologica scientia*, *inhumanati Verbi*

*naturalis theoria* → "natural *theoria*" (E4). The adjective *theoricae scientiae* has no E4 row;
rendered "theoric knowledge" so it keeps its root with *theoria* and stays apart from *contemplatio*
("contemplation"); *theologica scientia* "theological knowledge". *rationes inhumanati Verbi* →
"the reasons of the inhumanated Word" (E4). *naturalium virtutum adunationes* → "the unions … of the
natural virtues" (moral sense, Maximian). **Merge note:** peers should confirm "theoric" if the
adjective recurs.

## Col 0312C (chunk 0004) — *depellit rationabilem animum esse*

"drives away that he himself is a rational mind": literal. The sense is that the spiritual man no
longer thinks of Christ as (only) rational soul; *depellit* has no clean English construction with
the infinitive. Rendered as printed.

## Col 0313A (chunk 0004) — indirect question with *utrum … an*, one `?`

*Merito quippe quaeritur, quomodo … tollit, utrum … an … auferetur?* is rendered by Pattern 8b as one
question after a colon, keeping the single printed `?` (an earlier draft split it into two).

## Col 0313B (chunk 0004) — *humana natura* in the nominative: an unconstruable clause, and Floss's unplaced note

*quia jam ab originali peccato humana natura neque in omnibus communiter, neque in singulis
specialiter, re ipsa abolitio facta est perfectaque interemptio.* *humana natura* has no verb of its
own; the sense wants a genitive ("abolition … of human nature from original sin"). Rendered as
closely as the words allow: "that already from original sin human nature, neither in all in common,
nor in individuals specially, in very fact abolition has been made and perfect slaying." The
negation is carried only by *neque … neque*, as printed (no "not" added). ⚑ The foot of this page
prints a *Variae lectiones* note **¹ Sic cod., forte leg. *humanae naturae?*** — Floss keeping the
codex's reading and conjecturing the genitive. Its raised key was **not found** in the running text on
either witness, so it cannot be tied to this site with certainty; this clause is the only *humana
natura* on the page that fails to construe. **Human look wanted:** if the key can be located (a
higher-resolution copy), this crux is answered by Floss himself.

## Col 0315A (chunk 0005) — ἄνωθεν / *denuo*

E3 applied: the lemma's *denuo* is "anew" throughout (0314D, 0315C, 0317B); ἄνωθεν is carried in
Greek and glossed "from above" (*desursum*). The sentence *ut desursum dicamus pro denuo* renders as
"so that we say 'from above' for 'anew'"; the supplied quotation marks are ours (" "), since the words
are mentioned, not used.

## Col 0315B (chunk 0005) — *caelestium virtutum*, *caelestes virtutes*

"heavenly powers" (E4, angelic orders), at 0315B and 0315D.

## Col 0316A (chunk 0005) — *[re] nasci*: Floss's bracket and his note

The plate prints *[re]nasci¹* with brackets and the *Variae lectiones* note **¹ Corr., cor. *nasci***
(the second word reads *cor.* on both witnesses; *cod.* is expected; the letter is flagged, not
guessed). So the codex has *nasci* and *re* is Floss's supplement, bracketed by him. The English
keeps his bracket: "and be [re]born". The printed text, not the codex, is rendered (E9).

## Col 0316B (chunk 0005) — *nis quis* (twin) / *nisi quis* (plate)

## Col 0316C (chunk 0005) — *Sed et hoc dicendum*

"But to this also it must be said:" (E5 family). *Quaeritur, qua de causa …* ends on a full stop in
the plate, so it stays a statement ("It is asked for what reason …"), no `?` supplied.

## Col 0317C–D (chunk 0006) — Augustine's quotation, and *Spiritus* capitalization

Augustine's words (*In Joh. tr.* 12) stand inside Migne's guillemets 1:1 with the lemma italics he
prints inside them. The lemma *Spiritus ubi vult spirat* is rendered "The Spirit breatheth where he
will" because Eriugena's own gloss at once identifies it as *Spiritus sanctus*; the lower-case
*spiritus* of Augustine's and Eriugena's following sentences stays "spirit/it" as printed.

## Col 0318A (chunk 0006) — *sic est omnis?*

Eriugena proposes reading *sic est omnis* as a question; printed "?" kept: "is everyone so?"

## Col 0318B (chunk 0006) — *Ignorans spiritum divinae doctrinae refellitur judaica superbia*

*Ignorans* agrees with *superbia*; rendered "Ignorant of the spirit of divine doctrine, Jewish pride
is refuted", keeping the participle on pride as printed. The plate's stop after *mysteria.* (twin
lacks it) is followed.

## Col 0318D (chunk 0006) — *Inspice ergo* (plate) for *erga* (twin); inverted guillemet

"Consider therefore". Patch filed. On the same column the plate sets an **inverted** mark (`»`) to
open *Sermo, quem locutus sum*; the twin normalizes to `«`, and the English follows the twin's
`«` so that the builder renders an opening quotation. Typographic only; recorded, not marked.

## Col 0318D (chunk 0006) — John 3:12 lemma punctuation

The plate prints *dicebam, vobis, et non creditis quomodo, si dicam vobis caelestia, credetis?*,
with no comma after *creditis* (twin has one). ⚑ **E11 (2026-09-16):** the twin's comma is the
reading that construes, so the English follows it: "*I was telling you, and you believe not: how, if I
shall tell you heavenly things, will you believe?*" Patch withdrawn; the plate's form is recorded here. The faint mark
after *dicebam* was not trusted and is not reflected.

## Col 0319C (chunk 0006) — *humanatio*

*Exitus ergo ejus a Patre humanatio est* → "humanation". Not *inhumanatio* (E4 "inhumanation") and
not *incarnatio*; kept as its own word so the three stay apart. **Merge note:** add to the E4 table
if it recurs.

## Col 0319D (chunk 0006) — *quonium* in 1 John 3:2

The plate itself prints *Scimus quonium filii Dei sumus* (n196, clean on the archive). A single
letter, so under `PL122-WITNESSES.md` no `[sic:]` can be fired in this tome; rendered by its evident
sense "that" and logged here. **Human look wanted:** this is a sense rendering of a non-word without a
marker, which Pattern 7 (as amended) only licenses beside a `[sic:]`; the tome's single-letter rule
forbids the marker. The alternative (carry *quonium* in italics unmarked) would look like our term.

## Col 0320C (chunk 0006) — *Consepulti sumus … per baptismum, nisi in mortem* (Rom 6:4)

Printed *nisi in mortem*; the Vulgate has *in mortem*. The *nisi* is carried ("unless into death")
under 7a, although it breaks the quotation's sense; it plausibly echoes *nisi in morte Christi* a
line earlier (eye-skip). Scripture outside John: logged, no `[var:]` (the range's `[var:]` policy was
kept to the John lemmata), though the column is read and a marker would be licensed. **Human look
wanted:** a `[var: Vulg. *in mortem*]` or `[cj:]` is arguable here.
