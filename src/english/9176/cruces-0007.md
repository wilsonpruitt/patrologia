## Col 0321B (chunk 0007) — *neque dimittere ei sua peccata vult*: subject left as printed

*quoniam neque credit, neque dimittere ei sua peccata vult.* The printed clause makes the
unbeliever the subject of both verbs, so that he "neither believes, nor wills to forgive him his
sins"; the sense wanted is presumably that God does not will to forgive him (or that he does not
will his sins to be forgiven him, *dimitti*). Rendered with the printed binding (7a mechanism 5:
no agent supplied) — "since he neither believes, nor wills to forgive him his sins". No marker.

## Col 0322C (chunk 0007) — plate `demoratur`, twin `demorabatur` (English renders the plate)

The plate prints *ibique **demoratur** et purgat* (present, agreeing with *purgat*); our twin has
the imperfect *demorabatur*, pulled in from the lemma just below. Confirmed on Princeton PA321.
English: "there he tarries and purges it". Patch filed.

## Col 0322C (chunk 0007) — `[var:]` *baptizavit*

The lemma prints *Et illic demorabatur cum eis et **baptizavit***; the Vulgate has *baptizabat*.
Fired because the gloss turns on the perfect: *Ubi quaeritur, si Christus ipse baptizavit, quomodo
evangelista … dicit: Quanquam Jesus non baptizaret*. Column read.

## Col 0322C (chunk 0007) — an indirect question with no mark

## Col 0322D (chunk 0007) — *in* Αἰνὼν, *In Aennon*, *enos*: Floss's Greek over the codex

The lemma sets the place-name in **Greek type**, *in Αἰνὼν juxta Saleim*, and the gloss then reads
*In Aennon, hoc est in aquis; enos enim hebraice aqua dicitur*. Floss's *Variae lectiones* on n197:
¹ at Αἰνὼν, *Cod. Aeunon.* (third letter u/n uncertain); ² at *enos*, *Sic cod. pro Aennon.* So
the Greek in the lemma is **Floss's**, not the codex's, and the codex also wrote *enos* where the
name was meant (E9: the English renders Floss's printed text). Carried as printed: Greek in Greek
(E3), *Aennon* and *enos* as printed. His NOTAE (b) calls the gloss "right": *Recte J. Scotus
interpretatur aquis* and cites Jerome *Aenon, oculus, aut fons eorum*. English: "for in Hebrew
*water* is called *enos*", italics where printed.

## Col 0322D (chunk 0007) — *praecursor discipulus*

*Non enim debuerat praecursor discipulus praecursionis suae mysterium intermittere.* The two
nominatives stand in apposition; rendered "the forerunner, a disciple," rather than binding
*discipulus* to *praecursionis*. Judgment, no marker.

## Col 0323B (chunk 0008) — *Nullo modo … non solum … contulisse*: negations carried; the codex adds a third

*Nullo modo enim credendum est, non solum originalis peccati, verum etiam propriorum delictorum
remissionem contulisse.* Both negations carried: "in no way is it to be believed that it conferred
the remission not only of original sin, but also of personal offences." Floss's ² records the
codex as ***non** contulisse*, which would make it "that it did **not** confer…". The English
renders Floss's text (E9); this is exactly the site a later reader will want the note for.

## Col 0323C (chunk 0008) — *aurora quodammodo umbras excutere*: the verb is shared, not supplied

*et quemadmodum aurora quodammodo umbras excutere, ita doctrina et mysterium praecursoris tenebras
… inchoabat excutere.* The first member has no finite verb; *inchoabat* serves both. Plate
confirms the wording. Rendered with the printed ellipsis ("just as the dawn in a certain manner
to shake off the shadows, so … began to shake off"), nothing supplied.

## Col 0325A–0326A (chunk 0008) — *datum* / *donum*: "given" / "gift"

Eriugena's distinction (*Datum refertur ad naturam, donum refertur ad gratiam*) needs two English
words that do not collapse; *datum* is "the given" (plural *data*, "the givens"), *donum* "the
gift", throughout, including inside James 1:17 (*Omne datum optimum et omne donum perfectum* →
"Every best given and every perfect gift"), since the verse is adduced for exactly this pair.
The lemma's *fuerit ei datum* stays "be given him".

## Col 0325A (chunk 0008) — *quicquam accipere*: Floss's order against the codex

Floss prints the lemma *accipere quicquam*; ¹ records *Cod. quicquam accipere.* The later
repetitions all print *accipere quicquam*. No English exponent; noted only.

## Col 0326A (chunk 0008) — the plate sets *nisi fuerit e*

At the head of col. 326 the lemma reads *nisi fuerit e* with no visible *i*, on both copies; our
twin has *ei*. A single letter, so no `[sic:]` on this tome; not patched (see pending file).

## Col 0326B (chunk 0008) — *sed quia missus sum ante illum* repeated as a head

*Sed quia missus sum ante illum, missus sum in mundum prius, nascendo ante illum.* The first clause
re-quotes the lemma's *sed quia missus sum ante illum* and the rest glosses it; rendered "But that
I am sent before him, I was sent into the world first", with the printed comma (Pattern 8: no
colon supplied, though the clause is a re-quotation).

## Col 0326C (chunk 0009) — *et tu dicis, quia missus **est** ante illum*

The disciples, quoting John back to himself, say *missus est* ("that **he** is sent before him"),
where John had just said *missus sum* ("I am sent"). Plate confirms *est*. Rendered literally,
"thou sayest that he is sent before him". *es* ("thou art sent") is the likely intent; not a
negation or correlative, so below the Pattern 18 bar. Crux only.

## Col 0326C (chunk 0009) — *aut quantus*: Floss corrects the codex

not guessed. English renders Floss's *quantus*, "or how great".

## Col 0326D (chunk 0009) — ⚠ *audit cum*: FOR A HUMAN LOOK

The re-quoted lemma at the foot of col. 326 prints, in our twin, *qui stat et audit **cum**, gaudi,*.
Eleven lines earlier Floss sets the same lemma *Qui stat et audit **eum***. The archive plate shows
*cum*, **but this copy prints e as c elsewhere** (0329A *Qui credit in cum*, where our twin
correctly reads *eum*), and Princeton is too small to settle one letter. So the plate read cannot
decide it, and the English follows the twin literally: "who standeth and heareth with, rejoiceth
with joy". A preposition with no object has no grammatical slot (Pattern 12's test), but no
`[sic:]` may stand on a single letter in PL 122 and the plate may well print *eum*. **If a
higher-resolution witness shows *eum*, patch the twin and render "heareth him".** Filed as
"noted, not proposed" in the pending-patches file.

## Col 0327B (chunk 0009) — *augmentari potuissetis*

*Quod si cognosceretis, ex visibilibus signis augmentari potuissetis.* Literally "you could have
been increased from visible signs"; the sense is presumably that their opinion of Christ could have
grown. Rendered literally, no marker.

## Col 0327B–C (chunk 0009) — the solstice figure

*quod ipse luce inchoante crescere, tenebris autem decrescere natus sit; ego vero luce inchoante
minui, tenebris autem inchoantibus crescere natus sum*: Christ born at the winter solstice as the
days lengthen, John at the summer solstice. The first member prints bare *tenebris* where the
second has *tenebris … inchoantibus*; the English keeps the asymmetry ("with the darkness" /
"as the darkness begins").

lines later. Single letter, one copy: crux only, not patched. English "He that cometh from above".

## Col 0327D (chunk 0009) — `[var:]` *terra est* (John 3:31)

The lemma prints *Qui est de terra, **terra est***; the Vulgate has *de terra est*. Fired because
Eriugena's gloss is built on the printed form: *Et ideo **terra sum**, quia de terra natus sum*.
Column read.

## Col 0327D (chunk 0009) — `[var:]` *Terra es et in terram ibis* (Gen 3:19)

Printed *« Terra es et in terram ibis »*; the Vulgate has *pulvis es et in pulverem reverteris*.
The printed form is the Old Latin rendering of the LXX (γῆ εἶ καὶ εἰς γῆν ἀπελεύσῃ), and it is the
proof-text for *terra sum*: with "dust" the argument loses its word. Fired; column read.

## Col 0328B (chunk 0009) — *hominem venisse Christum*, *hominem … accepisse*

"we do not doubt that Christ came as man" and "to have received man before sin": *homo* here is
human nature as it was before the fall, which is his whole point; kept as "man", not "humanity".
Floss's ² at *credendus*: *Corr.; cod. credendum.* English renders *credendus*.

## Col 0328D (chunk 0009) — *se in Patre, et aequalitatem ipsius esse*

Rendered "he knew himself to be in the Father, and to be his equality": the printed accusative
*aequalitatem* is predicate to *se … esse*, so the Son *is* the Father's equality, not merely
"equal". Kept.

## Col 0329A (chunk 0009) — plate `Filii`, twin `Filius`; Floss's *[ad]* and his *sunt*

Plate prints *unigeniti **Filii** Dei*; twin *Filius*. English is the same either way. Patch filed.
*a se ipsis [ad] interitum praeparati*: Floss's bracket, carried as "[for] destruction". ¹ at
*credituri sunt*: *Corr., pro sunt cod. sed.*

## Col 0329B (chunk 0009) — *verax* / *verus*: kept apart (E3)

The lemma *signavit quia Deus **verax** est* is "that God is truthful"; the Greek reading Eriugena
offers, *Quia Deus **verus** est*, is "that God is true", and the rest of the sentence (*verus
Deus*, *veri Dei Filius*) follows the Greek. The whole point of *quod facilius intelligitur, si
graecus sermo legatur* is that the two words differ, so the English cannot use "true" for both.
*signavit* "hath sealed", which his gloss *quasi sigillo* needs.
