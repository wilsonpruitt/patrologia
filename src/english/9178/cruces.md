Governed by Pattern 7 and E1–E12. Stint C, 2026-09-16. Plate read in full: archive **n550** (corners 1023/1024;

## Standing decisions

- ***theoria*** italic (E4): Eriugena's term for the spiritual reading. *Alia in idipsum theoria* → "*Another theoria
  on the same.*" (the rubric is already italic, so the term takes no second italic).
- *ludus* → "play", *ludibrium* → "plaything", kept apart. *consequentia* → "sequence" both times (1023A), so the
  echo *Neque extra consequentiam* stays visible.
- Greek carried: πρωτοτύπων, glossed by his own *id est, principalium exemplorum*.
- ⚑ **Context, not translation:** the fragment is Eriugena's Latin of Maximus the Confessor (*Ambigua*) on Gregory
  Nazianzen's "play of God" (*Carm.* I.2.2 / *Or.* 7 on Caesarius). *magister / deifer magister* = Gregory; *Ipse … in
  epitaphio in Caesarium* = Gregory's funeral oration on his brother. Not written into the English.
- No `[var:]`, `[sic:]`, `[cj:]`. No scripture quoted.

## 1023A — the opening *Possident fluere et non stare.*

Subject lost in the lacuna (Floss's 16 points, carried dot for dot). "They possess flowing and not standing." The
*non* is carried.

## 1023A — *Si vero et nos ipsi … ludus dicti sumus Dei*: a conditional with no apodosis

As printed, *Si* opens a clause that ends at *magistro.* with no main clause. Rendered as printed ("But if we
ourselves too … have been called the play of God by this God-bearing teacher."); not repaired.

## 1023A — *floris oximori instar* "like the flower of the *oximori*"

Floss prints *oximori* as his correction; his note ¹ gives the codex *ocimori* with the gloss *i. amigdali*
("that is, of the almond") and the Old High German *mundalnuz*. The word is carried in italics as printed
(it is Floss's form, not a plate defect, so no `[sic:]`), with no nominative invented. Probably Maximus's
ἀμυγδαλῆ / a flower that blooms early and falls; the English does not commit.

## 1023A–B — *et omne, si quid aliud de hoc efficacius constitutum est*

"and so is all, if anything else concerning this has been established more efficaciously": "so is" supplies the
elided *ludus est*; logged because it is the only supplied predicate in the work.

## 1023B — *in Caesarium sui fratrem* "Caesarius his brother"

Floss's note ³ *Sic. cod.; f. suum?* conjectures *suum*. English "his" is neutral between them; no marker.

## 1024A — the Gregory quotation ends *adhuc eo longius* with NO stop on the plate (E11)

Both copies print *longius Ad ipsam*; the twin *longius.* The twin construes, so the English closes the sentence;
no patch toward the plate (recorded in the pending-patches file as withdrawn). *adhuc eo longius* "yet further
than that" (Gregory: the flight of a bird passing, and still more fleeting).
*fratres* plural → "brethren"; *viventium ad tempus* "of us who live for a time".

## 1023A — first rubric: plate *theoria.* with a stop, twin without

English follows the plate; patch filed.

## 1024A — *neque omnino esse aestimabitur … sicut neque ludibrium … esse universaliter judicatur*

Both negations carried: "will not be reckoned to be at all … just as neither is a plaything … judged universally to be."

## 1024B — *Fortassis autem et transmutabile … Ponderatam quippe suae naturae definitionem. . . . .*

Barely construable as printed (a Latin calque of Maximus's Greek, broken off by the lacuna): *transmutabile* neuter
with no verb of its own; *ferunt / perfugiunt / patiuntur* have the *materiales res* as subject; *magis vero nos
ipsos … non valentium* has no governing word. Rendered word by word ("the changeable character" supplies only
"character" for the substantival neuter), the dangling genitive left dangling ("but rather of those unable both to
hold and to be held, us ourselves, as being repulsed"), *non* and *nullum* carried. The last sentence stops at the
lacuna as printed. **Needs a human eye if the fragment is ever set against the Greek (PG 91, 1416).**

## Blind read (R6, 2026-09-16), a parse recorded, not applied

1024A *magis vero nos ipsos utpote repulsos et tenere et teneri non valentium*: R6 proposes (low confidence) that *valentium* agrees with the material things and that *nos ipsos* is governed by *tenere* alone, giving "of things unable both to hold us ourselves, as being repulsed, and to be held". The clause is already carried word for word as ungoverned (above), and a low-confidence reparse of Eriugena's Latin of Maximus is not applied. Worth settling against PG 91 (*Ambigua*) if that text is ever collated.
