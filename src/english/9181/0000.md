## Expositions on the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy of Saint Dionysius

Cod. ms. bibl. Univers. Lipsien. 188, membran., sec. XIII. | A.
Cod. ms. Vat. 177, membran., sec. XIV. | B.
Cod. ms. Sorbonn. 1355 bibl. Reg. Parisiensis, membran., sec. XIII. | C.

*[0265D]HERE BEGINS THE PROLOGUE OF THE SECOND BOOK.*

The second book, then, whose inscription is on the ecclesiastical Hierarchy, announces the unity of human nature, redeemed by the blood of our Saviour, ordered, namely, after the likeness of the heavenly priesthood, as far as is possible, for those still mortal. Its high priest is he himself, who fitted it to himself through his grace, who is also the beginning and middle and end of the whole hierarchy, of heavenly things indeed and earthly, to whom all things are referred. This our hierarchy, therefore, after the manner of the heavenly, is threefold, that is, into three hierarchies. Of which the first, in temporal [0266D] order indeed, but last in dignity, is made the legal priesthood. Which was distributed in very various and obscure symbols through the lawgiver Moses to a people still plainly carnal, and serving the letter, not the spirit. Its greatest character was that tabernacle shown figuratively on the Arabian mountain. The second, which is also the middle, the priesthood which now is, the Church of the New Testament I mean, established indeed under grace, ordered partly by the symbols of visible sacraments, but partly perfected by the contemplation of truth. And it is the end of the past first, but the beginning of the future hierarchy, which is the third, now begun in part in the first-fruits of contemplation, but to be perfected after the glory of the future resurrection, [0267A] when, the veils of the visible sacraments taken away, we shall contemplate the truth itself with transparent clarity. Therefore the present ecclesiastical hierarchy, as we have said, is the middle between the past legal and the future heavenly, since it not only performs and visibly considers the symbols of eternal things, as the past legal, but also does not cease to search out and find their spiritual understanding, as the future heavenly.

*Epigram on blessed Dionysius in this book on the ecclesiastical hierarchy.*

*The symbols of divine wondrous sacred things by uniform reason.*

*Into singularity thou hast resolved the one brightness of one light.*

[0267B] *Of Dionysius the Areopagite, bishop of Athens, to Timothy the bishop, on the ecclesiastical hierarchy, the second book begins thus*.

To blessed Timothy the great Areopagite Dionysius wrote the second book on the ecclesiastical hierarchy, that is, on the ecclesiastical sacred principate, distinctly by chapters, which follow below, most elegantly setting down the mystical sacrament first, and so subjoining to the several chapters the several *theoriae* of the mystical sacraments. In which he shows that all things which are done in the ecclesiastical sacraments and in the most sacred consecrations of the mysteries are worked from heaven by angelic ministry, according to the fitness of each order, together with the ministers of the altar, and especially the hierarch, that is, the priest, who is made one of the angels [0267C] according to what is right; most of all, in the ministry of the holy sacrifice of the Lord's passion, the most holy principalities of the heavenly host [0268A] with their consorts in their own order and office of praise always stand by, and in the consecration of the most sacred chrism, which is uniform and of one order with the symbols of the most holy eucharist. In which initiation of the chrism, namely, he clearly shows that the order of the Seraphim stands around the kindly Jesus, who, most holy in all things, sanctifies himself for us, and fills us with every sanctification, perfected in him, and born of God, and with that much-praising hymnology, which the prophet describes that he heard the Seraphim cry, praises intelligibly the same our Lord Jesus Christ, the other choirs of angels also attending, and sensibly singing Alleluia together.

*These are in this book on the ecclesiastical hierarchy, in all [0268B] seven chapters.*

I. *What is the tradition of the ecclesiastical hierarchy, and what its speculation.*

II. *On things to be perfected in baptism.*

III. *On things to be perfected in the synaxis.*

IV. *On things to be perfected in the chrism, and things to be offered in it.*

V. *On the priestly confirmations.*

VI. *On the orders of those to be perfected.*

VII. *On things to be perfected over those who sleep.*

The teaching of the preceding chapters he carries out according to a twofold mode. For first he sets this out: how the mystery of the chapter is performed; and then, in a certain *theoria*, the tradition: for instance, [0268C] first the exposition of the mystery of illumination, then secondly its *theoria*, again the exposition of the mystery of the synaxis, then its *theoria*, and in the same way concerning the rest.

*The rest is wanting.*
