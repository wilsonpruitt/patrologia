## The fifth day before the Ides · 9 January

[0631]

At Antioch under Diocletian and Maximian, the birthday of the saints Julian, and Basilissa, virgin, whose wondrous passions are read. From whose company also a venerable multitude of priests and of ministers of the church of Christ was burned with fire, which had fled to them on account of the savagery of the persecution. After whom Antoninus, presbyter, Anastasius and Celsus, a boy, together with his mother, and many others suffered. At Mauritania Caesariensis, of Saint Martiana, virgin. At Smyrna, of the saints Vitalis, Revocatus, and Fortunatus. [*In Bouillart . . . . .* suffered. [nt: Castellanus, the inspector of the codex itself, observes (the words are Sollerius's) that from that pretended autograph a leaf was once torn out and afterwards restored, in an old script too, on which that change was made, whereas the other Usuardine codices have: 'At Smyrna, of the saints Vitalis, Revocatus and Fortunatus': it is another leaf, I grant; whether another hand, I doubt; certainly the same cast of letters, the same age of both writings. Perhaps for a leaf fouled with erasures or corrections Usuardus substituted another; the same scribe may not have been at hand. Nor do I conjecture this rashly. For wherever the Dervensis codex differs from the Pratensis, in the latter there are erasures; or, if the page is clean, I notice a hand of the same period indeed, but yet another. To one inquiring into the cause of this, the following seemed probable, and approved by the constant practice of authors. Before Usuardus dictated the Martyrology he had corrected much, and after the dictation much: when a leaf had become too fouled for the writing to be read with ease, he tore the leaf out and substituted another. Grant, therefore, that Vitalis, Revocatus and Fortunatus belonged to the first autograph. In the second they gave place to Revolutus and Firminus. Whence were these sought? I do not know. New Acts of the saints emerge daily from the corners of libraries. Perhaps those which Usuardus saw will emerge. That Revolutus and Firminus are of the Africans or the Smyrnaeans whom the other Martyrologies record on this day cannot be affirmed, unless perhaps Usuardus was unlettered (ἀναλφάβητος). For neither does the number agree, nor is there any connexion of the names with one another. Bouillart.] In Africa, of the holy martyrs Revolutus and Firminus with three others.]

## The fourth day before the Ides · 10 January

[0635]

At Cyprus, of blessed Nichanor, deacon, of the first seven, who, admirable for the grace of faith and for virtue, was most gloriously crowned. In the Thebaid, the birthday of blessed Paul, the first hermit, whose soul Saint Anthony saw carried to heaven by angels among the choirs of the apostles and of the prophets.

## The third day before the Ides · 11 January

[0637]

In Africa, the birthday of blessed Salvius. On his birthday Saint Augustine made a sermon to the people at Carthage. At Alexandria, of the holy confessors Peter, Leucius, whose acts are extant.

## The day before the Ides · 12 January

[0641]

At Achaia, the birthday of blessed Satyrus, martyr, who, passing before a certain idol, when he had breathed out upon it, signing his own forehead, it at once fell down; for which cause he was beheaded. On the same day, of Saint Archadius, martyr, renowned in birth and in miracles. [In Bouillart: [nt: That this Archadius suffered at Caesarea in Mauritania, both the Acts of the same Archadius and other Martyrologies of Usuardus too attest. What shall we gather from this contest? That our codex, as it seems to Sollerius, was not written by Usuardus's hand? Not in the least. But that these Acts of Saint Archadius were either wholly unknown to Usuardus or not seen by him; and that hence he fell into that error which other later writers of his Martyrology corrected. Moreover, there could have come into Usuardus's hands some codices into which the same error had crept the more easily because at that time it seems to have been common. Our Ruinart, Act. select. Mart., p. 590, cites several, and by name two Colbertine ones, one of the Reverend Fathers of the Feuillants, and another given to the monastery of Saint-Remi as a gift of Hincmar, bishop of Reims, in which Saint Archadius is read to have suffered at Caesarea in Mauritania. To the same monastery the same prelate also gave a copy of the treatises of Saint Zeno, bishop of Verona, of which the thirty-ninth of the first book is headed thus: *On the birthday of Saint Archadius, who has his birthday on the day before the Ides of January in the city of Caesarea in Mauritania.* Bouillart.] In Caesarea of Mauritania, of Saint Archadius, martyr, renowned in birth and in miracles.]

## On the Ides · 13 January

[0645]

At Rome on the Lavican Way, the crowns of forty soldiers, which under the emperor Gallienus they deserved to receive for the confession of the true faith. In the city of Poitiers, the birthday of Saint Hilary, bishop and confessor, who, banished for four years into Phrygia on account of the catholic faith, among other virtues is said to have raised a dead man. At Reims the metropolis, the deposition of Saint Remigius, bishop, a man of eminent virtue. The proclamation of whose holiness, by the merit of its belief, the nation of the Franks solemnly recalls with the worship of due veneration. [Bouillart adds, in cursive type: [nt: Castellanus rightly observed that these words, 'On Mount Verziaco', etc., were added by a more recent hand. Yet this addition was the sole reason why Bollandus judged the Heriniensis codex purer than the Pratensis codex. Bouillart.] On Mount Verziaco, of Saint Viventius, confessor.

## The nineteenth day before the Kalends · 14 January

[0649]

At Nola of Campania, the birthday of Saint Felix, presbyter [Bouillart: *episcopi*] [nt: Here too Castellanus had eyes. The little space where 'episcopi' is read in the first hand was erased. Likewise the announcement of the second Saint Felix lies on an erasure, at least these words: *Also on the same day.* Whether it was enough for Usuardus that Saint Felix had been designated and elected bishop by the people, for him to call him bishop, I have no mind to inquire. I am bound to answer not for his meaning but for his words. As for his adding another Felix, if it is a fault, it was the fault of very many others, who differ in few things, as will appear below, and not of Usuardus alone. Ado himself mixed together the acts of two Felixes, of whom other martyrologists before Usuardus said that the one was the brother of the other. Lamentable is the lot of one who followed guides not at all safe; and in this matter Sollerius ought to be the fairer toward Usuardus, the more manifestly he is himself caught in the fault which he would reprehend in him. Bouillart.] and confessor, of whom blessed Paulinus writes that, when he was being sent into prison by the persecutors, and lay placed upon shells and potsherds, he was loosed by night by an angel and led out. On the same day, of Saint Eufrasius, bishop.

## The eighteenth day before the Kalends · 15 January

[0653]

Of Habakkuk and Micah, the prophets, whose bodies were found by divine revelation under the emperor Arcadius. At Egypt, of blessed Macharius, abbot, disciple of blessed Anthony, most celebrated both in life and in miracles. Also, of Saint Isidore, most distinguished in holiness of life, in faith and in miracles. In the city of Arverni, of Saint Bonitus, [0655] bishop and confessor, whose life shone with virtues. In the territory of Angers, of Saint [nt: Because Usuardus does not call Saint Maurus the disciple of blessed Benedict, Sollerius, not mindful enough of his own dialectic, concludes from this that Usuardus never thought of the disciple of Saint Benedict, and that, when the ninth century was already well advanced, Maurus of Angers was not customarily numbered among the disciples of Benedict by the Benedictines themselves. Whereas this one thing follows: that no other Maurus of Angers was known at that time besides the disciple of Saint Benedict, and that it therefore seemed superfluous to Usuardus to add any mark by which Maurus of Angers might be distinguished from another. But, he says, *who that is sober would believe that Usuardus would have kept silent about this, who a little before in the same text proclaimed Macarius the disciple of Antony?* No sober man fails to see why he said this of Macarius: namely, because there were several abbots named Macarius, one of them the disciple of Antony. But I ask in turn: what prudent man would believe that a holy abbot, who was the ornament and propagator of the Benedictine family, never came into the mind of a Benedictine and was passed over in compiling a catalogue of the saints? Bouillart.] Maurus, abbot.

## The seventeenth day before the Kalends · 16 January

[0657]

Rome, on the Salarian Way, the birthday of Saint Marcellus, pope, who for the defence of the catholic faith, at the command of the tyrant Maximinus, was first beaten with cudgels, then assigned under public custody to the service of the animals, and after many years of serving there, clad in a garment of haircloth, died. At Arles, of blessed Honoratus, bishop, whose life was illustrious in doctrine and in miracles. [Bouillart inserts: [nt: Sollerius struck out Saint Titianus, although the agreement of the manuscripts is such that he almost thought he must yield to the torrent. Why he nonetheless resisted, the cause was this observation of his informant: that in the Pratensis whatever had been written in the first hand between Arles and Saint Furseus had been deleted. It is true that there is an erasure here. But he who erased the place had written what stands above. He only packed the letters a little more closely, so that room might be given to Saint Titianus, whom he had passed over in the first autograph. That Titianus is the bishop of the city of Opitergium, whom the printed Martyrologies of Usuardus and the German Martyrology commemorate on this day; whose Acts Ferrarius compiled from ancient monuments of the church of Opitergium. Bollandus reports these things on this same day. Bouillart.] Also in the city of Odobergia, of Saint Titianus, bishop and confessor.] At the monastery of Parrona, of Saint Furseus, confessor.

## The sixteenth day before the Kalends · 17 January

[0663]

At the Thebaid, of blessed Anthony, monk, whose body, found by divine revelation under the emperor Justinian, was brought to Alexandria and buried in the church of Saint John the Baptist. At Langres, the birthday of the holy twins Speusippus, Elasippus and Melasippus. Who, when they were twenty-five years of age, were crowned with martyrdom together with their grandmother Leonilla, and Jonilla, and Neon, in the time of the emperor Aurelian. In the city of Bourges, the deposition of Saint Sulpitius, bishop, whose life and precious death are commended by glorious miracles.

## The fifteenth day before the Kalends · 18 January

[0665]

Of the Chair of Saint Peter the apostle, in which he first sat at Rome. And the passion of Saint Prisca, virgin. At Pontus, the birthday of the holy martyrs Moseus and Ammonius, who, when they were soldiers, were first assigned to the mines, and at the last given over to the fire. [Bouillart in the margin: At Paris, in the monastery of Saint Germanus, the dedication of the altar in honour of the most victorious cross, and of the blessed and glorious and ever virgin Mary, and of the holy martyrs Maurice and his companions.]

## The fourteenth day before the Kalends · 19 January

[0669]

[0669]In Smyrna, the birthday of blessed Germanicus, martyr, who under Marcus Antoninus and Lucius Aurelius, through the grace of the power of God, shutting out the fear of bodily frailty, of his own accord provoked the beast made ready: ground by whose teeth, dying for him, he deserved to be incorporated into the true bread, that is, the Lord Jesus Christ. At Spoleto, of Saint Pontianus, martyr, in the times of the emperor Antoninus, whom the judge, having had him beaten most vehemently with rods for Christ, ordered to walk barefoot upon coals: harmed not at all by these, he was ordered to be hung upon the rack and iron hooks, and so to be thrust into prison; where he deserved to be comforted by an angelic visitation, after which, drenched with boiling lead, he was at length struck with the sword. In the district of Dorcasino, of Saint Launomarus, presbyter.

## The thirteenth day before the Kalends · 20 January

[0673]

At Rome, the birthday of blessed Fabian, pope, who, when he had presided over the Church thirteen years, suffered martyrdom in the time of Decius, and was buried in the cemetery of Calistus. On the same day at the Catacombs, of Saint Sebastian, martyr, who, when he held the command of the first cohort, was ordered by the emperor Diocletian, under the charge of Christianity, to be bound in the middle of a field, and to be shot with arrows by the soldiers, and at the last to be clubbed until he expired. On the Cornelian Way, of the holy martyrs Marius, and Martha, with their sons Audifax and Abacuc, nobles of Persia, who had come to Rome to pray in the time of the emperor Claudius. Of whom, after enduring cudgels, the rack, fires, hooks, the cutting off of hands, Martha was put to death in a pool, the rest were beheaded, and their bodies burned.

## The twelfth day before the Kalends · 21 January

[0677]

The birthday of blessed Publius, bishop, who, second after Dionysius the Areopagite, nobly ruled the Church of the Athenians, and, most distinguished in virtues and shining in doctrine, is gloriously crowned for the martyrdom of Christ. At Rome, the passion of Saint Agnes, virgin, who under Symphronius, prefect of the City, was thrown into fires, but, these being extinguished through her prayer, was struck with the sword. In the Spains, in the city of Tarragona, of the holy martyrs Fructuosus, bishop, Augurius and Eulogius, deacons, who in the time of Gallienus were first thrust into prison, then cast into the flames, and, their bonds burned away, their hands spread out in the form of a cross, praying that they might be burned, obtained it. At Troyes, of Saint Patroclus, martyr.

## The eleventh day before the Kalends · 22 January

[0679]

The birthday of Saint Timothy, disciple of blessed Paul. He, ordained bishop at Ephesus by the same Apostle, after many contests for Christ, was crowned with martyrdom. On the same day at the city of Valencia, under Dacianus, governor of the Spains, of Saint Vincent, Levite, and martyr. The noble triumph of whose passion the eminent versifier Prudentius splendidly sets forth in verses: Saint Augustine also, in a certain sermon, commends the praise of the same martyr, [0681] how much it is to be received with the honour of praise. [Bouillart reads: [nt: Sollerius says that Bollandus, who preferred our reading, was deceived by the Pratensis codex; but had he not himself been deceived by his ill-sighted informant, he would have paid his colleague much more honour. For what Castellanus advised is false: that the eulogy which Sollerius presented was erased, perhaps by some unskilled person. The same hand that had first written Sollerius's text, and afterwards erased it, substituted Bollandus's text, which is ours, in its place. I appeal to judges expert in this kind of letters. But how do these things cohere? Nothing is easier to explain. Usuardus, as soon as he had written his copy, allowed it to be copied. Hence the exemplars from which Sollerius drew his eulogy of Vincent. But after he had received his codex back, he blotted out what seemed to him less right and set beneath it what pleased him more. So here Sollerius gives Usuardus pouring out whatever came into his mind; we with Bollandus have edited Usuardus revised and remade by himself. Bouillart.] At Valencia, a city of Spain, of Saint Vincent, Levite and martyr, the triumph of whose contest, in the times of Diocletian and Maximian, after excessive beating and grievous torments, the text of his passion itself declares to have been completed under the governor Datianus.] At Rome at the Salvian Waters, of Saint Anastasius, monk and martyr. Who, after very many torments of prison, of scourgings and of chains, which he had endured in Caesarea of Palestine at the hands of the Persians, was at last afflicted with much punishment, and at the very last beheaded, with seventy others. On this same day, in the city of Ebredunum, of the holy martyrs Vincent, Orontius and Victor.

## The tenth day before the Kalends · 23 January

[0683]

At Philippi, of blessed Parmenas, who was one of the seven deacons. He, handed over to the grace of God by the brethren, fulfilling with full faith the enjoined office of preaching, obtained the glory of martyrdom. At Rome, the birthday of Saint Emerentiana, virgin, and martyr, who, while she was praying at the tomb of Saint Agnes, and at the same time was defending those praying from the harm of the gentiles, was stoned by them. In Mauritania, in the city of Cneocaesarea, of the saints Severianus and Aquila his wife, burned with fires. At the city of Antinoum, of Saint Asclae, martyr, who, first most atrociously tortured by hanging, then burned with flames applied to his sides, at the last, cast headlong into the river, gave back his precious soul to God.

## The ninth day before the Kalends · 23 January

[0687]

At Antioch, the birthday of Saint Babilas, bishop, who in the persecution of Decius, after he had frequently glorified the Lord by his sufferings and torments, obtained the end of a glorious life in chains. There are said also to have suffered with him three little ones, that is, Urbanus, Prilidanus and Epolonus. In the city of Cneocaesarea, of the saints Mardonius, Muso, Eugenius, Metellus, who were all given over to the fire, and their remains were scattered in the river Axus.

## The eighth day before the Kalends · 25 January

[0691]

The Conversion of Saint Paul, which came to pass in the second year of the Ascension of the Lord. On the same day, of Saint Ananias at Damascus, who baptized the same apostle. In the city of Arverni, of Saint Praejectus, bishop, and of Amarinus, a man of God, who suffered at the hands of the nobles of the aforesaid city. Also in the city of Gavali, of Saint Severianus, bishop, a man of wonderful holiness and doctrine.
