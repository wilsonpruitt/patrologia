## The seventh day before the Kalends · 26 January

[0693]

At Smyrna, the birthday of Saint Policarpus, who, a disciple of blessed John the apostle, and ordained bishop by him, was the chief of all Asia. Afterwards, under Marcus Antoninus and Lucius Aurelius, while the whole people [0695] of the same City cried out against him in the amphitheatre, he was given over to the fire. With whom also twelve others, coming from Philadelphia, were consummated by martyrdom in the aforesaid city. On the same day, of the holy martyrs Theogenes with thirty-six others, who, despising temporal death, obtained the crown of eternal life. [Bouillart: . . . [nt: These words, *under the emperor Lizinius*, had been suspect to Sollerius. But after he was advised by Castellanus that in the Pratensis codex the commemoration of Queen Bathildis had been obliterated or half-erased, and these words *under the emperor Licinius* written over it, he now, all hesitation laid aside, deleted that addition, as he calls it, and restored the commemoration of Queen Bathildis: but surely no one will approve it. For besides the fact that exactly the same hand repaired the erasure, it cannot be imputed to Usuardus as a fault that he says Theogenes fell a martyr under the emperor Licinius. I grant it true, what is commonly believed, that his death took place under Valerian. But that emperor had not one name only. I have seen in Mezabarba, D. Vaillant and other collectors of coins that he was also named Licinius. It is agreed too that that emperor of two names raged against the Christians. In proof of which I will not cite a great many Martyrologies; I will not use the authority of Augustine; I study brevity. What sin, then, did Usuardus commit when he referred the death of Theogenes to the time of Licinius? The commemoration of Saint Bathildis was indeed obliterated, but by the same author, and transferred for just causes to another day, as will be noted below. Bouillart.] under the emperor Lizinius obtained the crown of eternal life, *omitting what follows.]* In the territory of Paris, of Baltildis, queen.

## The sixth day before the Kalends · 27 January

[0699]

The birthday of blessed John, bishop of Constantinople, surnamed Chrysostom, who by word and example greatly profited the Christian religion. Also, of Saint Marius, abbot of the monastery *Bobacensis*; whose life full of virtues the illustrious man and patrician Dynamius writes. In Bethlehem of Judah, the falling-asleep of Saint Paula. Saint Jerome, writing her Life, admirable for virtues, testifies that she was crowned with a long martyrdom. In Africa, of Saint Avitus, martyr.

## The fifth day before the Kalends · 28 January

[0703]

At Rome, the birthday of Saint Agnes, the second time. And in the city of Apollonia, of the saints Leucius, Thyrsus and Calinicus, martyrs, who in the time of Decius, tormented with divers kinds of torments, the first and the last by the cutting off of the head, the middle one, called forth by a heavenly voice, giving up his spirit, completed their martyrdom. At Alexandria, of blessed Cyril, bishop, who stood forth as a most illustrious champion of the catholic faith. In the monastery *Beomensis*, the deposition of John, presbyter, a man of God. [Bouillart adds: [nt: Sollerius excluded Julian from the number of the saints of this day, on this ground: that that blessed prelate, as Castellanus notes, was added in the Pratensis codex in a more recent ink. Learned men, your good faith! When it is certain that the hand is the same, has account ever been taken, or should it be taken, of the ink? For I should like to know by what mark the age of an ink is to be discerned. Is it when it is blacker than in the other lines? If the matter stands so, it is all over with the old monuments of letters. For I think there are few in which the ink is of the same blackness throughout. Does it not very often happen to us too, that when the ink is changed the colour of the writing is changed? Shall what shows another colour therefore have been inserted by another's hand? Next, by the same argument the eulogy of John ought to have been cut away. For these words, *presbyter, a man of God*, are written on the same erasure and in the same ink as *At Cinomanni*, etc. This, I suppose, Castellanus did not point out, or else Sollerius, having been told, keeps it down in prudent silence. For the rest, Usuardus fittingly commemorates Saint Julian on this day, on which the Church of Paris, in which he lived and wrote, venerated him. Bouillart.] At Cinomanni, of Saint Julian, the first bishop of that city.]

## The fourth day before the Kalends · 29 January

[0707]

At Rome on the Numentan Way, the birthday of the saints Papias and Maurus, soldiers, whose mouths Laudicius, prefect of the City, ordered at their first confession of Christ to be bruised with stones, and so to thrust them back into prison, and afterwards to be beaten with cudgels, and at the last with leaded scourges, until they expired. On the same day at Trier, the deposition of blessed Valerius, bishop, disciple of Saint Peter the apostle. In the territory of Troyes, of Saint Sabinianus, who at the command of the emperor Aurelian was beheaded for the faith of Christ. [Bouillart adds: [nt: Castellanus wrote to Sollerius that the octave of Saint Vincent had been added to the Pratensis autograph, and at the same time that the last five letters of the text, *us est*, had been erased so that there might be room for the addition. All false. Nothing erased. Only the five letters contracted into two in this manner: *t° ē*. Nothing added either. Nor indeed is it any wonder that Usuardus remembered the octave of Saint Vincent. It was the received custom among the Benedictines that the brethren assembled in chapter should be forewarned by the reading of the Martyrology what feasts the Church would celebrate, the memory of which saints it would keep on the day next following. That the Martyrology was composed by Usuardus for this use is shown by the Rule of Saint Benedict and the Necrology inserted in the codex, which used to be read after the Martyrology. Could a monk of the Prés forget the octave which he knew to be consecrated to the patron of his own abbey? Bouillart.] And the octaves of Saint Vincent, martyr.]

## The third day before the Kalends · 30 January

[0709]

At Antioch, the passion of blessed Ypolitus, martyr, who, somewhat deceived by the schism of Novatus, corrected by the working of the grace of Christ, returned to the charity of the Church, for which [Bouillart adds: and in which] he completed an illustrious martyrdom. At Jerusalem, of the most holy Matthias, bishop, of whom they relate things wondrous and full of faith, that, having suffered many things for Christ, he at the last ended his life in peace. On this same day, the passion of Saint Flavianus, martyr. At the monastery of Malbodium, of Saint Aldegundis, virgin. [In Bouillart: . . . . . Flavianus, martyr. In the territory of Paris, [nt: Queen Bathildis, commemorated in this place, is the reason why the captious censor of the Pratensis codex did not deign on this day to count it among the pure. Yet the anonymous author of the Life of Bathildis, a contemporary of that queen, in Mabillon, Act. SS. saec. II, and in Bollandus, tom. II of January, recorded Bathildis's death on the third day before the Kalends. The most ancient Kalendar of the abbey of Corbie, written 700 years ago, ascribed her death to the same day; on the same day the Martyrologies make mention of Bathildis: the one called Saint Jerome's, that of Pontoise of the twelfth century, that of Fossés, the Colbertine codices A and D; the Victorine A and B; that of Saint-Denis, the Feuillant B, the Monstiburg.; the Church of Paris, and the monasteries of Corbie and Chelles, raised and ennobled by the bounty of Bathildis, keep this day as a feast and a solemnity. But by all these arguments Sollerius could not be brought to believe that this day was assigned by Usuardus to the death of Bathildis. What holds him back, then? The authority of Castellanus, of course, who advised that Bathildis had been substituted in the place of Aldegundis, erased. I do not indeed deny the erasure; but the informant ought not to have omitted that the same hand which wrote out the rest of the codex announced the commemoration of Bathildis here. Let judges practised in this kind of learning come. We are ready to give access to the codex. Bouillart.] of Baltildis, queen.]

## The day before the Kalends · 31 January

[0713]

At Alexandria, the birthday of blessed Metranus, martyr, when he would not utter impious words at the command of the pagans, they bruised his whole body with cudgels; and piercing his face and eyes with sharp reeds, they drove him out of the city with torments, and there with stones cast out the spirit which still remained in him. Also at Alexandria, of the holy martyrs Saturninus, Thyrsus and Victor. At the city of Trent, of Saint Vigilius, bishop and martyr. [Bouillart omits: At the city of Trent . . . . . martyr. [nt: Sollerius adds to this article: *At the city of Trent, of Saint Vigilius, bishop and martyr.* So his codices Heriniensis and Tornacensis close this day's article. So too the Dervensis, far older. There is therefore no doubt that the first autograph of Usuardus made mention of Vigilius of Trent on this day. Usuardus had imitated the Martyrologies which Bede, Florus, etc., had composed before him. Afterwards, having read the acts of Vigilius, he corrected the error. How fittingly he did so will be told on 26 June. Bouillart.]]

## FEBRUARY HAS 28 DAYS.

[0717]

## On the Kalends · 1 February

[0717]

The birthday of blessed Ignatius, bishop and martyr, who ruled the church of Antioch third after Peter the apostle: and in the persecution of Trajan he is sent bound to Rome, where, in the presence of the same, the senate sitting around, he was first afflicted with most monstrous punishments of torments, then he is cast to the lions, and, choked by their teeth, becomes a victim of Christ. At Smyrna, of Saint Pionius, martyr, who after the filth of prison, where he strengthened many of the brethren by his exhortations to the endurance of martyrdom, harassed with many torments, fixed with nails, and placed upon a burning pyre, obtained a blessed end of life for Christ. There suffered with him also fifteen others. On the same day, of blessed Ephrem, deacon of the church of Edessa. In the town *Tricastrinum*, of Saint Paul, bishop, [0719] whose life both shone with virtues, and his precious death is commended by miracles. In Scotia, of Saint Brigid, virgin, whose life shone with miracles.

## The fourth day before the Nones · 2 February

[0721]

The *Ypapanti* of the Lord. At Caesarea, of the most blessed Cornelius, the Centurion, whom blessed Peter baptized. He, raised at the aforesaid city to the episcopal honour, rested. At Rome on the Salarian Way, the passion of Saint Apronianus, who, hearing together with Sisinnius the deacon, while still a gentile, a voice made from heaven, *Come,* saying, *ye blessed of my Father* and the rest; believing, was baptized, and so afterwards in the confession of the Lord received the end of life by a capital sentence. [0723] Also at Rome, of the saints Fortunatus, Felicianus, Firmus, Candidus. At Orléans, of Saint Fuscolus, bishop.

## The third day before the Nones · 3 February

[0725]

In Africa, the birthday of blessed Celerinus, deacon and confessor, and of the holy martyrs Laurentinus, Ignatius, and Celerina: of the glorious praises of all of whom there is extant an epistle of blessed Cyprian. [Bouillart inserts: [nt: In this whole article nothing is more corrupt, in Sollerius's judgment. Three lines and a half blotted out; the mention of Blessed Blasius and of Saint Trifon written over them; the commemoration of Saint Deodatus added: and it is wonderful to tell how hot he grows against these faults. Castellanus is the cause. Had the monks of the Prés been consulted, Sollerius would have received this answer: in the Pratensis codex whatever space lies between *sanctorum* and *Remedii* was erased. But what was written over it shows the same form of script, though in letters perhaps somewhat smaller and set closer to one another than usual. The mention of Blasius is to be reckoned of no account, since a much more recent hand smeared it onto the edge of the codex. Nor ought Deodatus to detain you any more. A hand older indeed than the one that added Blasius added him in the margin, yet not contemporary with the first. Trifon Usuardus added. Rightly. For in this month the Greeks, among whom he suffered, venerate his memory. And his Acts, edited by Thierry Ruinart, attest that Trifon and Respicius were tortured in the harshness of winter, in the violence of a storm. Which suits February better than November, in which the Roman Church honours Saint Trifon. Bouillart.] in the city of Nicaea, the passion of Saint Triphon in the time of the emperor Ducius. *And in the margin:* of blessed Blasius, bishop and martyr.--At Latiniacum, the birthday of Saint Deodatus, bishop and confessor.] At the town of Vapingum, the deposition of the bishops Tigris and Remedius.

## The day before the Nones · 4 February

[0729]

In a city of Egypt which is called Thmuis, the passion of blessed Fileas, bishop, and of Filoromus, tribune, with whom also an innumerable multitude of the faithful from the same city, following the example of their shepherd, was crowned with martyrdom, as the eighth book of the ecclesiastical History tells. At Rome, at Forum Simpronii, of the holy martyrs Aquilinus, Geminus, Gelasius, Magnus, Donatus. At Troyes, of Saint Aventinus, bishop and confessor [nt: After the commemoration of Saint Avienus almost the whole line has been erased, and that before the copy of the Martyrology was offered to Charles, since in the Devensis codex Saint Aventinus is announced last. Bouillart.].

## On the Nones · 5 February

At Sicily, in the city of the Catanians, the birthday of Saint Agatha, virgin and martyr, who, after blows and prison, after the rack and tortures, after the cutting off of her breasts, after rolling upon potsherds and coals, was at length consummated in prison under the judge Quintianus. At Vienne, of blessed Avitus, bishop and confessor, by whose faith, and industry and admirable doctrine, the Gauls were defended from the assault of the Arian [nt: It irks me to point out that the word *Arianae* is not omitted by the Pratensis codex, as Sollerius charges against it. Bouillart.] heresy.

## The eighth day before the Ides · 6 February

At Caesarea of Cappadocia, the birthday of Saint Dorothea, virgin, who, first harassed on the rack, then long beaten with palms, at the last was punished by beheading. At whose confession a certain Theophilus, a scholastic, was converted, and soon, himself most sharply tortured on the rack, at the last was slain with the sword. At Arverni, of blessed Antholianus, martyr. On the same day, of the holy bishops Vedastus and Amandus, whose life and death alike were [nt: Sollerius notes that in the autograph there is *existit* for *exstitit*. A diligent author! He passes over nothing, and hence conjectures that the letters of the autograph are twisted. Nothing is emptier than the conjecture.] glorious with very many miracles. Of whom the former ruled the church of the *Adartenses*, but the latter that of the *Trajectenses*.

## The seventh day before the Ides · 7 February

[0739]

In the Britains, in the city of Augusta, the birthday of blessed Augulus, bishop, who, completing the course of his time through martyrdom, deserved to receive eternal rewards. Also, of Saint Moyses, bishop, This man, first leading a solitary life in the desert, had become magnificently [nt: The Pratensis alone, observes Sollerius, omitted the word *magnifice*. The blame for the false observation I lay either on Castellanus or on him who first copied the codex at the order of Bollandus. Bouillart.] known by signs and virtues, but afterwards converted in great part to the faith of Christ the nation of the Saracens, to which he had been made bishop, and so, glorious in merits, rested in peace.

## The sixth day before the Ides · 8 February

At Armenia the Lesser, the birthday of the saints Dionisius, Emilianus and Sebastian. On the same day at Alexandria, of Saint Cointa, martyr, her the pagans, having seized her and leading her to the idols, were forcing to adore; which when she, execrating them, refused, binding fetters upon her feet, and dragging her through the streets of the whole city, they tore her apart with a horrible punishment. [Brouillart: [nt: Sollerius holds such an opinion of Usuardus that he would not have him sin against the laws of geography; and hence he denies that Lusitania was written by him, because in it he placed Cordoba. Whether Usuardus slipped in memory I do not know. Certainly his hand slipped. Bouillart.] In the province of Lusitania, in the city of Cordoba, of Saint Salominis, martyr. *And in cursive type:* At Verdun, of Saint Paul, bishop and confessor.] In the city of Cordoba, of Saint Salomon, martyr.

## The fifth day before the Ides · 9 February

[0745]

At Alexandria, the passion of Saint Apollonia, virgin, from whom the persecutors first knocked out all her teeth. Then, when they threatened that they would burn her alive, unless she uttered impious words with them, she, deliberating herself a little something, suddenly burst forth from the hands of the impious, and leapt into the fire which they had prepared. At Suevo near Cyprus, of the saints Ammon and Alexander. [Bouillart adds, *in cursive type:* At Fontenella, of Saint Ansbertus, bishop and confessor.]

## The fourth day before the Ides · 10 February

[0747]

At Rome, the birthday of the holy martyrs Zoticus, Hireneus, Jacinctus, and Amantius. In the East, of Saint Sotheris, virgin, who, beaten most heavily and very long with blows, when she had also overcome the other kinds of punishments, completed her martyrdom by the sword. Also at Rome on the Lavican Way, of ten soldiers. [nt: So that there might be room for Austreberta, the last line was deleted entire by Usuardus, and these words, *sister of Saint Benedict, abbot*, written in rather smaller letters. Saint Austreberta was lacking in the first copy. Sollerius ought to have followed his own codices: or, if the Pratensis seemed preferable in this place, it ought to have seemed preferable everywhere, since the script of this addition is the same as that of the rest. I do not point out here that Saint Austreberta is fittingly commemorated. That this holy virgin died on this day is the one opinion of all. Bouillart.] At the Castrum Cassinum, of Saint Scolastica, virgin, sister of Saint Benedict, abbot. [nt: In Bouillart these words are read, written by hand on the margin of the page: *These are not Usuardus's. See the Acta SS. of Mabillon, sec. III, part. I, p. 28 A.*] In the district of Rouen, of Saint Austreberta, virgin.

## The third day before the Ides · 11 February

[0751]

At Lyon, the birthday of Saint Desiderius, bishop and confessor. On the same day at the fort *Nantonense*, of Saint Severinus, abbot of the monastery of Agaunum, by whose prayers the worshipper of God, King Clovis, was freed from his long infirmity. At Alexandria, the deposition of Saint Eufrasia, virgin, who in the monastery shone by the virtue of abstinence and also by miracles.

## The day before the Ides · 12 February

[0753]

In the Spains, the birthday of Saint Eulalia, virgin, who, it is agreed, in the time of the emperor Diocletian received the glorious crown of martyrdom, in the city of Barcelona. In Africa. of the holy martyrs Saturninus, presbyter, Dativus, Felix, Apelius and his companions, who suffered for Christ under the proconsul Anolinus. At Alexandria, of Modestus and Ammonius, infants.

## On the Ides · 13 February

[0755]

The birthday of Saint Agabus, the prophet, at Antioch, of whom blessed Luke writes in the apostolic Acts. In the city Militana of Armenia, of Saint Polioctus, martyr, who is interpreted in Latin *much praying.* In the city of Angers, of Saint Lizinius, bishop, a man of venerable holiness.
