## The seventh day before the Kalends · 26 March

[0875]

At Pentapolis of Libya, the birthday of the saints Theodorus, bishop, Hireneus, deacon, Serapion and Ammonius, readers. At Rome on the Lavican Way, of Saint Castolus, martyr, who, as is read in the acts of blessed Sebastian, hung up by the persecutors three times, heard three times, persevering in the confession of the Lord, was sent into a pit, and, a mass of sand being let down upon him, passed to Christ by martyrdom. At Sirmium, of the saints Montanus, presbyter, and Maxima, drowned in the sea.

## The sixth day before the Kalends · 27 March

[0879]

In Egypt, the deposition of blessed John, hermit, who, among the other marks of virtues, full also of the prophetic spirit, foretold to the most Christian emperor Theodosius his victories over the tyrant. In Pannonia, of Saint Alexander, martyr. [Bouillart adds: [nt: That Saint Eucherius ought to have been placed by Usuardus on this day, Castellanus and Sollerius not wrongly deny. But that he was placed there, no one but an obstinate man, or one plainly a stranger in judging scripts, will deny. If Usuardus sinned, it is at his own peril. Bouillart.] On this same day, of Saint Eucherius, bishop of Tours.]

## The fifth day before the Kalends · 28 March

[0881]

In Caesarea of Palestine, of the holy martyrs Priscus, Malchus, Alexander, who in the persecution of Valerian, kindled by the divine heat of faith, going of their own accord to the judge, rebuke him for his savagery against the blood of the godly; whom he at once gave to the beasts to be devoured for the name of Christ. At the city of the Cabilonenses, the deposition of Saint Guntramnus, king, who so gave himself over to spiritual works that, leaving behind the pomps of the world, he paid out his treasures to churches and to the poor. At Tarsus of Cilicia, of Castor and Dorotheus.

## The fourth day before the Kalends · 29 March

[0883]

At Africa, the birthday of the holy confessors Armogastus, Archinimus and Satyrus, who, in the time of the Vandal persecution, under King Geisericus, having endured many and grievous punishments and reproaches for the confession of the truth, [0885] fulfilled the course of a glorious contest. On this same day, the deposition of Eustasius, abbot of the monastery *Luxoviensis*, who was the Father of almost six hundred monks, and, conspicuous for holiness of life, shone also with miracles. [Bouillart, after the African martyrs: [nt: This last commemoration of the saints Pastor and Victorinus is written on an erasure in the same hand as the rest of the article: after it a space of two lines and a half is left empty of all writing, nay, so scraped with the knife that not even the eyes of a lynx could detect a trace of any little letter. Other codices lack Pastor and Victorinus, and after the African confessors set a long commemoration (*commemoratiouem*, printed so) of Saint Eustasius. Hence Sollerius's complaints, that Pastor and Victorinus were added, Saint Eustasius expelled, and the codex therefore depraved. Had he said altered, I should find nothing to blame; for I freely confess that the Nicomedian (*Nicomendienses*, printed so) martyrs were absent from the Caroline autograph and that Saint Eustasius was present. But how would Sollerius prove that the codex was therefore depraved? Is it because, as he says, Pastor and Victorinus have been torn away from a larger band? But of the codices which he cites in great number there are scarcely two that agree with one another about that band, so that to Sollerius himself the number seems to be changed at will. Doubtless the other codices could with impunity increase and diminish the band at will; the Pratensis alone is at fault (*vftio*, printed so). I pass over what he adds, that those two martyrs are passed over by the other codices. For first, that is false; for the Rosweydinus and the Aquicinctinus, which Sollerius judges to be of the best mark, record them among others. The Gellone martyrology, too, made public by our d'Achery, records them. I do not add the exemplars of the Prés: that would be number, not authority. They are had, moreover, by the Matricula Carthus.-Ultraject. and the Galesinian edition. Next, even if they were lacking in all the codices, nothing would follow from that against the Pratensis. It is a correction of which the other Churches either were not advised, or which, being advised, they neglected. I do not doubt that Usuardus reserved Saint Eustasius for the second day of April for just causes, although Ado of Vienne placed him on this day. Without doubt he saw Acts which have now perished. Perhaps too he was led to it by the Hieronymian lists, whose most ancient codex, which I have before my eyes, is silent about Eustasius today and commemorates him on the second of April. Bouillart.] In the city of Nicomedia, the passion of the saints Pastor and Victorinus.]

## The third day before the Kalends · 30 March

[0887]

At Rome on the Appian Way, the passion of blessed Quirinus, tribune and martyr, who under the emperor Aurelian, after the cutting out of his tongue and hanging on the rack, and the cutting off of his hands and feet, consummated the contest of martyrdom by the sword. At Thessalonica, the birthday of the saints Domninus, and Victor. [Bouillart: [nt: Most codices add to Domninus as companion Victor, who was never a companion of Domninus. Ours first, followed by the Rosweyd., the Marchian., the Pratenses B, D, E, and the Conchensis, indicated the true companions, namely Filopolus and Achaicus. But by I know not what fate it happened that Usuardus, not knowing them at first, put I know not whom there; but afterwards, instructed by new information, he excluded him and gave room to the two others. And this is enough for Sollerius to strike the Pratensis codex off the roll of the pure, to affirm that Usuardus's sense has vanished, and to ascribe the correction to some smatterer. In vain will you show him the same hand: his codices give Victor. Filopolus and Achaicus, therefore, are not Usuardus's. And so the Pratensis codex must lose the title of autograph, because Usuardus, as soon as he had emended anything, did not take care to have it posted at all the crossroads of all cities, so that what he himself had corrected others too might correct. Nor is this the only fault in this article that Sollerius reproaches. He is also indignant that Saint Regulus, bishop of Senlis, was erased. He was certainly erased, and thrown back to the ninth day before the Kalends of May, on which day the Church of Senlis keeps his deposition. For what cause Usuardus changed the place of the commemoration will be told there. Bouillart.] At Thessalonica, the birthday of the saints Domninus, Filopolus and Achaicus, *omitting what follows.*] At the fortress of the Silvanectenses, the deposition of Saint Regulus, bishop and confessor.

## The day before the Kalends · 31 March

[0891]

Of Amos the prophet, whom Ozias, king of Israel, killed, pierced through the temples with a bar, and afterwards, half alive, he was carried to his homeland and there was buried. At Rome, of Saint Balbina, virgin, daughter of Quirinus the martyr, who, after the course of this world was overcome, is buried on the Appian Way beside her father. In Africa, of the saints Diodolus and Anesius.

## APRIL HAS 30 DAYS.

[0893]

## On the Kalends · 1 April

[0893]

At Rome, the passion of most blessed Theodora, sister of the most illustrious martyr Hermes. She, martyred under the emperor Aurelian, was buried beside her brother on the Salarian Way, not far from the city of Rome. On the same day, of Saint venantius, bishop and martyr. In Egypt, of the saints Victor and Stephen.

## The fourth day before the Nones · 2 April

[0897]

At Caesarea of Cappadocia, the passion of Saint Theodosia, virgin, who, in the time of Diocletian, joining herself of her own accord to the confessors in custody, was seized and nonetheless tortured on the rack, by the power of God afterwards rescued from chains, waters, beasts, at the last completed her martyrdom by the cutting off of her head. On the same day, the birthday of blessed Nicetius, bishop of Lyon, whose life shone with miracles, and whose precious death is nonetheless commended by miracles. At Palestine, [nt: Sirmond had once read Mary the Egyptian in the third place, and in the fourth Saint Eustasius. Afterwards Castellanus wrote to Sollerius that Mary the Egyptian had been struck out and Eustasius put in her place. For my part I pity the case of an author who uses other men's eyes. For how can these things be reconciled? If Sirmond read Mary the Egyptian, how was she struck out? If she was struck out, how did Sirmond read her? For my part, had I been in Sollerius's place, this alone would have brought it about that I should put no faith in Castellanus's eyes. Now I teach Sollerius how the codex stands in this place. The last two syllables of the word 'commendatur', the whole commemoration of Mary the Egyptian, and these words, 'item sancti Eustasi', lie on an erasure: of the following, 'abbatis monasterii Luxoviensis', the first runs out into the inner margin, the other two into the lower margin. All in the same hand, but in another ink; another, as I suppose, because the first was beginning to fail. Bouillart.] of Saint Mary the Egyptian, who is called the sinner. [Bouillart adds: Also of Saint Eustasius, abbot of the monastery *Luxoviensis*.]

## The third day before the Nones · 3 April

[0901]

In Scithia, in the city of Thomis, the birthday of the saints Evagrius and Benignus. At Thessalonica, the passion of the holy virgins Agapes and Chionia under Diocletian, who, first wasted in prison, afterwards sent into the fire, but untouched by the flames, after prayer poured out to the Lord, gave up their souls. At Tauromenium of Sicily, of blessed Pancratius.

## The day before the Nones · 4 April

[0903]

At Milan, the deposition of blessed Ambrose, bishop and confessor, by whose zeal, among the other marks of doctrine and of miracles, in the time of the Arian perfidy, the whole of Italy was converted to the catholic faith. At Seville, the deposition of Saint Isidore, prelate.

## On the Nones · 5 April

[0905]

At Egypt, the birthday of the holy martyrs Martiana, Nicanor, and Apollonius. At Thessalonica, of Saint Hirenis, virgin, who, after enduring prison, was struck with an arrow by Sisinnius the count, under whom her sisters also, Agapes and Chionia, were martyred together. At Caesarea of Licia, of Saint Amphianus.

## The eighth day before the Ides · 6 April

[0909]

At Rome, the birthday of blessed Sixtus, pope and martyr, who in the times of Hadrian, that he might gain Christ for himself, willingly endured temporal death. In Macedonia, of the saints Timotheus and Diogenes.

## The seventh day before the Ides · 7 April

[0911]

At Africa, the birthday of the saints Epiphanius, bishop, Donatus and thirteen others. On the same day, of Egesippus, a most holy man, who, near to the times of the Apostles, wove together all the histories of ecclesiastical deeds from the passion of the Lord up to his own age, so that he might express also the manner of speaking of those whose life he followed. At Alexandria, of Saint Pelusius, presbyter.

## The sixth day before the Ides · 8 April

[0915]

At Corinth, of blessed Dionisius, bishop, by whose learning and grace, which he had in the word of God, many profit even to this day. And this chosen one of Christ likewise shone in the times of Marcus Antoninus and Lucius Aurelius. At Tours, of Saint Perpetuus, bishop, a man of admirable holiness.

## The fifth day before the Ides · 9 April

[0917]

The birthday of blessed Procorus, who was one of the first seven deacons; he, renowned in faith and miracles, was consummated by martyrdom at Antioch. At Sirmium, the passion of the seven holy virgins, who, paying together the price of their blood, purchased eternal life.

## The fourth day before the Ides · 10 April

[0919]

Of Ezekiel the prophet, who, slain by the judge of the people of Israel at Babylonia, was buried in the tomb of Sem and Arfaxad. At Rome, of very many blessed martyrs, whom Saint Alexander, pope, baptized when he was held in prison. All these the emperor Aurelian caused to be put on board an old ship, to be led out into the deep sea, and there, stones being tied to their necks, to be sunk in the depth of the sea. At Alexandria, of Saint Appolonius, presbyter, and five others.

## The third day before the Ides · 11 April

[0921]

At Rome, the birthday of blessed Leo, pope, in whose times there was the holy synod of Chalcedon. At Crete, in the city of Gortina, of Saint Philip, bishop, who was endowed with great virtues and the best studies. And he flourished [0923] in the times of the emperors Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius. In Dalmatia, in the city of Salona, of Saint Domio, bishop, with eight soldiers. At Nicomedia, of Saint Eustorgius, presbyter.

## The day before the Ides · 12 April

[0925]

At Rome on the Aurelian Way, the birthday of blessed Julius, pope, who, having endured tribulations and exiles for ten months under the emperor Constantius, after his death returned with great glory to his see, and rested in peace. [Bouillart inserts: On the same day, [nt: The mention of Zeno, although made on another leaf, shows the same form of letters, the same ink, the same parchment, so that only a blind man, or a most unskilled judge of scripts, could blurt out that it was intruded after Usuardus. No erasure, a clean character, nothing that could cast suspicion into even captious judges. Almost all the codices known to Sollerius support it. Of those I have gone through, none dissents: four of the Prés of the best mark, two of which exceed five hundred years, two Victorine, the Conchensis, that of Pontoise, the Feuillant A, B, the Colbertine A, B, C, D, that of Fossés: the books more correctly and accurately edited agree, as Sollerius admits: an ancient martyrology written at the beginning of the eleventh century and carefully kept at Paris in the royal priory of Saint-Martin gives the same reading: the Roman Martyrology commemorates the same Zeno on the same day. And shall a reading propped by so many supports totter and be imperilled? Without doubt Sollerius saw codices to set against the Pratensis. So it is: he saw two, the Heriniensis and the Tornacensis; let him add the Dervensis too, it is nothing to me. It was no concern of the other Churches that whatever had been emended by Usuardus they too should emend. But, destitute of codices, he fights perhaps with arguments, and those indeed such as cannot be answered. He must be heard. Very rarely, he says, does Usuardus summon Transalpine saints. What then, pray? If very rarely, then sometimes. And Zeno will be one of those. But Usuardus's narrative is more than doubtful. Why then did Henschenius hesitate in doubt whether to assert Zeno pontiff and confessor only, or martyr as well? Has Sollerius found out anything new? But Henschenius pronounces expressly, with no argument indeed, but yet expressly pronounces, that Zeno was intruded by later men. I would not dare to answer. And what will the reader think of this urbanity of Sollerius? I would dare to suspect that there was someone, a fellow initiate (symmistes) of Usuardus... who thought his own style must be tempered to Usuardus's. Bouillart.] of Saint Zeno, bishop, who amid the storms of persecution wonderfully ruled the city of Verona, and in the time of Gallienus was crowned with martyrdom.] At Vapingum, the deposition of Constantinus, bishop.

## On the Ides · 13 April

[0929]

At Pergamum, a city of Asia, the birthday of the saints Carpus, bishop. Papirius, deacon, and Agathonica, an excellent woman, and of many other women, who for their blessed confessions were crowned with martyrdom under Antoninus Verus and Aurelius Commodus. With whom also the wonderful man Justin the philosopher, labouring very much in writing for the religion of Christ, received the gift of martyrdom as the reward of a faithful tongue. In Spain, of Saint Erminigildus, who, for the confession of the catholic faith, struck on the head with an axe, entered the heavenly kingdom in place of the earthly [sic: *terterreno*], a king and martyr. [Bouillart, at the edge of the page: On the same day at the monastery of Saint Germanus, the consecration of the altar in honour of the holy and undivided Trinity and of the life-giving cross, and also of the most blessed Mother of God, Mary, and of Saint Guandregisilus, and also of all the saints.]

## The eighteenth day before the Kalends · 14 April

[0933]

At Rome on the Appian Way, the birthday of the most blessed martyrs Tiburtius, Valerian and Maximus, Almachius being prefect, of whom the first were beaten with cudgels, struck with the sword, the last was beaten with leaded scourges so long, until he breathed out his spirit. At Alexandria, of Saint Fronto, abbot, whose life stood forth glorious in holiness and miracles. At Interamna, of blessed Proculus, martyr. Also, of Saint Domnina, virgin, crowned with her companion virgins.

## The seventeenth day before the Kalends · 15 April

[0935]

At Persis, in the city of Cordula, the birthday of the saints Olympias, and Maximus, nobles, who at the command of Decius were beaten with cudgels, and then with leaded scourges, at the last their heads were beaten with axes, until they gave up their spirit. In Italy, of the blessed martyrs Maro, Euticetis and Victorinus, who, first leading a long exile at the island Pontia in the confession of Christ, afterwards under the emperor Nerva, when they had converted very many to the faith, were ordered to be slain by various punishments.

## The sixteenth day before the Kalends · 16 April

[0937]

At Corinth, the birthday of the saints Callistus and Carisius, with seven others, all drowned in the sea. At Caesaraugusta, of the eighteen holy martyrs Quintilianus, Cassianus, Matutinus, Publius, Urbanus, Martialis, [0939] Faustus, Successus, Felix, Januarius, Primitivus, Evotus, Cecilianus, Optatus, Fronto, Lupercus, Apodemus, and Julius. These all together, under Datianus, governor of the Spains, were afflicted with punishments and slain.

## The fifteenth day before the Kalends · 17 April

[0941]

At Africa, the birthday of Saint Mappalicus, martyr, who, as blessed Cyprian writes in the epistle to the martyrs and confessors, was crowned with very many others. At Antioch, of the holy martyrs Peter, deacon, and Hermogenes. [Bouillart inserts: [nt: In the Pratensis codex, between the Antiochene and the Cordoban martyrs, mention is made of Nicephorus. In the same place the manuscripts Aquicinct., Bruxell. and Rosweyd., three of the Prés B, D, E, the Conchensis, the Montisburg. and the Lubeck-Cologne edition made mention of him. Yet the Pratensis was not worthy for Sollerius to follow, both on account of other codices which lack Nicephorus, and most of all because, as Castellanus advised, what is said of Nicephorus was wholly intruded, and that by a hand not very ancient. Am I to hear so often of the same thing? Am I so often to deafen the reader about the same thing? But here I wish Sollerius to learn more about our codex from me than he learned from Castellanus. The last line of the page, where Peter the deacon and Hermogenes are now seen, was once almost wholly erased, and restored in another hand, though one nearest to the age of Usuardus, and in another ink. Let Sollerius turn over his notes again. Perhaps his informant said of those martyrs what he himself understands of Nicephorus. Next, the first two lines of the following page, where Nicephorus and the Cordoban monks are now read, were likewise erased. But let me have credit with Sollerius that the hand which wrote those saints is the very same as that of the rest of the codex; in a smaller form of letters, however, so that room might be given to Nicephorus, whom Usuardus brought in here, deceived beyond doubt by certain Acts which falsely claimed that the feast of Nicephorus was kept on this day. Certainly in a manuscript Collection of Acts I read this title prefixed to the Acts of Saint Nicephorus: The Passion of Saint Nicephorus, martyr, which is kept on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of May. Bouillart.] In the East, of blessed Nicephorus, crowned under Valerian and Gallienus for the martyrdom of Christ.] Also at Cordoba, of the holy martyrs Helias, presbyter, Paul and Isidore, monks.
