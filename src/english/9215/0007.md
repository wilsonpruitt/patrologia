## The tenth day before the Kalends · 23 August

[0389]

The birthday of the holy martyrs Donatus, Restitutus, Valerianus and Fructuosa, with twelve others, who were crowned at Antioch with the most illustrious honour of confession. On the same day, of blessed Zacheus, bishop, who, fourth from James, ruled the church of Jerusalem most illustriously. At Lycia, in the city of Egea, of Saints Claudius, Asterius and Neon, who after bitter torments were fastened to the cross. At the Port of the city of Rome, of Saints Ypolitus, Quiriacus and Archillaus. At Alexandria, of Saint Theonas, bishop, venerable and truly worthy of God. [*Bouillart, in another order, with a few things added: . . . . .* were crowned, at Lycia, in the city of Egea, of the holy martyrs Claudius, Asterius, Neon, [nt: The Caroline autograph differs much. Zacheus, who is here in the fourth place, is put second in the Dervensis codex. Claudius and his companions, who occupy the second place in the Pratensis, the third in the Dervensis. Lastly, that Saint Claudius, who has four companions in the Pratensis, has only two in the Dervensis, namely Asterius and Neon. And to correct all these things, Usuardus blotted out five whole lines in his own codex. I will not say that the hand is the same; it is enough to have repeated that a hundred times. Let us see by what causes Usuardus was led to correct. For the order in which he arranged those saints, Sollerius, I think, will not summon the author to court. It is a thing of no account, on which it would be a scruple to dwell in defending it. Nor will the censor of the Pratensis codex deny that Domnina and Theonilla were rightly added to Claudius, Asterius and Neon. We have their proconsular acts, entire and genuine, where those five martyrs are called by name to confess the faith and are tortured with punishments; and at the end, *this passion was held in the city of Aegea, under the governor Lysias*, on the tenth *day before the Kalends of September, Augustus and Aristobolus being consuls*. In the Caroline autograph these words were not present, *who after bitter torments were fastened to the cross*, which Sollerius nevertheless inserted in his article as Usuardus's. The three martyrs Claudius, Asterius, Neon are named in the Dervensis codex with nothing added. Bouillart.] Domnina and Theonilla, who under the governor Lysias were tortured with various punishments and slain. At the Port of the city of Rome, of Saint Yppolitus, Quiriacus and Archillaus. On the same day, of blessed Zacheus, bishop, who, fourth from James, ruled the church of Jerusalem most illustriously. At Alexandria, of Saint Theonas, bishop, venerable and truly worthy of God.] Also the birthday of Saints Timotheus and Apollinaris, who, having consummated their martyrdom at the city of Reims, merited the heavenly kingdoms. At Lyon of Gaul, of the holy martyrs Minervius and Eleazarius, with eight sons.

## The ninth day before the Kalends · 24 August

[0393]

The birthday of blessed Bartholomew the apostle, who, preaching the Gospel of Christ in India, completed his martyrdom by beheading. The most sacred body of this apostle, translated first to the island of Lipari, then to Beneventum, is honoured with the pious veneration of the faithful. On the same day, at Carthage, of three hundred martyrs, who suffered in the time of Valerian and Gallienus: at length, after the tortures inflicted on them, confessing Christ the Son of God to be God, they were plunged into a burning kiln of lime. From which also that white-robed army of the blessed merited to be called the *Massa candida*, the White Mass. In the city of Rouen, of Saint Audoenus, confessor, who, wholesomely despising the world, by the merit of holiness obtained the honour of the episcopate. At Nevers, of Patricius, abbot.

## The eighth day before the Kalends · 25 August

[0397]

At Rome, the birthday of the holy martyrs Eusebius, Pontianus, Vincent and Peregrinus, under the emperor Commodus, who were first raised upon the rack, stretched also with sinews, and then beaten with cudgels, flames being applied about their sides; but when they remained most faithful in the praise of Christ, they were slain with leaded scourges until the giving up of the spirit. Also at Rome, of Saint Genesius, martyr, in the time of Diocletian Augustus, who, first beaten most cruelly with cudgels, hung upon the rack and harassed by a very long tearing of the hooks, set on fire also with torches, when he persisted in the faith, saying: There is no king but Christ, for whom, if I should be slain a thousand times, you will not be able to take him away from my mouth, nor him from my heart, merited the crown of martyrdom by the cutting off of his head. On the same day, of blessed Genesius of Arles, who, discharging the office of a notary, when he would not take down the impious edicts by which Christians were ordered to be punished, was seized and beheaded, and, baptized in his own blood, received the glory of martyrdom. In Spain [nt: The Dervensis codex: *in Spain, in the city of Italica*. Usuardus deleted *in Spain* in his own codex; for what cause, who could say? Was it because, speaking of a city well enough known, he judged it useless to name the region in which it lay? Of Saint Gerontius there is nothing in the martyrologists who are older than Usuardus. Beyond doubt he learned of his cult in Spain. The oldest thing extant about that Gerontius is an epigram of Isidore of Seville, which Juan Tamayo Salazar read written by hand and wove into his Spanish martyrology. In that epigram Gerontius is said to have suffered the punishments of prison and hunger under Nero. Whence Salazar gathers that he suffered about the year 68, when the cruelty of Nero raged most violently in Spain. Bouillart.], in the city of Italica, of Saint Gerontius, bishop, resting in prison.

## The seventh day before the Kalends · 26 August

[0401]

At Rome, the birthday of blessed Zephirinus, pope. And of the holy martyrs Hireneus and Abundius, who are read of in the passion of blessed Laurence. At the fort *Victimilium* of Italy, the birthday of blessed Secundus, martyr, a man of rank and a commander from the legion of the holy Thebeans. Also in the city of Pergamis, of Saint Alexander, who himself too was one from the legion of the Thebeans, and, since he confessed the name of the Lord Jesus most steadfastly, completed his martyrdom by the cutting off of his head. [*Bouillart: . . .* completed. [nt: Saint Aredius is lacking in the Caroline autograph; and in his place: *At Auxerre, of Eleutherius, bishop*. Afterwards Usuardus erased Eleutherius from here, having read, as may be conjectured, the Acts of that holy prelate, which record his death not on the 26th but on the 16th day of August. He is reckoned the sixteenth bishop of the Church of Auxerre, and they say he died in the year 551: if that is true, all the chronological marks which the Anonymous used in Labbe's new Bibliotheca, t. I, p. 419, are false. As regards Saint Aredius, Usuardus must have been deceived by certain Acts of that saint different from those which Mabillon, the chief ornament of our Congregation, made public, tom. I of the Acts, p. 349: for these relate that Aredius, on the ninth day before the Kalends of September, called his disciples together and warned them of his approaching death; and that he gave back his soul at the second hour of the night. If this second hour of the night means the second after midnight, Aredius will have died on the eighth day before the Kalends of September; but if it indicates only the second from sunset, he will have died on the ninth before the Kalends. Bernard Gui testifies, in Labbe's Bibliotheca, p. 631, that the feast of Saint Aredius is kept on the eighth *day before the Kalends of September*. Bouillart.] In the territory *Lemovicensis*, of Saint Aredius, presbyter and confessor.] At Auxerre, of Eleuterius, bishop.

## The sixth day before the Kalends · 27 August

[0405]

At Capua, the birthday of Saint Rufus, martyr, whom blessed Apollinaris, disciple of Peter the apostle, taught, this same Rufus being of patrician rank. In the city of Thomis, of the holy martyrs Marcellinus, a tribune, and his wife Mannea, John, Serapion and Peter. On the same day, the birthday of Saints Georgius, deacon, Aurelius, Felix, Nathalia and Liliosa, of whom the first, a monk of wonderful abstinence, coming from Jerusalem to Cordoba, merited to attain, with the rest, sprung of noble stock from that same city, the martyrdom long desired and shown to him beforehand by the Lord. In the city of Arles, of Saint Caesarius, bishop, a man of wonderful holiness and zeal for piety. At Augustudunum, of Saint Siagrius, bishop and confessor.

## The fifth day before the Kalends · 28 August

[0407]

At Rome, the birthday of blessed Hermes, an illustrious man, who, as is read in the acts of Alexander the pope, was first committed to custody in prison, then obtained martyrdom by the sword striking. In Constantinople, of Saint Alexander, bishop and confessor, a glorious old man, by the power of whose prayer Arius was condemned by the judgment of God. In Africa, the deposition of Saint Augustine, bishop, who, first translated from his own city to Sardinia on account of the barbarians, was lately brought back to Ticinum by the king of the Lombards, and honourably laid to rest. On this same day, the birthday of Saint Julian, martyr, who, in the time of persecution, at the urging of blessed Ferreolus, removed by a secret departure from the city of Vienne to the territory of the city *Arverna*, where at length, his throat cut, he was killed by a horrible death. At Sanctonas, of Saint Vibianus, bishop and confessor.

## The fourth day before the Kalends · 29 August

[0411]

The beheading, or rather the finding of the head, of the most blessed John the Baptist. For his beheading is proved from the Gospel reading to have happened about the Paschal solemnity; it is nevertheless kept here as a feast, when the head of the same was found a second time in the city of Emessa, and laid to rest in a church. At Rome, the birthday of the holy and most illustrious Sabina, martyr, who under the emperor Hadrian, struck with the sword, obtained the palm of martyrdom. In the district of Troyes, of Saint Sabina, virgin, who, having undertaken a most laborious pilgrimage for the love of Christ, glorious also for virtues and miracles, rested in peace. [nt: Here I would gladly let Sollerius go uncorrected. For although he found some fault in the Pratensis codex, he nevertheless deigned to number it among the pure. For which I thank him. But it will not be out of place to weaken the testimony of Castellanus even in the slightest matters. That most sharp-eyed inspector wrote that there is an erasure in the Pratensis from *Parisius dep.* to the end. This is true. Next, that there is now written on the erasure *ositio Mederici presbyteri et monachi*, etc. This I flatly deny: after *dep.* there follows next *sancti Mederici et monachi*. Lastly, that *Mederici episcopi* was once written there, as Sollerius charges, is most false: since the Dervensis codex calls Medericus not bishop but presbyter only. Nor was he ever a bishop; but first a monk, then abbot of one of the two monasteries which were at Autun at that time; at length at Paris, whither he had come for the sake of prayer, he exchanged life for death on the fourth day before the Kalends of September. On the same erasure on which Saint Medericus stands, Saint Candida stands too. The script of both corrections is the same. Yet of Saint Candida Sollerius says thus: *Certainly our codices sufficiently prove it to be Usuardus's.* What was added of Medericus, therefore, is equally Usuardus's. Now I ask Sollerius to come himself, to teach what difference there is between the script of this correction and that of the other corrections. ## The third day before the Kalends · 30 August

[0415]

At Rome, on the Ostian Way, the passion of blessed Felix, presbyter, under Diocletian and Maximian, emperors, who, after the torment of the rack, sentence being given, when he was being led to be beheaded, there met him a certain Christian; this man, while he confessed of his own accord that he was a Christian, was forthwith beheaded together with the same. Not knowing his name, [0417] the Christians called him Adauctus, because he was added to Saint Felix for the crown. Also at Rome, of Saint Gaudentia, virgin. [Bouillart adds: [nt: Castellanus was deceived, and deceived Sollerius, when he wrote that Saint Agilus was not entered in the first hand. Bouillart.] In the district *Meldensis*, of Saint Agilus, confessor.]

## The day before the Kalends · 31 August

[0419]

At Trier, the birthday of Saint Paulinus, bishop and confessor, who, banished into exile by the emperor Constantius for the Catholic faith, and worn out until death by the changing of exiles, even beyond the Christian name, at the last dying in Phrygia, received from the Lord the crown of a blessed passion. On the same day, at Athens, of blessed Aristides, most renowned in faith and wisdom, who offered to the emperor Hadrian books on the Christian religion, and, in the presence of the emperor himself, pleaded most brilliantly that Christ Jesus alone is God. At Auxerre, of Saint Optatus, bishop and confessor.

## SEPTEMBER HAS 30 DAYS.

[0423]

## On the Kalends · 1 September

[0423]

Of Jesus Nave and Gedeon the prophets. Also the birthday of the most blessed Anna the prophetess, whose holiness the Gospel's word makes known. At Capua, on the Aquarian Way, of Saint Priscus, martyr, who was one of the ancient disciples of Christ. At Reims, the deposition of Saint Sixtus, first bishop of that city. At Senones, of blessed Lupus, bishop and confessor, of whom it is related that on a certain day, while he was standing at the sacred altars with the clergy present, a gem fell from heaven into his holy chalice. At Cenomanni, of Saint Victor, bishop. [Bouillart adds, in italic type: [nt: An ancient hand set Egidius under this article, and so ancient that the difference is obscure. Yet Sollerius knew that it was other than the first, and we freely confess it. Why then, for so slight a cause, does he erase the Pratensis codex from the roll of the pure? Bouillart.] On the same day, the birthday of Saint Egidius, abbot.]

## The fourth day before the Nones · 2 September

[0427]

At Lyon of Gaul, the birthday of Saint Justus, bishop and confessor, who, withdrawing into the desert, when for some years he had led a life next to the angels, and a worthy end was at hand for such great labours, merited by a blessed gain to ascend to the kingdom of the heavens. His holy little body, with the bones of blessed Viator his minister, was afterwards brought back to Lyon. On the same day, at the aforesaid city, of Saint Helpidius, bishop and confessor. In Appamia, of Saint Antoninus, martyr.

## The third day before the Nones · 3 September

[0431]

The birthday of Saint Febe, of whom the blessed Apostle writes to the Romans. At Rome, the passion of blessed Serapia, virgin, under the emperor Hadrian, who, when she had been handed over to two lustful men and could not at all be abused, nor afterwards be set on fire with burning torches, was beaten with cudgels at the command of the governor, and so beheaded with the sword. The day of her passion is kept on the fourth day before the Kalends of August, but that of her burial on the present day, when also the memory of her martyrdom is celebrated more solemnly. In Capua, of the holy martyrs Antoninus, a boy of twenty years, and Aristeus, bishop, whose acts are extant. [Bouillart adds: [nt: With his usual fairness our censor did not judge the Pratensis codex pure, on account of the ordination of Saint Gregory added to the present article, although the difference of the scripts does not force itself upon the eyes. Bouillart.] At Rome, the ordination of Saint Gregory, pope.]

## The day before the Nones · 4 September

[0435]

Of Moyses the prophet. At Ancyra of Galatia, the birthday of the holy martyrs, three boys, Rufinus, Silvanus and Vitalicus. At Cabilonis, of Saint Marcellus, martyr, who, when he had been invited by the governor Priscus to a profane banquet, and, execrating feasts of that kind, rebuked with free reproach all who were present, why they were slaves to idols, the governor ordered him, by an unheard-of kind of cruelty, to be buried standing upright up to the waist; and so the holy martyr of God, persevering on the third day in the fear and praises of God, gave back an undefiled spirit. On this same day, of Saints Magnus and Castus.
