## The fifteenth day before the Kalends · 18 December

[0813]

At Macedonia, in the city of Philippi, the birthday of the blessed martyrs Rufus and Zosimus. These were of the number of the disciples through whom the primitive Church was founded among the Jews and the Greeks, of whose happy contest also Saint Policarpus writes in the epistle to the Philippians. In Africa, the passion of Saint Moyses, martyr, At Tours, of Saint Gatianus, bishop and confessor, who, sent from the city of Rome to that same city as its first bishop, illustrious for many miracles, rested in peace.

## The fourteenth day before the Kalends · 19 December

[0817]

At Egypt, of blessed Nemesius, martyr, who, tortured with redoubled punishments by the judge Emilianus, was ordered to be burned with robbers, bearing the likeness of the Saviour, who together with robbers endured the cross. In the city of Nicaea, of Saint Darius, martyr. Also at Auxerre, of Saint Gregory, bishop.

## The thirteenth day before the Kalends · 20 December

[0819]

At Alexandria, of the most blessed martyrs Ammon, Zeno, Ptolomeus, Ingenius and Theophilus, who, standing by the tribunals, when a certain Christian set in the punishments was trembling, and was now almost inclining to deny, were trying with face, eyes and nods to raise him up. And when for this cause the clamour of the whole populace leapt out against them, bursting forth into the midst, they testify that they are Christians. Through whose victory Christ at once most gloriously triumphed, and gave constancy of mind to his own. In Thrace, in the city of Gelduba, of Saint Julius, martyr. Also in Amphitrea, of Saint Liberatus.

## The twelfth day before the Kalends · 21 December

[0823]

The birthday of blessed Thomas the apostle, who, preaching the Gospel to the Parthians and Medes, suffered in India, and not long after was translated thence to the city of Edessa. On the same day, in Tuscia, of Saints John and Festus.

## The eleventh day before the Kalends · 22 December

[0825]

At Rome on the Lavican Way between the Two Laurels, the birthday of thirty martyrs, who all on one day, in the persecution of Diocletian, were crowned. At Alexandria, of blessed Ischyrion, martyr. He, when he was being forced to sacrifice by blandishments and injuries, and despised them; pierced through the vitals with a sharpened stake, is handed over to death. In the city of Ostia, of Saints Demetrius, Honoratus and Florus. [*Bouillart:* [nt: I confess that Basileus was not well announced here by Usuardus. For if he followed the Hieronymian lists, he ought to have written: *In the city of Ostia, of Saints Demetrius, Honoratus* (for of Florus there is no mention in our very old codex); or if he had a mind to commemorate Basileus, it was not *Ostia* but *Antioch* that ought to have been assigned as the city. As I sought the cause of this error, the 21st day of November came back to my mind, where I had read: *In the city of Ostia, of the holy martyrs Demetrius and Honorius*. At once I unrolled our codex of the Hieronymian Martyrology, which comes nearest to the age of Usuardus, and which is likely to have been copied from the one which Usuardus consulted in writing his own. There on both days, that is 21 November and 22 December, I see the same saints recorded in the same lines, at Ostia Saints Demetrius and Honorius, at Antioch Saint Basileus, with this one difference, that on 22 December *Honoratus* is read for *Honorius*. For my part I conjecture that the error flowed from that likeness of saints and cities. Perhaps Usuardus had closed his article with the commemoration of Saints Demetrius and Honoratus. Afterwards, recalling that the same saints had been recorded on the 21st day of November, suspecting a scribe's fault he deleted what he had written (for Castellanus falsely advised that there is no erasure), put Saint Basileus in their place, and by a slip of memory wrote *Ostia* for *Antioch*. However that may be, the Pratensis codex loses no authority from it, and it will be held in place of the autograph so long as there are those who prefer to draw from the source rather than to chase after the streams. Bouillart.] In the city of Ostia, of Saint Basileus.]

## The tenth day before the Kalends · 23 December

[0829]

At Nicomedia, of twenty holy martyrs, whom the Diocletianic persecution, tortured with most grievous torments, made martyrs to Christ, and joined them, blessed, to the number of the saints in the heavens. At Rome, the birthday of Saint Victoria, virgin, under the persecution of the emperor Decius, who, when she was betrothed to a certain pagan, and would neither marry nor sacrifice, after many miracles done, among which she had gathered very many virgins, was struck with the sword in the heart by the executioner, at the request of her betrothed. Also at Rome, of Saint Servulus, of whom blessed Gregory writes that from his earliest age to the end of his life he lay paralysed; at whose tomb miracles very frequently come to pass. At Paris, the dedication of the Basilica in honour of the holy cross and of Saint Vincent, martyr, and the deposition of the lord Childebert, the king.

## The ninth day before the Kalends [nt: (Here, in Bouillart's edition, the year begins.)] · 24 December

[0833]

The vigil [nt: All the manuscripts of the Prés, one excepted, begin the year here according to the ancient custom in use in the age of our Usuardus, and countless others follow them. Sollerius's Martyrology, however, takes its beginning from the Circumcision of the Lord, that is, from the Kalends of January. Bouillart.] of the birthday of the Lord. At Antioch of Syria, the birthday of forty holy virgins, who under the Decian persecution consummated martyrdom through various torments. At Spoleto, a city of Tuscia, of Saint Gregory, martyr and presbyter, in the times of the emperors Diocletian and Maximian, who, first beaten with knotted cudgels, and then, after the gridiron and prison, struck on the knees with iron cards, but also set on fire along his sides with burning lamps, was at length beheaded. In the city of Tripoli, of Saint Lucian.

## The eighth day before the Kalends · 25 December

[0835]

JESUS CHRIST THE SON OF GOD IS BORN in BETHLEHEM OF JUDAH in the forty-second year of Caesar Augustus [*Bouillart inserts:* [nt: It does not please Sollerius that the Usuardine Pratensis codex, with some others, reads between *in the year of Caesar Augustus* XLII and *of the olympiad* CXCIII, *in the week, according to the prophecy of Daniel*, LXVI. He does not think these words belong to the text, because they are not found at all in five codices which in this place he judges pure. But these codices are later in age than the Pratensis. Which is to be put before the other, the source before the stream, or the stream before the source? Why the other codices do not have them, I do not know. For there is no erasure here in the Pratensis. I suspect that they were first written by Usuardus; but that when he dictated the Martyrology to be offered to the king, he was alarmed by the variety of opinions on this matter and feared to hand down anything uncertain. Bouillart.] in the sixty-sixth week, according to the prophecy of Daniel.], of the one hundred and ninety-third olympiad. On the same day, the birthday of Saint Anastasia under the emperor Diocletian, who, for the confession of the Lord worn out by long custody, then carried with two hundred men and seven hundred women to the Palmarian islands, she herself indeed by fire, but the rest by various killings, celebrated martyrdom. At Rome, in the cemetery of Apronianus, the passion of blessed Eugenia, virgin, who in the time of the emperor Gallienus, after the marks of virtues, after the sacred choirs of virgins, long contending for Christ, at the last had her throat cut with the sword.

## The seventh day before the Kalends · 26 December

[0839]

At Jerusalem, the birthday of Saint Stephen the protomartyr, who was stoned by the Jews not long after the passion of Christ, namely in the same year as his Ascension. The venerable body of this man was found by divine revelation in the time of the emperor Honorius. At Rome, on the Appian Way, the deposition of Saint Dionisius, pope, who shone out illustrious for the teachings of the faith under the emperor Claudius. On the same day, of Saint Marinus, a most illustrious man, who obtained the triumph of martyrdom at Rome under the emperor Marcian.

## The sixth day before the Kalends · 27 December

[0843]

At Ephesus, the birthday of blessed John, apostle and evangelist, who, after the banishment of exile, after the revelation of the divine Apocalypse, after the writing of the Gospel, persevering until the times of the emperor Trajan, founded and ruled the churches of all Asia. And, worn out with old age, in the sixty-eighth year after the passion of the Lord, but of his own age the ninety-ninth, he died, and was buried near the same city. At Alexandria, of Saint Maximus, bishop: who was made quite illustrious and distinguished by the title of confession.

## The fifth day before the Kalends · 28 December

[0847]

At Bethlehem, the birthday of the holy Innocents, who were slain by King Herod. At Ancyra of Galatia, of Saints Euticus, presbyter, and Domitianus, deacon.

## The fourth day before the Kalends · 29 December

[0849]

At Jerusalem, of David the king. At Arles, the birthday of Saint Trophimus, whom Paul mentions, writing to Timothy, who, ordained bishop by the same Apostle, was sent as the first to the aforesaid city to preach the Gospel of Christ. From whose fountain, as Pope Zosimus writes, all Gaul received the streams of faith. In the district *Oximensis*, of Saint Ebrulfus, confessor. [*Bouillart adds:* [nt: Here Sollerius is surely to be wondered at, who, rejecting all the manuscript codices, even the ancient ones, clings to Molanus's edition alone. As if the Pratensis codex, by his own admission, if not the original, at least the most ancient, were not of greater authority. Nor does it matter that Saint Melania was added at the end of the article, since she is read in some codices, both those cited by him and in the Pratensis codices B, D, E and the Montisburg. Besides, this addition was written not by a strange hand but by the principal one; for what cause Usuardus commemorated Melania on this day is not known. Her feast day is commonly kept on the 31st. As regards Saint Thomas of Canterbury, whom the celebrated Castellanus, in that famous letter so often praised by Sollerius, asserts was written in a Gothic hand and added towards the end, that is wholly foreign to the truth. Of Saint Thomas there is not so much as a word in the Pratensis codex. Now from this let the reader who is zealous for the truth see how far Castellanus is to be believed in the rest. Bouillart.] Also at Jerusalem, of Saint Melania, consecrated to God.]

## The third day before the Kalends · 30 December

[0853]

At Spoleto, the birthday of blessed Sabinus, bishop, of Exuperantius and Marcellus, deacons, and of Venustianus with his wife and sons, under the emperor Maximian. Of whom Marcellus and Exuperantius, first hung upon the rack, then heavily wasted with cudgels, afterwards scraped with hooks, and roasted by the burning of their sides, completed their martyrdom. Venustianus, however, not long after, together with his wife and sons, was slain with the sword. But Saint Sabinus, after the cutting off of his hands and a long binding in prison, was beaten even to death. Their martyrdom, therefore, although it took place at different times, is nevertheless kept on one day. At Alexandria, of Saint Mansuetus, martyr, with ten others.

## The day before the Kalends · 31 December

[0857]

At Rome, the birthday of Saint Silvester, pope, whose illustrious acts are extant. At Senones, of blessed Sabinianus, bishop, and Potentianus, who, sent by the Roman pontiff to preach, made that same metropolis illustrious by the confession of their martyrdom. [nt: Sollerius contends that the Pratensis codex, with the Antuerp.-Maj. and the Rosweyd., goes too far, in that after *was cut down* they add *by order of the emperor Aurelian*. Who does not see that these words were written by the author himself, so that the time of Saint Columba's martyrdom might be shown more exactly? These words, *by order of the emperor Aurelian*, are indeed in an erased place, but written in the first hand. The same reading is had by the codices of the Prés B and D, the Colbert. B, the Victorin. B, the Conchensis, the Pontisar., the Sandionys. and the Montisburgensis. It is to be noted that in this same article these words, *who by the Roman pontiff to*, etc., lie on an erasure, of which there is no trace in the Dervensis codex, and which therefore had been made before the Martyrology was offered to Charles. There are therefore two erasures of different times in this article. I ask the learned to come and inspect whether the script is different. And at length the feasts close December, the last.

*TO THE GREATER GLORY OF GOD.*
