menda libraria fere reducuntur, ut Egypto, Schytiam, Hierosolimorum, Policarpus, Autisiodoro, etc.
Quae quia aetatem suam redolent, intacta pleraque in textu relicta sunt.* That is the edition's
policy, and it governs every codex spelling in this range: Pattern 9, no marker. *Taphnas* is an
accusative plural → Taphnae; *Schytia* is kept; Jerusalem is conventional.

**C4 · 0013 *Neopolis*.** Genitive of a nominative that cannot be recovered with confidence (Solier
mentions the *Neapolis* variant). The printed form is kept.

**C5 · 0015 *post vincula, carceres, equuleum, ungula et ignes*.** Plate n12 prints *ungula* in a
list of accusatives (the expected form is *ungulam*/*ungulas*). English "the claw" carries no case,
so there is nothing to render or to mark. This is a case slip with no English exponent, of the same
kind as Pattern 9's gender slips. Crux only.

**C6 · 0019 *Item in metalla Fanensi*.** A neuter plural *metalla* with the singular ablative
adjective *Fanensi*. Plate n14 confirms it, and Solier (VARIANTES) confirms the codices: *In metalla
Fanensi (non Fatensi, ut habet Antverp.-Maj.) legunt constanter codices.* Rendered "Also in the
mines *Fanensis*". The number of *metalla* is carried by "mines".

**C7 · 0023 *Hirenei, Peregrini et Hirenis*; *Niceti*.** *Hirenis* is read as the genitive of
Hirene (Irene), a woman among two men; a near-certain nominative. *Niceti* could be Nicetus or a
contracted Nicetius, so the printed form is kept.

**C8 · 0035 *Castro Vindicino, depositio beati confessoris.*** Plate n22: *beati* at a line end,
*confessoris* at the start of the next, and no name. The entry commemorates a confessor with no
name, unless *beati* IS the name (the confessor Beatus of Vendôme is kept on 9 May), in which case
the reading is "of Beatus, confessor". Rendered literally, "the deposition of the blessed
confessor". The English asserts nothing false, only less, so no `[cj:]` (Pattern 18's bar is a
false assertion). ⚑ If a later reader finds Solier's Observatio on this entry (n22 or n23; I did
not read the commentary), it may settle it.

**C9 · 0035 *Hermen*; *Nazanzi oppidum*; *ut Hieronymus … perhibet*.** Hermes: see Names. *Apud
Nazanzi oppidum* is "at the town of Nazanzus" (the genitive of the town's name, printed so).
The Gregory sentence is heavily parenthetical (*qui post multa documenta, in loco suo, ut
Hieronymus, ab eodem in divinis libris instructus, perhibet, episcopum ordinans, ruri vitam monachi
exercuit*). It is rendered in the printed order, with *in loco suo* taken with *episcopum
ordinans*, "ordaining a bishop in his own place".

**C10 · 0043 *sancti Mamerti episcopi et confessori*.** Plate n26 prints *confessori*, a dative, in
a genitive phrase. It is single-witness, a single letter, and has no English exponent. Rendered
"bishop and confessor" and logged as **single-witness**, per brief §9 (no second copy was
consulted for this site). Not a `[sic:]`.

**C11 · 0049 *in Amfibuli urbe*.** "in the city of Amfibuli": the printed genitive kept.

**C12 · 0055 the seven sees.** *Torquatus Acci, Tesifon Vergii, Secundus Abulae, Indalecius Urci,
Cecilius Heliberri, Esicius Carcesi, Eufrasius Eliturgi quieverunt*: locatives or indeclinables.
Nominatives given: Acci, Vergium, Abula, Urci, Heliberri, **Carcesi** (printed form kept; Carcesa?
Carcesum?), Eliturgi. *Lamosacum, passio* is an accusative used bare as a locative heading (Solier:
*Lamosacum, vel Lamosaco, vel Lampsaci legunt codices omnes*). The printed form is kept: "At
Lamosacum". Plate n32: after *quieverunt*, no point is visible before *Apud* on this very worn
page. The TEI's stop is kept (see the patches file).

**C13 · 0061 *III Kal. Maias*.** An in-entry Roman date with the month printed: "on the third day
before the Kalends of May", per brief §4. No modern date added.

**C14 · 0081 day head and *tetrachae*.** The archive copy (n45) prints *IX Kal. … Die 14.*; Gallica
f41 prints *Die 24.* cleanly. Our twin has *Die 24.*, which is the only number that fits the
sequence, so the English reads 24 May and no M1 disagreement arises. *Herodis tetrachae* is printed
so on both copies (for *tetrarchae*; Vulg. Act 13:1 *Tetrarchae*). It is a codex spelling of the
kind Solier keeps (C3): rendered "Herod the tetrarch" under Pattern 9, no marker.

**C15 · 0085 *Passecratis. Valentionis,*.** Our twin and Gallica f43 print a full stop inside the
list of names. The archive n47 prints a comma. Both are clean: a press variant. The English keeps
the twin's full stop (Pattern 8), which leaves a sentence fragment "Valentio, and two others
crowned together." That is the printed setting of one of the two printings, not our error.

**C16 · 0093 *civiate*.** The plate prints *civitate* (P1). The English renders the plate: "in the
city *Dorostorensis*".

**C17 · 0099 *ac ignis penuriam passi*.** Rendered literally, "the want of fire": an odd torment
after the frying-pan and the rack (Solier, VARIANTES: *Neutrum valet* of the alternatives
*injuriam* and *poenam*, *constanter legunt penuriam, ut nos in textu servandum putavimus*).
Bouillart's keyed note (above) records that the whole clause *Postea vero … ligneo* stands **in an
erasure** in his codex. This is Migne's text, plate-confirmed at n54. No marker.

**C18 · 0109 Bouillart's first bracket.** It opens *[ Addit Bouill.:* and is **never closed** on
the plate (n59, read to the end of the band). It is carried unclosed in the English. The altar
list prints *Macharii Dionysii* with no comma (Gallica f55 likewise); kept, so the English reads
"of Macharius Dionysius". *ad caput vero*: archive *ab*, Gallica and TEI *ad* (see the patches
file).
- **C18a · 0139 *Madalis*.** Printed so; the nominative (Mandal? Madalis?) is kept as printed.

**C19 · 0113 *Zachariae presbyteri, sancti diaconi, Epagati*.** In a list of the martyrs of Lyon,
*sancti diaconi* is the deacon **Sanctus** (Eusebius, *HE* V.1, which this very entry cites as *in
historia ecclesiastica*). The lower-case initial is Migne's on both copies. Rendered "Sanctus,
deacon", so that the list keeps its martyr. "The holy deacon" would silently drop a name from a
roll of names. This construes the printed word as a name, which in the list's grammar it is; it
does not emend it.
- **0113 *mu tos*** → "many" (P2). **0113 *Hi omnes*** → "All these". The archive has *Illi*
  (press variant). The English is the same either way.

**C20 · 0119 *praecipatus*; *ne ejus terrentur exemplo*.** *praecipatus* → "cast headlong" (P3,
press variant). *terrentur* is printed on both copies (an indicative after *ne*, where
*terrerentur* is wanted; the archive divides it *ter-|rentur*, and it is one word). English "lest
they be terrified" has no mood to carry the slip. Mood anomalies are the class Pattern 18a
declines, so crux only.

**C21 · 0145 *quem Anolinus … diu maceratum et afflictum in carcere, cum quo sanctissimum puerum
Celsum … gladio feriri jussit*.** An anacoluthon: *quem* is never governed, and *cum quo* takes the
accusative *Celsum*. Rendered literally, keeping the break. The same goes for *Horum corpora …
beatus Ambrosius … reperit*, which follows the printed order.

**C22 · 0155 *peregerunt [ Interserit Bouillart: … martyris.] Apud Mesiam*.** No full stop after
*peregerunt*: the bracket's own stop closes the sentence. The English carries it as printed
(Pattern 8).

**C23 · 0161 *[ Apud Bouillart, [0163] pro, Eod. die, . . . . . viri: …]*.** The column anchor
[0163] stands inside the bracket and stays in place. The leader dots are carried dot for dot (E7).
The quoted incipit–explicit of the replaced entry stays in italic Latin, since it is a locator:
"[In Bouillart, [0163] instead of *Eod. die, . . . . . viri*: …]".

**C24 · 0167 *acutos [clavos] acceperunt*.** Migne's own bracketed supply. It is carried in square
brackets as "sharp [nails]" and is **not** one of ours.

**C25 · 0169 *et ita incorrupta eorum corpora, ac si eodem die fuissent interempti*.** There is no
finite verb. Rendered as printed, "and their bodies so incorrupt, as if…": nothing is supplied.

**C26 · 0127 *juxta quem tres virgines filiae ipsius prophetissae*.** Usuardus prints **three**
daughters, where Act 21:9 has *filiae quatuor virgines prophetantes*. The English says three, as
printed. This is Usuardus's own content, not a quoted verse, so no `[var:]` (Pattern 14 is for a
verse quoted as a quotation).

**C27 · 0181 *Niceae, Romatianae civitatis episcopi*.** Printed genitive kept (Niceas? Nicea?).

**C28 · 0189 *percussus gladio in [0191] corpore, Christi martyrem fecit*.** Literally "struck with
the sword in the body, made a martyr of Christ". Presumably *se* or *eum* is understood (he made
himself Christ's martyr), but nothing is supplied, and the anchor stays mid-phrase where the plate
breaks the column.

**C29 · 0209 *Romae, natalis [ Bouillart. om. natalis] beatissimae Lucinae*.** The bracket sits
right after the word it omits, so the English places "[Bouillart omits *natalis*]" right after
"the birthday".

**C30 · 0213 *qui primis illic heremiticam vitam exercuit*.** Plate n111 prints *primis*, a real
form (dative/ablative plural) with no slot here: *primus* ("who first there practised") is the
likely sense. The English renders the printed plural as "among the first", which is literal and
does not assert anything the author denies, so it is below Pattern 18's bar. Crux only.
**0213 *sancti Salvi*.** The printed genitive is kept (see Names). Bouillart's note quotes the
autograph as *Salvii*.

**C31 · 0203 *ut scribit Hieronymus apostolorum temporum vicinum*.** *vicinum* agrees with *quem*
(Irenaeus): rendered "near to the times of the apostles", attached to Irenaeus, not to Jerome.

## Scripture checked (7a″)

No verse is quoted AS a quotation anywhere in 0009–0213. Allusions were phrase-searched in
`sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` so that the names and details would be checked:
- 0035 Rom 16:14 *Hermen* ✓ (the Vulgate prints the same form).
- 0081 Act 13:1 *Manahen, qui erat Herodis Tetrarchae collactaneus* ✓ allusion. Usuardus's
  *tetrachae* is his codex spelling (C14).
- 0081 Lc 8:3 *Joanna uxor Chusae procuratoris Herodis*: paraphrase, with *Chuzae* for *Chusae*.
  Not a quotation, so no `[var:]`.
- 0127 Act 21:8–9 (Philip, his daughters): paraphrase that diverges in number (three for four,
  C26). Not a quotation.
- 0213 *collectus est ad patres in senectute bona*: echoes Gn 15:15 / 25:8 (*in senectute bona*).
  An epithet, not a quotation.
- 0027 Acts (*quem in Actibus apostolorum beatus Lucas commemorat*, Lucius of Cyrene, Act 13:1) ✓
  as a reference.
- 0109 Jerome's *quae tanto, inquit, amplector …* is Jerome (*De vir. ill.* 75), not scripture.
  Rendered as printed, with no quotation marks, since Migne prints none.

## Rejected candidates (and why)

- **`[sic:]` on *mu tos* (0113), *praecipatus* (0119), *santae* (0183).** All three are clean
  non-words in our twin and on the Gallica copy. **The archive copy prints the sound word at all
  three sites**, so a `[sic:]` would accuse a plate that another printing of the same page
  contradicts. The English renders the sound reading, and the patches are filed for the
  orchestrator.
- **`[sic:]` on *civiate* (0093).** The plate prints *civitate*: it is our twin's defect, not
  Migne's (P1).
- **`[sic:]` on *confessori* (0043).** Single-witness, a single letter (brief §9).
- **`[sic:]` on *tetrachae* (0081), *Schytiam* (0009), *Cresi* (0109) and the other codex
  spellings.** Solier leaves them in the text on purpose (C3), so Pattern 9 applies.
- **`[cj:]` on *depositio beati confessoris* (0035), *primis* (0213) and *ignis penuriam* (0099).**
  The faithful English is odd, or it omits, but it asserts nothing false (Pattern 18's bar).
- **A `[d:]` on the doubled John-and-Paul entry (0193).** The second copy is Bouillart's own
  bracketed version of the entry (*Apud Bouillart:*), printed as his witness. It is not a
  dittography.
- **An M1 day-number crux at 0081.** The disagreement is in the archive copy only. Our twin and
  Gallica agree with the sequence (C14).
- **Every OCR-candidate "difference"** except the two real ones (*Hi*/*illi* at 0113, a press
  variant, and *santae*/*sancte* at 0183, which led to P4). The rest were archive OCR noise or
  misalignment: *dam*, *dio*, *christianas*, *sardinia*, *qua*, *finem*, *eoram*, *secundas*,
  *corduba*, *nomino*, *pii*, *collectas*. Verdicts are in the plate-reads JSON.
