Stint D, 2026-09-21. Cols 0219–0435: 2 July to 4 September. Plate witnesses and every page opened
are in `data/briefs/9215-PLATE-READS-0004.json`; the Latin's catch-up is in

**Markers fired in this range: none.** No `[sic:]`, `[var:]`, `[cj:]`, `[ed:]` or `[d:]`. Every
non-word our twin carries in 0004–0007 turned out, at the plate, to be either a transcription
error (both printings sound) or a press variant (Gallica defective, archive sound). In both cases
the English follows the sound reading and a patch is filed. Nothing was left to accuse Migne of.

## Text cruces (rendered as printed; no marker)

- **0219** *religiosae*: "a religious", i.e. a woman under vows. Bouillart's variant *Deo sacratae*
  is kept Latin as a reading (M2), and his keyed note (below) discusses it.
- **0229** *sectus in duas partes occubuit*: "cut into two parts, fell" (Isaias sawn in two; cf.
  Heb 11:37 *secti sunt*, not quoted).
- **0239** *ad Guttam jugiter manantem*: rendered "at the Gutta that flows continually". *Gutta*
  ("the Drop") is kept as a place-name.
- **0243** *Valeriani et Galiani*: the emperor is Gallienus; the plate spelling *Galiani* (not
  *Galieni*, as at 0313, 0383, 0393) is a spelling of the name, not a defect, and the emperor takes
  his conventional English form (Pattern 9; stint C's *Galieno* → Gallienus).
- **0261** *Omousii oppositione*: "by the opposition of the *Omousion*": the Nicene *homoousion*,
  kept italic Latin in the form printed.
- **0263** *sub Constantino principe*: placed, as printed, after "bishop and confessor"; the English
  keeps the order, so the phrase dates the bishop rather than the exile.
- **0291 / 0295** Two cities printed *Tyro*: Tyre (0239, *In civitate Tyro*) and *In Italia civitate
  Tyro* (0291; Christina's Tyrus, i.e. Bolsena). Both rendered "the city of Tyre", with "In Italy"
  kept to distinguish them, exactly as the Latin does.
- **0313** *Abdon et Sennes subregulorum*: *Sennes* printed (the saint is usually Sennen); kept.
  The sentence's subject changes mid-way (*cum … paterentur a Decio, ad ultimum duxit eos*): the
  English keeps the anacoluthon ("…at the last he brought them to Rome").
- **0319** *Spes, Fides et Charitas … Sapientia*: kept as Latin names (Hope, Faith, Charity,
  Wisdom). Translating them would make names into virtues.
- **0319** *ad sanctum Petrum ad vincula*: "at Saint Peter at the Chains". No *natalis* or *festum*
  printed; none supplied.
- **0339** *ad missam morando restauravit*: both printings read *morando*, "by lingering/tarrying".
  Gregory's *Dialogues* (I.7) has Donatus restore the chalice *orando*, "by praying"; the dropped
  or intruded *m* would be Migne's (or Usuardus's), not ours, and *morando* is a real word, so the
  English renders it literally: "restored, by lingering at mass, the holy chalice". A first draft
  rendered "by praying": that was a silent repair and was withdrawn before verification. A `[cj:]`
  was weighed and not fired: the faithful English is odd but asserts nothing false about the
  miracle, and Pattern 18's census is closed. Solier's VARIANTES on n174 do not discuss the word.
- **0339** *tempore Aurelii Commodi martyrizati*: no terminal stop on either printing; none supplied.
- **0357** *sic per cardetum et tribulos tractus*: "dragged thus through thistles and thorns".
  Solier's variants on n184 record *carduos*, *Carduetum* in other witnesses; our text is the plate's.
- **0365** *pro conditione carnis, eam migrasse non dubitet*: rendered with both its negations (*non
  invenitur … non dubitet*) and the *nescire* of the next sentence.
- **0373** *gladio ministrorum coronandus percutitur*: "he is struck with the sword of the
  attendants, to be crowned", the gerundive kept.
- **0377** *cum sociis suis duobus milibus quingentis nonaginta et septem*: "two thousand five
  hundred and ninety-seven". Numbers in range, each rendered in words exactly as the twin prints
  it (and read at the plate on the pages opened): thirty (0225), ten (0221), ten thousand two hundred and three (0239), four days (0247),
  about five hundred (0253), ten children (0261), ten (0277), eighty-three (0291), four (0301),
  fifty-two years (0309), seven and three sons (0319), eighteen (0339), twenty (0343), one
  hundred and sixty-five (0349), third milestone (0351), twenty (0355), nineteen (0357), thirty-third
  milestone, fifteen years (0373), twelve (0389), three hundred (0393), eight sons (0389), a thousand
  times (0397), twenty years (0431), third day (0435).
- **0379** *Augustus Archadius*: the archive spaces it *August us*; Arcadius, conventional.
- **0379** *Herio insula*: "On the island Herio" (Noirmoutier); nominative uncertain (Herium? Herius?),
  printed form kept.
- **0397** Genesius's words *Non est rex praeter Christum … auferre non poteritis* are his confession,
  not a scripture quotation (the nearest verse, Jn 19:15 *Non habemus regem nisi Caesarem*, is not
  quoted). Both negations carried.
- **0411** *quae tamen hic festiva recolitur*: "it is nevertheless kept here as a feast", the relative
  referring to the beheading.
- **0417** The anchor `[0417]` falls between *ignorantes* and *Christiani*; the English places it
  between "Not knowing his name," and "the Christians", so it stays in the same clause.
- **0423** *Jesu Nave et Gedeon prophetarum*: "Of Jesus Nave and Gedeon the prophets". *Jesus Nave* is
  the Greek-derived name of Joshua son of Nun; kept as printed, like Hieremias.
- **0431** *Hujus dies passionis quarto Kalendas Augusti*: rendered "the fourth day before the
  Kalends of August", month kept since it is printed (brief §4); no modern date added.
