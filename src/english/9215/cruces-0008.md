## §0 · Decisions

- Adopted wholesale: stint A's §0 and stint C's §0, `## Names` and `## Formulas`. *natalis* is
  "the birthday of", with no gloss (A owns the crux). Day heads follow M1 in the plain form of
  0000–0003.
- **Month heads:** *MENSIS OCTOBER HABET DIES XXXI.* → `OCTOBER HAS 31 DAYS.`;
  *MENSIS NOVEMBER HABET DIES XXXI.* → `NOVEMBER HAS 31 DAYS.` (see E1). The twin has both month
  heads followed by a doubled `[0525]` / `[0641]` anchor (one under the month head, one under the
  Kalends head). Both are kept where they stand.
- **Day heads:** every `Die n` agrees with its Roman date. *II Idus* (0573) → "The day before the
  Ides", per M1 (never "the second day"). The typographic variants *VII. Idus, Die 9.* (0555; the
  archive prints *VII Idus.*, Gallica *VII Idus,*), *VI Kal Die 27.*, *III Non Die 3.* and *Die 29*
  / *Die 17* without a stop normalize in the English head (Pattern 9, section labels).
- **Chunk 0010, 13 October (III Id.):** the twin carries no column anchor between [0567] and
  [0573]. That is Migne's layout, not a gap (brief §3), so nothing was added or reported.

## Cruces

**E1 · 0641 *MENSIS NOVEMBER HABET DIES XXXI.*** November has thirty days. The plate (n325) sets the
head in two display lines, *MENSIS NOVEMBER.* / *HABET DIES XXXI.*, so the XXXI is Migne's, not
the digitization's. The English follows the printed number, "NOVEMBER HAS 31 DAYS." (brief §4:
numbers exactly as printed). No `[cj:]`: the English says exactly what the Latin says, and the
reader can see from the Kalends of December (next stint) that the day count runs to 30. The
orchestrator may want to weigh this as a Pattern 18 case, since the rendered head asserts
something false about the calendar. Pattern 18a's closed census is the reason I did not fire one.

**E2 · 0439 Bouillart's bracket *[ Apud Bouillart., in pago Tarvenensi, pro Eodem die]***. Rendered
"[In Bouillart, in the district *Tarvenensis*, instead of *Eodem die*]", after C's *pro* → "instead
of". The replaced words stay italic Latin as a locator. His keyed note (above) explains the
erasure in the Carolingian autograph.

**E3 · 0441 *Item Cappadociae urbe Reaten, sancti Cottidi diaconi.*** *Reaten* is indeclinable as
printed, and *Cottidi* has an uncertain nominative. Both printed forms are kept (Names).
Plate not read (not a candidate site).

**E4 · 0503 *sub praeside Rictionaro*.** Plate n256 prints *Rictionaro*. A and C carry the same
persecutor elsewhere as *Rictiovarus*, from a printed *Rictiovaro*, and Solier's AUCTARIA on n315
print *Rictiovaro* too. This band prints *n*, so the English says **Rictionarus**, and I made no
normalization and no patch (the twin matches the plate). Single copy read. The *n*/*v* difference
could be a turned sort, so it is logged, not marked.

**E5 · 0505 *[ Addit Bouillart.: Item sancti Sollempnensis episcopi.]*** The printed form is kept
(Names).

**E6 · 0515 *qui beatus vir, quantum sibi extiterit parcus … sanctus Hieronymus memorabili
prosecutus est relatu*.** The relative *qui* is never construed (anacoluthon). Rendered literally,
keeping the break: "who, the blessed man, how sparing …, Saint Jerome has set out".

**E7 · 0533 *Romae ad Ursum pilleatum*.** A Roman place-name ("at the capped Bear"). Kept italic,
in the nominative, "at *Ursus pilleatus*".

**E8 · 0541 *In Eumenia, beati Trasei episcopi*.** *Trasei* is the genitive of a second-declension
*Traseus* (the Thraseas of Eusebius). Rendered Traseus. Not normalized.

**E9 · 0547 stop after *consummavit*.** The plate has it (P3). Carried.

**E10 · 0555 *gratia-directus*.** The hyphen is not on either printing (P4).

**E11 · 0557 *trecentis decem et Octo*.** The capital *O* is printed so (n283). It has no English
exponent: "three hundred and eighteen".

**E12 · 0575 *Romae via Aurelia, sancti Fortunati Lugduni, sancti Antiochi episcopi*.** The plate
(n292) prints no stop between the Roman entry and the Lyon entry, exactly as the twin does. The
English supplies none (Pattern 8), so it reads "of Saint Fortunatus at Lyon, of Saint Antiochus,
bishop", which attaches Lyon to Fortunatus as the printed punctuation does. It is presumably two
commemorations (Fortunatus at Rome; Antiochus at Lyon). That is a crux, not a repair.

**E13 · 0579 *In Africa sanctorum martyrum ducentorum septuaginta*, 0593 *In Antiochia Syriae
sanctorum martyrum*.** No comma after the place in either. The English supplies none.

**E14 · 0609 *iujuriam* → "injury"** (P5, press variant). *Ex quibus Germanus Emeritae Servandus vero
Hispali conditus est*: no comma on either printing. Carried as "Of whom Germanus at Emerita
Servandus however at Seville was buried."

**E15 · 0621 *in epistola ad confessores, Civitate Narbona*.** A press variant. The twin and Gallica
f311 print a comma, and the archive n315 a full stop. Both are clean, so this is crux only. The
English keeps the twin's comma and the capital: "…in the letter to the confessors, In the city of
Narbonne".

**E16 · 0629 *multitudinem. Christo*.** The stop is on neither printing (P6). The English construes
the *cum*-clause whole.

**E17 · 0637 *sub octavo Kalendarum Septembrium die*.** An in-entry Roman date with the month
printed: "on the eighth day before the Kalends of September" (brief §4). No modern date added.

**E18 · 0677 *ab imperatore Constantino haeretico*; *Cersonam Liciae provinciae*.** Historically,
Martin I was seized under Constans II and exiled to Cherson in the Crimea. The text prints
*Constantino* and *Liciae*, and the English renders both as printed. These are the author's
errors, not type (Pattern 7), so no `[cj:]`: the English says what the Latin says.

**E19 · 0685A *Emiliam* → "Emilianus"** (P7, press/wear variant).

**E20 · 0689 *interempti sunt [ Bouillart., ad oram paginae: Parisius, S. Gendulfi episcopi.]. Turonis*.**
Bouillart's bracket stands before the sentence's full stop, which follows the closing bracket. The
English keeps that order. The final bracket, *[ Bouillart., S. Eugenii antistitis. Malbodio …]*,
gives his reading of the Toledo entry and then an added entry. It is translated as a clause:
"[Bouillart: of Saint Eugenius, prelate. At the monastery Malbodium, …]".

**E21 · 0669 *statutum est ut anniversaria dies ipsorum sub nominibus supradictorum quinque martyrum recoleretur*.**
The four crowned ones are kept under the names of the FIVE martyrs just above. That is the
printed number, and it is rendered so.

**E22 · 0613 *Felix quinquagesimo sexto annorum virgo obiit*.** "died a virgin in the fifty-sixth of
his years": the printed numeral, no conversion.

**E23 · 0441 *diuturnos carceris squalores*** (plural). "long-lasting filth of prison": the formula
from A, with the plural having no clean English exponent.
