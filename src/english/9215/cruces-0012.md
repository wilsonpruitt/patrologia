## §0 · Decisions

- Adopted wholesale: A's §0, C's §0, and the `## Names` and `## Formulas` of A, C, D and E.
  *natalis* is "the birthday of", never glossed (A owns the crux).
- **Month head** (0755): *MENSIS DECEMBER HABET DIES XXXI.* → `DECEMBER HAS 31 DAYS.` The archive
  sets it on two display lines, *MENSIS DECEMBER.* / *HABET DIES XXXI.*, with a stop the twin
  lacks; the English normalizes as C and D did for JULIUS./AUGUSTUS. (Pattern 9). Gallica f378 did
  not load (two timeouts), so the stop is single-witness and nothing rests on it.
- **Doubled anchor** `[0755]` under the month head and again under *Kalendis*: kept, as D and E
  kept theirs.
- **Day heads**: every `Die n` agrees with its Roman date in our twin (November: 32 − d before the
  December Kalends; December: Nones 5, Ides 13, 33 − d before the January Kalends). The archive's
  head at 0711 reads *XII Kal. … Die 19.*; Gallica and our twin read *XIII*: that printing's
  fault, and no M1 crux arises (as C's 0081). *XV Kal. Die 17* (0703) prints without a stop;
  normalized in the English head.
- **The head note at 0833** is rendered `[nt: (Here, in Bouillart's edition, the year begins.)]` in
  the same place in the head (between the Roman date and the modern one). It is Migne's own inline
  note, not one of Bouillart's keyed foot notes, and it says something, so Pattern 15 applies. It
  and Bouillart's keyed note (a) on the same entry (below) say the same thing from two sides.
- **The close of the work** (0857): *Et tandem claudunt extremum festa Decembrem.* is a plain line,
  translated "And at length the feasts close December, the last." It is Wandalbert's closing
  verse (Molan's auctarium on n434: *Repeto ultimum Wandalberti versiculum, Martyrologii totius
  conclusionem*), printed as one centred line, so it stays one unlineated line (Pattern 8a).
  *AD MAJOREM DEI GLORIAM.* (italic caps) → *TO THE GREATER GLORY OF GOD.*
- **The Christmas proclamation** (0835) keeps the plate's capitals, which are small capitals on
  the page: `JESUS CHRIST THE SON OF GOD IS BORN in BETHLEHEM OF JUDAH in the forty-second
  year …`. *Bethleem Judae* → "Bethlehem of Judah" after E.

## Text cruces (rendered as printed; no marker)

- **0693** *de superioribus eum praecipitarent*: "cast him headlong from the heights".
  *Theodoti, et Philomini*: the comma before *et* is carried.
- **0695** *apud pagum Parisiacensem*: "at the district of Paris" (the §8 *Apud* formula kept).
- **0699** *diu intra septa speluncae sponte clausus, Christo servivit deinde, apud praefatam
  urbem, revelante angelo, in pontificali cathedra solemniter collocatus est.* There is no
  conjunction between *servivit* and *collocatus est*, and the comma after *deinde* binds it to
  *servivit*. Plate-confirmed (n354). Rendered as printed: "served Christ afterwards, at the
  aforesaid city, an angel revealing it, was solemnly set in the pontifical chair." Nothing
  supplied.
- **0703** *Aurelianis, sancti Aniani episcopi et confessoris cujus mortem in conspectu Domini
  pretiosam*: no comma before *cujus*; none supplied.
- **0711** *Item sancti Fausti diaconi, hic grandaevus et plenus dierum confessor perdurans*: the
  sentence changes construction after the comma (genitive entry, then *hic* + a finite verb). Kept:
  "Also of Saint Faustus, deacon, this man, very aged and full of days, persevering as a
  confessor, … was consummated". *positus est ad sanctum Sixtum*: "was laid at Saint Sixtus"
  (the basilica is meant; nothing supplied).
- **0715** *ubique fustibus mactatus*: printed *ubique*, "everywhere", on Gallica and in the twin
  (the archive leaf is low-res here). *ibique* ("and there") is the expected sense after
  *Sardiniam deportatus*. Rendered literally, "slain with cudgels everywhere". The English is odd
  but asserts nothing the author denies, so it is below Pattern 18's bar. Crux only.
  **Cabilone** (Chalon): nominative uncertain (Cabilo? Cabillonum? cf. D's *Cabilonis*), so the
  printed form is kept: "At Cabilone". **Ampeli**: Ampelus, or a contracted Ampelius; printed form
  kept: "of Saints Ampeli and Gaius".
- **0725** *[ Interserit Bouillart.: In Italia, beati Columbani abbatis, multorum fundatoris
  coenobiorum]*: the archive shows a stray `[` before *fundatoris*; Gallica and our twin do not.
  Columbanus is announced on the 21st in the main text as well; Bouillart's keyed note discusses
  the doubling. Not a dittography (his bracket is a witness).
- **0733** *fere cum clericis et laicis, sexcenti sexaginta*: *fere* stands before the phrase;
  kept in place, "about, with clerics and laymen, six hundred and sixty". *capite obtruncatur*
  → "is cut off by the head" (C's literal formula for *capite truncatur*).
- **0741** *Novissime beatum Agricolam interemerunt*: the master is killed last, after the torments
  of Vitalis, as printed. *isdem* (= *idem*) → "the same".
- **0743** *Sostenes*: printed so (Vulg. 1 Cor 1:1 *Sosthenes*), used as a genitive. Kept as
  printed, like C's *Hermen* and D's *Feben*.
- **0751** *ab Egea proconsule*: the nominative (Egeas? Aegeas?) is uncertain; printed form kept,
  "by the proconsul Egea". *Scithiam* → Scithia (after E).
- **0761** *execrabile duxit Christianorum neci deservire cruentus*: *cruentus* is nominative,
  agreeing with Cassianus. Rendered "held it execrable to serve, bloodstained, the slaughter of
  Christians". *exceptoris … officium* → "the office of a recorder".
- **0771** *gravissimis in innumeris suppliciis excruciati*: plate-confirmed (n390). The *in* stands
  between the two adjectives, where *et* is expected. Rendered as printed: "tortured with most
  grievous, in innumerable, punishments".
  *illud memorabile fertur quod imperatorem Constantinum ab interitu quorumdam, longe constitutus,
  ad misericordiam et monitis per visum deflexit et minis*: rendered in the printed order.
- **0775** *qui cum esset militaris et prohiberet …, clamor repente totius vulgi adversus eum
  extollitur*: an anacoluthon (*qui* never gets a verb); kept. *capite pro pietate damnatus est*
  → "condemned to the loss of his head for piety".
- **0777** *qui et ipse … trecentos quadraginta duos martyres manu sua sepelivit*: the relative's
  antecedent is Calistus or the pope; kept ambiguous as printed.
- **0781** The anchor `[0783]` stands inside Bouillart's bracket (*dura [ Bouillart, [0783]
  dira]*) and stays there: "the harsh [*Bouillart,* [0783] *dira*] custody of prison".
- **0785** *crudelissime caesi deinde in carcerem*: no comma before *deinde*; none supplied.
- **0789** *tarincas*: a rare word (iron points, spits). Rendered "iron spikes". *per eorum corpora
  jaculari* has no object: "to shoot through their bodies", nothing supplied.
- **0793** *clarus miraculis coronatus quievit*: "illustrious for miracles, crowned, he rested".
  *non segniter*: negation carried ("not slothfully").
- **0797** *nullatenus … nec funibus additis, aut boum paribus plurimis*; *nil laesa*: every
  negation carried ("by no means", "nor", "harmed in nothing").
- **0803** *defensione sanctimonii*: *sanctimonium*, "holiness" (a real neuter by-form of
  *sanctimonia*); rendered "the defence of holiness". *nullus eum neque in domo neque in agro*:
  all three negations carried.
- **0807** **Naulis**: nominative uncertain (Naulis? Naulus?); printed form kept.
- **0813** *passio sancti Moysetis martyris, Turonis, sancti Gatiani*: a comma where a full stop is
  expected, on the archive and in our twin (Gallica not read here; both printings would need to
  differ for a patch, and the English carries it either way). Carried: "martyr, At Tours".
- **0825** *et contemneret; praeacuta sude*: the semicolon is carried.
- **0833** *cardis ferreis in genibus percussus*: "struck on the knees with iron cards" (*cardus*,
  a carding comb or teasel). Solier's VARIANTES on n421: *Quid per cardos intelligatur, docet Molani
  adnotatio.*
- **0835** *ebdomada* (Bouillart's bracket, both printings) and *hebdomada* (his keyed note): both
  printed; "week" either way.
- **0843** *sexagesimo octavo … nonagesimo nono*: "sixty-eighth", "ninety-ninth", as printed.
- **0857** *gladio caesa est [ Addit Bouillart.: jussu Aureliani principis.] Retiariae*: no stop
  before the bracket; the bracket's own stop closes the sentence (as C22).
