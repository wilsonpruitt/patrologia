## Instruction to Louis the Stammerer

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0983D]To the lord LOUIS, the glorious king, be health and life always.

## CHAP. I.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Your Lordship has commanded me to hasten to come to you, because you wished to treat with me of the interests of yourself, and of holy Church, and of the kingdom: [0984D] whereupon I render you satisfaction with a humble and sure reply. ⟦s2⟧ The holy Apostle says [n: (Rom. XV, 4)]: Whatsoever things were written, were written for our instruction. ⟦s3⟧ We read in the ancient histories that often, when kings were set up, discord arose among the chief men of the kingdom, [0985A] because some wished to claim for themselves the establishing of the king without the counsel of others. ⟦s4⟧ And that discord was not pacified without hindrance. ⟦s5⟧ We read that good kings, once established, took to themselves good counselors, and that through good kings and good counselors the peoples of the kingdoms had many good things, and through bad kings and bad counselors the peoples of the kingdoms endured many evils.

## CHAP. II.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ To set before you examples from more recent histories: when Pippin your great-great-grandfather fell sick with the sickness of which he also died, he called together at the monastery of Saint Denis the chief men of his kingdom, and by their counsel arranged how, after him, his sons Carloman and Charles, who were with him, should peaceably govern his kingdom. ⟦s2⟧ And when he was dead [0985B] and buried with great devotion, Carloman came to Soissons and Charles to Noyon, and on the fifteenth day after his death, by the counsel and help of the chief men of the kingdom, they were raised up as kings; and each one of the chief men of the kingdom came over to his own king without any contention. ⟦s3⟧ About three years later, when Carloman had died at Samoussy, Charles came to Corbeny, and the chief men of the kingdom who had been with Carloman came over to him there, except a few, of whom it is not now needful to speak.

## CHAP. III.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But when the emperor Charles had died and had been buried in the palace at Aachen, the chief men of the kingdom who were with him sent to Louis your grandfather, who was in Thionville; and on the thirtieth day after his father's death [0985C] he came to Aachen, and with the counsel of the chief men of the kingdom he peaceably set the kingdom in order — until, from a cause arising which it is not fitting to speak of now, discord arose between him and his sons, on account of which a great part of the chief men of the kingdom set out with Lothar into Lombardy.

## CHAP. IV.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ When Louis the emperor, your grandfather, had died, certain of the chief men of this kingdom summoned Lothar, and the chief men who were with him, out of Lombardy into this kingdom. ⟦s2⟧ And certain of the chief men of the kingdom were with Charles your father, and certain with Louis your uncle. ⟦s3⟧ Meanwhile the chief men of the kingdom who were with the three brothers began to contend one by one over honors — which of them might obtain the greater and the more of them: and holding of little [0985D] account the oaths made about the division of the kingdom, and contending more for their own greed than for the safety of their lords and of themselves, and for the peace of holy Church and of the people, those who were with Lothar drove him to this, that he should disinherit his brothers and reduce to nothing the chief men of the kingdom who were with them, since he himself was firstborn and bore the name of emperor. ⟦s4⟧ But those who were with Charles and Louis said that their lords were Lothar's brothers, and that by oaths the kingdom had been divided among them, and that they were inferior neither in birth nor in power to those who were with Lothar, and that therefore they would not entrust themselves against them. ⟦s5⟧ Whence many and very great evils grew up in the land, until at last among [0986A] kinsmen by blood and Christians there came about at Fontenoy so great an evil and so great a peril as has not happened among Christians from that time when Charles first fought with Ragamfred at Vinchy. ⟦s6⟧ But not by that slaughter which was made at Fontenoy did peace come about in the kingdom; rather that misery remained among the Christian people and among kinsmen by blood until, willing or unwilling, both the lords and the chief men of the kingdom divided the kingdom into three parts, and confirmed by oaths that this same division ought to stand firm.

## CHAP. V.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ When Lothar had died, his sons divided his kingdom among themselves. ⟦s2⟧ Whereupon, while they were still living, an assurance was made by oath between your father and your uncle Louis. ⟦s3⟧ And when Lothar [0986B] the son of Lothar had died, after many disputes a division was made between your father and your uncle Louis concerning the portion of Lothar's kingdom. ⟦s4⟧ And when Louis the brother of Lothar had died in Lombardy, a share of his kingdom was demanded of your father by his brother and by his brother's sons: whereupon, while Louis was still living, and also in the past year, such miseries came about as are known to you; and in this year there has come about such a lamentable unhappiness as will deservedly be a reproach through almost the whole world and through the times to come.

## CHAP. VI.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For that reason it seems to my littleness that it is your counsel to attend as much as you can to the ancient histories, lest at the beginning of your reign discord arise among the chief men of the kingdom concerning your rule, which could not be settled without hindrance: [0986C] and that the chief men of the kingdom who are with you should so temper themselves and their own wills that they do not, by their greed or negligence, provoke the other chief men of this kingdom to scandal. ⟦s2⟧ Especially since you have no brother among whom the people ought to divide itself — just as it was not divided when the emperor Charles died, but united itself with Louis his only son. ⟦s3⟧ For if those good barons, after the death of Pippin, dealt with the two brothers by counsel so sound that there was peace between the brother kings and among the chief men of the kingdom and the people, then much more easily — unless envy and greed interpose themselves — will they be able, with God's help, to come along with you to the will of God and to their own salvation, if they attend to how great a misery arose from the discord which came about among the three brothers after the death of Louis [0986D] the emperor.

## CHAP. VII.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ You know that your father first arranged at Reims, with the chief men of his kingdom, concerning your establishment after him in the rule of the kingdom: where, so far as I recall, very nearly all the chief men of the kingdom were present, except the venerable abbot Hugh and Bernard count of Auvergne: and all consented, according to your father's arrangement, to your royal establishment. ⟦s2⟧ And when most recently at Quierzy he made arrangement about it, and gave you the names written out of those by whose counsel and help you were to order the affairs of the kingdom (among whom Boso too was enrolled), all were present except Boso himself, and abbot Hugh, and Bernard count of Auvergne: and all [0987A] who were present consented to your royal establishment, and so did those who set out with your father. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore send with speed to the abbots Hugh and Gozlin, and to Boso, and Conrad, and Bernard, and likewise Bernard, the counts: and ask them to provide such a place as shall seem more convenient to them for you and for those who are in these parts, and also for those who are in those parts, to meet at, and to send you word that you should come there with the chief men who are in these parts. ⟦s4⟧ And let each so come together that the kingdom be not plundered nor laid waste, so that by common counsel you may treat of the common need and advantage — how those chapters which your father most recently proclaimed at Quierzy may be able to come into effect; which chapters in the meantime do you re-read, both you and those [0987B] who are with you, and lay them up in mind.
