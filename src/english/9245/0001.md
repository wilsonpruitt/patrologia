## CHAP. VIII.

I believe also that your father, after he knew that he could not recover from his sickness, did not fail to command you certain things in particular concerning the ordering of the kingdom — such things as he knew to be for the will of God and the standing of holy Church and your honor and that of the chief men of the kingdom, and for the necessity and advantage of the people. Therefore it is needful, and it seems good, that those also should be present who were with him, and that all in common should treat of the common necessity and advantage. I too, though the least of them, and weak and infirm, will gladly come together for the service of God and of you, and for their attendance, the Lord granting it; and if I shall be able to contribute anything of use, I shall willingly do so, because it is needful that you treat and make arrangement with the faithful men of God and your own. [0987C]

First, how you may be able, in the rule of the kingdom, to hold your ground with honor and safety, and with a sufficiency of the things that are necessary, together with your kingdom and your household. Second, that the aforesaid chapter lately proclaimed by your father at Quierzy, concerning the honor of holy Church and the due privilege of the priests and servants of God, may be brought into effect: and that the churches in this kingdom be not afflicted by opportunistic circuit-levies and by undue customary exactions, which did not exist in the time of Pippin, Charles, and Louis, and were imposed less than twenty years ago. Third, how the chief men of the kingdom may hold their ground toward you with due security and honor, and how the rest of the noble men in the kingdom may have security, lest by [0987D] various contrivances they be stripped of whatever goods they have been able to have: because after greed, the root of all evils, blazed up in this kingdom, so that no one, or almost no one, could acquire or keep an honor or any good thing without a price, or have security, peace and counsel and justice and judgment have not had the place in this kingdom that they needed to have. Fourth, that you may find, with the faithful men of God and your own, how these robberies and plunderings in this kingdom may cease, and how this wretched people — which for many years now is afflicted by various and continual plunderings, and by exactions for driving back the Northmen — may have some remedy, and how justice and judgment, which among us are as if dead, may come to life again, so that God may give back to us strength [0988A] against the pagans: because up to now, for many years past, defense has had no place in this kingdom, but ransom and tribute; and they have already emptied out not only poor men but also churches once rich. Fifth, that you take pains, as far as you can, that the concord which is according to God — of which your father lately made mention at Quierzy — should be had and flourish among the faithful men of God and your own; and that you make yourself such toward them that they may be able and may dare to give you true counsel. For, as I have heard from many, much of what was useful in this kingdom has been lost, for this reason: that the counselors did not dare to say what they knew to be good and useful, nor had they any occasion for saying it. For no man is so wise as not to need another's counsel, as it is written: *The wise man hearing [0988B] shall be wiser, and the man of understanding shall possess the helm,* and, *he who trusts in his own thoughts shall act impiously [n: (Prov. I, 5; XII, 2)]*. Sixth, that you may find, with the faithful men of God and your own, how you may have peace and friendship according to God with your cousins, the sons of your uncle, and may render mutual help to one another for the will of God, and for the honor of holy Church and your own, and for the common salvation of your faithful men.

## CHAP. IX.

For the rest, as to how these things may come into effect, and the other necessary things be found and attained, God is to be entreated, who promises to every devout man: *If any man lack wisdom, let him ask of God, and it shall be given him [n: (Jac. I, 5)]*. And likewise he says: *I will show thee, O man, what is good, and what God requires [0988C] of thee: that is, to do judgment and justice, and to walk solicitously with thy God [n: (Mich. VI, 8)]*. Justice is to be done, not for any earthly gain, but for this very reason, that it is justice, as it is written: *Justly shalt thou pursue that which is just [n: (Deut. XVI, 20)]*. Judgment is to be given for the correction of the wicked, and for the setting right of those who suffer wrong; not for the avenging of ill-will, nor for the oppression of those who have a just cause. For those who judge unjustly do not give judgment — that is, a thing spoken by right and lawfully — but prejudgment. Whence, as the Lord says, *those who judge unjustly shall be justly judged and condemned.* Every man must walk solicitously with his God, and a king above all, who in the age to come will be under punishments as great as the number of those over whom he was in this age, in [0988D] which he did not correct himself from wickedness, and did not do judgment and justice, and did not walk solicitously with his God. For just as a subject man walks solicitously with his lord, so as to do those things which please him according to God unto salvation, and according to the world unto honor and profit, and if in anything he has done what displeases his lord he hastens at once to amend it, that he may come back into his favor: so it is needful for you to walk solicitously with your lord, the King of kings — that for things past which you have done against his will you make satisfaction to him by confession and contrition of heart and by good works, and that henceforth you do not turn aside from his commands: and if through frailty you slip in anything, then, just as a man who slips with one [0989A] foot helps himself with the other foot or with his hand so as to be set upright again, so do you again strive, by devotion and good working, to come back into the favor of your lord the Lord God; because those who sin and do not repent the Lord long bears with, that they may be converted, but those not converted he condemns the more harshly. As indeed in your own lifetime you can hear and see, and concerning yourself, lest the like befall you, you ought to fear and beware. And show yourself such toward divine worship, and holy Church, and the priests and servants of God, and your other faithful men abiding in every rank, and toward the people committed to you by God, that they may love you, and that it may delight them to render due attendance, both in spiritual service and in temporal help, wherever it shall be needful for you; and let the Christian [0989B] religion and humility of heart delight you more than empty pomp and the pleasures of the flesh: since, with however great masses of silver and gold the flesh be surrounded, with whatever precious garments it be clothed, what is it other than flesh? that is, rottenness; and the son of man, a worm, and dust, and ashes. Let it not delight you so much to pursue wild beasts in hunting as to fight against the pleasures of the flesh and against the enemies of God and of his holy Church, both bodily and spiritual: because, as the Lord says through the Apostle: *The flesh lusteth against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh [n: (Gal. V, 17)]*. And: *Our wrestling is not against [0990A] flesh and blood, but against spiritual wickednesses [n: (Ephes. VI, 12)]*. Let not the riches of possessions, of gold and silver and gems, so delight you that you be reckoned among those who adore idols — since, as the Apostle says, *Avarice is the service of idols [n: (Coloss. III, 5)]* — as let faith, hope, and charity, justice, truth, and just judgment delight you, which lead the man who perseveres in them to the eternal kingdom and to the fellowship of the angels.

## CHAP. X.

What I would now say in words to your Lordship, if I were present in body, this I put forward in a letter: because concerning the general affairs of the Church and of the kingdom, without the general counsel and consent of the chief men of the kingdom, I do not know how to give particular counsel, and I am not able, nor do I presume, to determine consent. Therefore, [0990B] worn down as I now am by age and by sickness and weakness, there is no need for it to be laid upon me by your Lordship that I labor without cause before the faithful men of God and your own come together at your assembly; and I, in whatever way I shall be able, the Lord favoring it, as I said before, will come for the service of God and of you and for their attendance. But if — which God avert — in the meantime any cause of disturbance shall arise, and it shall please you to make it known to me, I will labor faithfully in your service, according to my knowledge and power, both with counsel and with help.
