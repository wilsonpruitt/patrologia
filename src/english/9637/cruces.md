# Cruces — 9637 (Auctor incertus, Ordo ad regem benedicendum)

**Governed by `translation-style.md` Pattern 7** and by the **liturgical register
rules L1–L8** (written on this work, the corpus's first liturgical ordo — Fable
mini-pilot, 2026-08-15). The English renders what the plate prints; conjecture lives
here and never in the running text, except where Pattern 18's `[cj:]` or a Pattern 12
`[sic:]` with its 7a⁗ sense-supply is expressly noted below. The codex is a 12th/13th-c.
Vienna manuscript (Vindobon. theol. 277) of an earlier coronation rite, and its Latin is
rough in the manuscript's own right — several defects below are the exemplar's, not
Migne's compositor's, and nothing distinguishes them from our side of the page.

## Standing decisions for this work

- **Name-slot sigla.** `N.` and `ill.` (col 1114D) are the codex's placeholders for the
  king's name — sigla, not prose (register rule L6 family). Both are kept exactly as
  printed, roman, untranslated. `ill.` is not an abbreviation we failed to expand; it is
  the name slot in a different notation.
- **Truncated formula cues** are rendered word-for-word and left truncated (L4): "Per."
  → "Through." · "Qui tecum." → "Who with thee." · "Quod ipse praestare." → "Which he
  himself to grant." · "Quod ipse." → "Which he himself." · "per omnia." → "through
  all." The full form of the *Quod ipse* close prints once, at 1114C (*Quod ipse
  praestare dignetur, qui tecum vivit*), and anchors the reader for every truncation of
  it. "*Te rog.*" (1112D) truncates mid-word and is carried as printed, untranslated.
- **Chant cues** are rendered exactly as far as the plate prints them and no further
  (L3): *Ecce mitto Angelum* → "Behold, I send an Angel." · *Israel si me audieris* →
  "Israel, if thou shalt hear me." · *Domine salvum fac regem* → "O Lord, save the
  king." · *Desiderium animae* → "The desire of the soul." (not "of his soul" — *eius*
  is not printed) · *Te Deum laudamus* → "Thee, God, we praise." No `[var:]` fires on
  any of them (L3: an antiphon is its own received text, not a scripture citation).
- **Attested medieval spellings translated normally per Pattern 9** (no italics, no
  carry-through): *secuntur*, *preferri*, *quatinus/quatenus*, *adiutorio/adjutorio*,
  *iocunditate*, *habundantiam*, *ammodo* (= *a modo*, "from now"), *letania*,
  *redemtus/redemtor*, *charitatem*, *Paraclyti*, *subiicere/subiectus*.

## Col 1111D (chunk 0000) — *phaphones*, unresolved non-word
- **Reading (as printed):** `caeteri autem phaphones sint casulis adornati`
- **Note:** `phaphones` is not a Latin word and no attested medieval form is close
  enough to claim. The sense slot wants a class of clergy or attendants (they are to be
  vested in chasubles) — *presbyteri* or *capellani* would fit the rite, but this is a
  guess, not a conjecture with evidence behind it. Carried as printed under Pattern 12:
  "let the rest, the [sic: phaphones], be adorned with chasubles." The marker sits
  inside an italic rubric, where a distinguishing italic is impossible (the 11066
  nested-italics precedent), so the `[sic:]` alone does the flagging.

## Col 1113A (chunk 0000) — *suorum* in the king's own mouth
- **Reading (as printed):** `in quantum divino fultus adiutorio ac solatio omnium
  suorum valuero`
- **Note:** The king speaks in the first person, but the formula says *suorum* ("of all
  his own"), not *meorum* — the promise was drafted in the third person and set into
  direct speech without adjustment. A real word; rendered as printed: "stayed by divine
  help and by the solace of all his own." Same class as 11064 @1154C (person slip,
  declined for `[cj:]` under the 2026-08-15 ruling's scope).

## Col 1113D (chunk 0000) — *in offenso* for *inoffenso*: split type carrying a negation
- **Reading (as printed):** `per tramitem justitiae in offenso gressu semper incedat`
- **Note:** As printed the two words are real Latin ("with stumbled step") and assert
  the opposite of what the prayer asks — that the king walk the path of justice with a
  *stumbling* step. The intended reading is the single word *inoffenso* ("unimpeded"),
  split by the compositor. **Split type is non-word type** (Pattern 10): the whole run
  is quarantined, and because the run contains the negating prefix *in-*, 7a⁗ requires
  the sense in plain words beside the marker: "with unimpeded [sic: *in offenso*]
  step." This is 7a⁗-b's textbook case — a word-division defect whose quarantine would
  otherwise swallow a negation.

## Col 1114B (chunk 0000) — *locupletata* where the syntax wants an imperative
- **Reading (as printed):** `tum praesentem regem hunc cum exercitu suo per
  intercessionem omnium sanctorum uberi benedictione locupletata, et in solium regni
  firma stabilitate connecte`
- **Note:** The clause is governed by the imperative *connecte*, and the parallel wants
  a first imperative *locupleta* ("enrich"); printed *locupletata* is a real participle
  (fem. abl./nom.) agreeing with nothing the sentence can use. Rendered as printed:
  "then this present king, with his army, through the intercession of all the saints,
  enriched with rich blessing, and join him to the throne of the kingdom with firm
  stability" — the clause fails to construe in English as in the Latin. A mood
  anomaly, not a negation: declined for `[cj:]` under the 2026-08-15 ruling's scope.

## Col 1114B (chunk 0000) — *Jesu Nave*
- **Reading (as printed):** `sicut visitasti Moysen in rubo, Jesu Nave in proelio`
- **Note:** *Jesu Nave* ("Jesus [son of] Nave," the Greek-Latin style of Joshua son of
  Nun) stands two clauses after the same man appears as *Josuae* (1113C). Both printed
  forms are kept — "Joshua" at 1113C, "Jesus Nave" here — because normalizing would
  erase the codex's own double naming.
- The same oration prints the editor's parenthetical variant `illi (illius)` at
  1114B–C and his query `coaluus ( *an* coalitus?)` at 1114C: both parentheses are
  preserved verbatim and untranslated (register rule L8). *coaluus* is not a word and
  is carried `[sic: *coaluus*]`; the editor's own query beside it does the glossing, so
  no conjecture of ours is added (Pattern 18's "edition already glosses it" class).

## Col 1114C–D (chunk 0000) — *sicut* for *sic*: `[cj:]` under the 2026-08-15 ruling
- **Reading (as printed):** `et sicut benedixisti Abraham, Isaac et Jacob, sicut illum
  largis benedictionibus … irrigare, atque profundere dignare`
- **Note:** The second *sicut* stands where the correlative answer *sic* ("as thou didst
  bless … *so* deign") is required; as printed the sentence has two "as"-clauses and no
  apodosis marker. *sicut* is a real word and renders ("as"), leaving the English broken
  exactly as the Latin is. **A weakened correlative is the class Wilson ruled glossable
  on 2026-08-15** (11064 @1142D's *si* for *sic* is the twin case), so the page reads
  "as [cj: *sicut*; read *sic*, \"so\"] deign to water him."

## Col 1114D (chunk 0000) — *pata* (patria)
- **Reading (as printed):** `sit sanitas corporum in pata (patria)`
- **Note:** *pata* is not a word; the edition's own parenthesis supplies *patria*.
  Carried `[sic: *pata*]` with the parenthesis preserved verbatim (L8). No `[cj:]` —
  the edition already glosses it.
- The same oration's closing clause, `atque splendore quasi splendidissima fulgore
  maximo perfusa lumine videatur`, is genuinely tangled Latin (three ablatives of light
  jostling one feminine adjective); rendered as closely as the printed words allow, with
  no smoothing: "may be seen with splendor, as if most splendid, suffused with light in
  greatest blaze."

## Col 1115C (chunk 0000) — *Nullus … vertat* with no object
- **Reading (as printed):** `Nullus insidiantibus malis in iniustitiam vertat.`
- **Note:** *vertat* wants an object (*eum*, presumably — "let no one turn him to
  injustice"); none is printed. Rendered as printed, objectless: "Let no one, evils
  lying in wait, turn to injustice."

## Col 1116A (chunk 0000) — *exsecutio* with Migne's own conjecture in the note
- **Reading (as printed):** `et temporali regno iustis moderaminibus exsecutio [n: ( f.
  exsecuto, exsecutus)], aeternaliter cum eo regnare merearis`
- **Note:** Printed *exsecutio* (nominative noun) where the syntax wants a participle;
  Migne's own footnote conjectures *exsecuto* or *exsecutus*. A real word, rendered as
  printed — "the temporal kingdom with just governances — execution —" — with the note
  preserved verbatim where the plate sets it. No `[cj:]`: the edition already glosses it
  (Pattern 18 decline class).

## Col 1116B (chunk 0001) — *institiaeque* for *iustitiaeque*
- **Reading (as printed):** `virtutum triumpho gloriosus, institiaeque cultor egregius`
- **Note:** *institia* is not a Latin word (only *instita*, "a flounce," exists, and its
  genitive is *institae*); the intended reading is *iustitiaeque* ("and of justice").
  The carried run contains the enclitic conjunction *-que*, so 7a⁗ (question 3) requires
  the conjunction and the sense in plain words beside the marker: "and an excellent
  cultivator of justice [sic: *institiaeque*]."

## Col 1118A (chunk 0001) — *dei* for *det*: the rubric loses its verb
- **Reading (as printed):** `Tunc dei illis osculum pacis.`
- **Note:** The rubric plainly intends *det* ("then let him give them the kiss of
  peace"); printed *dei* is a real word (genitive of *deus*), so per Pattern 7 it is
  rendered as printed — "*Then of God to them the kiss of peace.*" — a rubric with no
  verb. Declined for `[cj:]` under the 2026-08-15 ruling's scope (the wrong word is a
  verb loss, not a negation or correlative), but **this is the second site at the
  ruling's boundary** alongside 11064 @1154C: a reader meets a visible hole, not a
  false claim, which is the letter of the decline — and one line reverses it if the
  ruling is read to reach lost verbs.

## Col 1118B (chunk 0001) — *indoneus* for *idoneus*, self-corroborated
- **Reading (as printed):** `ut famulus tuus N. … inveniatur semper indoneus`
- **Note:** *indoneus* is not a word (intruded *n* in *idoneus*, "fit"); carried
  `[sic: *indoneus*]`. Self-corroborated inside the work: the *Ad Complendum* at 1118C
  prints the same idea correctly as `populum idoneum illum reddat`, translated "render
  him fit" there.

## Col 1118C (chunk 0001) — the doubled *te*
- **Reading (as printed):** `et te in concilio beatorum collocato aeterna felicitate te
  ibidem tecum pariter gaudere mereatur`
- **Note:** After the ablative absolute *te … collocato* ("thou being placed in the
  council of the blessed"), the sentence has both an accusative *te* (subject of
  *gaudere*) and *tecum* ("with thee") — the subject of *mereatur* being the *people*,
  the result is a reflexive absurdity ("may merit that thou rejoice together with
  thee"). The likely reading is *se* for the second *te* ("may merit itself to rejoice
  there with thee"), which construes cleanly. All three printed words are real and all
  are rendered: "may merit that thou rejoice there together with thee equally in
  eternal felicity." Declined for `[cj:]` (a case slip, not a negation), recorded here.

## Scripture and near-scripture, all rendered as printed, none marked
- `omnis anima potestatibus sublimioribus subdita sit regi quasi praecellenti` (1113A):
  the *juxta apostolum* citation splices Rom. 13:1 onto 1 Pet. 2:13 (*sive regi quasi
  praecellenti*) as one sentence under one "apostle." The author's own weave, not a
  citation note claiming a single source — rendered as printed, no `[var:]`.
- `Accingere gladio tuo super femur tuum potentissime` (1116A–B): Ps. 44:4, verbatim.
- `Ego sum ostium: per me si quis introierit, salvabitur` (1116C): John 10:9, verbatim.
- `clavis David et sceptrum domus Israel: qui aperit et nemo claudit …` and `qui educit
  vinctum de domo carceris, sedentemque in tenebris` (1116C–D): the O-antiphon wording
  (*O clavis David*), woven into the prayer — liturgical text, not a citation (L3's
  boundary), no `[var:]`.
- `Sedes tua Deus in saeculum saeculi, virga aequitatis, virga regni tui` (1116D):
  *virga aequitatis* is not the Gallican Psalter's *virga directionis* (Ps. 44:7) — it
  is Heb. 1:8's and the Roman Psalter's wording, i.e. a received text in its own right.
  No `[var:]`.
- `Benedicat tibi Dominus, custodiatque te` (1117B): Num. 6:24. `mediator Dei et
  hominum` (1117D–1118A): 1 Tim. 2:5. `de rore coeli, et de pinguedine terrae` (1114D):
  Gen. 27:28 with *et olei* added by the author — his weave, rendered as printed.
