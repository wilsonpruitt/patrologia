## On the Monastery of Fruttuaria



## 1.

[0837D]Since scarcely anything is to be had concerning the monastery of Fruttuaria, whether in the *Life of William* written by Glaber, [0838D] or in the Benignian chronicler, it has seemed good to treat of it here a little more fully, since it holds this prerogative over [0839A] the other monasteries which our William governed, that it was founded by him upon the estate of his own paternal inheritance, and that therefore the knowledge of its beginning will be of much profit toward illustrating the deeds of that same blessed man. It is situated between two rivers, the Orco and the Malone, in Subalpine Gaul, within the bounds of the diocese of Ivrea. It was first founded and endowed by the liberality of the brothers of blessed William, and of others of his kinsmen, then enlarged by various donations of others, and at length confirmed and fortified by the diplomas and privileges of princes and supreme pontiffs; and it yielded to hardly any monastery of all Italy, whether in nobility or in the abundance of its riches. Its birth is set forth in few words by an old author in these verses, which Francesco Agostino della Chiesa, copied from the ancient [0839B] Chronicle of the monastery, inserted into his *History of Piedmont*:

If any prefer to know beforehand when
The work of the monastery of Fruttuaria had duly been begun:
It was then the sublime thousandth and third year
Of the virgin-born birth of the ethereal Prince.
The seven Kalends of March, or the first indiction,
Both accomplish their time or their course.
King Arduin, upheld by the governance of the scepter,
Reigns in Hesperia, stretches toward Ausonia.
The Lord himself aids the place, which he endows with his gift,
He took counsel for the affairs of the assiduous brethren.
What Bishop Ottobiano hallowed by the right of dedication,
Abbot William builds this for the Lord.

## 3.

In what year that instrument was drawn up is uncertain to me, since either William himself did not express it, or certainly he who copied it from the autograph omitted the date — he who also, whether through unskillfulness in reading, or for some other reason I know not, seems to have besprinkled that so precious monument with many errors. For the rest, from the various charters which are reported by Guichenon in the *Bibliotheca Sebusiana*, and by Ughelli, in the fourth volume of *Italia Sacra* among the bishops of Ivrea, it is plain that nothing was omitted by Abbot William whereby he might so firmly [0839D] establish the liberty of the monastery of Fruttuaria, both in temporal and in spiritual things, that in after times it should not easily be torn away. First he took care to have confirmed to himself by the emperor Henry whatsoever had been handed over to the monastery, whether by his own kinsmen or by other pious men. There is extant on this matter a diploma of that same Henry, given in the year 1014, from which not only are many things learned concerning the various possessions which the monastery of Fruttuaria already then possessed, but also from it there become known both the most noble lineage of our William, and certain other illustrious men who, having set aside the enticements of the world, at that time led the monastic life in that place; nay, even *Gonthard's adjudication,* praised above in William's instrument, is confirmed by Henry. « The lord abbot William, he says, approached our clemency, [0840A] asking that a certain monastery be corroborated by our authority, situated in the place which is called Fruttuaria, out of the proper inheritance of his brothers Gotfred, Nithard and Robert, built in honor of the holy Mother of God Mary and of Saint Benignus the martyr, even as is contained in the charter of adjudication issued by Gonthard the deacon. Whose petitions being received, we have first confirmed it by royal precept, » etc. Among the other benefactors of the monastery he reckons Otto William, the kinsman of our William, as we said above, of whom there are extant several charters given in favor both of the Benignian and of the monastery of Fruttuaria. « Let it have and hold by quiet right the castle of the Lombards with all its appurtenances, and all those estates and courts which Otto, who [0840B] is also William, count, son of Adalbert, grandson of King Berengar, gave in that place within the bounds of Lombardy out of the inheritance of his parents and kinsmen. We ordain also, by this firmity of our precept, that all those lands are to be held in perpetuity which the oft-named monastery ought to have out of the inheritance of Robert, once a most noble man, who was the father of the said abbot William . . . We confirm all that inheritance and the possessions which were owed, by paternal and maternal right, to Otbert, who was the son of Gerard, and grandson of Otbert the count, who made the holy church of Fruttuaria his heir, when his father and he himself forsook the world, giving themselves and all their goods to God, having become monks . . . » Toward the end he enumerates the cells, or priories, which already [0840C] then were dependent on that monastery; namely the cell « of Asti, the cell of Naviglio, the cell in Quaranta, the cell in Cavaglià, the cell in Paderno, » etc.

## 4.

In the year of Christ 1023, William obtained likewise another privilege from the same emperor, whereby he takes the monastery of Fruttuaria — « even as is contained in the charter of adjudication issued by Gunthard the deacon » — under his special defense, and confirms the privilege of Benedict VIII. « Henceforth, he says, that it may remain the more stable, we corroborate it by imperial authority, we receive it under our defense on this condition, that no duke, marquis, etc. We ordain also, and by God's authority and our own confirm, that the same place obtain in all things the above-mentioned liberty, and that whatsoever has been ordained concerning its liberty [0840D] and stability, concerning the election and consecration of the abbot, by the lord apostolic Benedict and by many bishops in privileges and synodal decrees, be preserved ratified for all time and resting upon unshaken firmity. » Hugh, abbot of Flavigny, observes in the *Chronicle of Verdun* that our William, whom he calls « a strenuous ruler and most excellent in religion, » was most acceptable to blessed Henry. And a little below he says that the same William « was called 'above the rule' from the rigor of his more fervent purpose. »

## 5.

After the death of Henry Augustus, Conrad his successor, being asked by blessed William, in almost the same words as Henry likewise corroborated by his own authority the liberty of the monastery of Fruttuaria, as is plain from his own [0841A] charter, which is reported by the same Guichenon, in which John, abbot of that same place, is mentioned — who in the Benignian Chronicle, above, is called *Joannetinus* — and Henry, and his successors the Augusti, are said to have been admitted « into the fellowship of the brethren. » « We have judged it worthy to confirm the monastery of Fruttuaria by written precepts, at the entreaty of the lord William, abbot of Dijon, who, building the aforesaid monastery from the foundations out of the estates and alms of his kinsmen and of the other faithful of Christ, gathered together there very many serving God according to the rule of Saint Benedict. We, desiring to have — for ourselves and our successors in perpetuity, even as he associated our predecessor Henry of divine memory in his and the brethren's company — a share in all his benefactions, [0841B] caused the first abbot of that same monastery, John by name, to be consecrated in his presence, and committed both him and the whole same place to our imperial guardianship, chiefly for this cause, that he might preserve for it in all things the same liberty which the monastery of Cluny is known to have. We therefore, taking counsel for our salvation, » etc. He confirms also the privileges and synodal decrees of Pope Benedict and of the other bishops in the same words as in the diploma of Henry praised above; which Francesco Agostino della Chiesa observes was done also by other emperors, nay even by the Roman pontiffs, in his *Catalogue of the abbots of Fruttuaria*, in chapter 27 of the *Chronological History of Piedmont*, where he says that the Abbot John here mentioned was the son of Count Guido [0841C] of Saint-Martin, and grandson of Arduin king of Italy.

## 6.

With no less devotion was Robert, king of the Franks, attached to the monastery of Fruttuaria than were the emperors, although it was situated outside his own dominion. Whence, when William had sought of him the confirmation of certain goods which had been granted to his monastery in France, the most pious king of his own accord gave him his assent, and by a general precept secured to the monastery of Fruttuaria whatsoever had either already been given to it, or might afterward perchance be handed over, within the bounds of his kingdom ( *See above under* ROBERT *the king, among his ecclesiastical precepts.*
