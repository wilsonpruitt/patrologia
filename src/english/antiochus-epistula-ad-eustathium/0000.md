THE LETTER of Antiochus, monk of the Laura of Abba Sabas, to Eustathius, superior of the monastery of Attalina, of the city of Ancyra in Galatia, concerning the holy Fathers there; in which are also one hundred and thirty chapters [lat: the Latin title gives *capitula 150*, one hundred and fifty], and a prayer of confession.

[1421] Since you have written to me, honoured Father Eustathius, that you have endured much affliction, exchanging one place for another and one land for another, for fear of the Chaldean storm that prevails; and that you have been put to the trial of hunger and of thirst — not of bread and of water, but, according to that which has been said, of hearing the word of the Lord; and since you are neither able to carry about with you the weight of books, nor, in the places where you sojourn, to find readily the things you seek: you charged my littleness to draw together in brief the whole of the divine Scripture, both Old and New, so that neither should the burden of the carrying become a weight to us, nor again should anything be left out of the things that tend to the profit and salvation of the soul. I yielded readily to your command, and having straightway set in order all the things sought by you out of the divine Scripture, into one hundred and thirty chapters [lat: the Latin gives *centum quinquaginta … capita*, one hundred and fifty], united to one another and yet distinguished from one another, so that the reader should not undergo confusion and jumbling — these I have sent to your God-loving reverence. Receiving them, send up to God the fitting glory and thanksgiving, to him who perfects his own power in weakness; and requite us with your acceptable prayers, that we may fulfil the remaining time of our life in a manner thankful to God. But if you should find anything, as is likely, grant pardon both to my want of learning and to the haste of the injunction.

And since you sent this word also, honoured Father, that I should signify to you concerning the holy Fathers of our Laura [nt: The moderns among the Greeks call a *laura* a notable monastery, as though because many waters flow together there.] — whether those things be true which you said you had heard from many, both concerning their manner of life and concerning their slaughter, things hard to believe and passing hearing — I judged it necessary first to signify to you in brief [1424] the things concerning them, and so thereafter to append the chapters of the divine Scripture.

Now concerning their virtuous manner of life, it belongs neither to the present season nor to my tongue to tell. This only do I say: that they were truly divine men — if indeed one ought to call them men, and not angels — grown old from youth in ascetic discipline and in stillness and in the labours borne for godliness; aged, of priestly bearing, grey in the hair and in the understanding, good, humble-minded, orderly, temperate, true, blameless, just, God-fearing, holding themselves back from every evil thing, lacking in none of the good things, and filled full of the divine love. Some of them had passed beyond fifty and sixty years [lat: the Latin gives *centum excesserunt annos et quinquaginta sex*, exceeded a hundred years and fifty-six] without going out of the Laura in any way at all; and some neither went forward into the church nor had seen the city from the time they became monks — adorned with virtues of every kind, earthly angels and heavenly men. Whence also they carried off such an end, and bore away the prize of the victory.

For when the Ishmaelites came upon our Laura one week before the holy city was taken, and plundered all the sacred vessels of the church, the multitude of the Fathers withdrew at once; but the steadfast servants of Christ remained behind in the Laura, not willing to leave the place. And the barbarians, having seized them and tortured them pitilessly for many days — supposing that they should find money with men in whose keeping was nothing of this age — at last, failing of their purpose and becoming frenzied, cut them all in pieces limb by limb. But the blessed ones, rejoicing with a bright countenance, gave up their souls to the Lord with thanksgiving, as having long had the desire to depart and to be with Christ. And when their bodies had been flung out unburied for many days, we came upon them from Arabia; and the abba Nicomedes, beholding the piteous spectacle of the Fathers, swooning away, was well-nigh — so to speak — taken up dead. But Modestus the most holy, coming forward and gathering up all the tabernacles of the saints, washed them, and having wept much over them and embraced their holy and steadfast relics, laid them in the tombs of the Fathers. And having performed over them the accustomed rule, he uttered over them the oracle of Isaiah which says: «Just men are taken away, and no one considers; from before the face of iniquity the just man has been taken away; his burial shall be in peace.» [n: Isa.
