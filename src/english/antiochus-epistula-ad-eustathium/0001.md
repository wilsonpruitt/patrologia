⟦s3⟧ Yielding therefore to his exhortation, we remained in the Laura about two months. ⟦s4⟧ And when a barbarian report arose again, taking fright we fled for refuge to a monastery near the holy city, about twenty stadia off, called that of the abba Anastasius, which had no one in it at that time. ⟦s5⟧ And having spent about two years in it, we were again entreated by the aforesaid most holy Modestus to return and to dwell in our own place. ⟦s6⟧ Persuaded then by his good counsel, some of us returned at once and settled in the Laura; but some a little more slowly, for fear of the Saracens who neighbour upon us; and some of us remained on in that same monastery, together with Justinus their most holy superior, who had renounced the world in the Laura and had spent very many years in it and had been distinguished in many virtues, so that he was honoured also with the dignity of the presbyterate. ⟦s7⟧ And this most God-fearing Justinus gathered to himself a great company in that same monastery by the grace of Christ, keeping the rule unaltered from that of the Laura, and the rest of its constitution; and one would not find in all Palestine, or in any other coenobium [nt: Of the coenobitic sort.], such a spiritual constitution. ⟦s8⟧ And it flourishes and increases day by day; and going forward it becomes great after the manner of Isaac, so that from this we rejoice, conjecturing rightly that in every land the seed of our holy Father Sabas shines out after the manner of luminaries.

⟦s9⟧ But we the lowly, by the grace of God and by the diligence of the aforementioned most holy Modestus, sit in stillness beyond the former years, giving thanks and glorifying God, who kills and makes alive and who alone does great wonders. ⟦s10⟧ For his own goodness had compassion upon us, and brought our place unto great advancement, so far as the times allowed, on account of the holy servants of his who had served him genuinely therein. ⟦s11⟧ And his goodness bestowed upon us a Father and shepherd worthy of his providence: Thomas the most holy, one gifted with knowledge and adorned with virtues of every kind, who from a youthful age practised a life of stillness; the gentle and brother-loving and most compassionate, the humble in heart, most fervent about prayer and about the rule. ⟦s12⟧ Wherefore God also, under his leadership, brought his disciples into a broad place.

⟦s13⟧ And the number of the Fathers who were slain is forty-four by name. ⟦s14⟧ And their memory is celebrated in the month of May, on the fifteenth.

⟦s15⟧ And by the grace of Christ and the diligence of the aforesaid most holy Father of ours [1428] Modestus, the remaining monasteries of the desert also are inhabited. ⟦s16⟧ Behold then, honoured father Eustathius, in brief the things which I have signified to your God-loving reverence concerning our holy ones and concerning the Laura; and other good tidings also, gladdening your soul and the soul of every Christian who hears them, I bring you. ⟦s17⟧ For the aforesaid most holy Modestus takes forethought not only for the monasteries of the desert, but also for the city and for all the country round about, God working with him in everything. ⟦s18⟧ For this man, as truly our new Bezalel or Zerubbabel, filled with the Holy Spirit, has raised up again the venerable churches that were burned: those of our Saviour Jesus Christ — both the holy Skull, and his holy Resurrection, and the august house of the precious Cross, and the mother of the churches, and his holy Ascension — and the remaining venerable houses; so that from this we have good hopes also concerning the things to come, and most of all for those who come from the outer lands to the veneration of the holy places; and that all glorify God, to whom belongs all glory, honour and veneration, now and ever and unto the ages of ages. ⟦s19⟧ Amen.
