[1612] OF DOROTHEUS: VARIOUS INSTRUCTIONS PROFITABLE TO THE SOUL.

[b: A] ANONYMOUS PROLOGUE.

It should be known that there have been two Dorotheuses and two Barsanuphiuses: the one pair sick with the errors of Severus, the other having embraced orthodoxy and perfect asceticism. And it is these last who are carried in this book; so we hold it acceptable, and full of virtue, and profitable to the soul as much as anything, being in truth the work of the blessed Dorotheus, who showed himself orthodox and eminent among the Fathers, and not of the other, the man of other mind and perverse. Just as our father and confessor of Christ Theodore, who showed himself the all-wise hegumen of the Studios, taught very well in his testament to his own disciples, after he had set down the mind of his own faith and summarily renounced the godless heretics, saying further [b: B] these words: "And I accept every divinely inspired book of the Old and New Testament; and moreover the lives and the divine writings of all the holy and inspired Fathers, both teachers and ascetics. This I have said because of Pamphilus, manifestly harmful, who came from the East and slandered these holy men, I mean Mark, Isaiah, Barsanuphius, Dorotheus and Hesychius; not the Barsanuphius [sic: *Βασανούφιον*] and Isaiah and Dorotheus who are fellow-headless with the Acephali, and fellow-sworn-horned with those called [sic: *λογομένους*] Decakeratae, and anathematized by Sophronius, now among the saints, in his libellus; for these are plainly others, besides the aforesaid [1613] [b: A] men; whom I accept as handed down from the fathers, through inquiry of Tarasius the most holy patriarch, who has already held the high priesthood, and of other trustworthy persons, both natives and Easterners. And because [sic: *τὰ τὸ*] the icon of Barsanuphius stands on the divine altar-covering of the Great Church with the holy Fathers, Antony, Ephrem and others; and since I have found no impiety in their teachings, but on the contrary much profit for the soul." See then: our great father Theodore distinguished the repute of the two Dorotheuses, and declared the teaching of the one carried here to be most profitable; as indeed it truly is, very profitable to the soul. Whoever orders his own life exactly by it will drive on to the perfect measure of virtue in Christ; [b: B] and having arrived, he will be adorned with the crown of dispassion, and will be deemed worthy of eternal life with the saints.

Letter to the brother who asked that the discourses of our holy Father Dorotheus which had been found be sent to him.

1. I praise thy purpose; I call blessed thy blessed and truly beauty-loving soul for its zeal for the good, most longed-for brother. For so laboriously to search out and so genuinely to praise the things concerning our truly blessed and God-worthy father, who bears the name of God's gift: [b: C] that is to praise virtue, and what follows upon so blessed a man, and to love God, and to take thought for the true life. For, according to the blessed Gregory, praise procures zeal; and zeal, virtue; and virtue, blessedness. So one may rejoice, and truly rejoice with thee, at such progress of thine; for thou seemest to follow the tracks of that man, the imitator of the meek and lowly of heart; who, having contemplated the spiritual renunciation of Peter and those with him, so stripped himself of attachment to things seen, so gave himself over to his own affairs according to God, that with boldness, I know well, he too could say to the Saviour, Behold, we have left all and followed thee. [n: Matth. xix, 27.] Whence, made perfect in a short time with God, he fulfilled long years; not dwelling in deserts and mountains of sense, nor setting great store by ruling body-devouring beasts; [b: D] but embracing the desert of the soul, and longing to draw near to the eternal mountains, knowing wonderfully how to give light, and how to tread on the heads of soul-killing serpents and scorpions. All this he was deemed worthy to attain swiftly, by the co-working of Christ, through the athlete's slaying of his own will; which procured for him the unerring road of the fathers; which showed him the blessed burden light, and showed the saving and kindly yoke to be kindly indeed.

[1616] [b: A] 2. From it he learned the best road to height, lowliness; and receiving "Be merciful and meek" in deed, and according to the word of the holy elders, he was adorned through these with all the virtues. Hence the blessed man always had on his lips that saying of the Gerontikon: "He who has come to cutting off his own will," he would say, "has come to the place of rest." For since he found, as he worthily sought, that self-love is the root of all the passions, and that it is compounded with our bittersweet will, he used this potent medicine and withers, root and all, the evil shoots too; and he becomes a pure husbandman of immortal things, and bears as fruit the true life, having gained the treasure hidden in the field, well sought and found by him, [b: B] and become truly rich in the things that are not emptied. I could wish I were sufficient in tongue and mind to be found worthy to set out his holy life in detail, for the common profit and as a most exact pattern of virtue; and how that blessed man ran the road that is narrow and spacious and beyond expectation. Narrow, because it is unerring and undissipated, and guards against the precipitous straying to either side: for so the friend of God, the truly great Basil, defines the narrowness of the afflicted and saving road. And spacious, because of its freedom from attachment and its liberality toward those who lead along it to God, and most of all because of the loftiness of lowliness, which alone, according to the great Antony, is above all the snares [b: C] of the devil. Wherefore in him truly was fulfilled, Thy commandment is exceeding broad. [n: Psal. cxviii, 96.]

3. But this, as a thing impossible for me, I shall pass over. For I know, besides all the blessed man's good things, these too: the sayings of the so-called outside philosophers, which he gathered like the truly wise bee, and where he found them to carry something useful he would put them forward without hesitation for teaching, in season; such as Nothing in excess, Know thyself, and the like. To passing over all this, if not a willing choice, yet the necessity of my weakness, as was said, drives me. But what your zealous and beauty-loving soul [b: D] commanded me, this I have boldly done, both dreading the weight of disobedience and fearing the penalty of sloth. And so with the present letter I have sent to you, the prudent bankers with God, the idle talent that lay with me: I mean the blessed man's teachings that have been found, which he himself was deemed worthy to receive from his own fathers, and which he handed on to his own disciples, doing and teaching after our first and true Master and Saviour. And if we have not been able to find all his holy discourses, but very few, and these gathered here and there by certain zealous persons by the providence of God, yet it will be enough [1617] [b: A] for thy dexterity that even the little be presented, according to the saying, Give a wise man occasion, and he will be wiser. [n: Prov. ix, 9.]

But first, more briefly, the things concerning the blessed Dositheus, who became the first disciple of the blessed abba Dorotheus while he was still in the monastery of abba Seridos and accomplishing the contest of subjection according to Christ.
