[1732] 6. Let us speak also about those who check the passion. There is one who at times hears a word and is afflicted within himself; but he grieves, not because he was insulted, but because he did not bear it. This one belongs to the state of those who strive; [b: B] he is of those who check the passion. Another strives and toils, but afterwards is defeated, because he is weighed down by the passion. Another does not want to answer badly, but is carried away by custom. Another strives not to say anything bad at all, but grieves that he was insulted; yet he blames himself for grieving, and repents of it. Another does not grieve that he was insulted, but does not rejoice either. See, all these are the ones who check the passion. But among themselves the two differ from the rest: the one who is defeated in striving, and the one who is carried away by custom, and the one who blames himself that he did not bear the insult with thanksgiving. The rest are in fear of the danger of those who act it out. And I have said that they are of those who check the passion, [b: C] for in their disposition they have checked the passion, and they do not want to act it out, but even grieve as they strive. And the Fathers said that every thing the soul does not want is short-lived. But they ought to probe themselves, lest, even if not the passion itself, they champion one of the causes of the passion, and for that reason are defeated or carried away. And there are some, he says, who strive to check the passion, but by way of a passion. For one keeps silence out of vainglory, another out of men-pleasing, or out of some other passion of whatever kind. These cure evil with evil. And abba Poemen said that wickedness in no way removes wickedness. These are with those who act it out, even if they scoff at it.

7. Now we want to speak also about those who uproot [b: D] the passion. There is one who rejoices when he is insulted, but because he has a reward. This one is of those who uproot the passion, but not with knowledge. Another rejoices when insulted, and holds that he deserves to be insulted, since he himself gave the cause. This one uproots the passion with knowledge; for when someone is insulted, to bring the cause back upon himself, and to accept what comes upon him as his own, is a matter of knowledge. For everyone who prays to God, "Lord, give me lowliness," ought to know that this is what he is asking: that God send him someone to insult him. When he is insulted by someone, he ought himself also to insult himself and to despise himself in his own [1733] [b: A] thought, so that the other humbles him from outside, and he himself from inside. There is another who not only rejoices when insulted and blames himself, but also grieves over the trouble of the one who insulted him. God bring us to such a state! See how much breadth there is in the three states. So let each of us, as I said, learn of which state he is: whether he still willingly acts out the passion and champions it; or does not do it willingly, but being defeated, or carried away by custom, acts it out, and after acting it out is afflicted and repents that he acted it out; or strives to check the passion with knowledge; or strives by way of another passion, as we said. There are times when someone keeps silence out of vainglory or out of men-pleasing, or, simply put, out of some human [b: B] thought; and whether he has begun to uproot the passion, and whether he uproots it with knowledge and does the opposite of the passion. Let each learn where he is, at what mile he is. [sic: *εἰ᾿*] For we ought to search ourselves not only every day, but also every season and every month and every week, and to say, "The first week I was weighed down in such and such a way by this passion; how am I now?" Likewise every season to say, "Last year I was overcome in such and such a way by this passion; how do I stand now?" And so each day to exercise ourselves, whether we have progressed a little, or are in the same place, or have got worse. God will give us strength, so that for now, even if we do not uproot the passion, at least we do not act it out, but check it. For truly it is a heavy [b: C] thing to act out the passion and not to check it. And I give you an illustration of what the one who acts out the passion and champions it is like. He is like a man shot with arrows by his enemy, who takes the arrows in his hands and drives them into his own heart. The one who checks the passion is like the man shot with arrows by his enemy who has put on a breastplate and does not take the arrow in. The one who uproots the passion is like a man shot with arrows who takes the arrows and breaks them, or turns them back into his enemy's heart, as it says in the psalm: Let their sword enter into their own hearts, and let their bows be broken. So let us too be eager, brethren: even if we cannot yet turn their sword back into their [b: D] hearts, at least let us not take the arrows and drive them into our hearts; but let us also put on breastplates, so that we are not wounded by them. May the good God shelter us from them, and give us watchfulness, and guide us into his way.

Instruction XI. *On striving to cut off the passions quickly, before the soul comes into an evil habit.*

1. Set your minds to things, brethren, [1736] [b: A] and see that you do not neglect yourselves; for even a little negligence brings us into great dangers. I visited a brother once, and found him in a way recovering from a sickness; and as we talked [sic: *λαβεῖν*] I learned that he had had a fever for only seven days, and see, he has had another forty days and still cannot regain his strength. See, brethren, what a misery it is for someone to fall into a disordered condition. One always despises a little disorder, and does not know that if his body comes to be a little ill-treated, and especially if he is rather weak, there is very great need of both toil and time before it is set right. This poor man had a fever for seven days, and see, so many days, and he finds no rest, nor can he regain his strength. So it is [b: B] with the soul too: someone sins a little, and he spends how long dripping his blood before he sets himself right! And in bodily sickness we find various causes: either the medicines, being old, do not work; or the doctor is inexperienced and gives one medicine instead of another; or the sick man is disorderly and does not keep what is ordered him by the doctor. But with the soul it is not so. For we cannot say that the doctor, being inexperienced, did not give the fitting medicines. For Christ is the doctor of souls, and he knows all things, and has provided for each passion its fitting medicine as well. For example: for vainglory the commandments about humility, for love of pleasure those about self-control, for love of [b: C] money those about almsgiving; and in a word each passion has as its medicine the commandment fitting to it. So the doctor is not inexperienced. Nor again do the medicines fail to work because they are old. For the commandments of Christ never grow old; but the more they are put into practice, the more they are renewed. So there is nothing hindering the health of the soul except only its own disorder.

2. Let us attend to ourselves then, brethren; let us be watchful ⟨while we have⟩ time. Why do we neglect ourselves? Let us do some good, so that we may find help in the time of temptation. Why are we wasting our life? We hear so much, and [b: D] we do not care, but despise it. We see our brethren snatched from our midst, and we are not watchful, though we know that little by little we too are drawing near to death. See, from the time we sat down to talk until now, we have spent two or three hours of our time and come nearer to death; and we see that we are losing the time, and are not afraid. How is it we do not remember the saying of that elder who said: "If someone loses gold or silver, he can find other in its place; but if we lose time, living idly and slothfully, we cannot find another. Truly we shall seek a single hour of this time, and not find it." How many long to hear the word of God and do not find it? And we hear so much and despise it, and are not roused? God knows, I am amazed at the [1737] [b: A] insensibility of our souls: that we can be saved, and do not want to. For while our passions are young we can cut them off, and we do not care; but we let them harden against us, so that we make the evil greater, to the uttermost. For it is one thing, as I have often told you, to uproot a plant, which comes away at once, and another to uproot a great tree.

3. A great elder was spending some leisure with his disciples in a place where there were various cypresses, small and large. And the elder says to one of his disciples, "Pull up this cypress." Now it was [b: B] very small, and at once the brother pulled it up with one hand. Then the elder shows him another, bigger than the first, and says to him, "Pull this one up too." And he shook it with both hands and pulled that up too. Again the elder shows him another, bigger still; and he pulled that one up too, with more toil. Then he shows him yet another, bigger again; and he, with more toil, after shaking it hard and toiling and sweating, got that one out too. Then the elder shows him one still bigger; and though he toiled hard and sweated, he could not get it out. So when the elder saw that he could not, he told another brother to get up and help him, and so the two of them together were able to pull it up. Then the elder says to those brethren, "See, so it is with the passions too, [b: C] brethren. As long as they are small, if we wish, we can cut them off with ease. But if we neglect them as small things, they harden; and the more they harden, the more toil they need. And if they grow up to full strength against us, then not even with toil can we cut them off from ourselves, unless we also have the help of some saints who take our part, after God." See how much power the things said by the holy elders have. And the Prophet likewise teaches us about this in the psalm, where he says: O wretched daughter of Babylon, blessed is he who shall repay thee the repayment which thou didst repay us; blessed is he who shall seize and dash thy little ones against the rock.
