⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Instruction XXII. ⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the same holy Dorotheus: an interpretation of some sayings of St Gregory which are sung with troparia at holy Pascha.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 1. ⟦s2⟧ I would gladly speak to you a little about the psalms we sing, so that you may not drift off after the tune, [sic: *ἀπομετεωρίζεσθαι*] but so that your mind itself may also be set on fire, in its measure, by the power of the words. ⟦s3⟧ What did we sing just now? ⟦s4+5⟧ *Day of resurrection: let us offer ourselves as fruit.* [b: C] Since in old times the sons of Israel, at feasts or at festal gatherings, brought gifts to the Lord according to the law, such as sacrifices, whole burnt offerings, first-fruits and all such things, St Gregory urges you to keep the feast to the Lord as they did, and he exhorts you, saying: *Day of resurrection*; that is, instead of saying, the day of the holy feast, the day of the divine festival, the day of the Pascha of Christ. ⟦s6⟧ What is the Pascha of Christ? ⟦s7⟧ The sons of Israel kept the Phasekh when they came out of Egypt; but the Pascha which St Gregory now urges us to keep is kept by the soul which comes out of the spiritual Egypt, that is, out of sin. ⟦s8+9⟧ For when the soul passes over from sin to virtue, then it keeps the Phasekh to the Lord, as Evagrius said: *The Pascha of the Lord is a passing over from evil.* ⟦s10⟧ So today is [b: D] the Pascha of the Lord, the day of a bright feast, the day of the resurrection of Christ, who crucified sin, and died for us and rose again. ⟦s11⟧ So let us too bring gifts to the Lord, sacrifices, whole burnt offerings, not of irrational animals, which Christ does not want; for sacrifice and offering of irrational beasts he did not wish, and whole burnt offerings of calves and sheep he took no pleasure in. ⟦s12⟧ And Isaiah says: *What to me is the multitude of your sacrifices? says the Lord*, [n: Isa. i, 11.] and so on. ⟦s13⟧ But since the Lamb of God was sacrificed for us, according to the Apostle, who says: *For indeed our Pascha has been sacrificed,* [1824] [b: A] *Christ*, [n: I Cor. v, 7.] that he might take away the sin of the world; and: *He became a curse for us*, as it is written: *Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree*, [n: Galat. iii, 13.] that he might redeem us from the curse of the law; and: that we might receive the adoption of sons: [n: Galat. iv, 5.] then we too ought to bring him something pleasing as our gift.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ 2. [sic: *Δʹ*] What gift, then, or what sacrifice ought we to bring to Christ on the day of the resurrection, so as to please him, since he does not want sacrifices of irrational animals? ⟦s3+4+5+6+7+8+9+10+11⟧ The saint himself teaches us this too; for having said, *Day of resurrection*, he added, [b: B] *let us offer ourselves as fruit*; as the Apostle too says: *Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to the Lord, your reasonable worship.* [n: Rom. xii, 1.] How then ought we to present our bodies a living sacrifice, holy to God? ⟦s3+4+5+6+7+8+9+10+11⟧ By no longer doing the wills of the flesh and of our minds, but walking by the Spirit and not fulfilling the desire of the flesh. [n: Galat. v, 16.] For this is to put to death the members that are on the earth. [n: Coloss. iii, 5.] This is called a living sacrifice; since the irrational animal that is led away for sacrifice is sacrificed at once [sic: *ἐνιαυτῷ*] and dies. ⟦s3+4+5+6+7+8+9+10+11⟧ The saints, offering themselves as fruit to God, sacrifice themselves to God while living, every day, as David says: *For thy sake we are put to death all the day long; we were [b: C] counted as sheep for slaughter.* [n: Psal. XLIII, 22; Rom. viii, 36.] This is what St Gregory says: *Let us offer ourselves as fruit*; that is, let us sacrifice ourselves, let us put ourselves to death all the day long, as all the saints too did, for the sake of Christ our God, for the sake of him who died for us. ⟦s3+4+5+6+7+8+9+10+11⟧ And how did they put themselves to death? ⟦s3+4+5+6+7+8+9+10+11⟧ By not loving the world nor the things in the world, as it says in the Catholic Epistles, but by renouncing the desire of the flesh and the desire of the eyes and the arrogance of life, [n: I Joan. ii, 15.] that is, love of pleasure, love of money, vainglory; and by taking up the cross and following Christ, and crucifying the world to themselves, and themselves to the world. ⟦s12+13⟧ Of these the Apostle says: *And those who are Christ Jesus' have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.* [n: Galat. v, 24.] See, this is how the saints put themselves to death.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ 3. [b: D] And how did they offer themselves as fruit? ⟦s3+4⟧ By not living for themselves, but making themselves slaves to the commandments of God, and leaving their own wills for the commandment and the love of God and of neighbour; as St Peter said: *See, we have left everything and followed thee.* [n: Matth. xix, 27] What did he leave? ⟦s3+4⟧ He had no money, did he, or possessions, or gold, or silver? ⟦s5+6⟧ He had a net, and an old one at that, as St John Chrysostom said; and he left, as he said, all his own wills, every attachment to this age; so that it is plain that if he had had money [1825] [b: A] or property, he would have despised that too; and taking up the cross he followed Christ, according to the saying: *I live, yet no longer I, but Christ lives in me.* [n: Galat. ii, 20.] See, this is how the saints offered themselves as fruit, putting themselves to death, as we said, to every attachment and to their own will, and living for Christ alone and for his commandments. ⟦s7⟧ So let us too offer ourselves as fruit, as St Gregory teaches; for he wants us, God's most precious possession. ⟦s8⟧ Truly, man is more precious than all the visible creatures. ⟦s9⟧ For those the Creator brought forth by a word, saying: *Let this be*, and it was; and again: *Let the earth bring forth this*, and it was; and: *Let the waters bring forth*, [n: Gen. i, 3.] and all such things. ⟦s10⟧ But man he formed and adorned with his own hands, and [b: B] all those things he appointed for the service and rest of man, and man himself he set up as king over all of them, and made him enjoy the delight of paradise. ⟦s11⟧ And what is more wonderful: when he had fallen from there through his own sin, he called him back again through the blood of his only-begotten Son; so that of all visible things man is God's most precious possession; and not only most precious, as the saint said, but also most his own. ⟦s11⟧ For: *Let us make man according to our image and according to likeness.* ⟦s12+13⟧ And again: *God made man; in the image of God he made him; and he breathed into his face the breath of life.* [n: ibid. 26.] And our Lord himself, coming to dwell among us, took [b: C] the form of a man, the flesh of a man, of a new man; and, to put it simply, he becomes everything that man is, except sin.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 4. ⟦s2+3⟧ And so he made man closely his own, so to speak, appropriated him. [sic: *Προσῳκειώσαμεν*] Well and fittingly then the saint said: *Man is the most precious possession*; then he adds still more plainly: *Let us render to the image what is according to the image.* ⟦s2+3⟧ How is this too? ⟦s4⟧ Let us learn from the Apostle, who says: *Let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit.* [n: II Cor. vii, 1.] Let us make our image pure, as we received it; let us wipe it clean [sic: *ἀποσμύξωμεν*] of the filth of sin, so that its beauty may show through the virtues. ⟦s5+6⟧ For [b: D] this beauty David prays, saying: *Lord, in thy will grant power to my beauty.* [n: Psal. xxix, 8.] So let us cleanse what in us is according to the image; for God wants it [sic: *αὐτῷ*] from us, and he gave it having no spot or wrinkle or anything of the kind. ⟦s7+8⟧ Let us render to the image what is according to the image. [sic: *τῷ κατ᾿ εἰκόνα*] Let us know our dignity; let us learn of what great goods we have been counted worthy; let us learn in whose image we have been made; let us not be ignorant of the great things given us by him, through his goodness alone, not through our own worth; [1828] [b: A] let us know that we have been made according to the image of God who made us. ⟦s9⟧ Let us honour the archetype. ⟦s10⟧ Let us not insult the image of God according to which we were made. ⟦s11⟧ Who, wanting to paint an image of the emperor, dares to put a rotten colour in the image, and to dishonour the emperor and be punished? ⟦s12⟧ Rather, he puts in all colours that are precious and bright and worthy of the emperor's image. ⟦s13⟧ And sometimes gold leaf too is set into the images of emperors, and the painter takes pains to set into the image, as far as he can, all the emperor's garments, [sic: *αἰσθητὰς*] so that one who sees the image containing the whole imperial likeness may think that he is almost seeing the emperor himself, the archetype itself. ⟦s14⟧ For the image is glorious and bright. ⟦s15⟧ So let us too not dishonour our archetype: we have been made according to the image of God; [b: B] but let us make our own image pure and precious, and worthy of the archetype. ⟦s16⟧ For if one who dishonours the image of an emperor who can be seen and is of the same kind as himself is punished, what ought we to suffer, who despise the divine image within us and do not render the image pure, as the saint said?

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 5. ⟦s2⟧ So let us honour the archetype; let us know the power of the mystery, and for what Christ died. ⟦s3+4⟧ The power of the mystery of the death of Christ is this: since we had blotted out what in us is according to the image through sin, and so had been put to death, [b: C] as the Apostle says, by trespasses and sins, God, who made us according to his own image, having pity on his own creature and his own image, became man for our sakes and accepted death on behalf of all, so that he might bring us, who had been put to death, back up to the life from which we had fallen through disobedience. ⟦s5⟧ For he himself went up on his holy cross and crucified sin, the sin for which we were also cast out of paradise, and *led captivity captive*, [n: Psal. LXVII, 19; Ephes. iv, 8.] as it is written. ⟦s6⟧ What is *led captivity captive*? ⟦s7⟧ That from the transgression of Adam the enemy had taken us captive and held us under his hand. ⟦s8⟧ So then the souls of men, going out of the body, went off to Hades; [b: D] for paradise had been shut. ⟦s9⟧ So Christ, going up to the height of the holy and life-giving cross, delivered us by his own blood from the captivity into which the enemy had taken us captive through the transgression; that is, he snatched us back out of the hand of the enemy, and, as it were, took us captive in his turn, having conquered and overthrown the one who had taken us captive. ⟦s10⟧ That is why it is said, *to take captivity captive*. ⟦s10⟧ This is the power of the mystery; for this Christ died for us, that he might bring us, who had been put to death, back up to life, as the saint said. ⟦s11⟧ So we have been delivered from Hades through the love of Christ for mankind, and it lies with us to go into paradise. ⟦s12⟧ For he no longer tyrannizes as before, nor holds us enslaved.
