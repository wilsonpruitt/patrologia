## Chunk 0001 (ca-001)

- **1401** · …spring flowing with the dew of ethical words — T. The collection's dedicatory lines, not a chapter. The card announces three iambic lines; the plate's Greek gives four (p0714.06–09), and the facing Latin also gives four («Hos amplectere, ac utilitatem condisces» = line 4). All four are rendered, one English line per Greek line.
- **1401** · …Prophet: *Prove me, O Lord, and try me.* — S. Ps 25:2 LXX (26:2 Heb.). P. The Prophet: David, as psalmist.
- **1401** · …but, *has set me upright to the end.* — S. Ps 17:36 LXX (18:35 Heb.), «ἡ παιδεία σου ἀνώρθωσέ με εἰς τέλος». The rejected clause «Thy chastening has overthrown me» is Maximus's foil built on the same verse, not Scripture.
- **1401** · …of the good is the fear of God; — S. cf. Ps 110:10 LXX (111:10 Heb.); Prov 1:7.
- **1401** · …practice, and a practice informed by the word. — T. λόγος ἔμπρακτος / πρᾶξις ἐλλόγιμος: λόγος is "word" throughout the chapter (the facing Latin «sermo»), the uttered word that «goes forth» (ὁ προερχόμενος).
- **1401** · …Before all the virtues go mercy and truth, — S. cf. Ps 84:11 LXX (85:10 Heb.); Prov 3:3.
- **1401** · …will be able to see its own end. — T. Migne prints «οὐδ᾿ ἑτέρα», read as «οὐδετέρα» (Latin «neutra»), "neither of them": mercy and truth, the pair just named.
- **1401** · …and thither among unprofitable things, like some heifer; — S. cf. Hos 4:16.
- **1404** · …practical and the contemplative, does what is due; — S. cf. Prov 31:10 («γυνὴ ἀνδρεία»), 31:18; Matt 25:1–13.
- **1404** · …vice, unless it passes through fire and water, — S. cf. Ps 65:12 LXX (66:12 Heb.).
- **1404** · …unto upbuilding, and for others unto pulling down. — S. cf. 2 Cor 10:8; 13:10.
- **1404** · …with pain, we flee for refuge to pleasure; — T. As printed, and the facing Latin agrees («Causa vero, doloris quidem, voluptas»): the explanation that follows runs from pain to pleasure. Not corrected.
- **1405** · …be able to behold the face of virtue — T. πρόσωπον here is the plain "face", not the Christological "person" of the glossary.
- **1405** · …her, to honour her as she herself demands. — T. ἔννοια = notion (card); ἀφελότης = artlessness, kept apart from ἁπλότης = simplicity (ch. 3). Migne prints ἀπατεῖ; the sense needs ἀπαιτεῖ, "demands".
- **1408** · …gone abroad, finds nothing of what he seeks. — S. cf. John 8:44; 14:30.
- **1408** · …by the immaterial divest themselves of the material, — T. ἀποκτῶνται, a play on κτῶνται, "acquire": literally "dis-acquire". The facing Latin has «abdicant».
- **1408** · …having come into the sense of the unfailing. — S. cf. Luke 12:33.
- **1408** · …everyone is to be rich in good things; — S. cf. 1 Tim 6:18.
- **1408** · …comes about outwardly, by the reaping of reproofs, — T. «ἀναθερισμῷ», "reaping (again)", as printed; the facing Latin «emundatione» may read «ἀνακαθαρισμῷ», "cleansing". The printed word is kept; logged as a doubt.
- **1408** · …other inwardly, by the renewal of the mind, — S. cf. Rom 12:2.
- **1408** · …against him who has involuntarily operated on thee; — T. ἀκουσίως: the facing Latin takes the involuntary as the patient's («qui manu curam invito adhibeat», "on thee unwilling"); the Greek leaves it open.
- **1409** · …found put out of the assembly through grief; — S. cf. John 9:22. T. ἀποσυνάγωγος, "put out of the synagogue": here estranged from the brethren; the facing Latin «praesentiam subduxisse».
- **1409** · …of wealth is gold; and of virtue, humility. — T. ὑπόστασις = "substance" here and in ch. 50 (Heb 11:1), not the glossary's Christological "hypostasis": a decision flagged for the sweep.
- **1409** · …men and before God *a workman not ashamed*. — S. 2 Tim 2:15.
- **1409** · …stranger to those reclining in the bridal chamber — S. cf. Matt 22:11–13.
- **1412** · …seen a fellow at the hearth of justice; — T. Migne prints ὀφθείσῃ; the sense needs ὀφθήσῃ, future.
- **1412** · …bread not with salt but with honey alone; — S. cf. Prov 25:16, 27. T. Migne prints βουλομένου; the sense needs βουλομένῳ (with τῷ).
- **1412** · …appears clothed in the guise of a slave — S. cf. Phil 2:7; John 13:4–14.
- **1412** · …going in*, it says, *and thy going out*: — S. Ps 120:8 LXX (121:8 Heb.); the optative φυλάξοι (LXX φυλάξει) is matched by the facing Latin «custodiat».
- **1412** · …flees the desire that comes from the eyes, — S. cf. 1 John 2:16.
- **1412** · …put away anger from the heart, and desire — S. cf. Eccl 11:10; the sentence continues in the next chunk.

## Chunk 0002 (ca-002)

- **1413** · …from the flesh, according to Ecclesiastes; — S. cf. Eccl 11:10, «ἀπόστησον θυμὸν ἀπὸ καρδίας σου καὶ παράγαγε πονηρίαν ἀπὸ σαρκός σου»; the sentence begins at the end of the previous chunk (ch. 52).
- **1413** · …and examine thy heart by self-control in words, — S. cf. Ps 25:2 LXX (26:2 Heb.); Ps 7:10 LXX (7:9 Heb.).
- **1413** · …incensive power for the service of good things. — T. τὸ ἐπιθυμητικόν / τὸ θυμικόν: the desiring and the incensive power, the two irrational faculties; θυμός alone stays "anger".
- **1413** · …out for him who loved the narrow way. — S. cf. Matt 7:13–14.
- **1413** · …he must pursue. 61. The house of prayer — S. cf. Isa 56:7; Matt 21:13.
- **1416** · …these, if thou hast given up toward those. — T. Migne prints ὀφθείσῃ; the sense needs ὀφθήσῃ, future.
- **1416** · …soul, voluptuousness; and of the mind, passionate attachment. — T. The family of πάθος held one word each through chs. 70–73: ἐμπάθεια / ἐμπαθής = impassionedness / the impassioned; ἡδυπάθεια / ἡδυπαθής = voluptuousness / the voluptuary; προσπάθεια / προσπαθής = passionate attachment / the passionately attached; ἀπάθεια / ἀπαθής = passionlessness / the passionless. The facing Latin glosses the last with the Greek («ἀπαθής»).
- **1416** · …the sinning element more forceful than his reasoning, — T. λογισμός here is the faculty set against the sinning element (the facing Latin «rationem»), hence "reasoning", not the ascetic "thought".
- **1417** · …as are the words of eternal life, drop. — S. cf. Song 4:11; Prov 16:24; John 6:68.
- **1417** · …partake of the sweet odour of her garments? — S. cf. Song 1:13; 4:11; 1:3 (4:10). T. Migne prints μεταβαλεῖν; the sense needs μεταλαβεῖν, "partake".
- **1417** · …will be stripped; but of that of sin, — S. cf. Rom 6:6.
- **1417** · …life here who have enjoyed its sweet things. — T. «οἱ τῆς ἐνταῦθα ζωῆς ὀλίγοι τῶν ἡδέων προσαπολαύσαντες»: rendered as printed, with the facing Latin («pauci ex illis qui vitae hujus deliciis potiti sunt»); ὀλίγοι may instead be meant predicatively, "those who have enjoyed but little of its sweets", which gives the expected sense.
- **1417** · …to sin who have hated it from disposition. — S. cf. Rom 6:2, 11.
- **1417** · …is, before the coming stripping? *Of the same.* — T. A couplet set between chs. 77 and 78 under the heading «Τοῦ αὐτοῦ», "of the same"; the facing Latin prints the two lines without a heading. Lineated one for one; not a chapter, and the numbering runs on past it.
- **1417** · …the same.* A soul wounded with bridal yearning — S. cf. Song 2:5; 5:8.
- **1417** · …and through the latter those of the spirit. — T. The author's «τούτου … ἐκείνου» refer to the first- and second-named terms respectively (sensible light, then noetic; darkness, then light), so they are rendered "the former … the latter". The facing Latin agrees: relation with darkness («ex affectu»), imagination with light.
- **1417** · …cares of sense, like waterless clouds flying round, — S. cf. Jude 12.
- **1417** · …passionate mind cannot enter within the narrow gate — S. cf. Matt 7:13–14.
- **1420** · …together with it the all-causing evil of boldness. — T. παῤῥησία is kept as "boldness" throughout (chs. 100, 101: boldness before God); here it is the vice, the over-familiar freedom of speech (the facing Latin «loquendi libertatem»).
- **1420** · …the hidden life of prayer, manifest to God. — S. cf. Col 3:3; Matt 6:6.
- **1420** · …them it is also wont to be broadened. — T. The source of «φησί» is not identified.
- **1420** · …mind, long ago divided from its own dwelling, — S. cf. 2 Cor 5:2.
- **1421** · …well smoothed and softened, as that of children, — S. cf. Matt 18:3.
- **1421** · …been cast into the earth out of sight; — S. cf. Ps 125:5–6 LXX (126:5–6 Heb.).
- **1421** · …is the key of the kingdom of heaven. — S. cf. Matt 16:19.
- **1424** · …thee will I sacrifice the sacrifice of praise*, — S. Ps 115:7–8 LXX (116:16–17 Heb.).
- **1424** · …alone has peered into the Holy of Holies — S. cf. Heb 9:3; 6:19.
- **1424** · …the things concerning that which surpasses every mind, — T. Migne prints [οὐσίας] after ὑπερεχούσης, a word supplied by the editor; the English follows it. ("The essence which surpasses every mind"; the participle is feminine and has no noun without it.) The facing Latin reads it («circa essentiam … omnem mentis excedentem sensum»). Without it the phrase echoes Phil 4:7 (the peace «that passeth all understanding»). S. cf. Phil 4:7.
- **1424** · …practice well, prayer sometimes, like an overshadowing cloud, — S. cf. Luke 9:34; Exod 13:21.
- **1424** · …him who prays to this. *Of the same.* — T. A second couplet, between chs. 107 and 108, again under «Τοῦ αὐτοῦ»; the facing Latin prints it unheaded. Lineated one for one.
- **1424** · …It is necessary for the man of knowledge — T. ὁ γνωστικός, "the man of knowledge" (the facing Latin: «spiritalis … γνωστικὸν vocant»).

## Chunk 0003 (ca-003)

- **1425** · …through thought, reason passes to the rational things; — T. «Thought» (λογισμός) here is the discursive power, paired with reason (λόγος) and the rational things (τὰ λογικά); the facing Latin «ratiocinatio». It is the same word as the ascetic «thoughts» of the following chapters, kept in one word.
- **1425** · …all of the things that are by thought; — T. The Greek has «οὐδὲν οὔτε τῶν κατὰ τὸν λογισμόν», an «οὔτε» with no second member. The facing Latin has two: «nothing of the things that are perceived by sense or known by reasoning». A member («οὔτε τῶν κατ᾿ αἴσθησιν») may have been lost.
- **1425** · …thing is one thing, and its principle another; — T. «The mind (νοῦς) of a thing»: its intelligible content, the facing Latin «intellectus».
- **1425** · …completion, begging the one who shares with it — T. «τὸν μέτοχον», «the one who shares with it»; the facing Latin likewise has «qui sit particeps».
- **1425** · …for divine things. 115. If thou canst not — T. The Greek prints «Εἴ τε μή», which has no correlative; the facing Latin «Sin minus» («but if … not»). We read «if … not».
- **1428** · …that the eyes of thy heart be opened, — S. cf. Eph 1:18.
- **1428** · …will be reckoned above honey and the honeycomb — S. cf. Ps 18:11 LXX (19:10 Heb.); Ps 118:103 LXX (119:103 Heb.).
- **1428** · …the earthly heart every upgrowth of a wish. — T. «every upgrowth (ἀνάστημα) of a wish (ἐνθυμίου)»; the facing Latin «germen omne rei desiderabilis», which, with «sprouting up» (ἐπαναβλαστήσασα), might point to βλάστημα, «shoot».
- **1428** · …thy last things, and thou shalt not sin*. — S. Sir 7:36.
- **1428** · …see the glory of God with unveiled face, — S. cf. 2 Cor 3:18.
- **1428** · …the Lord; I shall exult in his salvation*. — S. cf. Ps 34:9 LXX (35:9 Heb.); Hab 3:18. The words are a blend of the two verses.
- **1428** · …the veil of self-love lies upon its heart, — S. cf. 2 Cor 3:15.
- **1428** · …heart, so that the foundations of the world — S. cf. Ps 17:16 LXX (18:15 Heb.).
- **1428** · …of vice, can the leader of Israel's people — P. Moses, the leader who brought Israel out of Egypt.
- **1428** · …forth his power of sight and of oversight. — S. cf. Num 13:1–3, 17–21: Moses sends the spies into Canaan.
- **1428** · …of that blessed land by hearing alone … — T. After «the good things» the Greek prints dots and no verb; the facing Latin supplies «perceperunt», «perceived». The English renders what is printed.
- **1429** · …it keeps what is according to the likeness, — S. cf. Gen 1:26.
- **1429** · …sense, if it goes down lightly and fittingly, — T. Migne prints [μή] before «fittingly» (προσηκόντως), a word supplied by the editor (his note: "I added the negation"); the English follows it. (The bracket is garbled in our copy; the facing Latin reads «et non pro eo ac decet», «and not as is fitting», and the sense of the chapter, the general who wastes his force, requires the negative.)
- **1429** · …130. Bare thoughts grow upon the solitary life; — T. «Solitary» (μοναδικῇ ζωῇ), the monk's life alone, against the «life of two» (δυαδικῇ) and the «much-divided» (πολυσχιδεῖ) soul; not the logicians' «singular».
- **1432** · …the thought of vainglory, which is the seventh, — T. Vainglory is the seventh of the eight evil thoughts in the Evagrian list (gluttony, fornication, avarice, grief, anger, acedia, vainglory, pride); here the «eighth» put on in its place is the heavenly dwelling.
- **1432** · …prayer angel thoughts are wont to come near; — T. «Angel thoughts» (ἄγγελοι … λογισμοί): a noun beside the adjectives «spiritual» and «of natural philosophy»; the facing Latin has «cogitationes, angeli» too.
- **1432** · …that surround it like spears; and the other, — T. «The other» (τὸ δέ, neuter): the purity of contemplation; the facing Latin has «oratio», «prayer».
- **1432** · …the other, the thoughts that philosophize in temptations, — T. «Philosophize» (φιλοσοφοῦντας): in the ascetic sense, the practice of the monastic life.
- **1433** · …made beautiful cannot fly and be at rest — S. cf. Ps 54:7 LXX (55:6 Heb.).
- **1433** · …filled with fruits Of spiritual practice and contemplation. — T. Two verse lines stand between chapters 137 and 138 (the facing Latin prints them too): a scribe's or compiler's note, not a chapter.
- **1433** · …of old commanded to offer in the temple. — S. cf. Exod 22:29; Num 18:12, 27.
- **1433** · …is self-control and humility, truth, temperance and humble-mindedness; — T. The facing Latin lists more: «continentia, humilitas, veritas, temperantia et castitas, sensus modestia et sobrietas». Migne's Greek prints these five as they stand; the Latin expands, or follows another text.
- **1433** · …virtue mighty in the good pleasures of kindness. — S. cf. 2 Thess 1:11.
- **1433** · …passions; the end of the soul of knowledge, — T. Migne prints «γνωστικῆς», feminine singular, taken with an understood «ψυχῆς», as in ch. 159 («πρακτικῆς ψυχῆς» … «τῆς θεωρητικῆς»). The facing Latin «gnosticorum», «of the men of knowledge», reads «γνωστικῶν», parallel to «πρακτικῶν».
- **1433** · …in prayer; but with a most excellent cup — S. cf. Ps 22:5 LXX (23:5 Heb.).
- **1436** · …contemplative, as into courts of virtues, with hymns: — S. cf. Ps 99:4 LXX (100:4 Heb.).
- **1436** · …vital powers of the soul: the incensive power — T. «Incensive power» renders θυμός where it is the faculty, as here, one of the vital powers; elsewhere «anger».
- **1436** · …sea and, as it were, its surging motions, — T. Migne prints «ῥοθίζας», and his editor's note calls the word corrupt («Quin sit mendum in illa voce, nullus dubito») and conjectures «ῥοθίας» or «ῥοθιαζούσας», «surging»; the English follows the conjecture's sense (the facing Latin «frementes fluctus»).
- **1437** · …it, it will have it as a thorn — T. Migne prints «σκώλωπα» with «[σκόλοπα]» after it, the correct spelling of «thorn»; the English is the same either way.
- **1437** · …part the glory of God with unveiled face. — S. cf. 2 Cor 3:15–18; 1 Cor 13:12.
- **1437** · …reckoned as a body with a governing spirit; — S. cf. Ps 50:14 LXX (51:12 Heb.).
- **1437** · …because of knowledge, in that of the contemplative. — T. Migne prints «διὰ τὸν τῆς θεωρητικῆς», which does not construe (διά with the accusative is «because of»; «throughout» is poetic only) and looks like «διὰ τὸν» repeated from the line above; the facing Latin «in ejus [domo] qui studet contemplationi». The English reads «ἐν τῷ», «in that» (house).

## Chunk 0005 (ca-005)

- **1452** · ……; — T. The chunk opens on the tail of a word (…κας) broken from the previous column; the facing Latin's clause ends *inebriant*. The fragment is left to the previous chunk.
- **1452** · …his own mind singular among the things understood. — T. μοναδικός = singular (house term), "made his mind singular"; the facing Latin glosses *singularem (unius quasi generis)*. «Monad» (μονάς) here is lower case: not the Trinity's word. The negative μὴ ὀφθείς / μὴ … ἐργασάμενος is conditional ("unless").
- **1452** · …who prays toward the things above and kindred; — T. ὅτε βούλεται τῷ εὐχομένῳ συνάγεσθαι: taken as "when it wills, to be gathered for the one who prays"; the facing Latin has *cum oranti libuerit* ("when it pleases the one who prays"). Migne prints νοῦν ῥύμην; the sense needs νοῦ.
- **1452** · …are being purified, sending the mind on within — T. The Greek line reads εἴσω ἔξω; the facing Latin prints *intra res [extra res]*, so Migne very likely printed εἴσω [ἔξω] (see the OCR table). The main reading «within» is followed; the bracket offers «outside». A foot scholion on «Εἴσω τῶν ὁρωμένων» stands on this page.
- **1452** · …in longing for the things after its heart. — T. Greek ψυχῇ, "the soul"; the facing Latin has *orationem*, "prayer".

I have added the particle 'de' and in what follows changed to 'kai': 'hoi de hedeos; hoi kai hedeos', because the whole structure of the passage plainly requires it, and so everything is smooth and clear: for these are two clauses following by a fuller explanation. Furthermore this is most salutary teaching: beginnings are to be earnestly guarded against; evil grows little by little and at last becomes insuperable; and the example is well known: 'the solitary wild beast' (monios agrios), 'a singular wild one,' that is, a boar, which is also called by us 'sanglier': a wild and solitary pig; which you may restrain for a time in its tender age; but once grown, you cannot, by any industry, restrain it from tearing down the vineyard into which you have admitted it. That in Psalm lxxix, 14, the same animal is signified in both halves of the verse, and that the 'monion agrion' is the 'sus ek drymou' (boar out of the wood), Bochart tries to show at length in book iii, chapter 29, vol. i of his History of the Animals of Sacred Scripture. So the Seventy certainly understood it in rendering it thus, although Cyril has 'wild ass' (onon agrion), as Hesychius too advised, and the Arabic rendered it in the same manner, and the Polyglot has it. Others more commonly render 'beast of the desert,' as Symmachus also, so that it is rather a generic noun, the Hebrew [ziz], signifying 'beast' rather than a certain species. The Chaldee: 'wild cock.' R. David: 'wild beast'; R. Solomon: 'reptile'; Aben Ezra: 'birds, fowl.' Quite differently the Seventy in Psalm xlix, 11, 'beauty of the field' (horaiotes agrou), 'species agri', although the Hebrew words are the same, and the whole sequence demands to be explained of beasts in general or of wild animals. To say 'form or ornament of the field, or of the desert' would seem very remote and plainly enigmatic. The scholion from Symmachus at that Psalm lxxix, 'creatures of the wilderness,' is not well referred in the Sixtine edition to the words 'sus ek drymou,' by which a definite species of animal is denoted, in Greek as in Hebrew, one very well known; but to the following 'monios agrios,' to which corresponds in the Hebrew that word of such wide signification, which could signify any animal; and which is the same as the Greek 'knodalon' (beast).
