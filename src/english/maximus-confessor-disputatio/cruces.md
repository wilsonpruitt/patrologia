## Chunk 0001 (md-001)

- **0288** · …the presence of Gregory, the most pious patrician, — P. Gregory, patrician and exarch of Africa, who presided over the debate (Carthage, July 645); he revolted against Constans II in 646 and fell in 647.
- **0288** · …conducted by Pyrrhus, the former patriarch of Constantinople, — P. Pyrrhus, patriarch of Constantinople 638–641, Sergius's successor and a defender of the Ekthesis; deposed in 641, he was then in Africa.
- **0288** · …the month of July, in the third indiction: — T. Read γʹ (third indiction, Sept. 644–Aug. 645, so July 645) for the scaffold's γιʹ (13); myriobiblos has γʹ and the Latin "indict. 11]" is most likely a misread *III*.
- **0288** · …by himself and by his predecessor in Byzantium, — P. Sergius, patriarch of Constantinople 610–638, author of the monenergist and then monothelite settlement (the Psephos of 633, the Ekthesis of 638). Combefis's Latin names him here; the Greek does not.
- **0288** · …Pyrrhus began the discussion with Maximus, speaking thus:* — T. The recorder's title runs on into the narrative that introduces the first turn; the Latin breaks it the same way.
- **0288** · …evil have we done thee, lord abba Maximus, — T. κῦρι ἀββᾶ, "lord abba": a courteous address to a monk. From here on each speaker addresses the other alone: thou/thee.
- **0288** · …words, honoured or revered me as you did; — T. "You" (ὑμεῖς) is plural: Pyrrhus and his predecessor, as in Pyrrhus's "we".
- **0288** · …aside the way of thinking of the Christians? — T. φρόνημα = "way of thinking" here and below (κοινὸν φρόνημα, the common way of thinking), by the Disputatio's own ruling; the Opuscula's "prudent thought" does not fit a confession's way of thinking. Latin *Christiani sensus*.
- **0288** · …having set this forth through a new Ekthesis. — P. The Ekthesis (638), Heraclius's edict drafted by Sergius, which forbade speaking of one or two operations and confessed one will in Christ.
- **0289** · …man, plainly he willed dually, and not singularly, — T. δυϊκῶς … μοναδικῶς: "dually … singularly" (glossary: μοναδικός = singular). Latin *duplici ratione, non una et singulari*.
- **0289** · …has been said, the same one was volitive — T. θελητικός = volitive (possessed of the power of willing); ἐνεργητικός = operative.
- **0289** · …illicit mingling and confirmed it with a blessing. — P. Heraclius, emperor 610–641, married his niece Martina, a union the Church held incestuous; Sergius blessed the marriage. Maximus makes Pyrrhus party to it as well.
- **0292** · …if sin, yet God incarnate did no sin, — S. 1 Pet 2:22 (Isa 53:9): «who did no sin».
- **0293** · …things of intellectual beings are therefore not compelled. — T. The chain of acts follows the Opuscula: λογίζεσθαι reason, βούλεσθαι wish, ζητεῖν inquire, σκέπτεσθαι consider, βουλεύεσθαι deliberate, κρίνειν judge, διατίθεσθαι be disposed, προαιρεῖσθαι choose, αἱρεῖσθαι prefer, ὁρμᾶν have impulse, κεχρῆσθαι use. In the first list I read ὁρμῶμεν for printed ὁρῶμεν (see OCR table), since the second list, and the Latin *aggredimur* in both, have impulse.
- **0293** · …the third chapter against the censures of Theodoret, — S/P. Cyril of Alexandria, *Apologia against Theodoret*, defence of the third anathematism (cf.). Theodoret of Cyrrhus (d. c. 466) had attacked Cyril's twelve anathematisms.
- **0296** · …«is the truth for us, but in things». — S. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 29 (*Theological Oration* 3), 13 (cf.).
- **0296** · …from the Father in respect of the wills, — T. Greek θελήμασι τοῦ Πατρός: θελήμασι is the dative of respect (the plural "wills" are the two wills of Christ, in respect of which Pyrrhus is said to separate him), and τοῦ Πατρός is the genitive of separation, after χωρίζετε; so not "the wills of the Father" (blind read R1, 2026-11-15). The Latin has *a Patre voluntatis jure*.
- **0296** · …the hypostasis of those same . — T. Migne's Greek omits a clause here (confirmed on the plate): it prints only ὑπόστασις ἦν ὁ αὐτὸς ἀσυγχύτως τῶν αὐτῶν φυσικῶν, an eye-skip in the print from τῶν αὐτῶν to τῶν αὐτῶν (lost: … φύσεων, οὕτω καὶ ἕνωσις ἀδιαιρέτως τῶν αὐτῶν …). The bracketed words are supplied from Combefis's Latin, *suarum naturarum erat hypostasis, sic et unio indivise eorum quae ipsis natura competebant*.
- **0296** · …and another that from which the outrage is». — S. Leo, *Tome to Flavian* (Ep. 28), 4: *aliud est unde in utroque communis est contumelia, aliud unde communis est gloria*, cited in Greek.
- **0296** · …holy Fathers by the mode of the exchange. — T. ἀντίδοσις = exchange (of the idioms, *communicatio idiomatum*); ἐπαλλαγή = interchange.

## Chunk 0002 (md-002)

- **0297** · …the nod of the Word united to it? — T. νεῦμα: "nod", the monothelite image of the flesh moved at the Word's beck; Latin *nutu*.
- **0297** · …them and the recoil from what destroys them; — T. ἀνθεκτικὴ δύναμις: "a power that clings to"; ὁρμή = impulse, ἀφορμή = recoil (Latin *fuga*); συστολή = contraction.
- **0297** · …things, that is, sin, lie in us naturally. — T. δειλία: "fear" throughout this exchange (Latin *timor*), since Maximus distinguishes a δειλία according to nature, which is blameless; "cowardice" would prejudge Pyrrhus's point.
- **0297** · …it comes from a betrayal of the reasonings; — S. cf. Wis 17:11 (LXX 17:12): fear is «a betrayal of the aids that come from reasoning» (προδοσία τῶν ἀπὸ λογισμοῦ βοηθημάτων). Migne prints ἅτε δι᾿ ἐκ προδοσίας (confirmed on the plate); the English follows ἐκ προδοσίας, since δι᾿ stacked before ἐκ does not construe (Latin *ex proditione*).
- **0300** · …"the one nature of God the Word incarnate", — S. The formula μία φύσις τοῦ Θεοῦ Λόγου σεσαρκωμένη: Cyril of Alexandria, Ep. 45–46 to Succensus and elsewhere, received by him as Athanasius's (in fact Apollinarius, *Ad Jovianum*).
- **0300** · …contrary to the heretics? And the fifth synod, — P. The fifth synod: the second Council of Constantinople (553).
- **0300** · …the saints Athanasius and Basil and the Gregories — T. "The Gregories" (Γρηγορίων, plural): Nazianzen and Nyssen; Latin *utriusque Gregorii*.
- **0300** · …Gregories and certain others, specifically defined as approved — T. Printed ἐγκρίτων with Combefis's bracketed alternative [ἐκκρίτων], "select"; both mean "approved, chosen". The quotation is the council's own list of the Fathers it follows: cf. Constantinople II, Session 1 and the Sentence (*sequimur per omnia sanctos patres et doctores Ecclesiae, Athanasium, Hilarium, Basilium, Gregorium theologum, Gregorium Nyssenum …*).
- **0301** · …set down, has also handed down two wills. — T. Printed παραδεδώκασι ("they have handed down") with the bracketed alternative [παρέδωκε], "it has handed down"; I follow the singular, which has the synod as subject, as the Latin *tradidit* does.
- **0301** · …of life. MAXIMUS. If the property of each — T. [εἴδους] is printed in brackets in the Greek as an editorial supplement; kept as [species].
- **0301** · …Diadochus of Photice defined self-determination as being volition. — S/P. Diadochus, bishop of Photice in Epirus (5th c.), *Capita gnostica* 5 (cf.): self-determination is the volition of a rational soul. ὁ Φωτικῆς = "the [bishop] of Photice". The Latin turns the definition round (*voluntatem … definivit esse liberam potestatem*); the Greek here and at col. 304 (τὸ αὐτεξούσιον θέλησίς ἐστιν) makes self-determination the definiendum.
- **0301** · …no less than the things declared by God, — T. θεωδῶς ("in a manner befitting God", i.e. by God): read τῶν θεωδῶς ἐκπεφασμένων, Scripture and the Fathers; I read γὰρ for the printed ἃ (Latin *enim*).
- **0304** · …the Lord has the human will by appropriation. — T. οἰκείωσις in its Christological sense: appropriation (Latin *attributio*, *arrogatio*): Christ makes ours his own. σχετικὴ οἰκείωσις = relative appropriation; ψιλὴ σχέσις = bare relation.
- **0304** · …if man was made according to the image — S. Gen 1:26–27.
- **0305** · …an illusion. Then, if the sentence of Sergius — P. Sergius's Psephos (633), the "sentence" that forbade speaking of one or two operations, and so of two wills (cf. note 4 of chunk 1 on Sergius).
- **0305** · …will which Christ figured in himself is ours? — S. cf. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 30 (*Theological Oration* 4), 12 (τὸ ἡμέτερον … ἐν ἑαυτῷ τυποῦντος). τυποῦν = "figure"; τύπος below = "type".
- **0305** · …as the best type and pattern for imitation, — S. ὑπογραμμός, "pattern": 1 Pet 2:21.
- **0305** · …to him as the author of our salvation, — S. Heb 2:10: «the author of their salvation» (τὸν ἀρχηγὸν τῆς σωτηρίας).

## Chunk 0003 (md-003)

- **0308** · …but wishing to make plain the utmost union. — T. Pyrrhus's sentence runs across the page and chunk break without loss (p0171.44 | p0172.01: … κακῇ καὶ πανουργίᾳ | τινὶ τοῦτο εἶπον, checked 2026-11-14); Maximus's echo below («Not with evil intent, or with any cunning») repeats it.
- **0308** · …If this be granted to those from Severus, — P. Severus of Antioch (d. 538), the leading theologian of the anti-Chalcedonians, whose «one composite nature» Maximus sets beside «one will».
- **0308** · …manner in which David too did against Goliath. — S. 1 Kgdms (1 Sam) 17:51: David kills Goliath with Goliath's own sword. Maximus means that the Severians will turn the monothelites' own argument against them.
- **0308** · …David too did against Goliath. See: saying one — T. The bracketed *καὶ γὰρ ἓν* stands in brackets in Migne (Combefis's mark, apparently a variant or gloss); kept in brackets as printed. The Latin omits it.
- **0308** · …appellation. PYRRHUS. They say it is of inclination. — T. γνωμικόν, "of inclination": the «will of inclination» (γνωμικὸν θέλημα), the scholars' "gnomic will". γνώμη is "inclination" throughout, by the glossary, even in the list of its senses below.
- **0308** · …inclination, as the prototype, will be an essence. — T. Read εἰ ("if") for the printed ἢ, with the Latin (*si*). The argument is grammatical: a paronym (γνωμικόν) derives from a prototype (γνώμη), and the prototype of a derived predicate must be an essence (Aristotle, *Categories* 1).
- **0308** · …as, that it is a mode of life. — S. Cyril of Alexandria; the definition (γνώμη = τρόπος ζωῆς) is not located; cf. Maximus, *Opusc.* 1, which cites it in the same words.
- **0308** · …it present to us by selection, or not? — T. ἐπιλογή / ἐπιλέγεσθαι = "selection / select", kept apart from προαίρεσις ("choice") and ἐκλέγεσθαι ("pick out").
- **0308** · …too would have been converted infinitely many times. — T. "would have been converted": the Greek prints the indicative μετέβαλεν without ἄν; the Latin (*mutata esset*) and the sense require the unreal conditional, rendered so. A short word may be lost (see OCR table).
- **0309** · …be spoken of. For by his very being, — T. Migne prints αὐτὸ γὰρ [αὐτῷ τῷ] τὸ εἶναι, with Combefis's correction in brackets; the English follows the correction (αὐτῷ τῷ εἶναι, "by his very being"), as the Latin does (*ipso quod erat*) and as the phrase recurs at the end of the Basil quotation.
- **0309** · …teaching in the interpretation of the forty-fourth psalm: — S. Basil, *Homilia in Psalmum 44*, 8 (PG 29.405), quoting Isa 7:15–16 LXX. Ps 44 is LXX numbering.
- **0309** · …present equally in all who are connatural? MAXIMUS. — T. This whole turn stands in square brackets in Migne, in both columns: Combefis supplied it. Its label is damaged (ΜΞ.) and its last word broken (see OCR table).
- **0312** · …the iron's gleam and brightness according to nature. — T. The plate prints ἡ τοῦ σιδήρου κατὰ φύσιν αὐγὴ καὶ λαμπρότης (re-read at the plate in the blind read, 2026-11-15; the first image check's "τῇ" was a misreading of a speck under the eta, and our OCR's ᾑ is the same speck): "the iron's gleam and brightness according to nature" is the subject of the understood "shine through", set against ἀποβολῇ. The Latin paraphrases: *ferri splendor ac fulgor, qui natura inerat, patescit*.
- **0312** · …as is plain to those who read carefully. — T. In this list of the senses of γνώμη the glossary word "inclination" is kept every time, because the argument is about the one name; ordinary English would say "judgement", "counsel", "decree", "opinion" in the several places.
- **0312** · …Against thy people they plotted craftily a counsel. — S. Ps 82:4 LXX. The «other translator» is one of the Hexaplaric versions (cf. Symmachus); not identified in the text.
- **0312** · …recounts in the first oration On the Son: — S. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 29 (*Theological Oration* 3, the first On the Son), 1. The Latin column gives *orat. 4*.
- **0312** · …of a man pious and possessed of mind.» — T. φρόνημα is "way of thinking" throughout the Disputatio (§ IX), not the Opuscula's "prudent thought" (§ VII), which does not construe in the list of senses or in Maximus's charge that Sergius "stood firm in no way of thinking".
- **0312** · …in holy Scripture and in the holy Fathers. — T. "twenty-eight meanings": the Latin agrees (*viginti octo*).
- **0313** · …reason then do they turn away from Nestorius, — P. Nestorius, archbishop of Constantinople (428–431), condemned at Ephesus (431).
- **0313** · …teachings, the Ekthesis championed by them also testifies, — P. The *Ekthesis* (see note 9, md-001).
- **0316** · …is nothing contrary to God, being wholly deified»: — S. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 30 (*Theological Oration* 4), 12. T. Gregory's word is ὑπεναντίον, Pyrrhus's ἐναντίον: both are "contrary".
- **0316** · …by consequence, both the deified and the deifying. — T. The Greek is elliptical (οὕτω … καὶ τὸ θεωθὲν καὶ τὸ θεῶσαν); "deification brings in" is supplied in brackets from the series, as the Latin supplies *deificatio*.
- **0317** · …the body touches; through both the passion flees.» — P / S. Gregory of Nyssa, bishop of Nyssa (d. c. 395). The saying is from his *Antirrheticus adversus Apolinarium* (on the healing of the leper); there πάθος is the disease (Latin *morbus*). "Passion" is kept as the glossary word.
- **0317** · …either volition, or wish, or operation towards something.» — S. Attributed to Athanasius; not located in the genuine works (cf. the pseudo-Athanasian writings against Apollinarius).
- **0317** · …to need its own weapons against the opponents. — T. "its own weapons": the truth uses the opponents' propositions and never needs its own. The Latin agrees (*suis ipsa armis*).
- **0317** · …wish as reasonable appetite, or volition about something. — S. Clement of Alexandria, *Stromateis*, book 6 as Maximus cites it; the definitions are not located there (cf. *Strom.* 2.17.77).
- **0317** · …or volition about something. And «operation towards something» — T. The chunk ends in the middle of a sentence (Πρός τι δὲ ἐνέρ-|γειαν …); it continues in the next chunk ("… this divine teacher said, because …").

## Chunk 0004 (md-004)

- **0320** · …rational soul united to him according to hypostasis. — T. The chunk opens in the middle of a sentence of Maximus's turn (the previous chunk ends Πρός τι δὲ ἐνέρ-); the whole reads: «And "operation towards something" this divine teacher said, because …». It continues without a label. Printed Ἁ γειαν is the band letter Α leaked into ἐνέρ-γειαν.
- **0320** · …For the volition was of the Godhead alone.» — S. Attributed to Athanasius; cf. the pseudo-Athanasian *De incarnatione et contra Arianos* and the works against Apollinarius; not located. T: Migne prints κοινότητος, "commonness" (confirmed on the plate), the likeness of the nature he shares with us, and the English follows it; the Latin reads *novitatis*, i.e. καινότητος, "newness" (cf. Rom 6:4).
- **0320** · …it needs no help at all from reasonings. — T. λογισμοί here = "reasonings" (the logical sense, § VII); in the quotation and in Maximus's paraphrase of it, λογισμοὶ ἀνθρώπινοι = "human thoughts".
- **0320** · …his natures, volitive and operative of our salvation. — T. θελητικός / ἐνεργητικός = "volitive / operative" (§ VI), here and through the whole proof-text sequence below ("volitive as man").
- **0320** · …of the Spirit which had wholly interpenetrated them. — S. Cf. Matt 10:20.
- **0321** · …the morrow Jesus willed to go into Galilee. — S. John 1:43, where the Gospel has ἐξελθεῖν ("to go forth"); Maximus's text has εἰσελθεῖν ("to go into"), and his argument (he "was not there") rests on it; the Latin agrees (*ingredi*). Not corrected.
- **0321** · …having tasted, he did not will to drink. — S. A conflation of John 19:28 and Matt 27:33–34.
- **0324** · …and prepare for thee to eat the Pascha? — S. Matt 26:17.
- **0324** · …unto death, even the death of the cross. — S. Phil 2:8. Maximus names the Epistle to the Hebrews; the Latin agrees, so the slip is the author's (or his text's) and is left standing.
- **0324** · …and under the hand», says the divine Gregory), — S. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 30 (*Theological Oration* 4), 6.
- **0324** · …must know that self-determination is not said homonymously, — T. The plate is read οὐχ ὁμονύμως λέγεται μέν, ὥσπερ καὶ ἡ φύσις· ἄλλως δὲ … ("is not said homonymously …, but is taken in one way …"), and the μέν … δέ supports the negative; the second witness omits οὐχ, and the Latin (*eorum … quae non uno modo dicuntur*, "of the things said not in one way") reads as if the word were homonymous. Migne prints οὐχ ὁμονύμως (confirmed on the plate), and the English keeps the negative as printed.
- **0325** · …conceived as prior in time to the readiness. — T. Migne prints ἐπινοουμένης (confirmed on the plate): ἐπινοουμένης agrees with τῆς ἕξεως, a genitive absolute: in men the habit is conceived in time, before the readiness, whereas in the angels the readiness runs together with the habit (blind read R2, 2026-11-15; the Latin *praeintelligatur* says the same, "the habit conceived before the readiness").
- **0325** · …been saved; if indeed the unassumed is unhealed. — S. Gregory Nazianzen, *Ep.* 101 (to Cledonius), 32: «the unassumed is unhealed».
- **0325** · …Now your house is left to you desolate. — S. Matt 23:37–38 (Luke 13:34–35). Jerusalem is *thou*; its children, *you*.
- **0328** · …reigning over the Romans, and of the senate? — P. Vigilius, bishop of Rome (537–555); Menas, patriarch of Constantinople (536–552). The libellus is Menas's statement of faith said to confess one will; the *secretum* is the imperial council chamber, the reigning emperor Justinian.
- **0328** · …marvel how, being patriarchs, you dare the lie. — T. "you … dare": a plural, addressed to the patriarchs (Pyrrhus with Sergius); the number marker holds, and Maximus returns to *thou* at once.
- **0328** · …displayed», having been read by Constantine the Quaestor. — P. Sergius (see note 4, md-001), Pyrrhus's predecessor; Honorius, bishop of Rome (625–638), whose reply to Sergius was read as confessing one will; John IV, bishop of Rome (640–642), whose *Apologia pro Honorio* Maximus cites below; Constantine the Quaestor, the imperial official who read the libellus.
- **0328** · …who composed it in the person of Honorius, — P. Honorius's secretary, who drafted the letter in Honorius's name; elsewhere identified as the abbot John Symponus.
- **0329** · …who speak things out of their own heart? — S. Cf. Jer 23:16 (the false prophets who «speak from their heart»).
- **0329** · …to Constantine of holy memory, who became emperor, — P. Constantine III, son of Heraclius, emperor for some months in 641; John IV's letter to him is the *Apologia pro Honorio papa*.
- **0329** · …the weakness of God is stronger than men.» — S. 1 Cor 1:24–25.
- **0329** · …mere man, disposed deliberatively in our way , — T. Migne prints the alternative [διακείμενον] in brackets beside διατιθέμενον; kept in brackets as printed.
- **0329** · …who call it of the economy, the … — T. The chunk ends in the middle of the sentence (… τοὺς οἰκονομικὸν αὐτὸ λέγοντας, τὸ,); it continues in the next chunk.

## Chunk 0005 (md-005)

- **0332** · …absurdity follows on that account, he brought in; — T. The chunk opens mid-turn: Maximus is still speaking, continuing from md-004, and the subject of "he brought in" carries over from there. "He" in "he was without will" is Christ; the Latin's *eos sequens, qui oeconomicam … voluntatem dicerent* belongs to the preceding page.
- **0332** · …and to split the holy Church of God? — P. The "papers" (χάρται) are the Ekthesis (see note 9, md-001), posted in the narthex of Hagia Sophia.
- **0332** · …who a little before became patriarch of Jerusalem, — P. Sophronius, monk of Palestine, patriarch of Jerusalem 634–638/9, whose synodical letter first opposed the one operation.
- **0332** · …itself: when Sergius wrote to Theodore of Pharan, — P. Sergius (see note 4, md-001); Theodore, bishop of Pharan in Sinai, an early monenergist writer.
- **0332** · …also the libellus which he says is Menas's, — P. Menas (see note 24, md-004). The libellus to Pope Vigilius ascribed to him, alleging one operation and one will, was later condemned as a forgery at the Sixth Council.
- **0332** · …the mediation of Sergius Macaronas, bishop of Arsinoe, — P. Sergius Macaronas, bishop of Arsinoe in Egypt, the go-between for Sergius's letter.
- **0332** · …the One-eyed, who was also of the Severians, — P. Paul the One-eyed, a Severian (anti-Chalcedonian) leader in Armenia/Theodosiopolis (Erzurum) with whom Heraclius treated for union.
- **0333** · …to George surnamed Arsas, who was a Paulianist, — P. George Arsas, an Egyptian Paulianist (anti-Chalcedonian sect of Paul of Beth Ukame) leader.
- **0333** · …letter the blessed John, the pope of Alexandria, — P. John the Almsgiver, Chalcedonian patriarch of Alexandria 610–619/20; the Persian invasion of Egypt was in 618–619.
- **0333** · …when he wrote back to Cyrus of Phasis — P. Cyrus, bishop of Phasis in Lazica, later patriarch of Alexandria (631), author of the 633 Pact of Union.
- **0333** · …him, with the humble-mindedness befitting his monastic estate, — T. σχῆμα: the monastic habit (Latin *ordini suo ac professioni*); rendered "monastic estate" to keep "habit" free for ἕξις.
- **0333** · …his monastic estate, rolling himself at his footprints, — T. Migne prints προκαλινδούμενος [προκυλινδι] (confirmed on the plate): the bracketed, truncated form is the edition's own correction to προκυλινδούμενος; either form means "rolling himself before".
- **0333** · …this, not having been established, has been annulled; — T. πάντα τὰ περὶ τούτου πρώην μὴ συσταθέντα: Migne prints the negative (confirmed on the plate), and it construes: μὴ συσταθέντα describes the whole of what was put forward, "not having been established" (blind read R3, 2026-11-15). The English follows the print; the Latin (*omnia a me pridem in eam rem asserta atque probata*) has no negative.
- **0333** · …called us to the knowledge of his truth, — T. ἐκάλεσε has no expressed object; ἡμῶν ("our intent") suggests Maximus includes himself with Pyrrhus. "Us" supplied.
- **0336** · …on account of the singleness of the person, — T. τὸ μοναδικόν = singleness (glossary § II), the property of being one individual, as against τὸ διάφορον, the difference.
- **0336** · …the persons are brought in, according to Nestorius; — P. Nestorius (see note 21, md-003).
- **0337** · …same essence are also of the same operation» — S. The patristic axiom, cf. Gregory of Nyssa, *Ad Ablabium*; Cyril of Alexandria, *Thesaurus* 32; quoted again at col. 348.
- **0337** · …which Moses too is recorded to have done, — S. Cf. Exod 14:13–15 (Moses speaks to the people while crying to God).
- **0337** · …in the case of the uttered word likewise, — T. ὁ κατὰ προφορὰν λόγος = the uttered word (glossary § VIII), the spoken word with its inward concept.
- **0340** · …shall ask you the same things as above): — T. ὑμᾶς is plural: Maximus addresses Pyrrhus with his party; hence "you".

'The acclamatory discourse to Vigilius here present'; that is, 'dedicated to him', which I too have rendered; and Sergius himself explains it at the end of the letter, where he relates that Heraclius, when he was staying at Edessa, had enjoined that the authorities of the Fathers excerpted from that libellus, on one operation of Christ and one will, be sent to him: 'Τὰς ἐμφερομένας τῷ γενομένῳ, ὡς εἴρηται, παρὰ τοῦ ἐν ἁγίοις Μηνᾶ, πρὸς τὸν ἁγιώτατον Βιγίλιον δογματικῷ, περὶ μιᾶς ἐνεργείας καὶ ἑνὸς θελήματος, λόγῳ': that we might cull for him the paternal testimonies which are contained in the dogmatic book (as has been said) made by Menas of holy memory to the most holy Vigilius, on one operation and one will. So the acclamatory discourse, made for Vigilius and to Vigilius, is in Sergius the same as the 'ἐπαγορευθείς' of Maximus: 'dedicated to him'; and as to Maximus's denying that it is stated in that letter to have been presented, 'ἐπιδοθέντα', although the letter has that very word in its earlier place: 'προσφωνηθέντα καὶ ἐπιδοθέντα παρ᾽ αὐτοῦ ἐνταῦθα παρόντι Βιγιλίῳ' (Directing also the acclamatory discourse of Menas, of most holy memory, once patriarch of this God-preserved City, presented by him here to Vigilius of holy memory), one of two things we must say: either that Maximus slipped in memory, or that Sergius's letter is corrupted. And first, in a matter so grave and present, to ascribe it to an accurate man is harsher, especially as his adversary acquiesces, although he too confesses he does not retain it in memory. That that word is spurious in Sergius's letter can be an indication that, in repeating it at the end, he makes no mention of it, but has one word answering to that earlier one, as was said, 'γενομένῳ'. The one, then, says published and dedicated; the other says also presented, read and made public, or presented, in solemn assembly before prince and senate; discrepancies which Maximus argues, namely as though Sergius would not have kept silent, if these things too had been done, so as to show that the libellus was received by Vigilius and approved by him: which Pyrrhus was striving after, with this plagiarism of his detected by Maximus; whether otherwise the book of Menas is genuine, or was forged by the Monothelites and attributed to him. Turrianus, in that he renders 'τὸ ὑπηγορεύθη' as 'commonefactum de illo libello', is far from the good faith of an honest interpreter.

Jacques Goar, ὁ μακαρίτης, in his Theophanes could have treated more mildly what I, almost a beginner, with no different spirit in this matter, had written in my [treatise] on the heresy of the Monothelites.
