⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1308] the greater. ⟦s2⟧ Therefore, since through the transgression the law of sin, which is pleasure according to sense, was commixed with the flesh, on account of which the death of the flesh through toils was sentenced, devised for the destruction of the law of the flesh, he who has discerned that death entered in on account of sin for its destruction always has his soul rejoicing, as it beholds through manifold toils the law of sin passing away from his own flesh, for the reception of the blessed life to come in spirit, which it is not possible to attain otherwise, unless the law of sin has first been emptied, as from some vessel, out of the flesh in the present life, according to the relation of the inclination towards it.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 10. ⟦s2⟧ He who in virtue is grieved according to the flesh because of the toils rejoices in virtue itself according to the soul, as beholding as present the comeliness of the things to come; for the sake of which, according to the great David, [n: Psal. xliii, 22.] by the passing-away of the flesh by inclination, he dies every day, he who is always renewed according to the coming-to-be of the soul in spirit, inasmuch as he has both the pleasure that saves and the grief that profits. ⟦s3⟧ For we call grief not that which is unreasonable and belongs to the many, tormenting the soul at the deprivation of passions or of material things, as of those who make their impulses contrary to nature towards what they should not, and their flights from what they should not; but that which is reasoned, and approved by those wise in divine things, and signifying the present evil. ⟦s3⟧ For they say that grief is a present evil, formed according to the soul when pleasure according to sense prevails over rational discernment, and subsisting according to sense when the course of the soul according to virtue is accomplished unhindered; this course bringing toils upon sense in the same measure as it produces pleasure and joy in the soul, which is brought near to God through the kindred illumination according to virtue and knowledge.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 11. ⟦s2⟧ He calls saving pleasure the joy of the soul at virtue, and profitable grief the pain of the flesh for virtue. ⟦s3⟧ He, then, who clings to passions and things makes his impulses towards what he should not; and he who does not welcome the occurrences productive of the deprivation of passions and things makes his flights from what he should not.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 12. ⟦s2⟧ Neither does divine grace work illuminations of knowledge if there is not that which receives the illumination according to natural power; nor does that which is receptive work the illumination of knowledge without the grace that furnishes it.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 13. ⟦s2⟧ Neither does the grace of the all-holy Spirit work wisdom in the saints without the mind that receives it; nor knowledge without the receptive power of reason; nor faith without the full assurance, according to mind and reason, of the things to come and for the present unclear to all; nor gifts of healings without the love for man according to nature; nor any other of the remaining gifts of grace [1309] without the habit and power receptive of each; nor again will man acquire a single one of those enumerated by natural power, without the divine power that furnishes them. ⟦s3⟧ And all the saints show this plainly, seeking, after the revelations of divine things, the principles of what was revealed.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 14. ⟦s2⟧ He who asks without passion receives the grace of being able to work the virtues in practice; and he who seeks passionlessly finds, in natural contemplation, the truth in beings; and he who knocks passionlessly at the door of knowledge will enter without hindrance into the hidden grace of mystical theology. [n: Matth. vii, 8.]

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 15. ⟦s2⟧ He who seeks divine things passionlessly will surely receive what is sought. ⟦s3⟧ But he who seeks with some passion will fail of what is sought. ⟦s4⟧ For it says, *You ask, and receive not, because you ask amiss.* [n: Jac. iv, 3.]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 16. ⟦s2⟧ The Holy Spirit seeks in us the knowledge of beings, and searches; but he does not seek what is sought for himself, since he is God and beyond all knowledge, but for us who need knowledge; just as indeed the Word too becomes flesh, not for himself, but accomplishing for us the mystery through the flesh. ⟦s3⟧ For as without flesh noetically ensouled the Word did not operate, in a manner befitting God, the things of the flesh according to nature, so neither does the Holy Spirit work in the saints the knowledge of the mysteries without the power that according to nature seeks and searches for knowledge.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 17. ⟦s2⟧ As it is not possible for the eye to apprehend sensible things without the light of the sun, so without spiritual light the human mind would never receive spiritual contemplation. ⟦s3⟧ For sensible light by nature illumines sense for the apprehension of bodies; but spiritual light shines upon the mind for contemplation, for the comprehension of the things beyond sense.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 18. ⟦s2+3⟧ The seeking and searching powers for divine things human nature has essentially laid down in it by the Creator at its very passage into being; but the revelations of divine things the power of the all-holy Spirit, coming upon it, makes according to grace. ⟦s4⟧ But since in the beginning, through sin, the evil one nailed these powers to the nature of visible things, and *there was none that understood or sought out God*, [n: Psal. xiii, 2; lii, 3.] all who partook of the nature having the noetic and rational power circumscribed by the surface of sensible things, and possessing no notion of the things beyond sense, fittingly the grace of the all-holy Spirit, for those who had not come under the deceit by intent and inwardly, unnailing from material things the power that had been nailed, [1312] restored it; which, having received it back pure through grace, they first sought and searched, and so sought out and searched out, [n: I. Petr. i, 10.] through the same grace, namely, of the Spirit.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 19. ⟦s2⟧ The salvation of souls, namely, is properly the end of faith. ⟦s3⟧ And the end of faith is the true revelation of what is believed. ⟦s4⟧ And the true revelation of what is believed is the ineffable interpenetration of what is believed, according to the analogy of the faith in each. ⟦s5⟧ And the interpenetration of what is believed is the return of those who have believed to the beginning according to the end. ⟦s6⟧ And the return of those who have believed to their own beginning according to the end is the fulfilment of aspiration. ⟦s7⟧ And the fulfilment of aspiration is the ever-moving rest of those who aspire around the object of aspiration. ⟦s8⟧ And the ever-moving rest of those who aspire around the object of aspiration is the continual and unintermittent enjoyment of the object of aspiration. ⟦s9⟧ And the continual and unintermittent enjoyment of the object of aspiration is the participation in the divine things above nature. ⟦s10⟧ And the participation in the divine things above nature is the likening of the participants to what is participated. ⟦s11⟧ And the likening of the participants to what is participated is the identity, possible through likeness, of the participants with what is participated itself, according to activity. ⟦s12⟧ And the identity of the participants, possible according to activity through likeness, is the deification, through likeness to what is participated, of those deemed worthy of deification.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 20. ⟦s2⟧ Nature does not have the principles of the things above nature, just as it does not have the laws of the things contrary to nature. ⟦s3⟧ By «above nature» I mean the divine and inconceivable pleasure which God by nature is wont to produce when united according to grace to the worthy; by «contrary to nature», the unspeakable pain formed by the deprivation of it, which God by nature is accustomed to produce when united contrary to grace to the unworthy. ⟦s4⟧ For according to the quality of disposition underlying in each, God, being united to all, as he himself knows, provides to each the sense, according as each has been formed by himself for the reception of him who will surely be united to all at the end of the ages.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1313] 21. ⟦s2⟧ Those who seek out in spirit the salvation of souls, and search out the spiritual principles and modes of this salvation, the Holy Spirit guides to comprehension, not letting the power through which they are by nature apt to seek out divine things remain unmoved and without activity in them.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ 22. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ First one seeks to mortify sin to the choice, and the choice to sin; and so one searches how and in what mode one must mortify these to each other. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ And again, after their complete mortification to each other, one seeks the life of the choice according to virtue and of virtue according to choice; and so one searches how and in what mode one must create their life in each other. ⟦s4⟧ Seeking, then, is, to take it as in a definition, appetite for something desirable; and searching is a mode that accomplishes the appetite for the desirable.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 23. ⟦s2⟧ He who is to be saved must truly not only mortify sin to the choice, but also the choice itself to sin; and not only raise up the choice to virtue, but also virtue to the choice; so that the choice, divided as dead from dead, whole from whole sin, may not perceive it, and, living, may perceive living virtue, whole the whole of it, by an unintermittent union. ⟦s3⟧ For he who has mortified the choice to sin *has been planted together in the likeness of the death* of Christ; and he who has raised it up to righteousness has been planted together also *of his resurrection*. [n: Rom. vi, 5.]

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 24. ⟦s2⟧ Sin and choice, mortified to each other, have a double insensibility towards each other. ⟦s3⟧ And justice and choice, having life to each other, have a double sense.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 25. ⟦s2⟧ Christ, being God by nature and man, is inherited by us as God, above nature, by grace, according to the ineffable participation; and for our sake, in the form that is ours, appropriating us as man, he inherits himself with us according to the inconceivable condescension; whom the saints, having contemplated him beforehand mystically in the Spirit, were taught that the sufferings unto him for virtue's sake in the present must go before the glory to be manifested in Christ in the future because of virtue.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 26. ⟦s2⟧ The mind, moving according to aspiration alone, unknowingly, towards the cause of beings, only seeks; but reason, going over in various ways the true principles in beings, searches.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 27. ⟦s2⟧ Seeking is the first and simple motion of the mind towards its own cause with aspiration; searching is the first and simple discernment of reason about its own cause with some notion. ⟦s3⟧ Seeking-out, again, is the mind's motion of knowledge according to science towards its own cause with a fervent aspiration; searching-out is the discernment of reason according to the activity of the virtues about its own cause, [1316] coming about with some prudent and wise notion.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 28. ⟦s2⟧ The holy and divine prophets, having sought out and searched out concerning the salvation of souls, had the motion of the mind according to aspiration towards God ardent and fervent with science and knowledge, and the discernment of reason concerning divine things according to activity prudent and wise; those who imitate them seek out the salvation of souls with knowledge and science, and, searching out with prudence and wisdom, pursue the discernment in the divine works.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 29. ⟦s2⟧ The word knows the knowledge of divine things to be twofold: the one relational, as lying in word alone and concepts, and not having the sense, through experience, of what is known according to activity, by which we are governed in the present life; the other properly true, in experience alone, according to activity, without word and concepts, providing the whole sense of what is known by participation according to grace, by which in the lot to come we receive the deification above nature, activated unceasingly. ⟦s3⟧ And the relational, as lying in word and concepts, they say moves the aspiration towards the knowledge according to activity by participation; but that according to activity, providing through experience by participation the sense of what is known, takes away the knowledge lying in word and concepts.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 30. ⟦s2⟧ He says that knowledge is twofold: the one lying in word and divine concepts, not having the sense of what has been understood present in form; the other having, only according to activity, without word and concepts, the enjoyment in form of the true things. ⟦s3⟧ For since the word through knowledge is wont to signify the knowable, it moves the aspiration of those moved through it towards the enjoyment of what is signified.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 31. ⟦s2⟧ The wise say it is impossible for the word about God to coexist with the experience of God, or the intellection about him with the sense of God. ⟦s3⟧ By the word about God I mean the analogy, from beings, of the contemplation of knowledge about him; by sense, the experience in [1317] participation of the goods above nature; by intellection, the simple and unitary knowledge about him from beings. ⟦s3⟧ And perhaps this is recognized in the case of everything else too; if indeed the experience of this particular thing makes the word about it cease, and the sense of this thing makes the intellection about it idle. ⟦s4⟧ By experience I mean the very knowledge according to activity, which comes after all word; by sense, the very participation in what is known, which shines forth after all intellection. ⟦s5⟧ And perhaps the great Apostle, teaching this mystically, says, *Whether there be prophecies, they shall be done away; whether tongues, they shall cease; whether knowledge, it shall be done away*, [n: I Cor. xiii, 8.] speaking, evidently, of the knowledge lying in word and concepts.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 32. ⟦s2⟧ Truly it was fitting that he who by nature is the creator of the essence of beings should also become according to grace the very worker of the deification of the things that have come to be; so that the giver of being might appear also as the bestower of ever-well-being. ⟦s3⟧ Since, then, none of the beings knows at all either itself or another, what it is according to essence, fittingly none of the beings has by nature the foreknowledge of any of the things that will be, except God who is beyond beings, who both knows himself, what he is according to essence, and foreknew the existence of all things made by him even before they came to be; and who is to bestow lavishly according to grace on beings the knowledge of themselves and of others, what they are according to essence, and to make manifest the principles of their coming-to-be that pre-exist in him uniformly.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 33. ⟦s2⟧ The Word who created the nature of men did not create together with it, according to sense, either pleasure or pain; but he built into it a certain power according to the mind for pleasure, according to which it would be able to enjoy him ineffably. ⟦s3⟧ And this power, I mean the natural aspiration of the mind towards God, the first man, giving it to sense at the moment of coming to be, had, at its very first motion towards sensible things through the medium of sense, as pleasure activated contrary to nature; to which he who cares for our salvation affixed by providence pain, as a kind of avenging power; according to which the law of death was rooted with wisdom in the nature of the body, limiting the aspiration of the mind's madness moving contrary to nature towards sensible things.
