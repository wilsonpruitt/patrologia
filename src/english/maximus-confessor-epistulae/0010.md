⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0460] LETTER 12

⟦s2⟧ *Of the same, to John the Cubicularius, on the right doctrines of the Church of God; and against Severus the heretic.*

⟦s3⟧ Having reckoned it well not to hold back from announcing to you anything of what comes to me concerning yourselves, God-guarded ones — for this is what our law and lawgiver of true love, Love-itself, Christ, commands, teaching us to make one another our own through ourselves — I have made the present writing of a letter, to make known that in the month of November of the present fifteenth indiction there arrived here a certain Theodore by name, a cancellarius — well-born indeed, and not without share in liberality of manners, so far as I at least can conjecture from what is seen — bearing a letter from our common mistress, the God-guarded Patricia, inscribed, as he said, to the blessed servant of God, the all-praised prefect here, for the release of the nuns living here, both of the monastery of amma Joannia, the one from Alexandria, and of the monastery called "of the Sacerdos", who are of the heresy of Severus — Severus, who used against the truth such madness and abundance of falsehood as to suffice to trouble the holy Church of God even to this day. ⟦s4⟧ But to no one else, from no other at all of the officials assigned to serve the God-guarded Patricia there, did he bring so much as a bare syllable. ⟦s5⟧ Whence I too, with many others, wondered, puzzling within myself what the reason was for which my God-guarded master did not deign to make anything known to me through him. ⟦s5⟧ And much murmuring, as was likely — the thing that is wont to happen in such cases, as you know, master — did that letter cause among the faithful peoples; and it all but diminished the reverence in the catholic Church of God toward our mistress, the all-praised Patricia; and it would have shaken all who inhabit this country of the Africans, who had already been possessed by good preconceptions about her, had not the said all-praised prefect, using a method befitting his understanding, published everywhere that the letter was false and spurious, and pretended to be indignant against the cancellarius, and dealt as was fitting with those of the heretics, both from Alexandria and from Syria, who had been brought into the open on the pretext of such a letter: some he chastised with prisons, some also with blows, saying they were not to outrage the preconceptions of our mistress Patricia. ⟦s6⟧ For they insisted shamelessly, relying, as I said, on the letter, that she rejoiced in their doctrines; and [0461] they did not refuse, lying, to say it to all. ⟦s6⟧ Of whom Thomas also, their so-called bishop, happens to be one of those who come to her with free access and are greatly honoured by her — which above all troubled and scandalized everyone. ⟦s7⟧ So that I too, of necessity, being summoned then, went off, and refuted to their face those who had dared to say this, and persuaded those who had heard that by no means, by the grace of the Saviour Christ, had either she, or her consort who is now among the saints, been attached in any way whatever to heretics. ⟦s8⟧ And likewise many others of the most devout monks sojourning here, and especially the blessed servants of God and our Fathers, those surnamed the Eucratades, saying the same things, put the many outside such false suspicion. ⟦s8⟧ And, to speak simply, the said all-praised man went every way, having proposed to show her pure of every blame, and to make her manifest to all as faithful to God and honoured.

⟦s9⟧ But I have not yet become free of the uncertainty over this — I who above all make your affairs my own, the small and worthy of nothing, the affairs of you who are great and have been counted worthy of divine grace — still distressed about it, and not having anything I can judge and vote for. ⟦s10⟧ For knowing our common mistress, the all-praised Patricia, to have stood from her forebears on the unbroken rock of the faith, and to have been taught to breathe almost nothing else than the right faith toward God, the faith preached in the catholic Church of God, I cannot believe the letter to be hers. ⟦s11⟧ And again, seeing the said cancellarius pledging himself with dreadful oaths, and affirming that he had been sent here by her for this and this alone, I count it a fearful thing not to believe; and I stand in the middle, on the border between two thoughts, unable to find the one that prevails and can draw the soul's assent to itself. ⟦s12⟧ However, my blessed master, that I may speak the truth and declare all that I have been able to judge — and receive me, speaking out boldly from much pain of soul because of my love toward you — if indeed the letter on behalf of the said heretical women has come from her by the wish and inclination of you, the God-guarded — which I wonder at, and do not accept — I judge you not to be outside the weight of it. ⟦s13⟧ For I hear the divine Scripture teaching how Jehoshaphat, that pious and faithful king of Judaea, having allied himself with Ahab king of Israel, befouled with many defilements of idolatry, when Ahab once moved war against Syria, was the object of God's indignation and was charged by God, who spoke thus to him through the prophet: *Dost thou help a man hated by me, and hostile to the Lord God?* ⟦s14⟧ And if she did this persuaded by others, who perhaps suggested it, not even so do I side with what was done, [0464] calling to mind that Rehoboam, son of the great Solomon, who by preferring the counsel of the younger men to that of the elders cut away the greater part of his own kingdom; [n: III Reg. xii, 13.] and at the same time knowing that to lay hands on canonical matters is forbidden to those for whom it is not lawful, and especially to women, for whom the divine word has declared silence more fitting than breath. [n: I Cor. xiv, 34.]

⟦s15⟧ One must not, then, help heretics in any mode whatever, as heretics, even if it had been permitted to all to pursue all things without fear: both for the reasons stated, lest we strike against God unawares; and because it is not good to give them licence to parade in their own falsehood and to rise up against piety — lest, being brought into the light through us, like a serpent they should be able with the bite of deceit to shake some of the simpler from the safe footing of the faith, and we too be found, as we do not wish, sharing in the judgement that hangs over them for this. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ For thus at first the all-praised prefect, using his accustomed compassion toward them as well, went so far as to make them a gift of a house worth much gold for their dwelling, with a lavish supply besides of the rest of their needs; when he found them puffed up with the conceit of pride, he turned away. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ For at once, as if by a command, they began to gather, and to draw away the daughters of the faithful; to hide them and deny them to their parents, who sought them for a long time; and to dare to perform expiations and counterfeit baptisms. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ And having learned these things, he came to them often with much entreaty, and exhorted them at length to cease from such doings and to be united to the holy Church of God, and achieved nothing. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ At last, when he saw the evil now becoming great and unchecked, and the peoples of the faithful murmuring at what was happening, fearing lest something disorderly should befall them — the faithful multitude of the Romans seething with anger against them, and not bearing even the bare naming of the heretics — he judged it fitting and lawful that there should be an imperial counsel and inclination on this matter, and referred everything by report, together with the most holy archbishop and the most glorious notables here, to our most pious emperor and to the most holy patriarchs, of Rome and of Constantinople. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ And indeed, having received the serene letter of our most faithful emperor and the sacred letter of the most blessed patriarchs, ordering that all heretics who remained in their own wrong belief be driven from the country; and that the said women, if they were willing to come over to the holy and spotless and life-giving communion of the catholic Church, should keep their own coenobia untouched; but if they refused the truth and would not be persuaded to yield to the imperial decree, should be divided one by one, permanently, among the monasteries of the orthodox; and that their property, according to the law — [0465] the property on which above all they relied in lavishly maintaining those who led them astray — be made over to the public treasury: he did all zealously, as he was commanded. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ All the heretics from Syria, Egypt, Alexandria and Libya he persuaded, by the grace of Christ, to come over to the holy Church of God. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ Of the said women, those from the monastery of the Sacerdos, who had borne themselves senselessly toward the command of the pious master through their ingrained pride, he distributed among the hallowed monasteries of the orthodox, to fulfil what had been piously commanded; and some by entreaty, others also by falling at their feet, and becoming all things to all women for God's sake, he persuaded them — when at long last they had learned better what was right — to be united to the catholic Church of God, together with many other coenobia. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ Those of amma Joannia, who came over wisely, and very fittingly, with all joy, because of the truth of the Church's doctrines shining out before them, he restored, and to all, men and women, their own monasteries — having found a reward worthy of his greatness of nature in divine things: to be adorned with apostolic gifts of grace, and to be styled the genuine mouth of the Lord, according to the divine oracle that says, *If thou bring forth the precious from the worthless, thou shalt be as my mouth.* [n: Jerem. xv, 19.] Or rather, he obtained this great and wonderful work as a prelude of the divine recompense that awaits him, and a most faithful earnest of the glory with Christ.

⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ Not that I write this wishing the heretics to be afflicted, or rejoicing in their ill-treatment — God forbid! — but rather rejoicing and exulting together at their return. ⟦s16+17+18+19+20+21+22⟧ For what is more delightful to the faithful than to behold the children of God that were scattered gathered into one? [n: Joan. xi, 52.] Nor am I exhorting you to set the harsh before the humane — may I not be so mad! — but urging you to do and to operate good things toward all men with attentiveness and testing, and to become all things to all, as each has need of you; only toward joining in any way whatever with heretics for the establishment of their deranged opinion do I wish and pray that you be wholly hard and implacable. ⟦s23⟧ For I, for my part, define it as misanthropy and separation from divine love to try to give strength to error, to the greater corruption of those already possessed by it.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Exposition of the right confession.*

⟦s2⟧ But before all and over all, we must be sober and keep vigil and watch for the assaults of the thieves, lest we be despoiled by them unawares; and let us rather guard the great and first medicine of our salvation — I mean the fair inheritance of the faith — confessing it with soul and mouth, with boldness, as the Fathers taught us. ⟦s3⟧ They, following those who [0468] from the beginning were eyewitnesses and ministers of the Word, [n: Luc. i, 2.] said that the one of the holy and consubstantial, spotless and blessed Trinity, the Son of God, God the Word, the radiance of the glory and the impress of the Father's hypostasis, [n: Hebr. i, 3.] the Creator of all creation visible and invisible, the infinite, the unbounded, the invisible, the incomprehensible, the inconceivable, who both made and holds together all things by the sole nod of his will, he who is measureless in goodness, became man for us, and was incarnate of the holy, all-glorious, ever-virgin Mary, who is properly and in truth Theotokos; having united to himself according to hypostasis flesh from her, consubstantial with us, animated with a rational and intellectual soul, which did not pre-subsist even for the twinkling of an eye, but received both its being and its subsisting in God the Word himself; and that, being perfect God, he became perfect man: neither having put off being God because of becoming man, which he was not, nor having been hindered from becoming what he was not, man, because of having remained what he was and is, God; but, being this, he became that too, and, verifying each, confirmed the divine together with the human by divine miracles and human sufferings; and that according to his own nature and essence, the one he shares with the Father, he is uncreated, invisible, uncircumscribed, unalterable, unchangeable, impassible, incorruptible, immortal, Creator of all things; but according to the nature of his flesh, which is ours, the same is created, passible, circumscribed, containable, mortal: the same, but not in the same respect, because of the things from which and in which he has his being; consubstantial, the same, with God and Father according to the Godhead, and consubstantial, the same, with us according to the manhood; double in nature, that is, in essence, because, being mediator of God and men, [n: I Tim. ii, 5.] he must properly preserve the natural kinship toward the parties mediated, by being both; so that in very truth, in himself and through himself, having joined the earthly to the heavenly, and having brought to the God and Father the material nature of men, which had been set at war by sin, saved, befriended and deified — not by identity of essence, but by the ineffable power of the becoming man — he might make us too partakers of the divine nature, [n: II Petr. i, 4.] through his holy flesh from us, as through a first-fruits. ⟦s4⟧ Wherefore the same is known to be God and man together, in reality and not in name only; but not double in hypostasis, that is, in person, because he is one and the same both before the flesh and has remained so with the flesh, no addition having been made to the holy Trinity, nor subtraction, by the incarnation of the Word; the same having suffered in the flesh for us, but the same remaining always impassible in Godhead; so that our salvation also is in the death of the only-begotten Son of God — O the paradoxical and inexpressible mystery! — and the divine glory and identity of his essence toward the Father remains perpetually undiminished.
