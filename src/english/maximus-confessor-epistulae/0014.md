⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [0500] and God Jesus Christ, as the great evangelist John explicitly cries out; [n: I Joan. iv, 3.] which those who now war against the Church of God openly proclaim, denying the nature of the flesh by not enduring to confess that it is preserved and exists in Christ; and thus they do not shudder — the more pitiable thing, or, to say it more properly, the bolder — at putting themselves forward as heralds of the opinion opposed to God. ⟦s1+2⟧ But these men, having chosen to be so dangerously disposed in respect of the faith, reap [?] a fruit worthy of their own folly: to have acquired a refutation of their impiety that is easily detected by all.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A concise exposition of the acceptance of the expressions of the faith that are rightly confessed concerning Christ according to the catholic Church.*

⟦s2⟧ But we — with God be it said — who feed on the divine and pure pasture of the ecclesiastical teaching, having the glory of piety unassailable, make bold to confess before God and men, piously, all the pious expressions of the Fathers concerning Christ, receiving each with pious intention and fitting reasoning; just as from above and from the beginning, through those who became ministers of grace, we were catechized in the saving word of the faith. ⟦s2⟧ We confess, then, our Lord Jesus Christ from two natures, as composed of Godhead and manhood; and in two natures, knowing him as being in Godhead and manhood. ⟦s3⟧ For just as, saying "from two natures", we hold that he is from Godhead and manhood, so also, saying "in two natures", we fittingly signify that he exists in Godhead and manhood; as being separated, after the union, from none of the natures of which he is constituted, but both being and being known in them, from which he also always is. ⟦s4⟧ And again we confess him two natures united according to hypostasis without division, believing the same to be truly God and man, and that he verifies the names by the very realities. ⟦s5⟧ For as we believe him both to be and to be called Son of God and God by nature and in truth, because of the identity of essence with the God and Father, so also we say that he both is and is called Son of man, and man become, by nature and in truth, because of the natural kinship according to the flesh with her who bore him. ⟦s6⟧ For if we do not confess him two natures, as at once God and man, we shall be convicted of saying that he is composed of bare names and not of realities, and is not in truth that which is signified through the names to be. ⟦s7⟧ So that — by those very men's account — he who chooses to blaspheme could at last suppose, so to say, that what is worshipped and adored by us is nothing: which has passed beyond every excess of impiety.

⟦s8⟧ Wherefore, guarding ourselves [0501] against being brought under a charge of blasphemy, we do not cease confessing Christ from two natures, as from Godhead, as has been said, and manhood; and in two natures, as in Godhead and manhood; and two natures, as believing the same to be at once God and man; since we know him to be not only from these, but also these; nor these only, but also in these; as a whole from parts, and a whole in parts; and being a whole through the parts. ⟦s9⟧ For if he who gave being to the things that are also granted them the power for permanence, so that the species should not be commixed with one another nor be converted into one another, but each remain according to the principle by which it has come to be from him — so that, from the stability of the natural property of each, those who love contemplation and are diligent about divine things may conceive, as far as is attainable, the all-wise and knowing Maker and Marshal of the universe — much more would he confirm in himself his own infinitely powerful power: remaining what he was, and becoming what he was not; and not denying what he became, together with remaining what he was, by being both without deficiency and without change.

⟦s10+11+12⟧ And we say piously also one nature of God the Word incarnate — incarnate in flesh having the intellectual and rational soul — bringing in, by adding "Incarnate", the signification of our essence. ⟦s10+11+12⟧ For it is a periphrasis that shows in one and the same, by way of union, the two natures, through a name and a definition: through a name, "the one nature of the Word", which signifies the common of the essence together with the proper of the hypostasis; through a definition, the human nature, as the teacher himself says: «For what is the nature of manhood, except that it is flesh intellectually ensouled?» ⟦s10+11+12⟧ So that he who says "one nature of God the Word incarnate" shows that God the Word is with ensouled flesh, understanding the flesh to be something wholly other according to essence than God the Word — he, at least, who is the true and unadulterated disciple of piety. ⟦s13⟧ And saying "the union according to hypostasis", we both understand and confess that the union of the natures has come to be into one hypostasis; so that neither is or is understood at all by itself, but with the one compounded and grown together with it; nor again is it commixed with the other according to the essential principle, or has it suffered from the union, in any respect whatever, diminution of its natural perfection. ⟦s14⟧ And again we confess "the natural union" in the sense of saying "true", just as the holy Cyril himself both said and explained these expressions; and not for the destruction, after the union, of the two natures from which Christ is, or of one of them, as Apollinarius, Eutyches and Severus, following Simon Magus, Valentinus and Manes, have set it forth; as it is easy for those who wish to see, from the writings of Severus himself, through the agreement of the words, the [0504] identity of his doctrines with those of the impious men named.

⟦s15⟧ Likewise we say that the two births are of the same one Christ: the one from God the Father before the ages, and the one that came to be for us in the last times from the holy Virgin; and likewise we glorify as of the same both the miracles and the passions. ⟦s16⟧ We confess also the holy all-glorious Virgin properly and in truth Theotokos, as having become mother not of some mere man — even if formed beforehand, as in the twinkling of an eye, apart from the union with the Word, and deified by progress of works and by the summit of virtue — but truly of God the Word himself, the one of the holy Trinity, incarnate from her by an ineffable conception, and perfectly become man.

⟦s16⟧ These expressions, then, the holy Church of God, having taught her own piously both to say and to understand, has entrusted to be guarded by the divine and bright light of knowledge; exhorting, through both Prophets and Apostles, and through Christ himself, who by his own sufferings fastened her together and fitted her, to take up a life suited and fitting to the great profession of faith: Proving what is the will of God, the good and acceptable and perfect; and keeping watch unto it. ⟦s17⟧ For we have been taught that we need the best conduct no less than pious faith for attaining everlasting life; lest, building on the foundation of the faith things that do not belong to it, by wicked practices, as having outraged the calling and the grace of the adoption, we make ourselves liable, instead of joy and endless glory, to everlasting fire.

⟦s18⟧ Seeing, then, many ways of virtues, you blessed ones and sober lovers of the blessed beauty of Christ — ways given to men by the Lord of all, having one goal, and all arriving at one end, unto salvation — let us not neglect our own salvation, nor let slip the time given us for repentance, which it is impossible to attain again. ⟦s19⟧ But before we go, as the great Job says somewhere, whence we shall not return, to a land dark and gloomy, to a land of everlasting darkness, where there is no light, nor to see the life of mortals, let us be eager to propitiate God, each according to his own power. ⟦s20⟧ For in view of the variety and difference of the states of our souls, he, as good, has fittingly made many also the ways that lead those who travel by them blamelessly to the everlasting mansions; so that each, choosing the one suited to himself, and running the course of his life by it, may attain the all-glorious place laid up in the heavens according to our hopes.

⟦s21⟧ Therefore, as we have power, by the co-operation and grace of Christ, let us lay hold of our duties. ⟦s22⟧ Let us lighten with compassion the misfortune of the poor man, and let us not [0505] neglect to make his want more moderate. ⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ Let us not put off covering the nakedness of the naked, revering the garment of incorruption given to us. ⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ Let us suffer with those worn down in the affliction of prison and in weakness of body, and pressed down by the burden of exile, and let us be eager to make the hardships of others our own; so that, imitating, toward our own kind, him who Took our infirmities and bore our diseases, [n: Matth. viii, 17; Isa. liii, 4.] and honouring by our works his self-emptying, in love for man, for our sake, we too may be able to become worthy beholders and sharers of his glory; not practising these things for the sake of the glory from men, of which, after the present life, nothing subsists toward the reckoning of acceptance; but becoming wholly God's in truth, and serving him, as one who both is and becomes all things to all, [n: I Cor. ix, 22.] through love for man toward one another, let us both say and do all things by which we shall please him. ⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ For thus, by progress in virtue little by little, we shall be able — with God be it said — at some time also fittingly to pursue the divine philosophy, and to be transferred to the life above, having despised all things on earth; considering it better and more honourable to hold the last rank with God than to bear the first things of those on earth with the king below: in which, beginning to converse with ourselves, and having accustomed the senses of the soul, quietly, in stillness, to see creation wisely, we shall carry off the greatest gain — to be well guided through the turmoil and instability of visible things to the things that stand and are not moved; and not to remain among them, nor to endure being held fast in them; from which, hating the disorder of the one and longing for the firm footing of the other, we shall call on abyss with abyss; [n: Psal. xli, 8.] perceiving, through the mighty works in visible things, the principles in intelligible things, as far as is attainable to men; and thus, simply, through speechlessness, peering into the inmost sanctuary of the sanctuaries, we shall come to know our own worthlessness, being mystically taught through the oracles of the Spirit how, being earth and ashes, [n: Gen. xviii, 27.] we lay claim to divine glory, by the grace of him who was well pleased; and into what kind of blessedness what is by nature worthless and contemptible is refashioned. ⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ And thus, struck with amazement at the spectacles of the infinite, we shall gather humility, the strong safeguard of the virtues, God's greatest and first gift, which is the fruit of the true philosophy; through which, mortifying the causes of the passions — I mean the senses — together with the kindred affinity of sensible things toward them, we cut ourselves off from the world altogether, so as to be able to apply ourselves to intelligible things with an undisturbed eye of the soul. ⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ Out of which, or after which, the divine love, taking over the soul, now truly qualified by humility and henceforth become capable of receiving the image of the divine reflection, makes us friends instead of servants, bestowing the summary of the law, the: You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with [0508] all your soul, and so on, [n: Matth. xxii, 37.] in permitting none of the intellectual powers in us to reach out at all to anything else than, manfully, to God.

⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ Let us strive, then, while we have the time set before us by God, [n: Galat. vi, 10.] to bring to work the goal of the pious and Christian profession. ⟦s23+24+25+26+27+28+29⟧ And since Our wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but against the principalities, against the powers, against the world-rulers of the darkness of this age — the unseen tyrants and enemies, who do not join battle in the open, but entwine their wars in the hidden place of the heart — Let us take up the whole armour of God, as the divine Apostle exhorts us, that we may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having accomplished all, to stand; and having girded our loins in truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness, in which all the fiery darts of the evil one shall be quenched. [n: Ephes. vi, 12.] Let us persevere in all prayer and supplication, and receive the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, which divides — to put it briefly — the worse from the better, and teaches the pious the conduct fitting for Christians, which is the work of perfect discernment: exactness of the eyes, continence of the tongue, discipline of the body, a lowly mind, purity of thought, the abolition of anger, as the great Basil says somewhere; the mind's flight up to God; and to struggle against temptations for the guarding of precious things; to add more when pressed into service; not to go to law when robbed; to measure out love in return for hatred; to match endurance to persecutions; exhortation to blasphemies; to die to sin; to be crucified with Christ; to transfer love wholly to God, without relation.

⟦s30⟧ These things I have written, my blessed master, not being ignorant of your firmness of faith in Christ, and the dignity growing in him through the commandments; but wishing you to know of what kind and how great a struggle I have for you, ever burning up my heart with care; and I exhort you to watch the champions of the heresies more soberly, lest any of them, having beguiled you with the persuasive speech of falsehood, should be able to muddy the clear and living stream of the faith in you with the filth proper to the impious doctrines — which may it not be. ⟦s31⟧ For they possess a tongue that is a two-edged sword and a sharpened razor, slaughtering souls, and sending them on to the trap of Hades and the pit of darkness, and depriving them of the life in Christ through their deluded fair speech. ⟦s32⟧ But [0509] our Lord and God Jesus Christ himself, the only-begotten Son and Word of God and the Father, the Maker of all creation visible and invisible, who for his unspeakable love for man, as he himself was well pleased, accepted death for us, and rescued us by his own blood from the power of darkness: may he himself hold your right hand, and guide you into his fear, and fill you with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding; and persuade you to walk worthily of him, bearing fruit in every good work, and growing in the knowledge of his truth; and strengthen you with all power, according to the might of his glory, unto all edification and long-suffering; and, keeping you striving together, with joy and thanksgiving, for the gospel of his grace, deem you worthy of the portion of the saints in light, [n: ibid. 9-12.] by the prayers and supplications of our all-glorious Lady, the Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary, and of all the saints. ⟦s33⟧ Amen.
