⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ LETTER 14

⟦s1⟧ *Of the same, to the same, a dogmatic letter.*

⟦s2⟧ God, being good according to nature — nothing of what he naturally does is evil. ⟦s3⟧ But man, becoming good by diligence of inclination, is always shown rejoicing in the things God naturally does. ⟦s4⟧ For man has no other discernment of his being good than only his co-disposition toward what is done by God. ⟦s5⟧ For the making of good things is proper to God; to man, the co-disposition toward them. ⟦s6⟧ For a man's virtue is to show that what has been made by God is cherished by the disposition of his inclination. ⟦s7⟧ So then, if everything that has God as the cause of its own coming-to-be is good, then good, with reason, and the greatest of goods, is the union of those who stood apart in faith, since it is manifestly the work of God. ⟦s8⟧ And if this is altogether true, I know that you rejoice, beholding the scattered being gathered into one, being good as you are, through your co-disposition toward what is noble.

⟦s9⟧ I beg you, then, God-guarded ones, to receive as good men the bearer of this my modest note, the most God-loving deacon, my lord [0536] Cosmas, a man noble and understanding, and adorned with the manners that are according to God, and to enrol him among your own friends; and, if there should be need to lay a request on his behalf before the God-honoured pope, that he may recover his own rank of the diaconate in the Church in which he had been enrolled, not to shrink from it. ⟦s10⟧ For so genuinely has he accepted the pious account of the divine doctrines of the Church, as even to champion it ardently, and to know this as the definition and ordinance of piety: never to have refused to hold any of the things that Christ is after the inexpressible union, being at once, in the same respect, truly the same God and man. ⟦s11⟧ For he did not cast off what he was, becoming truly what he was not; for he is unchangeable. ⟦s12⟧ Nor did he alter what he became, remaining what he was; for he loves man. ⟦s12⟧ But he is wholly both, naturally, according to hypostasis, as one from both, and through his one self preserving both without change and diminution, as being truly these by union — the union according to hypostasis. ⟦s13⟧ For the union according to hypostasis, having its contradistinction against division, and not against the natural difference of the things united according to it, makes a complete abolition of the one and works a firm preservation of the other; of which number is the indication. ⟦s14⟧ Since no one could ever conceive a difference wholly without share in quantity, even were he the wisest in arguments and the hardest to fight.

⟦s15⟧ For so long as the parts of the whole by composition, from which the whole is constituted, are preserved without confusion, the natural difference to one another of the parts of the whole that are preserved by union is not unknown. ⟦s16⟧ And so long as the principle of the difference according to essence of the parts remains preserved in the whole, it is impossible that the quantity of the parts of the whole that differ from one another according to essence should be unknown — of which number naturally makes an indication, and not a division; because number is indicative not of the relation of realities, that is, of union or division, but only of quantity simply, and not of quantity in some manner. ⟦s17⟧ But if number naturally makes an indication of quantity and not of relation, he who piously uses number after the union only for the indication of the difference, preserved in Christ, of the natures from which he is, does not divide the union at all, but knows only that the natures have remained unconfused after the union; from which the one Christ and Lord, and Son and God, exists — incarnate, clearly, and perfectly become man. ⟦s18⟧ Therefore he also upholds after the union one nature of the Word himself incarnate, in flesh having the intellectual and rational soul, not ignoring the difference, but removing and, as it were, setting outside the division; that at once, through these very expressions, he may drive [0537] the confusion of Apollinarius and the division of Nestorius, evils diametrically opposed, from the divine court, and may become a pure worshipper of the mystery: confessing the same begotten from above, from the Father, before all the ages, and the same born below — by assumption, clearly, of flesh rationally and intellectually ensouled — from his mother, for our sake, in the last times. ⟦s19⟧ And for this reason believing the holy Virgin to be properly and in truth Theotokos, since, having conceived the Word himself in place of seed, she both carried and bore him, incarnate from her: the same operating the miracles; the same enduring by volition the experience of human sufferings; the same crucified, buried, risen on the third day according to the Scriptures; taken up incarnate into the heavens, from which, without flesh, he came down to us, as he himself knows; he who, as God according to nature, has no passage from places to places. ⟦s20⟧ For by nature he is infinite, and for this reason in no mode contained by the things that are, as being free of all circumscription; and by nature loving to man, and for this reason enduring by volition our fleshly birth, and becoming containable by what is ours: the same who will come with his own glory to judge living and dead, and to give to each the just distribution of the things prepared according to worth.

⟦s20⟧ The aforesaid most God-loving lord Cosmas having gladly accepted this confession, I too accepted him rejoicing, and, having come to know his simple disposition according to Christ, I preferred him to my own soul; and on the dogmatic chapters about which he asked, I have given him a concise answer in writing, which it is fitting that you too, God-guarded ones, should take and exercise. ⟦s21⟧ For you benefit him by your fine exposition of the text, filling up the sense of what was left out through brevity. ⟦s21⟧ And so much for this.

⟦s22+23⟧ I remind you, God-guarded ones, to watch and to pray, according to the Lord's command, that we may not be caught in the snares of the temptations that stand around us on every side. [n: Matth. xxvi, 41.] For if we watch and are sober, I know that we guard ourselves against the wiles of the demons; and if we pray, we draw the divine grace to our help, fighting alongside us and showing us victors over every opposing power, [0540] and delivering us from all error and ignorance. ⟦s22+23⟧ And this above all we ought to do now, when, even without the commandment, nature becomes the teacher of refuge in God, taking distress as its counsellor. ⟦s24+25+26+27⟧ For what is more distressing than the evils that now encompass the inhabited world? ⟦s24+25+26+27⟧ What more dreadful to those who perceive what is happening? ⟦s24+25+26+27⟧ What more pitiable or more fearful to those who suffer? ⟦s24+25+26+27⟧ To see a nation of the desert and barbarous overrunning another's land as if it were its own; and the gentle commonwealth being consumed by wild and untamed beasts that have only the bare shape of human form; and the Jewish people, rejoicing from of old in the blood of men, and knowing as the only divine good pleasure the murder of the creature; and for this reason raving the more, so as to become more conspicuous in the wealth of wickedness than all those notorious for vice; and thinking to serve God by the things God abhors; the only people of all the peoples on earth most faithless, and for this reason most fit for the reception of the opposing power; going before in procession, in every manner and disposition, of the sojourn of the evil one; and by what it does announcing the coming of the Antichrist, since it failed to know that of him who is truly Saviour; the hostile and lawless, hating man and hating God, and for this reason the more man-hating because God-hating, and permitted to revel in outrages against the saints, since the vengeance is near, that it may be punished the more justly, once its tyranny and insurrection against God have been made perfectly manifest through the facts; the people that is the patron of falsehood, the craftsman of bloodshed and the enemy of truth; the bitter persecutor of my faith, through which the polytheistic error was dissolved and the phalanxes of the demons were driven away. ⟦s24+25+26+27⟧ Through which All the nations have clapped their hands, according to the prophecy, and shouted to God with a voice of exultation; for the Lord is most high, fearful, a great King over all the earth. [n: Psal. xlvi, 2, 3.] The only-begotten Son, sojourning through flesh, was known to them mystically: Lord, as enjoining virtue, as on servants, on those who hold to the commandments through practice; most high, as provider of true knowledge to those who, by longing for wisdom, press on through contemplation to the apprehension of the mysteries bare of symbols; fearful, as judge and avenger of transgressions; king, as distributor to each of the things prepared according to worth; and [0541] great, as doing contraries through contraries above nature, and fashioning dispassion through passion and life through death, and by a paradoxical power, through his own privations according to the flesh, bestowing on nature the unchangeable habits of good things. ⟦s24+25+26+27⟧ And not even so is the senseless people ashamed, and does it cease persecuting faith and virtue. ⟦s28+29⟧ Which it plainly persecutes through envy, as having fallen from both and not knowing that it has fallen, limping on both hams, and wholly unable to rise up from the fall of impiety — or unwilling, to speak more fittingly; as ever betraying faith and virtue for the sake of pride and pleasure, as an apostate and foolish people and an undisciplined nation. [n: Deut. xxxii, 6.] Between which having divided his life, the apostate, full of lawless deeds, makes the one the mother of apostasy from God, the other the craftsman of hatred of man; so that both God and creation are outraged by him: the one despised, the other corrupted by the defilements of his practices.

⟦s30⟧ What of these things, as I said, is more fearful to the eyes or ears of Christians? ⟦s31+32+33+34+35+36+37+38⟧ A nation cruel and outlandish, permitted to lift up its hands in wrath against the divine inheritance. ⟦s31+32+33+34+35+36+37+38⟧ But the multitude of our sins has made these things happen. ⟦s31+32+33+34+35+36+37+38⟧ For we have not lived worthily of the Gospel of Christ. [n: Philipp. i, 27.] All of us have sinned, all have done lawlessly, [n: Dan. ix, 5.] all have abandoned the way of the commandments that said, I am the way; [n: Joan. xiv, 6.] and we have become beasts toward one another, not knowing the grace of love for man, and the mystery of the sufferings of God incarnate for us; and we have travelled pathless tracks, [n: Sap. v, 7.] ratifying, that is, by the disposition of our inclination, the relations to the passions of dishonour. [n: Rom. i, 26.] But let us watch and pray, lest our hearts be weighed down the more in surfeiting and drunkenness. [n: Luc. xxi, 34.] For surfeiting is the doubting about the word of faith in times of persecution; and drunkenness the casting away of the natural thoughts, through which the true discernment of the things that are naturally comes about, whenever from slackness the soul gives way under the assaults of temptations, and, darkened, suffers a tossing about the word, as I said, of faith. ⟦s31+32+33+34+35+36+37+38⟧ For if we pray and watch, we hold the more firmly our faith in our Lord and God Jesus Christ, beholding, and receiving by experience, the outcome of what he foretold; and we are not at all astonished, nor do we undergo a tossing in soul, as suffering contrary to the promise, but we hold the faith more firmly, seeing the Lord's prediction fulfilled through the facts. ⟦s31+32+33+34+35+36+37+38⟧ But if the outcome of what was foretold is wont to make a confirmation of faith, the more through what is happening [0544] we too shall confirm our faith, finding him who spoke to be true; and let us confess him God before men, dreading no death at all, that he too may confess us before the Father, [n: Luc. xii, 8.] and bring us, saved, through the good confession, which he himself began as a pattern of our good things, having borne witness before Pontius Pilate and confessed the good confession; [n: I Tim. vi, 12.] which may we be counted worthy to imitate, by his grace, whenever the time should call; not rashly leaping upon the dangers for the word of piety, but fleeing temptations as far as we can, both out of sparing for our persecutors and by the testing, according to full knowledge, of the natural weakness present in us; educating those who are infants, and awaiting the Word who calls; who, invisibly present in the contests of those who lawfully fight for piety, runs through the course of martyrdom, as he alone who is able to conquer sense and nature; since martyrdom is plainly a victory over sense and nature, through which there naturally comes about an insurrection of mind and reason against the passions, by which the longing for God is made firm, persuading its lovers, paradoxically, to cross over through death to the one longed for: before whom may we be counted worthy to stand, flashing with our confessions toward him, which may we also carry away with us as we depart hence and pass over to the endless world, by the prayers and intercessions of our all-holy Lady, the Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary, and of all the saints. ⟦s31+32+33+34+35+36+37+38⟧ Amen.
