⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ LETTER 16

⟦s1⟧ *Of the same, to the same.*

⟦s2⟧ When I was being tormented with many scourges of grief over what had befallen, through the calumny of those who do not fear the Lord, the noble guardian of the virtues, the lord [0577] George, thy holy letter, arriving in good time, consoled me, holy Father: on the one hand bringing good tidings of thy health, longed for by all; on the other making known the firm footing of thy right confession in the Lord Jesus Christ, our true God. ⟦s3⟧ This, confirmed by the testing of sufferings, has been transformed for thee into unchangeableness, since thou hast set at nought the misfortune of the present life, whose prosperity thou didst long ago hold easy to despise, and hast moored the whole longing of the soul toward the things hoped for alone; a longing which faith and a good conscience are wont to characterize: the one guarding the pious word, stained in no way by the falsehood of opinion; the other fashioning the life pleasing to God, brought to success by the keeping of the commandments. ⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ For of both — of word, I mean, and of life — there is need for him who has been zealous to prefer above all things the life lived together with wisdom; so that we may also piously proclaim the mystery of the unchangeable self-emptying, for our sake, through flesh, of the God of all things; confessing Christ in truth God and man together: the one being without cause, through himself; the other having become, later, for us. ⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ Neither putting off what he was because of what he became — for he is unchangeable; nor denying what he became because of what he was — for he loves man. ⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ For to him alone, who properly is, it was possible by an abundance of power to become, without change, without confusion, what he was not, and to remain properly both, what he was and what he became; at the extreme of his own hypostatic monad, which in its singleness admits no division, on account of the difference, naturally preserved in him after the union, of the things from which he was composed; since, united according to hypostasis to flesh intellectually ensouled, by the miracles he was shown remaining without change what he was, and by the sufferings he appeared preserving without alteration what he had become; and through both — the miracles, I mean, and the sufferings — he bestowed on us both the abolition of sin and the grace of deification.

⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ And let us honour the calling of grace by a conduct of life made splendid by the commandments, shaken in no respect by any pleasure or by pain of whatever kind; and let us show, by what we do or suffer, that we cling to God alone, who gave himself for us as a ransom and an exchange, [n: I Tim. ii, 6.] having entrusted to him our whole life, the present and the future: as being from him by the principle of our bringing-into-being, and existing through him by the mode of our conduct, and coming to our end in him by the mystery, according to grace, of the deification hoped for. ⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ And let us receive the ever-well-being through what now seems an "alas"-being, having shown, through what we suffer, our disposition toward God that lies hidden, altered in no way by the painful things that befall us.

⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ [0580] Let us not then lose heart in afflictions, precious Father, [n: Ephes. iii, 13.] knowing that affliction works patience, and patience proof, and proof hope, and hope does not put to shame, [n: Rom. v, 3-5.] having secure the enjoyment of the everlasting goods for which we suffer; but let us bear nobly what befalls us, looking to the author of our salvation, Jesus, [n: Hebr. ii, 10.] who, being true God, accepted, willing it, the death for our sake; so that, suffering, we may rejoice, having become planted together with him through death, [n: Rom. vi, 5.] leavened toward the resurrection of indestructible life. ⟦s4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ Since it is the wish of the divine law that we, doing good, should suffer unjustly, as disciples of Christ, for the condemnation of sin — the sin which the intelligible serpent brought in, having by deceit persuaded man to transgress the divine commandment and to prefer the temporal to the everlasting; whom we have received authority to tread upon, [n: Luc. x, 19.] guarding the guarding grace, which is kept safe by the keeping of the commandments; which keeping is achieved by those who love the Lord only by hardship of the flesh, voluntary and involuntary, with sober reasoning. ⟦s10⟧ Having nailed all thy desire to him, thou dost not feel the afflictions, holy Father, since thou hast the joy of the things hoped for overcoming the present affliction. ⟦s10⟧ May we all attain them, Christ our great God and Saviour [n: Tit. ii, 13.] distributing these things to us, as he knows, as each is worthy; by the intercession of his immaculate and all-holy Mother, ever-virgin, and of all the saints. ⟦s11⟧ Amen.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ LETTER 17

⟦s1⟧ *Of the same, to Julian the Scholasticus of Alexandria, on the Church's doctrine concerning the incarnation of the Lord.*

⟦s2⟧ With great joy and spiritual gladness the precious letter of my God-guarded master filled me, the lowly and sinful one, and with me, so to say, the whole holy catholic Church of God; bringing us good tidings of the firm and immovable inclination — his own and that of my master who is with him, the lord Christopemptus, the most wise scholasticus — concerning the right and pious and saving confession of the faith in Christ our true God; who became man for this reason: that he might gather the nature of men to himself, and bring it to a stand from being borne badly toward itself — or rather, from being at faction against itself and divided, and having no rest, because of the unsteady movement of the inclination in each one. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore, all of us giving thanks to God for you, most precious ones, make petitions without ceasing, sinners though we are, that you may preserve, unshaken and [0581] unmoved for all time, the saving confession of the faith; believing that the Son and Word of God and Father, having truly become man from men, for men, after the manner of men, without sin, was not changed from being God even in becoming man; nor indeed was he diminished in being truly man, having remained what he was and is and ever will be, God according to nature; but, being God according to essence and nature through himself, he truly became man without change, by way of economy, for us — by the assumption, that is, of flesh having an intellectual and rational soul. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore the same is God by nature and truly, and the same is man by nature and truly, as being, the same, in truth this and that without deficiency; inasmuch as none of the natures brought together into the union according to hypostasis in the mystery of his incarnation, that is, of his perfect becoming man, has in any way denied itself because of the union, but each keeps its own essential principle and definition — that is, its natural property — unconfused and untransmuted with respect to the other after the union. ⟦s5⟧ For the union does not abolish the difference of the things brought together into one hypostasis; rather, it drives out altogether their division part from part.

⟦s6⟧ Since, then, the things from which Christ is are preserved in Christ, undiminished according to nature, even after the union, how is it not just that the things from which Christ is should be confessed even after the union — as some hold, not seeing together how perilous it is and dangerous, and bearing every harm to the soul, to fight against what is plain, and to deny the truth that manifestly is; and that this is proper to those alone who do not expect resurrection and judgement at all? ⟦s7⟧ For to whom is the word of the truth not manifest and evident, as simple and unelaborate, and having nothing riddling at all? ⟦s8⟧ For if Christ is God and man in fact and in truth after the union, and is not said to be God and man only in name and appellation, it is plain that he who says and thinks Christ to be God and man together after the union has confessed, along with the names, that the natures whose names they are truly are — if indeed he does not think that the appellations are applied to Christ bare and altogether empty of realities. ⟦s9⟧ What absurdity, then, has the holy catholic Church of God committed, if it says that the things from which Christ is are preserved in Christ according to nature, by an unsundered union, without confusion and without division, even after the union? ⟦s10⟧ And what has it thought that is alien to the teaching of the holy Fathers, if it holds neither that the divine nature of the Word has been changed into the nature of the flesh, nor that the nature of the flesh itself has passed over into the nature of the Word because of the union, and teaches the faithful to think and to say so after the union? ⟦s11⟧ And what of the Fathers' tradition does it not rather confirm, teaching to think thus, and confessing one Lord and one Christ, and one Son, the same, and one nature of God the [0584] Word himself, who was incarnate — a nature incarnate in flesh having the rational and intellectual soul; taking the expression according to the interpretation of our holy and blessed Father Cyril, understanding that by saying "incarnate" the signification of our essence is brought in, and not denying the nature of the Word's flesh because of the union? ⟦s12⟧ For the things preserved according to nature, without confusion and without change and without division, by an unsundered union in Christ, even after the union, without any diminution whatever — it is altogether most fitting and most just that they should also be confessed after the union. ⟦s13⟧ For it is proper to true faith to confess what we have been taught to think. ⟦s14⟧ If, then, the things from which Christ is are preserved according to nature after the union, it is also altogether just to think that they are in Christ after the union. ⟦s15⟧ And if it is just to think that the things from which Christ is are preserved after the union, much more would it be at once most just and most fitting, and most urgent for all who take thought for salvation, to confess them; if indeed the proof of true faith is the confession of it that falls short in nothing. ⟦s16⟧ So much on this. ⟦s17+18+19⟧ For there is no need to say more, when one addresses the word to you, and to men who through grace are ignorant of nothing of what bears on the right faith of Christ.

⟦s17+18+19⟧ Concerning what you have written to me, most precious of all to me, I referred it to the blessed servant of God, the all-praised prefect, giving him also your precious syllables; and he promised readily to fulfil your every bidding. ⟦s17+18+19⟧ And I do not cease reminding him, until he bring your bidding fully to its end. ⟦s20⟧ And may the Lord and God of all things and Saviour Jesus Christ, who accepted willingly the death for us, and redeemed us by his own blood from the power of darkness, [n: Coloss. i, 15.] and gave us the commandment new, in our loving one another as much as we have been loved [n: Joan. xiii, 34.] — may he himself hold your right hand, [n: Psal. lxxii, 24.] and guide you into every good work, and direct your steps that are according to him; bringing you in the fullness of the fruits of righteousness into the wonderful place of his tabernacle, where is the dwelling of all who rejoice. [n: Psal. lxxxvi, 7.]
