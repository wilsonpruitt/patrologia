## Chunk 0001 (ps-001)

- **0857** · …teaching, when he burned* *Mesopotamia and Syria Soba; — T. Migne prints [erat Σωβάλ] after Σωβᾶ: the name stood as Σωβάλ before the editor's emendation (the editor's note b: «Sic emendo», after the codices in what follows, the LXX of 2 Kgdms and 1 Paral., and the Hebrew צובה). The English follows the printed Σωβᾶ, as does the facing Latin (*Soba*). The same line prints Μεσυποταμίαν, a misprint for Μεσοποταμίαν.
- **0857** · …and struck the valley of salt, twelve thousand.* — S. Ps 59:1–2 LXX (Ps 60 title, Heb.).
- **0857** · …and struck the valley of salt, twelve thousand.* — S. 2 Kgdms 8:1; 10:7 LXX (2 Sam 8:1; 10:7); 1 Paral 18:1 (1 Chr 18:1): the history behind the title.
- **0857** · …shall be changed* the present psalm is inscribed: — T. Migne prints [ἐπιγέγραπται] "has been inscribed" after ἐπιγράφεται "is inscribed"; the sense is the same.
- **0857** · …inclination and of choice which came to pass — T. γενομένην, "which came to pass"; the facing Latin has *futuram*, "which is to come". Translated as printed.
- **0857** · …teaching, when he burned Mesopotamia and Syria Soba*. — T. Migne prints [Fr. Σωβάλ] after Σωβᾶ: here the edition marked Fr. reads Σωβάλ.
- **0857** · …wont to be constituted in us, *burned Mesopotamia*, — P. Mesopotamia: in Greek "the land between the rivers"; hence the habit of vice "flooded" by the passions.
- **0857** · …that are contrary to nature. *And Syria Soba*: — T. Migne prints [etiam Fr. Σωβᾶ· et deinceps]: from here on the edition marked Fr. also reads Σωβᾶ. The editor's note b remarks that «seven», the sense Maximus gives the name just below, belongs to a different Hebrew word (שבע), not to צובה.
- **0857** · …by this age. For Soba is interpreted "seven"; — P. Soba: Zobah, the Aramean kingdom of Adraazar (Hadadezer), north of Damascus.
- **0857** · …the devil is wont to stir up nature — T. Φύσιν "nature" kept against a digital witness's Φησίν "he says": the facing Latin has *naturam*, and the pair nature and time runs through the passage.
- **0857** · …as Adraazar did the two Syrias against David. — S. cf. 2 Kgdms 8:3–8; 10:6–19 LXX (2 Sam 8:3–8; 10:6–19): Adraazar, king of Soba, and the Syrians he brought against David.
- **0860** · …and struck the valley of salt, twelve thousand.* — P. Joab: David's commander (2 Kgdms 8:16); for the victory in the valley of salt cf. 2 Kgdms 8:13; 1 Chr 18:12.
- **0860** · …the disobedience a place of *passions of dishonour*: — S. cf. Rom 1:26.
- **0860** · …through deceit, contrary to nature, with the tetrad — T. Migne prints τετριάδι with [τετράδι] after it; translated "tetrad", with the bracket and the facing Latin (*quaternioni*), since the sensible things are counted as four below.
- **0860** · …wast angry, and hast had pity on us.* — S. Ps 59:3 LXX (Ps 60:1 Heb.).
- **0861** · …it; heal its fractures, for it has tottered.* — S. Ps 59:4 LXX (Ps 60:2 Heb.).
- **0861** · …heart, giving us *the sword of the Spirit*, — S. Eph 6:17.
- **0861** · …the Spirit against the law of the flesh, — S. cf. Rom 7:23; 8:2.
- **0861** · …the bow; that thy beloved may be delivered.* — S. Ps 59:5–7 LXX (Ps 60:3–5 Heb.).
- **0861** · …him through the commandments, in the practical philosophy, — T. "Philosophy" here, as in Maximus generally, is the ascetic life; its practical part is the practice of the virtues.
- **0861** · …the contrary, through his manifold providence and judgement, — T. Migne prints χρίσεως («anointing»), a misprint for κρίσεως: the sense (beside «providence») and the facing Latin (*judicii*) need «judgement».
- **0861** · …love for man chastening us for our profit, — S. cf. Heb 12:10.
- **0861** · …mortifies the way of thinking of the flesh. — S. cf. Rom 8:6–7.
- **0861** · …soul unseemly to the passions. Therefore the Word — T. ὁ Λόγος: the Word speaking in the psalm; the facing Latin renders it *Scriptura*.
- **0864** · …error and from all the memories of sin — T. Migne prints F. ἁμαρτιῶν, "perhaps 'of sins'", after τῆς ἁμαρτίας "of sin"; the English follows the main reading.
- **0864** · …Sikima, and measure out the valley of tents.* — S. Ps 59:7–8 LXX (Ps 60:5–6 Heb.).
- **0864** · …*hidden from the ages and from the generations*, — S. Col 1:26.
- **0864** · …the economy in the flesh of Christ himself, — T. Migne prints a full stop after οἰκονομίαν; the participles that follow (βοῶν, διδούς) depend on «he speaks», so the English runs the sentence on, as the facing Latin does.
- **0864** · …crying out expressly, and giving to the world, — T. τῷ βίῳ, literally "to life", in the late sense of the world of men; so the facing Latin (*mundo*).
- **0864** · …the Only-begotten, he becomes able *to divide Sikima*: — P. Sikima: Shechem, the town of Ephraim; Hebrew *šekem* means "shoulder", whence the etymology that follows.

## Chunk 0002 (ps-002)

- **0865** · …to the habit of each, and a guarding — T. Migne prints [προφυλακὴν] "safeguard" after προσφυλακὴν "guarding"; the sense is the same.
- **0865** · …Scripture the body has been called a tent. — S. cf. 2 Cor 5:1, 4; Wis 9:15. T. Here σκῆνος, the "tent" of the body, against the psalm's σκηναί; one English word for both, since the exposition joins them.
- **0865** · …and Ephraim is the strengthening of my head.* — S. Ps 59:9 LXX (Ps 60:7 Heb.).
- **0865** · …Ephraim is the strengthening of my head.* Galaad, — P. Galaad: Gilead, the hill country east of the Jordan.
- **0865** · …king; Moab is the basin of my hope.* — S. Ps 59:9–10 LXX (Ps 60:7–8 Heb.).
- **0865** · …interpreted "confession". For confession made through prayers rules, — T. ἄρχει: "rules", answering "my king"; the facing Latin takes it as "begins" (*auspicatur*).
- **0865** · …and saying, *I confess to thee, holy Father*. — S. Matt 11:25 (Luke 10:21); cf. John 17:11.
- **0865** · …*I confess to thee, holy Father*. And Moab — P. Moab: the people east of the Dead Sea, sprung from Lot's elder daughter (Gen 19:37), whence the etymology "from a father".
- **0868** · …*Upon Idumea I will stretch out my shoe.* — S. Ps 59:10 LXX (Ps 60:8 Heb.).
- **0868** · …Idumea I will stretch out my shoe.* Idumea — P. Idumea: Edom, the land of Esau, "red" (Gen 25:30), whence "blood-like clay".
- **0868** · …knowledge of the one, sole and sovereign Word. — T. "Word": λόγου, the one Λόγος to whom the principles (λόγοι) of sensible things are gathered; the Greek keeps one word for both.
- **0868** · …Word. *To me the allophyles have been subjected.* — S. Ps 59:10 LXX (Ps 60:8 Heb.).
- **0868** · …were subjected to *the might of his strength* — S. Eph 6:10.
- **0868** · …his strength* not only the spirits of wickedness, — S. cf. Eph 6:12.
- **0868** · …of the Gospel, bowed down. And the allophyles — P. Allophyles: ἀλλόφυλοι, "men of another tribe", the LXX's word for the Philistines.
- **0868** · …*Who will bring me into the fortified city?* — S. Ps 59:11 LXX (Ps 60:9 Heb.).
- **0868** · …me into the fortified city?* The fortified city — T. πόλις περιοχῆς, literally "city of enclosure"; the exposition plays on περιεχομένη, "enclosed".
- **0868** · …is to say, as another of the translators — T. "Another of the translators": one of the other Greek versions of the psalm, which read "fenced about"; the facing Latin names Symmachus and Theodotion.
- **0869** · …who will guide me as far as Idumea?* — S. Ps 59:11 LXX (Ps 60:9 Heb.). T. Migne prints a full stop after Ἰδουμαίας; the LXX and the exposition («Or who…») make it a question.
- **0869** · …by analogy, the Author of coming-to-be is beheld* — S. Wis 13:5, the nouns in reverse order.
- **0869** · …body becomes a temple of the Holy Spirit, — S. cf. 1 Cor 6:19.
- **0869** · …the members of this earthly and perishable flesh. — S. cf. Col 3:5.
- **0869** · …thou, O God, who hast cast us off?* — S. Ps 59:12 LXX (Ps 60:10 Heb.).
- **0869** · …had pity through the bowels of thy mercy, — S. cf. Luke 1:78.
- **0869** · …not go forth, O God, with our powers.* — S. Ps 59:12 LXX (Ps 60:10 Heb.). T. The LXX line is usually read as a question ("and wilt thou not go forth …?"); the exposition takes it as a statement, God does not go forth with those who trust in their own powers, and the facing Latin prints it so.
- **0869** · …and ascribes his own salvation to the sword. — S. cf. Ps 43:7 LXX. T. ἑαυτοῦ goes with σωτήριον («his own salvation»); the facing Latin takes it with the sword (*gladio suo salutem ascribit*).
- **0869** · …tribulation, and vain is the salvation of man.* — S. Ps 59:13 LXX (Ps 60:11 Heb.).
- **0872** · …will set at nought those who afflict us.* — S. Ps 59:14 LXX (Ps 60:12 Heb.).
- **0872** · …in the highest degree, and entering wholly in, — T. ὅλος χωρῶν: "entering wholly in", with the facing Latin (*totusque immeans*); it could also mean "wholly making room".

- p. 0432 (numbered, marker illegible): I Reg. x, 19. — keyed to Davidem
- p. 0432 (lettered, a): Εἰς στηλογραφίαν. Assumit Maximus proclivi Græcorum usu, ut quasi ex titulo proscriptionem significet, seu damnationem (nempe malitiæ vitiique) factam primo in Christo, exque illo, ac per illum in sanctis. Potius tamen (hic præcipue) titulum victoriæ Christi videatur sonare. Habetur eadem vox Psal. xv, lv, lvi, lviii, ubi οἱ LXX sic reddentes, aliud videntur legisse quam modo Hebræa habeant : nempe מכתב, non ut jam legimus מכתם, quod Arabica voce rem absconditam significat, id est, servatu dignam, nisi etiam sic reddendo servandi modum explicare voluerunt, ut scilicet perenni titulo ad memoriam servetur, ac velut columnæ inscriptum. Sic sæpe illi, paulo aliter reddendo, non alias voces legerunt, sed ut eas ipsas, quas legimus, clarioribus aliis redderent, sibi indulserunt. Quidam instrumentum musicum volunt : alii insigne militare; cum ejus radix sit כתם, quæ auri optimi massam significat. Chald. exemplar, quod ipsum a tituli inscriptione non valde remotum, adjuncta præsertim voce εἰς διδαχήν, in documentum. — keyed to στηλογραφίαν — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): On 'Eis stelographian' (for a stele-inscription): Maximus takes up the Greek habit, in which the word signifies, as it were, a proscription or condemnation (of wickedness and vice) first carried out in Christ, and from him and through him in the saints. Yet the title seems rather to sound a victory of Christ. The same word occurs at Pss. 15, 55, 56, 58, where the LXX, translating thus, seem to have read something other than what the Hebrew now has: that is, mktb, not mktm as we now read, which in Arabic means a hidden thing, that is, worthy of being kept; unless by this rendering they also meant to express the manner of keeping, namely that it be kept in memory by a perpetual title, as inscribed on a column. So they often, rendering a little differently, did not read other words but allowed themselves to render the very words we read by clearer ones. Some take it for a musical instrument, others for a military standard, since its root is ktm, which means a mass of finest gold. Chaldee: 'exemplar', which is not far from the sense of the title, especially with the added phrase 'for teaching', 'for instruction'.
- p. 0432 (lettered, b): Τὴν Συρίαν Σωβά. Sic emendo, quod ita sequentibus codices habeant, non Σοβάλ, quod liquet non reddidisse τοὺς LXX, ut nec Hebræa habent, sed idipsum quod II Reg. et I Par. Ubique enim צובה, cum Sobal longe alia vox sit, aliudque significet, atque aliis prorsus litteris Hebraice scribatur, nempe שובל, uti et qua septem significant, שבע, mirumque venisse in mentem Maximo, sive ei, qui illi auctor etymi fuit, ut Soba septem interpretaretur; cum sit clare exercitus, militans, etc. — keyed to καὶ — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'Ten Syrian Soba': thus I emend, because the codices so have it in what follows, not 'Sobal', which clearly the LXX did not render, as the Hebrew texts do not have it either, but the same as II Kings and I Chronicles. For everywhere it is tsobah, since Sobal is a far different word, signifies something else, and is written in Hebrew with quite other letters, namely shobal; so too the word meaning 'seven' is sheva. It is strange that it came into Maximus's mind, or that of whoever supplied him the etymology, to interpret Soba as 'seven', when it clearly means 'host, army, warring', etc.
- p. 0433 (lettered, c): Λύων ἰσχύν. Nempe legit Maximus, seu qui illi præivit etymologistes הדדעזר, quasi a חדד, languidus, sicque scribitur, II Reg. viii; etsi I Paral. xviii, est ד loco ר pro tertia littera. Possit et a הדד amicum auxilium. Auctor explicationis propriorum nominum Biblicorum, per ר et ח legit. — keyed to ἰσχὺν — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'Lyon ischyn' (destroying strength): Maximus reads thus, or whoever preceded him as etymologist, reading Hadadezer as if from Hadad, 'languid'; and so it is written at II Kings 8, though I Chronicles 18 has a dalet in place of a resh as the third letter. It could also be from Hadad, 'friendly help'. The author of the explanation of Biblical proper names read it with resh and het.
- p. 0434 (lettered, marker illegible): Hebr. iv, 12. — keyed to spiritus
- p. 0435 (lettered, d): Ὠμίασις. שכם humerus est, ὠμίας· qui latis humeris est, Poll. l. iii, c. 30, unde sibi Maximus eam vocem habuit. Est autem humerus virtutis symbolum, quod athletæ eorum maxime firmitate victoria potiuntur, et quod sint ferendo onera. — keyed to Ὠμίασις — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'Omiasis': shkm (Hebrew) means 'shoulder', Greek 'omias', one who has broad shoulders (Pollux, book 3, chapter 30), whence Maximus took the word. The shoulder is a symbol of virtue, because athletes win victory chiefly by the firmness of the shoulders, and because burdens are to be borne.
- p. 0435 (lettered, e): Τὴν κοιλάδα τῶν Σκηνῶν. Videatur in vi potius nominis proprii accipi, quod ita singulariter vallis Tabernaculorum dicitur סכות, quod forte locus in Syria paulo humilior et populosissimus, ut Syria pro vastitate regionis, alia aliaque induebat nomina. Sic passim in regionibus alta et depressa dividimus; montana et convalles, quarum fere major fertilitas, ac proinde incolarum frequentia. Nec forte alia sit vallis Succot, quam vallis ipsa Salis, quam notiori nomine significare voluerit, qui titulum psalmo apposuit, vel Esdras, vel ipse David. Campestrium crebra mentio in Veteri Instrumento, ubi vel itinera narrantur vel prœlia. — keyed to Σκηνῶν — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'The valley of the tents': it seems to be taken rather as a proper name, since it is singularly called the 'valley of Tabernacles' (sukkot), which perhaps is a place in Syria somewhat lower and very populous; Syria, because of the devastation of the region, took on one name after another. So we commonly divide regions into high and low, mountains and valleys, of which the latter are generally more fertile and so more densely populated. Nor perhaps is the valley of Succoth other than the very 'valley of Salt', which the one who put the title on the psalm, whether Ezra or David himself, wished to signify by the better-known name. Mention of plains is frequent in the Old Testament, where either journeys or battles are narrated.
- p. 0436 (lettered, f): Κραταίωσις. Psal. cvii, 9, ubi eadem vox Hebraica, iidemque repetiti versus, est ἀντίληψις 'auxilium, susceptio, quam utramque vocem Maximus exponit; cum sit sola variatio interpretis. Ephraim quod πλατυσμὸς redditur, intelligatur ampla progenie secundum quod fructificare significat et fecundum esse. — keyed to κραταίωσις — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'Krataiosis' (strengthening): Psalm 107:9, where the same Hebrew word and the same repeated verses occur, has antilepsis, 'help, support', both of which words Maximus explains; the only variation is the translator's. Ephraim, which is rendered 'platysmos' (enlargement), is to be understood as ample progeny, according to the verb meaning to bear fruit and be fruitful.
- p. 0436 (lettered, g): Ἔντερον πατρός. באב simplicius a patre, quod incesto concubitu ex patre susceptus, Gen. xix, 37, mutato tzere in vau cholem. — keyed to ἔντερον — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'Enteron patros' (bowel of the father): the Hebrew (mo'av), more simply 'from the father', because he was conceived of his father by incestuous intercourse (Gen. 19:37), the tsere having been changed to vav-holem.
- p. 0437 (lettered, h): Αἱματώδης πηλός. אדם, rubere, rubrum esse, ruber, homo, terra rubri seu argillacei coloris, ex qua et nomen homini inditum, quod de illa formatus sit. — keyed to πηλός — **working translation** (a first reading by the apparatus reader, not yet reviewed): 'Haimatodes pelos' (blood-red clay): the Hebrew adom means 'to be red, to be red-coloured', 'red', 'man', 'earth of a red or clay colour', from which the name was also given to man, because he was formed from it.
