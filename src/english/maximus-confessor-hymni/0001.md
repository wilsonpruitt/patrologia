⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1417] *Hymns of Saint Maximus the Confessor.*
⟦s2⟧ (Daniel, Thesaur. hymnolog., III, 97 seqq.)
⟦s3⟧ *I. Hymn of thanksgiving and supplication to God.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ A meadow held me, wretched, [lat: the Latin has *beatum*, blessed]
⟦s2⟧ Of old, of the best plants,
⟦s3⟧ [1418] When, by the greatest God's
⟦s4⟧ Unspeakable providence
⟦s5⟧ And love of equal measure,
⟦s6⟧ I looked upon this light,
⟦s7⟧ Moulded of earthy dust
⟦s8⟧ And having received a spirit besides,
⟦s9⟧ [1419] Midway between earth and sky
⟦s10⟧ Set as a living creature, alas.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Shining by participation
⟦s2⟧ In the image of the great God.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But the serpent, the all-wicked,
⟦s2⟧ Creeping up in vehement envy,
⟦s3⟧ With ruinous counsels
⟦s4⟧ And ill-omened sayings
⟦s5⟧ Whispering dreadfully to me,
⟦s6⟧ Deceived me, alas,
⟦s7⟧ And out of the fair delight
⟦s8⟧ That was there he thrust me,
⟦s9⟧ And from the Father's orchards,
⟦s10⟧ Whose tiller, ah, he had set me,
⟦s11⟧ Banishing me utterly.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And it was a piteous spectacle
⟦s2⟧ To the august choir of the minds.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But since my wound
⟦s2⟧ Would bear no physician,
⟦s3⟧ The Moulder, skilled in every art,
⟦s4⟧ Seeing me with warm compassion,
⟦s5⟧ Strangely came down to me,
⟦s6⟧ In human form, purely,
⟦s7⟧ Taking from the unwedded
⟦s8⟧ Mary his birth.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thus he rescues me,
⟦s2⟧ Snatching from the wicked
⟦s3⟧ Dragon's hand,
⟦s4⟧ And snatching from the doom
⟦s5⟧ That was fated for me,
⟦s6⟧ Who from God's command
⟦s7⟧ Had heedlessly fallen away,
⟦s8⟧ For which he gave his own
⟦s9⟧ Body to a bitter doom.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he crushed its head
⟦s2⟧ In the waters
⟦s3⟧ Of the streams of Jordan.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And, of my sins,
⟦s2⟧ The dark shamefulness there
⟦s3⟧ Plunging under;
⟦s4⟧ But he once more, his shameless
⟦s5⟧ Tail swaying,
⟦s6⟧ Troubles my mind,
⟦s7⟧ Shaking it with motions
⟦s8⟧ That carry off into the pit,
⟦s9⟧ And trying to trip up
⟦s10⟧ The paths of my journey.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he goads, impurely,
⟦s2⟧ The force of unruly horses
⟦s3⟧ That carries to and fro.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Stirring my mind to lust
⟦s2⟧ After reckless shames
⟦s3⟧ And soul-destroying passions.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And forcing to fall in ruin
⟦s2⟧ The charioteer
⟦s3⟧ Who guides the reins.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he kindles a great blaze
⟦s2⟧ From fire-bearing darts,
⟦s3⟧ Which, evil-minded, he sets ablaze against me,
⟦s4⟧ Stirring the inward
⟦s5⟧ [1420] Fire in me, which is blended in.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But do thou grant me
⟦s2⟧ Thy hand, saviour of the people,
⟦s3⟧ Cutting out root and all
⟦s4⟧ The wiles of bitter Beliar;
⟦s5⟧ Thou who of old didst stay a stream
⟦s6⟧ Turned back most swiftly
⟦s7⟧ In a backward-rushing course,
⟦s8⟧ Achieving a strange work [lat: the Latin has *Cladem exterorum*, the destruction of the foreigners].

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou didst dry up the sea's
⟦s2⟧ Full-flowing flood,
⟦s3⟧ Bringing across on foot
⟦s4⟧ Thy people Israel,
⟦s5⟧ And thou didst speak, and all things
⟦s6⟧ By thy word were brought into being;
⟦s7⟧ And thou didst check the chariot of flame
⟦s8⟧ Of the swift-running giant,
⟦s9⟧ The greatest light-bringer.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou didst turn, in the sevenfold
⟦s2⟧ Furnace of the three children,
⟦s3⟧ The flame into deep dew.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou hadst mercy on the prodigal;
⟦s2⟧ Thou didst loose the harlot's knot
⟦s3⟧ Of evil-binding loves,
⟦s4⟧ As she shed hot tears.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thou didst stay, of her that flowed with blood,
⟦s2⟧ By a touch of thy hem,
⟦s3⟧ The ever-flowing defilement.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou didst raise from the dead
⟦s2⟧ Also the child of Jairus,
⟦s3⟧ And didst set breathing once more.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thou didst cleanse lepers,
⟦s2⟧ And to the blind didst give sight.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thou didst thrust out a spirit
⟦s2⟧ From the Sidonian girl.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Another took up his bed,
⟦s2⟧ Having gathered the slackness of his limbs,
⟦s3⟧ And the bent woman thou straightenest,
⟦s4⟧ And thou drivest out demons.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And of Peter's mother-in-law
⟦s2⟧ By a touch of the hand thou didst still
⟦s3⟧ The fiery heat.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thou didst ward off more besides [lat: the Latin has *egisti*, thou didst],
⟦s2⟧ As many as escape number.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What wonder, if thou cool
⟦s2⟧ The embers of my flame,
⟦s3⟧ From an ever-flowing bowl
⟦s4⟧ Letting thy dew drip
⟦s5⟧ Upon my coals?

⟦s4⟧ Endless goodness,
