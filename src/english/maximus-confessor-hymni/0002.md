⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1420] *II. Hymn of supplication to Christ, in the form of a confession.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Alas for me, for my evils,
⟦s2⟧ O King without beginning, Saviour,
⟦s3⟧ [1421] And for my mind's ill counsel
⟦s4⟧ And slavish constraint;
⟦s5⟧ And for the mischievous toil
⟦s6⟧ That I, being vain, took on,
⟦s7⟧ From childhood shutting
⟦s8⟧ Myself in dread fetters
⟦s9⟧ Of a life of evil ways,
⟦s10⟧ Of wanderings and of loves.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ I thought myself the best
⟦s2⟧ Builder of fair houses
⟦s3⟧ And of high-roofed towers.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But for the foundations I was laying,
⟦s2⟧ It was not firm rock
⟦s3⟧ That set a support within,
⟦s4⟧ But sand, which most easily
⟦s5⟧ The winds shake,
⟦s6⟧ And the streams from the springs
⟦s7⟧ Drag off with a roar,
⟦s8⟧ And the rain, falling on it,
⟦s9⟧ Shows soaked through.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thence the houses fall,
⟦s2⟧ And in ruins the roof
⟦s3⟧ Of the dwellings is piteously seen.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And what then were these
⟦s2⟧ That I, evil-minded, built
⟦s3⟧ To my soul's destruction?

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Stony was the heart's
⟦s2⟧ Will, in sins
⟦s3⟧ Ever resting at ease.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It, a strange, whitening
⟦s2⟧ Colour wearing outwardly,
⟦s3⟧ To the unknowing a festering
⟦s4⟧ Way of thinking put on,
⟦s5⟧ That hid within
⟦s6⟧ An unseemly face,
⟦s7⟧ Hypocrisy thou wouldst call it,
⟦s8⟧ Abhorrent to all.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And I kept house-slaves [lat: the Latin has *Damnosos … hospites*, ruinous guests],
⟦s2⟧ Who cared for glory
⟦s3⟧ Falsely so called, and for puffing-up,
⟦s4⟧ And which of old hurled the Typhon
⟦s5⟧ Swiftly from the sky,
⟦s6⟧ The darkened Morning-star,
⟦s7⟧ By God's just judgement.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And to them there were sons
⟦s2⟧ Numberless, and daughters,
⟦s3⟧ Who lent an obedient
⟦s4⟧ Ear to the behests of evil.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Then the rain coming
⟦s2⟧ And the winds blowing
⟦s3⟧ And the streams rushing in
⟦s4⟧ Shook it from the foundations,
⟦s5⟧ An ill-omened jumble,
⟦s6⟧ And the fall was strange to me,
⟦s7⟧ And for my soul a slipping;
⟦s8⟧ [1422] She gathered so great
⟦s9⟧ A harm to dwell within her,
⟦s10⟧ And dread anger from above,
⟦s11⟧ And the enmity of mortals.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But now I have resolved to lay
⟦s2⟧ The rock down as foundation,
⟦s3⟧ That the house may be kept safe,
⟦s4⟧ Having those stones
⟦s5⟧ Which are precious,
⟦s6⟧ With gold and with silver
⟦s7⟧ Spotlessly adorned,
⟦s8⟧ Riches to the godly,
⟦s9⟧ And the best of blessedness.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For what than august temperance
⟦s2⟧ Has been established better?

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Without which to behold
⟦s2⟧ God is not given to mortals.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Again, than humility,
⟦s2⟧ High-flying, what has been
⟦s3⟧ Better to those who know?

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Which true height
⟦s2⟧ Provides to those who possess it.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And than a pure conscience [lat: the Latin has *Consciente agno*, the Lamb being witness],
⟦s2⟧ Under the eyes of God
⟦s3⟧ Who looks into the heart,
⟦s4⟧ What will ever be better,
⟦s5⟧ Which, like to the angels,
⟦s6⟧ Presents a mortal [lat: the Latin has *Angelicum … Cibum*, angelic food]?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And a divine winging
⟦s2⟧ The mortal mind undergoes,
⟦s3⟧ If it is pure from above,
⟦s4⟧ With far-shining breaths,
⟦s5⟧ So as to behold the might,
⟦s6⟧ The greatest, and the root
⟦s7⟧ Self-sprung, and the breath
⟦s8⟧ Life-bearing in its blowing,
⟦s9⟧ And of glorious mysteries
⟦s10⟧ To tread the depths,
⟦s11⟧ And how much, under providence,
⟦s12⟧ Of good comes down to the world,
⟦s13⟧ And how much in times
⟦s14⟧ That are for ever there will be.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Who could name a thing more blessed?

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Such, O Saviour, do I,
⟦s2⟧ The best of buildings,
⟦s3⟧ Thou giving the strength,
⟦s4⟧ Upon the firm rock,
⟦s5⟧ The chief corner-
⟦s6⟧ Stone, which thou thyself art,
⟦s7⟧ Hasten to raise.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And mayest thou thyself supply me
⟦s2⟧ Thy grace as fellow-worker,
⟦s3⟧ To give these an end
⟦s4⟧ Such as may be best,
⟦s5⟧ And saving for me.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Neither violent rain
⟦s2⟧ Nor springing streams
⟦s3⟧ Nor furious winds
⟦s4⟧ Shall then be able to shake my house
⟦s5⟧ So much as a little,
⟦s6⟧ Built upon the foundation,
⟦s7⟧ Thee, Saviour, unshaken.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And mayest thou give me a hand,
⟦s2⟧ And be a fellow-labourer
⟦s3⟧ With me, and accomplish
⟦s4⟧ The whole, that I may hymn thee,
⟦s5⟧ [1423] Giver of the best things
⟦s6⟧ And their accomplisher,
⟦s7⟧ Which save mortals.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For apart from thee, O mighty one,
⟦s2⟧ Neither work nor counsel,
⟦s3⟧ Thought, nor utterance,
⟦s4⟧ Nor anything of all things,
⟦s5⟧ As many as are of the better lot,
⟦s6⟧ Shall at all attain its end.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou didst give me flesh
⟦s2⟧ And, breathing, a spirit too.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou also, raising me fallen,
⟦s2⟧ Didst lead me up skyward,
⟦s3⟧ Lifting me with thy flesh;
⟦s4⟧ Thou raisest me up, dead,
⟦s5⟧ By thy gifts of grace
⟦s6⟧ And boundless tender compassion,
⟦s7⟧ With which thou goest before me, Saviour,
⟦s8⟧ And deemest me worthy of thy flesh's
⟦s9⟧ Unspeakable communion.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thou wilt also save me at the last,
⟦s2⟧ If thou find not fruitless
⟦s3⟧ Altogether my journey,
⟦s4⟧ If I bear not a mass
⟦s5⟧ Of lead, weighing heavy
⟦s6⟧ And thrusting me into Gehenna
⟦s7⟧ And unquenchable fire,
⟦s8⟧ [1424] If not impure wood,
⟦s9⟧ Hay, and kindling for fire.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And freely, in those yonder
⟦s2⟧ Mansions thou wilt set me,
⟦s3⟧ Living eternally,
⟦s4⟧ A hymn-singer of thy glory,
⟦s5⟧ With the undefiled minds
⟦s6⟧ And all the just together.
