# Cruces — maximus-confessor-hymni

Translators' notes (kt method, 2026-11-15), moved out of the running English. Each is keyed by chunk, column and band, and quotes the English words it follows. Plate checks: `translate/maximus-hymni/PLATE-CHECKS.tsv`. Glossary: `translate/maximus-hymni/GLOSSARY.md`. Where the facing Latin verse departs in sense from the Greek, the English carries a `[lat: …]` mark; its ordinary freedoms are not marked.


## Chunk 0001 (hy-001)

- **1417** · …A meadow held me, wretched, — T. τλήμον(α), "wretched", said of the speaker looking back. The Latin has *beatum*, "blessed": a departure of sense, so `[lat: beatum]` stands here (V7). This is the model case; the Latin's reorderings and expansions elsewhere take no mark.
- **1419A** · …Shining by participation — T. V1 at work: the Greek's two lines are "Shining, of the great God / by participation in the image"; English wants the genitive last, so *of the great God* goes down one line and nothing leaves the sentence. μεθέξει is a noun, so "by participation" (M1).
- **1419A** · …And out of the fair delight — T. τῆς ἐκεῖθεν … καλῆς τρυφῆς, hyperbaton across the line: "the fair delight that was there". τρυφή is Eden's "delight" (Gen 2:15 LXX), fixed.
- **1419B** · …Banishing me utterly. — T. ὀστρακίζων: "banishing"; English *ostracize* has shrunk to a social sense the Greek does not have.
- **1419B** · …In human form, purely, — T. ἀγνῶς, "purely". The Latin has *invisibiliter* (as if ἀγνώστως): the breathing cannot be told at the plate, and the Latin may simply read it the other way, so no `[lat:]` is marked.
- **1419C** · …In the waters — T. Ps 73:13 LXX, the heads of the dragons crushed upon the water, read of the baptism in the Jordan; ἐμβαπτίσας four lines below is the verb of baptism, "plunging under". κάρηνον, "head", goes up one line and σκοτώδη, "dark", up one line (V1).
- **1419D** · …And he goads, impurely, — T. The soul's unruly horses and its charioteer (Plato, *Phaedrus* 246a–254e): the image runs to "the charioteer who guides the reins". ὀργᾶν, "to swell" with desire, is rendered "to lust".
- **1419D** · …From fire-bearing darts, — T. The fire-bearing darts of the wicked one (Eph 6:16).
- **1420A** · …Fire in me, which is blended in. — S. The top line of col. 1420 is badly broken at the plate; ἐμπέφυρται is read from traces (υ against ο not certain). T. From φύρω, "mix, knead": the fire "is blended in", kneaded into the speaker himself.
- **1420A** · …Achieving a strange work . — T. The stream stayed and turned back is the Jordan (Josh 3:13–16; Ps 113:3 LXX). ἔργον ξένον, "a strange work" (V10); the Latin takes ξένον of the foreigners and gives *Cladem exterorum complevisti*, "thou didst accomplish the destruction of the foreigners".
- **1420B** · …The greatest light-bringer. — T. ἐπέσχες, "didst check": the sun stayed for Joshua (Josh 10:12–13), the sun as a giant running his course (Ps 18:6 LXX). The Latin's *regis cursum*, "thou rulest the course", is the looser sense of the verb.
- **1420B** · …The flame into deep dew. — T. The furnace heated seven times (Dan 3:19), its midst made "as a wind of dew" (Dan 3:50 LXX).
- **1420B** · …As she shed hot tears. — S. The plate prints Θερμὴν (an η, shaped as in Σὴν at the head of this column; no round ο), not the base's Θερμὸν; base corrected (PLATE-CHECKS.tsv). Either form is adverbial with δακρυῤῥοούσης: "shed hot tears" (Lk 7:37–38).
- **1420B** · …didst stay, of her that flowed with blood, — S. The plate prints αἱμορούσης with a single ρ and Στῆσας with a circumflex; both kept as printed, the sense unaffected (Mt 9:20–22).
- **1420C** · …Having gathered the slackness of his limbs, — T. λύσιν μελῶν, "the slackness of his limbs", the palsy (Mk 2:3–12); ἀθροίσας, "having gathered", the limbs drawn together again. The Latin takes λύσις as release: *Membrorum solutione obtenta*.
- **1420C** · …And thou didst ward off more besides , — T. ἤρυξας, as printed and confirmed at the plate: aorist of ἐρύκω, "hold back, ward off" (evils, after the healings). The Latin's *egisti*, "thou didst", reads as if ἤνυσας, "accomplished", which suits the run of miracles better; the English renders the printed word, and the line is a crux.
- **1420D** · …And the Father's, and thy breath's. — T. πνοῆς σῆς, "thy breath": the Spirit, with the Father and the Son addressed. πνοή here and ἄημα in Hymn III are both "breath" in English; the Greek words differ.

## Chunk 0002 (hy-002)

- **1420** · …to Christ, in the form of a confession. — T. ἐξομολόγησις is both confession (of sins) and, in the Greek psalter, praise; the hymn is the first and ends in the second. The Latin title takes the second: *per modum laudationis*, "by way of praise".
- **1420** · …Alas for me, for my evils, — T. ὤμοι is οἴμοι in another spelling: "alas", as in Hymn I (V3). The genitives that follow (κακῶν, ἀβουλίας, ἀνάγκης, μόχθου) are the cause of the cry: "alas for".
- **1421A** · …And slavish constraint; — S. The plate prints the Greek question mark (;) after ἀνάγκης, confirmed at the plate, though the sense runs on; kept in the base, and rendered as a pause, not a question.
- **1421B** · …But sand, which most easily — T. The house on the sand (Mt 7:24–27): rain, streams and winds, carried through the hymn to the house built on the rock below.
- **1421C** · …Put on a way of thinking — T. ἥ γ', "it": the heart (κραδίης, three lines up). λευκάζον, "whitening", recalls the whited sepulchres (Mt 23:27). ὕπουλον is said of a wound healed over on top and festering beneath; the Latin takes it as a noun, *Plagam interiorem*. φρόνημα, "way of thinking", as in the Disputatio.
- **1421C** · …And I kept house-slaves , — T. οἰκέτριβας as printed (confirmed at the plate; the lexica have οἰκότριψ, a slave born and bred in the house): "house-slaves". The Latin, *Damnososque habebam hospites*, reads it as wreckers of the house (οἶκος + τρίβω), which suits the ruined house of this hymn; a crux.
- **1421C** · …And which of old the Typhon — T. τυφῶνα: the whirlwind, and Typhon, the monster hurled from heaven in the Greek myth; here in apposition to the Morning-star (ἑωσφόρος, Isa 14:12 LXX). The relative is the puffing-up (φῦσα) of the line before. The Latin has *exardescens*, "blazing up", for τυφῶνα.
- **1421D** · …An ill-omened jumble, — T. τύρευμα, properly a cheese-curd, then a mess or concoction: "jumble". The Latin has *congeries*, "heap".
- **1422A** · …Which gathered so great — S. The accent on ἡ is indeterminate at the plate (ἡ, ἣ or ἢ). T. Read as the relative or the epic demonstrative, either way the soul (ψυχῇ, the line before): "which". The Latin has *Ipsa*, "she herself".
- **1422A** · …Which are precious, — T. The foundation, and the building of gold, silver and precious stones, or of wood and hay for the fire (1 Cor 3:10–15), runs on to the end of the hymn ("impure wood, hay").
- **1422A** · …For what than august temperance — T. σαοφροσύνη (epic dress for σωφροσύνη, V3), "temperance"; σεμνῆς, "august", goes up one line. The Latin has *Sapientia*, "wisdom".
- **1422B** · …God is not given to mortals. — T. Heb 12:14: without holiness no man shall see the Lord.
- **1422B** · …And than a pure conscience , — T. συνειδότος ἁγνοῦ, "a pure conscience". The Latin, *Consciente agno*, "the Lamb being witness", reads ἁγνοῦ as if ἀμνοῦ: a departure of sense, so `[lat:]` (V7).
- **1422B** · …Presents a mortal ? — S. βροτόν, "a mortal", confirmed at the plate. T. The Latin's *Angelicum quoque / Cibum præstante*, "presenting angelic food", reads βρωτόν: `[lat:]` (V7).
- **1422B** · …If it is pure from above, — T. ἦν as printed (the accent is not clear at the plate). "Was" does not construe with πάσχει; the line is taken as ἤν, "if" (= ἐάν), with the participle: "if it is pure". The Latin has *Desuper ita fit purus*, "from above it thus becomes pure".
- **1422C** · …Self-sprung, and the breath — T. The three as in Hymn III: the self-sprung root and the life-bearing breath (ἄημα), with the might; the Latin reads them the same way (*Potentiam, originemque / Spontaneam, afflatumque*). πνεῦσιν, "its blowing" (πνεῦσις), kept apart from πνοή, "breath".
- **1422C** · …Stone, which thou thyself art, — T. Christ the chief cornerstone (Eph 2:20; 1 Pet 2:6). ἔπλες, epic for "art".
- **1423A** · …And, breathing, a spirit too. — T. God breathing into the moulded dust (Gen 2:7 LXX, ἐνεφύσησεν). The Latin has *Animam*, "soul", for πνεῦμα.
- **1423B** · …With which thou goest before me, Saviour, — S. Ἧ printed without iota subscript (confirmed at the plate). T. The relative ᾗ, "with which": the compassion. προφθάνεις, "goest before" (Ps 58:11 LXX).
- **1424A** · …If not impure wood, — S. ὕλην is read from traces (badly broken at the plate); the Latin's *lignum* agrees. T. ὕλη is both "wood" and "matter"; Paul's list has ξύλα, χόρτον, "wood, hay" (1 Cor 3:12).
- **1424A** · …Mansions thou wilt set me, — T. μοναί, the "mansions" of John 14:2. προῖκα, "freely": as a gift; the Latin has *absque merito*, "without merit".

## Chunk 0003 (hy-003)

- **1424** · …Remember me, of the root without beginning — T. The line initials of this hymn spell ΜΑΞΙΜΟΣ ΤΑΔΕ ΓΡΑΨΕΝ, "Maximus wrote these" (V6: noted, not imitated). The address runs through the three Persons: shoot (the Son) of the root without beginning (the Father), and the breath (the Spirit).
- **1424B** · …And strange breath, to both — T. ἀμφοῖν may be dative with ἶσον ("equal to both", taken here, γόνῳ καὶ ἀρχῇ in apposition) or genitive with ἄημα ("breath of both"). The Latin (*Utrisque gratus Spiritus, Filio Patrique compar*) does not settle it. The English keeps the word where the Greek has it and leans to the dative; a crux, and one that bears on the procession of the Spirit.
- **1424B** · …My three-sunned form; — T. μοι, ethical dative: "my". τρισήλιος, "three-sunned", the hymnographic word (V4).
