# Cruces — maximus-confessor-mystagogia

Translators' notes (kt method, 2026-10-27), moved out of the running English. Each is keyed by chunk, column and band, and quotes the English words it follows. Plate verdicts: `translate/maximus-mystagogia/IMAGE-CHECK.tsv`. Glossary: `translate/maximus-mystagogia/GLOSSARY.md`. In the English, ⟨…⟩ marks words we supply where the printed sentence needs them (whether the plate lost them or Migne's text never had them); each has a note below.


## Chunk 0001 (my-001)

- **0657** · …the synaxis have been established as symbols of.* — T. σύναξις is kept as *synaxis*, the liturgical gathering, as the title and Combefis's Latin (*synaxi, seu collecta*) keep it. μυσταγωγία = mystagogy (the work's own title is left as *Mystagogia*); σύμβολον = symbol throughout. Combefis's Latin title, printed after the Greek, is not translated.
- **0657** · …to his receiving, according to the divine Proverb, — S. Prov. 9:9 (LXX: «Give an occasion to a wise man, and he will be wiser; make known to a just man, and he will add to his receiving»).
- **0660** · …not from not wishing to grant you, beloved, — T. The Proem opens to one friend in the singular (ἔδειξας, πάντων μοι τιμιώτατε: *thou*), but here and below the Greek turns to the plural (ὑμῖν, ἠγαπημένοι, ὑμᾶς), as does the Latin (*vobis, charissimi*): rendered *you*, as printed.
- **0660** · …knowledge, and makes plain the tongue of stammerers, — S. Ps. 93:10 («he who teaches man knowledge»); Isa. 35:6 («the tongue of stammerers shall be plain»).
- **0660** · …mire of the passions; the poor in spirit, — S. Matt. 5:3; for «the law of the flesh» below, cf. Rom. 7:23, 25.
- **0660** · …the all-holy and truly God-revealing Dionysius the Areopagite, — P. Dionysius the Areopagite: the author of the corpus written under the name of Paul's Athenian convert (Acts 17:34), c. 500; Maximus reads him as that convert.
- **0661** · …a manner worthy of his greatness of mind, — S. Dionysius, *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* III (the mystery of the synaxis).
- **0661** · …through deceit in the transgression of the commandment. — S. The labourers hired for the vineyard, idle «all the day», and the denarius: Matt. 20:1–16; the denarius bearing the king's image: Matt. 22:19–21; the image plundered by the serpent's deceit: Gen. 3.
- **0664** · …be the removal of the One beyond being; — T. θέσις / ἀφαίρεσις = positing / removal (Combefis *positio / ablatio*), the pair of cataphatic and apophatic theology; κατάφασις / ἀπόφασις below = affirmation / negation.
- **0664** · …altogether uninterpretable, and beyond all affirmation and negation. — S. Dionysius, *Mystical Theology* V («beyond every positing and removal»); cf. *Divine Names* I.5.
- **0664** · …same activity as he by imitation and type. — T. ἐνέργεια = activity (card ruling for this work; not the dyothelite technical *operation* here); ἐνεργεῖν = work.
- **0664** · …to himself, both the intelligible and the sensible; — T. νοητός / αἰσθητός = intelligible / sensible throughout this work.
- **0665** · …alone by those who are pure in understanding, — S. «all in all»: 1 Cor. 15:28; «will be seen ... by the pure»: Matt. 5:8.
- **0665** · …up the principles of the things that are, — T. λόγοι τῶν ὄντων = the principles of the things that are (sense (b) of λόγος).
- **0668** · …truly worthy of Christ himself, our true head; — S. Eph. 4:15; 1 Cor. 12:12–27.
- **0668** · …but he himself is all and in all*: — S. Gal. 3:28 combined with Col. 3:11 (where Paul has «Christ»; Maximus «he himself»).
- **0668** · …of certain straight lines that proceed from him, — S. The centre and its radii: Dionysius, *Divine Names* V.6; cf. II.5.
- **0668** · …the faithful peoples, which we call the nave. — T. ἱερατεῖον = sanctuary (the part beyond the chancel, for clergy); ναός = nave (Combefis: *sacrarium*, *templum*); ἱερεῖς and λειτουργοί = priests and ministers.
- **0668** · …Again, it is one according to its hypostasis, — T. ὑπόστασις = hypostasis by the binding glossary; here of the building as one concrete reality (Combefis *substantia*), not in the Christological sense.

## Chunk 0002 (my-002)

- **0669** · …it as the beginning of its own mystagogy; — T. κατὰ τὴν δύναμιν / κατὰ τὴν ἐνέργειαν = in potency / in activity (Combefis *potentia / in actu*); αὐτὸν = the nave. δύναμις is *potency* in this pair and in δυνάμει below, *power* where it is a faculty.
- **0669** · …world, filled out by noetic and bodiless essences, — T. νοερός = noetic (card ruling for this work), kept apart from νοητός = intelligible: the intelligible world is filled by noetic essences.
- **0669** · …says Ezekiel, the wonderful seer of great things, — S. Ezek. 1:16.
- **0669** · …intellection of the things seen through the invisibles. — T. ἐπιστήμη καὶ νόησις = science and intellection, each in its own word.
- **0672** · …according to the image and likeness of God; — S. Gen. 1:26.
- **0672** · …through the divine altar, entering upon mystical theology. — T. Moral philosophy, natural contemplation, mystical theology: the Evagrian triad (πρακτική, φυσική, θεολογική), here distributed over nave, sanctuary and altar. φιλοσοφία = philosophy throughout.
- **0672** · …the Godhead, through another silence, talkative and many-voiced; — S. The silence of the Godhead: Dionysius, *Mystical Theology* I.1 (the «hidden-mystery silence»); cf. *Divine Names* IV.22.
- **0672** · …as it is, according to nature, without choice. — T. ἐξουσιαστικῶς κατὰ βούλησιν = with authority according to wish; ἀπροαιρέτως = without choice (προαίρεσις = choice).
- **0672** · …said, is called mind, and the practical reason; — T. In this chapter λόγος is the soul's faculty: *reason*, paired with νοῦς = mind; φρόνησις = prudence, σοφία = wisdom.
- **0673** · …to the truth through unforgetting and unceasing knowledge; — T. ἄληστος γνῶσις = unforgetting knowledge (Combefis: knowledge «that no forgetting effaces»); ἀκατάληκτος = unceasing.
- **0673** · …and the reason prudence, as the first activities. — T. ἐνέργεια = activity, the third term of the Aristotelian triad δύναμις – ἕξις – ἐνέργεια (power, habit, activity) that orders the rest of the chapter.
- **0673** · …Divine seems to be signified from its essence, — T. Migne prints μὴ (confirmed on the plate): ἀλλὰ τὴν μὴ ἀλήθειαν, «but the not-truth», which cannot stand, since the clause is answered by τὸ δὲ ἀγαθόν; the sense needs μὲν (Latin *verum quidem veritas*). The English follows μὲν. Marked for the blind read.
- **0673** · …and impassible thing, and one never escaping notice, — T. ἀλάθητος, «that does not escape notice», printed again below (ὡς ἀλάθητον γνωστόν); Combefis renders *indelebile*. Kept, as distinct from ἄληστος = unforgetting.
- **0673** · …and 'being', or 'having been set', or 'running', — T. The etymology of ἀγαθόν from ἄγαν, «exceedingly», with εἶναι «to be», τεθεῖσθαι «to have been set», or θέειν «to run». Migne prints θέσιν, «setting» (confirmed on the plate), which gives no etymology; Combefis's variant 19 reports θέειν in codex A (*c. A.*), and his Latin has *currere*: the English follows θέειν. Cf. Plato, *Cratylus* 412c, 422a.
- **0676** · …engaged about the one pair that signifies God. — T. συζυγία = pair (Combefis *conjugatio*), kept for each of the five and for the sixth.
- **0676** · …strings of the intelligible psaltery of the soul, — S. The ten-stringed psaltery: Ps. 32:2; 143:9; the decad of the commandments: Exod. 20; Deut. 5.
- **0676** · …able to know from suffering the things said — S. Dionysius, *Divine Names* II.9 (Hierotheus «not only learning but suffering divine things»).
- **0676** · …him and he in us, as he said; — S. John 15:4; 6:56; «for whom Christ died»: Rom. 14:15; 1 Cor. 8:11.
- **0677** · …without works is something dead, as is written; — S. Jas. 2:26 (cf. 2:17, 20).
- **0677** · …summarily, to make its own decad a monad, — T. μονάδα τὴν ἑαυτῆς δεκάδα ποιῆσαι: «to make its own decad a monad», the ten gathered into one; Combefis's Latin reverses it (*suam unitatem decadem fecerit*).

## Chunk 0003 (my-003)

- **0680** · …contain the other, blessed decad of the commandments. — T. The arithmetic: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10, so the tetrad (the four general virtues) is "in potency a decad". The "divine decad in the soul" is set against "the other decad of the commandments", the Ten Commandments. The square brackets in this sentence and in the four terms below (*[science]*, *[knowledge]*, *[love]*, *[peace]*) are printed in the Greek. Combefis's Latin glosses the first two "Graece ἐπιστήμην … Graece γνῶσιν".
- **0680** · …the simple and partless of the divine activity — T. ἐνέργεια is "activity" throughout the *Mystagogia*, as the card rules. It is not the Christological "operation" of the Epistulae, because nothing here touches the two operations of Christ.
- **0680** · …virtuous knowledge, and upon these the unforgetting knowledge, — T. ἄληστος γνῶσις, "unforgetting knowledge" (Latin *indelebilis*, *perpetua*). It is the fourth term of the mind's chain: wisdom, contemplation, knowledge, unforgetting knowledge.
- **0680** · …men, and the unerring compass of the virtues; — T. Migne prints *[γνώμην]*, "inclination", for the second bracketed term (confirmed on the plate; the bracket is Combefis's own, as on all four terms). The sense needs γνῶσιν, "knowledge": the list just given (θείαν ἐπιστήμην, καὶ γνῶσιν ἄπταιστον, καὶ ἀγάπην καὶ εἰρήνην) names the four terms that are here taken up one by one, and Combefis's Latin has *notitiam*, glossed *Graece γνῶσιν*. The English follows the sense.
- **0680** · …there that even reaches its watchtower?), and peace — T. Migne prints τὴ δὲ εἰρήνη (confirmed on the plate); the sense needs the nominative ἡ δὲ εἰρήνη, the subject of σταθερότης ἐστίν. Grammar only: the English is unchanged.
- **0680** · …the movements of the soul contrary to nature, — T. παρυφίστανται: the vices have no subsistence of their own. They "subsist alongside" the soul's movements that run contrary to nature, as Dionysius's παρυπόστασις of evil does (*Divine Names* IV.31–32).
- **0680** · …of God, according to his most unlying promise; — S. The promise is not named. Perhaps cf. Heb. 4:1–10 (the promise of entering into his rest). The "bed" (κοίτη, Latin *cubile*) is bridal, as the Ephesians text that follows shows.
- **0681** · …all-holy bed that awesome mystery of the oneness — T. ἑνότης "oneness", kept apart from ἕνωσις "union".
- **0681** · …is joined to the Lord is one spirit*. — S. 1 Cor. 6:17. The Greek has τῷ Κυρίῳ, "to the Lord", and the Latin *Deo*.
- **0681** · …be in reason and from reason by expansion; — T. κατὰ διαστολήν, "by expansion" (Latin *secundum distractionem*). It is the counterpart of κατὰ πρόοδον, "by procession", in the mind. The sanctuary (ἱερατεῖον) is the mind and the nave (ναός) is reason.
- **0684** · …that the whole of holy Scripture, taken together, — T. κατὰ συναίρεσιν: "taken together", the whole drawn into one, not the Christological "coalescence" of the glossary.
- **0684** · …to which the mind is directed, the soul. — T. "The mind (νοῦς) of what is written" is its sense (Latin *sensum*). The card's one word for νοῦς is kept so that the parallel with the soul's mind stays visible.
- **0684** · …as this man of ours, mastering by philosophy — T. φιλοσοφία: the ascetic life, as often in Maximus.
- **0684** · …much the inward is renewed day by day*. — S. 2 Cor. 4:16. Maximus gives it as ὅσον … τοσοῦτον, "as much as … so much", against the NT's εἰ καὶ … ἀλλ᾿, "though … yet". The Latin has *magnus Apostolus* for θεῖος, "divine".
- **0684** · …image and likeness of God who made him, — S. Gen. 1:26.
- **0685** · …the intelligible things hold the ratio of soul, — T. ψυχῆς λόγον ἐπέχειν, "hold the ratio of soul", that is, stand in the place of soul in the proportion. λόγος is used in sense (b), and τύπος, "figure", is its pair on the body's side.
- **0688** · …earthy tent burdens the mind of many cares*. — S. Wis. 9:15, verbatim from the LXX.
- **0688** · …taking away of the veil of the letter. — S. Col. 2:3 (the hidden treasures of wisdom and knowledge); 2 Cor. 3:3 (the tablets of the heart); 2 Cor. 3:16–18 (the veil taken away, the unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror).
- **0688** · …The first entrance, then, of the high priest — T. ἀρχιερεύς "high priest", and ἱεράρχης "hierarch" just below. Both mean the bishop presiding at the synaxis, but the Greek does not say ἐπίσκοπος.
- **0688** · …the Son of God and our Saviour Christ; — P. The first entrance: the bishop's entry into the church at the start of the synaxis, before the readings. It is the later "Little Entrance".
- **0688** · …been sold by itself to death through sin, — T. Migne prints παθοῦσαν ὑφ᾿ ἑαυτῆς τῷ θανάτῳ (confirmed on the plate), "having suffered at its own hands by death", which construes only loosely. Combefis's variant 37 reports πραθεῖσαν, "sold", in codex A and in his own edition (*c. A. et Fr.*), and his Latin has *venditum*; the English follows πραθεῖσαν (cf. Rom. 7:14, πεπραμένος ὑπὸ τὴν ἁμαρτίαν).
- **0688** · …giving himself as ransom for us and exchange, — S. Mark 10:45 / 1 Tim. 2:6 (λύτρον, ἀντίλυτρον). For ἀντάλλαγμα, "exchange", cf. Matt. 16:26.

## Chunk 0004 (my-004)

- **0689** · …… to the full knowledge — T. ἐπίγνωσις "full knowledge", kept apart from γνῶσις "knowledge" in the next clause.
- **0689** · …the victory-worthy crowns of the kingdom of Christ. — S. 2 Tim. 2:5 (ἐὰν μὴ νομίμως ἀθλήσῃ); cf. 1 Cor. 9:25.
- **0689** · …the divine chants, he said, manifests the reflective — T. ἐμφαντικήν "reflective" (glossary ἔμφασις, ἐμφατικός). The Latin paraphrases it: *voluptatem … sensum vimque exprimentem*.
- **0689** · …command of the high priest at each reading, — P. The "Peace be to all" that the bishop has called out before each reading. The title calls them προσφωνήσεις, "salutations", and the text ὑποφωνήσεις, "acclamations".
- **0692** · …in the annulling of the body of sin, — S. Rom. 6:6 (ἵνα καταργηθῇ τὸ σῶμα τῆς ἁμαρτίας).
- **0692** · …on behalf of virtue, the grace of passionlessness; — T. ἀπάθεια is "passionlessness" (card § Terms), not the Epistulae's "dispassion".
- **0692** · …heaven, contracts for them the way of thinking — T. φρόνημα "way of thinking" (glossary § IX); cf. Rom. 8:6, τὸ φρόνημα τῆς σαρκός.
- **0692** · …of their own salvation, which the divine Creed — T. τὸ θεῖον τῆς πίστεως σύμβολον, "the divine symbol of the faith", is the Creed (card § Terms).
- **0692** · …number of the angels through the thrice-holy hymn, — P. The thrice-holy hymn (Τρισάγιον), called the Trisagion from here on, is in this work the Sanctus of the anaphora ("Holy, holy, holy", Isa. 6:3), sung with the angels (ch. 19): its place in Maximus's order, after the Creed and before the Lord's Prayer, shows it, and it is not the processional "Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal" of the entrance.
- **0692** · …have been counted worthy to call God Father. — T. Migne prints να-|λεῖν, a non-word (confirmed on the plate); the sense needs καλεῖν, "to call". Read so.
- **0692** · …through the One is Holy and what follows, — P. «One is holy, one is Lord, Jesus Christ, to the glory of God the Father»: the people's response to the elevation and the "Holy things to the holy" before communion (ch. 21).
- **0692** · …ministers, the dismissal and expulsion of the catechumens — P. The catechumens, those being instructed for baptism, were dismissed after the Gospel and before the mysteries.
- **0693** · …the just, and the defiled from the holy; — S. Matt. 13:49; 25:32.
- **0693** · …who have walked by the Spirit of God; — S. Gal. 5:16, 25 (πνεύματι στοιχῶμεν); 2 Pet. 2:10 (ὀπίσω σαρκὸς … πορευομένους).
- **0693** · …to the worth of what he has lived. — S. Matt. 16:27; Rom. 2:6.
- **0693** · …The entrance of the holy and venerable mysteries — P. The entrance of the mysteries: the bringing in of the gifts for the anaphora, later called the Great Entrance.
- **0696** · …to come; through which the worthy receive kinship — T. οἰκείωσις in the affective sense, "kinship" (glossary § V). The Latin has *necessitudinem ac conjunctionem*.
- **0696** · …the mouth is a symbol of the word, — T. λόγος is both "word" and "reason" in this sentence (Latin *verbi … ac rationis*). The mouth is the symbol of the word, those who share in λόγος are "rational", and the Word is the cause of every word.
- **0697** · …God, in the incorruptible age of the intelligibles; — T. Migne prints συναγωγήν τε καὶ ἕνωσιν οηλοῖ, ἐν τί ἀφθάρτῳ τῶν νοητῶν αἰῶνι (both confirmed on the plate): οηλοῖ is a non-word, and the sense needs δηλοῖ, "shows"; τί cannot stand with ἀφθάρτῳ … αἰῶνι, and the sense needs τῷ. Read so.
- **0697** · …partake to be like the good by cause, — T. τῷ κατ᾿ αἰτίαν ἀγαθῷ, "the good by cause", that is, the Good that is so as cause. The Latin has *boni per essentiam*, which may reflect κατ᾿ οὐσίαν. The Greek is kept.
- **0697** · …both be and be called gods by adoption — T. θέσει "by adoption" (glossary § V).
- **0697** · …written, *Women coming from a spectacle, come hither* — S. Isa. 27:11 (LXX), γυναῖκες ἐρχόμεναι ἀπὸ θέας, δεῦτε. Maximus plays on θέα, "spectacle", and θεωρία, "contemplation".
- **0697** · …not willing, to know God through his works) — S. Rom. 1:19–21; Wis. 13:1–9.

## Chunk 0005 (my-005)

- **0700** · …… our true God and high priest, entering; — T. The chunk opens inside a sentence begun in my-004 (the Latin: «with the Word, and with the Word himself, our great and true God and high priest, as guide, entering»). The feminine accusative participles that follow (εἰσερχομένην, διδασκομένην, δεξαμένην, μεταβαίνουσαν …) all describe the soul, governed by a verb of saying in the preceding chunk; the masculine ones (διακρίνοντα, ἄγοντα, ἐκδιδάσκοντα) describe the Word. Rendered as English participles, with "the soul" and "him" supplied where the gender alone carries the subject.
- **0700** · …own side, it brings itself to the Word, — T. Migne prints πρὸς μὲν ἑαυτῆς τὰς οἰκείας δυνάμεις· πρὸς δὲ τὸν Λόγον, ἑαυτὴν καταντᾷ (genitive ἑαυτῆς, and the comma after τὸν Λόγον; both confirmed on the plate). Followed: πρὸς with the genitive, "on its own side", answers πρὸς δὲ τὸν Λόγον, and ἑαυτὴν is the object of καταντᾷ, used transitively, "brings itself to". The Latin («suas ipsa vires, seque ipsam in Verbo colligens … ad ipsum evadit») takes ἑαυτὴν with συναγαγοῦσαν instead.
- **0700** · …Monad in Trinity, and a Trinity in Monad; — S. The Monad-in-Trinity formula: cf. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 25.17 and 23.8; the list of negated prepositions (not beside, through, in, from another) guards against every relation that would make the hypostases parts.
- **0700** · …and Trinity, having the union unconfused and unconfusedly, — T. Migne prints ἀσύγχυτον, "unconfused" (confirmed on the plate); the English follows it. Combefis's Latin has «omni tum vacantem compositione», as if ἀσύνθετον, "uncompounded".
- **0701** · …suffer the divine things rather than know them, — S. Dionysius, *Divine Names* II.9: Hierotheus «not only learning but also suffering the divine things» (οὐ μόνον μαθὼν ἀλλὰ καὶ παθὼν τὰ θεῖα).
- **0701** · …to it whole in a manner befitting God — T. The bracket is Migne's own: a second form of the clause (ὅλον αὐτῇ θεοπρεπῶς ὅλως), printed in the text as an alternative reading. Translated and kept in brackets; the *variae lectiones* are read separately.
- **0701** · …that it is, as the all-holy Areopagite Dionysius — P. Dionysius the Areopagite: see note 7, my-001.
- **0701** · …goodness of the silence in the inner shrines. — S. Dionysius, *Divine Names* IV.22, of the angels, applied here to the deified soul. Dionysius's text has «the beauty of the good-formed likeness to God» (τῆς ἀγαθοειδοῦς θεοειδείας); Maximus's τοῦ ἀγαθοτύπου (Latin «exemplaris boni») is rendered "the Good that is its type".
- **0701** · …the silence in the inner shrines. CHAPTER 24 — T. Combefis's chapter number is mangled in the Kraken (κεΚαʹ, followed by a leaked "CAPUT XXIV"); restored from the sequence and the Latin.
- **0701** · …his leisure to the holy Church of God, — T. σχολάζειν, "to give one's leisure to", i.e. to frequent and attend on the church; the Latin «ut frequentes essent».
- **0704** · …found there, and remoulds him, as something true, — T. Migne prints ἀληθὲς (confirmed on the plate), the neuter adjective, not the adverb ἀληθῶς: taken as predicate with μεταπλάττουσαν, "remoulds into something true", toward the more divine.
- **0704** · …he is still among the infants in Christ, — S. 1 Cor. 3:1 («as unto babes in Christ»).
- **0704** · …paradoxical modes of our salvation. Through the Trisagion: — P. The Trisagion: see note 9, my-004 (here too the Sanctus).
- **0704** · …we are deemed worthy to call God Father: — S. The Lord's Prayer (Matt. 6:9); cf. Rom. 8:15, Gal. 4:6 for the adoption by which we cry «Father».
- **0704** · …Through the One is Holy and what follows: — P. «One is Holy»: see note 11, my-004.
- **0704** · …in the age to come, in truth, enhypostatically, — T. ἐνυποστάτως, "enhypostatically" (glossary ἐνυπόστατος): the gifts held now by faith will then be possessed in their own reality, as subsisting things; the Latin «ut reipsa ita sit».
- **0705** · …firm and inviolable promise of him who promised, — T. Migne prints ἐπαγγειλομένου (confirmed on the plate), a misprint: the sense needs the aorist ἐπαγγειλαμένου, "of him who promised". Read so.
- **0705** · …grace in faith to the grace in sight; — S. 2 Cor. 5:7: «we walk by faith, not by sight» (οὐ διὰ εἴδους).
- **0705** · …together with himself at the summit as creator, — T. The sentence turns from God (masculine συνέχων) to the Church (feminine συνάπτουσα) with no verb for the Church. Combefis's Latin has the missing clause: «eam quoque illa per fidei gratiam in fidelibus operetur», "this the Church too works in the faithful through the grace of faith". The plate confirms it: Migne prints no such clause, and the sentence has no verb for the Church; the Latin supplies one. `[?]` marks the place.
- **0705** · …practical and virtuous by one identity of inclination; — T. γνώμη "inclination" (glossary § II); the three orders of the faithful, the practical (πρακτικοί, those of the active life) and the knowers (γνωστικοί, those of knowledge, the contemplatives) run through the rest of the chapter, each in the same word.
- **0705** · …generally signifies the first coming of our God; — T. Migne prints τοῦ Θεοῦ ἡμῶν, "of our God", with no Χριστοῦ (confirmed on the plate); the English follows it. The Latin has «Christi Dei nostri», "of Christ our God".
- **0705** · …and unshaken against the wiles of the devil, — S. Eph. 6:11 («to stand against the wiles of the devil», πρὸς τὰς μεθοδείας τοῦ διαβόλου).
- **0708** · …the way of thinking according to the flesh; — S. Cf. Rom. 8:6–7 (τὸ φρόνημα τῆς σαρκός) and Col. 3:5.
- **0708** · …of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ, — S. Titus 2:13 («our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ»).
- **0708** · …away from them; of the practical, perfect passionlessness, — T. ἀπάθεια "passionlessness" (card § Terms); ἐμπαθής "passionate".
- **0708** · …doctrines, and to harmony of voice and piety; — T. Greek εὐσέβειαν, "piety"; the Latin has «gratiarum actionem», "thanksgiving" (= εὐχαριστίαν), which answers the general sense's fourth term («the thanksgiving for the modes by which we have been saved») and the Creed as fourth symbol. Migne prints εὐσέβειαν (confirmed on the plate); the English follows it.
- **0708** · …the third, and the proclamation of the Creed — T. Migne prints σοβόλου, a non-word (confirmed on the plate); the sense is συμβόλου, "of the symbol", i.e. the Creed (ἡ ἐκφώνησις τοῦ συμβόλου, answering «the proclamation of the Creed» above).

## Chunk 0006 (my-006)

- **0709** · …no longer pursue the divine and ineffable principle — T. λόγος here "principle" (Latin «divinam arcanamque rationem»): the knowers no longer seek the divine principle through the senses; the Word himself may be heard in it, but nothing in the sentence makes it a title.
- **0709** · …be all in all those who are saved, — S. 1 Cor. 15:28 («that God may be all in all»).
- **0709** · …used to call faithful and virtuous and knowers — T. Subject of ἐκάλει is the holy elder of the Proem, whose teaching the chapter reports. Migne prints a « at the head of this sentence and a » after ἐπαγγελιῶν (col. 712); they do not bound any quotation the sense supports (the span is reported teaching, then Maximus's own exhortation and a cento of Colossians) and are logged as strays.
- **0709** · …the three orders of those who are saved. — S. The three orders (slaves, hirelings, sons; fear, reward, love) after the parable of the two sons in Luke 15 (the hired servants of 15:17 and 15:19); cf. Basil, *Regulae fusius tractatae*, prooem. 3, and Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 40.13.
- **0709** · …the burden of the day and the heat, — S. Matt. 20:12.
- **0712** · …not know God, in the passion of desire; — S. 1 Thess. 4:5; «good works … pleasing God», cf. Col. 1:10.
- **0712** · …wrath, and anger, and filthy speech, and lying, — S. Col. 3:8–9. ὀργή "wrath", θυμός "anger" (glossary § II).
- **0712** · …is corrupted according to the desires of deceit, — S. Eph. 4:22; «with his practices», Col. 3:9.
- **0712** · …the might of his glory, unto all edification — T. Greek οἰκοδομήν, "edification"; Col. 1:11 and Combefis's Latin («in omni patientia») have ὑπομονήν, "patience". Migne prints οἰκοδομήν (confirmed on the plate); the English follows the plate.
- **0713** · …one's own, according to our power, as God, — T. ὡς Θεόν may be read "as God does" or "as one would God"; the Latin takes the second («haud secus ac Deum»), which the following argument (the poor man is God) supports. The English keeps the Greek's ambiguity.
- **0713** · …least of these, you did it to me*; — S. Matt. 25:40. The plural address of Scripture is *you* (glossary § IX).
- **0713** · …in each, always suffers mystically out of goodness; — T. πάθος "passion" (glossary), here in its plain sense of suffering: Christ takes the sufferings of each into himself and «suffers mystically» in each; so too the healer heals «the passions of those who suffer».
- **0713** · …that is, the minding of one's own affairs; — T. αὐτοπραγία and ἰδιοπραγία: the doing of one's own business and the minding of one's own affairs (Latin «sui illa privata est, ac rerum suarum agendarum cura»); cf. 1 Thess. 4:11 (πράσσειν τὰ ἴδια).
- **0716** · …receive mercy, and find grace for timely help. — S. Heb. 4:16.
- **0716** · …these things by the holy Dionysius the Areopagite, — S. Dionysius, *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* III (the synaxis), to which Maximus defers in the Proem as well.
- **0716** · …has not fallen far short of your desire, — T. The close addresses the reader in the plural (ὑμῶν, ὑμῖν, ἠγαπημένοι, ἐκελεύσατε), against the Proem's single friend, yet asks for «your blessed and holy soul» in the singular: either a plural of respect to the one friend or the friend with those about him. Rendered *you*, as the Greek has it.
- **0716** · …cast off the old law as a husband, — S. Rom. 7:2–3 (the law as the husband to whom the soul is bound while he lives).
- **0717** · …them as from her whole livelihood and life, — T. βίος and ζωή, "livelihood and life": βίος is the widow's «all her living» of Mark 12:44 (ὅλον τὸν βίον αὐτῆς).
- **0717** · …things, compared with the beholding principle of theology, — T. ἐποπτικός, "beholding", the word of the mysteries' last degree (the ἐπόπτης sees); Latin «divina inspectrice ratione».
- **0717** · …stamp; and perhaps they have over and above — T. The bracket [καὶ τὸ] is Migne's (a supplement in the printed Greek), kept as printed. κατὰ [καὶ τὸ] πλέον: the mites, equal to the gold coins in bearing the royal stamp, perhaps surpass them in carrying the widow's intent (Combefis's Latin *praestant*).
- **0717** · …fear of God that is pure and abiding, — S. Ps. 18:10 LXX («the fear of the Lord is pure, abiding for ever»).
