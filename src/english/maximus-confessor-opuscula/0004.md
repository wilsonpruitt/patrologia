⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0040] OPUSCULUM 2 — TO THE SAME MARINUS: FROM THE TREATISE ON OPERATIONS AND WILLS, CHAPTER 50

⟦s1⟧ *Of the same, to the same Marinus.* ⟦s2⟧ *From the treatise on operations and wills, chapter 50.*

⟦s2⟧ *That Severus maliciously says that the hypostasis is the same as the nature, in order that he may ratify the confusion through the one nature, and may escape the refutation by saying that he understood the nature to be hypostasis; and may again introduce the division, advocating the union from hypostases; and may seem to escape notice, asserting that he takes the hypostases in place of natures. ⟦s3⟧ And again, when accused of the non-existence of the natures through the bare difference, he may find a refuge of defence, the bare difference in natural quality. ⟦s4⟧ And that, just as Nestorius, saying the union was bare, introduced a real division, so Severus too, saying the difference of natures is bare, makes the confusion real.*

⟦s5⟧ But Severus, hard pressed by these arguments, and not bearing the testing of the truth, again, as is his custom, puts forward falsehood for himself as an ally: asserting that in the economy the hypostasis is the same as the nature, and the difference in natural quality the same as the real one and that according to nature, on the ground that every quality too is natural, and is called a thing; so that, if, caught by the refutations, he should be detected plainly, through the one composite nature, advocating the phantasy of Manes, and the confusion of Apollinarius, and Eutyches' coalescence of the things united into one essence after the union, he may, after the manner of a chameleon changing its colours, alter his own word, asserting that he understood the nature to be hypostasis. ⟦s6⟧ But if again, saying that the union came about from hypostases, that is, persons, he should be shown to think the division of Ebion, of Paul of Samosata and of Nestorius, and to decree the operation of the Word by grace, as in a prophet, he may [0041] again have falsehood ready as his advocate, declaring that he said hypostases in place of natures. ⟦s7⟧ But if again he should be justly accused, upon the bare difference, of dogmatizing the non-existence of the things united, he may find as a refuge for his defence the difference as in natural quality, saying that after the union there is in Christ a difference of natural qualities, but not of natures, that is, of essences: through the word that dissolves the confusion, that is, the difference, the crafty sophist binding the confusion the tighter; making contraries not, according to the law of nature, a dissolution of contraries, but, against the definition and law of nature, their constitution; and becoming, to the deceiving of his hearers, such concerning the difference as Nestorius was shown to be concerning the union. ⟦s8⟧ For that one, naming the union by the bare appellation alone, introduced in operation the division of the things; and this one, advocating the bare difference after the union, thinks the existence of the things differing to be in conception, but in operation contrives their confusion.

⟦s9⟧ For if Nestorius had not thought that the union came about bare, he would have said the one composite hypostasis of Christ, completed from the union of the natures; and this one, if he had not proclaimed the difference bare, would not have refused to say, in Christ after the union, the uncut and undivided quantity of the things differing, knowing that with every difference quantity is altogether introduced together, and with every quantity the number indicative of it is yoked. ⟦s9⟧ For it is impossible either for difference to be without quantity, or for quantity to be discerned apart from the number that indicates it.

⟦s10⟧ And the one, knowing in Christ only the quantity of the natures, and not knowing the singleness of the hypostasis according to composition from the natures, makes the bare appellation of the union a covering, through and through, of the division of the natures; the other, advocating the singleness according to the nature alone instead of that according to hypostasis, and not knowing the quantity according to nature, makes the bare difference in natural quality a covering of the confusion of the natures. ⟦s11⟧ And the one, advocating the union of the natures in a quality of inclination alone, denies the one hypostasis, not enduring to say that the concourse of the things according to essence came about true. ⟦s12⟧ For authority and worth, and a volition of such a kind, of which Nestorius asserted the union to be, are plainly movements of inclination, but not of nature. [0044] But the other, saying the bare difference after the union in natural quality alone, evidently makes an destruction of the things united, denying the difference according to essence, in natural otherness, of the very things united.

⟦s13⟧ And to speak of these briefly: the one devised the union of the qualities of inclination for the division of the things that are by nature; the other, on the contrary, contrived the difference of the natural qualities for the confusion of the things. ⟦s14⟧ Truly an evil pair of lawless men, raging evilly to tear apart through contraries the truth of the right doctrines. ⟦s15⟧ For the one, introducing a union of qualities of inclination, makes a division of the natures which in his view were in no way united; the other, saying that after the union there is only a difference of natural qualities, likewise introduces a confused alteration of the natures which in his view were in no way united; not knowing what they are saying. ⟦s16⟧ For they ought, if they had resolved to keep in line with themselves: Nestorius, saying that the union came about of qualities of inclination, not to divide the natures, of which he did not in the least advocate a union from the beginning, but to recognize after the union the difference of those of which he asserts the union to be; and Severus, introducing a difference of natural qualities, not to confuse after the union the natures, of which we do not concede to him to speak of the union so long as he is ignorant of their difference after the union. ⟦s17⟧ For of the things united the difference is contemplated after the union, even if Nestorius rave; and of the things differing after the union there is the union, even if Severus set himself in battle array, and make a great advocacy on behalf of falsehood.

⟦s18⟧ If then, according to Nestorius, a union came about of the wills of inclination alone, let him say their difference too after the union, letting go the division of the things in no way united. ⟦s18⟧ And he brought in, together with the difference of the wills of inclination, the variation of inclination too, by which not the difference according to nature is naturally shown, but that according to hypostasis, making in the difference of the wills of inclination a slackening and intensifying of goodness; and he made sinful the man deified by him by way of progress, as not having in every mode and principle the same volition of inclination as God. ⟦s19⟧ For the difference of the wills of inclination makes, as I said, the variation [0045] of inclination; and the variation of inclination introduces the diminution of goodness; and whoever is still held by it in any way whatever has not yet become perfectly sinless in inclination.

⟦s20⟧ So Nestorius, not accepting the one according to hypostasis from the concourse of the natures, dissolved also the union of the wills of inclination, not finding how to say what at all is the one shown forth from the union of the wills of inclination. ⟦s21⟧ For he cannot speak of identity of goodness, since the difference of the wills of inclination after the union plainly shows forth its otherness by intensifying and slackening. ⟦s22⟧ For of the things of which there is a concourse, of these one must altogether speak of the difference too. ⟦s23⟧ And in vain was the account of identity of counsel devised by him, even if it is fitting for showing the division of the persons. ⟦s24⟧ For identity of counsel is a union of counsels preserving the difference of the things united in it, since the division of the persons wishing is contemplated along with them unconfused.
