⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ OPUSCULUM 8 — TO NICANDER, ON THE TWO OPERATIONS

⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ *Copy of a letter written to the most holy bishop, the lord Nicander, by Maximus, who is among the saints, on the two operations in Christ.*

⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ Thou thyself, God-honoured and all-blessed Father, having, as thy name bears, together with manliness, the most sacred victory over all the enemies who secretly or openly make war on the holy catholic and apostolic Church of God; and having already received from him, in hope, the unfading crown for the fight on his behalf, ever-blooming with the gifts of grace and the operations of the Spirit; and having acquired, as in thy hands, through perfect convergence with it, the blessed enjoyment of what is by nature the object of aspiration; and from this plainly, if anyone [0092] else, delighting in the Lord himself: how ever, and in what manner, dost thou deign to attach this victory, even by a bare word — let alone to bring it through thy precious and blessed letters — to one feeble and cowardly, and not at all taught, even until now, to move for the battle-line of God, and for the holy war that is his and on his behalf, the practical powers of the mind, or the thoughts of the soul, which are like fingers of the hands, [n: Psal. cxliii, 1.] and in this way to rout nobly those who exalt themselves against the Lord Almighty with words and doctrines, and to drive them out of the good land, which is our Lord and God himself, and, what is the same, the orthodox faith: the truly firm seat and constitution of pious doctrines, and the ever-flowing fruit-bearing and nature of the virtues?

⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ But this thou hast done in imitation of Christ; so that, having plainly shown his good-loving self-emptying towards me — who by inclination stand apart from him, and by intent dwell far from him in the habit of vice as in a far country, and by operation feed the passions of dishonour like swine [n: Rom. i, 26.] — and having shown, by the greatness of thy condescension, the power of the divine achievements that are in thee, thou mightest rouse me to aspiration for it, and steel me without change towards a sacred and blessed zeal, once I have thrown off, as far as I can, the burden of sluggishness and negligence that weighs on me very heavily, beyond all others. ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ And marvelling at thee, blessed one, for thy wise economy and forethought concerning me the poor man, I have earnestly besought our Lord and God with tears, though I am a great sinner, that the preservation of thee, the good and noble shepherd and father, for more years and in more divine virtues, be granted to me and to all; so that, having thee especially now, when the truth of the outcome of events has plainly confirmed the prediction of the Truth that is by nature and really truth, and a tribulation such as has never been from the foundation of the world, no, nor ever shall be, [n: Matth. xxiv, 21.] has overtaken all everywhere because of our sins — thee who strengthenest and fortifiest us with the all-powerful staff of prayers that persuade God, and guidest us well and enlightenest us with the all-holy pipe of God-wise teaching, and makest us dwell through practice in a place of green pasture, [n: Psal. xxii, 2.] the ever-living habit of the virtues, and nurturest us through knowledge by the water of rest, the ever-gushing supply of the sacred streams in the doctrines, and keepest us safe in a strong place and a lofty fold, the catholic Church, and the Lord who espoused her to himself by his own and life-giving blood, according to volition — we may be freed from the onslaught of the adversaries; who, seizing on the opportune moment of the turmoil and confusion of everything, scheme to confound the great and ineffable mystery of the economy of Christ God that saves all, as the mystery which naturally circumscribes all ages and times and those in ages and times; [0093] as though the holy flesh from us — ensouled intellectually and rationally, assumed by him according to the good pleasure of the Father and the co-operation of the Holy Spirit, and united to him according to one and the same hypostasis — did not have after the union the things that belong to it according to nature, sin alone excepted, since that was not of the nature; or rather, as though the incarnate Word himself, who became man for our sake, did not preserve perfectly, as in us, the things that characterize our nature essentially; but either changed them over into his own divine nature, and in this way showed the deficiency of his own Godhead, as needing some addition for completeness according to essence; or made them pass entirely into non-existence, as not very good, and obliterated the work of his own fashioning by not having made them according to some reason and wisdom, and united them to himself according to hypostasis.

⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ For how is that which came to be by reason and wisdom not preserved indissolubly, by union, in the very Word and Wisdom? ⟦s5⟧ Or how, if it is preserved even after the union, is it not confessed? ⟦s5⟧ And for what cause? ⟦s6+7+8⟧ That through the confession it may be believed in truth that he who is God by nature became man? ⟦s6+7+8⟧ Or perhaps he did not even at the beginning assume, with the nature, the very things that belong by nature to the nature? ⟦s6+7+8⟧ But, taking some flesh soulless and irrational, and in no way from us or consubstantial with us — or not even taking it, but bringing it down from above, co-essentiated with him from the beginning — he united it to himself, and made a phantasm of the economy for us and towards us, approaching us in a human outward shape, but not consorting with us in human nature, that he might save the whole race from the old trespass and free it from the condemnation on its account.

⟦s6+7+8⟧ For these things, and more than these, are the opinions of all who do not confess in orthodox fashion that the natural things, sin apart, just as the natures themselves, are preserved altogether perfectly in Christ God, [0096] according to the holy Fathers, even after the union. ⟦s9⟧ For we do not behold the divine and uncreated nature as without existence, and without will or without operation; nor indeed do we recognize the created and human essence that is ours as without existence, and without will or without operation. ⟦s10⟧ And if we know that neither of these is bereft of natural existence, volition and operation, and from these — I mean divine nature and human nature — and in them, and of them, the one and only Christ and Son is in truth the hypostasis, how, if the same is properly God by nature and properly man by nature, did he not have also a divine will and operation properly by nature, and a human will and operation properly by nature, being deficient according to nature in neither of these; through which, verifying the names by the realities and again confirming the realities by these, the same was plainly known, having and willing and operating naturally the divine things and the human, and from this believed to be the things from which, and in which, and which he was?

⟦s11⟧ Since, if the natural will and the essential operation of both the divine and the human essence are destroyed, how will he who is from these and is these, and preserves them in one hypostasis, be God or man? ⟦s11⟧ And how will he be shown to be this or that according to essence, if he does not preserve without deficiency the property of each nature, apart from all sin? ⟦s12⟧ For what has departed from the things according to nature has come to be outside the essence too, having no existence, since it has no natural movement either. ⟦s13⟧ «For what has no movement», as says the great and holy Dionysius, revealer of God, «neither is, nor is anything; nor is there any positing of it at all.»

⟦s14⟧ For this reason, then, we who confess the natures of Christ God — the divine, I mean, and the human that is ours — must by all means, for the firm confirmation of their existence and the truth of our confession concerning them, also uphold their natural wills, equal in number, and confess as many essential operations; and not, under the pretext forsooth of the divine union according to hypostasis, diminish any of them or deny them altogether; knowing that the union according to hypostasis damages none of the natural things, just as it does not the natures themselves; even if it brings these into one through itself according to the one hypostasis, in no way removing them from being two by the natural principle; just as, then, uniting again throughout the natural idioms themselves, I mean, by their growing-together with one another, and in no way allowing either to be considered without the other or outside the communion of the one with the other, according to Leo, the celebrated pope of the Romans, it does not obscure at all, but perfectly keeps, their essential and natural difference; [0097] for by keeping it is kept, and by preserving it is preserved. ⟦s14⟧ For so long is there plainly a union of realities as their natural difference is preserved; since, when this ceases, that too ceases altogether, wholly obliterated by confusion.

⟦s15+16+17⟧ Since this, then — I mean the union — does not harm the realities nor their difference, but only unifies, fittingly, in the appellation, the things it concerns, the things that are different according to nature are always and altogether preserved in it without diminution and perfectly, suffering no deviation whatever, or confusion: not in the essences themselves, not in wills, not in operations, not in anything else natural. ⟦s15+16+17⟧ How then do some put this forward as a covering for their own — I do not know how to say it with fair words — fine thinking, and say that the incarnate Word is without will by nature, and without operation in what is ours, that is, in the human, as though the essence assumed from us were properly soulless and irrational in him? ⟦s15+16+17⟧ For what is without operation is plainly unmoved and soulless; and what has no rational volition is plainly irrational and mindless. ⟦s18⟧ And if the incarnate Word was by nature without these in what is ours, how will it be believed that he also became man? ⟦s18⟧ And how, rather, will he not be shown to have been changed in the divine nature, and to have fallen involuntarily under the passions of flesh according to it? ⟦s19⟧ For by the destruction of the natural things Arius and Apollinarius made their onslaught of such things against him. ⟦s19⟧ For the one teaching as doctrine that he was soulless, the other that he was irrational — which is without operation and without will — they blasphemed him as a creature and a thing made in himself. ⟦s20⟧ For each of them said that the Only-begotten was passible by the nature of Godhead, but not by the nature of flesh; even if Apollinarius, being still more lavish in impiety, also declared the perfect conversion into flesh, by the falling-away and change of his Godhead.
