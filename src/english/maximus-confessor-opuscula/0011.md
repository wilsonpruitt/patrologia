⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0108] nor indeed of manhood, to prevail over spirits, since not all have the strength; rather, the achievement is seen to be of an operation through the Spirit.» ⟦s2⟧ So the Father has spoken truly, insofar as flesh and man is understood, that the strength against the demons was the work of the Spirit, and not of the flesh, that is, of manhood. ⟦s3⟧ For the body of the Lord is admittedly holy, and has all power against every disease; but it was, and is, holy, not because it is understood simply as flesh, being in its own principles alone, but inasmuch as it is the temple of God the Word who dwells in it, sanctifying his own flesh through his own Spirit.

⟦s4+5⟧ So that, as this divine man says, in truth it is not the natural operation of flesh and the power of manhood that undoes Satan; nor does the nature of flesh operate this; since all men too, being in the flesh, and one according to the principle of manhood, would, had they chosen to operate, have had the strength against spirits as well. ⟦s6⟧ Having taught these things, then, he has clearly established, and has become the interpreter of his own words, that it is by indwelling, which is to be united according to one hypostasis, and not by being flesh, being in its own principles alone, that it is holy and life-giving, having power against every disease. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ Let us too, then, following the guidance of the saints, keep both the principle of the difference unimpaired, preserving the natures themselves, and what belongs to them according to essence, even after the union; and the mode of the economy wholly indissoluble, maintaining in unitary fashion the utmost growing-together of the things united; and let us drive from ourselves all division and confusion.

⟦s7+8+9⟧ But if we do not thus piously receive and understand, according to the principle of the union, the expressions spoken singularly by the saints, the theandric and the «one and kindred», but suppose this operation either as natural, or again as hypostatic, we shall of necessity teach as doctrine that Christ is one nature, partaking at all in neither of those of which he consists, since Christ is according to nature none of them. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ For a nature could never have communion with a nature according to nature; but, without participation, it is altogether different in essence from every other. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ This, then, will follow for us if we call the operation natural; but if again hypostatic, we shall alienate him from the Father and the Spirit according to the operation. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ For in nothing characteristic [0109] of his own hypostasis could the Son ever have communion with the Father and the Spirit.

⟦s10⟧ Let us by no means, then, wish to confess the operation in the very Word incarnate, and perfectly become man, to be natural or hypostatic. ⟦s11+12+13⟧ For none of the holy Fathers, from his divine descent to us until now, has handed down that we confess a natural or hypostatic operation in him; nay, not even «one» expressly, so far as I know, except only the divine Cyril. ⟦s11+12+13⟧ And as we receive this wholeheartedly, although it belongs to him alone, for the sake of the union, how is it not fitting also to receive joyfully the dual expressions of almost all the holy Fathers, and again of himself, for the sake of the natural difference? ⟦s11+12+13⟧ Lest, by our appeal to and acceptance of his one and only word, we bring about the casting-out and the invalidation of the words of himself and of the other saints; as though none other of them but Cyril alone had been entrusted with the pious exactness of the proclamation; and he not in his other words, those which show the difference, but only in the «one and kindred operation».

⟦s11+12+13⟧ Besides, how do some say that the operation in Christ God is simply one? ⟦s14⟧ For it must of necessity belong either to his parts, or to him altogether as a whole. ⟦s15⟧ But if to the parts, it will be partitioned, being cut, that is, toward both; and suffering this, it will be dissolved altogether, and will dissolve with itself the things united according to it. ⟦s16⟧ But if to the whole, it will be receptive of contraries, being mortal and immortal, created and uncreated, circumscribed and uncircumscribed. ⟦s17⟧ And if this, Christ had his own operation by habit and privation, being and not being; through which, as operating fittingly according to it, he is receptive of contraries in the same respect, and not in one respect and another: than which what is more impious?

⟦s18⟧ The same may also be said concerning the will. ⟦s19⟧ And how then did the Word take his own flesh as a fellow-worker, according to Cyril the wise, if it did not have the operation according to nature? ⟦s20⟧ For what has neither operation nor natural movement is altogether dead and soulless; and for what reason would he admit the dead into co-operation? ⟦s21+22+23⟧ For what is without operation lies idle in total immobility. ⟦s21+22+23⟧ And how did he show it able to give life, as Cyril himself again taught, through its motions according to nature, such as touch, voice, walking, if these were not its own, or in it according to essence? ⟦s21+22+23⟧ For if they were its own according to essence, the flesh altogether had a natural operation; for the movement of the hands and of the feet, and the voice in utterance, belong to its essential operation. ⟦s24⟧ But if [0112] these are not its own according to nature, the Saviour did not show it able to give life.

⟦s25⟧ These small things out of many, having been taught by the teachings of you and of the God-wise saints like you, I have made bold, at your most sacred bidding, to inscribe through this modest letter, and to venture on things beyond my power, being small and of no account; and having neither a mind apt in its aim for grasping, nor speech pliant for clarity, nor a well-ordered character for the accomplishing of the better things; but rather being sheltered by grace, through your mediation, by the all-good God, who also covers the multitudes of my sins. ⟦s26⟧ And I entreat you with tears, falling down, as though present, before your honoured footsteps, since the Holy Spirit has made you overseers and saviours of souls, not to shrink from making a kindly correction where I have written without exactness; so that from this you may show yourselves mediators of God rather than imitators, not only having obtained by habit his illumination upon all, and his mercy, but also furnishing it by dignity. ⟦s26⟧ To whom I also beseech you to commend the weakness of me, the poor and needy, that I may be sheltered from the flame, visible and invisible, that devours all because of my sins; and may rest and be guarded continually in the dew of his all-holy and orthodox faith, and of sacred virtue. ⟦s27⟧ Amen.
