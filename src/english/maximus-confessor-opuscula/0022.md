⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0204] and circumscribing in it its own procession.

⟦s2⟧ Whence for this reason, according to the most wise Cyril, the name «Christ» has neither the force of a definition (for it is not a species predicated of many that differ in number), nor indeed does it signify the essence of anything. ⟦s3⟧ For it is not an individual, referred up to a species or a genus, or circumscribed by these according to essence; but a composite hypostasis, identifying in itself to the utmost the natural division of the extremes, and leading them into one by the union of its own parts.

⟦s4⟧ But if perhaps, against those who, through the Lord's not being without operation according to the flesh, confess his innate and human operation, they infer that he is not without hypostasis either, this is to do violence to the hypostasis. ⟦s5+6⟧ For the nature's not being without hypostasis does not make it to be a hypostasis; just as neither does the body's not being shapeless make every body whatever a shape; nor does the begotten's or the visible's not being unbegotten or not invisible make it begetting or sight. ⟦s7⟧ And simply, anything else whatever, being denied to be that which it is not, makes the thing affirmed, that about which the relation itself is naturally contemplated, to be a relation.

⟦s8⟧ Besides, even if this perhaps were so, when it is not, it would be nothing to the natural operations, which are not rejected by the holy Fathers equally with the hypostases, nor do they divide the one into two, as the hypostases do. ⟦s9⟧ For who is he that could show that it is not good, for the declaration of the essential difference, to hold the natural operations in the same [Christ]? ⟦s9⟧ And for what reason, and how, and what is the word that lays this down, or what approved Father, so that they may not merely pronounce it? ⟦s10⟧ For if it is not good to confess the natural operations, neither is it to confess the natures themselves. ⟦s11⟧ Or how these, but not those? ⟦s12⟧ But if both are good, for what reason do they range themselves against the good, and construct the hypostasis from the nature's not being without hypostasis?

⟦s13⟧ For if they knew that the operations, like the hypostases, were censured by the Fathers, not unreasonably would they cast them away. ⟦s14⟧ But if we know the Fathers proclaiming these, and confessing a divine and a human operation, as indeed also a nature, and exhorting us to confess them, but a divine or human hypostasis no one in any way whatever, except only the divided and man-worshipping Nestorius, why then, if the teaching of the saints is not to be rejected by them, do they play the sophist in such ways against it, toward the removal of the natural operations? ⟦s15⟧ And whose is it to infer these things, and thus, except Severus, the sophist and deranged, who zealously destroys his own positions and in no way prevails over the pious, because of the conspicuousness of the truth, even if he contrives with the shameless manner of sophistical drivel?

⟦s15⟧ [0205] For if the nature's not being without hypostasis makes it a hypostasis, then surely, by consequence, the hypostasis's not being without essence presents it as an essence. ⟦s16⟧ And those who say this, how do they not divide the account of theology by natures equal in number to the hypostases, if indeed, according to them, the hypostases that are not without essence are altogether essences; and how do they not confuse that of the economy, by the singleness of the nature on account of the one hypostasis? ⟦s17⟧ Being consistent with themselves, then, they will make doctrines of such things against themselves too.

⟦s18⟧ But for those who confess the natural operations, even to infer them thus is a grace, since through all things they gather the truth for themselves according to the tradition of the saints; according to which the not-without-hypostasis does not make the nature to be a hypostasis, but enhypostatic, so that it may not be taken as an accident by conception alone, but may be contemplated really as a form. ⟦s19⟧ So too the not-without-essence does not make the hypostasis an essence, but presents it as enousial, so that we may know it not as a bare idiom, but properly together with that in which the idiom is. ⟦s20+21⟧ As then there the enhypostatic signifies the enhyparctic, and the enhyparctic is that which partakes of essential and natural existence; so here too the operant, that is, the operative, properly signifies the potent; and the potent is that which has essential and natural power.

⟦s22⟧ Therefore to confess that the natures in Christ are not without hypostasis or without operation is not to infer hypostases or operating subjects, but to confess orthodoxly their essential and natural existences and operations, unto a true assurance and confirmation of God the Word incarnate, who is from them and in them and according to them (I mean the natures), operating according to the indissoluble union.

⟦s23⟧ And by what reason do they themselves, referring many operations, more than number, to one and the same, by saying «every divine and human operation» (for «every» suggests that we think not one nor two at all, nor three, [0208] but infinite operations exceeding number, just as also «all things» and «everything» do) in no way suspect division; and yet this division, against those who confess the natural two according to the divine Fathers, to the extent of knowing the difference, they infer, I know not how or why, along with the operating subjects? ⟦s24⟧ For if the many and different by nature do not divide and cut the one and the same into many operating subjects, how do the two? ⟦s25⟧ And who is it that rather blames the assault as unreasonable? ⟦s26⟧ For if they did not say and refer these as to the same and of the same, and as grown together with one another by the interpenetration toward one another through the whole, and preserved in him according to the indissoluble union, and moved by him, yet according to one thing and another, uniformly; through which he both put forth divinely the power of the miracles, as God by nature, and willingly accepted the experience of the sufferings for us, as man by nature; then their assault would after all not be implausible. ⟦s27⟧ But as it is, since they do not say this, but refer these as of the same and lead them up to the same, whence and how do they infer the operating subjects? ⟦s28⟧ And why, if it is reasonable, do they not first infer the many against themselves, so far as concerns the many operations? ⟦s29⟧ For as the two infer the two, so surely of necessity the many infer the many. ⟦s30⟧ But if not even this, how that follows I cannot see; and I think neither can anyone else of those who prefer the truth to seeming.

⟦s30⟧ And how again is the one put forward by them, as if contradictorily, in his case against the many? ⟦s31⟧ And what is this one, and how do they make it a doctrine? ⟦s32⟧ For it is not possible for the same to have both many and one. ⟦s33⟧ For if many, not one; and if one, how again many? ⟦s34⟧ For either that one will altogether thrust these away, or these will thrust that one away, according to their own account, by which they put forward the one for the overthrow of the natural two. ⟦s35⟧ But if perhaps they say that the one is produced from many, it is not the nature, as has been said, of things in a subject to be compounded. ⟦s36⟧ Nor again is it the nature of the one from many to be cut up into many. ⟦s37+38⟧ Let them then first themselves destroy the more through the one operation, lest they introduce many operating subjects against themselves; and through the many, again, the one, lest they make a doctrine of co-essentiation; and so let them make war on those who hold the two, if indeed they are not feeble. ⟦s39⟧ For it is utterly impossible for those who overthrow themselves together with their own positions, needing nothing else at all for this, to attack others. ⟦s40⟧ For that which has a base, that which is, is firmly founded by it, [0209] suffering on no side any downfall whatever. ⟦s41⟧ For that alone is truly vigorous and strong which has the truth confirming it; but not that which receives its dissolution from another, let alone from itself.

⟦s42⟧ Having let these men go, then, it befits us altogether, being pious, and confessing really the existence of each of the natures preserved in the one God the Word incarnate, to confess altogether also the innate operation, as also the will; and through the pious and most true belief concerning each, the divine together with the human, of the same, and concerning the things belonging to him naturally, to make firm the orthodox teaching of the holy Fathers, who handed down to the holy catholic Churches of God both to think and to say these things about him; and to deny nothing of what he was from the beginning and became later for us, nor of all the idioms that characterize these naturally. ⟦s43⟧ For they taught that the same is perfect in both, and in both natures, and preserving without lack the natural property of each of those from which he was, and in all things like us, and likewise tempted according to volition, without sin alone.

⟦s44⟧ Hearing, then, these blessed voices, we deny utterly nothing in his case, neither of what is ours and natural, nor of what is the Father's and divine; but we hold that the same has different natures and natural operations and essential wills, that is, two. ⟦s45⟧ For neither our nature nor indeed the Father's stands outside essence, or without innate will or operation. ⟦s46+47+48⟧ And if this is so, it is clear that he who subsisted from these, I mean Godhead and manhood, according to one and the same hypostasis, and who is properly the same God and man, is consubstantial with us and of the same operation, having the same innate volition according to the manhood; and consubstantial with the Father and of the same operation, possessing the same volition according to the Godhead; and wholly akin to those above in all that is akin, and wholly akin to those below in all that is akin, without sin alone. ⟦s46+47+48⟧ And he who does not so hold does not receive the God-inspired Fathers who proclaim these things; but it is clear whom he receives, and of what sort.

⟦s46+47+48⟧ May it be granted that all of us, by the mediation of your God-wrought prayers, guarded always in soul and body in the temple of God, the catholic and apostolic Church, may hold this sincere and orthodox confession of our great God and Saviour before him and all creation, and in place of all things and before all things [0212] may bring it to him at his all-glorious appearing; and may be deemed worthy by him of the like portion, and partake of the everlasting and blessed gifts promised to those who confess him unashamedly before men, and enjoy in experience, according to our prayers, those which we have now tasted, deemed worthy by faith; by the prayers and intercessions of our all-glorious and all-holy, immaculate Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, and of all the saints. ⟦s46+47+48⟧ Amen.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 1. ⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The invisible is the negation of what is seen. ⟦s1+2⟧ But what is affirmed is not affirmed toward sight; rather, the visible is affirmed; which is the relation of the not-invisible, that is, that toward which the not-invisible has its relation. ⟦s3⟧ And of sight, the relation toward which it is affirmed is not the not-invisible, but blindness.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ 2. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Against those who say that there is no nature without hypostasis, one must convert it: neither is there a hypostasis without essence. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Then one must add that, just as saying that the hypostasis is not without essence does not present it as being an essence (for so it would be to divide and to confuse, the one the mystery of theology, the other that of the economy), so neither does saying that the nature is not without hypostasis introduce the hypostasis.
