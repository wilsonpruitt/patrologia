⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ OPUSCULUM 21 — ON QUALITY, PROPERTY AND DIFFERENCE

⟦s1+2+3⟧ *Of the same, on quality, property, and difference, to Theodore, presbyter in Mazarium.*

⟦s1+2+3⟧ I do not think that you, most sacred men, need anything else at all for knowledge, who have already acquired, [0248] through simple virtue, [the power] to call upon in your mind the one thing that makes wise, the Holy Spirit; from whom you also pour forth divine things for others as from a spring, according to the infinitely-giving outpouring of wisdom, making an outflow of your notions through the utterance of speech, so as to extend it with love for man, very fully, even to the lowly like me, who have no share in any learning, toward divine science and learning; even though I know that you are using condescension by your question, in order to advance us, and still more, through the utmost lowering, to raise to your own height of knowledge us who lie somewhere below and are pressed by ignorance; so that, cleansing the sluggishness of my understanding by your stooping and its folly by your inquiry, you may lead [me] to a habit of knowledge, and to an eagerness vigorous and practical, from which salvation comes by grace to those being saved, through those like you who have been counted worthy to save the worthy.

⟦s4⟧ I say, then, about the things you bade [me] examine further, instructing [me]—I mean quality, and property, and difference—that their signification among those outside is manifold, and it would be long to unfold their divisions of these things, and to stretch out the discourse, since these matters belong not so much to the measure of a letter as to the labour of a treatise. ⟦s5⟧ But with the divine Fathers the declaration of these is coherent and concise, taken not of any subject, that is, of essence and nature, but of the things considered in the essence, and indeed of those considered in the hypostasis.

⟦s6⟧ Quality, then, they say is: essential, as in man the rational, or in a horse the neighing; and hypostatic, as of a certain man the hook-nosed or the snub-nosed, or of a certain horse the dapple-grey or the chestnut. ⟦s7⟧ And so it is also with all the other originated essences and hypostases, [quality] being considered in beings commonly and specifically, that is, universally and particularly; according to which also the difference of species and [0249] of individuals from one another is known, distinguishing clearly the truth of the realities.

⟦s8⟧ But in the case of the unbegotten and monarchic nature, quality could not be said at all, or properly. ⟦s9⟧ For the Divine is not from some essence and accidents; otherwise it would by all means be created, as composite and compounded from these. ⟦s10⟧ But by catachresis, and so far as one might conjecture from the things that are ours the things that are above us—since we are able neither otherwise than so, and barely, to receive dimly the knowledge of them, and to make it plain in speech at least somewhat, if not perfectly—natural quality is the all-holy, the all-powerful, the all-perfect, the more-than-perfect, the self-perfect, the self-ruling, the all-overseeing, and whatever other such thing is said that is natural and divine, and befits God only, as superessential. ⟦s11⟧ And hypostatic quality is: of the Father the unbegotten, of the Son the begotten, and of the Holy Spirit the proceeding; that is, unbegottenness, and the birth, and the procession; which they also name properties, because these belong to him alone and to no other [that is said concerning God], naturally or hypostatically. ⟦s12⟧ From these the essential and hypostatic differences are constituted: by catachresis, as I said, in the case of God; but properly and according to nature in the case of all originated things.

⟦s13⟧ Hence they say that these are the same as one another—I mean quality and property and difference—and that they hold the account of accidents, but not of any subject, that is, of essence. ⟦s14⟧ They differ, however, in that quality is more universal, and [is said] simply of all things, since none of the things that are, apart from God, is without quality, as [none is] without relation or without form. ⟦s15⟧ Property is particular, as compared with that, and is said not of all, but simply and in such a way [in any way whatever and simply, but in such a way], and of this essence and not another; and difference [is said] as constitutive of beings and distinguishing. ⟦s16⟧ Hence they name it thus, calling it constitutive difference, which distinguishes clearly, as I said, beings according to essence and nature, and according to person and hypostasis, and drives off every change and confusion of all things.

⟦s16⟧ This, then, and so standing, according to the teaching of the Fathers, is the treatment and clarification concerning quality and property and difference, to speak in summary. ⟦s17⟧ But it is impossible for any of these ever to be, or to be able to be conceived, without the essence that is their subject, of which each of them both is and is said; [0252] for quality and property and difference are said to be of an essence, and not of themselves; and he who does not say this has lost his reason, departing contrary to reason from the principle according to nature.

⟦s18⟧ From which [principle] Severus alone and beyond all, the deranged sophist, [departed], as one who imagines and fashions for himself things that exist nowhere at all, and speaks of a difference of qualities without essence in Christ, so as to declare him without existence, by the removal of the natures from which and in which he consists, and again by the co-removal of the qualities themselves. ⟦s19⟧ For they could not [subsist] when the natures are destroyed, so that one could no longer make Christ subsist either in these, or at all from these—I mean the qualities—even if the wretch tells the marvel of him as composite, and from this makes a complete denial of him. ⟦s20⟧ For the composite [nature] is not God or God's nature; otherwise every composite nature too would be God by nature. ⟦s21⟧ But if every composite nature is God by nature, then according to him the Father, who is incomposite, is not God; for if the composite is God by nature, the incomposite is not God by nature. ⟦s22⟧ And if the incomposite too is God by nature, the godless and god-opposing man would have shown himself a polytheist, not knowing the one who is truly God and became flesh, but kept, even in the composition of the hypostasis, his simplicity and incomposition according to nature in relation to the Father, as God by nature and from God, even though he was born man from men, through man, above men, by the assumption of flesh having the intellectual soul; even though the inhuman man does not even confess God who became man to be man, since he denies in him the nature of man, as also the divine. ⟦s23⟧ For he supposes him not two-natured, that is, double in nature, as the Fathers [do], but a certain composite and bastard nature, emulating the abomination of Apollinarius.

⟦s24⟧ In this way, removing the great mystery of the economy concerning us, the scoundrel has misused, ingeniously, like a clever rhetor, the appellation of names, making his stealthy theft of the realities hard to detect, and as it were tickling the sense into the acceptance of impiety. ⟦s25⟧ For he calls him, forsooth, God and man; but he removes from either appellation its properness, [that is,] its truth, as not confessing the natures whose appellations these manifestly are. ⟦s26⟧ And he speaks of difference while recognizing no difference, since according to him the realities that differ do not underlie [it] by nature; so that he may both slip in confusion through the one composite nature, and legislate non-existence by the denial of the natures; and may seem to cover the destruction with the names, and to cast off the confusion with the difference. ⟦s26⟧ So great is the truly ill-minded contrivance and conception of the ill-minded man, covered by the gloom of ignorance, and darkening those who are caught.

⟦s27⟧ To these, as men contending for him, we must turn our discourse, and say this as well: if, as you say, Severus, following Cyril of blessed [0253] memory, confesses the difference, avoiding the coalescence, how is it that he does not confess the natures too along with him, and in them, as that man does, recognize the essential difference, removing only their division, but sets the difference in bare qualities only, against that man's law and definition? ⟦s28⟧ For that the blessed Cyril confesses the natures, whose difference he also teaches, is plain from his not disdaining to share even with Nestorius, the divided, in the very confession of the natures, though not at all in [the confession] of the meaning. ⟦s29⟧ «For what,» says the teacher, «is common to me and Nestorius? ⟦s29⟧ To say the two natures [are] two, so far as to know the difference of the flesh and of God the Word; for this is other according to natural quality, and is not of one genus in essence with him.»

⟦s30⟧ If, then, he confesses the natures, and recognizes the difference in them, and piously renders the cause of the confession, saying, «because the flesh is other than the Word according to natural quality»—which is the same as to say, [according to] essence and operation, in relation to the difference of God the Word—how is it that the deranged Severus does not deign to share even with Cyril himself, whom he has feignedly honoured, and to utter the natures with him so far as to know the difference according to his teaching, not according to natural quality only, but also [according to] the not-being-of-one-genus in essence, so as to recognize at once the difference both of the natures and of the natural qualities, that is, of essences and operations; but, denying also the not-being-of-one-genus in essence of the flesh relative to the Word, which is the difference according to nature, should bring in instead the difference in qualities without existence (for every quality is wholly without existence apart from the essence that is its subject)? ⟦s31⟧ Doing this maliciously, and rising up against the true teaching of the wise Cyril—which is the same as to say, of all the God-approved Fathers—he shows himself alien [to it], and a partner and advocate of falsehood.

⟦s32⟧ Does he then, speaking of a difference of qualities in Christ, speak of the difference after the union, or before the union? ⟦s33⟧ For if before the union, he thinks of a division by all means, and not a difference, and teaches the union [as made] from divided things, as it were from things pre-subsisting by themselves; or he has dissolved it through the difference and confession of the qualities; or he declares that these were not united at all from the beginning. ⟦s34⟧ And if he says they were not united, it is plain that he does not think the natures whose qualities they are to be united either, even if he pretends to say so; for it is not possible that the ones should be united and the others ever divided, but [they must] undergo the same as each other, together with each other, whether union or division. ⟦s35⟧ If then, rejecting division, he declares that with the natures their qualities too were united, it is wholly necessary that he, keeping in step with himself throughout, either speak of one composite quality, just as of one composite nature, because of the union—and he has been convicted of recognizing not even the difference in quality, but of teaching the confusion in both, I mean of essences and of [0256] qualities—or, granting the qualities as different, and not fearing division, he would call the natures too different, not taking fright, nor making a pretext of a cut right through for turning away from and refusing the confession of them.

⟦s36⟧ For if according to him the qualities, confessed by him forsooth, have not suffered a cutting-through because of the difference, how will the natures be cut for the sake of the confession and the difference? ⟦s37⟧ But if he does not confess these, but confesses, forsooth, only the qualities, he is plainly destroying the natures, and teaching Christ as an aggregate of qualities, as we also know the natures of material things to be: constituted, however, in the subject of matter, and not considered in the bare qualities alone, as he depicts Christ, and for this reason names him a composite nature, meaning, clearly, that [nature] and no other which he fashioned and put together out of bare qualities. ⟦s38⟧ For of whatever he says the difference is, of these clearly [he says] also the union. ⟦s39⟧ For the difference is not of some things and the union of others, but of the same, and not of others.

⟦s40⟧ Therefore the deranged man declares Christ to be from qualities and qualities, and again shows him divided according to them, since he teaches not one composite [quality] because of the union, but different qualities in him—if indeed this is his best definition of the union. ⟦s41⟧ But if one law prevails for natures and again another for qualities, it is the work of his diligence—or of his stupefaction, to speak more fittingly—to show us this, so that we may learn how, according to the union, he recognizes the two natures as one nature, but not the different qualities as one quality; or by what reason he speaks of a difference of qualities, but does not speak of a difference of natures. ⟦s42⟧ And how is it that the qualities have not remained unconfused without difference, but the natures [have remained] unconfused even without it? ⟦s43⟧ And how are the qualities not divided according to the difference, but the natures are divided according to it? ⟦s44⟧ Or how, not being divided, are they not confessed together with the qualities? ⟦s45⟧ And why does he compose the natures but not compose the qualities, and how, when these have not been united into one composite quality, does he tell the marvel that those have been united into one composite nature? ⟦s46⟧ May whoever is an impious lover and advocate of that man make these things plain to us who are in perplexity, so that, providing their solution, he may dissolve the blame along with them; or, not being able [to do] this justly, he may carry off [the blame] with that man, with an even greater condemnation, because he has dared to call him in accord with the Fathers who is in everything at odds even with himself.

⟦s47⟧ Let these things be said by me in passing, God-honoured master, at your bidding, for the refutation and destruction of that man and of the shield-bearers of his faction. ⟦s48⟧ But it would be for thy God-minded understanding to instruct and teach the greater and more perfect things to the imperfect and infants like me, and [0257] to lead [them] to a more divine knowledge, so that thou mayest bring [them] into kinship with God through the true science of beings, of which the Word himself, who is above beings by nature, is the cause, our Lord Jesus Christ; to whom do not cease to commend me, the unworthy, in prayers, most holy and God-honoured Father.
