⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0785] *Of the same.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS, AND INTERROGATIONS, AND SELECTIONS

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *of various perplexing chapters.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION 1

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Which are the virtues of the soul, and which of the body?

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ The virtues of the soul are these: love, humility, meekness, long-suffering, forbearance, freedom from rancour, freedom from wrath, being without anger, being without envy, not judging, being without vainglory, almsgiving, temperance, freedom from avarice, compassion, being without arrogance, being without pride, compunction. ⟦s3⟧ And the virtues of the body are these: fasting, sleeping on the ground, vigil, self-control, poverty, freedom from distraction.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [0788] QUESTION 2

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is the saying in the Apostle: «*I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the mind also*»? [n: I Cor. xiv, 15.]

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ One sings with the spirit when he brings forth through the tongue only the utterance of what is sung. ⟦s3⟧ And he sings with the mind when, knowing the power of what is sung, he is gladdened by contemplation.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 3

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ What is: «*I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins did my mother conceive me with craving*»? [n: Psal. l, 7.]

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2+3⟧ Since the leading purpose of God was that we should not be begotten through marriage, out of corruption; but the transgression of the commandment brought in marriage, because Adam acted lawlessly, that is, set aside the law given him by God: all, therefore, who are begotten from Adam are conceived in iniquities, falling under the condemnation of the forefather. ⟦s4⟧ And the words, *And in sins did my mother conceive me with craving*, signify that Eve, the first mother of us all, conceived sin with craving, as though swelling with desire for pleasure. ⟦s5⟧ For this reason we too, falling under our mother's condemnation, say that we are conceived with craving in sins.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 4

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ What is that said in the Apostle: «*To be anathema from Christ for my kinsmen*»? [n: Rom. ix, 3.]

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ I prayed, he says, to be handed over from Christ [0789] to the devil, so as to be scourged, and to undergo on Israel's behalf the punishments which by justice they owed for their unbelief toward God, only that they might be saved; in the way in which the Lord also handed himself over, and became a curse for us.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 5

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ In how many modes does man sin?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ I think that man sins in four modes: by being carried away, by deceit, by ignorance, by disposition. ⟦s3⟧ And the first three come easily to full knowledge and repentance; but he who sins from disposition, and comes to repentance neither by experience nor by time, has a punishment that is incurable.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION 6

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ What does the Lord show in the Gospel, saying: «*Two sparrows are sold for an assarion*»? [n: Matth. x, 29.]

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Some say that the assarion is a piece of ten nummi. ⟦s3⟧ And by the ten is shown the letter iota; and this is the beginning of the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. ⟦s4⟧ Through the name of the Lord, then, are bought back the old and the new people, and the inner and the outer man.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 7

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is that taken up by Elisha, said of Elijah: «*Where is the God Aphphu?*»?

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s1+2⟧ It is interpreted according to three approaches: either, «Where is the God of my Father?»; or, «Where is the God of my great one?»; or, «Where is the God of the hidden one?»

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [0792] QUESTION 8

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ In how many modes are allegories, and what is tropology?

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Allegory is that concerning inanimate things: for example, mountains, hills, trees, and the rest. ⟦s2⟧ And tropology is that concerning our members: for example, head, eyes, and the rest. ⟦s3⟧ For tropology is so called from being turned.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 9

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ What is that said in the Psalm: «*Sitting, thou didst speak against thy brother, and against thy mother's son thou didst set a stumbling-block*»? [n: Psal. xlix, 20.]

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ He who speaks evil of the action of one of the same faith, and as it were sits down to the reviling of him, this one is fittingly said also to speak against his brother; and he who, carried along by envy, slanders the one adorned with wisdom and causes all to stumble, this is the one who sets a stumbling-block for his mother's son.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 10

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Since in the holy Diadochus, in the hundredth chapter, it is written that some will be judged through fire and purified in the age to come, I beg that the purpose of the Father be uncovered by clarification.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2+3⟧ Those who have acquired the perfection of love toward God, and through the virtues have raised aloft the wing of the soul, are, according to the Apostle, caught up in the clouds, and do not come into judgement. [n: Joan. v, 24] But those who have not wholly acquired the perfection, but have acquired both sins and right deeds, these come into the court of judgement, and there, through the weighing against each other of their good and bad deeds, being as it were tried by fire, [0793] if the scale of the good weighs down, they are cleansed of the punishment.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 11

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Concerning different justices.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Those wise in divine things say that there are three justices: human, angelic and divine. ⟦s3⟧ And the human, they say, is the equity and gratitude in the sensible things of this world; and the angelic, the ungrudging imparting of divine knowledge; and divine justice they define as suffering on behalf of those who sin.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 12

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For what reason did the Lord, rebuking Peter, say to him, *Satan*? [n: Matth. xvi, 23]

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ It was not, as some suppose, insultingly that the Lord called Peter Satan; but since the Lord's privations became habits for us: for example, his death became life for us; his dishonour became glory for us. ⟦s3⟧ And not knowing this, the apostle Peter, when the Lord said that he was about to suffer, supposed, following the nature of things, that it is impossible for the Life to die, or for so great a glory to be dishonoured. ⟦s4⟧ The Lord, then, destroying this supposition, on the ground that one ought not to seek the sequence of nature in things above nature, has deliberated to do these things through their contraries: through death, life; through dishonour, glory. ⟦s5⟧ So then, as [against] this opposing supposition of his, he says, *Get thee behind me*; that is, Follow me in counsel, and do not go before me, seeking a sequence of things. ⟦s6⟧ And the name Satan, they say, [0796] is interpreted «adversary»; which the Lord brought out not insultingly, but as though he were saying, «Thou who opposest my purpose».

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 13

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Since Gregory of Nyssa in his writings appears, to those who do not understand the depth of his lofty contemplation, to hint everywhere at a restoration, I beg thee, say what thou knowest about this.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church knows three restorations. ⟦s3⟧ One, that of each man according to the principle of virtue, in which he is restored when he has fulfilled the principle of virtue that lies with him. ⟦s4+5⟧ The second, that of the whole nature in the resurrection: the restoration to incorruption and immortality. ⟦s6⟧ And the third, of which Gregory of Nyssa has made most use in his discourses, is this: the restoration of the powers of the soul, which had fallen under sin, back into that for which they were created. ⟦s7⟧ For just as the whole nature must, in the resurrection of the flesh, receive back incorruption at a hoped-for time, so also the perverted powers of the soul must, in the stretching-out of the ages, cast off the memories of vice lodged in it; and, having passed through all the ages and finding no rest, the soul must come to God, who has no end. ⟦s8⟧ And thus by full knowledge, not by participation in the good things, it must receive back its powers, and be restored to its original state, and the Creator be shown to bear no blame for sin.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 14

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Why is it that, though there are many heavier insults, the Lord in the Gospels determines that he who calls his brother Fool is liable to the gehenna, but says that he who says Raca is subject to the council? [n: Matth. v, 22]

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ They say that the name Fool is [0797] taken of the senseless and witless; and Raca in the tongue of the Hebrews is interpreted «one to be spat upon». ⟦s3+4⟧ Since, then, the name fool is said of the godless and unbelieving, according to: *The senseless man hath said in his heart, There is no God*; [n: Psal. xiii, 1.] and according to Moses, who says, *This people is foolish and not wise*; [n: Deut. xxxii, 6.] whoever calls his brother, one of the same faith, godless and idolater, or heretic and unbeliever, this one says «fool», and justly becomes liable to the gehenna. ⟦s5+6⟧ But whoever says Raca, that is, «thou spat-upon» and «unclean», this one slanders his brother's life, and fittingly is subject to a lighter penalty.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 15

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ How must one understand what is said in the Creed: «Incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of Mary the Virgin»?

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Some of the saints say that from the Holy Spirit, after the manner of a man's seed, the soul was laid down; and the flesh was formed from the virginal bloods.

⟦p49⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 16

⟦p50⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Whom does the Apostle mean, saying «*Those who first hoped in Christ*», writing to the Ephesians? [n: Ephes. i, 12.]

⟦p51⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Every one of the saints before the Lord's sojourn who practised any virtue whatever, even if he did not know the whole mystery of the economy, yet, moved [0800] in part naturally, hoped and expected that he who made nature would himself also save it when it had been corrupted.

⟦p52⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Herod is interpreted «of skin»; one must, then, abhor fleshly thoughts.

⟦p53⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 17

⟦p54⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is it, according to the Apostle: That *flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God*? [n: I Cor. xv, 50.]

⟦p55⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Flesh is desire; and blood, anger. ⟦s3⟧ And fittingly, then, he who is not pure of these cannot inherit the kingdom of God.

⟦p56⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 18

⟦p57⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is the diapsalma?

⟦p58⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ I think that it is the conversion from concept to concept; or also from one mode of teaching to another mode.
