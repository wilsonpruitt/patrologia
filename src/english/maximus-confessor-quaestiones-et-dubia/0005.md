⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 64

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What does the paralytic signify, let down through the roof [0837] by four, [n: Luc. V, 19.] and was it possible, according to the history, for the house to be unroofed?

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the history, the house was certainly unroofed; since those who have become eyewitnesses of those places say that the roofs of the houses are of pumice stone and very light, so that one wishing to unroof can do this easily and quickly. ⟦s3⟧ But according to the mode of anagogy, the paralytic is every mind sick in sins and not able, through natural contemplation (for this is the door), to see the Word; but, having through faith unroofed the covering of the thickness of the letter of the law, it is let down through the four from its vain height to the Word who emptied himself, [n: Philipp. II, 7.] and by faith and practice receives its footing.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 65

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For what reason, when Elisha the prophet was mocked by the children, did he pray, and bears came out and dug up forty-two children? [n: IV Reg. II, 23.]

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Some say that the children were not Israelites, but of the foreigners; and not children in age, but in prudence. ⟦s3⟧ Since, then, they heard of the prophet doing many signs, and did not believe, but mocked him; and there were present some who did not share in their vain inclination; so that these might not be harmed, and the blasphemy run back up to God, God went out against them. ⟦s4⟧ But according to contemplation, every mind that is, like Elisha, bald, that is, pure of material concepts, is much mocked in the ascent of knowledge by the thoughts concerned with sensible things, and matter and form; forty signifying the sensible things, while matter and form, added, make up the number forty-two. ⟦s5⟧ The mind, then, is jeered at by such thoughts, so as to be thrown into reluctance toward virtue. ⟦s6⟧ These, then, it destroys through prayer, and through the bears, that is, through pleasure and desire; for such passions, handled by inversion toward the better, kill the mocking thoughts.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 66

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ How ought we to understand the Son's ignorance concerning the consummation? [n: Marc. XIII, 32.]

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Ignorance is twofold: the one blameworthy, the other blameless; and the one is in our power, the other not in our power. ⟦s3⟧ And the blameworthy and in our power is the ignorance [0840] concerning virtue and piety; but the blameless and not in our power is whatever we do not know though we wish to know: such as the things happening far off, the things about to come to be. ⟦s4⟧ If, then, in the holy prophets the things far off and not in our power were discerned by grace, how much more did the Son of God know all things, and through him the human, not by nature, but by the union with the Word? ⟦s5⟧ For as iron made red-hot has all the idioms of fire (for it gives light and burns), yet is not fire by nature, but iron; so too the human of the Lord, inasmuch as it was united to the Word, knew all things, and displayed in itself these things befitting God; but inasmuch as human nature in him was not united, it is said to be ignorant.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 67

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What does the monastic garb signify, and, before this, what is the tonsure of the hair?

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ As of all the members in the body the head has precedence, so also in the soul the mind holds the type of the head. ⟦s3⟧ This, then, must be shorn of all worldly concepts. ⟦s4⟧ And the colobium, [0841] since it covers the whole body but leaves only the hands bare, signifies that we must be clothed with moral philosophy, stripped of the practical activities of sin; and that this must always lie upon us, both upon potency and upon activity. ⟦s5+6⟧ For the power of the opposing dominion of the evil one is, according to Job, under the navel of the belly; and souls, according to blessed David, are filled with demonic mockeries. [n: Psal. xxxvii, 8.] And the mockeries are the various activities of fornication. ⟦s7⟧ And the analabos, since it has the cross both before and behind, signifies that we must, according to the Apostle, not only be crucified to the world, but the world also to us; [n: Galat. vi, 14.] so that, as we flee the world, we may have no hindrance, nor be held fast in our relation to it through the deceit that shows itself on its surface; nor again, pursued from behind out of the world by involuntary temptations, slacken the tension of piety; but may remain insensible and dead toward both the involuntary and the voluntary passions. ⟦s8⟧ And the cowl shows the grace of God guarding and covering our mind. ⟦s9+10⟧ For he who has shorn off the concepts of the world receives the helmet of salvation. [n: Eph. vi, 17.] And the sandals have this meaning. ⟦s11⟧ Since they are of dead skins, and lie under a small part of the body, in the same way as the whole body stands to the soles of the sandal, so must the soul use the body, and make it dead to the passions contrary to nature. ⟦s12⟧ And the mantle, since it is square, and the world too is of four elements, signifies that we must be clothed with natural contemplation, so as not to look at the things seen with a view to sense and passions, but through the principle in them to be led up to their creator. ⟦s13⟧ And that the left hand is bared signifies that our good actions must shine out in us, according to the Saviour's word: *that men may see our good works, and glorify our Father which is in the heavens.* ⟦s14+15⟧ And that the garments are black signifies that we must be invisible to the world, as having our *citizenship in heaven.* [n: Philipp. iii, 20.] And if anyone should take the pallium, being square, as standing also for the four generic virtues, he will fittingly not err.
