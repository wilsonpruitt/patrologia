⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 72

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Why is it that, when Luke says in the Acts concerning Paul that «*He was hastening, if it were possible for him, to keep the day of Pentecost at Jerusalem*», [n: Act. xx, 16.] he appears to have made genuflexions, which is forbidden by the canons?

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not speak of the Pentecost itself, on which the Spirit came down; but since the fifty days are called days of Pentecost, Paul was hastening to keep at Jerusalem the first day after the Pascha; so that it is plain that he bent the knee in the fasts.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 73

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is: «*If any man buildeth upon this foundation gold, or silver, or precious stones; wood, hay, stubble: the fire shall test them. ⟦s2⟧ If any man's work then abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward; but if any man's work shall be burned up, he shall suffer loss; but he himself shall be saved, yet so as through fire*»? [n: I Cor. iii, 13-15.]

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ The foundation is the faith of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ And one builds upon it, as gold, the theological mystagogy; as silver, the translucent life; as precious stones, the pious thoughts; as wood, the passionate attachment to sensible things; as hay, that which feeds on unreason; as stubble, that which works corruption. ⟦s4⟧ And in the case of those who have the good actions, the day of knowledge shows that in fire, that is, in the Spirit, the revelation of these has come about. ⟦s5⟧ But in those who sin, the works are burned up, the scrutiny burning through [0848] the conscience, and lessening the sins, and saving the man; but fining the idleness in the virtues of the time that went before. ⟦s6⟧ But in the age to come too, the works of sin will pass into non-existence, the nature receiving back its own powers whole through the fire and the judgement.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 74

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is it that David sings, saying, «*Give thy might unto thy servant, and save the son of thine handmaid*»? [n: Psal. lxxxv, 16.]

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Since by nature we are slaves of God, and sons of his handmaid, Wisdom, he prays that the might be given to us first, that is, the dominion over the passions; and then through this salvation comes to us.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 75

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Since in the Kingdoms it is written that God incited David to number the people, [n: II Reg. xxiv, 1.] but in the Paralipomena it says the devil, how will the divine Scripture be shown to agree with itself?

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Since the Apostle calls the devil *the god of this age*, [n: II Cor. iv, 4.] the passage of the Kingdoms is to be taken in this sense. ⟦s3⟧ Or also otherwise: since without the [0849] providence of God nothing comes to be, but all things come to be either by good pleasure, or by economy, or by permission, what is written in the Kingdoms, that God incited, it is pious to understand as standing for «permitted»; but in the Paralipomena, the devil as the one who effected it and became its cause. ⟦s4+5+6+7⟧ And seventy thousand fall, those found with Absalom: those who have the passion of conceit and pride. ⟦s4+5+6+7⟧ But according to contemplation this signifies that every David who, not by disposition but by being carried away, has begotten a thought of elation, having changed his mind asks God, and the thoughts concerned with temporal and transient things die. ⟦s4+5+6+7⟧ For the number seventy signifies temporal motion, because of the weekly cycle. ⟦s8⟧ For this reason, then, it is better to beg to be pursued by the hostile demons, and not famine of hearing the word of God. [n: Amos viii, 11.]

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 76

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What is that which is said in the Apostle, «*I would they were even cut off*»? [n: Galat. v, 12.]

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ This has been said instead of: They will lament and beat their breasts, coming to repentance for the sins they committed in unsettling the faithful.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 77

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For what reason are Moabites and Ammanites forbidden to enter into the temple of the Lord, until the third and fourth and tenth generation, and even for ever? [n: Deut. xxiii, 3; I Esdr. xiii, 1.]

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ Since Moabite is interpreted «father's bowel», and Ammanite «mother's father», it [0852] is signified by these that whoever takes from another an example of vice, this man has laid bare the bowel of the father; but whoever begets sin from himself, this man is father of a mother, that is, of his own sin. ⟦s3⟧ Such men, then, will not enter into the house of the Lord until the third and fourth and tenth generation, and even for ever. ⟦s4⟧ That is: he who does not, through good habit and practice (for this is the third and fourth generation of the virtues), and through the name and faith of the Lord Jesus, and for the sake of the ten commandments of the law, and through the regeneration of the age that abides, which is to be, bring himself into kinship with God, will not enter into the house of God: that is, into the heavenly city, in which is *the dwelling of all who rejoice.* [n: Psal. lxxxvi, 7.]

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 78

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Why is it that he who is leprous in part is unclean according to the law, but he who is leprous all over is clean?

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ He who is leprous in some part has dead the part in which the leprosy is. ⟦s3⟧ For as the vital blood withdraws, the place becomes hollow; and when the hollow of the leprosy sank beneath the priest's touch, it defiled such a man. ⟦s3⟧ So too he who has become leprous in some part of the soul, the soul being hollowed, that is, brought low, through the withdrawal of the vital power of virtue, becomes unclean. ⟦s4+5⟧ But he who is leprous all over has the colour of the leper, but has the vital blood throughout the whole body sown in the surface. ⟦s6⟧ This, then, signifies that he who has come to the uttermost limit of vice, and then repented, takes up again the vital power of virtue, and has only his conscience coloured by the former preconceptions. ⟦s7⟧ For he cannot, as though he had not done them, not take account of the things he did. ⟦s8⟧ For this reason, then, the law declares such a man clean.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION 79

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ What do the five sacrifices offered according to the law signify: the sheep, the ox, the goat, the turtle-dove, the dove?

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦s2⟧ According to one approach, the ram is taken, as the ruling part, for the rational power; the bull for the incensive power; the goat for the desiring power; the turtle-dove for temperance; the dove for sanctification. ⟦s3⟧ But if one must also inquire naturally into the things that belong to each animal, and fit to each of them its proper contemplation, those who have examined such matters say that the sheep offers three things to its owner: [0853] the fleece, the milk, the lamb. ⟦s4⟧ And the pupil of its eye goes round together with the sun, and each day it voids three hundred and sixty-five droppings. ⟦s5⟧ And the rational sheep, man, if he has been eager to offer himself as a sacrifice to God who acquired him, owes to give, as fleece, moral practice; as milk, natural contemplation (for this nourishes the mind); and as lamb, begetting through teaching the one who is being discipled, and being eager to bring him to God in all things like himself and perfect. ⟦s6⟧ And such a man also has the pupil of the eye going round together with the sun: that is, the mind goes round together with the *Sun of righteousness*, who carries our affairs through the provident governance of beings; that is, it follows, giving thanks both in afflictions and in prosperity, and at the providence that leads all things well. ⟦s7⟧ And such a man casts off each day three hundred and sixty-five droppings: that is, the things under time and coming-to-be, corruptible and superfluous, he casts away at every moment.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And concerning the ox they say that it possesses a triangular heart, and a five-lobed kidney; and it also has three stomachs; and by day it has eyes like light, but by night like fire; and for a third of the day and a third of the night, looking toward the east, it breathes, blowing. ⟦s2⟧ Its right nostril is fragrant with musk; and its blood is destructive of all the tame animals, except the dog. ⟦s3⟧ We too, then, carrying like an ox the yoke of Christ, handling it, must cut open the stony hearts, and uproot the thorns of the passions, and broaden the earth of the heart for the reception of the divine word. ⟦s4⟧ And we must have the heart triangular too, that is, have the account of the Trinity sound in the heart; so that, as the heart naturally distributes life to the body, so too piety toward God may hold together the members of the soul. ⟦s5⟧ And we must have the kidney five-lobed, that is, the desiring power not turned toward the passions, but surveying creation through the five senses unto the sole desire and love of the Creator. ⟦s6⟧ And we must possess three stomachs: the stomach, first receiving the food, and through its digestive power sending on the pure part of it to each of the members, gives the rest to the belly. ⟦s6⟧ So, then, it befits us, being led through practical and natural and theological contemplation, and apportioning what is fitting to all the members of the soul, to take forethought also for the lesser part, that is, the body; for this is the belly. ⟦s7⟧ And for a third of the day and a third of the night, looking toward the east, to breathe: that is, looking to the threefold providence of God, conserving and converting and chastening, to give thanks both in the night of temptations and in the day of prosperity; and to have the eyes by night like fire, but by day like light: that is, a contemplative power that in the day of the virtues receives from the *Sun of righteousness* the rays of knowledge, and in the night of the temptations brought on in close succession for virtue's sake, [0856] grasps the principles of the abandonments. ⟦s7⟧ And to have the right nostril fragrant too. ⟦s8⟧ The right part of man is the soul, since we are twofold, of soul and body. ⟦s9⟧ He, then, who through the virtues of the soul has drawn to himself the grace of the Holy Spirit becomes fragrant to the others. ⟦s10⟧ And the blood of such a man is destructive of the tame animals: that is, anger, by perversion, is destructive to the men who imitate it, but food for the demons that bark against us.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And the goat has such a nature as this: climbing the mountains easily, it walks on cliffs, and takes delight in musical tunes; and its right horn, if it is burned as incense in any place whatever, produces the appearance of an earthquake. ⟦s2⟧ And it voids each day two hundred droppings. ⟦s3⟧ So our soul too must leap over the precipices of temptations with good courage, and, climbing the prophetic mountains, hunt the height of knowledge, and take pleasure in it; and not only take pleasure, but also, through the reed-pipe, that is, the practised word, teaching, put pleasure into the hearers. ⟦s4⟧ And the right horn signifies that he who has brought the contemplative part of the soul to God through prayer, becoming fragrant to the hearers, shakes the habit of the fleshly passions in them. ⟦s5⟧ Such a man at every moment casts off the error concerning sensible things. ⟦s6⟧ For the senses, entwined with sensible things …
