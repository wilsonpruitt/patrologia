⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 11

⟦s1⟧ Abba Peter, priest of the laura of our father among the saints Sabas, told us about Agiodulus: "When he was superior of the laura of blessed Gerasimus, it happened that one of the brethren there died, and the elder did not hear of it. ⟦s2⟧ And when the canonarch struck the wood for all the brethren to gather and escort the dead man, the elder came, and seeing the brother's body lying in the church, he was grieved that he had not embraced him before he went out of this life. ⟦s3+4⟧ And going to the bier he says to the dead man, 'Rise, brother, give me a kiss.' And [2861] [b: A] he rose up and kissed the elder. ⟦s5⟧ And the elder says to him, 'Sleep now, until the Son of God comes and raises thee.'"

⟦s6⟧ The same abba Agiodulus, going up along the banks of the holy Jordan, was turning over in his thought what had become of the chosen stones that were thrown in by Joshua son of Nun in place of those taken up by him. ⟦s6⟧ And as he was reasoning on these things, at once the waters parted this way and that, and he beheld the twelve stones; and making a prostration to God, he withdrew.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 12

⟦s1⟧ A brother asked abba Olympius, priest of the laura of abba Gerasimus, saying, "Tell me a word." ⟦s2⟧ He says to him, "Do not sit with heretics, and keep hold of thy tongue and thy belly, [b: B] and wherever thou sittest, say constantly, 'I am a stranger.'"

⟦s2⟧ CHAPTER 13

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ They used to say of abba Mark the anchorite (he lived near the monastery of Penthucla) that for sixty-nine years he kept this practice, fasting the whole week through, so that some thought he was without flesh. ⟦s1+2⟧ He worked night and day for the sake of Christ's promises, and gave the proceeds to the poor, and never took anything at all from anyone. ⟦s1+2⟧ Hearing about this, Christ-loving people came to give him a love-offering; but he said, "I do not take it. ⟦s1+2⟧ My own handiwork feeds me, and those who come to me for God's sake."

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 14

⟦s1⟧ Abba Polychronius told us again: "In the coenobium of Penthucla there was a brother who paid great heed to himself, and an ascetic. ⟦s2⟧ He was warred against with fornication, and not bearing the warfare, he went out of the monastery and went off to Jericho to fulfil his desire. ⟦s3⟧ And as he went into the house of harlotry, at once he became leprous all over. ⟦s3⟧ And seeing himself in such a state, at once he turned back to his monastery, giving thanks to God, and saying, 'God has brought this sickness on me, so that my soul may be saved.' And he greatly glorified God."

⟦s4⟧ CHAPTER 15

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ They used to say of abba Conon, superior of Penthucla, that one day, as he was going off to the village of Biton Hagion [?], Hebrews met him and wanted to kill him. ⟦s1+2⟧ And baring their swords, they ran at the elder, and coming close they stretched out their hands to strike him; and their hands stayed in the air, motionless. ⟦s1+2⟧ And the elder made a prayer for them, and they went away rejoicing and glorifying God.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [2864] CHAPTER 16

⟦s1⟧ An elder lived in the laura of abba Peter near the holy Jordan, named Nicholas. ⟦s2⟧ He told us: "Once when I was living at Raithou, three of us brethren were sent on a service into the Thebaid. ⟦s3⟧ And as we were going through the desert, we lost the way and wandered about in the desert. ⟦s4⟧ So when our water was spent, and we found no water for some days, we began to faint from thirst and from the heat. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10+11+12⟧ And at last, being unable to walk, we found tamarisk trees in that same desert and laid ourselves down, each of us where he found a shady place, waiting for death from thirst. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10+11+12⟧ And as I lay there I fall into a trance, and I see a pool full of water [b: B] and overflowing, and two men standing up on the edge of the pool and drawing water with a wooden cup. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10+11+12⟧ And I began to beg the one, saying, 'Do me a kindness, sir, give me a little water, for I am fainting.' But he would not give it me. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10+11+12⟧ The other says to him, 'Give him a little.' He answers him, saying, 'Let us not give him any, for he is very lazy and neglects himself.' He says to him, 'Yes, yes, that he is lazy, he is lazy; but because he is a stranger, let us give to him.' And so he gave to me," he says, "and to those with me. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10+11+12⟧ And we drank, and we journeyed three more days without drinking; and then we reached inhabited country."

⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10+11+12⟧ CHAPTER 17

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ This too the same elder told, about a great elder in that same laura, [2865] [b: A] that he spent fifty years sitting in his cave, neither drinking wine nor eating bread, but only bran; and he partook three times a week.

⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 18

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Abba Polychronius told us again: "Another elder lived in that same laura of abba Peter, and often he would withdraw and stay on the banks of the holy Jordan; and wherever he found a lion's lair, there he slept. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ So one day he finds two lion cubs in a cave, and he carries them into the church in the cloak he had on, and he said, 'If we kept the commandments of our Lord Jesus Christ, these would have had to fear us. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ But because of our sins we have become slaves, and it is rather we who fear them.' And the brethren, greatly profited, withdrew to their caves."

⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ CHAPTER 19

⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ [b: B] Abba Elias the grazer told us: "Once when I was living in a cave in the parts of the Jordan, because I would not communicate with abba Macarius, bishop of Jerusalem, one day, about the sixth hour, when the heat was very fierce, someone knocked at my cave. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ And going out I saw a woman, and I say to her, 'What art thou doing here?' And she answered and says to me, 'Lord abba, I too follow this way of life, and I live about a mile from thy cell, in a small cave.' And she showed me the place, towards the south. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ And she says to me, 'Crossing this desert, I have grown thirsty in the great heat; but do me a kindness, give me a little water.' And I brought out my flask and gave it to her. ⟦s7⟧ She took it and drank, and I sent her away. ⟦s8⟧ And after she had gone, the devil began to war against me over her, and to cast thoughts into me. ⟦s9⟧ And being overmastered, and not bearing the burning, I took my staff and went out of the cave, with the heat scorching even the stones, and set off towards her, to fulfil the desire. ⟦s10⟧ When I had gone about one stade, with the desire inflaming me, I fall into a trance; and I saw the earth opened, and myself being dragged down, and I see dead bodies lying there, rotted and burst open, and filled with a stench past imagining; and a man of holy appearance showing me these things, and saying to me, 'See, this is a woman's, this is a man's. ⟦s10⟧ Enjoy whichever thou wilt, as much as thou wilt, of thy desire. ⟦s11⟧ See how many toils thou wouldst lose for such a pleasure; see for what sort of sin you would deprive yourselves of the kingdom of heaven. ⟦s12+13+14+15⟧ Alas for human nature! ⟦s12+13+14+15⟧ For one hour wilt thou be stripped of all that labour?' And I, from the great stench, fell to the ground. ⟦s12+13+14+15⟧ And the one who appeared to me, [2868] [b: A] the man of holy appearance, came and raised me up, and stopped the warfare. ⟦s12+13+14+15⟧ And I withdrew to my cell, giving thanks to God."

⟦s12+13+14+15⟧ CHAPTER 20

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ One of the Fathers told me that a dragonarius told him, saying: "We had a war in the parts of Africa with the Mauritanians, and being beaten by the barbarians, we were put to flight. ⟦s2⟧ So the barbarians pursued after us, and killed many of us. ⟦s3⟧ And one barbarian overtook me too," he says, "and stretched out his spear to strike me. ⟦s4+5⟧ And as I saw it, I began to entreat God and say, 'Lord God, who didst appear to thy handmaid Thecla, and deliver her out of lawless hands, deliver me from this distress, and save me from this bitter death, and I will go away into the desert and keep stillness.' And turning round," he says, "I saw none of the barbarians. ⟦s6⟧ And at once I came to this laura of Kopratha. ⟦s7⟧ And behold, by the grace of God, I have been in this cave thirty-five years."

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 21

⟦s1⟧ Abba Gerontius, superior of the monastery of our father among the saints Euthymius, told me: "We were three grazers beyond the Dead Sea, towards Besimous. ⟦s2⟧ And as we were walking on the mountain, another grazer was walking below us along the shore of the sea. ⟦s3⟧ And it happened that Saracens passing through those places met him. ⟦s4+5⟧ So when they had gone past him, one of the Saracens turned back and beheaded the anchorite, while we watched from afar; for we were up on the mountain. ⟦s6⟧ And as we wept for the anchorite, behold, suddenly a bird came down over the Saracen, and seizing him it carried him up into the air, and let him fall to the ground, where the Saracen was broken limb from limb."

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [2869] CHAPTER 22

⟦s1⟧ [b: A] An elder lived in the coenobium of our father among the saints Theodosius the archimandrite, named Conon, a Cilician by race, who kept this rule for thirty-five years: once a week he partook of bread and water, working without ceasing, and never absent from the church.

⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 23

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Another elder we saw in that same monastery, Theodulus, formerly a soldier, fasting every day, and never sleeping on his side.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 24

⟦s1⟧ [b: B] There was an elder living in the cells of Choziba, and the elders of that place told us about him: "When he was in his village, this was his practice: whenever he saw someone in his village unable, for great poverty, to sow his own field, he would go off by night, without the owner of the field knowing, and take his own oxen and his own seed and sow the other man's field. ⟦s2⟧ And when he came into the desert and stayed in the cells of Choziba, the elder had the same compassion again. ⟦s3⟧ For he would go off to the road that leads from the holy Jordan to the holy city, carrying loaves and water. ⟦s4⟧ And whenever he saw someone worn out, he carried his load and went up as far as the holy Mount of Olives; and he came back again the same way, carrying, if he found others, their loads as far as Jericho. ⟦s5⟧ So one could see the elder, now carrying a great load and sweating, now bearing a child on his shoulder; and there were times when he carried two. ⟦s6⟧ At other times he would sit mending shoes, if they were torn, a man's or a woman's; for he carried what was needed. ⟦s7⟧ Some he gave to drink from the water he carried with him; to others he gave loaves as well. ⟦s8⟧ And if he found someone naked, he gave him even the garment he was wearing. ⟦s9⟧ And one could see him toiling all day long. ⟦s10⟧ And whenever he found a dead man on the road, he sang the office over him and buried him."

⟦s10⟧ CHAPTER 25

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Abba Gregory, who had been of the Scholarii, told us: "There was a brother in the coenobium of Choziba who had learned the prayer of oblation of the holy anaphora. ⟦s2⟧ So one day he was sent to bring the blessings, and as he was coming to the monastery he said the prayer of oblation as though in the course of the psalm-singing. ⟦s3+4⟧ And the deacons set out those same blessings on the paten at the holy altar. ⟦s3+4⟧ And [2872] when abba John was making the offering (he was a priest then, surnamed the Chozebite, and later became bishop of Caesarea in Palestine), he did not behold, as was usual, the descent of the Holy Spirit. ⟦s5+6⟧ And grieved lest he himself had sinned in some way, and for that the Holy Spirit had departed, he went into the diaconicon weeping, and throwing himself on his face. ⟦s5+6⟧ And the angel of the Lord appears to him, saying, 'Since the brother, carrying the blessings on the road, said the holy anaphora, they were consecrated, and they are perfected.' And from that time the elder laid down a rule that no one who did not have ordination should learn the holy anaphora, nor say it just anyhow at any time at all, without a consecrated place."
