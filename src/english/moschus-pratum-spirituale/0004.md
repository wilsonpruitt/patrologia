⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 33

⟦s1⟧ One of the fathers told us that there was in Theoupolis an archbishop named Theodotus, who was so good that once, when a feast day came round, he invited some of the clergy under him to a banquet; and when one of them would not be persuaded and did not answer the invitation, the patriarch kept quiet at the time, but another time he went to him himself, begging him to come and share a common table.

⟦s2⟧ He also told this about the same archbishop Theodotus: that he was so meek and humble-minded that, [b: C] once when he was on the road with one cleric, he himself riding in a litter and the cleric sitting on a mount, the patriarch said to that cleric: "Let us divide the length of the road, and let the things we are sitting on be exchanged between us." ⟦s3⟧ But the cleric could not bear to do it. ⟦s4⟧ For he said it would be an insult to the patriarch for himself to get into the litter while the [2884] [b: A] patriarch sat on a mount. ⟦s5⟧ But the godly Theodotus did not give in; he persuaded his minister that what was being done was no insult to him, and made him do so.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 34

⟦s1⟧ This same Theoupolis had another patriarch, named Alexander, who was so merciful and compassionate that once one of his notaries stole his gold and fled in fear, and went off to the Thebaid of Egypt. ⟦s2⟧ As he wandered there, the blood-drinking barbarians of Egypt and of the Thebans fell in with him and carried him off to the farthest parts of their country. ⟦s2⟧ And when the godly Alexander learned of it, he bought him back, captive as he was, for [b: B] eighty-five gold pieces. ⟦s3⟧ And when he came back he treated him so kindly and so well that one of the inhabitants of the city once said: "Nothing is more profitable for me than to sin against Alexander."

⟦s4⟧ Another time one of his deacons insulted the godly Alexander in front of all the clergy; and the godly Alexander made a prostration to him, saying: "Forgive me, my lord brother."

⟦s4⟧ CHAPTER 35

⟦s5⟧ Abba Polychronius used to say of abba Elias, the archbishop of Jerusalem, that he did not drink wine when he was a monk, nor when he had become patriarch, but kept that same rule.

⟦s6⟧ They used to say of the archbishop of Jerusalem [b: C] and of Flavian, the archbishop of Antioch, that the emperor Anastasius exiled the two of them on account of the holy council of the Fathers at Chalcedon: Elias to Aila, and Flavian to Petra. ⟦s7⟧ So one day the patriarchs sent word to each other: "Anastasius died today; come, let us go too, that we may be judged with him." ⟦s8⟧ And after two days they departed to the Lord.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 36

⟦s1⟧ One of the fathers told us about the blessed Ephraim, patriarch of Antioch, that he was very zealous and fervent for the Orthodox faith. ⟦s2⟧ So one day he heard about a stylite in the parts of Hierapolis, that he was one of Severus's people, those out of communion, and of the Acephalites; and he went off to him [2885] wishing to persuade him. ⟦s3⟧ When he had come to him, [b: A] the godly Ephraim began to admonish and beg the stylite to have recourse to the apostolic see and to communicate with the holy catholic and apostolic Church. ⟦s4⟧ The stylite answered him and said: "I will not communicate with the council just like that." ⟦s5⟧ The godly Ephraim says to him: "And how wouldst thou have me satisfy thee, seeing that by the grace of Christ Jesus and of our Lord God the holy Church has been set free from every stain of heretical teaching?" ⟦s6⟧ The stylite says to him: "Let us light a fire, my lord patriarch, and let both I and you go into it; and whoever comes out unharmed, he is Orthodox, and him we must follow." ⟦s7⟧ He said this to frighten the patriarch. ⟦s8⟧ The godly Ephraim answers the stylite: "Thou oughtest, child, [b: B] to have listened to me as to thy father, and to ask nothing more of us. ⟦s9⟧ But since thou hast asked a thing beyond my wretchedness, I have put my trust in the mercies of the Son of God, and for the salvation of thy soul I will do even this." ⟦s10⟧ Then the godly Ephraim says to those standing by: "Blessed be the Lord. ⟦s10⟧ Bring wood here." ⟦s11⟧ When the wood had come, the patriarch lit it in front of the pillar and says to the stylite: "Come down, and according to thy own judgement let us both go in." ⟦s12⟧ The stylite was astounded at the patriarch's trust in God and would not come down, and the patriarch says to him: "Was it not thou who proposed that this be done? ⟦s13⟧ And how is it that now thou wilt not do it?" ⟦s13⟧ Then the archbishop took off the omophorion he was wearing, went near the fire, and prayed, saying: "Lord Jesus Christ our God, who for our sake didst deign to be truly incarnate of our Lady the holy Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary, show us the truth." ⟦s14⟧ And when he had finished the prayer he hurled his omophorion into the middle of the fire. ⟦s15⟧ The fire lasted about three hours, and when the wood had been consumed they took the omophorion out of the fire safe, unharmed and whole, with no trace of the fire found on it. ⟦s16⟧ Then the stylite, seeing what had happened, was convinced; he anathematized Severus and his heresy, came over to the holy Church, communicated from the hands of the blessed Ephraim, and glorified God.
