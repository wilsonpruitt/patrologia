⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 43

⟦s1⟧ In Thessalonica there was an archbishop by the name of [2897] [b: A] Thalelaeus. ⟦s2⟧ This man neither feared God nor dreaded the recompense that is prepared; he trampled on the doctrine of the Christians and set the dignity of the priesthood at nothing, the wretch, and was found to be, instead of a shepherd, a savage wolf. ⟦s3⟧ For he refused to worship the holy and consubstantial Trinity (be merciful to me, O Lord!) and worshipped idols. ⟦s4⟧ But those who governed the holy churches at that time deposed him by a canonical vote. ⟦s5⟧ After a little time had passed, this man, filled with every lawlessness, wanted to return to the priesthood again. ⟦s6+7+8⟧ And since, according to the most wise Solomon, all things obey gold, he was recalled, as one who was to go back to his own see (for he was in Constantinople, where dwell the rulers spoken of by the prophet Isaiah, "who justify the ungodly for gifts, and take away the righteousness of the righteous"). ⟦s6+7+8⟧ But God did not overlook his Church; but the vote brought in for him [?] according to the apostolic canons he condemned once more, as not pleasing to him. ⟦s6+7+8⟧ For one day he dressed himself finely, meaning to go to the rulers, that by their vote he might receive back the priestly dignity; then, as he was about to go out of his house, his belly griped him to go to the privy; and when he had stayed there about two hours and not come out, some of those standing outside went in, meaning to tell him to come out; and they found him with his head down in the drain of the privy and his feet up, having met an eternal death equal to that of the impious Arius, the enemy of God. ⟦s6+7+8⟧ For that man too, when he hoped by the help of the rulers to break in upon the Church like a tyrant, the wonderful Angel of great counsel of the holy Church of God scattered in the privy those bowels that had laboured to give birth to blasphemy; and this man too, when he hoped by the unjust concourse of the rulers to do worse than those before him, the angel who governs the Church of the Thessalonians, forestalling him together with the great martyr Demetrius, in the very place where he sat and, in league with the unclean demon who stirred him up, contrived his plots against the holy Church of God, there nailed the unholy [?] of that useless servant, and left hanging in the air his feet that would not walk in good order, bearing the tokens of the judgement that was to receive him, and of how fearful it is to fall into the hands of the living God.

⟦s6+7+8⟧ CHAPTER 44

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ One of the elders told us, when we had gone up to the Thebaid, that an elder lived outside the city of Antinoë, a great one, who had spent some seventy years in his cell. ⟦s2⟧ He had ten disciples, and one of them was very neglectful of himself. ⟦s3⟧ So the elder often admonished and begged him, saying: "Brother, take thought for thine own soul; thou must die and go away to punishment." ⟦s4⟧ But the brother always disobeyed the elder, not accepting what he said. ⟦s5+6⟧ So it came about after some time that the brother died. [2900] The elder was much grieved over him, for he knew that he had gone out of this world in great despondency and neglect. ⟦s7⟧ And the elder began to pray and to say: "Lord Jesus Christ, our true God, reveal to me what has become of the soul of this brother." ⟦s8⟧ And then, falling into an ecstasy, he sees a river of fire, and a multitude in that very fire, and in the middle of it the brother, plunged in up to the neck. ⟦s9⟧ Then the elder says to him: "Was it not because of this punishment that I begged thee to take thought for thine own soul, child?" ⟦s10+11⟧ The brother answered and said to the elder: "I thank God, Father, that at least my head has relief; for through thy prayers I am standing on the crown of a bishop's head."

⟦s10+11⟧ CHAPTER 45

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [b: B] One of the elders told us that abba Theodore the Eliote used to say that there was a recluse on the Mount of Olives, a great fighter. ⟦s2⟧ The demon of fornication made war on him. ⟦s3⟧ One day, then, as it pressed him hard, the elder began to lose heart and to say to the demon: "How long wilt thou not give me respite? ⟦s4⟧ Leave me now; thou hast grown old with me." ⟦s5⟧ Then the demon appears to him, plainly to his eyes, saying: "Swear to me that thou wilt tell no one what I am about to say to thee, and I will war on thee no more." ⟦s5⟧ And the elder swore: "By him who dwells in the heavens, I will tell no one what thou sayest to me." ⟦s6⟧ The demon says to him: "Do not venerate this icon, and I will war on thee no more." ⟦s7⟧ Now the icon bore the image of our Lady the holy Theotokos Mary, carrying our Lord Jesus Christ. ⟦s7⟧ The recluse says to the demon: "Let me think it over." ⟦s8⟧ So the next day he sends word to abba Theodore the Eliote, the one who told us the story, who was living at that time in the laura of Pharan; and when he came, he tells him everything. ⟦s9+10+11⟧ And the elder says to him: "Truly, abba, thou hast been made sport of, in that thou didst swear; still, thou hast done well to speak out. ⟦s9+10+11⟧ It is better for thee to leave no brothel in this country that thou dost not go into, than that thou shouldst refuse to venerate our Lord Jesus Christ with his Mother." ⟦s9+10+11⟧ So having strengthened and fortified him with many more words, he went off to his own place. ⟦s9+10+11⟧ And the demon appears to the recluse again, and says to him: "What is this, thou wicked old man? ⟦s9+10+11⟧ Didst thou not swear to me that thou wouldst tell no one? ⟦s12⟧ And how hast thou told it all to the one who came to thee? ⟦s13⟧ I tell thee, wicked old man, thou wilt be judged as a perjurer on the day of judgement." ⟦s13⟧ And the recluse answered: "That I swore, and that I have forsworn myself, I know; but it is by my Master and Maker that I have forsworn myself, and thee I will not obey. ⟦s14⟧ For thou, as the cause, wilt have the inescapable penalty required of thee, both for the evil counsel and for the perjury."

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 46

⟦s1⟧ We once visited abba Cyriacus, priest of the laura of the Calamon on the holy Jordan. ⟦s2⟧ And he told us: "One night I saw in my sleep a woman of grave and modest bearing, clad in purple, and with her two men of priestly look and venerable, standing outside my [2901] [b: A] cell. ⟦s3⟧ I took the woman to be our Lady the Theotokos, and the two men with her to be saint John the Theologian and saint John the Baptist. ⟦s4⟧ So I went out of my cell and begged them to come in and say a prayer in my cell; but she would not consent. ⟦s5⟧ So I kept on a long while entreating her and saying, 'Let me not be turned away humbled and put to shame,' and much else. ⟦s6+7⟧ When she saw me pressing my request, she answered me sternly, saying: 'Thou hast my enemy in thy cell, and how wouldst thou have me come in?' And saying this, she went away. ⟦s8⟧ When I woke I began to be distressed, and I wondered whether perhaps I had sinned against her in thought; for there was no one else in the cell but me alone. ⟦s9⟧ So I searched myself a long while, and did not find that I had [b: B] offended against her. ⟦s10⟧ When I saw myself drowning in grief, I got up and took a book to read, so that through the reading I might cast off the grief. ⟦s11⟧ The book I had borrowed was one of the blessed Hesychius, the priest of Jerusalem; and opening the book I find two discourses of the impious Nestorius written at the end of it; and at once I knew that this was the enemy of our Lady the holy Theotokos. ⟦s12⟧ Then I got up and went off and gave the book back to the one who had lent it to me. ⟦s13+14⟧ And I said to him: 'Take thy book, brother; for I have not had as much profit from it as it has brought me harm.' And when he asked what the harm was, I told him what had happened; and, filled with zeal, he at once cut the two discourses of Nestorius out of the book and gave them to the fire, saying: 'The enemy of our Lady the holy Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary shall not stay in my cell.'"

⟦s13+14⟧ CHAPTER 47

⟦s15⟧ Heliopolis is a city of Phoenice Libanensis. ⟦s15⟧ In it there was a mime named Gaianus, who used to blaspheme the holy Theotokos in the theatre and make a show of it. ⟦s16⟧ And the Theotokos appears to him, saying: "What harm have I done thee, that before so great a crowd thou disparagest me and blasphemest me?" ⟦s17⟧ But he got up and not only did not mend his ways, but blasphemed her still more. ⟦s18⟧ A third time she appears to him, saying the same and admonishing him. ⟦s19⟧ So when he did not mend his ways but blasphemed even more, one noon as he was sleeping she appears to him saying nothing; only with one of her fingers she scored across his two hands and his two feet. ⟦s20⟧ And when he woke he was found with his two hands and his two feet cut off, lying a mere trunk. ⟦s21⟧ So the wretch confessed this to everyone, making it plain what recompense he had received for his blasphemy; and that out of loving-kindness.

⟦s21⟧ [2904] CHAPTER 48

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Anastasius, priest and keeper of the treasures of the holy Anastasis of Christ our God, told us that Cosmiana, the wife of Germanus the patrician, came and wanted, one night of holy Sunday, to venerate alone the holy and life-giving tomb of our Lord Jesus Christ, the true God; and as she drew near the sanctuary, our Lady the holy Theotokos met her, plainly to her eyes, together with other women, saying: "Since thou art not ours, thou dost not come in here, for thou art not one of ours" (for she was of the heresy of Severus the Acephalite). ⟦s1⟧ And she begged hard to be let in. ⟦s2⟧ The holy Theotokos answered her and said: "Believe me, woman, thou shalt not come in here until thou communicatest with us." ⟦s3⟧ And knowing that she was prevented from going in because she was a heretic, and that unless she came over to the holy catholic and apostolic Church of Christ our God she would not go in, she sent at once for the deacon; and when the holy chalice had come, she partook of the holy body and blood of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ; and so, without hindrance, she was found worthy to venerate the holy and life-giving tomb of our Lord Jesus Christ.

⟦s4⟧ CHAPTER 49

⟦s4⟧ The priest Anastasius also told us this: that when Gebemer became duke of Palestine, he came at the start to venerate the holy Anastasis of Christ our God. ⟦s5⟧ And as he was coming to go in, he saw a ram rushing at him and wanting to butt him. ⟦s6⟧ He, [?] seized with great fear, stepped back; and the cross-warden Azarias, standing by, and the whip-bearers standing by say to him: "What is it, then, master, that thou hast? ⟦s6⟧ Why dost thou not go in?" ⟦s7⟧ He says: "Why did you bring this ram in?" ⟦s8⟧ They were astonished and peeped inside into the holy tomb, and seeing nothing, they say to him, bidding him go in: "There is nothing of the kind inside." ⟦s9⟧ Again he started to go in; and again he saw the same ram rushing at him and not allowing him to enter. ⟦s10⟧ When this had happened many times, he seeing it and those standing by seeing nothing at all, the cross-warden says to him: "Believe me, master, thou hast something on thy soul, and that is why thou art prevented from venerating the holy and life-giving tomb of our Saviour; but thou wilt do well to confess to God. ⟦s11⟧ For he loves mankind, and wishing to have mercy on thee he has shown thee such a wonder." ⟦s12⟧ He, with tears, says to him: "I am guilty before the Lord of many great sins." ⟦s13⟧ And throwing himself on his face, he stayed a long while weeping and confessing to God. ⟦s14⟧ And he got up again to go in, and could not, for the ram that appeared hindered him no less. ⟦s15⟧ Then the cross-[2905] [b: A] warden says to him: "Truly it is something else that is hindering thee." ⟦s16⟧ He says: "Can it be that I am kept from going in for not communicating with the holy catholic and apostolic Church, but with that of Severus?" ⟦s17⟧ And he asked the cross-warden that he might partake of the holy and life-giving mysteries of Christ our God. ⟦s18⟧ And when the holy chalice had come, he communicated; and so he went in and venerated, seeing nothing any more.

⟦s18⟧ CHAPTER 50

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Scythopolis was the metropolis of Second Palestine. ⟦s2+3⟧ There we met abba Anastasius, and he told us about abba George the recluse, saying: "One night I got up to strike the wood (for I was canonarch), and I heard the elder weeping; and I went and [b: B] begged him, saying: 'My lord abba, what ails thee, that thou weepest so?' But he answered nothing. ⟦s4+5⟧ Again I begged him: 'Tell me the reason.' And groaning deeply from the bottom of his heart, he says to me: 'How should I not weep, when our Master does not wish to be reconciled concerning us? ⟦s6⟧ For I thought, child, that I was standing before someone seated on a high throne. ⟦s7⟧ Many myriads were round him, begging and entreating about some matter; and he would not be won over. ⟦s8+9⟧ Then a woman clothed in purple comes up to him, falling at his feet and saying: "Be moved, at least for my sake." ⟦s10⟧ But he remained no less unmoved. ⟦s11+12⟧ And that is why I weep and lament, fearing what is to come.' He told me this as Thursday was dawning; and the next day, which was Friday, at the ninth hour, the earth quakes violently, and the cities of coastal Phoenice are thrown down."

⟦s13+14⟧ This abba Anastasius also told us this about the same elder: "Some time later, as I was standing at the window, the elder began to weep and to say to me: 'Woe to us, brother, for we have no compunction, but are in negligence; and I fear that we are at the doors, and the wrath of God has overtaken us.' And the next day the fire appeared in the sky."
