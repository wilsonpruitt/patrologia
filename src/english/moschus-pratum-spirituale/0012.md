⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ CHAPTER 90

⟦s1+2+3⟧ There were two anchorites above Rhossus [?], on the mountain called Pterygion, near the river Piapi and the monastery of abba Theodosius at the Scopelos. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ And when the elder [?] had been perfected, his disciple said a prayer and buried him on the mountain. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Then after a few days the anchorite's disciple came down from the mountain and came near the inhabited country, and finds a man working his land; and he says to him, "Do me a kindness, good old man: take a pick and a shovel and come with me." ⟦s4⟧ And the farmer at once obeyed the anchorite. ⟦s4⟧ And when they had gone to the mountain, the anchorite showed the layman the grave of his elder, the anchorite's tomb, and says to him, "Dig here." ⟦s5⟧ And when he had dug the grave, the anchorite stood in prayer; and having finished the prayer, he embraced the layman, saying, "Pray for me, lord brother." ⟦s6⟧ And he went down and laid himself on top of his elder, and gave up his soul. ⟦s7⟧ And the layman covered the grave over again, and gave thanks to God. ⟦s8⟧ And when he had come down from the mountain about a stone's throw, he said to himself, "Truly, I ought to have taken a blessing from the saints." ⟦s9⟧ And so he turned back, and did not find the grave of the saints.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 91

⟦s1⟧ Some of the fathers used to say of abba George the anchorite that for thirty-five years he went about naked in the deserts.

⟦s2⟧ They said again of him that when he was in the mountains of the monastery of abba Theodosius at the Scopelos, he had a disciple. ⟦s2⟧ And when the disciple died, the elder had no tools to dig and bury the brother's body, so he went down from the mountain to the sea, and he found a ship lying at anchor. ⟦s3⟧ And he begs both the shipmaster and the sailors to come up the mountain with him and bury the brother. ⟦s4⟧ And they readily obeyed the elder; and taking what was needed, they went up with him, and digging, they buried the brother's body. ⟦s5⟧ So one of the sailors, named Thalaeleus, pierced with compunction at the elder's virtue, begged him that he might stay with him. ⟦s6⟧ And the elder says that he could not bear the toil of the ascetic life. ⟦s7⟧ But the young man said, "Yes, I can bear it, certainly." ⟦s8⟧ And so he stayed with the elder, and spent one year with him, toiling very hard indeed in the ascetic life. ⟦s9⟧ So after the year, brother Thalaeleus makes a prostration to the elder, saying, "Pray for me, father, for God through your prayers has taken the toil from me, and I toil no longer, nor do the weathers wear me down;

⟦s9⟧ [2949] [b: A] for I am not scorched in the heat, nor again do I shiver in the winter; but I am in great rest." ⟦s10⟧ And the elder blessed him. ⟦s11⟧ And when brother Thalaeleus had spent another two and a half years, he foreknew his end. ⟦s12⟧ And he begged the elder, saying, "Take me to Jerusalem, so that I may venerate the holy Cross and the holy Resurrection of Christ our God. ⟦s13⟧ For in these days the Lord takes me to himself." ⟦s14⟧ And the elder took him and went to the holy city. ⟦s15⟧ And when they had venerated the holy and revered places, and had gone down to the holy Jordan as well and been baptised, after three days brother Thalaeleus fell asleep. ⟦s16⟧ And the elder buried him in the laura of Copruthas. ⟦s17⟧ And some time after, abba George the anchorite was laid to rest; and the fathers of that same laura of Copruthas buried him in their own church.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 92

⟦s1⟧ Our holy father abba George, [b: B] archimandrite of the monastery of our holy father Theodosius, which lies in the desert of the holy city of Christ our God, told me and brother Sophronius the sophist: "I had a brother here named George the Cappadocian; and he had a service at Phasaelis. ⟦s2⟧ So one day, when the brethren were making loaves, brother George was firing the oven. ⟦s3⟧ Then, after he had fired the oven, he did not find anything to swab out the oven with, for the brethren had hidden it, to test him. ⟦s4⟧ So he went in and swabbed out the oven with his garment; and he came out of the fire without any harm at all. ⟦s5⟧ And when I heard it, I rebuked the brethren for having tested him."

⟦s5⟧ The same abba George, [b: C] our father, told us this too about the same brother George: "One day he was keeping pigs at Phasaelis, and two lions came to carry off a pig. ⟦s6⟧ And taking his staff, he chased them as far as the holy Jordan."

⟦s7⟧ Again the same father of ours told us: "I was about to build the church of Saint Cyricus at Phasaelis, and they dug the foundations of the church. ⟦s8⟧ There appears to me in sleep a monk, a great ascetic, wearing a colobion of cord, and on his shoulders a little wrap of rush matting; and he says to me in a gentle voice, 'Tell me, lord abba George, did it simply seem good to thee, after so many toils and so much ascetic life, to leave me outside the church thou art building?' And I,

⟦s9+10⟧ [2952] in awe of the elder's sacred bearing, say to him, 'Truly, sir, who art thou thyself?' And he said, 'I am Peter, the grazer of the holy Jordan.' And getting up in the morning, I extended the plan of the church. ⟦s11+12+13⟧ And as I dig, I found his body lying just as I had seen him in my sleep. ⟦s11+12+13⟧ And having built the oratory, I made a very fine tomb in the right-hand aisle, and there I laid him."

⟦s11+12+13⟧ CHAPTER 93

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The same father of ours, George, told us: "One day I went to abba Sisinnius the anchorite. ⟦s2⟧ This elder had left his own bishopric for God's sake, and had come near a village called Betanabaris (it is about six miles from the holy Jordan), keeping stillness. ⟦s3+4⟧ So when we went to him, and had knocked a great deal, after a long while his disciple opened to us, and says to me thus: 'Lord abba, the elder has been sick unto death, and he prayed God that he should not depart this life until he heard that thou hadst come into this country.' For I had been in Constantinople on the coenobium's business, with the most pious emperor Tiberius. ⟦s5+6⟧ So when the disciple had gone up to the elder and told him about me, he stayed a long while, then came down, saying to us, 'Be pleased to come up.' And going up, we found the elder perfected. ⟦s7⟧ So I knew that as soon as he heard that it was I who was knocking, then he went to the Lord. ⟦s8⟧ And as I embrace him, the dead man says to me gently, 'Welcome to my abba'; and he fell asleep again. ⟦s9⟧ Then I sent word to the estate, for them to come and bury the elder. ⟦s10+11⟧ So when they came and were digging the tomb, the elder's disciple says to those digging the grave, 'Do a kindness, and dig it a little wider, so that it holds the two of us.' And while they were digging, he laid himself on the mat and was laid to rest. ⟦s12⟧ And we buried the two together, the elder and his disciple."

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 94

⟦s1⟧ The same father of ours, George the archimandrite, told us about abba Julian, who became bishop of Bostra, that after he had left the coenobium and become bishop in Bostra, some of the landowners of the city, being haters of Christ, wanted to make away with him by poison. ⟦s2⟧ And so they bribe his cupbearer with money, and give him a deadly drug, so that as he mixed the metropolitan's cup he should put the poison in his cup. ⟦s2⟧ And the servant did

⟦s3⟧ [2953] [b: A] just as he had been taught. ⟦s3⟧ And when the servant had handed the cup, bewitched, to the divine Julian, he took it, and knew from God the plot and the men who had made it. ⟦s4+5+6⟧ So having taken the cup, he set it in front of him, saying nothing at all to the servant; and he sends and summons all the landowners, among whom were also those who had contrived the plot against him. ⟦s4+5+6⟧ And the truly divine Julian, not wishing to make an example of those who had done it, says gently to them all, "If you think to murder the lowly Julian by sorcery, look, I drink it in front of you." ⟦s4+5+6⟧ And he sealed the cup three times with his finger, and said, "In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit I drink this cup"; and he drank it in front of them all, and remained unharmed. ⟦s4+5+6⟧ And seeing it, they made a prostration to him.

⟦s4+5+6⟧ CHAPTER 95

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There was an elder living [b: B] in the monastery of our father Theodosius, by birth of Sebastopolis in Armenia; his name was Patricius, a very old man (for he said he was a hundred and thirteen years old), gentle and quiet. ⟦s2⟧ So the fathers of that place told us about this virtuous elder, that he had been superior of the coenobium of Abazanus, and that he left the superiorship, fearing the judgement (for he said it belonged to great men to shepherd rational sheep), and came here into subjection, judging this the greater profit to his soul.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ CHAPTER 96

⟦s1+2⟧ And they told us this too about him. ⟦s1+2⟧ There was another elder here, an Arab by race, by name Julian, blind. [b: C] This abba Julian once took offence at Macarius, archbishop of Jerusalem, and hesitated to communicate with him. ⟦s3⟧ So one day abba Julian sends word to abba Symeon of the Wondrous Mountain (the mountain is nine miles from Theoupolis), saying: "I am blind, I cannot go anywhere, nor have I anyone who can take care of me, and I hesitate to communicate with Macarius. ⟦s4⟧ But show me, father, what I ought to have done about the brother who committed fornication, and about the one who took an oath with him." ⟦s5⟧ And abba Symeon sends word to abba Julian, saying: "Do not withdraw, and do not wish

⟦s5⟧ [2956] [b: A] to separate thyself from the holy Church. ⟦s6⟧ For it has no evil in it, by the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God. ⟦s7⟧ But know this too, brother: if anyone offers the oblation in your coenobium, you have an elder there named Patricius; and this elder stands outside the sanctuary, below all the rest, near the west wall of the church; and he too says the holy prayer of the offering, and the holy anaphora is reckoned as his."
