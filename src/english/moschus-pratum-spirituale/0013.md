⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ CHAPTER 97

⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ Abba John the anchorite, surnamed Pyrrhus, used to say that he had heard abba Stephen the Moabite tell this: "When I was in the coenobium of Saint Theodosius the great coenobiarch, there were two brothers there who had sworn an oath that neither in their life nor in their death would they be parted from one another. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ So while they were in the coenobium, edifying everyone, the one brother was warred on by fornication; and not being able to bear the war, he says to his brother, 'Let me go, brother, for I am warred on by fornication, and I want to go off into the world.' But the brother began to plead with him and to say, 'Don't, brother, do not throw away thy toil.' But he says to him, 'Either come with me, so that I may do the deed, or let me go off.' And the brother, not wanting to let him go, went off with him to the city.

⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ "So the one who was warred on went into the house of fornication; and the other brother, standing outside, took dust from the ground and kept throwing it on his own head, and beat himself. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ So when the one who had gone into the brothel came out, having done the deed, the other brother says to him, 'What hast thou gained from the sin, my brother? ⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ And what harm hast thou not suffered? ⟦s1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9⟧ Let us go back to our place.' But he says to him, 'I can no longer go into the desert. ⟦s10⟧ So thou go. ⟦s11+12⟧ For I will stay in the world.' So when, for all he did, he could not persuade him to follow him into the desert, he too stayed with him in the world, and the two of them laboured at day-work, so as to feed themselves.

⟦s13⟧ "At that time abba Abramius — who earlier had founded the monastery in Constantinople called that of the Abramites, and who afterwards became archbishop of Ephesus, the good and gentle shepherd — was building his own monastery, the one called that of the Byzantines. ⟦s14⟧ So the two brothers went and laboured at work there, and received their wage. ⟦s15⟧ And the one who had fallen into fornication would take the wage of both, and every day he would go off to the city and spend it on debauchery. ⟦s16⟧ But the other fasted all day, and did his work in all stillness, not speaking to anyone. ⟦s17⟧ The craftsmen, seeing him every day neither eating nor talking, but always lost in thought, [2957] [b: A] told the things about him and about his conduct to abba Abramius, who is among the saints. ⟦s18+19+20+21+22+23⟧ Then the great Abramius sends for the workman to his cell, and asks him, saying, 'Where art thou from, brother? ⟦s18+19+20+21+22+23⟧ And what is thy work?' And he confessed everything to him, and that 'I endure it all for my brother's sake, so that God, seeing my affliction, may save my brother.' Hearing this, the divine Abramius says to the brother, 'The Lord has granted thee thy brother's soul as well.' So when abba Abramius had dismissed the brother and he had come out of his cell, behold, his brother too, crying out, 'My brother, take me into the desert, that I may be saved.' So straightaway he took him and went off to the cave near the holy Jordan, and shut him in; and after a little time, having advanced greatly in spirit according to God, [b: B] he departed this life. ⟦s18+19+20+21+22+23⟧ And the brother stayed in that same cave according to the oath, so that he too might end his life there."

⟦s18+19+20+21+22+23⟧ CHAPTER 98

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ While this brother was living at the holy Jordan after his brother's falling asleep, an elder from the laura of Calamon visited him, and says to him, "Tell me, brother: in so long a time of stillness and ascesis, what hast thou achieved?" ⟦s2⟧ The brother says to him, "Go, and come after the tenth day, and I will tell thee." ⟦s3⟧ And the elder came on the tenth day and found the brother had died, and a potsherd written on thus: "Forgive me, Father, for never, while doing my rule, did I let my mind down to the earth."

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ CHAPTER 99

⟦s1+2⟧ The Fathers of that same monastery told us: "Before these times there was here an elder named Ianthus, who all his life used to go off to the Koutila. ⟦s3⟧ So one day, when he was in the desert, behold, Saracens came into those parts, and seeing the elder, one of them bared his sword and came at the elder, meaning to kill him. ⟦s4+5⟧ But the elder, seeing the Saracen coming at him, gazed up into heaven and said, 'Lord Jesus Christ, thy will be done.' And at once the earth opened and swallowed the Saracen. ⟦s6⟧ And the elder was saved, and came into the monastery glorifying God."

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Again the Fathers of that same place told us: "There was here a priest named Peter, a Pontian by birth, who had done many great feats of virtue. ⟦s2⟧ About this elder Theodore, who became bishop of Rhossus, used to tell us: 'One day he came close to me at the Jordan, at the laura of the Towers, for I was living there. ⟦s3⟧ And he says to me, "Do me a kindness, brother Theodore: come with me to Mount Sinai, [2960] [b: A] for I have a vow." ⟦s3⟧ And I, not wanting to disobey the elder, say to him, "Let us go." ⟦s4⟧ So when we had crossed the holy Jordan, the elder says to me, "Brother Theodore, come, let us make a prostration, that neither of us eats until Sinai." ⟦s5⟧ I say to him, "Truly, Father, that I cannot do." ⟦s6⟧ And the elder made a prostration, and took nothing until Sinai. ⟦s7⟧ But when he had partaken of the holy mysteries at Sinai, then he took food too. ⟦s8⟧ Likewise from Sinai we went off to Saint Menas of Alexandria, he taking no food. ⟦s9⟧ And there, having communicated, he took food. ⟦s10⟧ And from Saint Menas we came to the holy city, he tasting nothing at all on the way. ⟦s11⟧ And having communicated at the holy Anastasis of Christ our God, he then took food. ⟦s12⟧ So on all that long and great journey the elder ate only three times: once at Mount Sinai, once at Saint Menas, and once at the holy city.'"

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Fathers of that same monastery also told us about another elder, saying: "There was here one by name abba Paul, who went to his rest a little while ago, a Roman by birth. ⟦s2⟧ So one day he went off with the mules; and at the hostel there happened to be a little child, and by the devil's doing, without abba Paul knowing it, the mule trod on the child and killed it. ⟦s3+4⟧ So abba Paul, grieved at this, withdrew and went off to Aronan; and there he became an anchorite, ever lamenting the child's death and saying, 'I committed the murder of the child, and as a murderer I am to be judged at the judgement.' Now there was a lion nearby, and every day abba Paul would go to the lion's lair, goading it and enraging it, that it might get up and eat him; but the lion did him no harm at all. ⟦s5+6⟧ Then, when he saw that it was no use, he says to himself, 'I will lie down on the lion's path, and as it comes along and goes to the river to drink water, it will eat me.' So when he lay down, behold, after a little the lion came too, and as if with a man's understanding, in all stillness it leapt over the elder, and did not touch him at all. ⟦s7⟧ Then the elder was assured that God had forgiven him the sin. ⟦s8⟧ And coming back to his monastery, he lived on, profiting and edifying everyone until his going to rest with God."

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 100

⟦s1⟧ When abba Sophronius the sophist, my brother, was about to die, there stood by him I, and abba the Scholasticus, and abba Cyricus, and some others of the Fathers. ⟦s2⟧ And he says to us, "I was going along a road, and before me young girls, making a ring, were dancing and saying, 'Welcome, Sophronius! ⟦s3⟧ Sophronius is crowned!'"

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [2961] CHAPTER 101

⟦s1⟧ The Fathers of that same monastery used to say about abba Strategius, superior of that same monastery of our father among the saints Theodosius, that he had acquired these three advantages above every monk of this generation: fasting much, keeping vigil much, working much.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 102

⟦s1⟧ In the coenobium of our father among the saints Theodosius, abba Theodosius, who became bishop of Capitulias, told us about abba Nonnus, saying: "One day, before the night semantron was struck, as I lay on my little bed, I heard someone saying 'Lord, have mercy' in a gentle and quiet voice. ⟦s2⟧ And when I had counted five hundred of the 'Lord, have mercy', I wanted to know who it was that was saying it; and peering through the window of my cell into the church, I see the elder down on his knees, and a shining star above his head, lighting up for me who the elder was."

⟦s3⟧ CHAPTER 103

⟦s3⟧ Another of the elders of that same coenobium told us about the same abba Nonnus, saying: "One day, before the semantron was struck, I came out of my cell and was going to the church, and I see the elder standing in front of the church, with both hands stretched out to heaven, and praying. ⟦s4⟧ And his hands were shining like torches of fire. ⟦s5⟧ And I was afraid and withdrew."

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 104

⟦s1⟧ Being in Alexandria, we visited abba Theodulus, who was at Holy Sophia on the Pharos. ⟦s2⟧ And the elder told us, saying: "I renounced the world in the coenobium of our father among the saints Theodosius, which is in the desert of the holy city of Christ our God; and there I found a great elder named Christopher, a Roman by birth. ⟦s3+4⟧ So one day I make him a prostration, saying, 'Do me a kindness, abba: tell me the work thou hast done from thy youth.' And the elder, after I had pleaded with him much, and knowing that I was asking him for the profit of my soul, told me, saying: 'When I renounced the world, child, I had great fervour for the monastic life; and by day I gave myself to the rule, and by night I went off to the cave where Theodosius among the saints [2964] [b: A] and the other holy Fathers lie, to pray. ⟦s3+4⟧ And going down into the cave, at each step I made to God a hundred prostrations. ⟦s5⟧ And the steps are eighteen in number. ⟦s6⟧ And when I had gone down all the steps, I stayed on until the semantron was struck; then I went up to the rule. ⟦s7⟧ So after I had done this work for ten years, with fasting and much self-control and toil, I come one day, as was my custom, to go down into the cave; and after I had completed the prostrations at each step, as I was about to come down onto the floor of the cave, I fall into ecstasy, and I see the whole floor of the cave filled with lamps; and some were lit, and some not. ⟦s8⟧ And I see two men too, wrapped in cloaks and clothed in white, tending those same lamps. ⟦s9⟧ And I said to them, "Why have you set these lamps here, and will not let us come down and pray?" ⟦s10⟧ And they answered me, saying, "The lamps are the Fathers'." ⟦s11⟧ And again I said to them, "And why are some lit, and some not lit?" ⟦s11⟧ And again they answered me, "Those who wished lit their own." ⟦s12+13⟧ Then I say to them, "Do me a kindness: is my lamp lit or not?" ⟦s14⟧ And they said, "Pray, and we light it." ⟦s15⟧ And I say, "Still 'pray'! ⟦s15⟧ And till now, what was I doing?" ⟦s16⟧ And at this word I came to myself, and turning round I saw no one. ⟦s17⟧ Then I say to myself, "Christopher, if thou wilt be saved, there is need of more toil." ⟦s18⟧ And early in the morning I withdrew from the monastery, and went off to Mount Sinai, taking nothing with me but what I wore, and no more. ⟦s19+20⟧ And after I had spent fifty years there in ascesis, a voice came to me, saying, "Christopher, Christopher, go off to thy coenobium, where thou hast fought well, that there thou mayest end thy life with thy Fathers."' And a little time after he told me this, his holy soul went to rest in the Lord in exultation."

⟦s21+22⟧ CHAPTER 105

⟦s21+22⟧ The same abba Theodulus again told us about the same abba Christopher. "The elder," he says, "used to say: 'One day I went up from the monastery to the holy city to venerate the holy Cross. ⟦s23⟧ And after I had venerated it,' he says, 'as I was going out, I see a brother in the gateway of the forecourt of the holy Cross, neither going in nor going out. ⟦s24⟧ And I see two ravens too, flying shamelessly in his face, and beating at his eyes with their wings, and not letting him go into the shrine. ⟦s25⟧ And I, knowing them to be demons, say to him, "Tell me, brother, why art thou standing in the middle of the gateway, and not going in?" ⟦s26+27⟧ And he,' he says, 'says to me, "Forgive me, my lord abba: I have thoughts. ⟦s26+27⟧ The one says to me, 'Go in, venerate the precious Cross'; and another says to me, 'No: go, see to thy business, and thou wilt venerate another time.'" And I, when I heard it, took him by the hand and led him into the shrine; and at once the ravens fled from him. ⟦s28+29⟧ And when I had had him venerate the holy [2965] Cross and the holy Anastasis of Christ our God, I sent him away in peace.' And the elder told me this," he says, "because he saw me much distracted with services, and neglecting prayer."
