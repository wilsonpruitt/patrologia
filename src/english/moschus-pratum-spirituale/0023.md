⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 174

⟦s1⟧ Abba Gregory the anchorite told us: "As I was coming down from Byzantium on a ship, there came aboard too a scribo with his wife, who was to pray in the holy city. ⟦s2⟧ The shipmaster was very devout, and a faster. ⟦s3⟧ As we sailed, the scribo's servants were spending the water without stint. ⟦s4⟧ When we came out into the open sea, our water ran out, and we were in great distress; and it was a pitiful sight to see, women and boys and little children fainting with thirst, and lying as if dead. ⟦s5⟧ When we had been some three days in such distress, and had no more hope of life, the scribo, not bearing the affliction, drew his sword, meaning to kill both the shipmaster and the sailors. ⟦s6+7⟧ For he said, 'These are to blame for our perishing, since they did not fill water enough for our need.' But I [3044] [b: A] besought the scribo and said, 'Do not do this, but rather let us pray to our Lord Jesus Christ, our true God, who does great things and unsearchable, of which there is no number. ⟦s8+9⟧ For see, the shipmaster too, as thou seest, has been giving himself to fasting and prayer three days now.' And when the scribo grew quiet, on the fourth day, about the sixth hour, the shipmaster rose and cried out with a loud voice, saying, 'Glory to thee, O Christ our God!', so that we were astounded at his voice. ⟦s10⟧ And he said to the sailors, 'Let down the hides.' And as they spread them out, behold, a cloud came over the ship and rained so much water that it filled all our need. ⟦s11⟧ And the wonder was great and fearful, for as the ship ran before the wind the cloud kept following us, and outside the ship it did not rain."

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 175

⟦s1⟧ One of the fathers told us about the emperor Zeno, that he wronged a woman in the matter of her daughter. ⟦s2⟧ And she gave herself up to prayer in the church of our all-holy Lady, the Theotokos Mary, beseeching her, and saying with tears: "Avenge me on the emperor Zeno." ⟦s3⟧ When she had done this for many days, the all-holy Theotokos appeared to her, saying: "Believe me, woman, I have often wished to avenge thee, but his right hand stops me." ⟦s3⟧ For he was very merciful.

⟦s4⟧ CHAPTER 176

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Abba Palladius told us: "I heard one of the fathers named Andrew, whom we too met, telling this: 'When I was younger, I was very disorderly. ⟦s2⟧ And when war and disorder came, I and nine others fled to Palestine; and of these one was a philoponos, and another a Hebrew. ⟦s3⟧ When we came into the desert, the Hebrew fell sick to the point of death, and we were in great despondency, not knowing what to do for him. ⟦s4⟧ Yet we did not leave him, but each of us carried him as far as his strength went. ⟦s5⟧ We wanted to bring him to a city or a trading-post, so that he would not die in the desert; but since the young man, from want of food and a most violent fever and measureless toil and the thirst from the heat, was utterly spent, and on the point of failing (for he was now at his departure, and could not even be carried), we resolved with many tears to leave him in the desert and go away, fearing that death by thirst would come on us too. ⟦s6⟧ So when we had laid him on the sand, weeping, and he saw that we were about to go away, he began to adjure us, saying, "By the God who is to judge the living and the dead, do not let me die a Jew, but as a Christian. ⟦s7⟧ Have mercy on me, and baptize me, so that I too may depart this life a Christian and go to the Lord." ⟦s8⟧ But we said to him, "Truly, brother, it is not lawful for us to do any such thing, for we are laymen; this is the work of bishops [3045] [b: A] and priests; besides, there is no water here." ⟦s9⟧ But he persisted, adjuring us with tears by the same oaths, and saying, "Do not deprive me of such a gift, Christians." ⟦s10⟧ So when we were at a complete loss, the philoponos, moved by God, said to us, "Raise him up and undress him." ⟦s11⟧ And with much toil we stood him upright and undressed him. ⟦s12⟧ Then the philoponos filled his hands with sand and poured it three times over his head, saying, "Theodore is baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"; and we answered the Amen at each name of the holy, consubstantial and worshipful Trinity. ⟦s12⟧ And as before the Lord, brethren, so did Christ the Son of the living God heal him and strengthen him, that no trace of sickness was to be seen in him any more; but sound [b: B] and strong, with all eagerness, he ran on ahead of us the rest of the way through the desert. ⟦s13⟧ When we came to Ascalon, we referred the matter to the blessed Dionysius, now among the saints, its bishop: how it had befallen the brother on the road. ⟦s14⟧ When the truly holy Dionysius heard these things, he was struck dumb at this strange wonder; and calling together all the clergy, he laid the matter before them, whether the pouring of the sand ought to be reckoned to him as baptism or not. ⟦s15⟧ And some said that it ought to be reckoned to him, because of the strangeness of the wonder; but others said, No. ⟦s16+17⟧ For Gregory the Theologian counts up all the baptisms, speaking of a Mosaic baptism, but in water, and before that a baptism in cloud and sea; and John's, no longer Jewish, *for it was not in water only, but also unto repentance; Jesus also baptizes, but in the Spirit: this is the perfection. ⟦s18+19⟧ I know also a fourth baptism, that through martyrdom and blood; I know also a fifth besides, that of tears.* "So with which of these was he baptized, that we should even ratify it? ⟦s20⟧ Above all, when the Lord says to Nicodemus, *Unless one be born of water and the Spirit, he shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven*." ⟦s20⟧ And others spoke against these: "What then? ⟦s21⟧ Since it is not written of the apostles that they were baptized, do they not enter into the kingdom of heaven?" ⟦s22⟧ And to these others again replied, saying: "Yes, truly they were baptized, as Clement the Stromatist records in the fifth book of the *Hypotyposes*. ⟦s22⟧ For expounding the apostolic saying that runs, *I give thanks that I baptized none of you*, he says that Christ is said to have baptized Peter alone, and Peter Andrew, Andrew James and John, and they the rest." ⟦s23⟧ When these and many other things had been said, it seemed good to the blessed bishop Dionysius to send the brother to the holy Jordan and have him baptized there. ⟦s24⟧ And that philoponos he ordained deacon.'"

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [3048] CHAPTER 177

⟦s1⟧ Abba John the Cilician used to say: "When we were living at the Ennaton of Alexandria, the Egyptian monk visited us and told us: 'A brother from abroad came to the laura of the Cells wishing to settle there; and making a prostration to the priest, he begged to settle in the cell of Evagrius. ⟦s2⟧ The priest told him he could not settle there. ⟦s3⟧ But the brother said, "If I do not stay there, I am certainly going away." ⟦s4⟧ The priest said to him, "Truly, child, a cruel demon lives there. ⟦s5⟧ For the one who led Evagrius astray, and drew him away from the right faith, and put the abominable doctrines into him, lets no one settle there." ⟦s6⟧ But the brother persisted, saying, "If I stay here, it is there that I must settle." ⟦s7+8+9⟧ Then the priest said, "On thine own judgement be it: go, sit there." ⟦s7+8+9⟧ So the brother went off and stayed one week; and when holy Sunday came, he came to church. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ And when the priest saw him, he was comforted. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ But the following Sunday he did not come to church; and the priest asked two brothers to go off and find out why he had not come to church. ⟦s7+8+9⟧ And the brothers went off to the cell and found the brother with a rope put round his neck, hanged.'"

⟦s7+8+9⟧ CHAPTER 178

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Abba George [?], the priest of the coenobium of the Scholarii, told us that at Monidia there lived a great elder, a man of hard labour [?]; but he was simple about the faith, and wherever he happened to be, he communicated without discernment. ⟦s1+2⟧ One day an angel of God appeared to him, saying: "Tell me, elder, if thou diest, how wouldst thou have us bury thee? ⟦s3⟧ As the Egyptian monks bury, or as the people of Jerusalem?" ⟦s4⟧ The elder answered him, saying: "I do not know." ⟦s5⟧ Then the angel said to him: "Think it over; I am coming after three weeks, and thou shalt tell me." ⟦s6⟧ So the elder went off to another elder and told him what he had heard from the angel. ⟦s7⟧ When that elder heard it, he was struck dumb at the hearing; and gazing at him a long while, moved by God, he said to him: "Where dost thou receive the holy mysteries?" ⟦s8⟧ He answered him: "Wherever I happen to find them." ⟦s9⟧ Then the elder said to him: "Never again think fit to communicate outside the holy catholic and apostolic Church, where the four holy synods are named: that at Nicaea of the three hundred and eighteen, and that at Constantinople of the hundred and fifty, and the first at Ephesus of the two hundred, and that at Chalcedon of the six hundred and thirty. ⟦s10+11⟧ And when the angel comes, say to him, 'As the people of Jerusalem, I want.'" [3049] [b: A] So after three weeks the angel came and said to the elder: "How is it, elder? ⟦s10+11⟧ Hast thou thought it over?" ⟦s12⟧ The elder said to him: "As the people of Jerusalem, I want." ⟦s12⟧ The angel said to him: "Good, good." ⟦s13⟧ And straightway he gave up his soul. ⟦s14⟧ All this happened so that the elder should not lose his toils and be condemned with heretics.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 179

⟦s1⟧ We visited abba John the anchorite who was called Pyrrhus, and he told us: "I heard abba John the Moabite saying: 'There was a nun in the holy city, very devout, and advancing in the ways of God. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ So the devil, envying the virgin, cast into a certain young man a satanic passion for her. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ But that wonderful virgin, seeing [?] the demon's plotting and the young man's ruin, took a few soaked pulses in a little bag and went into the desert, procuring for the young man by her withdrawal his freedom from trouble and his salvation, and for herself the safety of the desert. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ After a long time, by God's dispensation, so that her virtuous way of life should not stay unknown, an anchorite saw her in the desert of the holy Jordan and said to her: "Amma, what art thou doing in this desert?" ⟦s5⟧ She, wishing to escape the anchorite's notice, said to him: "Forgive me, for I have lost my way; but do me a kindness, Father, for the Lord's sake, and show it to me." ⟦s6⟧ But he, knowing from God how things stood with her, said to her: "Believe me, amma, [b: C] thou hast neither lost thy way, nor art thou looking for a way; but as one who knows that the lie is from the devil, tell me in truth the reason for which thou camest here." ⟦s7⟧ Then the virgin said to him: "Forgive me, abba: a young man was scandalized on my account, and for this I came into this desert, judging it better to die here than to become a stumbling-block to anyone, according to the Apostle's word." ⟦s8⟧ The elder said to her again: "And how long hast thou been here?" ⟦s9⟧ She said to him: "By the grace of Christ I have seventeen years." ⟦s10⟧ Again the anchorite said to her: "And where dost thou get thy food?" ⟦s11⟧ She brought out the little bag and the soaked pulses in it and said to the anchorite: "Look at this little bag that thou seest: it came out of the city with me, holding these few soaked pulses; and God has made such a dispensation for me, the lowly one, that for so long a time I have eaten of them, and they have not grown less. ⟦s12⟧ And know this too, Father: His goodness has so sheltered me that in these seventeen years no man has seen me, but thou alone today; but I saw everyone." ⟦s13⟧ And when the anchorite learned these things, he glorified God.'"
