⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [3052] CHAPTER 180

⟦s1+2⟧ The most holy Dionysius, priest and sacristan of the most holy church of the Ascalonites, told us about abba John the anchorite: "Truly he was a great man in God's sight in the present generation." ⟦s1+2⟧ And in proof of how well he pleased God, he told this wonder about him. "The elder," he says, "lived in a cave in the region of Sochous, the estate, a little under twenty miles from Jerusalem; and the elder had in his cave an icon of our all-holy, spotless Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-virgin Mary, carrying our God in her arms. ⟦s3⟧ So whenever he wanted to go off to some place, either into the far deserts, or to Jerusalem to venerate the holy Cross and the venerable places, or even to Mount Sinai to pray, or to martyrs lying many long distances away from Jerusalem (for the elder was a lover of the martyrs, and now he would go off to Saint John at Ephesus, now to Saint Theodore at Euchaita, and again to Saint Thecla of the Isaurians at Seleucia, and again to Saint Sergius at Saraphas; and he travelled now to this saint, now to that), he would make the lamp ready and light it, as was his custom; and standing in supplication, and beseeching God to direct his journey for him, he would say to the Lady, fixing his gaze on her icon: 'Holy Lady Theotokos, since I am about to walk a long road that has a term of many days, take care of thy lamp, and keep it from going out, as I intend; for I go with thy help as my fellow traveller.' And having said these things to the icon, he would go out. ⟦s4+5⟧ And having made the journey he had set before himself, he would return, sometimes after a month, sometimes after two or three, and at times even after five or six; and so he would find the lamp made ready and burning, just as he had left it when he set out on his journey; and he never once saw it gone out of itself, neither on rising from sleep, nor on coming back from a journey, nor on returning from the desert to the cave."

⟦s4+5⟧ CHAPTER 181

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The same Dionysius the priest also told us this about him: "One day the elder was walking in the bounds of Sochous, the estate, where his cave also was; and as he walked he saw a great lion coming from the opposite way and drawing near. ⟦s1⟧ He was walking along a very narrow path lying between two hedges, with which the farmers are used to fence [3053] [b: A] their fields, planting thorny shrubs; and the path was so narrowed by the thorns that it would barely take one man going on foot and carrying nothing, and would not let the one going along it walk without hindrance. ⟦s2⟧ When they drew near each other, the elder and the lion, neither did the elder turn back to give the lion passage, nor could the lion turn back, because the path was so very narrow, nor was there room for both to pass. ⟦s3⟧ So the lion, seeing that the servant of God wanted to go forward and would by no means turn back, stood up on its hind feet, and rising upright, leaned back against the hedge on the elder's left; and with the weight and strength of its body it made a little room, and gave the just man unhindered passage; [b: B] and so the elder passed, brushing against the lion's back. ⟦s3⟧ And then, after he had passed, the lion got up from the hedge and went on its own way."

⟦s4⟧ A brother visited abba John and found nothing in his cave. ⟦s5⟧ And he said to him: "How dost thou live here, Father, not having thy needs?" ⟦s6⟧ The elder answered: "This cave, child, is a wrestling-ground: give, take."

⟦s6⟧ CHAPTER 182

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There is a monastery near holy Bethlehem, Saint Sergius's, about two miles off, called Xeropotamus. ⟦s2⟧ Its superior was a very devout man named abba Eugenius, who later became bishop of Hermopolis in Egypt, which lies on the borders of the First Thebaid. ⟦s3⟧ He told us, when we visited him at the monastery, about abba Alexander the Cilician: that after he had grown old in the caves of the holy Jordan, he took him into his own monastery; and towards his end he became bedridden for three months. ⟦s4⟧ And ten days before he departed to the Lord, he was possessed by a demon, out of the devil's envy. ⟦s5⟧ So the elder began to say to the demon: "Thou hast come at evening, wretch. ⟦s6⟧ This is nothing great, when I lie loosed upon a bed and cannot move. ⟦s7⟧ Thou hast fully shown thy weakness, wretch, even against thy will. ⟦s8⟧ For if thou wast mighty and strong, thou oughtest to have come near me fifty or sixty years ago, so that, through Christ who strengthens me, I would have shown thee thy weakness, and cast down thy insolence, and crushed thy unbending neck. ⟦s9⟧ But now it is not my weakness, but that which wears me down. ⟦s10⟧ Yet I give thanks to God, to whom I am departing, and before whom I shall show the wrong I have suffered from thee: that after the hardship of so many years of ascesis, at my departure thou didst set upon me without mercy." ⟦s11⟧ Saying these things every day, together with many others, on the tenth day, in all stillness, he gave up his spirit to Christ the Master in peace.

⟦s11⟧ CHAPTER 183

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Abba Theodore the Cilician used to say: When I was living [3056] [b: A] in Scetis, there was an elder there named David. ⟦s2⟧ One day he went off to reap with other monks too. ⟦s3⟧ For it is the custom among the Scetiotes to go off to the estates and reap. ⟦s4⟧ So the elder went up to an estate and worked for a day's wage. ⟦s5⟧ A farmer hired him. ⟦s6⟧ As the elder was reaping, one day about the sixth hour there was a great heat, and the elder went off into a hut and sat down. ⟦s7⟧ The farmer came, and seeing him sitting, said to him angrily: "Why art thou not reaping, elder? ⟦s8⟧ Dost thou not know that I am paying thee a wage?" ⟦s9⟧ He said to him: "Yes; but since it is very hot, and the grains of wheat are falling out of the ears, I am waiting a little for the heat to pass, so that thou mayest not suffer loss." ⟦s10⟧ The farmer said to him: "Get up, work, even if it all burns." ⟦s11⟧ The elder said to him: "And dost thou want it to burn?" ⟦s12⟧ The farmer said to him in anger: "Yes, I told thee." ⟦s13⟧ Then the elder got up, and behold, suddenly the field began to burn. ⟦s14⟧ Then the farmer was afraid, and came to the other part where the other elders were reaping, and besought them to come and beseech the elder to pray and put out the fire. ⟦s15⟧ When they came, they made a prostration to the elder. ⟦s16⟧ But he said: "He himself said it should burn." ⟦s17⟧ Yet, besought by them, the elder went, and standing between what was already burnt and what was not burnt, he prayed; and at once the fire in the field was quenched, and the rest was saved; and they all marvelled and glorified God.
