⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ As we were going from holy Gethsemane to the holy Mount of Olives, there is a monastery called that of abba Abramius, which the great Abramius founded, he who was of the New Church of the all-praised Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, having become its superior after Eudoxius. ⟦s2⟧ In this monastery the superior was abba John [3065] [b: A] of Cyzicus. ⟦s2⟧ So we asked him one day, saying, "How does one acquire virtues?" ⟦s3⟧ And the elder answered: "If someone wants to acquire a virtue, unless he first hates the vice diametrically opposed to it, he cannot acquire it. ⟦s4⟧ So if thou wouldst have mourning, hate laughter. ⟦s5⟧ Wouldst thou have lowliness? ⟦s5⟧ Hate pride. ⟦s6⟧ Wouldst thou be self-controlled? ⟦s6⟧ Hate gluttony. ⟦s7⟧ Wouldst thou be chaste? ⟦s7⟧ Hate lust. ⟦s8⟧ Wouldst thou be without possessions? ⟦s8⟧ Hate matter. ⟦s9⟧ Wouldst thou be merciful? ⟦s9⟧ Hate the love of money. ⟦s10⟧ He who wants to live in the desert hates the cities; he who wants to practise stillness hates familiarity; he who wants to be a stranger hates making a show of himself; he who wants to become master of his anger hates living among laymen; and he who wants to be free of rancour hates abuse; [b: B] he who wants to be undistracted will stay alone; he who wants to master his tongue, let him stop his ears so as not to hear much; he who wants always to have the fear of God will hate bodily rest and will love affliction and straitness; and so he will be able to serve God sincerely."

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8+s9+s10+s11+s12+s13+s14+s15+s16⟧ Abba Theodore, the superior of the Old Laura, told us: "There were two brothers in Constantinople, silversmiths, Syrians.
