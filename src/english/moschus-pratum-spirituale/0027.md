⟦s1⟧ Athanasius of Antioch, of the household of Zemarchus, told us about abba Brochas the Egyptian, at Seleucia near Antioch: "He found a deserted place outside the city, and sought to make himself a little cell there. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ And when he had built it up, he had no timber to roof it. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ And going into the city one day, he finds Anatolius, called 'the Hunchback's', who was a landowner of Seleucia, sitting outside his house. ⟦s2+3+4+5+6⟧ And he went up to him and says, 'Do me a kindness: give me a little timber, so that I may roof my house.' And he, in a black temper, says to him, 'There's timber: lift it and be off', showing him a great mast that he had lying in front of his house, which would serve for a ship of five myriads. ⟦s7+8⟧ Abba Brochas says to him, 'Bless, and I will lift it.' Anatolius, again in a black temper, says to him, 'Blessed be the Lord.' And he put his arms round the mast, and raised it from the ground alone, and set it on his shoulder; and taking it, he went off to his cell. ⟦s9⟧ And Anatolius, struck with amazement at the strangeness of the wonder, made him a gift of that great timber for whatever need of his he wished; and out of it he not only roofed his aforesaid cell, but also made many other things for the monastery beside him." ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ The priest named Peter, who came from Rome, told us about Gregory, among the saints, the pope of that same city: "Having become pope, he built a great monastery of men, and gave a commandment that none of the monks should have anything of his own, not even so much as an obol. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Now a brother of the monastery had a brother who was a layman, and he asked him, saying, 'I have no shirt; but do me a kindness: buy me one.' The layman says to him, 'Here are three gold pieces: take them and buy thyself whatever kind pleases thee.' So the monk took the three gold pieces, and went and told the superior; the superior, hearing it, went and reported it to the most holy pope; and the blessed Gregory, learning of it, excommunicated him, as having broken the canons of the monastery. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ So after a little time the excommunicated brother died, without the pope's learning of it. ⟦s6⟧ So after two or three days the superior went and told him that the brother had gone to his rest. ⟦s7⟧ At this he was grieved beyond measure, that he had not loosed him from the penalty of excommunication before he departed this life; and writing a prayer on a slip he gave it to one of the archdeacons, bidding him go and read it over the brother. ⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ And it was a prayer loosing him, the dead man, from the excommunication. ⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ So the archdeacon went, as he had been charged, and read over the brother the letter that had the prayer in it; and that same night the superior sees the brother who had died; and the superior says, 'And where wast thou until today?' And the brother says to him, 'Truly, lord, in prison; and until yesterday I was not released.' So it became known to all that in the very hour the archdeacon said the prayer over the tomb, in that hour he was loosed from the excommunication, and his soul was set free from condemnation."

⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ He told us about abba Apollinarius among the saints, the pope of Alexandria, that he was very merciful and compassionate; and they told this to confirm it: There was in Alexandria a young man, a son born of the first men of the city. ⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ When they had died and left much of theirs [?] in ships and in gold, he went astray in managing it, and lost everything, and was driven into the last poverty: not by eating it up, nor by spending it on riotous living, but by falling into various shipwrecks. ⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ And from great he became small, according to what is said in the Psalms: "They go up to the heavens, and go down to the depths"; so the young man too, having been far up in money, became far lower still.

⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ [3073] [b: A] Blessed Apollinarius, having heard this, and seeing the young man come into such straits and poverty, and knowing his parents too, and understanding how they had been in wealth, wished to show him mercy and give him a little charity for his keep, and was ashamed to. ⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ And whenever he saw him in his audience-chamber, he was stung in his soul, seeing his soiled clothing and his gloomy face, which are the signs of the last poverty. ⟦s8+9+10+11+12+13⟧ So while the pope was in such anxiety, one day, moved by God, he devises a plan truly wonderful and befitting his blessedness.

⟦s14⟧ And he sent and brought the notary of the most holy church; and taking him aside he says to him, "Canst thou keep a secret for me, sir notary?" ⟦s15+16+17⟧ And he says to him, "I hope in the Son of God, master, that if thou biddest me, I will not tell a soul, nor will anyone learn from me anything of what thou entrustest to thy servant." ⟦s15+16+17⟧ Then pope Apollinarius says to him, "Go, make out a bond of debt for fifty pounds of gold, as from the most holy church to this Macarius, the father of this young man; and put on both witnesses and the completion, and bring it me [?]." ⟦s15+16+17⟧ And the notary did at once, that very moment, what the pope had bidden, and brought the document and gave it to him. ⟦s18⟧ Now since the young man's father had been dead ten years or even more, and the paper was new, the pope says to him, "Go, sir notary, keep the paper in wheat or in barley, [b: C] and after a few days bring it me." ⟦s19+20+21⟧ And he went off, and came after the said days bringing the bond to the pope. ⟦s19+20+21⟧ Then the pope says to the notary, "Go now and say to the young man, 'What wilt thou give me, and I give thee a bond that will profit thee?' ⟦s31⟧ [3076] [b: A] So, taking the bond, he goes up to the most holy pope, and makes a prostration to him, and hands him the bond. ⟦s32⟧ So when the pope had taken it and read it, he began to make himself out troubled. ⟦s33⟧ And he says to him, "And where wast thou till now? ⟦s34⟧ Thy father died more than ten years ago. ⟦s34⟧ Go, sir; I make thee no answer." ⟦s35+36⟧ And he says to the pope, "Truly, master, I did not have it myself, but the notary had it, and I did not even know; but God have mercy on him, for he has just now given it me, saying, 'Looking for papers I found it.'"
