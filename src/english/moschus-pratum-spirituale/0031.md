⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ CHAPTER 208

⟦s1+2⟧ A brother held fast by grief asked an elder, saying, "What shall I do? ⟦s1+2⟧ For my thoughts set upon me, saying, 'Thou didst renounce out of season; thou canst not be saved.'" ⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [3101] CHAPTER 209

⟦s1⟧ [b: A] One of the saints said: "When we pray to the Lord saying, 'Lead us not into temptation,' we do not say it so that we may not be tempted, for that is impossible; but so that we may not be swallowed up by the temptation by doing something displeasing to God. ⟦s2⟧ For this is what it is not to enter into temptation. ⟦s2⟧ For the holy martyrs too, tempted by tortures and not overcome, did not enter into temptation; just as neither does the man who fights with a wild beast, so long as he is not eaten by it; but when he is eaten, then he has entered into the temptation. ⟦s3⟧ So too with every passion, so long as one is not overcome by the passion."

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 210

⟦s1⟧ One of the Fathers told us that two bishops, near neighbours of each other, once fell into coolness toward each other. ⟦s2⟧ One of them was rich, the other humbler; and the rich one sought to do the other harm. ⟦s3⟧ The other heard of it, and knowing what he was going to do, he said to his clergy, "We shall win, by the grace of Christ." ⟦s4⟧ And they said to him, "Who can stand against that man, my lord?" ⟦s5⟧ He said to them, "Wait, and you will see." ⟦s6⟧ So he watches his time; and when his fellow bishop was holding a feast of holy martyrs, he takes his clergy and says to them, "Follow me, and we shall win." ⟦s7⟧ They said among themselves, "What can he be going to do?" ⟦s8⟧ And he comes to him. ⟦s9⟧ And as the other was coming with the procession, as they drew near, he falls at his feet with his clergy, saying, "Forgive us, my lord; we are thy servants." ⟦s10⟧ And the other, astounded at what he had done and pierced in his soul, God having changed his heart, takes hold of his feet, saying, "Thou art my lord and father." ⟦s10⟧ And from then on a great love arose between them. ⟦s11⟧ And he said to his clergy, "Did I not tell you that we should win by the grace of God? ⟦s12⟧ So you too, when you have anything against one another, do this, and you will win."

⟦s13⟧ The elder said again: "The humble man outdoes even the king himself in glory; for the one is praised to his face only, but the humble man is praised and called blessed both to his face and behind it."

⟦s13⟧ CHAPTER 211

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ One of the superiors told us: "There lived near our coenobium a certain elder; and he was good in the things of the soul. ⟦s2⟧ And a brother lived next to him. ⟦s3⟧ And while the elder was away, the brother, being worked upon, opened [3104] his cell, went in and took his books and his belongings. ⟦s4⟧ So when the elder came and opened the cell and found his things carried off, he went to the brother to tell him; and he found the things still lying out in the middle, for the brother had not yet put them away. ⟦s5⟧ And the elder, not wanting to shame him or to convict him, made as though his stomach were troubling him, and went out and stayed a good while as though at the call of nature, until the things could be put away out of the middle. ⟦s6⟧ And coming back after this, the elder began to ask him about some other matter, and did not convict the brother.

And he went away and appealed to some of the great men, for he was known to them for his virtue; and they sent and had him brought out of the prison."

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ CHAPTER 212

⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ One of the elders used to say: "A virtuous elder visited us; and we were reading in the garden the sayings of the holy Fathers. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ For the elder always loved to go through them, and he breathed them in within himself, which is why he had reaped every virtue from them. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ And when we came to that elder to whom the robbers came and said to him, 'We have come to take everything in thy cell'; and he said, 'Take whatever seems good to you, children.' ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ "So when we had read this, the elder says to me, 'Dost thou know, abba? ⟦s6+7⟧ This saying profited me greatly.' I say to him, 'How, father?' And I said to him playfully, 'Did they come back, like those men to the elder?' And he says to me at once, 'No; God forbid! ⟦s16⟧ For that was not what I wanted, that they should come back.'"

⟦s17⟧ CHAPTER 213

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ One of the elders used to speak of divine wonders that take place even now in the Church of God, because of the godless heresies that have sprung up and are springing up, and above all because of the schisms of Severus the Acephalite and of the other destroyers: for the security and confirmation of the weaker souls, and for the conversion of those very men, if they would have it. ⟦s1+2⟧ For besides [?] the signs that come about daily through the divine Fathers, and from of old through the holy martyrs, in the catholic Church of God, [there are these] even today.

⟦s1+2⟧ CHAPTER 214

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Soruda is a village in the territory of the city of the Coaneni, in which there is a font which on the holy Theophany sweats, and from the sweat is filled over three hours; and after the baptism, again, not all at once but little by little, over the same three hours, it goes down.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [3108] CHAPTER 215

⟦s1⟧ And there is also in the village of Kedrebata, which is in the territory of the city of the Oenoandans, a font which at the paschal feast of the Resurrection, though it is of a single stone, fills of itself, suddenly, and keeps its water until Pentecost; and suddenly after Pentecost the water disappears. ⟦s2⟧ Both these wonders are in the province of Lycia. ⟦s3⟧ If anyone will not believe them, it is no great trouble to go as far as Lycia, for full assurance of the truth.

⟦s4⟧ CHAPTER 216

⟦s4⟧ Another time, when I was in the holy city, a certain Christ-loving man comes and says to me, "Since we have had a little unpleasantness with each other, I and my brother, and he will not be reconciled to me, do me a kindness and speak to him." ⟦s5⟧ I received the word gladly, and calling his brother, I spoke to him the things that lead to love and peace. ⟦s6⟧ And he seemed to be persuaded by me. ⟦s7⟧ But afterwards he says to me, "I cannot be reconciled to him, for I swore by the cross." ⟦s8+9⟧ And I smiled and said to him, "Thy oath has this force: 'By the precious cross, O Christ, I will not keep thy commandments, but I will do the will of thine enemy the devil.' For we ought not only not to stand by what was wrongly resolved by us, but rather to repent and grieve over the things we wrongly resolved against ourselves, as the God-bearing Basil says. ⟦s10⟧ For Herod too, if he had repented and not stood by his oath, would not have wrought that great sin of beheading the forerunner of Christ." ⟦s11⟧ And besides, I brought him the text of holy Basil, which he brought from the Gospel, when the Lord wished to wash the feet of holy Peter and he refused.

⟦s11⟧ CHAPTER 217

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ An elder said: "Little children, salt is from water, and if it comes near water, it dissolves and vanishes. ⟦s2⟧ So too the monk is from woman, and if he comes near a woman, he dissolves, and comes to his end: to not being, that is, a monk."

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ CHAPTER 218

⟦s1⟧ Abba Sergius, the superior of the monastery of abba Constantine, told us: "Once, when we were travelling with a certain holy elder, we lost our way; and without our wishing it, and without our knowing where we were going, we found ourselves in the sown fields, and we [3109] [b: A] trampled a little of the crop. ⟦s2⟧ And the farmer, who happened to be working there, noticed it and began to abuse us a great deal in anger, and to say: 'You monks? ⟦s3+4+5+6+7⟧ You fear God? ⟦s3+4+5+6+7⟧ If you had the fear of God before your eyes, you would not have done this.' Again the man abused us in a rage. ⟦s3+4+5+6+7⟧ Again the elder says, 'Thou speakest in truth, child: if we were true monks, we would not have done this. ⟦s8+9⟧ But for the Lord's sake, forgive, for we have sinned.' And the blessed Sergius said that he did in truth follow with them, and came and received the habit.

⟦s10+11⟧ CHAPTER 219

And being greatly profited, I glorified the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, to whom belong might and majesty unto the ages of ages. ⟦s19+20+21⟧ Amen.
