# Cruces — moschus-pratum-spirituale

Translators' notes (kt method, 2026-10-27), moved out of the running English. Each is keyed by chunk, column and band, and quotes the English words it follows. Plate verdicts: `translate/moschus-pratum/verify/VERDICTS.tsv`. Glossary: `translate/moschus-pratum/GLOSSARY.md`. In the English, ⟨…⟩ marks words we supply where the printed sentence needs them (whether the plate lost them or Migne's text never had them); each has a note below.


## Chunk 0001 (mp-000)

- **2852** · …DEDICATION John Eviratus — P. The heading names the author John Eviratus, Migne's form for Moschus's byname; the addressee is his disciple Sophronius, later patriarch of Jerusalem.
- **2852** · …are planted by the outgoings of the waters; — S. Ps 1:3.
- **2852** · …more lovingly over the things that are here; — T. *περὶ τὰ τῇδε μᾶλλον ἐμφιλοχωρεῖν*, rendered as printed; the sense of *τὰ τῇδε* ("the things here") is unclear, and the Latin paraphrases the whole clause (*fidesque recta*).
- **2852** · …saints, wanted to make superior of the monastery. — T. The doubled article (*ὁ ἐν ἁγίοις ἡμῶν ὁ ἀρχιεπίσκοπος*) wants a name between, and the Latin has *sanctus Elias*; see the OCR table. The Latin also names the elder *Joannes*, which the Greek here does not.
- **2853A** · …of the drought, the one opposite the Jordan. — S. 1 Kgs (3 Kgdms) 17:3. P. Sapsas and the torrent Chorath (Cherith) lie east of the Jordan, opposite Jericho.
- **2853C** · …by name, a Cilician, priest for the baptism. — T. The Latin reads *Alexandrinus genere*; the Greek has *Κίλιξ*, Cilician.
- **2853C** · …wanted to appoint a deaconess for the purpose. — P. Peter, patriarch of Jerusalem 524–552.
- **2856** · …the right side of the altar. And seized — T. *ληφθεὶς* stands alone; "with fear" is supplied from the sense (Latin *magno pavore correptus*).
- **2856** · …days he went to his rest." CHAPTER 6 — T. No head is printed before this story or before chapters 8 and 10 in our Greek. Numbers 6, 8 and 10 are set by the sequence (V and VII, IX and the Latin CAPUT X); the star story is taken as part of chapter 6, as the Latin links it with *enim*.
- **2857A** · …went to his rest, as he had said." — T. The Latin has *tertia die*; the Greek says the second day.
- **2857C** · …such hardness of life that he became dropsical. — T. The clause has no verb; probably *ἤλασεν*, as in chapter 2 (*εἰς τοσαύτην ἤλασεν ἀρετήν*), has dropped out.
- **2857C** · …that I may be delivered from eternal punishment." — T. The Latin names the archbishop *Eustochius*, who was patriarch of Jerusalem 552–563; the Greek as given reads *Εὐτύχιος*. Rendered as printed, marked for the plate.

## Chunk 0002 (mp-001)

- **2861A** · …the Son of God comes and raises thee.'" — P. Agiodulus (Latin *Agiodulus*, *Ragiodulus*), superior of the laura of Gerasimus near the Jordan. The canonarch led the chanting and struck the wooden board (*ξύλον*, the semantron) to summon the brethren.
- **2861A** · …in place of those taken up by him. — S. Josh 4:9. P. Greek "Jesus son of Nave". T. Read *ἀρθέντων* ("taken up") for the printed *ἀρχόντων*, with the Latin *quos inde sustulerat*; see the OCR table.
- **2861B** · …say constantly, 'I am a stranger.'" CHAPTER 13 — T. Heads 13, 15, 17, 19, 21, 22, 23 and 25 are not printed in our Greek, and the printed numerals of 16 (*xv*) and 18 (*xvii*) fall one short. This OCR drops final *i*s (*xχχi* for XXXII, *σΑΡυΤ i.* for III), so the chunk is numbered without gaps from XII to XX, one story to a chapter. The Latin column shows no numerals here to check against.
- **2861B** · …so that some thought he was without flesh. — T. The Latin reads sixty-three years (*sexaginta tres*), and attaches them to his dwelling near Penthucla; the Greek has sixty-nine.
- **2861B** · …and never took anything at all from anyone. — T. *περὶ τῶν ἐπαγγελιῶν τοῦ Χριστοῦ … ἐδίδου αὐτὰς*: the feminine pronoun has no plain antecedent but the promises; "the proceeds" gives the sense (Latin *distribuebat pauperibus omnia*).
- **2861B** · …paid great heed to himself, and an ascetic. — T. *ἡμῖν* is printed twice ("told us again, saying to us"); rendered once. See the OCR table.
- **2861B** · …Hebrews met him and wanted to kill him. — T. The village name is uncertain; the Latin reads *Betamarim*.
- **2864** · …were sent on a service into the Thebaid. — P. Raithou, on the Sinai coast of the Red Sea; the Thebaid, Upper Egypt.
- **2865A** · …bran; and he partook three times a week. — T. Read *πίτυρα*, "bran", for the printed *πίτυ*, with the Latin *furfureum*. *μετελάμβανεν* may mean "ate" or "communicated"; the Latin takes it as *communicabat*.
- **2865B** · …CHAPTER 19 Abba Elias the grazer told us: — P. The *βοσκοί*, "grazers", were anchorites who lived on wild plants. T. The Latin adds that his cave was near the monastery of the Eunuchs and that it was the month of August; the Greek as given has neither.
- **2865B** · …not communicate with abba Macarius, bishop of Jerusalem, — P. Macarius II, patriarch of Jerusalem (552, and again 564–575); his first appointment was disputed on Origenist grounds.
- **2868A** · …told me that a dragonarius told him, saying: — P. A dragonarius carried the cohort's dragon standard. T. The Greek says the Father told "me" and the soldier told "me" again; the indirect "him" smooths the second.
- **2868A** · …Thecla, and deliver her out of lawless hands, — P. Thecla, the virgin companion of Paul in the *Acts of Paul and Thecla*, delivered from the beasts and the fire.
- **2868A** · …our father among the saints Euthymius, told me: — T. The Latin reads "told me and Sophronius the Sophist".
- **2868A** · …where the Saracen was broken limb from limb." — T. *ὅπου καὶ μέλος γέγονεν*, literally "where he became a limb"; perhaps *μέλη*. The Latin says only that he died.
- **2869A** · …without ceasing, and never absent from the church. — T. The Latin reads thirty years (*triginta*).
- **2869B** · …an elder living in the cells of Choziba, — P. Choziba, the monastery in the Wadi Qelt between Jerusalem and Jericho.
- **2869B** · …Abba Gregory, who had been of the Scholarii, — P. The Scholarii, the palace guard at Constantinople.
- **2869B** · …the prayer of oblation of the holy anaphora. — T. *προσκομιδή* here is the prayer of offering within the anaphora; the *εὐλογίαι* (eulogiae) are the loaves brought to be offered. P. John the Chozebite, later bishop of Caesarea in Palestine.

## Chunk 0003 (mp-002)

- **2872B** · …elder was great in the sight of God. — P. Calamon, a laura by the Jordan near Jericho. T. The name is printed *Κυικός*; read *Κυριακός* with the Latin *Cyriacus*.
- **2872B** · …ask the elder about the thought of fornication. — P. Dara, the fortress-city on the Persian frontier in Mesopotamia (Latin *Dora*).
- **2872B** · …his goodness will reveal the truth to thee." — T. The Latin turns this into a command (*spem in Deo habeto*, "have hope in God"), as if from *ἔχε*; the Greek as given has *ἔχω*, "I have", and that is rendered.
- **2873A** · …years with him, he fell asleep in peace. — T. The Latin reads four years (*quatuor … annis*) for the Greek *ἔτη ἱκανά*, "a good number of years".
- **2873B** · …Cilicia there is an estate named Mardardus . — P. Aegeae (Ayas), a port of Cilicia. T. The Latin spells the estate *Mardandos*; rendered as printed, marked for the plate.
- **2876** · …the aforesaid abba Julian the Stylite, told this: — T. The Latin names the narrator *Cyriacus*; the Greek as given reads *Κύριλλος*. Rendered as printed, marked for the plate. The father is named Conon below.
- **2877B** · …CHAPTER 30 Tadae is a trading-town of Cyprus. — T. The Latin reads *Dade*. P. Melitene, in Armenia Minor.
- **2880** · …Jesus Christ, in the time of the Passion.' — S. John 18:22 (cf. Matt 26:67).
- **2880B** · …thrust away the harlot who came to him." — S. Luke 7:37–50.
- **2880B** · …sense; and from her I heard these things. — T. The Latin adds *Erat autem illi nomen Maria*, "her name was Mary"; the Greek has no name.
- **2880B** · …for the kingdom of heaven is at hand." — S. Matt 4:17.
- **2881A** · …of the walls of the city's tower ; — T. *εἰς ἕνα τῶν τειχῶν τοῦ πύργου* does not agree (masculine *ἕνα* with neuter *τειχῶν*) and reads oddly; the sense is probably "one of the towers of the city wall", as the Latin has it (*in una turrium civitatis*). Rendered as printed, marked for the plate.

## Chunk 0004 (mp-003)

- **2881** · …fathers told us that there was in Theoupolis — P. Theoupolis ("City of God"), the name Antioch took after the earthquake of 528; Moschus uses it throughout.
- **2884C** · …Chalcedon: Elias to Aila, and Flavian to Petra. — P. Elias of Jerusalem and Flavian II of Antioch, both deposed by Anastasius I (491–518). Aila is Aqaba on the Red Sea; Petra, the city of Arabia.
- **2884C** · …us about the blessed Ephraim, patriarch of Antioch, — P. Ephraim of Amida, formerly Count of the East (see ch. 37), patriarch of Antioch 527–545.
- **2885B** · …it?" Then the archbishop took off the omophorion — P. The omophorion is the bishop's band worn over the shoulders; the Latin renders it *stola*.

## Chunk 0005 (mp-004)

- **2885** · …that time Ephraim was Count of the East, — T/P. Κώμης printed; read Κόμης (Latin *comes*). Ephraim of Amida, Count of the East, later patriarch of Antioch (ch. 36).
- **2888** · …the city had fallen down in the earthquake. — P. Antioch was wrecked by the earthquakes of 526 and 528.
- **2888B** · …our true God purchased with his own blood. — S. Acts 20:28.
- **2888B** · …told you, strive for almsgiving and for orthodoxy; — T. The bishop turns to the plural here (εἶπον ὑμῖν … ἀγωνίσασθε) after a singular address; the Latin keeps the singular (*dixi … insiste … decerta*). Rendered as printed.
- **2888B** · …for with such sacrifices God is well pleased." — S. Heb 13:16.
- **2889A** · …Constantinople, and exiled them to Euchaita in Pontus — P. Euphemius (490–496) and Macedonius II (496–511), patriarchs deposed by Anastasius I; Euchaita in Helenopontus.
- **2889A** · …them out, he says, with his own finger. — T. "Fourteen" has no noun in the Greek or the Latin; it is usually taken as fourteen years struck from his life.
- **2889B** · …of the church telling this. "The patriarch Anastasius," — P. Anastasius I, patriarch of Antioch 559–570 and 593–599.
- **2892B** · …oath, by him who said, "Do not lie": — S. Cf. Col 3:9; Lev 19:11.
- **2892B** · …was at Theoupolis with abba Gregory the patriarch, — P. Gregory, patriarch of Antioch 570–593.
- **2892B** · …from Jerusalem, he of the laura of Pharon, — P. Pharon (elsewhere Pharan), the laura of St Chariton in the Judaean desert.
- **2893A** · …making three prostrations and saying a priest's prayer, — T. εὐχὴν πρεσβυτέρου, "a priest's prayer" (Basil is a priest); the Latin has him praying for the elder (*seni orationem facientem*). Rendered as printed.
- **2893A** · …saying to the bishop: "Do not touch me; — S. Cf. John 20:17.
- **2893A** · …And they said, "See, here are two swords." — S. Luke 22:36, 38, as printed.
- **2893A** · …I was in the desert near the Calamon, — P. The Calamon and the laura of the Towers were lauras in the Jordan valley near Jericho.
- **2896A** · …stillness, content with the blessing of the church — T. εὐλογία, "blessing": the gift of blessed bread given out by the church (Latin: *panis eleemosyna, quam ecclesia dabat*).
- **2896A** · …In the same place we saw abba Auxanon — P. The Latin prints the name as *Anaxanon*.
- **2896A** · …that every four days he ate one offering — T. προσφορά, "offering": the small loaf baked for the eucharistic offering, here a loaf of twenty lepta (the Latin has *minutorum viginti*).
- **2896A** · …the saints Sabas, sends him a little cloth — T. μαλάκιον, a small soft cloth or wrapper; the Latin has *sudarium*.
- **2896A** · …Eustochius and Gregory, who are among the saints; — P. Eustochius, patriarch of Jerusalem 552–563/4, and probably Gregory of Antioch (ch. 40); the Latin names only Eustochius.

## Chunk 0006 (mp-005)

- **2896** · …there was an archbishop by the name of — T. The opening line (Ἐν Θεσσαλονίκῃ ἦν τις ἀρχιεπίσκοπος τῷ ὀνόματι, p0062.50) was read by the OCR but dropped from our working text; restored from the plate 2026-11-04 (`RESTORED-LINES.tsv`). The Latin has *Fuit quidam Thessalonicae archiepiscopus, Thalelaus nomine*.

- **2897A** · …an elder lived outside the city of Antinoë, — P. Antinoë (Antinoopolis), in the Thebaid.
- **2901A** · …be turned away humbled and put to shame,' — S. Ps 73(74):21 (LXX), as printed.
- **2901B** · …of the blessed Hesychius, the priest of Jerusalem; — P. Hesychius, priest of Jerusalem (d. after 450), the exegete; the Latin takes the genitive as the lender (*acceperam mutuo a beato Isychio*).
- **2901B** · …but blasphemed her still more. A third time — T. "A third time" presupposes a second appearance the Greek does not narrate; the Latin has one (*Noli, quaeso, noli ita laedere animam tuam*). Rendered as printed.
- **2901B** · …for his blasphemy; and that out of loving-kindness. — T. The column ends without a full stop; read as complete ("and that out of loving-kindness", Latin *clementer cruciatus*).
- **2904** · …48 Anastasius, priest and keeper of the treasures — T. κειμηλιάρχης, keeper of the treasures (sacred vessels) of the church of the Anastasis, the Holy Sepulchre; Latin *ornamentorum custos*.

- **2905A** · …night I got up to strike the wood — T. The wooden semantron, struck to wake the brethren for the office; the canonarch's duty (the Latin glosses it so).
- **2905B** · …the cities of coastal Phoenice are thrown down." — P. Probably the earthquake of July 551, which destroyed Berytus and the coastal cities of Phoenice.

## Chunk 0007 (mp-006)

- **2905** · …a laura called the laura of the Egyptians. — T. Chapter number 51, and 52–53 after it, from the sequence: no Latin CAPUT head survives before LV, and the Greek prints only `σΑΡΥΤ i.` here. LI–LIII fit the heads that do survive (LIV, LV, LVI) if the two Stephen Trichinas stories form one chapter, as the two Irenaeus stories do under LV. σημεῖον as a distance is rendered "mile".
- **2908** · …a hair shirt, a cloak, a mantle , — T. μεγαλείου is not a garment word I can place; the Latin omits it (and adds *et librum*, which the Greek lacks). "Mantle" is a guess.
- **2908** · …the coenobium of the Cave of abba Sabas, — P. The Cave (Spelaion), a coenobium founded by Sabas near the Great Laura.
- **2909A** · …laura I got a book of the elders, — T. βιβλίον γεροντικόν, a book of the sayings of the elders (a Gerontikon); the next sentence quotes one of its chapters.
- **2909A** · …reason for my coming to this place ." — T. ἐν τόπῳ reads short; the Latin *in hunc locum* suggests ἐν τῷ τόπῳ τούτῳ, which the English follows.
- **2912A** · …of Aegae there stood a stylite named Symeon. — T. Greek τεσσάρων "four"; the Latin has *quadraginta* "forty". The number could have been misread on either side, so the English keeps the Greek and marks it.
- **2912A** · …Julian the Stylite, the one on the Gulf, — P. Julian the Stylite "of the Gulf": the Gulf of Issus, near Aegae in Cilicia.
- **2912B** · …Abba Peter, the priest of the same laura, — P. "The same laura": the last laura the Greek names is Sabas's (ch. 53); the Latin makes Stephen Trichinas superior of Sabas's monastery.
- **2913B** · …the holy Theotokos Mary of the New Church; — P. The Nea, Justinian's church of the Theotokos in Jerusalem.

## Chunk 0008 (mp-007)

- **2916** · …he saw the priest crucified, as Christ was; — T. ὡς ἐπὶ τοῦ Χριστοῦ, "as in the case of Christ"; the Latin has *crucifixum cum Domino*.
- **2917** · …nature of men can wrestle with this one?' — T. The Greek runs straight from Theodosius's question into the young man's command with no change of speaker. The Latin has more here (*Ait autem ad me juvenis ille praeclarus: Prorsus cum illo te luctari opus est*). Either a line was lost or Migne's Greek is shorter than his Latin. The plate needs checking.
- **2917C** · …say , he made it known by signs." — T. εἴπει is not a form. The Latin *si loqui voluisset* suggests something like ἤθελεν εἰπεῖν, and the English follows the sense. διὰ ψήφου is literally "by a pebble"; rendered "by signs" after the Latin *signo*.
- **2917C** · …the holy Theotokos Mary of the New Church, — T. No chapter head is printed here in either column. 68 comes from the sequence: the Latin gives LXVII just before, and the Greek gives LXIX next. P: the Nea in Jerusalem.
- **2920A** · …and robbers came up and stripped the shirt — T. Printed κου σούλλιον. It is probably κουκούλλιον "cowl", or else a diminutive of the καμίσιον "shirt" he had just been given. The Latin has *pallio*. The English keeps the shirt of the story.
- **2920A** · …God, who had his monastery at the Lithazomenon; — P. The Lithazomenon, a quarter or site at Alexandria, otherwise unknown to me.
- **2921A** · …him who had nowhere to lay his head." — S. Matt. 8:20.
- **2921A** · …said again: "Affliction, according to Paul, works patience; — S. Rom. 5:3.
- **2921A** · …the world, nor the things in the world." — S. 1 John 2:15.
- **2924A** · …when their faces will shine like the sun? — S. Cf. Matt. 13:43.
- **2924A** · …he shut himself up on the other side — T. εἰς τὸ ἀπὸ μέρος does not construe as printed. The Latin *ad aliam partem* suggests εἰς τὸ ἄλλο μέρος.
- **2924A** · …Palladius told us again, saying: "In the Arsinoite — P. The Arsinoite nome, the Fayum. Moschus calls it a city of the Thebaid.

## Chunk 0009 (mp-008)

## Chunk 0010 (mp-009)

- **2932A** · …story when we came to him at Theoupolis: — P. Theoupolis, "City of God", the official name of Antioch after 528.

- **2937A** · …all thought, was seen not by two, three, — T. The Greek as given reads οὐχ οἱ δύο, οἱ τρεῖς; the Latin has *non unus, aut duo, aut tres*. Something may be lost.

## Chunk 0011 (mp-010)

- **2945A** · …the second hour; and about the ninth hour — T. The Latin has *sextam*.
