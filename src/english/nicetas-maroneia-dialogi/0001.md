GR. Nature is one thing beside person, and essence another beside hypostasis. For so it has seemed good, even if sometimes among some men essence — that is to say, nature — and hypostasis, being different names, are taken one for the other, that is, of one and the same thing. But hypostasis and person are the same, just as again essence and nature are the same. And consequently with these, the things called essential and natural are the same, while other beside these are the hypostatic and personal, these too being the same as each other. For the essential and natural are one set of things and the hypostatic and personal another, even if neither are the essential and natural apart from the hypostatic and personal, nor these apart from those. For indeed essence and nature are, as I said, one thing beside hypostasis and person, but neither of them is apart from the others; for neither is essence without hypostasis, nor hypostasis without essence.

[0177] LAT. Next, what do you take these to be — I mean essence and nature, or the essential and natural things — and what the others, hypostasis and person, or the hypostatic and personal things?

GR. What is common and one and the same to Father and Son and Spirit, this I hold to be essence and nature; and the things that belong to these in common and unitedly and identically, essential and natural; but each of the three, hypostasis and person; and whatever is proper to each single one, this hypostatic and personal.

LAT. Perhaps you say these things well; only make plain by examples too what you have said.

GR. The supernatural and superessential Godhead we are accustomed to call the nature of the holy Trinity, one and the same, and an essence both simple and unvarying, holding itself the same and in the same way in the Father and in the Son and in the Holy Spirit. But natural and essential — even if, strictly, those things are so called which belong to some subjects according to essence, yet now in the case of the Trinity we so call, catachrestically, the things contemplated about the essence in common and identically and undividedly, since the things that are according to essence have altogether the hidden and the unknowable, and are nameless and above name. For to us both the knowledge of God and the naming of him, and every name and every thought, comes not from the things according to him, but from the things about him.

We say, then, that these are natural and essential in the case of the holy Trinity: the supergood and all-good goodness; the superpowerful and all-powerful power; the superwise and all-wise wisdom; the all-working, the creative, the demiurgic, the providential, the sustaining of all creation; the everlasting; the timeless and above time; the eternal and above the age; that which is without beginning in respect of time; the uncreated; the ingenerate (written with a single ν); the unending; the incomprehensible; the ungraspable; the uncircumscribed; the invisible; the infinite; that which is above all sense-perception and all thought — simply, all things whatsoever that are common and the same to Father and Son and Holy Spirit we call essential and natural, not strictly, as belonging to them according to essence, but catachrestically, as contemplated about the essence in common and identically and undividedly.

And hypostasis and person we call each of the three, the Father, the Son, the Spirit; and hypostatic and personal, the things that belong distinctively to each of these: paternity, sonship, the property of the Holy Spirit — according to which, namely, the Spirit is named proceeding; the unbegotten (written with two ν), the begotten likewise, and the proceeding; unbegottenness, generation, and procession. And these do not shift about, but each is the characteristic property and mark of each hypostasis, unmovably and unalterably: of the Father, unbegottenness, because he is not from anyone, and it is neither the Son's nor the Spirit's; of the Son, generation, and it is neither the Father's nor the Spirit's — for he alone is begotten from the Father, wherefore also Only-begotten; [0180] of the Spirit, procession, and it is neither the Father's nor the Son's; for the Spirit alone is proceeding.

3. LAT. What then? Since the Father is unbegotten, because he is not from anyone, and this is proper to him, shall nothing on that account be from him either?

GR. Truly the Father is both unbegotten and not from anyone; but from him have come forth the Son and the Spirit.

LAT. And what then? Since there is a certain going-forth from him, is this one in mode and without difference?

GR. It is not. For the mode of the going-forth is different, and is manifestative of different persons. For one is the mode of the going-forth according to which the Son is from him, and it is called generation; and another the mode according to which the Spirit is said to come forth from him, and this is called procession. But both are according to essence — not that they introduce a difference of essence, far from it, but because from the essence of the Father is both the Son, yet by way of begetting, and the Spirit, yet by way of procession, and not from outside, out of that which is not, as the creatures are. And the Father is said to beget the Son, but to put forth or to emit the Spirit, or I know not how I shall say it more fittingly. And each mode has obtained its own name, which the Holy Spirit appointed; but to us it is unknown and incomprehensible; and that the one is this and the other that, we know from the things said differently about each. But what and of what sort each is, and what the difference is, is known to the Trinity alone; for nothing further concerning these things has been disclosed to us out of the divine oracles. Or do you not yourself say so too?

LAT. I do say so; for in these things I am of one mind with you and of the same opinion. But now let us enter upon what is in doubt and under inquiry, and about which the difference between us lies, and on account of which perhaps we are also divided. Let this, then, be inquired: whether one must say that the Spirit proceeds from the Father alone, or also from the Son.

GR. That, namely, which the Holy Spirit itself unveiled through the divinely inspired Scriptures, and which the holy Fathers and teachers of the Church, initiated through the Holy Spirit into the doctrines of the evangelical and apostolic faith, handed down in their own divinely inspired teachings; and it is in nothing contrary in any way to right and pious reason, nor indeed does it have any absurdity following upon it.

LAT. But tell me at least one thing, that I too may know.

[0181] 4. GR. If the Spirit that proceeds from the Father proceeds also from the Son, the things pertaining to the properties are confounded — either as passing over from one hypostasis into another, or as the same hypostasis taking on one property from another; which is not far from the opinion of Sabellius. For if the Father and the Son are one and the same according to Sabellius, it follows altogether that the Spirit, proceeding from the Father, proceeds also from the Son; and perhaps even from itself.

LAT. But among us the paltry opinion of Sabellius has no place; for neither do we confound the hypostases and the persons, nor do we transpose the properties, nor do we transfer the one and the same hypostasis into one property and another; nor do we hold the three to be one person, or one hypostasis to be three-personed; but we say that the hypostases of the Godhead are three, and we keep each hypostasis in its unmoved property. For we lay it down that the unbegotten is proper to the Father, the begotten to the Son, and the proceeding to the Holy Spirit. But just as of the Father there is not the unbegotten only, but also the generative and the putting-forth, that is, *προεκτικόν*, if one must so speak, so also of the Son there is not the begotten only, but also the putting-forth; and of the Spirit, the proceeding indeed, yet not simply, but thus from the Father and thus from the Son — that is, immediately from the Son, but mediately from the Father, because from the Father indeed through the Son, but from the Son through no other.
