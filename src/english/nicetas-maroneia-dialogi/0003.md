Therefore neither when I say that the Spirit has its procession immediately and mediately, or that the Son is both begotten from the Father and puts forth the Spirit, do I bring in any doubleness and composition. For just as the different going-forth from the Father — as you too say — shows his relation to be different, [0188] the one toward the Son as of a Father, the other toward the Spirit as of one who puts forth according to a proper mode, and yet does not indicate the paternal hypostasis to be composite, so neither does the Spirit's proceeding from the Father and from the Son give one to conceive composition, either in the case of the Son or in the case of the Spirit. And yet in the Father's case the begetting and the putting forth are not the same, nor in the same way; whereas in the Spirit's case the proceeding is one and the same, even if from the one mediately — that is, from the Father through the Son — and from the other immediately, because from the Son, and no longer through any other.

The Son, then, is not composite, because he is not begotten only but also puts forth the Spirit; nor the Spirit, because it proceeds not from the Father alone but also from the Son; for neither is the Father, because he is not unbegotten only but also begetter of a Son, nor because he is not begetter only but also putter-forth of the Spirit. For indeed not even on the common opinion of ourselves and of you are the Son and the Spirit composite, because each of them has, together with the essence, the proper mark of its hypostasis — the one the begotten only, the other the proceeding from the Father.

For if each hypostasis were composite on account of its own properties, then the essence of the three hypostases would also be composite on account of the marks common to the three that are about it — the good, I mean, the wise, the powerful, the creative, the all-working, the demiurgic, the providential, the sustaining, the encompassing and comprehending of all things, the uncreated, the unoriginate, the immortal, the unending, the infinite, the eternal, the timeless, the above-time, and whatever else of this kind is said of God, whether by position or by abstraction, whether according to cause or according to eminence and transcendence. But these are not the essence, but things contemplated about the essence, even if they are somehow called essential and natural; nor are they parts of the essence, for the Godhead is partless. So too there belong to fire both lightness and upward tendency, and the illuminative and the combustive — different powers — yet fire is not on that account composite, but simple, so far as it can be in natural and elemental bodies, according to the last, so to speak, echo of simplicity. And yet here these things are essential to fire, whereas there some are contemplated about the essence, and others severally about each hypostasis; wherefore much more will the simplicity suffer no harm, neither that of the essence nor that of each of the hypostases.

Nor yet, in all cases in which some variety and distinction is contemplated, is composition thereupon also brought in. For every essence has both power and activity, even if it be a simple one, and we shall not on that account call the simple one composite in every case. For it must be either that the simple too is composite — and then none will be simple — or that the simple is without activity and power, which is plainly false. For neither is there activity apart from the power out of which it comes forth, nor power without the essence, nor indeed essence apart from the power of acting. But if these things are impossible, then the absolutely simplest essence too exists together with the power inherent in it, [0189] having also the activity that comes forth from the power. And for these reasons there is no composition about it, in that about the same and one thing, the essence, the power is one thing and the activity another; and in that one and the same thing is out of essence and power and activity, if one must so speak; and in that the power is distinguished from the essence in account, and the activity from the power. For thus too the essence is one and simple.

How could anyone dare to say, of the truly simple and uncomposite nature — that is, of each of the unitary hypostases of the super-divine Godhead — that composition is introduced out of the natural or hypostatic properties? What is simpler than the monad among numbers? And yet its being is one thing, its power another, and its activity another. For it makes all things the same and keeps them unaltered; yet it slips into well-nigh infinite relations, being added and subtracted and generating all the species of numbers: now becoming the half and the subduple, now a third and the subtriple, now a fourth and the subquadruple [lat: the Latin column gives *dimidium et duplum … tertium et triplum … quartum et quadruplum*, "the half and the double … a third and the triple … a fourth and the quadruple" — the reciprocals of the Greek's ὑπο- compounds], and at another time something else, and all of them in the same respect, and this to infinity; now conceived as figureless, now as figured, and at one time triangular, at another square, at another pentagonal, and one thing and another and all at once, and this to infinity; and now plane, now solid; and now without relation, now having relation, and that in manifold ways. And though it appears manifold on account of its powers and its activities, it is nevertheless most simple, and this very variety does not harm the simplicity of the essence — I mean the essence that belongs to it. For what being is to each thing, this is also its essence.

If, then, among things at our own level the things contemplated about each simple thing do not bring in doubleness and composition, much more is no doubleness and no composition brought in, among the hypostases of the divine principle, out of the interweaving of certain properties. Neither, then, is the Son composite, if he is not only begotten from the Father but also putter-forth of the Spirit; nor the Spirit, if it proceeds not from the Father only but also from the Son. For neither indeed is the Father, being unbegotten and begetter of the Son and putter-forth of the Spirit. For just as the unbegotten and the begotten and the proceeding, contemplated simply, are different hypostatic properties, each of the three marked off to each of the three persons, and yet do not divide their one nature, so also, even if the properties about each of the three hypostases be more than one, not even so do they cut or divide any hypostasis of them, nor indeed do they show any of them to be composite.

And as the Father is not divisible because from him are the Son and the Spirit (for these are not parts of the Father, nor is the going-forth of each of them known by way of any cutting or flowing), so neither is the Spirit divisible because it is from Father and Son; nor does it have the one part from the Father and the other from the Son, but the simple and one thing which it is, is from the Father and from the Son: partless from the partless, and simple from the simple. And if you say, How? — the mode is unutterable [0192] and inexpressible.
