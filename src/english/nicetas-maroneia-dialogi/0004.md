6. GR. But if it proceeds from the Father and from the Son, as you say, its procession will be double. And if the procession is double, that which proceeds is also double. But the double is not simple; therefore the Spirit is not simple, according to you. But what is not simple is composite and divisible; and what is such is not God. But the Spirit is God; therefore it is not double. And if it is not double, nor composite, neither is the procession double; and if the procession is not double, there will not be one from the Father and another from the Son, but either from the Father or from the Son. And since it is from the Father (for this is what both the Fathers and the Gospel say), it remains that it is not from the Son.

LAT. Not even so does your argument carry necessity, most wise Greek. For in the first place we do not say that the procession is double, but one and the same and simple, the Spirit also being simple as God; for there is no doubleness or composition in God. For it is not double because it is from two, but simple because that which proceeds is simple — the same procession having one relation and another in the same respect, and being mediate as from the Father, immediate as from the Son. For just as the flashing forth of light in the air, coming forth from the sun and from the sun's rays — immediately from the rays, but mediately from the sun (for it is through the rays) — nevertheless neither has itself any doubleness in respect of its own existence (for the light in the air is simple and uncomposite: what is its composition? and out of what elements, tell me, is light compounded?), nor yet is its [0193] going-forth double, although the things from which the going-forth comes are two, the sun and the ray: so also the procession from the Father and from the Son is simple in itself, even if the persons from which it comes are two, and the Spirit is simple.

Besides, if some composition were contemplated about the Father and the Son, from whom the procession comes, then what is from them would also be double and composite, and there would be doubleness about the procession too. But since the Father and the Son, being one according to essence and existing in one another according to the word I am in the Father and the Father in me, are understood to have no composition toward one another (for their unity is above composition), the conjoining of the Father and the Son is not composition (for otherwise the conjoining of the whole Trinity would be composition); nor is the going-forth of the Spirit, which we say to be from both, composite; nor will the Spirit thence have any composition. So too the activity of the simple essences that come after God is not composite because it comes forth from the essence and from the essence's own power; nor is the going-forth of the activity from both composite. For neither is the coexistence of essence and power a composition; otherwise no essence would be simple, since none is without power — for nothing whatever among the things that are is without power, according to the wise Dionysius. But if among the things after God the going-forth from two is neither itself composite, nor shows that which comes forth to be composite, how much more in God's case?

But let us grant, if you like, that we do lay down a double procession, the one from the Father and the other from the Son, the one mediate and the other immediate: not even so is there doubleness about the Spirit, nor will the Spirit's procession be composite on that account. For these things are about the essence, not according to the essence — unbegottenness, generation and procession — and they are distinctive of hypostases, not divisive of nature; and there is no absurdity in the simple having two properties. For indeed the Father is both father and putter-forth, and there is no composition in him. And besides, the mediate and the immediate are certain relations, and a different relation does not work composition. For if three monads be set out one after another, let us say, the one between the extremes has, in relation to the first, the second place, but in relation to the third, the first; and because the same one is both first and second, it is not on that account composite. And in relation to the first it is perhaps conceived as holding the right-hand part, but in relation to the second the left-hand; and because the same one is both right and left, it does not on that account lose its own simplicity. Again, each of the three monads is not simply a monad but has an order as well; wherefore the one monad might be called first, another middle, another last; yet composition is said to be about none of them.

For if composition is judged from the things contemplated about the essence, then the essence conceived as simple, even apart from the activity that belongs to it and from the power inherent in it — that which it is — would appear composite, in that it is both essence and simple. But it is not composite. For [0196] simplicity is not the essence for simple things, because neither is composition the essence for composite ones, but it is about the essence. And the things about the essence, even if they be more than one for a single thing, do not make the essence composite. So that whether the procession be simple or not simple, the Spirit is simple, and that which proceeds is not composite; for the procession is not essence but is about the essence, the proper characteristic mark of the hypostasis.

7. GR. Next: if you say that it proceeds from the Father and proceeds also from the Son, and that on this account the procession is the one from the Father and the other from the Son, how is not each of the two imperfect, or one of them superfluous? For either the one from the Father is perfect, and the one from the Son superfluous; or the one from the Son is perfect, and the one from the Father superfluous. For the one of them, whichever it be, being perfect, what need is there of the other? Or else each is imperfect when contemplated apart from the other; but in that way the Father too, conceived by himself, would be imperfect. But in fact each person is perfect and productive of perfect activity, contemplated both by itself and together with that which is of one nature with it.
