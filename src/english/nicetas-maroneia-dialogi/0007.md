Whence the sons of the physicians also say that some of the drugs are of the first order, others of the second, others of the third, and others of the fourth. And likewise also in the acting and the being acted upon; for the more and the less are in these too, and the most. But also in place; for the above or below, or to the right or to the left, or in front or behind or round about or in the middle, has a certain order. And in time: the yesterday and the today and the tomorrow and the earlier and the now and the later belong to order. But also in the relatives; for whether the earlier and the later, or also the simultaneous, be in them, these too belong to order. And in the same way in all cases.

And simply, in each of the things contemplated singly and by themselves there would be no order; but where some number is contemplated, or a relation to another, there order also is, whether the thing be treated as of nature, or more logically, or contemplated mathematically, or even theologically. I mean something of this sort. The monad, when it is contemplated by itself, is both without relation and alone and above order; but when it is contemplated in relation to the dyad and the triad and the numbers that follow, it is not called monad simply, but also first and principle of numbers. The element likewise: such as earth, water and the rest, when they are thus contemplated and spoken of by themselves; but in relation to the whole world and to the things that come from them, they are both elements and principles. The twenty-four letters are letters in themselves, but elements as principles of the syllables and of the rest that follow. The day likewise; for it too is one by itself, but first as in relation to those after it. Whence perhaps Moses also, contemplating the first day by itself, as the following days had not yet come to be, named it "one"; but when the following days had come after it, it is possible to call it first also in relation to them. And I am not indeed ignorant of the reasons for which the saints said that day to be one and to be so called; nor, knowing them, do I disown them, but I both know them and heartily accept them.

Again, God is both one and alone; but when he is contemplated in relation to mind and soul and bodies, then he both is and is called first according to order, as principle of all things. But in the divine nature also there is number, the super-numerical Triad; and in the nature of the angel and in that of man the corresponding number, for there are many angels and many men; then order too is contemplated in them, yet not according to essence, but according to certain properties; and the one is as it were a kind of principle, while the other has a different order. Thus there is a ruler among angels and archangels, and among [0209] men Adam is head of the earth-born; and in the Trinity, then, the Father is principle.

But do not conclude to me, from the fact that there are many angels and many men, that there are many gods also. Away with that Hellenic folly! but rather that in Father and Son and Holy Spirit there is order too, yet an order higher than all order. For as the nature transcends all natures (for it is above nature and super-natural), so also the order transcends all orders (for it is above order and super-ordered), and the Father is principle, super-principial. Nor again, from the fact that Adam is head of the earth-born among men and the commander-in-chief Michael is chief of the angels — so to speak — among the angels, conclude that the archangel is some father and begetter of the multitude of the angels, as we said Adam, or those from Adam, was of the multitude of men. And yet as there is fatherhood on earth, so also in the heavens; but the fatherhood is proper to each of the two natures and befitting the nature.

There is, then, a certain order in the case of the Trinity as well; yet one that does not bring in a division of nature (for the nature is one and unvarying for the three), nor a positioning in place (for the Trinity is uncircumscribed and above every place), nor an appearance of temporal motion or of anything else of the things contemplated in what comes after God (for both ages and times are after God), but according to the differences of the properties of each supremely divine hypostasis; and because the Father is without principle and is principle of the Son, while the Son is principle and not without principle, in respect at least of having the Father as principle — since according to time he too is without principle — and the Spirit is itself principle also, together with the principle; for there is principle and principle and principle, yet not three principles, but one and in this way principle, since both the Son and the Spirit are referred, through the Son, to one principle; and so also God and God and God, yet not three Gods, but one God.

If, then, there is order in the Trinity too, as the saints also said, and order is the first and the earlier or second and the simultaneous (for in whatever things this is, some one of these is also present in them), it is necessary that in the Trinity too either the simultaneous be contemplated, or the first and the earlier and the second, or both.

[0212] GR. This beginning we lay down once more, for our joint inquiry with you concerning the Holy Spirit: how it is not absurd to say that it proceeds not from the Father alone, but also from the Son. For it is necessary either that it be proceeding from the essence of the Father, or from the property. But if from the essence, then, since the essence is common and the same to the Father and the Son and the Spirit, the Spirit would proceed not from the Father and the Son only, but also from itself — which is not the case. And if from the property, and the property is not essence but about the essence, how will that which is by itself, namely the Spirit, be proceeding from that which is not by itself but about another, that is, from the property? Either, then, it is not capable of proceeding at all, or, if it is capable of proceeding and proceeds from the Father and from the Son, it will be proceeding from itself also — which never is.

LAT. This argument shows not that it does not proceed from the Son, but that it does not proceed from the Father either, nor is it capable of proceeding at all.… [lat: the Latin column gives *non quod procedit ex Filio*, "not that it proceeds from the Son," without the Greek's negative μή]
