The establishing and the demonstration is either through an argument framed by art, proceeding from true principles, or through examples, or through testimonies, or through certain things that follow upon the testimonies and the scriptural usages; since he who brings in anything apart from these seems to say nothing persuasive nor reasonable, let alone true or reverent; and he seems also to bring in paradoxical notions — not to say heterodox ones — apart from testimonies and establishing.

LAT. This indeed I will do, only tomorrow; but for the present let us here hold our tongue.

[ed: A centered heading across both columns stands here on the plate (leaf 103) — ΛΟΓΟΣ ΤΕΤΑΡΤΟΣ, "Fourth Discourse" (the Latin column: DIALOGUS QUARTUS) — which our Greek source omits.]

GR. Come now, let us see, O Latin, whether you have any images or examples, either fashioned by yourself or taken from somewhere else, for a plainer demonstration [0213] of the procession of the Holy Spirit from Father and Son, as you say; and things through which it is possible to picture clearly what is being laid down as doctrine, if anywhere it is possible to discern how of one and the same thing this is a cause and that also; and principle and principle, and not two principles, nor two causes; so that one who grants that the cause of the Spirit, and likewise the principle, is not the Father alone but the Son also, may not seem to bring in two principles or two causes, but in this way also may guard the doctrine of the monarchy.

LAT. And indeed, O Greek, a plain example is not to be found that displays the relation or the order of the holy Trinity; yet there are many things able to picture dimly what is being said, of which some are gathered from Scripture and others found from the very nature of things and of notions.

GR. Bring these forward to me, then, and let us consider them together; for perhaps we too shall set over against them examples more apposite.

LAT. I speak, then; and do you listen with understanding. Does not every essence have both a power connatural to itself and a natural operation?

GR. I say it does.…

LAT. Consider for me, then, this example also. There is the essence of fire, and its burning power, and the operation of the power, the burning; and the effect, the coal, the ash. So also the Father.…

LAT. There is point and line and surface; these three are one principle of what are called mathematical bodies, as there the essence, the power and the operation are one principle of the things operated; or, if you will, monad, dyad, triad — this arithmetical triad is principle and cause and fountain of every number.… For conceive for me a finger and a whole hand, on which the finger depends, and a whole man, whose the hand and the finger are.… You know an eye and a fountain and a river.… Again another example, and this too of the same thing, taken by the Saint from Scripture: Adam, Eve, Seth.… But I will tell you yet another example, in which there is no such defect.

GR. What is it?

LAT. That of the sun again, and of the ray and of the light, as bearing on what is useful for the doctrine.…

That the διά in Scripture is used indifferently with the ἐκ.

Recapitulation of the examples that have been set forth.

[ed: A centered heading across both columns stands here on the plate (leaf 105) — ΛΟΓΟΣ ΠΕΜΠΤΟΣ, "Fifth Discourse" (the Latin column: DIALOGUS QUINTUS) — which our Greek source omits.]

[0216] GR. For the rest, if you have any other argument for the doctrine, O Latin, declare it; since for us the evangelical saying suffices, which plainly presents the Spirit as proceeding from the Father.…

LAT. Hold back, O Greek, and let us both still reserve the argument from the testimonies, and this as the last one; but for now, tell me: if the generation and the procession are simultaneous, how is that which proceeds not from that which is begotten? If the Son and the Spirit are simultaneously from the Father, how is the Spirit not also from the Son?

GR. I too will tell you: if Father and Son are simultaneous, how is the Son from the Father and not the Father also from the Son? If Spirit and Son are simultaneously from the Father, how is the Spirit from the Son and not the Son also from the Spirit, and that which is begotten from that which proceeds?…

LAT. I say, then, that "to proceed," in whatever cases it is found to be said, signifies a motion of some kind, yet one proper and corresponding to the matter under consideration.…

LAT. I perceive that I am already being pressed to come to the scriptural testimonies; but here, having brought this argument too to its completion and making another beginning from this point, I shall in that way make mention of the testimonies and of the establishing that comes from the teachings of the Fathers. For toward the demonstration of things above nature the argument framed by art is weak, and so is the one that perhaps concludes directly, and the one that leads back to absurdity, and simply every syllogism; and the one that proceeds through certain images and examples is dim and ineffectual — for the matter is above demonstration; since if demonstrations are of the things that are, that which is above the things that are is assuredly above demonstration also.

[ed: A centered heading across both columns stands here on the plate (leaf 105) — ΛΟΓΟΣ ΕΚΤΟΣ, "Sixth Discourse" (the Latin column: DIALOGUS SEXTUS) — which our Greek source omits.]

GR. These things I too say. But what, O Latin, may the testimonies be, both the scriptural and the patristic — it is now time to say — through which it may become [0217] plain that to say that the Spirit proceeds from the Son also is not contrary to the teachings of the apostles and to the traditions of the Fathers and to the ecclesiastical and orthodox doctrines from of old. For it is marvelous to us, and beyond expectation.

[ed: The editor's Latin bridge that stands here on the plate (leaf 106) is lost in our Greek source: the Latin thereupon brings forward much testimony for his own opinion, and the Greek then continues as follows. Without it the two speeches of the Greek run together, as though he answered himself.]

GR. You yourselves, then, sounding forth the "and from the Son" in the creed, will be found contrary-minded to us and out of concord for ever, and you will never be able to come together with us into the same things, and you will always be reckoned as uttering things opposed to the wise John. For though he is one, yet he is also the disciple of Cyril and of Athanasius and of the other Fathers, and before these of Christ. And besides this, in sounding forth in the creed the very thing which the Saints did not say, you will be taken altogether not to follow them all, but to speak with another voice; for it is needful to keep the exposition of the faith uninnovated and untampered with, not only in respect of the doctrines and the notions, but in respect of the very words.

Consider, then, how many synods have taken place since the exposition of the creed of the faith, and how many doctrinal ambiguities have been stirred up; yet no synod after the second synod, which made clear the doctrine of the Holy Spirit, either took away or added even a single word, or altered any of the things defined; but rather the greater part of the ambiguities were resolved from the words of the holy creed itself, and the confession of these cast out every suspicion and ambiguity; and the fact that this and that was understood in this way and in that by these men and by those made no innovation in the sayings that are in the creed; but the genuine meaning of the ambiguous matter was handed down through other expositions and definitions, and the spurious was cast out. So it was at the third, so at the fourth, so at those that followed; but the holy creed of religion has been preserved as it was set forth from the beginning. For if it were granted, on account of the ambiguities that fall in the way, to make additions upon additions and subtractions, or exchanges of certain words, we should little by little be shifting the boundaries of the Fathers unawares. Since even in the time of those very holy Fathers who made the exposition, many ambiguities afterward arose concerning the *homoousion*, which is one single [0220] word of the creed (for many who were contrary-minded to the Fathers put forward the same word for the concealment of their own impiety, but had another meaning within themselves), yet they were not willing to add to the creed any determination declaratory of the reverent meaning or exclusory of the impious construction; but privately and by themselves they each contended concerning the word and its signification, and both composed and published an interpretation clear and reverent, plainly setting forth the orthodox meaning and refuting the heretical one, as the great Athanasius also did; but addition or subtraction or alteration of the word they in no way made.
