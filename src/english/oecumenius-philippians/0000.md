[1260] THE EPISTLE OF PAUL THE APOSTLE TO THE PHILIPPIANS.

Paul and Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons: grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

Here, as writing to men of equal honor, he does not set down his dignity — that is, his apostleship — but has used the name common to all. A servant, he says, of Jesus Christ. «To the saints in Christ Jesus.» For there were Jews also who called themselves saints; but, To those in Christ Jesus, he says. «With the bishops and deacons.» Why ever does he write here to the clergy, though this is not his custom? Because it was the clerics themselves who had sent Epaphroditus with the supplies. «With the bishops.» Not because there were many bishops in one city; rather, he calls the presbyters bishops. For at that time they still shared the names in common, and the bishops were called deacons and presbyters, and conversely the presbyters, bishops.

CHAPTER 1. Thanksgiving for the virtue of the Philippians, and a prayer for their perfecting.

I thank my God upon all my remembrance of you, always in every supplication of mine for you all, making the supplication with joy for your fellowship in the Gospel, from the first day until now.

Since they had advanced in virtue, he remembered them with thanksgiving toward God. «Upon all my remembrance of you.» Whenever I remember you, he says, I give thanks; and I remember always. «With joy.» For it is possible also to remember with grief, in the case of those who are not virtuous. «For out of affliction, he says elsewhere, and anguish of heart I wrote to you.» But to remember with joy was a sign of their virtue. «Making the supplication.» And making the supplication for your fellowship — that is, your almsgiving. For what, he says, do I thank God? For your fellowship. Something great and apostolic he attests of them. For, he says, from the time you believed until even now, you do everything so as to become fellows of the Gospel. As though always present with me, he says, by sending what [1261] met the need. «Fellowship.» What fellowship? That from the first day on which you believed, he says, until now. «For your fellowship in the Gospel.» — [ΦΩΤ.] As for the words From the first day: either, From the first day from which you believed until now I thank my God for you all, making the supplication with joy; or, For your fellowship in the Gospel, ministered from the first day until now. And what follows seems rather to agree with the former construal. For how were you giving thanks from the beginning, when you had not yet seen them confirmed in the fellowship? Trusting, he says, that he who had implanted a beginning so eager and brimming with warmth would himself also carry it forward to the perfection of the work.

And trusting in this very thing, that he who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ; even as it is right for me to think this on behalf of you all, because I have you in my heart, both in my bonds and in the defense and confirmation of the Gospel, you all being fellow-partakers with me of the grace.

And I thank God for your fellowship, trusting this: that he who has begun to do a good work in you will both complete it and bring it to fullness until the consummation. And see how he has referred everything to God, that they may not think highly of themselves; yet even so it is great praise that they were able at all to draw God to work in them. For this would not have come about without the greatest virtue. «Until the day of Jesus Christ.» For not about you alone, he says, am I confident, but also about those who shall come from you. «Even as it is right.» Trusting this very thing, even as it is right — that is, since it is right for me, who know exactly the things that concern us [lat: the Latin gives *quae apud vos sunt*, the things that are with you], to think this on behalf of you all. What? That he who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ. «To think this on behalf of you all.» For he thinks — and reckons — that his disciples are accomplishing things good and great, who has laid out great zeal and care upon them. Or: it is right for me to reckon this, who have such a disposition toward you that not even in my bonds do I forget you, but carry you always in my heart. And what is the cause of his holding them in his heart and beyond forgetting? Because they are, he says, fellow-partakers with me of the grace — either of the spiritual gift supplied to them through faith, which was then working in them in manifold ways; or simply of grace, that of the rebirth and adoption through baptism. Or: I said that I have you in my heart, as being fellow-partakers with me both in my bonds and in the defense and confirmation. And of the grace stands for: through the grace. For without divine grace no one is counted worthy of such fellowship. «Because I have you.» He stated the cause according to which it was right for him to think this — that God who has begun will also bring it to fullness: because I have you, he says, in my heart. «You in my heart.» O what praise, [1264] to be in the heart of Paul! For this was no light thing. And the words, «And in my bonds, and in the defense,» are greater still. «And confirmation of the Gospel.» By confirmation of the Gospel he means the trials and the afflictions and the bonds. How? Because he chose them. And indeed, though it was open to him not to endure them, he confirmed the resurrection and the recompense. For I would not, he says, have borne to endure these things, were the things preached by me not true. — [ΧΡΥΣ.] For it is not only the dead being raised, nor lepers being cleansed, but we too, being bound, who confirm the Gospel. How, and in what manner? Because, suffering countless things and not giving way, but becoming the more eager, we furnish sufficient proof that we are heralds of truth, and that a certain divine power is in us, both easing all these things and not permitting the multitude of the trials to prevail over those who preach. For it is not within human power to master so many hindrances. And these things are a confirmation of the Gospel not for the others only, but for our very selves; for it renders us more approved, and stronger to despise the plots against us. «Fellow-partakers with me of the grace.» And why do I attest these things to you, and why do I have you in my heart? Because, he says, you are fellow-partakers with me of the grace — meaning either of the apostleship, or of the afflictions. «For my power, he says, is perfected in weakness.» For they were afflicted along with him, as with a teacher and a spiritual father. And see how he did not say, partakers, but «fellow-partakers», showing that he himself also is a partaker. For he himself says elsewhere, «That I may become a partaker of the Gospel» — that is, that I may share in the good things promised to those who proclaim the Gospel.

For God is my witness, how I long after you all in the bowels of Jesus Christ. And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more in knowledge and all perception, that you may prove the things that excel, that you may be sincere and without offense unto the day of Christ, filled with the fruit of righteousness which is through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.
