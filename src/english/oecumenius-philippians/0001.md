⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ «For God is my witness.» ⟦s2⟧ Not as though he were disbelieved does he call God to witness, but because he both speaks the utter truth and loves them all exceedingly. ⟦s3⟧ For they were all virtuous. ⟦s4⟧ «In the bowels.» ⟦s5⟧ The intensity of his love he signified through the bowels. ⟦s5⟧ «Of Jesus Christ.» ⟦s6⟧ That is, in the love that is for Christ's sake, because you are believers. ⟦s7⟧ And he says this so that they may not suppose that they are loved by him because they sent him what he needed for his want. ⟦s8⟧ Or: that I long for you with the bowels given me by Christ. ⟦s9⟧ «In the bowels of Jesus Christ.» ⟦s9⟧ Such as are not human, nor feel anything human, but are wholly disposed according to Christ Jesus. ⟦s10+11⟧ Great indeed it is to be longed for by Paul; [1265] but "in the bowels" is greater; and "in the bowels of Jesus Christ" leaves no room for any surpassing. ⟦s12⟧ «That your love.» ⟦s13⟧ Being so loved by them, he prays to be loved yet more and more. ⟦s14+15⟧ For there is no limit to the love that is according to Christ — or he speaks also concerning the others, that they may love them too. ⟦s16⟧ «In knowledge and all perception.» ⟦s17⟧ That is: that you may not love all men indiscriminately, but with testing and knowledge; lest somehow, he says, friendship toward heretics harm you. ⟦s18⟧ For if you love in this way, he says, you are able to test what is profitable — whom you ought to love, namely the faithful, and whom not, namely the unbelievers and heretics. ⟦s19⟧ How then does he say elsewhere, "If it be possible, so far as it lies with you, live at peace with all men" [lat: the Latin column omits Εἰ δυνατόν, giving the command unconditionally — *Quantum ex vobis est, cum omnibus hominibus in pace vivite*]? ⟦s20⟧ And we say that to live at peace is one thing, and to love another. ⟦s21⟧ And by "the things that excel" he means the things that are profitable. ⟦s22⟧ «That you may be sincere.» ⟦s23⟧ That no spurious doctrine, he says, may flow into you under the guise of love. ⟦s24⟧ Sincere, that is, as toward God; without offense, as toward men. ⟦s25+26⟧ For it offends many to see a believer having friendship with an unbeliever or a heretic; and to be an offense to anyone falls under punishment. ⟦s27⟧ «Filled with the fruit of righteousness.» ⟦s28⟧ For besides rightness of doctrines, care must be taken for one's life as well. ⟦s29⟧ And he did not say simply, That you be righteous, but, That you be filled in this. ⟦s30⟧ And by the fruit he means the one that is through Jesus Christ. ⟦s31⟧ For there is a righteousness in appearance, but not according to Christ — such as that of the Greeks, who for vainglory and to please men make a show of caring for what is just. ⟦s32⟧ And if you are such, the matter will be to the glory of God, not to my glory. ⟦s33⟧ For it is not that I the teacher may be glorified, he says, that I exhort these things and pray. ⟦s34⟧ For what is being done, he says, advances to the glory and praise of God, who also works together toward this.

⟦s35⟧ CHAPTER 2. ⟦s36⟧ Narrative of his own contending manner of life, and of his eagerness.

⟦s37+38+39+40⟧ But I would have you know, brothers, that the things concerning me have fallen out rather to the advancement of the Gospel, so that my bonds have become manifest in Christ in the whole Praetorium and to all the rest, and that most of the brothers in the Lord, being confident by my bonds, are much more bold to speak the word without fear. ⟦s37+38+39+40⟧ Some indeed proclaim Christ even out of envy and strife, but some also out of good will. ⟦s37+38+39+40⟧ The one sort announce Christ out of contentiousness, not purely, thinking to bring affliction upon my bonds; but the others out of love, knowing that I am set for the defense of the Gospel. ⟦s37+38+39+40⟧ What then? ⟦s37+38+39+40⟧ Only that in every way, whether in pretext or in truth, Christ is proclaimed; and in this I rejoice, yes and I shall rejoice.

⟦s37+38+39+40⟧ It was likely that they, on learning that he was bound, were troubled, as though the preaching were hindered thereby. ⟦s41⟧ This, therefore, he says, showing that the bonds have not only been no hindrance to the Gospel, but even an advancement. ⟦s42⟧ And he states the cause. ⟦s43+44+45⟧ «That the things concerning me.» ⟦s43+44+45⟧ It was the part of one who loves, to make plain the things concerning himself. ⟦s43+44+45⟧ «So that my bonds.» [1268] For neither were my bonds, he says, which are for Christ's sake, hidden, but they have become manifest both in the Praetorium — that is, the palace — and among the rest. ⟦s46⟧ And what follows from this? ⟦s47⟧ That from it, he says, courage came to the faithful, and a way of conversion to many of the unbelievers. ⟦s48+49⟧ «Have become in Christ.» ⟦s48+49⟧ Either the bonds that are in Christ — that is, those for Christ's sake, so that the construction is by hyperbaton; or he means the bonds that have been made manifest in Christ, that is, made manifest by the good pleasure and dispensation of Christ. ⟦s50⟧ For there was nothing that Paul suffered or accomplished aright which he did not refer to Christ. ⟦s51⟧ Or: the bonds of those men are made manifest in Christ who do nothing for display, but act in such a way that those who see are benefited and glorify God who is in heaven. ⟦s52⟧ «And that most of the brothers in the Lord.» ⟦s53⟧ Since he had said that the bonds furnished courage, and the saying seemed a great one, he adds, «In the Lord,» that is, For the Lord's sake. ⟦s54⟧ «Being confident by my bonds.» ⟦s55⟧ For they took courage, being confirmed toward the faith through Paul's bonds. ⟦s56⟧ For if the preaching were not divine, he says, Paul would not have borne to be bound for it. ⟦s57⟧ This he said above also: In the confirmation, he says, of the Gospel. ⟦s58⟧ For the bonds of holy Paul are a confirmation of the preaching. ⟦s59⟧ «Much more bold.» ⟦s59⟧ For they had spoken before as well, but now more. ⟦s60⟧ «Some indeed even out of envy.» ⟦s61+62⟧ Since Paul had been arrested, many of the unbelievers, wishing to provoke Nero to anger so that he should by all means kill him, proclaimed Christ, that Nero, hearing the preaching sown abroad, might put to death Paul the author of it. ⟦s63⟧ Or: that they themselves, wishing to be honored (for great was the honor of Paul), even being unbelievers, proclaimed Christ. ⟦s64⟧ «But some also out of good will.» ⟦s65⟧ For faith's sake, for love's sake, out of a good mind and a good purpose. ⟦s66⟧ «The one sort out of contentiousness,» out of rivalry and envy and strife. ⟦s67⟧ «Not purely.» ⟦s68⟧ That is, not from a pure heart, supposing by this to bring him into greater danger and affliction, according to the things written above. ⟦s69⟧ «Thinking to bring affliction.» ⟦s70+71⟧ For it did not turn out as they supposed; for I rejoiced the more at these things. ⟦s72⟧ «But the others out of love.» ⟦s73⟧ But the others, he says, out of the love toward the Lord and toward me. ⟦s74⟧ «Knowing, he says, that I am set for the defense of the Gospel.» ⟦s74⟧ They know, he says, that I have been charged by God to preach the Gospel, and that I shall render account for this; and it lightens for me, he says, the account concerning it, that many are catechized and that the preaching has run its course. ⟦s75⟧ Knowing this, therefore, he says, those men, in order to lighten for me the account to be rendered to God, catechize many, preaching the word. ⟦s76⟧ What then? ⟦s77⟧ Either, For what else, he says, but this? — as though to say, What else, but that I am set for the defense of the Gospel? ⟦s78⟧ Or, Why, he says, do I speak at length? ⟦s78⟧ Or, What concern is it of mine, he says, with what aim a man preaches, so long as he preaches rightly? ⟦s79⟧ «Whether in pretext,» because of envy toward me, «or in truth Christ is proclaimed.» ⟦s80⟧ From these words certain foolish men have supposed that the Apostle [1269] gave entry to all the heresies, by saying, «Whether in pretext or in truth Christ is proclaimed.» ⟦s81⟧ But do you attend. ⟦s82⟧ First, he did not say, Let him be proclaimed, that this might be taken for laying down a law, but, Is proclaimed, narrating what was happening. ⟦s83⟧ Then, even if he had spoken as one laying down a law — for many of the heretics also falsified the Scripture, as though it read, Let Christ be proclaimed — not even so has he given entry to the heresies. ⟦s84⟧ How?
