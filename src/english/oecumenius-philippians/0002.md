⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Because these men, of whom he speaks, were not preaching spurious doctrines; rather they preached the doctrine rightly, but with an evil mind, as wishing either to be honored or to cast the Apostle into greater danger. ⟦s2⟧ But the heretics, besides having an evil mind as well (for the greater number have this too), also introduce spurious doctrines: some not confessing that he who was crucified is God; others saying that there are two hypostases after the union; others that he did not take flesh consubstantial with us; others that the Son is less than the Father, in that he is and is understood to be God; and others telling monstrous tales of a confusion in the holy Trinity. ⟦s3⟧ «And in this I rejoice.» ⟦s4⟧ Those men, he says, do these things to grieve me, but I rejoice. ⟦s5⟧ Why do I say, I rejoice? ⟦s6⟧ I shall rejoice, he says, with the eternal joy.

⟦s7⟧ For I know that this shall turn out for me unto salvation through your supplication and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ, according to my earnest expectation and hope, that in nothing I shall be put to shame, but with all boldness, as always, so now also Christ shall be magnified in my mouth [var: Gk σώματί μου, "in my body"], whether through life or through death.

⟦s8⟧ «This.» ⟦s8⟧ Either his being bound, or Christ's being preached by them, or even his being plotted against by them. ⟦s9⟧ «Unto salvation.» ⟦s10⟧ And by salvation he means the martyrdom that is all but come [lat: the Latin construes the phrase the other way — *quod nondum pateretur martyrium*, that he was not yet suffering martyrdom]. ⟦s11⟧ «Through your supplication.» ⟦s12⟧ See his humility: having salvation owed to him from countless right achievements, he says, through your supplication. ⟦s13⟧ For if, he says, I am well supplied with your prayers, there will be for me an abundance and, as it were, an increase of spirit. ⟦s14⟧ «According to the expectation.» ⟦s15⟧ By earnest expectation he means the vehement and strained hope, which a man watches and scans about for, craning his very head. ⟦s16⟧ «And my hope.» ⟦s17⟧ From these words he teaches that one must not take courage in the prayers of others alone, unless one contribute something also from oneself. ⟦s18⟧ Accordingly, he says, I too contribute my hope, than which nothing is greater. ⟦s19⟧ «For hope, he says elsewhere, does not put to shame.» ⟦s20⟧ And, «Let thy mercy, O Lord, be upon us, according as we have hoped in thee.» — «That in nothing I shall be put to shame.» ⟦s21⟧ Because, he says, the plotters shall not prevail; for if they kill me, then most of all have I prevailed. ⟦s22⟧ «But with all boldness.» ⟦s23⟧ That is, plainly, openly, not in a veiled manner. — «And now Christ shall be magnified.» — [ΦΩΤ.] Always was Christ magnified in Paul: both when he gave him over to be worn down by bonds and by scourges, and when he drew him out and ransomed him. ⟦s24⟧ For through the former the preaching was confirmed and made strong, since those who saw [1272] Paul's bonds and afflictions reckoned that, if his preaching were not something divine and wholly true, he would not have given himself over rejoicing to countless evils, when it was open to him to stop and be rid of them. ⟦s25⟧ Therefore with reason was Christ magnified, the preaching being confirmed. ⟦s26⟧ And again, when he drew him out and snatched him from the dangers, he was magnified from that very thing. ⟦s27⟧ For if the grace that delivered Paul were not something divine and above man, how were the bonds loosed? ⟦s28⟧ How were the doors opened? ⟦s29⟧ How had those countless wonders come to pass? ⟦s30⟧ Christ, then, bore witness to Paul by the things in which he delivered him from the dangers; and Paul in turn, by the things in which he endured in the trials, bore witness to the preaching; and through both Christ was magnified. ⟦s31⟧ So then he says now also: Whether Christ delivers me from death, or does not, he shall be magnified in me. ⟦s32⟧ For I, both while living, shall not stand off from the boldness that is for the preaching; and if I must die, with this same boldness shall I die. ⟦s33⟧ «In my body.» ⟦s34⟧ Those men, by proclaiming Christ out of contentiousness, thought to destroy him. ⟦s35⟧ But it shall not be so, he says; rather Christ shall be magnified in my body, as one who always delivers me, as is his custom, and ransoms me. ⟦s36⟧ Then, since having escaped the first defense he was afterwards put to death, Christ, he says, is magnified. ⟦s37⟧ «Whether through life.» ⟦s38⟧ Because he snatched me out of the mouth of Nero. ⟦s39⟧ «Whether through death.» ⟦s39⟧ Because he put sinew in me to despise death for his sake. ⟦s40⟧ And this he says, not as one about to die at once, but making them secure, that they be not troubled when this comes to pass.

⟦s41⟧ For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. ⟦s42⟧ But if to live in the flesh, this is for me the fruit of labor; and what I shall choose I do not make known. ⟦s43+44⟧ I am constrained by the two, having the desire to depart and to be with Christ, which is much rather better; but to remain in the flesh is more needful for your sake. ⟦s45⟧ And trusting this I know that I shall abide and continue with you all, for your advancement and joy of faith, that your boasting may abound in Christ Jesus in me, through my coming to you again.

⟦s46⟧ Elsewhere he says, But the life that I now live in the flesh, I live by the faith of the Son of God who loved me. ⟦s47⟧ This also he says now: Him I have as my life. ⟦s48⟧ For neither did he who despised the world live the worldly life, but the life of Christ, the life that Christ willed. ⟦s49⟧ And he calls dying gain, because of being with Christ and obtaining the crowns. ⟦s50⟧ «But if to live in the flesh.» ⟦s51⟧ He had said that to die is gain. ⟦s52⟧ But that no one may suppose that he is disparaging life, see what he says: «But if to live.» ⟦s53+54⟧ Since, he says, to live in the flesh is the fruit of labor — for one can use this life for the fruit of good works: For, he says, bearing fruit and increasing — since, then, living also contributes to the bearing of fruit, I do not know, nor do I make known, what I shall choose and prefer: to live, he says, and bear [1273] fruit to God, or to die and be with Christ. ⟦s55⟧ And observe that he showed himself lord of living and of not living. ⟦s56⟧ «I am constrained by the two.» ⟦s57+58⟧ Since, then, what I shall choose I do not make known, with reason am I constrained by the two — both by departing and by continuing in life. ⟦s59⟧ But, O Paul, you who so love Christ, do you doubt at all and waver as to what you ought to choose? ⟦s60⟧ And yet it ought to have been beyond doubt to you which is good: to depart and be with Christ. ⟦s61⟧ I too know this, he says; indeed, having the desire to depart and to be with Christ, and knowing that this is much rather better for me, I said that I am constrained by the two. ⟦s62⟧ And why this? ⟦s62⟧ For your salvation, he says, and for your sakes, which I judge to be more needful, since I look not to my own advantage but to what is profitable for you. ⟦s63⟧ Do you see a soul preferring the benefit of the disciples to its own advantage? ⟦s64⟧ For by remaining he both confirmed those who had been catechized before, and took others to himself. ⟦s65⟧ «Much rather better.» ⟦s66⟧ The word "having" is taken in common — as though to say: Having the desire to depart, and having what is much rather better, to be with Christ. ⟦s66⟧ «More needful for your sake.» ⟦s67⟧ And it is more needful in another respect also: that to bring more men to God through faith makes him more Christ's own. ⟦s68+69⟧ «And being confident of this I know,» namely that continuance in the flesh is more needful. — «And trusting this I know.» — [ΦΩΤ.] It stands for: And this, having taken confidence and not doubting, I know. ⟦s70⟧ And what is it that, being confident and not doubting, he knows? ⟦s71⟧ That it is more needful, for their sake, to remain in the flesh.
