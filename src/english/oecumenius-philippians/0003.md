Then, I know that I shall abide and continue with you. For the word "I know" is taken in common. Or the whole is taken in common — as though to say: Being confident, I know not only that it is more needful for me to remain in the flesh, but that I shall both remain and continue with you. For he who was initiate of the unspeakable and supercelestial mysteries did not know even his own life in doubt, and that he should yet live on now. And one may also arrange the saying otherwise and so take it more readily: To remain, he says, is more needful for your sake. Then: And being confident in this, that it is more needful, I know, he says, that I shall abide and continue with you, and what follows. «And I shall continue with you all.» I know, he says, that I abide and shall continue with you all. For since I am confident that to remain is more needful, I know that I shall remain, not only in bonds, but also together with you, and that I shall see you. And I shall remain, he says, for your advancement and joy of faith. For to advance in faith is possible: namely, to be the more confirmed in it and to rejoice with the spiritual joy. And this comes from one who is gently putting them in fear as well: for if I remain for your sake, see that you do not put my perseverance to shame. «That your boasting.» And how should their boasting abound? By their being made firm in the faith, and by their living rightly, plainly. «In Christ Jesus in me.» Since he had said that his remaining was for their advancement, he now wishes to show that their advancement also tends to his own benefit — that he may not seem to burden them, as one who for their sakes overlooks his own advantage — because, he says, Your boasting abounds in me also. For if you are approved, the boast runs back to me [1276] your teacher as well. «Through my coming.» For Paul's coming was a benefit to them all. And he showed that he would depart.

CHAPTER 3. Exhortation to the manner of life that is according to God, and to the life inspired by God.

Only, conduct yourselves worthily of the Gospel of Christ, that whether I come and see you, or be absent, I may hear the things concerning you, that you stand in one spirit, with one soul, striving together for the faith of the Gospel, and not being terrified in anything by the adversaries — which is to them indeed a token of perdition, but to you of salvation. And this is from God; because to you it has been granted, on Christ's behalf, not only to believe in him, but also to suffer for him.

«Only worthily.» I for my part, then, shall continue with you for your advancement; only do this, he says: conduct yourselves worthily of the Gospel — that is, increase toward virtue. «And see you.» That I may be gladdened, he says. «Or be absent.» He did not say this as having changed his mind about departing, but he said what would come about. For if, he says, you conduct yourselves worthily of the Gospel, then coming and seeing you I shall be gladdened (for this must be supplied), and before I come, being absent and hearing of you, I shall be gladdened. «In one spirit.» In one gift of love and concord. «With one soul.» So bound together by love as to seem to have one soul, taking hold with one another in the contests and struggles for the Gospel. — «And not being terrified.» — [ΦΩΤ.] I do not say, he says, not falling down, but not being terrified — that is, not being thrown into confusion in anything, though the adversaries threaten death, though they cast my bonds in your teeth. «Which is to them indeed.» If you are not terrified, he says, by the adversaries, their rising up against you will be turned for them into a token of perdition. That is, either that through your steadfastness the things of perdition will be shown and made manifest to them, and they will stand off from their vain glory and their pursuits, and from this there will be for you the greater wealth of salvation, as having procured for others also to flee perdition and to gain salvation. Either, then, it is to be understood thus, or more simply: that their attempt and their war against you, when you are not terrified, will show and procure for them nothing else but their own perdition, while for you, in place of harm, it shall be reckoned both a ground of salvation and an occasion of more perfect virtue. «A token of perdition.» Which attempt of theirs will be to them a proof of perdition, if you stand unshaken. «How?» For if, having set in motion every manner of plot, they — I do not say fail to cast you down, but — fail even to throw you into confusion, they will know that our affairs are true and divine, and procure salvation, but that theirs are false [1277] and a cause of perdition. And he speaks of certain unbelievers who were opposed to the preaching. «Because to you it has been granted.» See how he knows no difference between the will of the Father and that of the Son. For having said that this too is from God, he adds, Because to you it has been granted by Christ — setting down the matter as a common gift of Father and Son. «The thing on Christ's behalf.» Making it a greater and more considerable thing to be harassed by those opposed to the preaching, he says that this has been given them by God: to believe in Christ, and to be able to contend in the trials.

Having the same contest which you saw in me, and now hear of in me. If then there be any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any bowels and compassions, fulfil my joy, that you may mind the same thing, having the same love, joined in soul, minding the one thing; nothing according to contentiousness, or according to vainglory, but in lowliness of mind counting one another to excel yourselves; let each of you look not on your own things, but each on the things of others also.

Ah, what a thing it is to have Paul's contest! For Paul became to them a pattern, having contended at Philippi, of how one must strive for the faith. «If then there be any consolation.» If, he says, any account of me is made by you, and if you wish to give me any consolation in the trials, and any comfort, being moved out of love, fulfil my joy. «If any fellowship of the Spirit.» If you have, he says, fellowship with me in the Holy Spirit, and if there is in you anything of bowels and compassions, fulfil my joy, he says, by loving one another — as though he said: If you owe me any recompense for my labors toward you, in this render it to me, in loving one another. «That you may mind the same thing.» And what is it that you wish to have furnished you by us in place of all things? That you may mind the same thing, he says, and that no one raise faction against another; or he speaks also of doctrine, seeking in place of all things a like judgment as to the faith. «Having the same love.» Not much toward one and little toward another, but all of you the equal and the same. «Becoming of like soul.» For this is what "joined in soul" means. Again he doubles the like-mindedness. «Nothing according to contentiousness.» Next he teaches also how it is possible to be of like soul. Doing nothing, he says, according to contentiousness — that is, out of jealousy or rivalry — or according to vainglory. «But in lowliness of mind.» Ah, what a thing he says! Through lowliness of mind, he says, let each count the other to excel him, and to be greater than himself in all things. For it is a great thing that even the one party should count that So-and-so takes precedence of me. For if he shall count this, he will both honor him and stand in awe of him and love him, and will not even envy him; for what excels is stronger than envy, since envy is wont to arise toward a rival.
