For of the Godhead he has set down the word Being, showing the coeternity; but of the manhood, the words Taking and Being made, showing this to be a thing acquired. «Being found in fashion as a man.» Such as a man. And see the note below. «He humbled himself.» If, then, he was a mere man, as it seems to the heretics, how was he humbled by becoming what he was? And again the word Himself shows us the voluntariness of it. «Becoming obedient.» As a Son, not as a servant. For by this most of all he showed that he is a true Son to him whom he obeyed. For this reason he said, He humbled himself, that you might think nothing servile. And for this reason he said obedient, that he might show the honor toward the Father as toward the cause. «Unto death.» Many are the excesses of the humbling: to become man, to die, and that by the cross.

Wherefore God also highly exalted him, and granted him a name, the name that is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven and things on earth and things under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Whenever the Apostle takes hold of the incarnation, he thereafter utters all the lowly things without fear, since the flesh admits the lowly things. «And granted him a name.» Take these words of the flesh, in no way dividing. And what is the name that was granted? Christ, Son, God. For by these names he is called even with the flesh. — [ΒΑΣΙΛ.] If it was after the incarnation, because of the obedience, that the Father granted the name above every name to the Son who was God, and that every tongue confessed him Lord — while before the incarnation he had neither the name that is above every name, nor the confession by all men that he is Lord — then he became greater after the incarnation than he was before the [1285] incarnation. Which is absurd. And of this sort is also the saying, «All authority in heaven and on earth has been given me.» These things, therefore, must be understood of the incarnation, and not of the Godhead. «Every knee should bow.» For adoration, plainly. «Of things in heaven and things on earth.» Of angels and of men and of demons. For the demons too recognized him. (Wherefore they say, «We know thee, who thou art,» that he is the Holy One of God.) And they shall recognize him, when he sits as judge. Or that both the righteous and the sinners shall bow. «And every tongue confess.» What shall it confess? That Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. For it is the glory of the Father to have such a Son, Master of all and Maker and God, and one to whom every knee bows and every tongue shall make confession.

Wherefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for God is the one who works in you both the willing and the working, for his good pleasure.

For exhortations that come with praises everyone receives readily. «As you have always obeyed,» as though he said: Imitate yourselves now also, and what you were doing, do again. «Not as in my presence only.» For then perhaps you seemed to be doing everything out of respect for me; but now, if you heighten your virtue, you will be seen to be doing what is required for God's sake. «With fear and trembling.» For it is not possible to do any good thing without reverence and without fearing and trembling. And how does one tremble? If we consider that God is present everywhere, and sees and hears all things, and that the accounts are hard. «Serve the Lord, he says, in fear, and rejoice unto him with trembling.» With fear and trembling. — [ΦΩΤ.] Well said. For each man must fear and tremble in working out his own salvation, lest at some time he be tripped up and fall away from it. For many are the trials, and many those who plot; seeing that for some men the very practice of virtue has turned out to the contrary, becoming an occasion of madness and of pride. It is needful, therefore, on account of these things, that men work out their own salvation in fear and trembling. For unless God, seeing the contest and the zeal that goes with fear, takes hold along with them, the willing itself will not come out to a good end, nor could the working be judged a good deed. For Cain willed to bring a sacrifice to God — willing a thing that was then most of all praiseworthy — and he did the work; for he sacrificed. But neither of the two was accepted by God. For it was not with fear and trembling. Therefore neither did the Divine take hold along with him, nor did these things come out for him to a good end. And with reason does he say that God works. For indeed, taking hold along with us, it is he who brings to completion, and who as it were gives form to our own willing and working. [1288] And the words, For God is the one who works, and what follows, may also have been said as causes of doing all things without murmurings and disputings. For why will you murmur, he says, as one who is toiling? God is the one who works all things; and why do you dispute about the laboriousness of virtue, having God as fellow-worker? «For God is the one who works.» Since he had said, in fear and trembling, he says: But do not be terrified on this account. For God is the one who works in you both the willing and the working — that is, the doing of the good. So that you need only bring a reverent judgment, and the willing of good things with fear and trembling. For when this is so, God thereafter increases the willing itself, and will work the accomplishing of the good things. Or that out of his great thankfulness Paul says that even the willing itself is worked by God — as when he often calls his own right achievements gifts of grace, not doing away with free will. Away with the thought! Rather, being exceedingly thankful and referring all things to God, that even for the things which we accomplish we may have thanks toward God. «In you.» In you who work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for with such as these he works together. «For his good pleasure.» That his good pleasure and his counsel may be fulfilled in you.

Do all things without murmurings and disputings, that you may become blameless and pure, children of God without blemish, in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as luminaries in the world, holding forth the word of life, for a boast to me in the day of Christ, that I did not run in vain, nor labor in vain.

«Without murmurings.» For he who murmurs is unthankful to God; and he who is unthankful blasphemes. And by disputings, he says, he means doubts. For when God's commandment is set before us, we must not dispute whether it is to be done or not done, but do all things. «That you may become blameless.» That you may become, he says, sincere and without offense and beyond reproach. «In the midst of a generation.» He shows that there were many who warred against them and led them toward murmuring. But this, he says, is a work of virtue: that the man who is goaded should neither murmur nor fall into doubt or wavering. «Among whom you shine.» Among whom, being crooked and perverse, you shine as luminaries. For just as the luminaries shine in darkness, so you also shine in the midst of the darkness of their sins. «Holding forth the word of life.» That is, having the seeds of life and provisions for the way — as though he said: Being known for this, that you are of those who shall live the immortal life.
