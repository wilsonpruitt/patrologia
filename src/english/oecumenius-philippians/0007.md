But why at all — for this must be asked as among Christians, if to depart and be with Christ is good — does he call the life here a mercy? And we say that for the same cause for which he said above, But to remain in the flesh is more needful for your sakes, for this cause he says this now also. «But on me also.» See how he makes him worthy of reverence, in saying that by that man's health mercy was shown to himself. «That I might not have grief upon grief.» Besides the grief from his sickness, that from his death also; that is, that I might not have the grief of his sickness upon the grief of [ed: our Greek source breaks off in mid-clause here; the plate's continuation is recorded in the crux].

Therefore I sent him the more diligently, that seeing him again you may rejoice, and that I may be less sorrowful. Receive him therefore in the Lord with all joy, and hold such men in honor; because for the work of Christ he came near unto death, hazarding his life, that he might fill what was lacking on your part in the ministration toward me.

«Again you may rejoice.» Not from the report only that he was made well, but from the sight of him as well. «And that I may be less sorrowful.» For if you rejoice, I too rejoice; yet he did not say, I rejoice, nor even, I shall be without sorrow, but less sorrowful — showing that he was never at any time free of the sorrow he bore for his disciples, and of that for the war over the faith. For in hope he always rejoiced. «Receive him therefore.» Receive him, then, either for the Lord's sake, or because God so wills. «And hold such men in honor» — not for their own sakes, but for yours who receive them, that your reward may abound. «Because for the work of Christ.» When this man had gone away to Rome to convey to Paul the things for his need, it is likely that he found him in great danger, so that it was not permitted to anyone even to come near him, and there was danger also for those who met with him. Epaphroditus, then, despising such danger, both met with him and gave the things for his need, and [1296] ministered to him. And such a thing he calls a work of the Lord. For he who gives me rest, he says, does this not for my sake, but for the Lord's. — «For the work of Christ.» [ed: an attribution siglum stands here; its letters are lost in our source] Rightly does he call it a work of Christ. For if he who receives the disciples receives Christ himself — «For he that receiveth you, he says, receiveth me» — then he also who ministers to the disciples in anything ministers to Christ. And in another way too it is a work of Christ, because it is Christ's commandment to lay down one's own life for one's friends. And if for friends, how much more for teachers! «Hazarding his life.» For what if God, he says, saved him beyond expectation? Still, he himself took the hazard, and threw himself into danger. «That he might fill up your lack.» You sent me, he says, the things for my need, and remembered me in my absence, and showed your love. There was lacking, then, to the whole work, that I should receive what had been sent; and this alone was wanting. This man therefore filled the lack that remained over, toward the ministration to me, hazarding his life. And he shows that for this there will be a reward for those who sent as well, seeing that what was wanting for the completing of the work that is according to God, this Epaphroditus supplied besides. And in saying, Your lack, and, of the ministration, he showed that it is a debt owed by those who are in safety to send to those who are in dangers. And this he says to check them, and to show that they have paid a debt.

CHAPTER 5. Concerning the spiritual life, the life not lived as in the flesh, which is an imitation of the death of Christ.

For the rest, my brethren, rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you is to me indeed not irksome, but for you it is safe. See the dogs, see the evil workers, see the concision. For we are the circumcision, who serve God in spirit, and boast in Christ Jesus, and have not confidence in the flesh, though I myself have confidence in the flesh.

For if I, he says, shall not die now, and the Gospel comes to increase, and the enemies shall be put to shame, you ought to rejoice in the Lord. For what has befallen tends to the glory of the Lord. [ed: an attribution siglum stands here; its letters are lost in our source] And further, if the apostle has revived you and has been sent back to you, and I have been rid of the grief I bore over him, and your lack has been filled up, you ought to rejoice with a joy that is spiritual and worthy of the Lord. «See.» They were hard to discern. Therefore he says, See — that is, watch yourselves and be on your guard. «The dogs.» There were certain men from among the Jews, supposed believers, who mingled Judaism also with the faith and corrupted the preaching; these he calls dogs. And the Gentiles were once called dogs, because they did not know God; for dogs bark even at him who gives to them. But now these men are dogs (for they who [1297] deny the Son do not know the Father either) — either for their shamelessness, or as returning to their own vomit, or for their biting in secret. «The evil workers.» For they do work, but unto evil; for they tear up what has been well planted. «The concision.» He means those who are circumcised. But since circumcision was no longer performed lawfully among the Jews [lat: the Latin gives *nondum facta erat apud Judaeos vera et legitima circumcisio*, there was not yet among the Jews a true and lawful circumcision] (for the lawful circumcision now is to be circumcised in the soul by the spirit), he called it a concision, showing that circumcision is nothing else than a cutting-up of the body. For when the truth has come, the types must needs fall idle. Or: because those who preach circumcision attempt to cut in pieces the body of the Church. «Who serve God in spirit.» Those who serve God spiritually, and are spiritually circumcised. For otherwise no one is able so much as to serve him. «And boasting in Christ Jesus.» See how he showed the difference. For which is better, to have confidence in Christ, or in the flesh? Clearly in Christ. Therefore ours is the true circumcision — ours who boast in Christ, and not theirs who boast in the flesh. «Though I myself.» If I wished, he says, to boast as those men do, I too have rather more confidence in the flesh. That is, I too could, like them, boast in the circumcision of my flesh, and more than they. «Having confidence.» If the Apostle had been of the Gentiles, someone might have said this — that he was cheapening the circumcision of the Jews as one deprived of their nobility. But as it is he says: Though having confidence in the flesh also, I say these things — not because this was a boasting to him (for this is what confidence means) — but for their sakes he spoke thus. «And in the flesh.» I have it, he says, in Christ, but I have also the confidence that seems to be in the flesh. Therefore I do not blame them as one deprived of the [ed: our Greek source breaks off in mid-phrase here; the plate's continuation is recorded in the crux].

If any other man thinks he has confidence in the flesh, I more: circumcised the eighth day, of the race of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; as touching the law, a Pharisee; as touching zeal, persecuting the Church; as touching the righteousness which is in the law, having become blameless.

Well said is the word Thinks: either as though those men had not so great a confidence as he himself has, or as though the confidence that is in the flesh were not properly a confidence at all. And he said, Another, showing that he would never have come to these words, had not others, who had confidence in the flesh, brought him to it.
