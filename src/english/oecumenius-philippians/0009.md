«For whose sake I suffered the loss of all things.» Not only having let the law go, so as not to keep it and the things in it after the flesh, but all the things in the world as well. For the word, I suffered loss, is as if to say, I let them go and was deprived of them. «Not having my own righteousness.» My own, he says — that which is, as it were, of my own works and labors according to the law. For such righteousness, he says, is not properly righteousness at all — for what indeed shall a man do? — but that which is through grace and faith in Christ is properly righteousness. If then he who had a righteousness of works ran to the righteousness of faith that he might be saved, what shall he suffer who neither has one nor runs to it?

The righteousness which is of God upon faith, of knowing him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, being conformed to his death, if by any means I may attain unto the resurrection of the dead. Not that I have already received, or am already perfected [lat: the Latin gives *aut jam mortuus sim*, or have already died]. But I pursue, if I may also lay hold of that for which I was also laid hold of by Christ Jesus. Brethren, I do not yet reckon myself to have laid hold. But one thing: forgetting the things behind, and stretching forward to the things before, I pursue toward the mark, unto the prize of the calling above of God in Christ Jesus.

«The righteousness which is of God.» For that which is through faith in Christ is of God, not of the law. «Upon faith.» The righteousness which is of God, he says, rests and is founded upon the faith of knowing him. «For he that knows the Son knows the Father, and conversely.» — «And the power of his resurrection.» For if faith shall not make plain to us the power of the resurrection, what argument will present it? «And the fellowship of his sufferings.» For that one must have fellowship in the sufferings of Christ we have both learned through faith and perform. For he who does not believe that in suffering with him he shall also reign with him would never suffer with him at all. «Being conformed.» That is, being made like.» For just as he himself, he says, died for men, [1305] so I too die for the profit of men. And this is also to die for Christ. «If by any means I may attain.» If by any means, he says. Do you see him, after so much, still in doubt? «Unto the resurrection of the dead.» That glorious resurrection which leads to him, concerning which he says elsewhere, «And we shall ever be with the Lord.» «Not that I have already received.» Since he had said, Being conformed to the death of Christ — and the word was a great one — he was not content only to say, If by any means I may attain unto the resurrection, but showing us yet more plainly that even one who is conformed to the death of Christ does not yet do anything worthy of the cross of Christ, he adds: Not that I have already received, he says, the prize through being conformed to the death of Christ, nor that I am already thereby perfect. «But I pursue, if I may also lay hold.» But I pursue, he says, the prize, if indeed I may lay hold of it. And see how much force the words have, I pursue, and, if I may lay hold. For he who pursues, and is in doubt whether he shall lay hold, sees nothing else and reckons nothing else than to attain what he pursues. «That for which I was also laid hold of by Christ Jesus.» That is, in that matter for which I was taken hold of by Christ. For he laid hold of me already, he says, as I was fleeing, and made me believe. As to the things, then, for which I was laid hold of — as, to preach, to bear his name before nations and kings — whether I may lay hold so as to become such as I was chosen to be. Or understand the words, for which, as meaning since, so that the sense is: But I pursue, if I may lay hold, since I too was laid hold of — calling the thing a debt. «I myself.» At the same time, by the example drawn from himself, teaching them also to be modest in mind and not to be puffed up as having laid hold. «I do not yet reckon.» He says everything as of a runner. And what is it that you do not reckon to have laid hold of? The mark, he says, for which I was sent, the prize, the being worthy of the apostleship. And if Paul, so great a man, has not laid hold, what shall we say? How then does he say elsewhere, «I have fought the fight, and have finished the course»? Because he was then near his departure. «But one thing.» One thing I do, he says, and I become wholly of one thing only. The words, But one thing, must be thought to be said either by ellipse, as those before us took them — for in this figure too he is frequent, so that there is wanting: but one thing I care for, one thing I study and am anxious about. What is this? The things behind, he says, and so on. Either, then, it must be taken thus, or it rather falls under another figure, that of taking a word in common. For the divine Paul abounds in this too, so that the words, I reckon to have laid hold, are taken in common: that is, I reckon that I have laid hold of nothing, save one thing. And what is it that I reckon to have laid hold of? That, forgetting the things behind and stretching forward to the things before, I pursue toward the mark, and so on. For he must needs have laid hold of this: that he pursues toward the mark, and that his course is not in vain. Therefore he says elsewhere also: «So I run, not as uncertainly; so I fight, not as one beating the air.» And again: «So run, that you may lay hold.» — «Forgetting the things behind.» [1308] For this it was that made him stretch forward to the things before: the forgetting of the good deeds behind. And before, he says, is with regard to the prize laid up. For always accounting it to be before me and far distant from me, I run toward it. «And stretching forward to the things before.» The word, stretching forward, shows the swift course [lat: the Latin gives *continuum cursum*, the continuous course], as if he had said: I am still in the course, since the prize is before me and far distant. For that man stretches forward who, while his feet are still running, has already bent the rest of his body toward the mark, and all but outstrips his feet by the leaning of the rest of the body. And this comes of great eagerness and warmth. «I pursue toward the mark.» And what is the mark of his course? The kingdom of the heavens, which he also calls the prize. For interpreting himself he says: And what is the mark? The prize, he says, of the calling above. «In Christ Jesus.» He refers all to Christ: for in Christ, he says, I run. For without Christ and his inclining to help it is not possible to accomplish this course, even if we should wish.

As many of us then as are perfect, we are of this mind; and if in anything you are otherwise minded, this also God will reveal to you. Nevertheless, whereunto we have attained, to walk by the same rule, to be of the same mind.

«We are of this mind.» Of what? That one must forget the things behind and stretch forward to the things before. For this is what makes men perfect: to account that one falls short of the prize. «And if in anything otherwise.» If in anything otherwise, he says, you have succeeded — namely, that you do not fall short of the prize, but have already attained it — God will reveal to you that you are not rightly so minded. For he who is so minded becomes the more slothful. And he says, This also, as all the rest as well; for he refers his own teaching to God. «Nevertheless, whereunto we have attained.» For the present, he says, while God is revealing that other thing, let us stand in the measure and rule whereunto we have attained — that is, in not losing what has already been accomplished. And the word, to walk by rule, is as if to say, to be content, to obey, to stand fast.
