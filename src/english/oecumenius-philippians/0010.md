Or: he calls faith a rule. For just as with a rule, whether you take away or add, you have ruined the whole, so also with the faith. — [ΦΩΤ.] For the meantime, he says, until he reveal to you the truth, let us not lose what we have laid hold of. And how is it lost? If we grow negligent of our former zeal and are turned aside from the course. But one must walk by the same rule — that is, keep up the same contest, display the same manner, be of the same mind, both concerning the conduct of life and concerning the doctrines. For as he who has altered any one of the doctrines [1309] has dissolved all that went before as well, so too he who has shifted any point of the conduct of life and let it slacken and does not keep the same exactness has ruined what preceded also. Then, that he may not seem to be enjoining a thing hard to accomplish and difficult — to be trained through the whole of life in the virtues and in piety — he adds: Be imitators of me. What I require, he says, is a very easy thing, and nothing impossible; I too am a man of like passions: as you see me contending, so contend yourselves also.

To walk by the same rule, to be of the same mind. This passage is either exhortation or prayer. But if it is exhortation, the word "must" is wanting: You must walk by the same rule, be of the same mind. If you are bound to walk by the same rule, you are bound to be of the same mind. But if it is a prayer, the words "he will reveal" will be supplied from the common construction: as, He will reveal to you to walk by the same rule, and so on. For he knows that without the divine impulse nothing is rightly accomplished. To be of the same mind, he says, in that which we have attained in the faith — no more, no less.

Be fellow-imitators of me, brethren, and mark those who so walk, even as ye have us for a pattern. For many walk, of whom I often told you; and now I tell you even weeping, the enemies of the cross of Christ, whose end is destruction, whose God is the belly, and whose glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things.

«Be fellow-imitators of me.» For this blessed man taught not by words alone, but in his works also he became their pattern. «And mark.» Above he was saying, Beware of the dogs and the evil workers — namely, those who even after the faith were judaizing. But now he gives them a general observation: that you may mingle, he says, with these, who bear the pattern of my conversation among you. — «Those who so walk.» [ΦΩΤ.] To walk he takes here simply for to live and to conduct oneself. Since, he says, many walk, then, for the distinguishing of those who walk rightly and those who do not, mark who walk according to the pattern in which we walked, and follow these. «For many walk.» Badly, he says, and otherwise than I. «And now I tell you even weeping.» Behold the bowels of Paul, weeping over the evils of others. For since the disease was straining upon them, he mourns for them. «The enemies of the cross of Christ.» What sort are these whom you bid us guard against? The enemies, he says, of the cross of Christ. For there were some who were Christians in name, but lived in luxury and ease, whom he calls enemies of the cross. For they do things contrary to it; for it requires a noble soul, drawn up in array against dangers and imitating its own Master, whereas these are slaves to the belly. «Whose end is destruction.» Well did he call the end destruction — if they remain such to the end; for it was likely that they should also be changed. «Whose God is the belly.» For they serve it as a mistress, or rather as [1312] a god. Of whom he says elsewhere: «Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.» For there too he was speaking of the gluttonous. «And their glory is in their shame.» Many say that by these words he was hinting at circumcision; but it is not so. Rather, he says, they boast in those things for which they ought to have hidden their faces and been ashamed. «Who mind earthly things.» For they who seek after ease and luxury mind earthly things.

For our commonwealth is in the heavens, whence also we look for a Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ, who shall transfigure the body of our humiliation, that it may become conformed to the body of his glory, according to the working whereby he is able even to subject all things to himself.

We must therefore seek the things in heaven, where our commonwealth is. For there we serve as soldiers, there we live as citizens. «Whence also a Saviour.» From heaven we look for our Lord and Saviour, who shall appear in the Father's glory, at the second coming, with his holy angels; who, he says, shall both transfigure and change the present body. And he called it a body of humiliation because it is subject to corruption and to passions and pains. And he will make it, he says, like to that body of his own, which sits at the right hand of the Father, which is adored by the incorporeal powers. For our body also shall be changed into incorruption, by the grace of our God Jesus Christ. Woe to those who fall away from this glory and are ranked with the demons. «Who shall transfigure.» Many suppose that another body, a spiritual one, is raised at the resurrection, and not this one which we now wear about us, because it is said, He shall transfigure the body of our humiliation into the body of his glory. But see what Methodius says in his discourse On the Resurrection. This body of ours also was once, before the transgression, a body of glory; and it was so glorious that now, after the transgression, it is called a body of humiliation. For had it not been glorious, it would not now have been called a body of humiliation. For it is the glorious that is humbled by a fall. Since of that which is always lowly no one would say that it was humbled through the transgression. For indeed of him who stands, if he fall, we say that he fell; but of him who always lies, no one would say that he fell by sinning. So then it is this body that rises, become incorruptible and glorious, and not another. «That it may become conformed.» He lays down a strong and indisputable ground of belief that the body of our humiliation shall assuredly be transfigured — if only we are willing — so that it may become conformed to his glory. For if, he says, our Lord Jesus Christ comes for this, who will dispute but that this shall assuredly be? [1313] Then, do you want yet another demonstration? He wills to subject all things to himself. «For it behoves him, he says, to reign until all things be subjected to him» [var: Gk 1 Cor 15:25 reads *ἄχρις οὗ θῇ πάντας τοὺς ἐχθροὺς αὐτοῦ ὑπὸ τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ*, "till he put all his enemies under his feet" — the form Migne prints at 1316]. And: «But when all things shall have been subjected to him.» If then the Son himself reigns in order that he may subject all things to himself, and all things shall assuredly be subjected to him, it is plain that the body of our humiliation also — if only we ourselves shall be willing — shall be subjected to him. And it is subjected when it bears in itself nothing seditious and hostile toward him who subjected it, but all things concordant and in tune, both in faith and in the conduct of life. And when the body of our humiliation has thus been subjected to him, it is plainly transfigured from the humiliation that is according to sin and corruption, and becomes conformed to the body of his glory. Vain, then, is the fear.
