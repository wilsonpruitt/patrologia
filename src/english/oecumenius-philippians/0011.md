⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ «For it behoves him to reign until he put all his enemies under his feet.» ⟦s2⟧ And: «But when all things shall have been subjected to him.» ⟦s3+4⟧ Since, then, the divine oracles say these things plainly, how must it not be said that our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ is himself both he who transfigures the body of our humiliation and he who works the subjection of all things unto himself? ⟦s3+4⟧ And when the Son subjects, the Father also subjects; for the things of the Father and of the Son are common. ⟦s5⟧ «According to the working.» ⟦s6⟧ Then, in what follows, he gives also the reason of a thing so marvellous. ⟦s7⟧ For it is marvellous, that our body should become conformed to that divine body. ⟦s8⟧ For this, he says, God and Father will do out of the working — that is, according to that working by which he is able, and was able, to subject all things to his Son. ⟦s9⟧ He will therefore, he says, subdue it, so as to change it into incorruption. ⟦s10⟧ And see how great a concord of the Holy Trinity he knows: that, having above laid it down that the Son effects the change of our body, he now says that the Father does this. ⟦s11⟧ Surely, then, it is not rash, if we so understand it, that the Son will do this according to that power of his in which he subjected all things to his own body? ⟦s12⟧ But if we shall so understand it, there is fear lest we seem to divide, after the union, the one incarnate nature of God the Word.

⟦s13⟧ CHAPTER 6. ⟦s14⟧ Exhortations proper to certain persons, and common to all.

⟦s14⟧ Therefore, my brethren beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand fast in the Lord, beloved. ⟦s15⟧ I beseech Euodia, and I beseech Syntyche, to be of the same mind in the Lord. ⟦s16+17⟧ Yea, I ask thee also, genuine yokefellow, help these women, who laboured with me in the Gospel, together with Clement [1316] and the rest of my fellow-workers, whose names are in the book of life.

⟦s18⟧ Great are the praises — to be brethren of Paul. ⟦s19⟧ And not this only, but also beloved and longed for; and not only that, but also his joy and his crown. ⟦s20⟧ «So stand fast.» ⟦s21⟧ So — how? ⟦s22⟧ As he now says: unswerving and steadfast in the faith of Christ. ⟦s22⟧ «I beseech Euodia and Syntyche.» ⟦s23⟧ These women were exceedingly among the well-approved. ⟦s24⟧ «To be of the same mind in the Lord.» ⟦s25⟧ Which very thing they now think. ⟦s26⟧ «I ask thee also.» ⟦s27⟧ I beseech thee also, he says, as I did those women. ⟦s28⟧ «Genuine yokefellow.» ⟦s29⟧ From this some have supposed that Paul had a wife, and was writing to her. ⟦s30⟧ But such a notion is foolish. ⟦s31⟧ For what a thing it would be, if at Philippi he had left her behind in a strange and alien land, and neither at Tarsus, where he had been born, nor at Jerusalem, where he had been brought up? ⟦s32⟧ But we shall say either that he was the husband of one of the two women he mentioned, or that it is a proper name — some man of good repute, to whom he also commends the said women. ⟦s33⟧ «Help them.» ⟦s34⟧ Work together with them in the things that pertain to God. ⟦s35⟧ «They laboured with me.» ⟦s36⟧ He hints at dangers, which these women too endured along with him. ⟦s37⟧ «And of the rest of my fellow-workers.» ⟦s38⟧ There were indeed, he says, others also who were fellow-workers toward the preaching; nevertheless these women too laboured with me. ⟦s39⟧ «Whose names are in the book of life.» ⟦s40⟧ What is greatest of the things God said to his own disciples, this Paul says also concerning these women. ⟦s41⟧ For what says Christ? ⟦s42⟧ «Rejoice not, he says, that the demons are subject unto you, but that your names are written in the book of life.»

⟦s43⟧ Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, rejoice. ⟦s44⟧ Let your forbearance be known unto all men. ⟦s45⟧ The Lord is at hand; be careful for nothing, but in everything by prayer and by supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. ⟦s46⟧ And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, shall guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

⟦s47⟧ Christ says: «Blessed are they that mourn,» and: «Woe unto them that laugh.» ⟦s48⟧ How then does Paul say, Rejoice? ⟦s49⟧ Neither did Christ enjoin the worldly mourning — such as mourning over the loss of kinsfolk — but the spiritual, the mourning for sins. ⟦s50⟧ Nor does Paul speak of the worldly joy. ⟦s51⟧ Wherefore he also adds, In the Lord. ⟦s52⟧ So that that mourning becomes the occasion of this joy. ⟦s53⟧ For he who mourns his own sins shall have the joy that is in the Lord, having by this blotted out his sins through the mourning, and rejoicing according to God with a conscience privy to Christ. ⟦s54⟧ And what does his doubling it mean — «Again I will say, rejoice»? ⟦s55⟧ They were in grief because of the trials and the afflictions which they were enduring. ⟦s55⟧ He shows, then, that to suffer and to be grieved for Christ's sake is a procurer of the true joy. ⟦s56⟧ Or: that the grief for Christ's sake which comes about from the persecutors is itself joy, since the joy that lies in expectation overshadows [1317] the grief that is at our very feet. ⟦s57⟧ «Let your forbearance be known unto all men.» ⟦s58⟧ Since above he had accused certain men, saying, Whose God is the belly, and their glory is in their shame, it was likely that on account of these things the faithful would be set at war with those men. ⟦s59⟧ He says therefore: Do not treat them as enemies, but with forbearance; and not toward them only, but toward all men also. ⟦s60⟧ «The Lord is at hand; be careful for nothing.» ⟦s61⟧ For why are you distressed, he says, at their plots, or at the other trials? ⟦s62⟧ The Lord is at hand, he says, the judgment is upon us, and both you and they shall have your recompenses. ⟦s63⟧ Be careful, then, he says, for nothing: «but in everything» — that is, in every matter — use prayer and supplication. ⟦s64⟧ Now it is a very great comfort to those who are afflicted that the Lord is at hand. ⟦s65⟧ And behold, he adds yet another medicine, the prayer that is with thanksgiving. ⟦s66⟧ For this is prayer in the proper sense. ⟦s67⟧ Through the prayer, then, and the supplication that is with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known unto God. ⟦s68⟧ So that prayers which have not thanksgiving are not even made known unto God. ⟦s69⟧ For he who gives no thanks for the things that are past, how shall he either ask or receive the things to come? ⟦s70⟧ But in every matter use prayer and supplication, through which let your requests, with thanksgiving, be made known. ⟦s71⟧ «And the peace of God.» ⟦s72⟧ By peace he means God's reconciliation toward us. ⟦s73⟧ And well did he say peace. ⟦s74⟧ For we had been set at war with him, but through the blood of the Only-begotten he reconciled us to himself. ⟦s75⟧ Or else he speaks of that peace of which Christ says: «My peace I give unto you, my peace I leave unto you» [var: Gk John 14:27 has the clauses in the reverse order — *εἰρήνην ἀφίημι ὑμῖν, εἰρήνην τὴν ἐμὴν δίδωμι ὑμῖν*, "peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you" — and "my" with "give" only]. ⟦s76⟧ For when he bids us be at peace even toward our enemies, does it not surpass all understanding? ⟦s77⟧ «Which surpasses all understanding.» ⟦s78⟧ And if the peace of God surpasses all understanding, what would one say to those who busy themselves about his essence? ⟦s79⟧ «Shall guard your hearts.» ⟦s80⟧ So as not to be swamped by trials, nor carried about by deceit. ⟦s81⟧ «In Christ Jesus.» ⟦s82⟧ Either he will guard you in him, so that you abide and do not fall away from him; or he will guard you through him.

⟦s83+84⟧ Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever seemly, whatsoever just, whatsoever pure, whatsoever lovely, whatsoever of good report — if there be any virtue and if any praise, these things consider. ⟦s85⟧ What things ye also learned, and received, and heard, and saw in me, these do; and the God of peace shall be with you.

⟦s85⟧ All things have been told you; this alone now remains.
