⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And ye yourselves also know, Philippians, that in the beginning of the Gospel, when I went forth from Macedonia, no church had fellowship with me as concerning an account of giving and receiving, but ye only. ⟦s2⟧ For even in Thessalonica ye sent once and again unto my need. ⟦s3⟧ Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that abounds unto your account. ⟦s4⟧ But I have all things and abound. ⟦s5⟧ I am filled, having received from Epaphroditus the things sent from you, an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, well-pleasing to God. ⟦s6⟧ And my God shall fill up every need of yours according to his riches, in glory, in Christ Jesus.

⟦s7⟧ For the rebuke spoken above, where he said, Now at length ye have flourished again, he now quietly makes his apology. ⟦s8⟧ For do not suppose, he says, that I have spoken as one in need or as one demanding, but because I am confident of you, he says. ⟦s8⟧ And it is you who gave the occasions of this confidence, in that ye supplied me in the beginning of the Gospel. ⟦s9⟧ «In the beginning of the Gospel.» ⟦s10⟧ Before, he says, ye were confirmed at all. ⟦s11⟧ «When I went forth from Macedonia.» ⟦s12⟧ It cannot be said, he says, that ye gave to me as to one who was among you. ⟦s13⟧ When I went forth, he says, not only out of your city, but out of the province also. ⟦s14⟧ «No church had fellowship with me.» ⟦s15⟧ He did not say, No church gave to me, but, «Had fellowship.» ⟦s16⟧ For the thing is a fellowship. ⟦s17+18⟧ For I gave spiritual things, he says, and received carnal, as he says elsewhere also: «If we have sown unto you spiritual things, is it a great matter if we shall reap your carnal things?» ⟦s19⟧ And the phrase, As concerning an account of giving and receiving, carries its condemnation in yet another way. ⟦s20⟧ For they neither gave carnal things, nor were they willing even to receive spiritual. ⟦s21⟧ «For even in Thessalonica.» ⟦s22⟧ This is yet more: that while he was living in their very metropolis, he was maintained by these men. ⟦s23⟧ Wherefore he says, In Thessalonica, unto my need — that is, for the necessary expenses. ⟦s24⟧ «Not that I seek the gift.» ⟦s25+26⟧ Since he had spoken lowlily in saying, unto my needs, that they might not be lifted up thereby, he says: Not that I seek your gift do I say these things, but I seek your fruit, which abounds unto [1325] your account, not mine. ⟦s27⟧ Do you see that they themselves were benefited by supplying? ⟦s28⟧ As though he were saying: For your advantage I seek these things, that ye may bear fruit. ⟦s29⟧ «But I have all things.» ⟦s30⟧ By this again he makes them more eager. ⟦s31⟧ I have, he says, all things, and abound — that is, the things due for the former time also, in which ye sent not. ⟦s32⟧ «And abound.» ⟦s33⟧ That is, I count that I have received more than the things that were passed over. ⟦s34⟧ «I am filled.» ⟦s35+36⟧ All things, he says, that were owing to me; for he counts the thing a debt. ⟦s35+36⟧ I am filled, he says, having received from Epaphroditus. ⟦s37⟧ Through him they had sent. ⟦s38⟧ «An odour of a sweet smell.» ⟦s39⟧ He says that they are an odour of a sweet smell and a sacrifice acceptable to God. ⟦s40⟧ Ah, to what a place has he carried up the things that were given! ⟦s41⟧ Ye gave, he says, not to me, but to God. ⟦s42⟧ For I received the things from you, he says, as being an odour of a sweet smell and a sacrifice to God. ⟦s43⟧ For indeed incense and a rational sacrifice are offered to God alone. ⟦s44⟧ And the gifts from you, he says, prove to have been offered to God. ⟦s45⟧ «And my God shall fill up every need of yours.» ⟦s46⟧ Since he said above, «But ye lacked opportunity» — that is, Ye were in want — he now prays for them self-sufficiency, so as to be able to supply others also. ⟦s47⟧ «According to his riches, in glory.» ⟦s48+49⟧ This he said, showing that it is also easy for him to give; for he is rich. ⟦s50⟧ And he will give to you in glory. ⟦s51⟧ That is, so that the giving may advance unto his glory. ⟦s52⟧ «With Christ Jesus» [lat: the Latin column prints *Per Christum Jesum*, and its note glosses ἐν — *Nam ἐν capitur pro… id est In pro Per* — not σύν]. ⟦s53⟧ Or: he will give through Jesus Christ. ⟦s54⟧ For he who has been filled with the things needful gives thanks to him who gave.

⟦s55⟧ Now unto God and our Father be the glory unto the ages of the ages. ⟦s56⟧ Amen. ⟦s57⟧ Salute every saint in Christ Jesus. ⟦s58⟧ The brethren who are with me salute you. ⟦s58⟧ All the saints salute you, but chiefly they that are of Caesar's household. ⟦s59⟧ The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. ⟦s59⟧ Amen.

⟦s60⟧ Since he said, Through Jesus Christ he shall fill up every need of yours, he glorifies the Father also, who through the Son gives the self-sufficiency. ⟦s61⟧ «The brethren who are with me salute you.» ⟦s62⟧ For there were many with him, save that Timothy alone was like-souled, as he says above. ⟦s63⟧ Yet see the bowels of Paul, that he calls these also brethren. ⟦s64⟧ «All the saints salute you.» ⟦s65⟧ It was a sign that Paul praised the Philippians among them, and therefore they salute them. ⟦s66⟧ «But chiefly they that are of Caesar's household.» ⟦s67⟧ He braced them up by indicating that the preaching had reached even the imperial household. ⟦s68⟧ For had they not believed, they would not have saluted the Philippians. ⟦s69+70⟧ Besides these things he teaches this also: If they who are in the palaces despised all things and believed in Christ, how much more must ye who are poor do this? ⟦s71⟧ «The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with you all.» ⟦s72⟧ As is his custom, with a prayer he ends the Epistle now also.

⟦s73⟧ The End, with God's help, of the Epistle to the Philippians. ⟦s74⟧ It was written from Rome by Epaphroditus.
