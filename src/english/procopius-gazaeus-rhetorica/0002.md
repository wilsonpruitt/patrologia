⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [2805] [b: A] each man brings along with him, fixing together a makeshift hut wherever he chances: men of this kind, from what daring would they hold back? ⟦s1+2⟧ To these the cities lay open for plunder, cities once happy and splendid, but then bereft of defenders and deprived of allies; and one was held, another about to be, and another was being emptied of its people; and the fear of what was to come grieved more than the suffering; and a rumour of no good fortune shook the ears of all, telling one thing upon another, and all of it dreadful. ⟦s3⟧ For one could hear of a city taken, of happiness plundered, of women driven off, forced into wantonness, of children outraged, grey hairs tormented, youth dragged away, and maidens led, not to a happy bridal chamber and a blessed bridegroom, as their former hopes had been, but to the outrage of an enemy and a barbarian, [b: B] grievous even to look upon; for all lay open to them. ⟦s4⟧ And he who had not suffered formerly gave thanks to fortune alone; but now all hymn thee together with her, O emperor, some because through thee they have not suffered, others because they do not expect to suffer again. ⟦s5⟧ For the barbarian, having received the blow he deserved, bows down, and knows his master, and is temperate against his will. ⟦s6⟧ And the cities are free of their tragedies; and of their walls, those grown old grow young again, and others now rise for the first time; and the cities are temperate, displaying to the barbarians a secure adornment; and forts are thrown out everywhere before the subjects, and a youthful body of soldiers, fenced close with strength and numbers: men seething in their prime, with something youthful in their look, and, as Homer would say, "servants of Ares"; not, as before, weak and grown old, trembling in dangers, afraid along with us, and themselves in greater need of a saviour. ⟦s6⟧ And a witness for us close at hand is he himself, who orders such things for the emperor, and is fit to serve him for the common benefaction. ⟦s7⟧ Whence indeed he calls thee father, and holds thee faithful, and praises thee with every vote, and has admired thee, and will never cease.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 8. ⟦s2⟧ But the speech, ever holding to the sequence of events, has, like some stream borne along, carried us off by force to somewhere here. ⟦s3⟧ So we must go back to where we turned aside. ⟦s4⟧ For thou didst reckon that it equally befitted a rule that was to be in good state, not only to be cleansed of foreigners and kept clear of the dangers from that quarter, but also, if any part of it is already sick and [2808] festering beneath, and rises up against the rest, to bring this too into good order, and to show one harmony running through all, as in a body. ⟦s5⟧ For thou knewest that men of one mind, standing with one another, are both fearful to their adversaries and quicker to repel those who harm them; but that, at strife among themselves, they have their ruin from within, and need no enemy, but rather by this very thing bring upon themselves foes and haters, who count the calamity of those sick among them an opportunity of their own. ⟦s6⟧ How the matter stood, then, I shall relate from a little further back.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 9. ⟦s2⟧ Next to Cilicia dwell certain men hard to fight, the nation of the Solians, running up from the sea into the mainland. ⟦s3⟧ These, then, as though unable to remain in happiness, both lift their neck on high and are haughty against the rule of the Romans, and were boastful to their own misfortune. ⟦s4⟧ And what most stirred them and gave them confidence out of season was both the country, which offers a nature hard to capture — for there is a height there, stretching every kind of peak up into the very aether — and also the men's training for such things. ⟦s5⟧ For these are lightly equipped, and step nimbly, and ride upon the mountains as though winged. ⟦s6+7⟧ Elated by this they form a conspiracy against us; and they harried and plundered the goods of their neighbours, and threatened terribly, resounding with a boast of words. ⟦s6+7⟧ And they had kings' commands [?], from men to whom they did not belong, and a crowd of generals, and the rest of the apparatus; and they were splendid in their hopes: as on the stage one man suddenly seems to be Agamemnon, another bears the likeness of Achilles in his costume, and one mimics one man and another another, beggars, some of them, and homeless; then comes the end of the play, and the stage is broken up.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 10. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ And these too, then, were before long to learn how great an emperor's humanity they had drawn into a fitting anger, even if they did not meet with a penalty to the measure of their deserts. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ But thou didst conquer again, O emperor, both them when bold, by battle, and them when unfortunate, by humanity; and thou didst show that the virtues truly follow one another. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ For those whom thou didst overcome by courage and generalship and a varied benefaction [?] in the contests, these kindness, following her [2809] [b: A] sisters, bade thee save; and thou didst set up a double trophy, the second conquering the first. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ For to master enemies might perhaps come of fortune alone; but to add to what went before a sequel to match it, this both adorned the victory of the battle, and this trophy at least is of virtue alone. ⟦s5⟧ Not like Philip the Macedonian — a barbarian in truth, and flourishing upon the calamities of the Greeks — who took two and thirty Chalcidian cities by deceit and guile, didst thou destroy so cruelly that none can tell whether they had ever been; and I say nothing of the most wise Athenians, how they dealt with the Melians and the Scionaeans. ⟦s6⟧ But thou, though thou hadst taken just grounds for anger against the wrongdoers, didst not so pursue them, O emperor; but, like a good father, as thou didst call thyself, having schooled them only to be temperate, thou didst give them a city which thou thyself hadst built, and a happy country; and now they use their strength as they ought, and are set as a bulwark before the others. ⟦s7⟧ This is an adornment that befits an emperor, and one greater than all hope. ⟦s8⟧ And thou seemest to me to have surpassed the famous Alexander in kingly spirit; who, when he had conquered the Indian and held him in the battle, made his enemy his counsellor, how he ought to deal with him; and when the other said "Royally," asked whether he meant to add anything to his counsel; and when he said that by one word he had declared the whole of virtue, Alexander, put to shame, I think, by the advice, was seen to be humane, perhaps against his will. ⟦s9⟧ But thou, knowing the good by nature, hadst thy counsel from within; and the vanquished are grateful, I think, and count it their one misfortune to have grieved such a man.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 11. ⟦s2⟧ I think, then, that if I had only these things to say, the speech would have reached a sufficient end, and could pride itself not a little on what has been said. ⟦s3⟧ But now my words, vying with the emperor's virtue, think it dreadful and full of all unmanliness if they cannot endure even to tell barely what he, doing good in deeds, is neither vexed by for their number nor ceases from. ⟦s4⟧ For since there are two seasons among men, of peace and of war, he divides his virtue between both, and attends to each as though he knew it alone and understood nothing of the other. ⟦s5⟧ And of his valour in wars sufficient proofs are those of which I have just ceased speaking; but of the goods of peace the greatest is this, the cause of many in particular, and that which most leads cities to happiness. ⟦s5⟧ For since men have been by nature subject to rule, and the name and the thing of king came forth, each of those in power, unable to be present everywhere to his subjects through one body, [2812] holds the cities together through the governors in each place, and through them, doing good, seems to be present to all; and what is lacking to nature he contrives by judgment. ⟦s6⟧ These things, then, so well determined from the beginning, certain bastard men, enrolled in the empire against right, who had made the common commonwealth their private gain, corrupted and turned to the contrary of their use. ⟦s6⟧ For like men set over some trade, they made the offices saleable; and one could see, under the guise of office, cities sold to those who had resolved to give money and to take it, not to those able to do good. ⟦s7⟧ Accordingly, no sooner did they come to the cities than that was done for which they had come; and from the common calamity the leaders of men grew rich.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 12. ⟦s2⟧ But since thou upholdest the cities for us by thy nod, we have learned that the insatiable lust of money, which keeps justice from dwelling among men, could not, it seems, conquer all things, but may sometimes, against expectation, be defeated. ⟦s3⟧ And yet for its sake a father betrays his child, and a wife her husband, and kindreds arm themselves against one another. ⟦s4+5⟧ But when thou sittest beside the tribunal, justice is before thine eyes; and as though she were watching, thou bringest the vote upon those who are to be judges; and he wins thy judgment who holds out not gold, but is rich in virtue, and knows how to change men into happiness. ⟦s6⟧ But Lysander, though he was a Spartan, and was proud because of his former successes, yet belied the Spartan by his deeds: for, making the choice of the governors improperly, he filled the race of the Greeks with slaughter and confusion, and through one man's judgment the cities were in misery.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 13. ⟦s2⟧ But having said one thing or a second, still holding to the benefaction common to all and over all, I shall so turn the speech also to thy humanity toward each in particular. ⟦s3⟧ For you know — and let no one be troubled in his mind — that a grievous tribute, like some dread thing, fell upon the cities at intervals, and went through all, having no just pretext, a name of ill fortune to the subjects: gold and silver come together into one appellation. ⟦s4⟧ For all men who worked with their own hands and were in want of their daily needs, and had placed every hope of life in their hands, paid it as a wage, as it were, for their sweat and misfortune; and this was laid upon them along with their fortune. ⟦s5⟧ The farmer groaned among his trees; for he had a double calamity, both toiling in body and remembering his grievances; the fisherman by the sea let down his [2813] [b: A] net, praying to take a catch in which others would share. ⟦s6⟧ And a merchant, after storm and sea and savage fortune, barely setting foot on land and seeing a city, did not have the sight of it without paying. ⟦s7⟧ And why need I speak of the others? ⟦s7⟧ For the women of the brothels, whose lot was a life of forced reproach, did not possess even their misfortune in security, but made the outrage of their body a thing for sale; and a wage was paid down for their not being chaste; and, to speak simply, the emperor's treasury was filled from the common misfortunes. ⟦s8⟧ Some of those who ruled before, who had a name for humanity, pitied these things, but could not help; they only wished it, and so far displayed their virtue; but to carry it into deed they marvelled at among things impossible. ⟦s8⟧ For this too had to be kept for thee and to become thy work along with the rest, that so great a virtue might not lie hidden for want of occasions worthy of it, through which it should show itself. ⟦s9⟧ Whence, disregarding every thought of unfitting gain, and admiring a loss that wins praise, thou didst will it, thou didst nod, it came to deed. ⟦s10⟧ Nor did thy benefaction to us stop even there; but thou didst not allow it to come again even to the wicked men who come after; for every document concerning it became the work of fire, and with the things that grieved us their memory has been abolished. ⟦s11⟧ For thou didst order them to be burned out like the Hydra's heads, lest it rise again, imitating thine own Heracles.
