⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ You then, as most radiant children of God, hear also the most radiant good tidings of God, through which you have become children of God by grace, and have obtained a heavenly nobility, casting off the earthy ignobility, and have taken on a divine birth, putting away the human descent. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ You then, hear the sacred good tidings, you who have been counted worthy of the divine initiation into their most perfect faith.

⟦s1+2+3⟧ [3229] But what are our good tidings? or what is their achievement? ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Listen faithfully as we speak. ⟦s4⟧ For we shall speak most fittingly, as he himself gives to us, he who through us now brings good tidings to you.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 11. ⟦s2⟧ So God, having in the beginning created out of what was not the universe, all that is after him and was brought by him into being, things visible and invisible, sensible and intelligible, heavenly and earthly, creates man too after his own image, framing him of a rational soul and a body; whom he also made the image and likeness of his own goodness, granting him a self-determining life, and making him a dweller in a divine paradise; and having fashioned him good, he laid down for him a divine and saving commandment, so that he might not be puffed up by the greatness of the image, nor, from his likeness to God, fancy for himself in his folly the nature of God. ⟦s3⟧ But he, having received such gifts from God who fashioned him (for he had been set forth by him as king of the things on earth, while plainly ruled as king and governed by God himself who created him, as all created things are too), did not bear the greatness of the gifts, but is eager to take sameness with God, and the created man desired to be God; and wickedly fancying, by the deceit of the serpent, the divine and lofty and supreme things, he is borne down to death and corruption, and falls a most terrible fall, he who wished to snatch the dignity of the divine nature. ⟦s4⟧ For this [b: C] was also most just, that he who wished to exalt himself beyond his worth should be humbled with the utmost humbling. ⟦s5⟧ Since also «whosoever exalteth himself shall be humbled», as one who wickedly tries to snatch what God has not given him. ⟦s6⟧ «For no man taketh the honour unto himself,» as the great Apostle said, «but he that is called of God who giveth it.»

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 12. ⟦s2⟧ So he was held by death, corrupted along with corruption, coming forth from the earth, he who made bold to snatch the honour of God, and dragged back again into the earth; today born by a fleshly birth, and tomorrow put to death by a fleshly death; today seen among the living, and tomorrow numbered among the dead. ⟦s3+4+5⟧ And this penalty man bore, who wished to wrong God, suffering it of his own counsel and purpose, having used ill his self-determination, which he had received when he was honoured by the one who created him, for his most comely likeness to him and the splendour of the image. ⟦s3+4+5⟧ «For God made not death, neither rejoiceth he in the destruction of the living.»

⟦s3+4+5⟧ [3232] And truly pitiable and most wretched was he who of old was blessed: when he appeared as the possessor of paradise, when he had been set as paradise's first dweller, when he had a life without toil and without labour, when he passed his days naked and without a tunic, when he did not fear the changes of the air, when he did not know the freezing of the cold, when he was not acquainted with the blaze of the burning heat, when going about naked he felt no shame either; when he did not receive the assaults of passions, when he did not feel the pain of sicknesses, when he was released from human slavery, when he had been freed from barbarian tyranny, when he did not hear the harassments of tax-gatherers, when he did not see the encounters of creditors, when he did not dread the mangling of wild beasts, when he possessed the utmost well-being, when he was fed on the fruits of paradise, when he enjoyed all good things ungrudgingly, when he received the radiance of God, when, though by nature created, he reigned over earthly creatures, when he bore the name of God's image, and was wonderfully made bright with the likeness of God.

⟦s3+4+5⟧ Of all these he is orphaned, having transgressed the saving commandment of God, as one who had stumbled against God; and he is made naked and a stranger to his former splendour, and from paradise he is at once banished, and to the earth again, having become corruptible through sin, he is returned; and through the multitude of his folly he is likened to the beasts, putting off the former honour with which God who created him had beautified him. ⟦s6⟧ So he is a slave to corruption and death and passions, having thought to be rich in Godhead by tyranny.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 13. ⟦s2⟧ But he who created him and brought him forth when before he was not, seeing him in such a state, pitied him; and having pitied, had mercy; and having had mercy, is moved with compassion; and being moved with compassion, as loving man — being, as he is, good and loving man — he rises up with a divine compassion for his salvation and recall; and he fulfils a counsel ancient and loving man, which he had counselled in love for man from the beginning. ⟦s3⟧ For he did not bear, as the moulder, to look on his moulding being corrupted, and perishing into earth and corruption.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 14. ⟦s2⟧ And what is the counsel that God counselled for the salvation and redemption of men? ⟦s3⟧ Let Isaiah the wonderful come forward and cry it, he who foretold the seedless conception of God, and recounted beforehand his virgin birth; who, at the fulfilment of the divine purpose, singing to God a song of thanksgiving, cried aloud: «O Lord, my God, I will glorify thee; I will hymn thy name, for thou hast done wonderful things, [3233] [b: A] an ancient counsel, true; so be it. ⟦s3⟧ For thou hast made strong cities a heap, that their foundations should fall; the city of the ungodly shall never be built. ⟦s4⟧ Therefore shall the poor people bless thee; and cities of wronged men shall bless thee. ⟦s5⟧ For thou hast been a helper to every lowly city, and a shelter to them that are disheartened through want. ⟦s6⟧ From evil men thou shalt deliver them.»

⟦s7⟧ These, brethren, are our divine good tidings; these we bring as good tidings to you today, not as things about to happen afterwards, but as things already divinely done, which have saved man from his condemnation, and brought him back to his former brightness [b: B] and blessedness, and made him a friend of God as before.

⟦s8⟧ 15. ⟦s9+10⟧ And how is our salvation wrought by God? or how is the recall accomplished? ⟦s9+10⟧ Hear with understanding and all wisdom (for these things are wise, and more than wise, and beyond all human wisdom), taking up a divine mind, and one worthy of a divine promise. ⟦s11⟧ He who moulded man from the beginning and made him after his own image judges to save man and redeem his own image by no other means than by becoming man by nature, and making his own image his own garment. ⟦s12⟧ Hence the unmingled are mingled; hence the undiluted are blended without dilution; hence the uncompounded are compounded; hence the unconnected are bound fast; hence the divine things become human, that the human may become more divine; hence the Word of God, remaining God and the Word of God, is made thick; hence the uncreated, as begotten of the uncreated God, remaining uncreated, is created; hence the Most High is announced lowly, and the invisible is beheld visible, and the intangible admits touch; and the bodiless comes forth embodied, and the fleshless is recorded as flesh; and the impassible is proclaimed passible; and he who has an immortal nature is declared mortal by nature, that he might lead passible and mortal manhood to a bright immortality and [3236] impassibility, and make kin to God and the Father the manhood that was hostile to God and at war with him.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 16. ⟦s2⟧ And how is this fulfilled, and done, and carried through into a deed that does not lie? ⟦s3⟧ For it is fearful and astounding, and most incredible to the ears of men who understand the most divine things by human reasonings. ⟦s4⟧ But where God is proclaimed, the paltriness of human reasonings is idle. ⟦s5⟧ For all things are most easy for God, and possible, and readily provided, whatever he might wish to do. ⟦s6⟧ And this is the most conspicuous and singular mark of the divine and supreme power, and one wholly incommunicable to every created nature; for hence the things above human nature are wonderful and astounding, and surpass every created power.

⟦s7⟧ And how is this accomplished by God, and brought to a renowned end? ⟦s8⟧ Luke the wondrous teaches us clearly, who writes our Gospel, the most sacred of all sacred things, so that none may disbelieve the greatness of the deed, so that none may falsely charge God with weakness, being himself weak and powerless, and unfit for every more divine action. ⟦s9⟧ As you heard but now when he cried aloud: «In the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent from God unto a city of Galilee, named Nazareth, to a Virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the Virgin's name was Mary.» ⟦s9⟧ Hearest thou that God sent him, and wilt thou disbelieve the deed? ⟦s10⟧ Disbelieve not the ordinance of God, but rather believe most piously that God is most mighty to fulfil whatever he may wish to do. ⟦s11⟧ And by whom else could an angel be sent, but wholly by God alone, who made him and gave him essence out of what was not, and fashioned all angelic nature? ⟦s12⟧ If then God is discerned as the sender, and an angel is proclaimed as the one sent, how is what is said not true? ⟦s13⟧ Let all marvel at these things; but let them not disbelieve the proclamation, knowing the strength of God, that it is all-mighty and truly almighty.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 17. ⟦s2+3⟧ And what does the most blessed angel, being sent, say to the Virgin who knew not man? or how does he announce to her the declaration of such good tidings? and all that such a mighty work of God brings to birth through her? ⟦s4⟧ «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee.» ⟦s5⟧ With joy the angel of joy begins to her. ⟦s6⟧ For he knew, and understood, that his message becomes the procurer of joy to all men, and likewise to all created things, and the destroyer of all grief for all; for he knew that from this [3237] [b: A] the knowledge of God would shine on the world; he knew that the mist of error would vanish; he knew that the sting of death would be blunted; he knew that the power of corruption would be corrupted; he knew that the victory of Hades would be pulled down; he knew that perishing man would be saved, who of old had been a slave to these, since he was driven from the paradise of luxury and driven out of the blessed sojourn there. ⟦s7⟧ For this reason he makes joy the prelude of his good tidings; for this reason he speaks joy first of his words; for this reason joy went before these good tidings, as they would be joy to all who believe. ⟦s8⟧ For the divine messages of joy had to begin from joy-making words and sayings. ⟦s9⟧ For this reason the angel utters joy before all, knowing the outcome of his own good tidings, and that his discourse, brought to completion, would appear a joy to all the world.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 18. ⟦s2⟧ For what joy and delight does it not surpass, the message that came to the blessed Virgin, the mother of joy too? ⟦s3⟧ Hail, O bearer of the heavenly joy; hail, O midwife of the supreme joy; hail, O mother-city of the saving joy; hail, O cause of the immortal joy; hail, O mystic lodging of the unspeakable joy; hail, O admirable ploughland of the ineffable joy; hail, O all-blessed spring of the unfailing joy; hail, O God-bearing treasure of the everlasting joy; hail, O most flourishing [b: C] plant of the life-giving joy; hail, O unwedded Mother of God; hail, O Virgin unrobbed after childbirth; hail, O spectacle most marvellous of all marvels. ⟦s4⟧ Who will be able to tell thy splendour? ⟦s5⟧ Who would dare to speak thy wonder? ⟦s6⟧ Who will make bold to proclaim thy greatness? ⟦s7⟧ Thou hast adorned the nature of men; thou hast conquered the ranks of Angels; thou hast hidden the radiances of the Archangels; thou hast shown the presidencies of the Thrones second to thee; thou hast made small the height of the Dominions; thou hast outrun the leaderships of the Principalities; thou hast unnerved the strength of the Authorities; thou hast come forth a power mightier than the Powers; thou hast surpassed with earthly eyes the many-eyed of the Cherubim; thou hast overpassed the six-winged of the Seraphim with the God-moved wings of the soul; and thou hast excelled [b: D] all creation, as shining with purity more than all creation, and receiving the Creator of all creation, and bearing him in the womb and giving him birth, and Mother of God, born out of all creation.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ 19. [3240] For this reason I address thee, «Hail, thou that art full of grace», since thou hast been graced more than all creation; and I recognize and know the cause of such joy and grace in thee; wherefore I add, crying aloud and uttering it: «The Lord is with thee»: he who, as Creator, is lord of all creation, but is with thee, as carried in thy womb, and borne in the womb with an unutterable child-bearing. ⟦s3⟧ «The Lord is with thee»: he who was begotten everlastingly of the Father, and is ever beheld with the Father, but now comes to be with thee in the conception, and has wrought from thee a fearful incarnation. ⟦s4⟧ «The Lord is with thee»: he who has a first, everlasting birth, and receives a second birth from thee. ⟦s5⟧ «The Lord is with thee»: he who with the Father is lord of all creation, but [b: B] from thee puts on the form of a servant, and frees mankind from slavery. ⟦s6⟧ For this is why the Lord becomes a servant, that he may show the servant a lord by grace. ⟦s7⟧ «The Lord is with thee»: he who formerly was outside all creation, but now is seen with thee, the created one, and through thee has become numbered with created things; for in thee the uncreated is created, and from thee the Creator comes forth created. ⟦s8⟧ I see the uncreated become a creature in thee, and I behold the fleshless made perfect as flesh.
