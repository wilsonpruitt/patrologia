⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 20. ⟦s2⟧ Above, when he sent me to thee, O Virgin, he was fleshless and bodiless; but here I behold him become flesh and body. ⟦s3⟧ Above he was uncreated, set apart from all creation; but here I find him become a creature and a thing made, without change, in thee the Virgin. ⟦s4⟧ Above I saw God bare of manhood (for where would manhood be beheld in heaven? ⟦s5⟧ Let not the Origens again talk nonsense; let not the Didymuses again rave; let not the Evagriuses again, fighting in passion, run mad, recounting empty myths, and fancying a pre-existence of souls, and making truce with the abominable Greeks); but here I see him become man without alteration, and a wonder within a wonder: that he is both God unalterable, and man inexplicable; twofold in nature, but not twofold in hypostasis, having one and the same person; and known in twofold natures, neither divided because of the twofoldness of the natures, nor, because of the sameness and singleness and unpairedness of the hypostasis, working the pouring-together of the natures of which he is; nor increasing the numbering of the Trinity above, sovereign of all things, and being shown to men as one [3241] [b: A] of men. ⟦s5⟧ And I find the same one God and man, and I marvel at the greatness of the wonder.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 21. ⟦s2⟧ «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee.» ⟦s3⟧ And what could be higher than this joy, O Virgin Mother? ⟦s4+5⟧ Or what could be greater than this grace, which thou alone hast inherited from God? or more gracious and more radiant? ⟦s6⟧ All things walk behind thy wonder; all things lie below thy grace; all things of note take second place, and are allotted the lesser share of brightness. ⟦s7⟧ «God is with thee»: and who would dare to vie? ⟦s8⟧ God from thee: and who will not at once be worsted, and rather, rejoicing, yield to thee the pre-eminence and the excellence? ⟦s9⟧ For this reason I utter to thee the greatest things, beholding thy prerogatives among all created things: «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee.» ⟦s10⟧ For from thee joy is not only graciously given to men, but is given also to the powers above.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 22. ⟦s2⟧ Truly «blessed art thou among women»: for thou hast changed the curse of Eve into blessing; for thou hast made Adam, who was at first accursed, to be blessed through thee. ⟦s3⟧ Truly «blessed art thou among women»: for through thee the blessing of the Father has dawned on men, and has freed them from the old curse. ⟦s4⟧ Truly «blessed art thou among women»: for through thee thy forefathers are saved; for thou wilt bear the Saviour, who works divine salvation for them. ⟦s5⟧ Truly «blessed art thou among women»: for without seed thou hast put forth a fruit, which bestows blessing on all the earth, and redeems it from the thorn-bearing curse. ⟦s6⟧ Truly «blessed art thou among women»: for, being a woman by nature, thou wilt become Theotokos in fact. ⟦s7⟧ For if he who is to be born of thee is God incarnate in truth, thou wouldst most justly be called Theotokos, as bearing God in truth.

⟦s8⟧ 23. ⟦s9⟧ But the blessed and inspired Virgin, seeing the wondrous angel thus come to her and addressing her with such words, was troubled in her holy soul; and she turned the words over in [3244] her reasonings, reckoning with herself and searching out the force of the angelic words; for she was amazed, and marvelled at the quality of the angelic greetings. ⟦s10⟧ «For she,» it says, «seeing him, was troubled at his saying, and cast in her mind what manner of salutation this should be.» ⟦s11⟧ For she was, she was full of human understanding too, and admitted nothing of what was said without testing it. ⟦s12⟧ For she feared and trembled, knowing the deceit of Eve, lest the crafty serpent should deceive her a second time, as he did that one; and for this reason she proved all things with understanding, lest a serpent-shaped address should cast her out of divine brightness.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 24. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ And the heavenly angel sent to her, beholding her thus in anguish, frees her from all fear, and from terror, and drives her out of all trouble and agitation, and says to her most cheerfully and most graciously and most sweetly: «Fear not, Mary.» ⟦s2+3+4⟧ I am not like that one who deceived thy mother; I am not like that one who of old tripped up man; I am not like that one who formerly banished thy forefathers from the paradise of luxury; I am not like that one who cast man out of the blessed life, and fitted him to a life of pain; I am not like that one who by envy gave birth to death, and made all men mortal; I am not like that one who made all men, who came from the earth and received their moulding by the hand of God, transgressors of the divine commandment, and returned them again to the earth; I am not like that one who showed Adam, who at first bore the name of friend of God, an enemy of God.

⟦s2+3+4⟧ I am an angel of God almighty, and I lead [?] a certain angelic host, and among the angels of God I am named Gabriel. ⟦s2+3+4⟧ And I have been sent from God the Father almighty to tell thee, the spotless and blameless Virgin, of the descent upon thee the Virgin of his only-begotten Son, who brought all things into existence and called them out of what was not into being, and of the much-hymned conception in thee, and the all-supreme incarnation from thee, and the much-marvelled pregnancy in thee, and the incomprehensible birth from thee.

⟦s5⟧ Through which all the enemy's endeavours are undone; through which all the enemy's inventions are destroyed together with their inventor himself; through which man [3245] [b: A] is saved, and rises up from the corruption of death; through which the night of error vanishes; through which man, who had become an exile from paradise, dwells again in paradise; through which he who had been stripped of friendship with God by the counsel of the dragon is made again a friend of God.

⟦s6⟧ And for this reason I cry to thee, O blessed Virgin, who art to become the bearer of God, the Saviour of all things: «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women»: since thou hast been counted worthy to obtain such great things; since both the heavenly joy will be born from thee to the world, and the inspired blessing, which blunts the curse of old, wells up through thee for men.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ 25. ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ I too myself praise thy caution, O Mother of God, and I fully approve thy understanding: that thou fearest the snares of the crafty one; that thou fearest the devices of the man-hating beast; that thou dreadest the hidden arrows of the devil; that thou art afraid and hast feared the subtle and truly death-bearing blasts of the serpent, lest thou too shouldst be named another Eve like that one.

⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ «Fear not, Mary: for thou hast found grace with God» — the immortal grace; thou hast found grace with God, the more than bright; thou hast found grace with God, the much-prayed-for; thou hast found grace with God, the more than luminous; thou hast found grace with God, the saving; thou hast found grace with God, the unshakeable; thou hast found grace with God, the immovable; thou hast found grace with God, [b: C] the invincible; thou hast found grace with God, the everlasting.

⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ [3248] Many saints have been before thee; but none has been graced like thee; none has been made blessed like thee; none has been hallowed like thee; none has been magnified like thee; none has been purified beforehand like thee; none has been shone round like thee; none has been enlightened like thee; none has been exalted above like thee; for none has drawn near to God like thee; and none has been enriched with God's gifts like thee; none has received the grace of God like thee. ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ Thou conquerest all the excellences among men; thou conquerest all the gifts given by God to all; for more than all thou art rich in the indwelling of God. ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ None has been able so to contain God; none has had strength so to receive the presence of God; none has been counted worthy so to be enlightened with God. ⟦s1+2+3+4⟧ And for this reason thou hast not only received God, the Creator and Master of all, but thou hast him also from thee paradoxically being made flesh and borne in the womb, and after this being born, and redeeming all men from the ancestral condemnation, and bestowing on them a salvation that will not cease.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 26. ⟦s2⟧ And for this reason I have cried to thee, and again I will say aloud: «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women.» ⟦s3⟧ Be not troubled [3249] [b: A] hearing my words; fear not, learning of my discourse with thee; I bring no deadly deceit to men; I have not come to procure for my fellow-servants envy, the begetter of death; drive away all trouble from thy heart; banish all doubting from thy mind; chase away all fear from thy soul. ⟦s3⟧ I have not come to thee fearful, even if to all the others I appear most fearful; I have not used the dignity of my rule; for I know with whom I am present and discoursing; I know to whom I have been sent from God; I know whom I have been appointed to serve.

⟦s4⟧ On the contrary, I am astounded beholding thee, and am filled with fear and dread; on the contrary, I marvel at thy greatness, and am filled with trembling and terror; I dread thy grace; I am amazed at thy brightness. ⟦s5⟧ I am a servant of God and a minister; but thou wilt be the Mother of God, my Master and Creator, nay more, his bearer too, and a nurse beyond telling, nourishing him with the streams of virgin breasts, him who gives to all flesh «food in due season». ⟦s6⟧ I am a worshipper of God, and a minister of answers; but thou hast God himself in thy womb, dwelling in thee in the flesh, and coming forth from thee as a bridegroom, and dispensing joy to all, and bestowing divine radiance. ⟦s7⟧ For in thee, O Virgin, «God», as in a heaven most pure and shining, «set his own tabernacle»; and «from thee, as a bridegroom out of his chamber, he will go forth»; and imitating a giant's course, he may run a way in life that will be saving for all in life, stretching from the heights of the heavens to their heights, and filling the universe with inspired heat, and with it a light-living brilliance.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 27. ⟦s2⟧ Wherefore again I cry to thee the blessed address of joy: «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee.» ⟦s2⟧ I have come bringing joy, not deadly fear to men; I am a minister of joy, I am not known as a servant of grief; I have appeared an angel of joy, I am not recognized as a herald of grief. ⟦s3⟧ Why fearest thou him who, on the contrary, fears thee? ⟦s4⟧ Why dreadest thou him who dreads thy dignity? ⟦s5⟧ Why art thou afraid of him who is afraid of thy grace? ⟦s6⟧ Why art thou wary of him who is wary of thy splendour? ⟦s6⟧ I am a servant and the least worshipper [b: D] of thy Child and offspring. ⟦s7⟧ He made me, when I was not; he sent me to thee from the heavenly places, announcing to thee his joy-making coming; I cry to thee his uncircumscribed conception; I proclaim to thee his inexplicable incarnation; I utter to thee his birth, the bearer of all joy.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 28. ⟦s2⟧ And for this reason I say to thee, all-hymned Virgin: «Fear not, Mary: for thou hast found grace with [3252] [b: A] God.» ⟦s3⟧ Thou hast found grace which no one of women at all has found; thou hast found grace which no one has beheld; thou hast found grace which no one has received.

⟦s3⟧ And what is this? ⟦s4⟧ Hear. ⟦s4⟧ «Behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name Jesus. ⟦s5⟧ He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: and he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.» ⟦s6+7⟧ These things I have been sent from heaven to cry to thee; to bring these things to thee clearly as good tidings, he who is to be born of thee has sent; to make these known to thee in words and sayings, the Word of the Father has commanded; to discourse of these with thee, the only-begotten Son [b: B] of God has decreed. ⟦s6+7⟧ Above he gave me the message, and here I behold him; in heaven he entrusted me with the word, and in thee the Virgin I behold him dwelling. ⟦s8⟧ Everlasting was he who has come to thee the Virgin; timeless was he who is conceived of thee in time; fleshless was he who is to be born of thee in the flesh; uncircumscribed was he who in thee submits to circumscription.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 29. ⟦s2⟧ «Behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb.» ⟦s3+4⟧ And do not, I pray thee, think, O Virgin, of the conception of other women.
