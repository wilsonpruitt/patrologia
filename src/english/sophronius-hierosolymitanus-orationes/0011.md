⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 36. ⟦s2⟧ «Thou shalt conceive, then, in the womb,» as I have said; or rather, as I see, thou hast already conceived, since I spoke the "Hail" to thee and addressed to thee a joy-making utterance. ⟦s2⟧ «And thou shalt bear a son,» who made thee when thou wast not, and fashioned all creation before thee. ⟦s3⟧ «For by the Word of God all things have come to be;» and the Word of God, and God, O Virgin, thou wouldst beget, [3265] [b: A] not bare of manhood, as he has been begotten eternally from the Father, but become flesh and man, without change and confusion, without transformation and diminution, without bodily alteration; for God, who is also the most mighty creator of these things, is by nature without experience of them; for he defined the bounds for you, not for himself.

⟦s4⟧ Thou shalt bear, then, a Son, as I said before, the eternal Son of God, who is also the Word of the Begetter; and him not fleshless, but interwoven with flesh and blood, and having an intellectual and invisible soul, and all that the man seen according to nature is; who, coming down upon thee, the undefiled, «as rain upon a fleece,» taking to himself flesh from thy virginal blood, and himself creating a soul, as he wills, [b: B] and having united these and mingled them with himself according to hypostasis into the uncut hypostasis of one Son, shall be born of thee paradoxically, keeping thee a virgin who bearest, and making thee Theotokos who knowest not a man. ⟦s5⟧ But thou shalt bear when thou hast fulfilled the time of child-bearing. ⟦s6⟧ For the Lover of man wills, he wills, in this too to make his likeness to men, that he may become man in all things, neither departing from being God, nor partaking of human sin. ⟦s7⟧ Do not then any longer say to me with human thoughts: «And how shall this be to me, since I know not a man?»

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 37. ⟦s2⟧ To whom again the all-holy Virgin, having undaunted courage, answers with untrembling lips: Hear, O angel of my paradoxical conception and of my virginal childbed and birth: how can I not marvel at thy words? ⟦s2⟧ How can I not be astounded at thy utterances? ⟦s3⟧ For thou speakest to me of matters most high, such as have not yet till now come about among men. ⟦s4⟧ I know that God accomplishes all things, whatever he purposes to effect. ⟦s5⟧ But what thou thyself announcest to me in words has not yet come to pass till this day.

⟦s6⟧ For often women who had barrenness, and imitated dry rocks, have borne, God afterwards decreeing it. ⟦s7⟧ But a virgin having incorrupt purity, and clean of union with a man, [b: D] has not yet till this day borne. ⟦s8⟧ I know that Sarah passed many years barren; but afterwards she engendered Isaac, when she was seen walking towards old age, and bore white hair as witness of her own oldness; since God had ordained it, and it was impossible that it should not come to pass. ⟦s9⟧ I know that Rebecca was barren, and afterwards brought forth twin offspring, which Jacob and Esau the brothers filled, coming forth after many years from one father and one manifestly barren mother. ⟦s10⟧ I know Anna, loosing her childlessness, and weeping her barrenness, [3268] lamenting the deadness of her own womb, and for this reason abstaining from food and drink, since she could not bear the pain of barrenness; but she too, supplicating with tears the God of all, brought forth Samuel, and set aside the ordinances of barrenness, since God heard her voice and fulfilled her request, as being mighty. ⟦s11⟧ With these I shall number also her who brought me forth after barrenness, and from supplication bore me, Mary. ⟦s12⟧ For she too bore the name of Anna, and with the name the barrenness. ⟦s13⟧ But she too, entreating the God over all, and offering him many petitions, received to become a mother; who bears, God ordaining it, setting at nought the ordinances of sterility. ⟦s13⟧ And many barren women I can number, become mothers by divine command. ⟦s14⟧ But a virgin giving birth, in the whole of life, I have not yet till this day heard of — which thou thyself, as thou sayest, hast come to say.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 38. ⟦s2⟧ I do not then disbelieve God's command; for he is shown the worker of all things that he may wish to work. ⟦s3⟧ But I marvel at the power of the things said, and am amazed at their paradox and strangeness alike, and their unaccustomedness to the ears of men; and I ask justly how this shall come about, or what manner of fulfilment it will have. ⟦s4⟧ Hence, making my answer to thee, I did not say, "This shall not be to me, as thou hast said, since I know not a man," but «How shall this be to me,» I asked thee, «since I know not a man?» ⟦s5⟧ For it has never come to pass, nor is a virgin seen having given birth; wherefore I sought to learn the manner in which I shall become a virgin mother, and shall bear a child, not having received the seed of child and offspring. ⟦s6⟧ And for this reason, asking thee again, I say most soberly: «And how shall this be to me, since I know not a man?» ⟦s7⟧ For I do not utter words of unbelief, but I seek the demonstration of exactness. ⟦s8⟧ Tell the manner of the conception, and interpret the cause of the birth, and thou shalt have me not disbelieving thy words, but obedient to thy utterances. ⟦s9⟧ For it is among impossible things that what seems good to God should not come to pass.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 39. ⟦s2⟧ Having heard these things from her who would truly become Mother of God, the archangel, the herald of the good tidings, marvelling at her answer and her understanding, answered: Excellently indeed and most wisely, O Virgin Mother of God, hast thou discoursed of how of old women [3269] who were barren before became mothers of children afterwards, and in a manner forced their own nature, and bore children beyond nature, God having compassion, and the Master and Creator and Fashioner of nature having pity. ⟦s3⟧ For where God nods assent, the boundaries of nature are idle.

⟦s4⟧ Just as those women, then, God ordaining it, conquered the passions of their own nature, and, having loosed great passions of nature, brought forth fruit of the womb; so thou too, who didst spring from barren Anna, and didst conquer the nature of her who bore thee, being a pure virgin, and remaining a virgin afterwards, shalt bear paradoxically a son without seed and without ploughing, he himself who is born commanding thee. ⟦s5⟧ For he is also Lord of nature, being its Creator and ruler. ⟦s6⟧ For he is God incomprehensible, even if as man he shall be born of thee, the Virgin. ⟦s7⟧ Since it also befitted him, and was owed, becoming man in love for man for the redemption of all men, to show forth his childbirth from a Virgin alone, having made the conspicuous and saving incarnation from thee apart from marriage pleasure and union.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5⟧ 40. But if the fact that a virgin pure and undefiled has not yet till this day borne, nor has a mother of a seedless child ever been proclaimed, makes thy mind doubtful, and thou seekest to learn when a virgin has given birth; I too ask thee and inquire, when, then, did God become man? Or when did God accept to be incarnate? Or when has God come to be man? Or when was God conceived without seed? Or when did God endure child-bearing? Or when did God show forth a fleshly birth from a woman? Or when did God suck at women's breasts? Or when did God give forth an infant's wailing? Or when did God receive growth in stature? Or when has God lived as a citizen with us men? Or when did God die for men? Or when has God had the nailing of a cross? Or when was God pierced in the side with a lance? Or when did God undergo a three-day burial? Or when was God seen suffering all these things, that he might rescue man from all passions, [3272] [b: A] and destroy the assault of death and corruption, and raise up incorruptible and immortal his own image, man? If then these things, O Virgin, have never come to pass, but had been kept for the present time, and had been foretold to be fulfilled through thee, how could another virgin before thee have given birth, that thou shouldst seek to learn this from me for assurance and proof of thy virginal childbearing? Never has a virgin borne, O Virgin, never has she borne; nor will another virgin after thee, O Virgin, ever bear; nor, O Virgin, has a virgin become Mother of God; nor after thee, the only Mother of God, O Virgin, does any other virgin become so. For thou alone of women wouldst give birth as a virgin, and thou alone among women wouldst be styled Mother of God. «For once, at the consummation of the ages,» the only-begotten Son of God and the Father will become man, and will show forth the wonderful [?] from thee and thee alone.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 41. ⟦s2⟧ And that this promise of God was to receive its fulfilment through thee and thee alone, I shall assure thee by prophetic words. ⟦s3⟧ Isaiah, then, the wonderful, foreseeing the incarnation of the Word and God from thee, and a conception divine and seedless, and a child-bearing painless, and a birth unutterable, and a suckling unspeakable, thus clearly cried out beforehand: «Behold, the virgin shall have in the womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel.» ⟦s4⟧ Seest thou, Virgin pure and undefiled, how he incontestably proclaimed a virgin her who was to bear Emmanuel? ⟦s5⟧ Who thou alone art, and no other before thee or after thee will be beheld. ⟦s6⟧ For the God-inspired one does not say, "Behold, the virgins shall have in the womb, and shall bear sons, and they shall call their names Emmanuel," so that I might present to thee another virgin also giving birth. ⟦s7⟧ For one is Emmanuel, whom thou, O Virgin, shalt beget according to the flesh, and no other Emmanuel either came to be before or will come to be after these things, so that another virgin also might bear a paradoxical childbirth; and one art thou, O Virgin, his divine begetter, and no other before thee has been reported Mother of God, or will be added after thee.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 42. ⟦s2⟧ Therefore most finely and most fittingly I have cried to thee: «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee.» ⟦s3⟧ For Emmanuel, spoken in the Hebrew tongue, being changed into the Greek speech, is taken as «God with us.» ⟦s4⟧ But when has God come to be with men, and been numbered with you men, [3273] [b: A] except now in the last times of the age, when he also «took hold of the seed of Abraham,» and took to himself the flesh from thee?

⟦s5⟧ For Emmanuel, interpreted, is of twofold meaning: of human and of divine nature, from which he is also composed, in which he is also made known. ⟦s6⟧ For being perfect God before the ages, he became perfect man in thee, taking to himself the human nature from thee, and having compounded it with himself according to hypostasis, keeping unalterable the natural quality of each nature, and their natural and essential marks. ⟦s7⟧ For both God the Word has remained God the Word, even if he naturally clothed himself in the nature of flesh; and the flesh rationally ensouled, which he took to himself from thy stainless blood, and ensouled with the spirit of rational life, has remained flesh rationally ensouled, even if it was made known as the flesh of God the Word alone, united to him according to hypostasis, and running together into the uncut concourse of one person and one hypostasis, nor indeed changing the things united, and bearing them into identity of essence.

⟦s8⟧ Even if, then, Christ preserved the difference of both the natures and essences, one Son and Christ will appear from thee to the world, not showing a difference of sons and christs. ⟦s9⟧ For he who is true God will also be born true man, showing no falsehood in whatever he may be called and said. ⟦s10⟧ For this reason he will be called Emmanuel, as being by nature both in truth: both God, the same, uncircumscribed, and man, the same, in truth; the one according to the eternal birth from the Father, the other according to the temporal and embodied gestation from thee, the Mother. ⟦s11⟧ For so he was well pleased to save human nature: in that he who cannot be emptied let himself down into so great an emptying, that the manhood emptied into the earth of corruption he might raise again from the earth, and make it partaker of heavenly inheritances, in place of the enjoyment in paradise before.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 43. ⟦s2⟧ And how this shall come about, O God-receiving Virgin, I will tell and declare in words, since I have also come to say this to thee: «The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee; wherefore also that which is born holy shall be called Son of God.»
