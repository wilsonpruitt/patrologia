⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ 6. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Here then Zacharias too, having come (for he was worthy of such a ministry), and standing by the altar, and having besought [?] the Begetter concerning the sojourn of the Word with the persistence of his prayer, perceived, at the right of the altar on which he was offering the fragrance of incense, a heavenly angel standing, bearing him tidings of the right hand and from heaven. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Gabriel was the minister of these messages, who even by his name, before his words, made the announcement plain; for he has come to announce the preludes of the divine becoming man of God the Word, for which the elder too was earnestly supplicating. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Him the divine angel, seeing shaken at the vision, and increasing the shaking with fear — «For Zacharias was troubled, it says, when he saw, and fear fell upon him»; for he was imaging in himself the tossing of the law, and was prefiguring the transfer to the evangelic polity — first takes away the turmoil of fear, and only then begins the good tidings. ⟦s4⟧ For the things spoken were not of fear, but procurers of fearlessness and delight. ⟦s4⟧ Saying what? ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ «Fear not, Zacharias, for thy prayer is heard.» ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ All but crying: Why art thou afraid, O elder? [3332] Why art thou afraid, receiving an answer concerning what thou askest? ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ Why dost thou fear, being redeemed from the burden of the law? ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ Why does it trouble thee, seeing the passing of the shadow? ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ Why does it dismay thee, seeing the removal of the things that are shaken? ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ For fearful indeed are the things announced by me, but not productive of fear in those who hear; and I have great mysteries to tell thee, but it is not fitting that thou, listening closely to them, be held by trouble and fear, but rejoice with me and be glad; for these are productive of gladness and joy. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ The redemption of men is at hand; the raising of the fallen has come; the end of the law has arrived; the season of grace has dawned. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ And before long thou shalt see in thine eyes the sum of these: God the Word made flesh of a Virgin, and born like us men, and rescuing the whole race of mankind. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ And of these things which thou didst desire to see thou shalt not only be a beholder, but thou shalt also become a blessed servant. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ And that thou mayest believe what is said by me, I lead thee by a strange wonder also to faith, telling thee those things which thou didst no longer believe thou wouldst see. ⟦s5+6+7+8+9+10⟧ What are these? ⟦s11⟧ «Thy wife Elisabeth shall bear thee a son, and thou shalt call his name John; and thou shalt have joy and gladness, and many shall rejoice at his birth: for he shall be great before the Lord, and shall drink neither wine nor strong drink; and he shall be filled with the Holy Spirit from his mother's womb, and many of the sons of Israel shall he turn to the Lord their God; and he shall go before in the Spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of fathers to children, and the disobedient to the prudence of the just, to make ready for the Lord a people prepared.»

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 7. ⟦s2⟧ Seest thou what are the glories of John, attested by angelic voices even before his very conception in the womb? ⟦s3⟧ For Samuel too was born of a barren woman, but not of an aged one, nor of a father advanced and old. ⟦s4⟧ Isaac too was born of aged parents, who had passed the season and the power of bearing; but he was not filled with the Holy Spirit from her womb. ⟦s4⟧ But none of these either was adorned with all the other things with which John is adorned even before his birth. ⟦s5⟧ And Samuel was a prophet, and was truly styled a seer, for he saw the things to come as present; and he drank no wine nor strong drink; but he did not dwell in the desert, nor was he nourished on foods strange to men; nor at his birth did he cause joy to many, as John the greatest ran, born beforehand as leader of the joy of the whole world. ⟦s6⟧ Joseph the most wise too was the fruit of a barren woman; and before him, at least, blessed Jacob, who begot him of the barren Rachel; and Joseph was chaste (for he set up against the Egyptian woman the admirable trophy of chastity), but he did not become a general of virginity, as John did; and how great is the distance between virginity and chastity the very dignities of the virtues cry out, and the place better than sons and daughters, which is given there only to those who have honoured [3333] [b: A] virginity in Christ in soul and flesh together, and have become and been imitators of the undefiled Virgin who bore him. ⟦s7⟧ And this the Saviour himself made manifest, plain to all, saying that not all are capable of this — of the gift of virginity, I mean — but only those whom Jesus has prepared as worthy according to divine foreknowledge. ⟦s8⟧ Jacob too was loved by God while still living in his mother's womb; for it was said by a divine voice to her who carried him in her bosom, «Jacob have I loved, and Esau have I hated»; but he did not become full of the Holy Spirit while he was carried in the womb. ⟦s8⟧ And he had the strength to wrestle with God all night long — for a night of ignorance of God was then poured over all — but he had not been able to run before God and to baptize God, which John alone among men dared to do. ⟦s9⟧ For the dawn of the knowledge of God had risen, and thereafter the brightness of the true day was shining; and from the wrestling the unthrown one limped on his thigh, because of those who would be born from him out of his thigh, and could not run swiftly at all, foretelling plainly in the riddle their slowness and sluggishness towards divine grace when it sojourned in life, and the all-destroying lameness that would appear in them from this; which John did not undergo at all, although descended from Jacob as they were; but he becomes a swift runner towards it, and Forerunner, leaving far behind the mysteries of the Jewish unbelief and of night.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 8. ⟦s2⟧ I wished also to bring Samson forward for comparison. ⟦s3⟧ For he too had been born of a barren woman, and appeared as the shoot of prayer to God, and of his own will did not shear his head — «For a razor, it says, did not come up upon his head» — and he wore as long locks certain other divine gifts too; but Delilah, the little harlot, overpowering him nobly even now once more, did not allow him to follow us to John. ⟦s4⟧ And all were great before the Lord, but they do not appear to have turned many of the sons of Israel to the Lord their God; nor did they go before Christ God in the power and spirit of Elias, nor are they shown to have transposed the hearts of fathers to children — of those reared under the law to those born in grace — nor did they guide the Jews of disobedience to the prudence of the just, those justified in Christ, nor did they prepare a people made ready for the Lord when he shone forth on earth, nor did they have things greater than these. ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ For what is greater than to see the Lord in the flesh and to baptize him with waters — and they had their birth from barren mothers? ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ And they flourished with certain other advantages, great if they should be compared with the virtues of others, but very small if they should dare to be measured against the gifts of John. ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ At which, through a surpassing beyond all surpassing, Zacharias too, who was then about to be shown the father of John, struck in mind as at things inconceivable, received the sting of unbelief; and indeed, smitten with it, he said to the blessed angel those words of dread [3336] unbelief: «Whereby shall I know this? for I am an old man, and my wife well stricken in her days»; which it was altogether unfitting for John's father to say. ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ But he spoke them not as the father of the voice crying great and extraordinary things, but as bearing the person of the narrow-voiced and slow-tongued law; for Moses, who wrote it, is recorded as narrow-voiced and slow-tongued: whence straightway it was his, to whom everything was enjoined, also to image in himself the silencing of the Mosaic law, which fell silent when Christ, the great Lawgiver, appeared to us in the flesh, as having willed to become a prefiguring of the law. ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ For he obeyed the angel purifying his silence: «I am Gabriel, that stand in the presence of God; and I was sent to speak unto thee, and to bring thee these good tidings; and behold, thou shalt be silent, and not able to speak, until the day that these things come to pass, because thou believedst not my words, which shall be fulfilled in their season.» ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ And very fittingly he lays voicelessness on him as a penalty, even though he was to become the begetter of the Voice: not only because he consented to become a type of those under the law who disbelieve, but because he did not believe that the voice sent to proclaim these things would come forth from him; and, having disbelieved the coming forth of the voice, he was justly deprived of voice. ⟦s5+6+7+8⟧ And since «By what things a man sins, by these, as the wise Solomon says, is he punished,» that he might learn not to disbelieve the voice coming forth from the desert — for John leapt forth from barren ground, that is, a womb — and [b: C] crying in the desert to the souls of the Jews that bore no fruit of faith, or also to the Church from the Gentiles, as having formerly no fruit both cultivated and nourishing, and able to nourish Christ, after the food at Samaria; for to each of these John in the desert, the great voice and crying great things, has truly cried the saving proclamation, which is able to change each barren desert into fruitful land.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4⟧ 9. For why then did that elder — both priest, and trainer and teacher of the law, and awaiting the consolation of Israel, and ever offering earnest prayer for it — not believe its good tidings, unless he was portraying beforehand in himself the unbeliever under the law? For if he thought him a holy angel who stood by the altar and uttered these things to him from there, why did he not hear what was said with faith, ordering himself for belief by the example of Abraham and Sarah? And yet, even if this were not so, he ought by all means to have believed God when he decreed that he would work something strange and above nature: for what is there among the things that are which does not run together into existence more quickly at the all-powerful command of God? This Job too, the wonderful, being taught by God himself through the voice that persuaded [?] him in the cloud, wondrously cried out: «I know that thou canst do all things, and nothing is impossible to thee.» But if he supposed it some hostile and God-opposed power that spoke to him, why did he seek [3337] [b: A] the assurance of what was spoken, saying, Whereby shall I know this? — knowing that he is a liar from the beginning, and does not know how to speak truth; and that, having lied the first time and been shown [?] wholly in the lie, he will not be at a loss to lie the second time also, so that he may carry off with greater deceits as a captive the one taken by his first assaults. But Zacharias, the father of the great John, was not such a simpleton — away with it! Nor from a root so foolish did this sweetest fruit John grow, who perhaps for this very reason was also fed on honey: because to all who love the choice life he is pleasant and most sweet, and calls and urges them, through a godly pleasure, to the imitation of himself. But the elder, as we said, I think, underwent an ecstasy like that Isaac, that he might show beforehand the silence of the law and the unbelief of those under the law, and foretell the thunder of the Gospel that resounds through all the ends of the world, and the faith of the Gentiles under grace.
