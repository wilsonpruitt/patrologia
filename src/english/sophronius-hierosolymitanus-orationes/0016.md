⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [3315] ENCOMIUM OF THE HOLY ARCHANGELS AND ANGELS AND THE OTHER HEAVENLY POWERS *Of Sophronius, bishop of Jerusalem.* *Theodore Peltanus translator. Compare Biblioth. Concionat. VIII, 226.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O divine hosts of God, free of matter! O intelligences endowed with mind and reason! But it is better to proclaim you, to magnify you, and to invoke you by degrees and in order. O Seraphim, who, adorned with six wings and set in the first rank of the heavenly hosts, set all the others on fire with your ardour: you are they who, because of your unshaken stability and your freedom from bodily concretion, and then because of the eternity communicated to you and your power of knowing and of inflaming, bear before you a certain express image of the divine likeness; you are rightly called secondary lights, proceeding by a wholly blessed emanation from the first light, so that you receive, as far as is lawful, without any medium, the immense and infinite splendour of that threefold and single principality, perfect in all its numbers, and, having received it, make it common to the other minds and intelligences. Since then, because of the power of burning and of purifying in which you are strong, and by which you are wont to consume and purge away all malice and all foulness, you [3316] are designated by the names of burners and warmers, purge, I beseech you, the inward approaches of my soul from the filth of vices; and when the thorny matter that clings to it has been burned and consumed, plant the divine word in my breast; and when the darkness of my heart has been wholly banished and scattered, illumine the inmost senses of my mind.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4⟧ O you Cherubim, full of eyes and four-formed, who, amply and magnificently adorned with inward powers akin to your form and answering to it in fitting proportion — the reasoning power, that is, and the irascible; strength, again, and heavenly loftiness — are full of all wisdom (wherefore also you are called fountains and springs of knowledge): polish me with the knowledge of the divine word, and so kindle me against the old serpent, and carry me up powerfully from the earth on high.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4⟧ O Thrones, kindled with ardour and unapproachable, who are the seat, the chariot and the resting-place of God, Best and Greatest, and are above all humble and abject servitude, as having embraced among you that supreme King of all: deliver me from all fraud and from accursed work, and make me worthy of the glory and majesty of God.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O venerable Dominions, who have received this name for this cause, that you express the divine authority in your office, and receive the communication of heavenly goods truly and properly, without any hindrance at all: grant me that [3317] I may rule over the passions of the soul, which corrupt the mind, and master the snares of the demon, which do grievous harm to the soul, up to the last breath of life.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ O Powers, who, having obtained a name agreeing with your function, can be pressed down by no servitude at all, shaken by no whirlwind, and who have received power and firmness from that sole principality from which all power flows: impart to me power against the visible and invisible enemies who rise up against my soul, and strive to bring my mind into captivity, and labour to overturn my steps, lest they be directed according to God.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O unmoved and manly Virtues, who, because of your strength of soul and fortitude and the unconquered excellence of your might, foreshadow the divine power and force: gird me, weak and infirm, about with divine strength and fortitude, and make me fit to drive back manfully and trample those spiritual serpents and scorpions.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O Principalities, who know not how to be bent or moved, who received both the name and the dignity of principality beyond dispute from the divine principality, which is without any beginning and is the author of every principality, and who are set over the third choir of angels: stand by me, faithful ones, and labour diligently that I may triumph over my most hostile enemies, and resist them fearlessly and without a wound to the end.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O thrice most holy, and supremely to be loved and venerated, prince of the sacred host and minister, Michael, chief of the angels, most worthy of all worship and of all praise and celebration; who wast once the leader of the Jewish people, and with the highest faithfulness didst show it the plain way to better things and things nearer to salvation; and who art one day to sound that huge and dreadful trumpet that scatters its noise far and wide through all things: at whose terrible blast those who had been dead from the beginning of the world will rise again out of the hidden receptacles of the earth; and the earth, whirled like a sling, will be moved from its foundations; and the elements will be dissolved; and the sun will be suffused with a black colour, and darkened after the manner of a sackcloth of hair; and the moon will be wholly turned into blood; and the stars, like unripe figs from a fig tree when they are shaken by a strong force of wind, will fall from heaven to earth; and the whole air, as though covered with foul clouds, will be turned into night; and heaven, rolled up like a book, will pass out of the way; and every mountain, and valley, and island, and city will be moved out of their seats; and the great men, and the mighty, and the captains, and the judges of the earth, and the rest of the peoples will be humbled; and because they will see that the time of most grievous pains, and of groanings, and of gnashing of teeth, and of every kind of punishment, is already at hand, they will swoon for fear and horror. ⟦s1⟧ When, then, all who are still among the living [3318] die in a moment, and as it were in the twinkling of an eye, and are at once changed; when the sea and the rivers and hell, shaken with great dread and fear of the evils impending, give up the dead whom they held; when the just, borne up on the clouds of the air, are caught up to meet Christ the Lord, to live with him for all eternity, but the sinners are sent away to the punishments that are appointed for them; when at last all those wondrous things that are awaited are consummated through the sound which thou art to give: then, O holy arch-satrap, guide of the erring, raiser of the fallen, champion of souls, preserver of bodies, exterminator of demons, and illuminator of the whole creation; then, I beseech thee, then be not loath to stand by me as the faithful patron of my cause.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O most holy Michael, who by the command of God didst bring the Israelite people across through the sea and establish them in the land of promise, and didst arm Jesus Nave against his adversaries, and didst deliver the tabernacle of Moses from the bloodthirsty enemy, and didst illumine the souls of many other saints: enlighten my inward senses too, and illumine the inner eye of my soul; and strengthen my heart, tossed by the waves of this life; and lift up my mind, bent towards earthly things, to heavenly wisdom; and strengthen my slackened hands against enemies practised in war; and make firm my deadened feet, lest they forsake the path that leads to heaven; and raise up my neck, laden with sins; and heal my scars, which breathe out a foul smell and are wholly rotting; and quench the fire of burning lusts and passions; and, last, cure the other troubles of my miseries and labours. ⟦s1⟧ Again, Michael in deed and in name, I pray thee, and adjure thee with all the feeling I can, that when I am about to go out of the course of this life thou appear glad and at peace, and hide me under the honoured covering of thy wings, and, snatching me from the narrow and dark places of the lower world, set me in the places of the wonderful tabernacle, leading me «even to the house of God, with the voice of exultation, and of confession, and of the sound of those keeping festival.» [n: Psal. xlvi, 2.]

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Thou also, most divine Gabriel, most excellent of the angels, who art the most longed-for of the world, and the best giver of gladness and joy; who art the most knowing interpreter of the divine secrets, and the chief minister and steward of all good things; who, finally, art the most august leader and prince of the sacred ministers: come, instruct my mind and my heart, and direct them to all the judgments and justifications of God. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Thou who didst open to Ezekiel [n: Ezech. i, 1 seqq.] at Chobar that wholly divine vision, dissolve the stain of my barrenness. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Thou who by the bidding of God didst loose the womb of Elisabeth, before unfruitful, from the bond by which it was held, and didst make it fruitful, [n: Luc. i, 36.] [3319] flood my unhappy and afflicted soul too with a certain grateful loveliness, and with inward joy and light; thou who didst imbue with great joy, by a glad and saving annunciation, the most blessed and most blameless soul of our most sacred and most pure Lady, the Theotokos Mary, [n: Luc. i, 28.] and didst transform the sadness of Eve, and her mourning, into gladness by the pledge of sure salvation; in a word, thou who didst turn the grief of the whole world into joy, and, the garment of mourning torn apart, didst clothe all with the robe of gladness: thee again and again, O Gabriel, reconciler and giver of true gladness, I ask and adjure, that thou mercifully visit me, weighed down by grievous sickness, and fill me with divine power and joyfulness of soul; and also that, delivering me from the torments of Belial and from the perils of visible and invisible enemies, thou join me, through thy authority and intercession with God, to the company of the just and of the saints, and at last set me safely in those places which abound in all delights.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ O you, all the rest of the angels and archangels, heavenly company, impenetrable crown, most excellent phalanx, immense multitude, numberless army, inaccessible loftiness, incomprehensible greatness, incomparable choir, knowledge beyond all searching, inexplicable joy, pomp supremely wonderful, quantity exceeding every thought, subtlety that knows no measure, agility to which no other is like, order infinitely manifold, unspeakable swiftness, essence that cannot be circumscribed, glory that knows not how to be grasped by the thought of the mind, force greater than every might and strength: you are the ministers of the King of all; you are winds, and spirits, and waters, and Princes, and horses, and armies, and deacons, and apostles, and preachers, and prophets, and evangelists, and interpreters of divine things, and guardians, and presidents, and preservers, and drivers-off, and conductors, and guides on the way, and inquirers, and weighers, and reckoners, and curators, and illuminators, and givers of riches, and luminaries, and lamps, and torches, and mountains, and hills, and clouds, and judges, and receivers, and reporters, and defenders, and contenders, and champions, and divine mirrors, and likenesses, and images, and sailors, and ships, and pilots. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ You are they who cross heaven in most rapid course; who run through the air with most swift and most impetuous motion; who fill heaven and the ether; who stand by every man, none excepted, as guardians and companions; who accomplish the will of the Creator unswervingly; who hear the voice of his words without intermission; who ever grow and advance, and take continual increase (for God alone is he upon whom no change falls); who are called heavenly bodies; who are named minds and tabernacles of God; who are called coals, and whirlwinds, and flames; who are styled initiates, and interpreters of mysteries, and friends of the King of all, and musicians, and also ministers of sacred things, and deacons. [3320] Since, then, you are adorned with such and so great glory, and loaded with so many prerogatives, rightly do I ask and beseech your kindness, O good angels and archangels, free of every stain: to your magnificence, O undefiled spirits, I make supplication, to preserve my life without offence, my hope unbroken, my ways blameless, my love towards God and my neighbour entire and free from every offence. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Lead me, I pray, by the hand, and lead me on, and direct me to every saving path acceptable to God.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Again, O divine spirits, I would have you asked and adjured again and again, that when, at the last gasp of my life, I am compelled to go out of this vessel of clay, and you are to weigh all my actions in the balance, and to reckon up all that I have done, whether openly or in secret, then either all of you, or at least those whom God shall then send from your order to receive my unhappy and wretched soul, may show yourselves gentle and merciful to me, and may deftly lift up the scale which I have weighed down to my ruin with my evil works, and, as ministers of the only merciful God, may be zealous to unburden it by every means and art. And furthermore, in place of good and honourable works, of which I might have conceived hope, set forth in the midst the immense mercy of the Lord, praying the beneficent and merciful God, O most praised and most excellent minds, that through your intercession he may deign to admit all the afflictions, and diseases, and calamities which I have ever borne in life, in compensation for my crimes, and for the remission and pardon of the condemnation and of the uncommon torments which I had justly deserved, and to hold and reckon them in their stead and place: so that, the enemy having once been perfectly overcome and vanquished by divine grace, great joy may arise in heaven over my wretched soul, saved; the thrice most kind Maker calling you together, and saying: «Rejoice with me, friends, for I have found the sheep» [n: Luc. xv, 6.] that once had strayed in the mountains.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6⟧ Finally, when the day of the consummation has come, and the end of the world now presses at the doors, and the heavens tremble, and the firmament totters, and the foundations of the earth are shaken, and the abyss of the abyss stiffens for fear, and the lower world shudders with dread, and the divisions of the immense sea are turned into fire, and all the elements melt like wax before the face of the fire, and those who lie buried in the recesses of the earth are raised up, and the fashion of this world passes away, and the dreadful Judge stands at the very threshold; and you, going before his coming, run through the bounds and ends of the earth, to gather into one all mortals who from the beginning of the world had met death, and to set them all together before the incorrupt Judge, so that each may render account of every work, and of every thought, and of the conceiving of his mind: then, I beseech you (and never, up to the end of my life, shall I cease to beseech you), you who survey all things, and discharge the public ministries of God not without authority, and by this very fact [3321] bear before you a certain royal dignity, that you carry sceptres, and rods, and spears (for by these you plainly show the power of coercing and of punishing in which you are strong): then, I say, then, I beseech you, be present and propitious, and, as princes of that thrice-best King, spare one wretched and unhappy, and through your intercession with God deliver me from that sad and wrathful sentence by which it sends men away into the eternal fire. But on the contrary make me, together with my fellow-workers and kinsmen, whose names and calling he knows who is ignorant of nothing, a partaker of that glad and pleasant calling, and of the life that lacks all grief and wants for nothing, and declare me heir, at last, of the joy that never makes an end.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O Lord, Saviour, God and Word, who holdest dominion on high: who lackest beginning, and containest all things: who art strong in great might, and bringest life to all, fear to all; who didst found heaven and earth, and all that is contained in their compass, by thy nod alone; [n: Esther. xiii, 10.] who first conceived the angels in thy mind, and thy conception was a work: for what thou hadst conceived in mind, that thou didst fulfil and complete by a word alone. ⟦s2⟧ Then with like art and ease thou didst form man from the dust of the earth according to thine image and likeness, [n: Gen. i, 26, 27.] and, having formed him, and made him a little less than the angels, [n: Psal. viii, 6.] and adorned him with free will, thou didst set him as king and lord of all created things; that is, so that he might enjoy those good things which thou hadst prepared for him in paradise, which he did indeed enjoy for a while, until, taken and seduced by the serpent and by his own wife, he transgressed thy divine commandments, by tasting, namely, the fruit of the death-bearing tree: wherefore he fell forthwith from his royal dignity, and, shut out of paradise, incurred a punishment worthy of his deeds, to wit, weeping and a servile condition, sweat, toil and trouble, sadness and pain, and, what is truly the bitterest of all punishments, a just death at last, and dissolution into the earth from which he had first been taken up and brought forth. ⟦s3⟧ But thou, Lord, truly merciful and compassionate, [3322] didst by no means spurn thy own possession, dear to thee beyond all others, which the tyranny of the devil had oppressed; but, being about to fulfil that tremendous mystery which thou hadst determined before all ages, thou didst empty thyself, who art the Father's wisdom, and mind, and Word, and character, and boundary, and image, and seal, and Son, and expressed form. ⟦s4⟧ For thou didst dwell in the chaste Virgin through the Holy Spirit, and didst take from her a birth that exceeds all reason. ⟦s5⟧ For neither didst thou at all loose the virginal enclosures; but, being without flesh, having entered into her in a manner which thou knowest, clothed with flesh thou didst go out from her again, the door of the womb being left closed, as it was before. ⟦s6⟧ Then thou didst undergo the cross, given to drink gall and vinegar mixed together, and pierced with a lance driven into thy side. ⟦s7⟧ Suffering death at last of thy own will, thou didst slay death itself, and didst endow the whole world with immortality; and thou didst restore the fallen and deformed image to wholeness, and didst blot out the old curse which had been brought on through the tree. ⟦s8⟧ Thou therefore, King and Maker of all things, who alone art the architect of things so great and so wonderful, and didst found all things that still exist for the common profit of mortals, and art wont to punish those liable to punishment not without a father's affection: when, sitting on the tribunal, thou art to lead all to judgment, deem me worthy of a standing on the right hand, with all those whose memory I hold in mind. ⟦s9⟧ O overseer of all, and knower of hidden things, consigning to oblivion the crimes which I have at any time committed, open to me the door of the immortal bridal chamber, that is, heavenly glory. ⟦s10⟧ O immortal Bridegroom, make us hear the voice of blessing. ⟦s11⟧ O beneficent one, enrol me in the number of the just, and make us partakers of thy joy, by the intercessory prayers of thy blameless Mother, and of the holy angels, and of the apostles, and prophets, and martyrs, and all the just who have been from the beginning of the world; who with thy Father and the Holy Spirit art blessed now and ever, and to the ages of ages. ⟦s12⟧ Amen.
