⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5⟧ 16. We were saying that the name of John signifies grace of God, since he was sent as the forerunner of this too, dispatched as a good informer. And the grace of God is believed to be the Word of God, who for us that were weighed down with the shames of sin had his manifestation in flesh like ours; and who redeemed us from such shames, and filled us with his own glory and grace. For thus the God and Fashioner and ruler of all shone forth upon all alike — Jews, Greeks, barbarians, the unlettered, the wise, the unwise, slaves, masters — and set before all equally the radiance of his own love for man, even if he was born of the Jews according to the flesh, on account of the promise and converse with Abraham the patriarch, who had his birth from the nations, but begot the race of the Jews. «Or,» says Paul the most wise, «or is God the God of the Jews only, and not of the nations also? For truly God is the God of the nations also; since God is one and only, who shall justify the circumcision by faith, and the uncircumcision through faith»: [3348] [b: A] so then, the manifestation of the divine grace to both of these, equal and alike. Again Zacharias, moved by the prophetic Spirit, brought forward in word and in writing the wonderful name of John, showing by the matching of the two utterances that one grace of adoption will be given to all, to those from the law and from the nations who turn to it, and admit it, and receive it with true faith and purity of soul. «For as many as received him,» it says, «to them gave he power to become children of God, which were born not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God.» For it is quite plain that the Jews were signified by the letter of the tablet, as serving the letter of the law; but the nations by the Word and Spirit of the mouth, as partaking rather of the Word, and having come over by the Spirit to the things of the law: since the law itself too was known to be twofold, the one part having what is fleshly and in the letter, the other what is rational and in the Spirit. This is the cause, interpreting to us this evangelic mystery of John's utterance — twofold at once and mystical — that has been grasped; which we were not able to find from them, but John himself, the unquenchable lamp, flashing and illumining hidden things, showed us. Whence we now wonder more than they did: they of old, for their part, wondering at the things seen alone. And thus John comes forth into life, running before the Sun of righteousness with his leaps, like some morning star in its gleam, both most brilliant and astonishing all by the greatness of his own brilliance; which the great and conspicuous and inseparable advance of the Sun rising straight after him granted him to display, through excess of sparkle and of the utmost light and shining-forth.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5⟧ 17. He, being born, and called John, and having made known by his own name the power of him who was to be born after him of a Virgin, looses the tongue of him who begot and named him — for the law [?] — a tongue that before this was in bondage to the bonds of slowness of tongue; and the voice grants to the heavy-voiced law and to the father a piercing voice, to proclaim the Word who would appear after him, to him who drives away also the heaviness of voice that belonged to Moses the lawgiver. For how was it possible, while the voice was being born before the Word, the Word [?], for him who had begotten it to be held fast in voicelessness? Or for his tongue to be pressed by bonds of speechlessness, when the Word was all but about to come forth and be born? For it is quite plain that after John, the voice, Jesus, the Word, having come, freed the tongue of the law from the bonds of slowness of tongue. For the hidden things of it and of the prophetic promise had been sealed; and he gave it a great and well-measured voice, driving away from it its former seeming voicelessness, and taking out of it, by the manifestation of his own shining-forth, the voicelessness that before invisibly oppressed it. For this reason both things were mystically fulfilled in Zacharias, who was, for his own part, priest and prophet, as clearly imaging each: by the priesthood, the law; and by his aged and old estate, the legal oldness; and by the gift of prophecy, [3349] [b: A] the assembly of the prophetic time. «For his mouth,» it says, «was opened immediately, and his tongue, and he spake, blessing God.» For indeed, when Christ truly appeared on earth to those on earth — who is both the Word and the wisdom of the Father — the seal of the prophetic mouth has been unsealed, and the bond of the legal tongue loosed; and both alike, the law and the prophets, speak openly the mystery of Christ, and bless him as God before the ages, contending together with the evangelic proclamation and thundering with it. For these reasons the gathering of those then present and of those who had their dwelling as neighbours were held fast with great fear; and they spoke about him anxiously. And what then and who John would be, they continually considered: because, at his being born, such wonderful things had happened as we have not understood of any other.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ 18. But I shall say in answer to their consideration: [b: B] since no other like him, up to him, is born of woman, that such wonderful and paradoxical things too should happen. For since he is witnessed to be greater than those among the born of women, for this very reason he was counted worthy to come into so great a glory, that through this too he might glorify Christ, who was to be born after him for us. And not only with this glory through portents did Christ clothe his own Forerunner, but also, being invisibly with him as God, he made him appear great and glorious before all. For the narrative of the evangelic voice has it so concerning these things: «And fear came upon all that dwelt round about them, and all these sayings were noised abroad in all the hill country of Judaea; and all they that heard them laid them up in their heart, saying: What then shall this child be? And the hand of the Lord was with him.» For the hand of the Lord — the Only-begotten Word of God — was in no way barred from being with him in Godhead, and from magnifying him as his first and greatest herald: for he himself is named the right hand of God, and hand, and arm, as of one who does through him all things that have come to be; even if, incarnate, he had been conceived in a virginal womb, and was lying royally as in a bridal chamber. Whence John, being born, was shown surpassing all those born of women, and begot and brought forth a very great fear, not only for those who dwelt with him and were of one mind and longed for the divine manifestation of Christ to us, but also for those who dwell round about and remain outside grace because of their dwelling, hard to tear away, in what is written in the law. For these think that they in some way dwell round about us, receiving the law in part, and keeping in single file with the letter; through which they have also become outcasts from among us, and been exiled from the grace understood as theirs. Those wretches, then, were thus cast out by their own ill-will from the life hidden in the law; but its herald and Forerunner, born with such power, is suckled; and after the breast he goes out into the desert, renouncing in everything the haunts among men, as not worthy of his conduct and manner of life, which was above men. For how could he who had nothing earthbound and [3352] human — no house, no food, no shelter, no friendship, no dealings, no conjunction with kin, no concord with a wife, nothing else of what is done in villages and cities — dwell with men in cities, or dwell in villages? And he goes out not in emulation of Moses the best — even if he is schooled in divine things more than Moses, and returns legislating, and redeems a people gone astray, and guides it well to piety when it was evilly oppressed by Egyptians of the mind; nor in imitation of Elias of old, who wrought great portents on earth — even if he has been called a new Elias, and goes forth in the power and Spirit of Elias, and returns to chasten Herod, the new Ahab, and is persecuted by Herodias, the other Jezebel, and, persecuted, is slain — setting before himself as the aim of his own life neither of these, nor any of those like them; since it is not with those inferior in virtue, but with the better, that the rivalry of the inspired and temperate is wont to be. But if nature did not bring forth, to be seen among men, [?] a man better than John — a male, I mean, on account of the all-holy Virgin and august Mother of God, who is above all human beings, female and male — nor did Christ allow it to be conceived, it is plain that he goes out into the desert as one who will imitate not any men, but the heavenly angels, since he had been counted worthy of their very title.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ 19. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ And justly did he have such a good and God-loving strife with them, the one bodily angel who appeared on earth, as being himself a fellow-partaker of the ethereal dignity, and as one who was to bring to Christ the equal ministry when he was baptized in the streams of the Jordan, and all the other service which, performing it angelically through the whole of his earthly life, he brought in. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ With such desire, then, John, leaving his father's hearth, dwelt in the desert as in some lofty and heavenly place, giving all to see in the riddle that Christ, the grace of God, would leave the synagogue that bore him according to the flesh and was his fathers', and would dwell in the desert Church of the nations; and would do this not for some time, but for ever, and until he appear again a second time from heaven with much glory and power, shown forth to the true Israel. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ «And the child grew, and waxed strong in spirit, and was in the deserts till the day of his showing unto Israel.» ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ For those crowned with the Fathers' acuteness know that Christ, having said to the peoples of the Jews, «Behold, your house is left unto you desolate,» has made the desert Church of the nations his own house, both holding the called as his own body and lying over them in the place of a head. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ For we are the body of Christ, and members in particular, and we have him as head, as the vessel of election says in its teaching. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ He grows, making his own the growth of us, the members, and of the body; and he is strengthened in spirit who strengthens our spiritual strengthening, reckoning it his own because of us, his own. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ And well do the Oracles say that he was in the deserts: for this shows them saying it more expressively, [3353] [b: A] that Christ is and comes to be not in one nation alone, as formerly in Israel only, but in all the nations and in the Churches that have had their constitution from them. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ For thus in many places of Scripture they are named in the plural: now the high mountains, now hills, now springs, and at other times the islands of the nations, and elsewhere ships of Carthage, signified figuratively. ⟦s1+2+3+4+5⟧ Thou, then, O blessed Baptist (for I will call thee Christ's baptist, even if the Word has not yet shown thee doing this), both thus and for these reasons didst take possession of the deserts, as I have been able to understand thee and to tell of thee, bringing them good tidings in some more mystical way of the saving arrival of the divine grace that would soon overtake them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2+3⟧ 20. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ But I, having the purpose of following thee in discourse, am barred and strongly hindered: on the one hand by human weakness ([b: B] for I am not ashamed to tell thee the truth, who knewest it even before our speaking); on the other, because the time of the holy liturgy, as thou seest, has come, and forces us all to hold to it, and does not let us go with thee to the beloved desert; which, even for a short while, is neither desired by thee, nor possible for me, nor fitting for thy festival. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Wherefore, bringing thee my request for pardon, I beseech thee to remain with us in the Spirit, even if in the body thou goest to the desert, and to provide thy help unceasingly to us who unceasingly lay hold [?] of it; for no place bars thee from helping once thou hast nodded assent, present in heaven in the Spirit, and easily surveying us all. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ And grant us to keep this thy august festival with fitting delight, both as is dear to thee, the Forerunner of grace, and as befits thy way of life, well-pleasing to God; and mayest thou so lead and tend the remaining portion of the present life, as the law of the Spirit commands, of which thou thyself hast been set before us in Christ as the type; so that, becoming fulfillers of it by thy inspirations, Christ, the provider of this life, may call us also, when we are parted from the life here, into one of the cities ranked under thee, which Christ promised to give to thee and to the divine men after thee — for I will not dare to say "like thee" — as to one who here too, briefly and in some way, obeyed thy holy ordinances; so that we too, coming to be in them, and joined to thee as shield-bearer and protector and guardian, lawgiver and instructor of the brilliant way of life, and to thine, that is, to those under thee, who as fellow-servants finished their course here, and to thy true disciples, may become sharers of the eternal life laid up for thee, and to Jesus our Lord; through whom and with whom to God and the Father, with the Holy Spirit, be glory, honour, might, unto the ages of ages. ⟦s1+2+3⟧ Amen.
