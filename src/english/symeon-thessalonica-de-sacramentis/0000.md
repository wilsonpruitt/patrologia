[0176] [b: D] *Bishop.* Brother, what is asked is beyond me. But know that, moved for love's sake beyond the bound of caution and of humility, I said for your sake whatever I said to you, trusting in this alone, that I select these things from the divine Scriptures. [0177] [b: A] And so now too, since thou longest to learn about the mysteries of the Church, I will speak to thee about these also, as far as I may have strength, fulfilling the part of love. But do not thou rest content with what shall be said by us; rather, taking whatever thou mayest perhaps find profitable in these things, seek always, and be drawing near to those who have their mind loftier and purer, and asking; and thou shalt not miss what thou longest for.

*Cleric.* For this too, Father, we give thanks, that thou hast given us very many occasions of humility. And may the Lord grant us, through thy holy prayers, not to think more highly than we ought to think, but to think so as to be sober-minded. But make plain to us, we beseech thee, the matters of the mysteries.

[b: B] CHAPTER 33. That the mysteries of the Church are seven.

*Bishop.* Since you seek it, I will speak in Christ according to my power. Seven are the gifts of the Spirit, as Isaiah says, and seven the mysteries of the Church that are wrought through the Spirit. And these are baptism, chrism, communion, ordination, marriage, repentance, and holy oil. And in brief I will make plain to thee concerning each of these also, even though earlier, in the interpretation of the divine liturgy, we spoke of them dimly, as far as there was room.

**62** CHAPTER 34. [ed: The plate carries the subtitle Περὶ βαπτίσματος, "On baptism," which our Greek source omits.]

Baptism, then, regenerates by the Spirit, since we lost the first passionless birth, and we were conceived in iniquities, [b: C] and in sins, as David sings, did his mother conceive each one of us.

CHAPTER 35. On the chrism.

And the chrism puts in the first seal, and that which is according to the image, which we lost through disobedience; and further also the grace which we received in the soul by the divine inbreathing. Wherefore it also bears the power of the Spirit, and is rich in his fragrance, and is a marking and a seal of Christ.

CHAPTER 36. On the divine communion.

And the communion unites us to the Master himself, and we truly partake of his flesh and his blood; and since through eating we died, [b: D] and were parted from paradise and from God, through the communion we take up eternal life again, and casting off corruption we are united to the Immortal, who for our sake became mortal through flesh.

CHAPTER 37. On ordination.

And ordination affords the authority and power of him who made us. And since none of the things that are is without him, and he himself came to lead us to well-being, having taken up this power of his from among us, he afforded it to us through his priesthood; and through it all the rites are wrought for us, and nothing is holy without a priest. And further, having from the beginning established us as rulers of the things that are seen, [0180] [b: A] again in the present, in a better manner, he establishes us rulers through the priesthood. Wherefore David also says, «Thou shalt establish them rulers over all the earth.» For he has delivered to us the keys of heaven.

CHAPTER 38. On marriage.

And marriage is a gift of God's condescension, for the begetting of children, so long as this universe stands together with corruption. For God did not will that the irrational constitution, from a flux and filthy, should be in us; but since we died of our own will, he let the succession of the race work in this way, as also among the irrational creatures, that we might know into what we have been brought down. And this until the Incorruptible, who both died and rose for us, shall raise up [b: B] and immortalize our nature; wherefore he himself also blesses marriage, that the first-fruits of our life may not come to be without a blessing.

**63** CHAPTER 39. [ed: The plate carries the subtitle Περὶ μετανοίας, "On repentance," which our Greek source omits.]

And repentance works our setting-upright again after the fall. And since after baptism there is no other recall, by grace and as a gift and without struggles and toils, except through conversion and tears and the declaring of one's faults and departing from evils, this great gift has been given, in which indeed the habit of the monks also is brought in, as being a pledge of repentance.

[b: C] CHAPTER 40. On holy oil.

And holy oil has been delivered, this too as a sacred rite and a type of the divine mercy, supplied for redemption and sanctification to those who turn back from sin. Wherefore it also affords loosing of sins, and raises up from infirmities, and fills with sanctification. And all these things Jesus Christ our God delivered, and his divine disciples through him. For since we are twofold, of soul and of body, twofold also were these things afforded us by him, since he himself too truly became twofold for our sake, being truly God and having truly been called man, so that by the grace of the Spirit he might intelligibly sanctify our souls, and by the sensible things — water and oil, and bread and cup, and the rest [b: D] that are sanctified by the Spirit — he might hallow our bodies also, and afford entire salvation.

CHAPTER 41. On the resurrection, that is, the raising of bodies.

He, that he may also make us perfect as he fashioned us, will further raise us up also when we die; and he will restore us with the bodies with which we lived and conducted our lives, that we may receive back what we did. Since, then, he will raise us perfect (for he fashioned nothing in vain), neither will he leave our body in corruption; for he made all things to be, and wills that nothing perish, being perfect God, and he alone truly being, and he became man like us, taking a body with a rational and intellectual soul, that he might also cleanse [0181] [b: A] our rational souls, and fill them with the grace of the Holy Spirit, and make our bodies incorruptible and at the same time sanctify them. For this reason, then, he also gave these mysteries in a twofold manner: sensibly, as being from the things that are seen, yet accomplished by the Holy Spirit, and bestowing the divine grace on souls and bodies alike. And he delivered them not in words only, but also in deeds; and the Word of God, having first become flesh in himself, wrought this in his love for man, that, he being for us the first-fruits of good things, all we who are his might receive from him as from a fountain. For for this cause also he became man, that we might be united to him, and that we might be sanctified through him. Because he who fashioned us from the beginning is this same Word of God, and he again it is who refashions us, by the good pleasure of the Father [b: B] and the co-working of the Holy Spirit. For the Trinity, God, who made the whole, also administers the whole, and most of all us who had fallen away, the most precious of things seen and according to the image of God. The Word of God, then, the living image and seal and radiance, takes up into himself his own image, us, the rational living creature. And not through some other creature does he renew us. Since it is not even possible.

*Cleric.* And how, master, was it not possible through an angel also,

*Bishop.* In no way: because every creature is circumscribed, and does not reach everywhere, and does not have by nature the power of saving.
