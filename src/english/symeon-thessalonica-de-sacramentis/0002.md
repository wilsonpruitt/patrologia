And that God anointed him with the Holy Spirit, Peter says again elsewhere. And Paul, «In him, he says, dwelt all the fullness of the Godhead bodily.» This chrism, then, is the grace of the Spirit, all of it; which, as has been said in the baptism, he received in human fashion, as was also seen by John. And this he had always, as God the Word, it being undivided from him; and he had it by grace also as soon as he became man, since his Spirit, connatural and undivided, was always with him, and by the Holy Spirit he constituted for himself that divine flesh; yet he showed it also at the baptism, descending upon him for our sake. Since the pure one also underwent baptism **66** for our sake, that, being baptized, we might [0188] [b: A] receive this. We too, then, of necessity need this spiritual myron, and above all in baptism; and no one will be perfectly baptized who has not also received the myron. For by baptism he is regenerated, and is loosed from his sins by the Spirit; but he does not yet also have the pledges of grace, nor the odour of life, nor the seal of the Spirit. Wherefore, as it is necessary to be baptized, so there is necessity also to be anointed with the myron. And for this reason Peter and John laid their hands on those who had been baptized by Philip, as having received baptism only, and they received the Holy Spirit. Which indeed is the myron. For the laying on of hands afforded the myron, as took place also among the apostles, and through them for many others. [b: B] And then it was the laying on of hands, but now, in place of the hands, it is this, the sanctified and so-called great myron, sanctified not by presbyters, but by patriarchs and bishops with sacred prayers and blessings, who are rich in the power of Peter and John; which indeed, being sent out also to the farthest bounds, has the power of the laying on of hands. And it is necessary that every believer be sealed with this at baptism, that everyone baptized may have the most divine baptism also perfect in himself. For if the Saviour received the Spirit when he was baptized, and those baptized by Philip received the Spirit by the laying on of the hands of Peter and John, that they might not be imperfect and unsealed by the Spirit, so also those being baptized, [b: C] the faithful, ought to have been anointed with this myron in baptism, and not (as the infants of the Latins, or of some others) remain imperfect and unsealed, not receiving the grace of the Spirit, nor being marked with the marking of Christ. For the myron is the seal of Christ in the Spirit. And the priest cries this aloud when he seals with the myron, saying, «The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen» Without grace, then, and unsealed for Christ is he who has not received the myron. And the myron is not simply oil, but that which is sanctified with prayers at the altar by the bishops of Christ, who are rich in the power of the apostles, or rather of Christ himself. Nor is the myron that which comes from holy relics, such as that from the body of the divine and myrrh-streaming Demetrius, [b: D] or of some other of the saints, as is noised abroad also, in the land of the Cypriots, of the great martyr Mamas, or of a holy icon, as of the one called Sittē the holy, or from the oil of the lamps that hang before the holy icons or relics. For these things are venerable, and have divine grace, and afford healings, but not the power of that most divine myron; for this power is its own, and is supplied through the episcopate and the episcopal prayers. And it is not wrought simply, but is placed on the altar at the time of the celebration, on the holy and great Thursday, consecrated with sacred words in prayers at the end of the celebration, and brought to completion with the divine seal of the cross, even as the divine mysteries also are. And just as the bread and the cup that have not [0189] [b: A] been celebrated through the prayers of the priest will not be the body and blood of Christ, neither is that the great myron which has not been celebrated through the most sacred prayers of the most divine bishops. And from this baptism and myron we are both called Christians and are so, and more from the myron, which is also called chrism, since Christ too is so called because he was anointed with the Holy Spirit bodily. And this both Peter says, as we said, and David. «Therefore God, thy God, anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows.» That is, Thee he filled with all the gifts (for thou didst receive the fullness of the Godhead); and thy fellows, as from a fountain, in proportion. And thus the myron, at the baptism, when the Spirit descended, was received **67** by the Saviour. But also when he was about to suffer [b: B] he was anointed with myron, saying that this was for his burial; showing by this that even while dying for us he was living, being God, and had the living energies of the Spirit undivided in himself.

CHAPTER 44. That the Saviour himself performed the celebration.

And the celebration of his all-holy body and blood, it is plain that he himself celebrated it, and has delivered it to us to celebrate. «For this, he says, do for my remembrance.» Because he himself is also a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek (in bread, that is, and wine), in no way ceasing; and rather an eternal priest, because he both sacrificed himself [b: C] willingly through the cross, and sacrifices, and offered to the Father, and brings near. And he lies a perpetual victim, and an eternal propitiation for us; whom also we have as a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God; and we hold fast his confession. And such a high priest truly became us, holy, harmless, undefiled, separated from sinners, and made higher than the heavens, humbled for us sinners.

CHAPTER 45. That the Saviour has been ordained both priest and high priest bodily.

And that he has been ordained our priest and high priest, the Saviour himself made plain, being the Word of God, [b: D] and made flesh for us, and both borne witness to by the Father and ordained [sic: *χειρονοτονηθείς*] by the Spirit, as Isaiah too foretold: «A rod shall rise up from the root of Jesse, and a flower shall come up from the root. And the Spirit of God shall rest upon him,» and what follows. And see the matters concerning the ordination, how he received it, this wonderful and most splendid one; though he was God by nature, and had almighty power, all-working and holy; for he is the living Word of God, holy and almighty; but nevertheless for our sake he has also been ordained bodily by the Father. And first in the baptism itself; for at that time, being baptized and at the same time touched by the hand, he was as one being ordained, John laying his hand on him, and receiving the Spirit; [0192] [b: A] but being ordained in a special way by the Father, the Spirit descending in place of a hand, and the Father himself saying, «This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.» And the second time he received the ordination more splendidly on the mount of the transfiguration, when he himself shone exceedingly with the light of the divine glory, being the Sun of righteousness; and he came to be in the midst of that luminous cloud, which signified the Spirit, which was always with him,
