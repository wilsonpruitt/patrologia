And from the cloud the Father was then perfecting him also according to the flesh, at once bearing witness and being well pleased in him, and proclaiming his own living wisdom a teacher. «For this, again, he says, is my beloved Son in whom I am well pleased.» And he added, «Hear him;» which indeed belongs to teaching. This also [b: B] Christ himself, giving it to the disciples, said, «He who hears you hears me.» Dost thou see also how he has been ordained by the Father in the Holy Spirit?

CHAPTER 46. That Christ also blessed honourable marriage, and wrought it in himself through the Church in the Holy Spirit.

And see also how he, as creator [lat: the Latin gives *prout minister*, "as minister"], established marriage and blessed it. For thou hast learned **68** that he came to a marriage in Cana. But he was also born of one betrothed to a man [lat: the Latin gives *ut homini socius permanens*, "that, remaining a companion to man," and has no betrothal], that he might sanctify our birth.

[b: C] CHAPTER 47. That Christ preferred virginity to marriage.

Yet he preferred virginity to marriage, since his Father too is virgin, begetting him without flux and without body. And the mother also, who according to the flesh bore God himself made flesh, both was a virgin, and remained so, and remains, having brought him forth without corruption, since she also conceived without seed. But consider also his marriage, wonderful and truly virginal, and divine. For the beautiful bridegroom desired our souls, and loved us, as he said; and for love of us he laid down his life, and gave himself to us as a dowry, and betrothed us to himself in his own spotless blood, his pure Church. And this Paul, the good bride-giver, cries aloud, «I have betrothed, he says, [b: D] you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ. And the matters of this marriage of the splendid and beautiful Son of the king he himself sets forth in the Gospels, the bridegroom fair in beauty above all the sons of mortals, as being pure and blameless. Thus he wrought the matters of marriage also in himself and through himself.

CHAPTER 48. That virginity is the marriage of Christ.

Wherefore him who is able and has room to be wedded to him, and who has made himself a eunuch for the kingdom of heaven, as having room to receive this Word in himself, the pure and all-holy one splendidly receives, and is united to him, and as to one pure [0193] [b: A] lets himself be seen, and dwells wholly in him but if not, he blesses the marriage that is in him, and does not turn away from the begetting of children. «Through this, for the most divine Paul says, some are able to be saved.» «And they shall be saved, he says, through childbearing.» Because Christ himself also became a child, though impassibly; and he was praised by children, and blessed children and took them in his arms. But fornication and adultery he turned away from, even to a glance and a movement of the heart. And the Sodomites he both burned up from the beginning, and teaches that they are still punished. For he says that it will be more tolerable in the day of judgement for Sodom and Gomorrah than for those who have not received the preaching. So that for such men too, together with the ungodly, the punishment is eternal. But having thus done and taught the matters of marriage, concerning repentance and [b: B] confession he did something in himself.

*Cleric.* Concerning the former matters, holy master, we have been sufficiently profited, having heard about necessary things, and above all how he has been ordained who alone by nature is strong in all things, and alone is holy, and how he was wedded, and in all-holy fashion came to marriage, the spotless and pure one, joined in holy fashion to the holy in divine and fervent love; but how then the sinless one should also come to repentance, we are at a loss.

[b: C] CHAPTER 49. That the Saviour also showed the matters of repentance through himself.

*Bishop.* Thou hast spoken well. And he does not come for his own sake, but for ours. For he himself took our iniquities, and bore our diseases. He did not, then, repent, but he taught the matters of repentance. And this he says first, knowing how prone we are to slip, «Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.» For how would he repent who alone among men was blameless? **69** or rather, who alone was holy and pure? But he taught repentance for our sake. «For I came not, he says, to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.» Wherefore he did not refuse to eat and drink with publicans and sinners. And on this account he is blamed by the Jews. And he receives harlots gladly. And [b: D] indeed they touch his feet, and pour myron upon his head, and wash his feet with tears, and wipe them with their hair. And he does not send away the Canaanite woman, a Greek. And the woman taken in adultery, not condemning her, he receives, saying only that she should not sin; and the false accuser and extortioner who paid back his unjust gain readily and manifold; and the covetous man and publican who left off gathering taxes; and the murderer, and that a robber, by a single word upon the cross; and him who denied three times, and that his first disciple, as having only wept, and having thrice confessed him again in love; and the blasphemer and persecutor, Paul, who turned to a zeal inspired by God. And through all these things, and the parables he spoke, of the prodigal and [0196] [b: A] the rest, he set before us and practised the matters of repentance.

CHAPTER 50. That Christ also wrought the works of repentance for our sake.

And in himself too he wrought the works of repentance, not owing them, that he might show us the way of repentance and humility. For he fasted, and prayed, and lived cheaply and humbly and continently, nourished on barley loaves and small relishes. And often he appears without loaves, but for the sake of those with him multiplying the few through [b: B] blessing, and going on foot, and living as a stranger. and not having (as he said) where to lay his head; and enduring insults and revilings and stonings; and not returning evil, but rather doing good; and at the last betrayed by a friend; and suffering at the hands of his own servants, for whom he was contending; and bearing it when slandered; and spat upon, and struck with fists, and scourged naked, and condemned, and put to death upon the cross as a condemned man, with robbers, the Maker of all things. Whence he was also called a curse, that he might loose the first curse in us, and afford a blessing. And at the last put to death, that, as to the living, so to those in Hades also he might grant the gifts of repentance. For there too he saves those who believed. And having risen, he awarded to the whole inhabited world the [b: C] resurrection and refashioning through repentance. For through it the faithless and unclean, turning to the faith and holiness of Christ, and to chastity and virginity, were seen to be equal to angels; and up to now Christ saves, both drawing the faithless to faith, even if they have wrought countless dreadful things, granting forgiveness as a gift through baptism, and he saves again the faithful who sin, who come to him through confession, through tears and contrition, mercy, and other good works according to their power.
