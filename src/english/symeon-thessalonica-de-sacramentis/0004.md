[0197] [b: A] CHAPTER 52. That the monastic habit too is comprised in repentance. And why it is called the angelic habit, and the garment of repentance. And what its power is.

In this repentance, indeed, the most sacred habit of the monks is also comprised, which indeed both is and is called angelic, as imitating and professing their purity, poverty, hymns, prayers, obedience and cleanness. And it is called the garment of repentance, as mournful, as humble and cheap, as unadorned and broken off from all human love of finery, as not only unmixed with all worldly thoughts and words and deeds, but also advancing toward the dissolution and flight of worldly affairs. [b: B] For it is a symbol of the life above the world, and it teaches the corruptibility of the things that are seen, and the transience of all things among men; and it practises the philosophy that is above, and philosophizes, and remembers death and the end of the things here. Wherefore it is also black, since it is mindful of death and mourning, and does not live in this life, but desires another life, the incorruptible, and presses on to run toward that Wherefore also if anyone is a true monk, according to Paul, he both loves Christ here, and nothing can separate him from the love of Christ; and he desires to depart, and to be with Christ. And this he shows through his works, living as an exile in deserts and mountains and monasteries for Christ's sake. And he hastens to be one with [b: C] Christ, that Christ with the Father and the Spirit may dwell in him. Wherefore he also imitates the whole life of Christ, and is humbled, and becomes poor, and is subject, and takes thought for none of the things here, and is crucified to life, and professes to keep virginity, and to possess nothing, and to give himself to fastings and prayers and supplications, and to abide in these and such things until death, and to bear all things for Christ's sake. All which indeed are works of repentance. And every believer ought to persevere in these, save only virginity, which belongs to the monks. For this only the monks owe, in a special way; but also those in the world not yet yoked to a wife, and everyone who has professed it; but also every believer, and above all he who has fallen into sins (and who is he that has not fallen at all?) [b: D] ought to walk in the footsteps of the Lord, and to carry out the works of repentance. Which things the Baptist also, who first, together with Elijah too, prefigured the habit, taught, «Bring forth, he says, fruits worthy of repentance. And repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand;» and he set before each the conduct of life that suited him.

CHAPTER 53. That the Saviour also showed the holy habit through himself.

And our Saviour and God, having afforded this great gift of [0200] [b: A] repentance, and having proclaimed it through the prophets and the Forerunner, and through himself and his disciples, afterwards also showed and delivered the holy habit likewise through the apostles and himself, «If anyone wills, he says, to follow after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.» And so that no one may take this only of all who believe, that all the faithful ought, even unto death, for the faith **71** to take their stand, being bound to follow Christ, who for the good confession of the Father and of himself and of the Spirit bore witness under Pontius Pilate, but rather that this precept is also for those who are bound to work the martyrdom according to conscience, which is the life according to [b: B] Christ, and above all, in a special way, the conduct of life of the monks; hear what he says to Peter, when he had said, «Lo, we have left all, and have followed thee.» For he promises, saying, «You who have followed me, in the regeneration, when the Son of man shall sit on the throne of his glory, you also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.» Which indeed belongs to the episcopal grace, which he afforded to the disciples. Then he adds, «And everyone who has left houses, or brothers, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my sake and the Gospel's, shall receive a hundredfold, and shall inherit eternal life.» Which is plainly the life [b: C] of the monks. And the word is at work also in martyrdom, for many, leaving all things, died for Christ; but this is made manifest particularly in the conduct of life of the monks, which the Saviour himself also lived, and his disciples: he himself living as a virgin, and being poor, he the rich one, and praying on the mountain, and being subject to the Father unto the death of the cross, and not doing his own will, but the will of the Father who sent him, which he kept even to the passion; «Nevertheless not my will, he says,» that of the flesh, that is, «but thine be done,» that is, the one divine will of the Father and of himself; and elsewhere too, giving the life of the monks, «He who loves, he says, father or mother more than me is not worthy of me;» and «He who loves son or daughter more than me is not worthy of [b: D] me.» And «Whoever does not renounce all that he has cannot be my disciple;» and to the young man, «If thou wilt be perfect, sell thy possessions, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven. And come, follow me;» which belongs not to martyrdom, but to the monastic life; and to him who spoke of going away and burying his own father, what does he say who teaches «Honour thy father and thy mother?» Raising us to the more perfect, the godlike life of the monks, he exhorts us here to disregard even our parents, and that in the very moments of need. Why? Because God who made us is to be honoured before all. And if for his sake we wish to live according to him, there is no need for us to be anxious about our own according to the flesh; because, being [0201] [b: A] provident, he will make us, who follow him purely and genuinely, equal to angels, among whom there is nothing of passionate attachment, but only the most passionless love. And those whom we overlook for his sake he will provide for through others.

CHAPTER 54. What is meant by «Let the dead bury their own dead.»

Wherefore he also prevented that man, saying, «Let the dead bury their own dead.» For he led him up to incorruption, and joined him to the immortal angels, shown to be their imitator through his freedom from attachment.
