Thou art not dead, he says; wherefore be not anxious either about dead fathers who are being dissolved.

[b: B] CHAPTER 55. [ed: The plate carries the subtitle Τί τὸ, «Οὐδεὶς βαλὼν τὴν χεῖρα αὐτοῦ ἐπ᾽ ἄροτρον.», "What is meant by «No one having put his hand to the plough»," of which our Greek source keeps only the last word.]

And to another, too, who wished to bid farewell to those in his house, that is, to his wife and the rest, what does he say? «No one having put his hand to the plough and turned to the things behind is fit for the kingdom of the heavens.» Thou hast begun a husbandry, he says, **72** not a human one, but thou art tilling in heaven. Thou wilt not be fit if thou turnest toward the things of the moment. And learn also what thou art getting in exchange for what: for thy house and those in the house, the kingdom of the heavens. We find, then, that the Lord both lived the way that is according to the monks, and delivered it. And among the apostles this life existed; wherefore he also exhorted them not to have two tunics, nor staff, or wallet, or shoes, and the rest, which belongs to the monastic manner of life. [b: C] And it was not simply with words alone and prayers that he strengthened the work toward this, as also toward teaching; but blessing them as well, he sent them out. And in sending them out he afforded them power against unclean spirits, and to heal every disease and every infirmity, which was not only among the apostles then, but also among the saints afterwards, and most of all among the ascetics. And that there was a rite from the beginning, and a mystery of monastic perfecting, and that from the apostles themselves, the great Dionysius bears witness to this, writing, after the other sacred rites, also the mystery of monastic perfecting. They err, then, who say that the habit is a rather recent thing, and not given by the Saviour or by his apostles. [b: D] For it has been shown that Christ our God too had delivered this, and his disciples, and that there was a mystery of the perfecting of the monks delivered by them, and a clothing in a habit other than the worldly habit. For this too the divine Dionysius says. And as for what is written of the holy Pachomius too in the sayings of the Fathers, that the habit was given afterwards by an angel, it has been written well and truly. For certain signs of the habit are given, which have a more perfect teaching beyond the former habit. For since little by little the things of piety both grew and advanced, and the good orders of the Church became greater, and the things of the sacred service and of baptism were composed more splendidly, and [0204] [b: A] sequences and orders of psalmodies and prayers, by the holy men of each age who were moved by the Spirit, the things of the habit of the monks also were shown to Pachomius by an angel, toward greater good order and gravity. And of the tunic, a more reverent clothing; then the girdle and the analabos; next the cowl, and the pallium, and sandals and the mandyas, affording a symbolic sense of certain divine things. Which indeed are seven, as the vestments of the bishop also are, bearing witness to the most perfect life, from the seven gifts of the Spirit. But concerning these we will speak more plainly, whenever we speak also of the things done at each mystery.

[b: B] CHAPTER 56. That the mystery of the holy oil, that is, of the oil of prayer, was given by the Saviour.

And now it is necessary to speak also of the seventh of the mysteries, of the holy oil: how this too exists as delivered from our Saviour, and he himself was anointed with it, not for his own sake, but for ours, as he also used the rest of the mysteries; and what power it has. He delivered it, then, when he sent out his disciples two by two before his face. Who also, going forth, he says, preached that they should repent. Seest thou how this oil too perfects the things of repentance? And they cast out many demons. And they anointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them. [b: C] Behold, the oil, being sanctified, is given to those who repent. And it heals those who are sick, and cures not bodies only, but also souls. And concerning this the Brother of God bears witness, «Is there, he says, any sick among you? let him call for the presbyters of the church, and let them pray over him, having anointed him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith shall save the one who is ill, and **73** the Lord shall raise him up. And if he have committed sins, it shall be forgiven him.» Here another opinion too of the innovating Latins is overthrown. For these say, The holy oil ought not to be given to those who possess hope of life, but to the dying only, since it affords remission of sins; [b: D] both thinking and acting contrary to the apostolic words, here as in all things. For while the Brother of God cries, «And the Lord shall raise him up,» these say that it is to be afforded to those who are not being raised up, and they confine the remission of sins to the oil alone; although indeed this remission of sins is granted always by confession also, and by this holy oil, which is celebrated at all times, and by the communion of the body and blood of Christ and our God, to those only who approach with repentance. And that the Saviour was also anointed with oil for our sake, one may learn from the myron with which the harlot who came to him anointed him: not Mary of Lazarus, near the passion, who was also chaste, and that myron was for his burial, as he said; as also the woman in the house [0205] [b: A] of Simon the Leper, if indeed she was another and not Mary — for this too took place at the time of the passion, and of this too he says that it is for his burial; but that which the harlot written of in Luke brought, who also wetted his feet with her tears, and anointed them, and wiped them with the hairs of her own head; which indeed is the oil of repentance too, fittingly brought to him by the sinful woman, through which she also, being anointed through the hairs with which she wiped, received the remission of her many sins, as the Gospel itself also clearly sets forth. For the Lord speaks to Simon, who had the house, first the parable of the two debtors, of whom the one owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty; and, when they had been forgiven, which of the two will love more him who forgave the debt? [b: B] And when Simon had said, «That he to whom he forgave the more,» the Lord said, «Thou hast said well.» And turning to the woman, he said to Simon, «Seest thou this woman? I entered into thy house, thou gavest no water upon my feet; but she has wetted my feet with tears. With oil thou didst not anoint my head; but she has anointed my feet with myron. For which cause I say to thee, Her sins, which are many, are forgiven, because she loved much. But he to whom little is forgiven, loves little.» Seest thou what this holy oil signifies? Having sinned, we approach divine men; and repenting, we make the confession of the things in which we have stumbled. At whose bidding we offer holy oil to God as a type of his mercifulness and gladness, [b: C] in which also the divine and glad light of grace flashes forth. For we offer lights also.
