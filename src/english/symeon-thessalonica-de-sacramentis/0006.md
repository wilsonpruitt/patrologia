CHAPTER 57. Why there is oil both in the holy and great myron, and oil in the oil of prayer.

And how is there oil both in the divine and greatest myron, which bears the seal of the Spirit, and oil in the oil of those who repent and of the sick? Because both there and here the oil has a type of the divine mercy. For there, in the myron, it signifies salvation for us, and the redemption from death, and the seal of the life-giving and glad Spirit, [b: D] through whom also we were delivered from the flood of sin. Wherefore also in the flood in the time of Noah a dove, as a type of the Spirit, carrying a twig of olive in its mouth, **74** made salvation plain. And in the flood of sin, too, at the baptism of the Lord the Spirit came down in the form of a dove, bearing witness to the divine mercy and to the grace. And here too, as a type of the divine — as we have said — mercifulness and compassion toward us, this oil, being offered, is sanctified, and through it we draw the mercy of God upon ourselves. Are these things sufficient for the demonstration of the sacred mysteries of the Church, or have you need of more also?

[0208] [b: A] *Cleric.* They are sufficient, master, and we have no need of others. But we beseech thee to set forth each of the mysteries, and to unfold to us the sacred symbols, and to teach us what the power of each of the things celebrated is.

*Bishop.* This is beyond us, as we have said, brother, and belongs to a power and a wisdom more lofty. For if Dionysius and Maximus, with a reserve and a reverence so great as cannot be told, undertook to set their hand to such sacred matters, how then will it be possible for us to interpret such things? But since confidence toward God can do all things — for he himself affords to those who believe according to their faith, and supplies also on account of love, because he is also love — for the rest we too, speaking on account of your love, [b: B] believe that we shall receive a word in the opening of our mouth. And behold, through obedience, in the love of Christ, we set our hand to accomplish your request. And concerning each of the sacred symbols we will speak what we have learned from the most sacred Scriptures. And whatever our Fathers taught us we will tell, so far as we have had strength to know. And first indeed we speak concerning the divine baptism; since this too is the beginning of the mysteries that come to us. But because a fleshly birth comes to us even before being baptized, we must speak also of the prayer that is celebrated first at it. For since, having been born in the flesh, we are thus guided to the regeneration that is through the Spirit, the Church of Christ, as being spotless and sanctified in Christ, [b: C] at once cleanses among the pious our sinful birth, which is from the flesh.

CHAPTER 58. Why at the birth of the infant a prayer is made by the priest.

And when the infant has been born of a pious woman, the priest comes, and glorifies God, giving thanks that a man has been born into the world; and having sealed it, he blesses the child that was born, and asks that it may continue, and receive baptism and the chrism. And praying for the mother the things that are for salvation, he imparts to her and to the women with her grace and sanctification, and grants leave to do their works [lat: the Latin gives *facultatem praebet sanctificare opera*, grants leave to sanctify works], and in nothing at all to be hindered, [b: D] or to partake of defilement, or to waver at the envious phantasms of the evil one, since they have ministered to the birth that is of sin and from pleasure, which indeed some say is a prelude of corruption and of death, and so it is.

CHAPTER 59. A setting-forth, part by part, of the things celebrated before baptism, and in baptism, and after baptism.

But having also sealed water with the marking of the cross, as a prelude of the divine baptism he sprinkles the house. And having sealed the infant also on the forehead, on account of the mind, and on the mouth, on account of the word and the breath, and on the heart, on account of the vital power, so that it may remain guarded for the [0209] [b: A] saving baptism, having thus dismissed them, **75** he departs. And on the eighth day the infant is brought to God by certain persons, and before the gates — for it is not yet sanctified by baptism — the priest seals it again on the forehead, and the mouth, and the breast; and gives it a name through a sacred prayer, whatever those who bore it may wish, with which it is also baptized.

CHAPTER 60. That on the eighth day too the Lord received the name, being called Jesus.

Just as this took place in the Lord also; for on the eighth day, being circumcised, he was called Jesus. But we do not have circumcision; for the Saviour has fulfilled the things of the law on our behalf, and has rescued us from the bondage of the law. [b: B] And he has given another circumcision in the whole of nature, the divine baptism, which does not cut flesh, but cleanses sins. And each of the infants is baptized in its own name, as Jesus the Christ was. Not, however, are all baptized John and Mary, as some of the simpler and unlettered folk say. On the eighth day, then, circumcision was for the Jews. But now we the faithful receive the name on it, because the eighth is a renewal. For when the seventh is fulfilled, which this life possesses, revolving through seven of the days, it begins again on the eighth. Which is a sign of the resurrection, and a prelude of eternal life. For on the eighth the Lord also rose again, and we hope for the last day, that of eternal life. Which too shall be without end, as we are taught. [b: C] On this eighth, then, the Jews were circumcised as to the males only, as being imperfect, sealed on the member of pleasure, themselves too bearing witness beforehand to the resurrection by the eighth, and signifying by being circumcised that the pleasure of the flesh shall cease at some time, and life shall be incorruptible, and not wrought through the flesh; but not understanding this, nor perceiving the true circumcision, the putting-off of sin, which is the holy baptism. For this teaches the being wholly incorruptible and unharmed, and not maimed in any respect; for he who is circumcised in the flesh has been maimed, and does not carry about the entireness of nature; and further, that our being born from pleasure is not passionless. Wherefore this too has been loosed, the being born from pleasure. [b: D] And there shall be given to us through baptism to be born not of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God. And this is to be regenerated through the washing of rebirth. Whence on the eighth day too the infant receives the name, just as the Saviour was called by the saving name, Jesus; and having been sealed, and having received a sacred prayer, as one brought to the Lord, and signed with the cross on the forehead, as I said, for the intellectual part, on the mouth for the rational part, and on the heart for the life-giving part, and written in the eternal book through the name that was laid upon it, it returns to the mother.
