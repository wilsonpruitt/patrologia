And on the fortieth day it is brought again by the mother to the temple, and this is presented to God as a gift. For the priest, standing before the doors of the temple (for it is not lawful to [0212] [b: A] enter before the prayer), and having sealed the mother together with the infant, and sanctified them with prayers, and having afforded to the mother the cleansing from the birth that is pleasure-laden and defiled — she having completed the forty days after the bearing, in which the infant too was perfected in her and began to leap; and having given her the entrance into the temple, she being until then not worthy to enter, nor to have communion with what is pure — himself taking the child into his arms, figuring the part of Symeon, who took the Lord, an infant, in his arms, cries aloud the «Now lettest thou depart,» the priest praying that the infant be loosed from sin, and see the light, Christ, the revelation of the Gentiles and the glory of the new Israel, **76** having itself also been born by the Spirit, and seeing God face to face. [b: B] And if the infant has been baptized, he brings it in even into the altar, and carries it round about, as though making an obeisance too, showing that it is an offering to God; and it does obeisance to him who made it. But if the child has not yet been baptized, standing before the rails, and making an obeisance at the altar through the infant, and handing it over to the mother, he dismisses them. From this point, then, the infant is a catechumen; and the mother has received leave both to enter the temple, as having been cleansed, and to partake of the mysteries, whenever she is ready and wishes. And these things are so.

CHAPTER 61. A contemplation of the things celebrated in the divine baptism.

Learn now the things of the most sacred [sic: *ερωτάτου*] baptism; and first, that there is no set time of days or hours for it, lest at any time the infant should depart unenlightened. [b: C] But if there be fear of death, all the things done according to the form are done at once, upon its being born; but with attention, and by a priest both reverent and sober [lat: the Latin gives *nisi quod abest oratio atque pius sanctusque sacerdos*, except that the prayer and a pious and holy priest are lacking]; and if indeed the time allows, and there is no fear of death, at once after the sacred service, that the infant may also partake of the mysteries. And concerning this Dionysius says that all the mysteries perfect us by the communion. For this is the end of all. And the priest ought indeed both to study the sacred prayers well and to say them, and with attention, and slowly, unless there be some necessity, reading them in the hearing. For we have heard from the Fathers that those who are often terrified by phantasms suffer this for this reason, because the priests who baptized them did not say [b: D] the exorcisms with attention, and the other sacred prayers. If, then, there is leisure, one ought to say the exorcisms from the beginning, and the other prayers, not once only, but many times. For indeed in the Euchologion it is written that they be said eight or ten times, because there was also an ancient custom of the Church that these were said to those being baptized on each day, for seven days, by the catechizing priests, and on the eighth again by the bishop, or by the priest who was about to baptize; and so they were baptized by the bishop or the priest. But we have seen reverent priests saying these three times without omission, and so baptizing. And the most divine baptism is done thus. He who is about to be baptized is brought, if an infant, held by a woman, with the sponsor present; but if he is of age, [0213] [b: A] alone, since he must also come of his own will, and the sponsor with him. For he is his surety toward Christ, that he will keep the things of the faith, and live as a Christian; and the name too shows this. For ἀνάδοχος, sponsor, is as it were one who takes him up into himself.

CHAPTER 62. That the sponsor of him who is being baptized must be orthodox and pious.

Here indeed one must also take heed to make sponsors of lovers of piety, and almost teachers of the faith. But I have heard of something exceedingly out of place and grievous. For some call persecutors and insulters of the faith, godless men and heretics, to the sponsoring (alas!) of their children, for some human reason; and these set the mystery at nought; and they do not enlighten the children, [b: B] but rather darken them. And the priest who ministers is shown to be a partaker with those who are strangers to God. And how would the blasphemer against God teach piety? But what communion has light with darkness? Or what part has a believer with an unbeliever, as it is written? He who does such things is condemned; and he who receives the infants of the impious, unless these first confess that baptism is holy and divine, and cleansing of sin, **77** and regenerating in Christ, and that he who receives it is a Christian (for if this is so, the one will confess that the infant will be faithful, while he himself hides under some fear), it is good to receive and sanctify the infant, lest it should also be taken thus by God. [b: C] The one being baptized, then, stands with the sponsor, showing that he has come of his own will, and has the one who stands surety. And when the priest has blessed, for one must give thanks to God from the beginning and always over the salvation of him who was lost, at once, while he is naked and uncovered, with a wrapping only, the priest breathes upon the catechumen three times in the shape of a cross. And through the breathing-upon he rekindles that first inbreathing in Adam, since, «And he breathed into his face, it says, the breath of life.» And the priest represents Christ God. Whence it is also good to baptize and to inbreathe after the communion of Christ. And he breathes upon him three times on account of the Trinity, because the grace and the operation are common to the Trinity; and in the shape of a cross on account of him who was incarnate for us, and suffered in the flesh, Christ, one of the Trinity, [b: D] and set up the victory by the cross. Wherefore also the cruciform figure signifies his sacrifice, both humiliation and victory, because through it he loosed the pangs of death, and abolished him who had the power of death, the devil, and triumphed over the most wicked opposing powers. By which cross we too conquer the devil in Christ, and turn the demons away. And the cross is a sign of the Saviour, and a seal, and a trophy. Having, then, breathed upon the catechumen three times, the priest, having also sealed him three times again with his hand with this cruciform sign, on account of the «Thy hands have made me and fashioned me,» both on the forehead, as I said before, for the intellectual part, and on the mouth for the rational part, and on the heart for the vital part, lays [0216] [b: A] his hand upon him, which is sanctified and fashions in the Spirit; and he pronounces over him the sacred prayers. But before breathing upon him and sealing, as has been said, he frees the catechumen of his girdle and unclothes him, and presents him bareheaded, and with his feet bare, looking toward the east. What does he show in this? That he who approaches God, and now looks toward the true light, which the east signifies (for thence comes the light), must be free of the passions, and loosed from unbelief, and stripped of all wickedness, and a stranger to the garment of sin, and showing forth his servitude to God.
