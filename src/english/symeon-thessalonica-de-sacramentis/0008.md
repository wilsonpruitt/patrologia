For this reason, then, he stands naked. And further, because man became naked through sin, [b: B] and lost the God-woven garment, this figure of nakedness bears witness to it. For since he comes to cast off the filth of sin and of unbelief, and to become wholly new, and to put on the form of the new Adam, and to return to what is better than what was before, he shows the nakedness, and bears witness to the shame; and standing thus he is cleansed, seeking the re-formation. But that we may not, by lengthening the discourse, produce satiety in the ears, let us now speak summarily about the matter in hand. The bishop and priest who baptizes figures the Lord himself, who works all things in all, since he also bears his power — him who also fashioned us before, and refashions us again; [b: C] and the one being baptized figures the Adam who had fallen away, since his approaching too shows the willingness of his purpose. And if it is an infant brought by some faithful person, the being brought signifies in faith and not by compulsion; and he who undertakes for him signifies the one who stands surety, both as to faith and as to life. The putting off of clothes and the being loosed, standing naked with a loincloth, signify the casting away of all wickedness, and being freed from the bonds of sin, and coming to Christ naked of the things of error and corruption; and that he became naked when he went astray, having lost the former **78** robe, which he now comes to put on more splendidly through baptism. And he looks toward the east, seeking the true light, which is the Trinity, and the one of the Trinity, Christ, incarnate, who came from Thaiman, that is, from the east; and also the paradise, [b: D] from which he fell, planted toward the east. And he is breathed upon by the bishop on account of the first breathing-upon; for he is being renewed. And he is breathed upon three times, on account of the divine operation of the life-giving Trinity. For by it he was fashioned, and is refashioned. And he is sealed by the bishop's hand by means of the cross, because the hands of God fashioned us, the Son and the Spirit. And the cross is a sign of Christ and a trophy. For having conquered through it, he shattered the demons; and now, when the cross is traced, the demons flee from it. And he is sealed first on the forehead, that the one being initiated may be sanctified in mind, error having been put to flight. And second the mouth, that he may be sanctified in his word, and confess the truth. And third the breast, that he may be pure in heart, and see God, [0217] [b: A] and hide nothing, and live in him, and move, and abide. After this the bishop blesses God, because it is fitting to bless him for the finding of what was lost, and for the salvation of man. Then the laying on of a hand, on account of the grace that is imparted and the refashioning. And the first prayer is in the name of the Trinity. For by it he is committed to the only God, and through it he is sanctified. And the prayer entreats that the name of the Trinity remain undenied in the one being baptized, and that he be perfected by the divine baptism and chrism, and by the sacred communion. And the second prayer is an exorcism, scaring off and pursuing the devil and all his operation by the dread and divine names and mysteries. And it drives him out of the man, and his demons. [b: B] And it charges him to flee from him, and not to molest him. Likewise the third too, being itself also an exorcism, drives out the demons by the divine name. And the fourth prayer, again, is one offered up to God, asking that he wholly drive out the evil one from the divine handiwork, and establish him in the faith. Whence he at once makes the fifth prayer also, which is more necessary, and through it he dedicates the one being baptized to God. In which prayer too he again breathes upon him a second time; which is the firmer work, the re-quickening and the renewal being shown by this. And he seals him three times again on the forehead, and the mouth, and breast, on account of his mind and word and life; [b: C] and because the Trinity, our God, is mind and word and Spirit, which re-quickens him and gives that which is according to the image, which he lost. «And drive out from him, it says, every evil and unclean spirit, hidden and lurking in his heart.» For the demons love to dwell in the souls of the unbaptized, wishing to lead them astray and defile them. And they have this power from that transgression. Wherefore they dwell openly in the hearts of the uninitiated and unbelieving, and both defile them and stir them up to the things hateful to God. And at the end of the prayer the renunciation and the joining take place thus. The bishop turns the one being baptized as toward the west, the darkness being shown by this, in which the devil is. And he bids him raise his hands up, it being shown [b: D] that the one being baptized has nothing of the things of the evil one hidden within himself; and that he begins to imitate him who was crucified for us. And he is bidden by the bishop to say, «I renounce Satan, and all his works,» and what follows. And the one being baptized makes the renunciation of the evil one, and the perfect denial of his things. And this he says three times, for the confirmation of the renunciation of the enemy; and because he flees from the evil one with his whole soul; and further also for the honour of the holy Trinity, which confirms **79** and perfects all things. And having asked again three times, «Hast thou renounced Satan?» and the one being baptized having agreed three times, and said, «I have renounced,» [lat: the Latin gives the reply in the plural, *Abrenuntiamus … Abnegamus et rejicimus*, we have renounced … we deny and cast off] that is, I have denied and cast off the evil one — for the renunciation of evil must be manifest and firm — [0220] [b: A] the bishop says, «Breathe upon him,» that is, cast him out of thy heart, and set him at nought from the heart, and put the enemy to shame. And this is done three times, for the security of the renunciation of the evil one, and to the glory of our God, the only one in Trinity, who cast down the evil one. Then he turns him toward the east; for he leads him up from the darkness to the light, and from the lie to the truth. And the bishop says, «Say, And I join myself to Christ, and I believe in one God, the Father almighty,» and what follows. For he must confess purely the thrice-sunned light, so that he may receive its brightness in the heart too. He says, then, the whole confession of the sacred Symbol splendidly with the mouth. And this is done three times, confirming the confession, [b: B] and at the same time also for the honour, as we have said, of the three-hypostatic Godhead. And he confesses this openly, since he proclaims the truth; and because he must proclaim it before angels and men, through which he will also be saved. And one must proclaim with boldness the God of all things — the consubstantial Trinity, I mean, the only truly existing God — and also the incarnation from the Virgin, for our sake, of the one of the Trinity, the Word of God. When, then, he has done this three times, and has been asked again three times, «Hast thou joined thyself to Christ?» and has answered three times from the heart, and said, «I have joined myself to him,» [lat: the Latin gives the reply in the plural, *Fatemur eum*, we confess him] the bishop says, «And do obeisance to him,» and when he has sealed himself with the cross by his hand, which signifies the confession of Christ and a marking, [b: C] and has done obeisance three times, showing his servitude, and his true subjection together with the confession, the bishop turns toward the east and blesses God, who wills all to be saved. For thus one must give thanks for all things, and most of all for the salvation of men.
