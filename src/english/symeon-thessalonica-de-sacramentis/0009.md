And so, having taken the catechumen himself by the hand, as one who has found him that was lost, he brings him into the temple, as into heaven, plainly, or into paradise, showing that he is bringing him to the Master who had lost him. And showing the joy which he rejoices with the angels over a sinner who repents, he says, singing, «Hail, thou that art full of grace,» and goes in. For she is the first-fruits of the saving joy, the saving gate, she who dispensed salvation for us, and who bore for the world the true joy, God the Word made flesh from her, holily and virginally, for our refashioning. [b: D] And the bishop, figuring forth the things of Symeon, brings the one being baptized before the altar, showing how that he offered him. And he makes him bow down three times to God; because he too has been reconciled to God through conversion, like that prodigal, and to the adoption and incorruption, the first robe of the Spirit, he is coming in next to no time, in a greater and better manner, through the divine baptism. And so that both the conversion and the confession may be secure, the bishop also calls the sponsor. And he tells him to bow down three times to the altar, in which God dwells, showing by this that he gives his word to guard the one being baptized securely in the faith and in pious conduct of life. Then, if the one about to be baptized [0221] [b: A] is an infant, the sponsor, stooping, takes him as he lies before the holy doors, as from the hands of Christ; but if he is of age, he takes the one being baptized by the hand, showing the same thing, that he has received him from Christ, and has become his surety unto Christ, and will be his spiritual father. Wherefore also, holding him, he brings him to the sacred font, showing that the one being initiated comes to baptism willingly and not by force, and before witnesses: first and pre-eminently **80** God, and then the hierarch and the faithful who happen to be present, who are all witnesses of what is being performed, and the sponsor himself, who also brings him forward, and above all the divine and all-pure angels, invisibly present then, and rejoicing at the [b: B] birth from God in the Spirit and the salvation of the one being baptized, and enrolling him in the heavens, and becoming his acquaintances and friends, from the cleansing out of the divine laver and the seal of the myron. Wherefore it is also necessary that then nearly all the faithful be present with reverence and joy, and believe that angels are present, who rejoice over one sinner who repents; and that the one being baptized is then handed over to a holy guardian angel; and this one will be with him always, driving off the envious demons and every assault of the most guileful enemy, if only the one baptized keeps his baptism pure in faith and works; and he will bring him safe through into the kingdom of the heavens. Since, then, the work of the most divine baptism is very great, for this reason [b: C] formerly the Church too, through the bishops, used to perform the baptisms of those who came forward in the catholic churches, and at the time when, on the feasts at the divine liturgy, we chant «As many as were baptized into Christ,» and above all on the Great Sabbath before the divine sacred service, while the many readings from the divine prophets are being read. Since the most sacred baptism is both regeneration and refashioning, cleansing and illumination and adoption, and gift and sanctification, and the countless things, as many as the Fathers say. And it typifies pre-eminently the death of Christ, and the rising on the third day. Which indeed that season too presents more purely. For from the death of Christ and the three days' burial [b: D] and the resurrection we received immortality and incorruption, and we hope for that resurrection and renewal to come, and the life eternal, of which Christ has become the first-fruits, he who alone is sinless, having suffered for us sinners, and he who was not obliged to die, as not having sinned at all, having died for us. For sin is the sting of death, as Isaiah and Paul say, that is, its beginning and root and procurer. And Christ himself, not having done sin, and not being obliged to die, died for our sake; and for this reason, having abolished death, he has been raised, and has loosed our passions and the condemnation, the passionless one having suffered by his own act; and having become the author of our life, he was taken up into the heavens. And we who have put him on [0224] [b: A] in baptism await him from heaven; when he will also raise us up, and supply the incorruption perfectly, whose earnests he has now given through baptism. Nevertheless at every season too it is necessary to baptize, lest at some time some hindrance arise, and someone depart uninitiated, that is, without baptism. And very great loss will come of this. For he who is not baptized will not be saved. And it is needful, as far as there is room, to perform the baptisms with splendour and reverence. And the priest must be attentive and sober. For he works through the greatest work of God, the refashioning of men. But let us come to the matter before us. The bishop going before, the sponsor brings the one being baptized to the sacred font; he who stands surety for the one who comes willingly. [b: B] And the bishop stands, figuring forth Christ himself, having his power. And the font is filled with water, typifying the Jordan. And lights are lit, showing the illumination of the Spirit; and that the one being baptized comes out of the darkness to the light, and now becomes a son of light. And the bishop censes the font round about [lat: the Latin gives *Circum altare*, around the altar], showing the fragrance and holiness of the Spirit, who washes out all the foul odour of sins. **81** For holy is the Spirit. Then the bishop blesses and hymns the kingdom of the holy Trinity, the only God. For it alone reigns; and because it has now reigned, having rescued from the evil one him who is just now being regenerated. Then, while the prayers of peace [b: C] and the petitions are being said by the deacon, and the people are crying out «Lord, have mercy,» — for truly there the great mercy of God is awarded, as he who was before an enemy and accursed is adopted as a son — the bishop prays within himself for himself, seeking to find from God cleansing and propitiation, lest, while cleansing others, he himself be found defiled with sins. And he asks for strength and grace to minister to the mystery well and as is fitting. For great is the mystery: angels standing round, Christ being formed in the waters, the Holy Spirit coming upon them, and the one being baptized becoming, from filthy and sinful and unclean and an enemy and corruptible, holy and pure and a son of God and incorruptible. [b: D] O the great grace! O the gift of God! O the beneficence and grace toward us! Wherefore also, the petitions being completed, the bishop, rising up from the prayer, «Great art thou, O Lord, he cries out, and wondrous are thy works.» And this he cries aloud three times, hymning the threeness of the Godhead, and magnifying the Benefactor.
