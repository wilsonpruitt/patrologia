Then, offering up the prayer to the Son of God who was made flesh for us, and recounting all his wonders from the first, at the end he also brings to remembrance his incarnation from the Virgin in the Spirit, and the baptism in the Jordan, through which he refashioned us, having crushed the heads of the dragons upon the water, as the Prophet says. Wherefore he also entreats him to be present through the coming of the Holy Spirit, and to sanctify the water. For the Son is inseparable [0225] [b: A] from the Father and the Spirit, and the grace is common to them. And he seals the water three times with his hand in the form of a cross, imprinting Christ upon it. And he prays that he make it a fountain of incorruption, and the rest. Then after a little he again breathes upon the water three times, putting the divine grace into it more abundantly, imitating Christ, and bearing witness that the «The Spirit of God was borne upon the water,» was from the beginning on account of baptism. And he seals it again with his hand, figuring forth a cross, and says, «Let all the adverse powers be crushed beneath the sign of the figure of thy cross.» And he says the rest also, so that the water may be spiritual and holy, and that nothing of the evil one may lie hidden in it, but rather that it may be contrary to him and a thing that drives him away, and able to refashion and to sanctify and to adopt as sons those who are baptized [b: B] in it, and to make them children of light. Having thus prayed over it, then, and having given peace, which is Jesus Christ — for there is present there the peace that passes all understanding, Christ — and the hierarch having received the peace in return, and having bowed his head, he prays again by himself, in stillness, calling upon Jesus, the peaceful and still, and that everywhere, «Thou, saying, who camest to the Jordan and didst sanctify the waters, receive us also, who fall before thee as servants; and sanctify these waters and us; and let them be for us unto health of soul and body.» And rising up, showing that Christ raised us up when we had fallen, he hymns him, having proclaimed him aloud as the sanctification of all. Then, therefore, the font is full of the Spirit, and the water is holy, and bears Christ invisibly in itself. And since the type of the divine mercy too must be present, holy oil also is brought into action. For the water signifies the washing-out and the cleansing, and has already been sanctified; but the oil shows the divine mercy, and the gladness of his kindness. Which indeed is also afforded to those who have been wounded by the common enemy and bruised, as in the Gospels **82** Christ says. This was shown in the case of Noah too. For a dove came to those who were still in the water with the ark, carrying a twig of olive in its mouth. And the water prefigured baptism, and the dove the Holy Spirit; and the twig of the olive which it carried in its mouth was for a token of the mercy, and of the boundless kindness of God. Whence here too in baptism holy oil is brought forward. And it is sanctified thus: it is brought in some clean vessel to the bishop, or to the priest, if the bishop is not present; and he breathes upon it. And he seals it three times with the figure of a cross with his hand, by the breathing showing the grace of the Spirit, which the bishop received from Christ by the divine breathing upon the apostles; and by the cross figuring him who was crucified for us, who through the cross accomplished salvation for us. Wherefore the cross itself is also perfecting, and without a cross none of the divine things is accomplished. Then, having bowed his head, he prays that the oil be sanctified by the Holy Spirit, by him who also in Noah's time sent the twig of the olive by a dove, and who gave this oil in the law, and afforded it to the apostles. And having completed the prayer and given thanks, he first pours the holy thing that was sanctified into the water three times in the form of a cross, singing the much-hymned song, the Alleluia.

[b: C] CHAPTER 63. What the Alleluia signifies.

And this song, the Alleluia, the prophets who were inspired by the Holy Spirit used to say. Which indeed signifies a sojourning of God, and «God comes, and praise ye him,» having been set down and being sung prophetically, because the Lord was to come, both at his first and at his second coming, and to send his grace always among us in the Holy Spirit. Wherefore the Church also ever chants this song, having received Christ, and awaiting him from heaven, as we have said. Now the divine Dionysius says that myron is poured into the sacred font [0228] [b: A]; but now this oil is poured in, being a prelude of the myron; and the myron afterwards. And it is possible to reckon that the sacred Dionysius too calls this oil myron as being a prelude of the myron, since through it the one being baptized receives the first-fruits of sanctification, and is anointed for the divine contests, and afterwards is strengthened perfectly and perfected and sealed with the most perfect myron of grace. Wherefore he also makes mention of these two in baptism. And the bishop, after sanctifying the oil, perfects the one who has been baptized. But what need also was there for the one being baptized to be anointed afterwards with myron, if the myron was in the baptism? From this oil, then, he himself first anoints the one who is now about to be baptized, saying, «N. is anointed [b: B] with the oil of gladness in the name of the Trinity.» And the gladness is of the Spirit, who saves us with goodness and with cheer. «For restore unto me the gladness of thy Salvation, David cries out, and establish me with a governing Spirit.» Then from the head the whole body is anointed by some deacon or attendant. For he is made ready entirely for the divine contests. For he has not come forward to lie back at ease, but to imitate Christ, whom he puts on, and to wrestle against the evil one and to play the man. And those who contend in the flesh, too, were of old anointed with oil. Then, therefore, he is brought into the water wholly naked, just as he was also born, and as he was first fashioned. But at first he had that divine robe, and being naked, was not ashamed; [b: C] but now, having become naked, he bears from the flesh the marks of the shame of the transgression. Yet having entered the holy water, he casts off the shame of sin; and receives back the glory **83** and the garment of incorruption. For the hierarch, or the priest, taking hold of his head, in which all the senses and the governing faculty operate, and plunging him into the water once, cries out, «The servant of God N. is baptized in the name of the Father,» And at once he brings him up. Then he plunges him again, saying, «And of the Son,» And again he brings him up. And having plunged him a third time, he says, «And of the Holy Spirit.» And thus he leads him up, initiated by the most divine baptism, at once both refashioning and perfecting him through the holy Trinity, [b: D] and proclaiming the three hypostases in the one Godhead, saying "in the name," which shows the singleness of the Godhead, then "of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit," which is the confession of the three divine persons, undivided and unconfused; and at the same time, through the three plungings and risings, hinting at the three days' burial and the resurrection of him who was crucified in the flesh for us and rose.
