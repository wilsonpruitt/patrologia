⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he chants the «As many as were baptized into Christ,» with those who are singing. ⟦s2⟧ Then, while the hymn-singers say the «Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven,» [b: B] and what follows of the Psalm, he himself, going before the one baptized, brings him before the altar, and there gives him a share of the awesome communion.

⟦s2⟧ CHAPTER 68. ⟦s3⟧ That the end of baptism and of the myron is the communion of the mysteries.

⟦s4⟧ For this is also the end of every mystery: that, having been set free from error and from the filth of sin, and having become pure and been sealed to Christ in the Holy Spirit, we may partake of the flesh and the blood of Christ himself, and be united to him in the highest degree. ⟦s5⟧ For this is what he says also through the Prophet: «I will dwell in them and walk in them;» and what he himself prayed to the Father, saying, «That they may be one, as we,» and the «Lo, I am with you all the days.» ⟦s5⟧ Since until then he will be with us through the mysteries also, and invisibly. ⟦s6⟧ But then he will be seen as he is, [b: C] as the beloved says. ⟦s7⟧ And for this reason he was made flesh from the Virgin, that he might be united to us. ⟦s8⟧ And for this cause he was crucified, and poured out his blood for our sake, that we might partake of him. ⟦s9+10⟧ And before the cross, through the sacred service, he gave the mysteries, that he might be with us, and that we might be partakers of him, both now and in the age to come, and that we all might share in his good things, because he loved this. ⟦s11⟧ And his Father so loved the world, that he gave his only-begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have life eternal. ⟦s12⟧ And for this reason in like manner he partook of flesh and blood, that we might have communion with him. ⟦s13⟧ Because this is the true God and the life eternal, as the beloved again says. ⟦s14⟧ To him, the true light, be the glory unto the ages. ⟦s15⟧ Amen

⟦s15⟧ So the bishop, after giving the one baptized a share [b: D] of the communion, having given thanks to the Saviour, and having dismissed them, and having blessed the one baptized together with the sponsor, departs; and the one baptized goes to his house, escorted with lights, as being now a son of light; and those escorting him chant the «As many as were baptized into Christ». ⟦s16⟧ For the one baptized has put on Christ in himself, and Christ has been formed in himself; and the grace of the Spirit has come to dwell in him. ⟦s17⟧ Who indeed ought both to pray, and to wear the sacred vestments — the cowl, that is, and the mantle, which is also called the emphotion, and was formerly a garment, and those who had been illumined used to put it on over all — until the seventh [0236] [b: A] day, on account of the holy chrism, and not to wash at all; but to wipe his hands with these vestments, as being sanctified; and after the seventh, which shows the passing-over of this sevenfold life, to come into the temple and to the priest, and **86** to be washed by him, the priest with sacred prayers washing him off with sponge and water in the places of the head and of the hands where he was anointed with the most holy myron, except the other parts of the body. ⟦s18⟧ For these the one baptized washes off himself in a place set apart, so that nothing of those holy things — of the water of baptism, I mean, or of the myron — may fall out and be trodden underfoot. ⟦s19⟧ Wherefore concerning this let him who is of age receive a charge from the priest to keep himself, as also to pray, and to assemble at the temples, [b: B] and to keep pure, and continually to partake of the mysteries; for this is the true life; but if it is an infant, let the mother take care concerning this and have the charge, after the seventh day to bring the infant to be washed by the priest, and that it be washed off by her over its whole body, and the washing-water be poured out in a place set apart and clean. ⟦s20⟧ But she ought also to wash separately the garments in which she wrapped the infant during the seven days, and continually to bring the infant to the communion of the mysteries. ⟦s20⟧ For thus it will rather be kept safe and grow, by Christ.

⟦s21⟧ CHAPTER 69. ⟦s22⟧ Against the Latins, that one must not keep the infants away from the communion.

⟦s22⟧ [b: C] And not, as some do, to neglect it, innovating unreasonably in this too, and to keep the infants away from the communion. ⟦s23⟧ Because the infants do not know, they say, of what they partake. ⟦s24+25⟧ Alas for the unreason and at the same time the absurdity! ⟦s24+25⟧ And why dost thou baptize, then? or why dost thou anoint with the myron? ⟦s26⟧ But rather the Latins do not even anoint, as we learn, at baptism; and all things with them are contrary to the catholic Church. ⟦s27⟧ But he who thinks rightly — just as he himself in faith brings the infant to God, and through a faithful sponsor makes the confession, and the infant being baptized is regenerated unto the kingdom of the heavens, and is anointed with the myron and perfected, and, if it dies, is of the kingdom of God — so let it also be brought through a faithful one to the communion. ⟦s28+29⟧ Because this is the life eternal. [b: D] And just as «he who has not been regenerated through water and the Spirit shall by no means enter into the kingdom of the heavens, so also «he who has not eaten the flesh of the Son of man (as the Lord himself said), nor drunk his blood, shall not have life eternal.»

⟦s28+29⟧ These things concerning the divine baptism, according to my power, having spoken them in summary, I also wrote to you, beloved; and what is according to power must be accepted. ⟦s30⟧ But loftier words will come to you from those who are loftier in the divine things. ⟦s31⟧ For these things we, the lowly, have written for your sake, who asked, and perhaps for the sake of some who do not search at all, nor know at all the reasons of the things that are done. ⟦s32⟧ For which cause we will speak to you also concerning the remaining mysteries, as you have sought.
