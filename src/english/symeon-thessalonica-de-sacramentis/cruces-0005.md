## Genuine cruces

- **0201 τῶν λυομένων πατέρων.** "dead fathers who are being dissolved" — λύεσθαι of dying
  (the body's dissolution). Latin *parentes solutos* concurs. Rendered literally.
- **0201 ἐνίσχυε τὸ ἔργον πρὸς τοῦτο, ὡς καὶ πρὸς τὸ διδάσκειν.** The object is "the work," not
  "them": "that he strengthened the work toward this, as also toward teaching." Rendered as
  printed; the sense (he equipped the apostles for the monastic life as for preaching) is the
  reader's. Latin OCR-broken here and settles nothing.
- **0204 καίτοι γε ταύτην τὴν ἄφεσιν … χαριζομένην ἀεί.** An accusative participle with no
  governing verb (an implied "we see/know"). Rendered as a finite clause, "although indeed this
  remission of sins is granted always…"; no word added that changes the claim. Latin *hanc certe
  remissionem … concessam* has the same shape.
- **0204 ἐνταφιασμὸν ἦν αὐτοῦ.** ἦν is slightly blurred on the plate (Calfa `ἐνταφιασλν ύ`); it is
  the only reading that construes ("and that myron was for his burial"). The whole Mary/Simon
  sentence is anacoluthic (οὐχ ἡ … Μαρία … ὡς καὶ ἡ ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ … ἀλλ᾽ ὅπερ ἡ … πόρνη); the
  English keeps its run, setting the parenthetical clauses off with dashes (anchor precedent).
- **0205 Βλέπεις, ὅτι τοῦτο δηλοῖ τὸ ἅγιον ἔλαιον;** ὅτι read as ὅ τι, "Seest thou what this holy
  oil signifies?" (Latin *Videsne quid hoc sacrum … oleum?*). Not an emendation — the plate's
  spacing of ὅτι/ὅ τι is unreliable in this fount (cf. Ὅ τι δέ at col 201).
- **0208 ὑμετέραν (plural) after ἀδελφέ (singular)** inside one speech. Pattern 17 carried as
  printed: "brother … on account of your love … your request." The Cleric's δεόμεθά σου is
  singular ("we beseech thee").
- **0209 καὶ ἔτι οὐκ ἀπαθὲς ἐξ ἡδονῆς τίκτεσθαι ἡμᾶς.** An infinitive clause with no verb of its
  own; taken as continuing ἐκδιδάσκει: "and further, that our being born from pleasure is not
  passionless." Calfa had `καὶ παι οὐκ`; the plate's ἔτι is clear. The οὐκ is carried. Latin
  *quoniam nequaquam absque peccato ex voluptate nascitur* (causal) — looseness, not a `[lat:]`.
- **0209 τὸ Σωτήριον Ἰησοῦς ἐπικληθείς.** Neuter adjective (capitalized on the plate) with no
  noun: rendered "called by the saving name, Jesus" — "name" supplied from τὸ ὄνομα in the same
  sentence. Latin *nomine salutifero* concurs.
- **0209 τῆς νομικῆς δουλείας ἐρρύσατο** has no object; "us" supplied from ἀντὶ ἡμῶν just before
  ("has rescued us from the bondage of the law"). Latin *nos eruit*.
- **0212 πλὴν μετὰ προσοχῆς, καὶ ἱερέως εὐλαβοῦς τε καὶ νήφοντος** — see `[lat:]` below; the
  Greek is clean on the plate and is what the English says.
- **0213 μήποτε καὶ ληφθείη οὕτω παρὰ Θεοῦ.** The sense of the close of ch. 62 is obscure: "it is
  good to receive and sanctify the infant, lest it should also be taken thus by God." μήποτε may
  be the later-Greek "perhaps" ("in case it may thus be accepted by God"), which suits the
  argument better; but "lest" is its first sense and the Latin reads it so (*ne sic a Deo
  deprehendatur*). Rendered "lest" — the English does not choose the friendlier sense. The
  sentence is also anacoluthic (ὁ τὰ τῶν ἀσεβῶν δεχόμενος βρέφη … καλὸν δέχεσθαι), carried.
- **0213 μετὰ τὴν κοινωνίαν Χριστοῦ βαπτίζειν τε καὶ ἐμπνέειν.** Literal: "to baptize and to
  inbreathe after the communion of Christ" — i.e. that the priest who represents Christ should
  baptize having communicated. Latin *post communicationem Christi baptizare et insufflare*
  concurs. Rendered as printed.
- **0216 Ὁ μὲν ἀρχιερεὺς καὶ ἱερεὺς βαπτίζων** — singular participle with two nouns ("the bishop
  or priest who baptizes" is the probable sense). Rendered with the printed "and". Latin
  *Pontifex quidem et sacerdos baptizans* concurs.
- **0220 στρέφεται πρὸς ἀνατολάς, καὶ εὐλογεῖ τὸν Θεὸν ὁ ἀρχιερεύς.** The subject of στρέφεται
  is unexpressed (catechumen or bishop). Rendered "he turns toward the east, and the bishop
  blesses God", leaving it as open as the Greek.

## Pattern 16 pass (Latin twin, every paragraph)

**Fired — four `[lat:]`**, every one with the LATIN read at the plate first (the twin is rough OCR):
- **0208 (ch. 58)** ἄδειάν τε παρέχει ἐνεργεῖν τὰ ἔργα αὐτῶν ‖ Latin col 207 *eisdemque
  facultatem praebet sanctificare opera* — "leave to do their works" vs "leave to sanctify works".
  A different act granted to the women. Plate p.109 confirms *sanctificare*.
- **0212 (ch. 61)** πλὴν μετὰ προσοχῆς, καὶ ἱερέως εὐλαβοῦς τε καὶ νήφοντος ("but with attention,
  and by a reverent and sober priest") ‖ Latin col 211 *nisi quod abest oratio atque pius
  sanctusque sacerdos* ("except that the prayer and a pious and holy priest are lacking"). A
  polarity divergence: the Greek requires the careful priest in an emergency baptism; the Latin
  says he is absent. Plate p.111 confirms the Latin.
- **0217 (ch. 61 cont.)** «Ἀπεταξάμην», τουτέστιν ἠρνησάμην τε καὶ ἀπεβαλόμην (singular, the one
  being baptized) ‖ Latin col 218 *«Abrenuntiamus,» id est: Abnegamus et rejicimus* (plural).
  Who answers is the point of the rite. Plate p.114 confirms.
- **0220** «Συνεταξάμην αὐτῷ» (singular) ‖ Latin col 219 *«Fatemur eum»* (plural, and "confess"
  for "join myself to"). Plate p.115 confirms. The verb is Allatius's usual looseness (he renders
  συντάσσομαι *fateor* throughout); only the number fires.
