[344] received mildly and very gladly;[^1] in which, laying down one operation as doctrine, he said that by the same he operated both the divine things and the human,[^2] fighting not only with holy Scripture and the holy Fathers, but also with the very nature of the things that have come to be. For none of the things that are, while remaining in what is according to nature, is naturally constituted to do contraries. For fire does not heat and cool; nor does ice cool and heat; nor does earth dry and moisten; nor does water moisten and dry. If then this is contemplated in none of the things that are, how are you not afraid to say that the incarnate Word, who became man essentially, accomplished by one operation both the miracles and the passions, which differ from each other by principle of nature?

PYRRHUS. Why then did Cyril, the luminary of the Church, lay down as doctrine things contrary to the pious concept that has now come to light from the inquiry, saying that he, Christ, that is, «displayed one kindred operation through both»?[^3]

MAXIMUS. The present testimony does not fight at all with the two operations; on the contrary, it even establishes them. For he did not say one natural operation of the Godhead of Christ and of his manhood; since otherwise he would not have said elsewhere that «One operation of maker and thing made no one in his right mind would grant»;[^4] but he said it wishing to show that the operation of the Godhead is one, both without flesh and with flesh. As if someone, wishing to show that the operation of fire is one with matter and without matter, should say that fire burns both without matter and with matter. So too the Father[^5] did not say one operation of the two natures; but he called one the divine and paternal operation, which exists essentially in the incarnate God Word, by which he not only accomplished the divine signs incorporeally by an all-working command, as he himself says, since even after the incarnation he is co-worker with his own Begetter, who operates incorporeally; but also showed them bodily by the touch of his own flesh. For this is what «Through both» means. So that the giving of life to the girl, or the recovery of sight of the blind man, or the blessing of the loaves, or the cleansing of the leper,[^6] done through word and all-working command, stands kindred according to nature to that which is accomplished bodily through touch; that he might demonstrate the flesh too to be life-giving, as being properly his and not another's, by the pure union with him. For through both of these, I mean command and touch, the divine operation was made known through the very deeds, in no way harming the natural operation of the flesh, human as ours is and suffering; on the contrary, preserving it for its own manifestation; just as the soul too, through [345] its own body as through an instrument, and through its natural operation, brings forward its own things and its own operation according to nature. For the stretching out of the hand, and the touch, and the grasping, and the mixing of the clay by it, and the breaking of the bread;[^7] and simply, all that was accomplished through the hand or another member or part of the body, belonged to the natural operation of the manhood of Christ; according to which, as man, he himself was operative, he who is God by nature and operates the divine things naturally too; so that through each he might confirm each according to nature, showing himself to be in truth perfect God and perfect man, apart from sin alone.

The Father then was not ignorant of the pre-eminent property of each nature, the one comprehensive of the other natural idioms: I mean the creative operation, and the vital operation given from the soul to his body; which the incarnate God Word, preserving in himself, showed distinct and unconfused: the creative, in creating essence and quality and quantity, from which, and in which, and which the existence of the things that are both is and is contemplated. For even if those who philosophized among the Greeks draw out the things that are into ten principles,[^8] yet by these the whole is held together and enclosed. Essence, by filling up the lacking eyes of the blind man; quality, by converting the water into wine; quantity, by increasing the loaves;[^9] and the vital operation he showed in breathing, speaking, seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating and drinking; moving the hands, walking, sleeping, and the rest, as many as display in all those under the nature the invariance of the operation according to nature.

PYRRHUS. Piously and without forcing hast thou set out the Father's concept, as not fighting with, but concurring through all things with, the two operations. But what do we say about holy Dionysius, who said in the letter to Gaius the therapeut[^10] «a certain new theandric operation», of Christ, «conducted among us»?[^11]

MAXIMUS. Is newness a quality or a quantity?

PYRRHUS. A quantity.

MAXIMUS. Then it also brings in with itself a nature of the same sort; since the definition of every nature stands as the principle of its essential operation. And not only so; but also when the Apostle says, Behold, all things have become new,[^12] he says nothing other than, Behold, all things have become one; and whether you wish to call this by nature or by operation, let this be in your power.[^13] But if newness is a quality, it does not signify one operation, but the new and ineffable mode of the manifestation of the natural operations of Christ, fittingly to the ineffable mode of the interpenetration of Christ's natures into each other, and his way of life as man, which is strange and paradoxical, and unknown to the [348] nature of the things that are, and the mode of the exchange according to the ineffable union.

PYRRHUS. Does not the theandric either signify one?

MAXIMUS. No; on the contrary, the word, by periphrasis, has handed down through the natures that are numbered their operations; since, by negation of the extremes, there is no middle in Christ. But if it signifies one, Christ, as God, will have the operation other than that of the Father. The Son then will be of an operation other than the Father; since that of the Father is not theandric; besides its also characterizing and constituting a theandric nature. For the operation, being natural, is the constitutive and innate character of a nature. And those who took up the considerations about these things said that the genus of quantity is one, and that of quality another.

PYRRHUS. Newness is neither quantity nor quality, but essence.

MAXIMUS. I marvel how thou hast dared to say this. What is set in contradistinction to essence?

PYRRHUS. Not-being.

MAXIMUS. And what to newness?

PYRRHUS. Oldness.

MAXIMUS. So then newness is not essence, but quality. And how, if we take the word to mean one operation, do we not in this way bring in this God-revealing teacher[^14] as opposed both to himself and to the other Fathers? For all, expressly and in common, both said and taught that things that are of the same essence are also of the same operation; and things that are of the same operation are of the same essence;[^15] and that things differing in essence differ in operation too; and things differing in operation differ in essence too.

PYRRHUS. This was said by the Fathers in the case of theology, not however in the case of the economy.[^16] Whence it does not stand with a truth-loving understanding to transfer to the economy what was said by them in the case of theology, and to bring in with it such an absurdity.

MAXIMUS. If it was said by the Fathers in the case of theology only, then after the incarnation, according to you, the Son is not theologized together with the Father. And if he is not theologized together, neither is he numbered together in the invocation of baptism; and faith and preaching will be found empty.[^17]

And again, if after the incarnation the Son is not theologized together with the Father, neither will he be of the same essence; for things differing in principle will wholly differ in nature too.

And again, if the Son is not theologized together with the Father after the incarnation, to whom shall we assign, My Father is working until now, and I am working?[^18] And, Whatever he sees the Father doing, the Son also does likewise?[^19] And, If you do not believe me, [349] believe my works?[^20] And, The works that I do bear witness about me?[^21] And, As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so the Son too gives life to whom he wills?[^22] For all these show him not only being of the same essence with the Father even after the incarnation, but also of the same operation.

And again, if the providence over the things that are is an operation of God, and this is not only of the Father and the Spirit but also of the Son, even after the incarnation, then he is of the same operation with the Father even after the incarnation.

And again, if the miracles are an operation of God, and from the miracles we have known him to be of the same essence with the Father, then from the same operation he was shown to be of the same essence with the Father; and he is theologized together with him even after the incarnation.

And again, if the creative operation belongs essentially to God, and what belongs essentially is also not to be taken away, it is necessary that they either, not saying that he is of the same operation with the Father even after the incarnation, should not say either that he is of the same essence with him (for where the operation according to nature is not, neither will the nature ever be); or, saying that he is of the same essence with him, should say also of the same operation, and theologize him together with the Father even after the incarnation; for where the nature is, there also will be, without omission, the operation common according to it.

PYRRHUS. We do not say the one operation for the destruction of the human operation; but because, set in contradistinction to the divine operation, it is called passion on this account.

MAXIMUS. By this account, those who say one nature also do not say it for the destruction of the human; but because the nature, set in contradistinction to the divine, is called passible on this account.

PYRRHUS. What then? Did not the Fathers, by the demarcation from the divine operation, call the human movement passion?

MAXIMUS. God forbid. For the existence of nothing, to speak generally, is known or defined from juxtaposition or from comparison; or else in this way things will be found to be causes of each other. For if, on account of the divine movement being operation, the human is passion, then wholly too, on account of the divine nature being good, the human will be wicked. And likewise, by the reciprocation with contrariety,[^23] on account of the human movement being called passion, the divine movement is called operation; and on account of the human nature being wicked, the divine will be good. But away with it! for these things are truly of much stupidity.

PYRRHUS. What then? Did not the Fathers name the human movement passion?

[352] MAXIMUS. They named it in many ways, with reference, that is, to the concepts underlying it.

PYRRHUS. How dost thou say this?

MAXIMUS. They called it both power, and operation, and difference, and movement, and property, and quality, and passion; not by contradistinction to the divine; but as sustaining and unalterable, power; as characteristic and displaying the invariance in all things of the same species, operation; as distinguishing, difference; as indicative, movement; as constitutive and belonging to it alone and not to another, property; as form-giving, quality; as being moved, passion. For all the things from God and after God undergo passion by being moved, as not being self-movement or self-power. Not then by contradistinction, as has been said, but on account of the principle placed in them creatively by the cause that constituted the whole. Whence also, uttering it together with the divine, they called it operation. For he who said, «For each form operates with the communion of the other»,[^24] what else has he done? Or he who said, «For having remained fasting forty days, he afterwards hungered; for he gave to the nature, when he willed, to operate its own things»?[^25] Or those who said his operation is different? Or those who said double? Or those who said one and another?

PYRRHUS. In truth, the inquiry about the operations too has shown the one operation to be absurd, in whatever mode it is said of Christ. But I ask pardon both for myself and for those who went before me. For from ignorance we were carried off into these absurd concepts and arguments; and I beg that a mode be found, that this imported absurdity may be abolished, and the memory of those who went before me be preserved.

MAXIMUS. There is no other mode than that the persons be passed over in silence, and such doctrines be anathematized.

PYRRHUS. But if this happens, Sergius and the synod held in my time[^26] are found to be cast out together with them.

MAXIMUS. It comes to me to marvel how thou callest a synod that which was not held according to synodical laws and canons or ecclesiastical ordinances. For neither was an encyclical letter issued by agreement of the patriarchs; nor was a place or day of meeting fixed. There was no one bringing in the case, or accuser. Those who assembled had no letters of commendation, neither the bishops from the metropolitans, nor the metropolitans from the patriarchs. No letters or topoteretae[^27] were sent from the other patriarchs. Who then, having any share of reason, would endure to call a synod that which filled the whole inhabited world with scandals and discord?

PYRRHUS. If there is no other mode than this, preferring my own salvation to all [353] things, I am ready to do this with all full assurance; asking one thing and one only:[^28] that I be deemed worthy, first, of the veneration of the apostolic shrines, or rather of the chief apostles themselves; and then also of seeing the most holy pope face to face;[^29] and of presenting to him a libellus on what was done absurdly.

When he had said this, Maximus and Gregory the patrician[^30] said: Since thy proposal is good and of service to the Church, so let it be done.

Having come then with us to this city of the Romans of great name, he fulfilled the promise, condemning the doctrines of the impious Ekthesis,[^31] and uniting himself, through the orthodox confession, to the holy, catholic and apostolic Church, by the grace and co-operation of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
