30. One must know that the devil, having deceived us by a guile wickedly contrived through self-love, by the assault of pleasure, set us apart by inclination from God and from one another, twisting what is straight, and in this manner partitioning nature, and cutting it up into many opinions and imaginings.

31. There are three things that are the greatest and the sources of evil, and, to speak simply, begetters of all vice: ignorance, I say, and self-love and tyranny, dependent on one another and constituted through one another. For from ignorance about God comes self-love; and from this, tyranny toward the kindred. And no argument will gainsay that the evil one made these subsist in us by the mode of misuse of our own powers, reason and desire and anger.

32. It is needful, instead of ignorance, to be moved by reason through knowledge, by way of inquiry, toward God, the one most alone; and by desire, pure of the passion of self-love, to be driven by longing toward God alone; and by anger, separated from tyranny, to struggle to attain God alone; and to make, out of these, and for whose sake these are, the divine and blessed love, which joins to God and shows the lover of God to be god.

33. When self-love, as the beginning, as I said, and mother of evils, has been plucked out, all that comes from it and after it is wont to be plucked out with it; since, if it is not, no form or trace of vice whatever can in any way subsist at all.

34. It is needful to cleave to ourselves and to one another as far as Christ himself, going before, showed through himself, enduring to suffer for us.

35. Because of love all the saints stood against sin, making no account of the present life, and underwent the many-formed modes of death, that they might be gathered to themselves away from the world, and to God, and might unite in themselves the ruptures of nature. For this is the true and blameless divine wisdom[^2] of the faithful, whose end is the good and the truth: if indeed love for man is the good, and love for God according to faith the true, the marks of love; as it joins men to God and to one another, and for this reason has the permanence of good things unfailing.

[1193] 36. The activity and demonstration of perfect love toward God is the genuine disposition toward one's neighbour through voluntary goodwill. *For he who does not love his brother, whom he has seen*, says the divine John, *cannot love God, whom he has not seen.*[^3]

37. The way of truth is love; naming himself this, the Word of God presents those who journey in it, made pure of all things, to God and the Father.[^4]

38. This is the door, through which he who enters comes into the Holy of holies, and is established as worthy to become a beholder of the inaccessible beauty of the holy and royal Trinity.[^5]

39. Fearful indeed, and beyond all condemnation, is it willingly to put to death, through love for things that perish, the life given us from God by the gift of the Holy Spirit; and they surely know this fear who have practised preferring the truth to self-love.

40. Let us use peace as we ought, and, setting aside the friendship that wickedly arose in us toward the world and the world-ruler, let us, though late, put an end to the war set up through the passions against God; and, making unbreakable treaties of peace with him, in the abolishing of the body of sin,[^6] let us cease from enmity toward him.

41. It is impossible for us to be made friends with God while we are at faction against him through the passions, and endure to pay tribute through vice to the wicked tyrant and slayer of souls, the devil, unless we have first been made wholly enemies of the wicked one. For until then we stand as enemies and foes of God, even if we wrap about ourselves the name of the faithful, so long as we wish to be slaves to passions of dishonour;[^7] and no profit will thereafter accrue to us from peace in the world, while the soul is ill disposed, and at faction against its own maker, and does not endure to come under his kingdom, being still sold to countless cruel masters, who press it on into vice, and prepare it deceitfully to prefer the road that leads to perdition to the one that saves.[^8]

42. For this God made us, that we might become sharers in the divine nature, and partakers of his eternity, and might appear like him by the deification that is from grace;[^9] for whose sake is the whole constitution and permanence of beings, and the bringing forth and coming-to-be of things that are not.

43. If we aspire both to be named and to be of God, let us strive not to betray the Word to the passions, as Judas did, nor to deny him, as Peter did.[^10] For denial of the Word is the refusal, out of fear, to do the good; and betrayal is deliberate sin in act, and the impulse toward sin.

[1196] 44. The end of every affliction is joy; and of every labour, rest; and of every dishonour, glory. And simply, the end of all the pains endured for virtue's sake is to come to be and to be with God for ever, and to enjoy the everlasting relief that has no end.

45. God, wishing to unite us to one another by inclination as well as by nature, and truly pushing the whole human toward this, engraved for us, in his love for man, the saving commandments, and for this reason simply legislated to show mercy and to be shown mercy.[^11]

46. The self-love and intelligence of men, having either thrust away or outwitted one another and the law, cut up the one nature into many portions; and, having introduced its insensibility that now prevails, armed nature itself against itself through inclination.[^12] For this reason, everyone who by sober thought and nobility of prudence has been able to dissolve this unevenness of nature has had mercy on himself before others, making his inclination accord with nature, and drawing near to God by inclination because of nature, and showing in himself what is the mode of the principle of «according to the image», and how God, having fittingly in the beginning made our nature like himself, and a manifest copy of his own goodness, established it identical with itself in all things, unwarring, peaceful, without faction, bound fast by love toward God and toward itself; according to which we cleave to God with aspiration, and to one another with fellow-feeling.

47. The God who loves man became man for this reason: that he might gather the nature of men to himself, and stop it from being borne along wickedly, at faction with itself, or rather against itself, and partitioned, and having no rest because of the unstable motion of inclination about each thing.[^13]

48. There is nothing among the things after God more honourable for those who have mind, or rather dearer to God, than perfect love, since it gathers into one those who are divided, and is [1197] able to make in the many, or in all, one identity without faction according to inclination. For it is proper to love to show forth as one the inclination of those who seek what is according to it.

49. If by nature the noble is unifying and holding-together of what is divided, evil, plainly, is divisive and corruptive of what is united. For evil by nature is scattered, and unstable, and many-formed, and divisive.

50. Deliverance from all evils, and a short road to salvation, is the true love of God in full knowledge, and the entire renunciation of the soul's affection toward the body and this world; by which, casting off the desire of pleasure and the fear of pain, we are freed from evil self-love, being raised up to the knowledge of the Creator; and, receiving in exchange for the wicked a good noetic self-love, separated from bodily affection, we do not cease worshipping God through this noble self-love, ever seeking from God the constitution of the soul. For this is true worship, and truly pleasing to God: the exact care of the soul through the virtues.

51. He who does not aspire to bodily pleasure, and does not fear pain at all, has become passionless. For with these, together with the self-love that bore them, he has killed with one accord all the passions that come through them and from them, together with ignorance, the most original of evils; and he has become wholly of the noble that stands and abides and is ever the same by nature, remaining utterly unmoved with it, and with unveiled face beholding as in a mirror the glory of God; from the light-like brilliance in it contemplating the divine and inaccessible glory.[^14]

52. Let us deny, so far as we can, the pleasure and the pain of the present life, and we shall be wholly delivered from every conception of passions and from demonic wickedness. For through pleasure we love the passions, and through pain we flee virtue.

53. Since every vice is naturally destroyed together with the modes that constitute it, man, finding through experience itself that pain is altogether the successor of every pleasure, had his whole impulse toward pleasure, and his whole flight from pain: fighting for the one with all his power, fighting down the other with all zeal; supposing, what was impossible, that by such scheming he would set these apart from one another, and have [1200] self-love joined to pleasure alone, wholly untried by pain; not knowing, under the passion, as it seems, that pleasure can never be without pain. For the toil of pain is blended into pleasure, even if it seems to escape those who have it, because of the prevailing of pleasure in the passion; since what prevails is naturally always visible, covering the sense of what lies beside it. So, laying claim to pleasure because of self-love, and again striving to flee pain for the same cause, we devise the unspeakable generations of corrupting passions.

54. One does not know the experience of sense in pleasure and pain when he binds, or rather glues, his mind, set free from bodily relation, to God, the truly beloved and yearned-for and aspired-to.

55. As one cannot otherwise worship God purely, unless he utterly cleanses the soul, so neither can he worship the creation, unless he cherishes the body;[^15] in respect of which body man, performing the worship that makes for corruption, and becoming self-loving in respect of it, had pleasure unceasingly, and pain at work, ever eating from the tree of disobedience, the tree of good and evil,[^16] which has, through experience, the knowledge mingled in the same respect according to sense. And perhaps one who says that the visible creation is *the tree of what is known of good and evil*[^17] will not miss the truth; for by nature it has a partaking that makes pleasure and pain.

56. Where reason does not rule, the rule according to sense naturally comes in; in it the power of sin is somehow mixed, drawing the soul down through pleasure to pity for the flesh kindred to it according to hypostasis; by which, having put into its hands as its natural work the passionate and pleasure-steeped care of the flesh, it leads it away from the life according to nature, and persuades it to become the creator of vice that has no hypostasis.

57. Vice of a noetic soul is the forgetting of the noble things according to nature, which comes upon it from the passionate relation to the flesh and the world; and this relation reason, as general, makes vanish according to spiritual science, searching out the coming-to-be and nature of the world and of the flesh, and driving the soul toward the kindred region of intelligibles; to which the law of sin makes no crossing,[^18] not having any longer, as a bridge carrying it over to the mind, sense, which has already been dissolved from the soul as to relation, and cast down among the sensible spectacles, whose relation and nature the mind has crossed beyond, and does not perceive at all.

[1201] 58. As reason, ruling the passions, makes the senses an instrument of virtue, so too the passions, ruling reason, shape the senses toward sin. And one must soberly consider and meditate how the soul will fittingly make the noble reversal, using the things through which it formerly offended for the coming-to-be and hypostasis of virtues.

59. The holy Gospel introduces the denial of fleshly life, and the confession of spiritual life. And I speak of those who are always dying according to man, I mean the human life in the flesh in this age, but living according to God by the Spirit alone, like the divine Apostle and those about him, who in no way live their own life, but have Christ living in themselves according to the soul alone.[^19] Thus those who for God's sake are dead in the flesh in this age are judged, having many afflictions and torments and straits, and enduring with joy persecutions and countless kinds of temptations.[^20]

60. Every passion is constituted by the interweaving of some sensible thing and sense, and a natural power: anger, say, or desire, or reason turned aside from what is according to nature. If then the mind, contemplating the end of these in their composition with one another, of the sensible, of sense, and of the natural power that bears upon it, is able, distinguishing each of them, to lead it back to its own principle by nature, and to contemplate the sensible by itself, without the relation of sense to it, and sense apart from the intimacy of the sensible to it, and desire, say, or some other of the powers according to nature, without the passionate disposition toward sense and the sensible, such as the qualified motion of the passion makes the contemplation become: then it has scattered and ground fine, like Israel's calf of old, the constitution of whatever passion occurs, and sown it under the water of knowledge,[^21] making wholly vanish even the bare imagination of the passions, through the restoration to themselves of the things that by nature make it up.

For each of men naturally shows through, as from a garment, by the conduct of his life, whether righteous or unrighteous: the one having a clean tunic, the virtuous life; the other possessing a life soiled with wicked works.
