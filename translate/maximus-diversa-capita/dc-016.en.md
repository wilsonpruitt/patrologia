title: Various Chapters, 16
---
[1352] 7. Conceit[^1] is the mixture of pride and vainglory; toward God having contempt, according to which it is wont blasphemously to slander providence; and toward nature having acquired alienation, according to which it handles all the things of nature contrary to nature, corrupting the comeliness of nature by the mode of misuse.

8. The spirit of burning heat[^2] signifies not only the temptations, but also the abandonment by God, which takes away from the Jews the supply of the divine gifts of grace. And the affinity[^3] according to soul through the Spirit loosens the relation of choice to the flesh, and binds it fast to God, nailed to him by longing.

9. The natural law, when it does not have sense overreaching reason, persuades all, without teaching, to embrace what is kindred and of one race, since they have nature itself as teacher of help for those in need, and [persuades] all to wish for all that which each thinks agreeable when it is done to himself by others.[^4] And, teaching this, the Lord says: *Whatever you wish that men should do to you, do you likewise to them.*[^5]

10. He says that the work of the natural law is the relation, by inclination, of all toward all, one in mind. For those whose nature is governed by reason naturally have one disposition. And of those whose disposition is the same, both the mode in character and the course of life are naturally, and manifestly, one. And of those whose mode of character and course of life is the same, one, clearly, and the same is the bond of relation toward one another by inclination, leading all by one inclination to the one principle of nature; in which there is in no way the division of nature which now prevails through self-love. But the written law, restraining by fear of penalties the disorderly impulses of the more foolish, accustoms them by teaching to look only to the distribution of the equal, according to which the strength of righteousness, confirmed by time, passes over into nature, making fear a disposition, gently and little by little strengthened by the inclination toward the good, and custom a habit, purified by the forgetting of former things and bringing forth together with itself mutual love.

11. The written law, he says, preventing injustice by fear, accustoms to what is just; and in time custom makes a disposition that loves justice, from which there supervenes a fixed habit for the good, bringing on forgetfulness of the wickedness that went before.

12. But the law of grace teaches those who are led by it to imitate God himself immediately, who so loved us above himself, if it is lawful to say so, and that when we were enemies through sin,[^6] that he came even [1353] into the essence that is ours without change, being above all essence; and superessentially accepted nature, and became man, and willed to be reckoned one of men, and did not refuse to make our condemnation his own; and deified us by grace as far as he himself by economy became man: that we may learn not only to hold to one another naturally and to love one another as ourselves spiritually, but also to care for one another above ourselves divinely, and to make this the proof of love toward one another, eagerly to prefer, by virtue, the voluntary death for one another. *For there is no other greater love than this,* he says, *that one lay down his life for his friend.*[^7]

13. The law of nature is, to say it in sum, natural reason taking sense under its hand for the removal of irrationality, according to which there is the division of things naturally joined. The written law is natural reason which, after the removal of irrationality according to sense, has taken on also a spiritual longing that holds together the mutual cohesion toward what is kindred. And the law of grace is reason established above nature, transforming nature without change to deification, and showing incomprehensibly to the nature of men, as in an image, the archetype that is above essence and nature, and providing the permanence of ever-well-being.[^8]

14. To hold the neighbour as oneself is to care only for his life unto being; which belongs to the natural law. To love the neighbour as oneself[^9] is to provide for the neighbour also unto well-being according to virtue; which the written law enjoins. And to love the neighbour above oneself is most proper to the law of grace.

15. He who is kept from the occasions of the body that lead to pleasure learns principles of Providence, which restrains the inflaming matter of the passions; and he who receives the scourges of the body that lead to pain is taught principles of judgement, which cleanses him by involuntary toils from previous defilements.

16. If the word of Scripture set the prophet,[^10] grieving over the booth and the gourd (I mean the flesh and the pleasure of the flesh), over against God, who holds fast to Nineveh,[^11] it is clear that, of the things precious and lovely to men, this which has appeared dear to God is far better and more precious than all beings, much more than the things that are not,[^12] and that seem to be by prejudice alone, according to a mistaken judgement, of which there is no principle at all as to existence; but only an imagination, deceiving the mind and supplying to the passion an empty shape for things that are not, but not a hypostasis.[^13]

[1356] 17. The exact knowledge of the oracles of the Spirit is naturally revealed only to those worthy of the Spirit; who, having through much care for the virtues cleansed the mind of the soot of the passions, like a clean and translucent mirror, at the first approach receive the knowledge of divine things imprinted on them and falling in upon them like a face.[^14] But for those whose life has been spotted all over with the stains of the passions, it is hardly possible even from some likely conjectures to infer the knowledge of divine things, let alone to presume to understand and express it with exactness.

18. He who has received, as the shape of the mind, the knowledge from the virtues in the divine Spirit is said to suffer divine things, because he has taken it on not by nature as to existence, but by grace as to participation. But he who has not received the knowledge that is from grace, even if he says something of knowledge, does not know by experience the power of what is said. For bare learning does not give knowledge by habit.

19. A mind purified to the summit through the virtues is naturally, and fittingly, taught thoroughly the principles of the virtues, making the knowledge stamped from them in godlike manner its own face. For every mind in itself is formless and unstamped, having an acquired shape: either the knowledge that subsists from the virtues in the Spirit, or the ignorance that supervenes from the passions.

20. Everyone who has fallen from divine love has the law in the flesh reigning through pleasure,[^15] which is neither able nor willing to keep any divine commandment. For having set the pleasure-loving life above the life and kingdom conducted according to virtue in the Spirit of God, he has drawn upon himself ignorance instead of knowledge.

21. He who does not cross over by mind to the divine comeliness of the letter of the law that is within, in the spirit, naturally begets the pleasure-loving relation of inclination, that is, worldly passionate attachment and love of the world, from the utterance alone, in word, of the learning of the law.

22. Shame of mouth is the world-loving meditation of the thoughts in the mind, and body-loving. For in that in which the world is naturally born, by the relation of inclination, from the bodily fashioning that appears in the letter of the law (that is, the disposition that loves the world), there is also the pleasure-loving meditation of the thoughts in the mind. For toward that to which we are disposed relationally, toward that we also possess meditation in the mind.

[1357] 23. Or again, shame of mouth is the movement of the mind that brings form upon the passions and fashions, for sense, the beauty that is according to pleasure. For without the inventive power in the mind, passion is in no way led to the fashioning of a shape. Or anathema[^16] is also the material and unlovely and unfigured movement of the passions; and shame of mouth, the movement of the mind that gives form to the passion for sense, and furnishes to the conceptions matter suited to the passion.

24. He who believes that God ordained according to the law the sacrifices and the feasts, the Sabbaths and the new moons, for bodily luxury and rest[^17] will altogether and in every way come under the liability of the activity of the passions, and the shame of the filth of the shameful thoughts upon them; and he will be under the world that is corrupted, and the body-loving meditation in thoughts; and under the matter and the form of the passions, able to hold nothing else precious but the things that are corrupted.

25. He who has been persuaded that it is a divine ordinance to live luxuriously in bodily fashion according to the law takes gluttony as a gift of God to live with in joy, from which he begets the modes that defile the activity of the senses by misuse.

26. When the contemplative part of the soul embraces, to live with it, the luxury according to the law in the letter as divine on account of the commandment, it pursues the use of the senses contrary to nature, allowing the use of no activity to show through according to nature. For it begets both the habit and the activity of the passions, and brings gluttony home as divine, for the coming-to-be of the modes that soil the senses by misuse, to the destruction of the natural principles and seeds in beings.

27. No one who attends only to the bodily worship of the law could at all admit a natural principle or thought, because the symbols are not naturally the same as nature. For he who remains in the symbols of the law cannot see the nature of beings according to reason, and secure the principles placed essentially in beings by the creator, because of the difference of the symbols from the nature of beings.

28. He who considers the belly God, and prides himself in shame as in glory,[^18] knows to hold with zeal, as divine, only the passions of dishonour; and for this reason he tends only temporal things, that is, matter and form, and the fivefold activity of the senses by misuse. For sense, entwined with matter and form by mixture, is wont to produce passion, and to kill and destroy the principles according to nature. For passion and nature, in the principle of being, in no way coexist with each other. For the principle of nature cannot [1360] at all appear together with passion, just as neither is passion born together with nature in coming-to-be.

29. He who does not believe that Scripture is spiritual does not perceive his own poverty in knowledge, but is wasted by famine. For famine is, truly, the failing of the goods known by experience itself, and the total lack and scarcity of the spiritual foods that hold the soul together.[^19] For how will anyone consider famine or loss the removal of things never at all known to him?

30. Truly the people of the faithful and of those who have come to know the truth suffers famine, and the soul of each one, having let go the spiritual contemplation in grace, and having become the typical servitude's in the letter, not nourishing the mind with the grandeurs of the concepts, but filling the sense with passionate imagination by the bodily figurations of the Scriptural symbols.

31. Everyone who does not admit the spiritual contemplation of the holy Scripture has thrust away together with it, like the Jews, both the natural and the written law, and has been ignorant of the law in grace, according to which deification is given to those led according to it. He, then, who takes the written law bodily does not nourish the soul with virtues; and he who does not apply himself to the principles of beings does not feast the mind lavishly with the manifold wisdom of God;[^20] and he who does not know the great mystery of the new grace does not exult in the hope of the deification to come. Therefore the lack of the contemplation according to the written law has following upon it the want of the manifold wisdom of God understood according to the natural law, which has following upon it, in every way, the ignorance of the deification to be given by grace according to the new mystery.

32. Every mind according to Christ, clear-sighted and strong in sight, always aspires to and seeks the face of the Lord.[^21] And the face of the Lord is the true contemplation and knowledge of divine things according to virtue, seeking which he is taught the cause of his own want and lack. For as the face is characteristic of each one, so also spiritual knowledge characterizes the Divine reflectively.[^22] Which he who seeks is said to seek the face of the Lord.
