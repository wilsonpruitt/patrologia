---
[1361] … taking the commandment as pleasure of the flesh,[^1] and clinging bodily, according to sense, to the matter alone of the letter.

33. He who worships bodily according to the Law begets, as matter, sin in activity, and, as form, materially fashions upon it the mind's assent to the pleasures of the senses suited to it. But he who receives Scripture spiritually puts to death the activity as matter, and the assent as the form of sin, together with the modes of the misuse of sense for pleasure, through the natural thoughts, on the height of contemplation.

34. The matter and the form, and the five modes of the five senses that are about the matter and the form by misuse (I mean the passionate and contrary-to-nature entanglement of the senses with sensible things, that is, with the things under time and flux), after the passing-by of the bodily worship in the Law according to the letter, and the crossing-over of ignorance, the spiritual law, or mind, hands over to be put to death by the loftier principles and thoughts of natural contemplation: that these, having come to be on the height of the law of spiritual contemplation, may abolish and put to death the most universal relation, in the symbols, of the things under time toward sense and body.

35. Without natural contemplation no one discerns the incongruity[^2] of the symbols of the Law with respect to divine things. For unless one, having first examined naturally the incongruity of divine and intelligible things in the symbols, shall long to come in mind, having put sense wholly outside the divine sanctuaries, to the comeliness of the intelligibles, he cannot be released once for all from the bodily variety in the types; in which, as long as he walks clinging to the letter, he fittingly does not relieve the want that comes from the famine of knowledge, having condemned himself, like the deceiving serpent, to eat the earth of Scripture, that is, its body;[^3] and not, according to Christ, feeding on the heaven of Scripture, that is, its spirit and soul, which is the heavenly and angelic bread: I mean the spiritual contemplation and knowledge of the Scriptures in Christ, which God gives, abundantly supplied, to those who love him, as it is written: *He gave them the bread of heaven; man did eat the bread of angels.*[^4]

36. The reception of Scripture by sense toward the body, as being plainly begetting of passions and of the relational disposition toward temporal and flowing things, that is, of the passionate activity of the senses toward sensible things, we must abolish like the children and descendants of Saul,[^5] [1364] through natural contemplation on the mountain, that is, in the anagogy of the divine oracles, if indeed we aspire to be filled with divine grace.

37. The Law understood according to the letter alone wrongs the truth, and so does the people of the Jews, and anyone who is their imitator in understanding, circumscribing the power of the Law by the letter alone, and not admitting, for the manifestation of the mystical knowledge hidden in the letter, natural contemplation, which is the middle between types and truth: leading away from the former those who are being led, and leading them back up to the latter; but as it were denying it altogether, and putting it outside the mystagogy of divine things. Those, therefore, who tend the divine spectacles must kill this bodily and temporary reception of the Law, as being under time and flowing, through natural contemplation, as on a mountain, on the height of knowledge.

38. He utterly abolishes the bodily meaning[^6] of Scripture who by practice, through natural contemplation, kills the pleasure-loving and body-loving relation toward unstable and flowing matter that arose in the soul from the written Law; slaughtering, as the children and descendants of Saul, the earth-bound understanding of the Law, through the mediation of natural contemplation, as on a mountain, on the height of knowledge; and making manifest before the Lord, through confession, that is, the former reception of the Law toward the body. For this is what the lovers of learning can understand by *to expose to the sun before the Lord*. *And they exposed them to the sun*, it says, *on the mountain before the Lord*:[^7] bringing into the light, through knowledge, that is, the preconception that went astray according to the letter; which is, to make manifest on the height of contemplation that the letter of the Law is dead, through knowledge in the spirit.

39. *The letter*, it says, *kills; but the spirit gives life.*[^8] Therefore what is of a nature to kill must be killed by the life-giving spirit. For that the bodily element of the Law and the divine, that is, the letter and the spirit, should coexist with each other in activity at once and in the same respect is utterly impossible; since what can take life away is not of a nature even to agree with what provides life according to nature.

40. The spirit is what bestows life; the letter is what takes life away. Therefore the letter and the spirit cannot both act in the same respect, just as the life-giving cannot coexist with the corrupting either.

[1365] 41. Mystical circumcision is the complete removal of the mind's passionate relation to the introduced coming-to-be. For, applying ourselves naturally to things, we know that the removal of the integrity which is from God according to nature is not perfection. For nature, mutilated by art and through contrivance putting off what belongs to it from God by the principle of its making, does not make perfection; lest we bring in art as stronger than God for the confirming of justice, and make the contrived deficiency of nature a filling-up of a deficiency in justice according to its making. But from the local[^9] position of the member that is circumcised we are taught to perform by inclination[^10] the circumcision of the passionate disposition in the soul; by which the inclination is regulated to run together rather with nature, correcting the passionate law of the acquired coming-to-be.

42. The foreskin is natural. And everything natural is a work of divine making, and very good, according to the voice that says: *And God saw all things that he had made, and behold, they were very good.*[^11] But the Law, commanding the foreskin to be removed through circumcision as unclean, brings in God correcting his own work through art, which is most impious even to conceive. Therefore he who applies himself naturally to the symbols of the Law knows that God does not correct nature through art, but commands the passible part of the soul that is obedient to reason to be circumcised, which is typically signified through the bodily member; and this, knowledge, through courage in practice, is of a nature to put away from the inclination. For the circumcising priest signifies knowledge, which has, like iron against passion, the courage of reason in practice. For the tradition of the Law is abolished when the spirit prevails over the letter.

43. The Sabbath is rest from the passions and from the motion of the mind about the nature of beings; or complete inactivity of the passions, and universal cessation of the mind's motion about things made, and complete crossing-over to the Divine; in which he who has arrived through virtue and knowledge, as far as is permitted, must not, as with sticks of wood,[^12] take thought for any matter whatever that kindles the passions, nor indeed gather up the principles of nature at all; lest we dogmatize God, after the manner of the Greeks, as delighting in passions or measured by the limits of nature: him whom perfect silence alone has cried aloud, and complete unknowing by way of pre-eminence presents.

[1368] 44. A crown of goodness[^13] is pure faith, flowered, after the manner of precious stones, with the lofty utterances of the doctrines and with spiritual words and concepts, binding round the God-loving mind as a head. Or rather, the crown of goodness is the very Word of God, who by the variety of the modes of providence and judgement, that is, by self-control over voluntary passions and endurance of involuntary ones, encircles the mind as a head, and by participation in the grace of deification makes the mind itself more beautiful than itself.

45. Here he says[^14] that self-control is a work of Providence, as purifying the passions of inclination; and endurance, he says, is an achievement of judgement, as arrayed against involuntary temptations, and being a symbol of practical philosophy, bringing across to virtue, as from Egypt, from sin, those held fast by it.

46. It was not because he wished the days to be honoured by men that God commanded the Sabbath and the new moons and the feasts to be honoured; for so he would have decreed, by commandment of the Law, that they worship the creation rather than the Creator,[^15] supposing the days to be venerable by nature and therefore to be worshipped; but he indicated that he himself is honoured symbolically through the days. For he himself is the Sabbath, as the rest of the soul from its toils in the flesh and the cessation of labours for justice; the Pasch, as the liberator of those held fast by the bitter slavery of sin; Pentecost, as the beginning and end of beings, and the principle in which all things by nature subsist.[^16] Consider how the Law destroys those who understand it bodily, persuading them to worship the creation rather than the Creator, and to consider venerable by nature the things that came to be for their sake, having failed to know him for whose sake they themselves came to be.

47. The world is a limited place and a circumscribed rest; time is a circumscribed motion; whence also the motion of the life of the things in it is subject to alteration. But when nature, having passed through place and time in activity and in notion, that is, through the things without which it is not, namely limited rest and motion, is joined immediately to providence, it finds providence to be a principle, simple and stable by nature, and having no circumscription in any way, and therefore no motion at all.

48. Existing temporally in the world, [1369] nature has its motion subject to alteration, because of the limited rest of the world and the course of time by way of becoming-other; but when it comes to be in God, because of the natural monad of him in whom it has come to be, it will have an unmoved rest and a stable motion-in-sameness, coming to be eternally about the same and one and only; which the word knows[^17] to be immediate about the first cause, the abiding foundation of the things made by it.

49. The mystery of Pentecost is the immediate union of the things provided for with Providence; that is, the union of nature with the principle according to the conception of Providence, in which there is no reflection whatever of time and coming-to-be. Again, the Word[^18] is our trumpet, as sounding divine and ineffable knowledge into us; the expiation, as, having become like us, dissolving in himself the charges against us, and deifying by the gift of grace in the Spirit the nature that sinned; the feast of Tabernacles,[^19] as the practice of our unchangeableness about the good according to the God-imitating habit, and the bond that holds together our transformation into immortality.

50. He who delights in bare bloody sacrifices, being passionate, prepares those who sacrifice to be zealous about the passions. For what genuinely reveres is wont to delight in the things in which what is worshipped delights. Hence the word knows as sacrifices rather the slaughter of the passions and the offering of the natural powers: of which the ram is the type of reason; the bull bears the symbol of anger; and the goat is the indication of desire.

51. We know spiritual sacrifices to be not only the mortification of the passions slaughtered by *the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God*,[^20] and the motion[^21] by intent, as of blood, of the whole life in the flesh, but also the offering of the habits of philosophy and of all the powers according to nature, dedicated to God and offered whole as a burnt offering by the fire of grace in the Spirit unto the divine lot.

[1372] 52. The earthly meaning of Scripture, when it rules the soul, casts off the natural principles, obliterating them by the misuse of the powers according to nature. For this one, while living, truly consumes and persecutes and destroys,[^22] circumscribing the Law by the flesh alone, the principles and thoughts according to nature, I mean, honouring as divine the passions of dishonour;[^23] which the thoughts according to nature, receiving licence from the law of the Spirit, kill and put to death together.

53. As soon as one pursues rationally the philosophy of the virtues, at once he has also naturally transferred the reception of the Scriptures to the spirit, worshipping God practically in newness of spirit through the lofty objects of contemplation, and not in oldness of letter,[^24] through the lowlier reception of the Law by sense toward the body, as one who becomes, like the Jews, a nurturer of passions and a servant of sin.

54. As soon as one ceases receiving Scripture by sense toward the body, at once he also runs up in mind toward the spirit through the mediation of nature, doing spiritually those things which the Jew, performing bodily, has God angered.

55. Every mind lofty and raised up according to God slaughters in the same act both the activity of the passions and the unseemly motion of the thoughts; and besides, the licentious modes of the activity of the senses by misuse. For the passions are destroyed by the lofty thoughts of nature, being led in triumph[^25] in the lofty contemplation.

56. The grace of holy baptism is of a nature to abolish the might of sin, that is, *the way of thinking of the flesh*;[^26] and obedience in activity to the divine commandments, to kill it with *the sword of the Spirit*,[^27] that is, with the word of divine knowledge in the Spirit, crying mystically to the passion of sin, as the great Samuel to Agag:[^28] *Because thy sword has made women childless, thy mother shall be made childless today among women.*[^29]

57. The passion of gluttony has made many virtues childless, as with a sword, with the full-grown thought of pleasure. For it kills the seeds of temperance through incontinence; it corrupts the equity of justice through greed; it cuts through the continuity from nature of love for man through self-love. And to say it briefly, the passion of gluttony is destructive of all the offspring of virtue.

58. The passion of gluttony is of a nature to kill all the divine offspring of the virtues; but it itself is killed by the grace of faith and by obedience to the divine commandments, through the word of knowledge.
