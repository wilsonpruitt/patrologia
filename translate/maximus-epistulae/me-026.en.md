title: Letters 28–36
---
[620] LETTER 28

*Of the same, to Cyrisicius the bishop.*[^1]

Having been allotted, honoured Father, according to the grace of the [621] high-priesthood given thee, to be on earth an imitator of the divine goodness, thou hast left none of the manners that characterize it unfulfilled; but, having them all following one upon another in order, thou hast been zealous to adorn the mystery of the high-priesthood with the tunic of the virtues, woven from above through the Spirit[^2] — which tunic the demons can never rend, not even by a multitude of temptations, though they crucify you. Be zealous, then, to gather into one the scattered children of God.[^3] For this too is a characteristic mark of the divine goodness; and, having become as head of the honoured body of the holy Church of God, fit the members together with one another through the architecture of the Spirit;[^4] and, appointed herald of the divine teachings, call with a loud voice both those far off and those near;[^5] and bind them to thyself with the indissoluble bond of the love of the Spirit — if, to be sure, the expectation of the enemies has fled and has utterly vanished, on account of which they have been estranged so long from the land that bore them[^6] — so that thou too mayest be able to say, as befits Christ: Behold I, and the children whom God has given me.[^7] But count thy lowly and unworthy servant and suppliant worthy of thy honoured syllables[^8] on this matter, holy Father; and do not disdain, offering a sacrifice of praise, to commend him to God through prayers.[^9]

LETTER 29

*Of the same, to the same.*

God, seeking, came down from heaven, and, longing for the sheep, put it on; and so, having saved the wandering one, placing it upon his shoulders, he brought it back with compassion to its fellows of the pasture.[^10] But thou, all-holy Father and shepherd, only filling with the Holy Spirit the reed-pipe of thy teaching, hast brought back to thyself complete the herd of rational sheep, which unwillingly departed from its homeland, but willingly runs to thy voice, the voice of the good and skilled shepherd, as having perfect discernment of what is profitable. For it did not cross the long seas wandering, having disdained the divine pasture, but for the sake of a safety that preserves the divine offerings of the soul. But receive a flock that has not only remained unapproachable to wolves but has even become a destroyer of wolves, and is rich in many goods of the soul. And stretch forth, and prosper, and reign, because of truth and meekness and righteousness,[^11] as teacher of the divine doctrines of piety, and guide of a pure life and a dignified [624] conduct, and lawgiver of right discernment in all things; receiving especially, together with the said holy flock, also the sanctified lord abba George the presbyter,[^12] a holy man, possessing a life equal to his word, and through both a destroyer of the wicked power. But since I have been swallowed up by great grief, not bearing the separation from these, either receive me too and give me rest under your wings, or strengthen me through prayers to bear nobly the pain at the separation from those dearest.

LETTER 30

*Of the same, to John the bishop.*[^13]

Those who have examined by reason the nature of the things that are say that the power of fire draws to itself every subject; and the interpreters of the divine mysteries, comparing God symbolically to it, say that he too draws all who wish to obey his laws and who consent to embrace living holily;[^14] and, saying that the priesthood is, as in an image, a painting that aptly has the imitation of him, they declare that it too, by the equal law of sympathy according to grace, draws all who are under the same nature. Thou, then, holy Father, having been allotted to have on earth this image of God, call to thyself by manners and words of sympathy those who long to return to thee and desire, as in their prayers, to be shepherded by thee; and confirm with true promises, through thy honoured syllables, thy providence toward them in all things, with God — if the foul expectation of the enemies has utterly ceased, on account of which those endured so great a flight who held it safe to follow the law of God, which cries expressly: If they persecute you out of this city, flee into the other;[^15] and who preferred a praiseworthy cowardice to rashness — so that, trusting, after God, in thy words, they may make their return without fear, and may find, as rest from the toils by which, for virtue's sake and for obeying God who bids them flee their persecutors, they were bruised in this fearful sojourn abroad, thy merciful support of them in all things. Do not then disdain, holy Father, to receive thy suppliant and servant who beseeches thee; but honour with thine own syllables, as one honourable, him who is worthy of no honour; and through prayers commend him to Christ, who alone is honourable, and who through himself honoured most honourably man, who was without honour; imitating whom in all things as Master and Lord, thou hast made thyself, by the grace of the Spirit, another such as he to those who see thee.

LETTER 31

*Of the same, to the same.*

As a ray gently draws the healthy [625] sight, which by nature delights in light, and gives it a share of its own brightness, so the true priesthood too, being through all things a characteristic mark of the blessed Godhead for those on earth, draws to itself every soul that is God-loving and divine in its habit, and gives it a share of its own knowledge, peace and love; that, having brought each power of the soul to the end of its own operation, it may present to God, deified through all things, those whom it initiates into the mysteries. For the end of the soul's rational operation is true knowledge; of the desiring, love; of the incensive, peace; as also the end of the true priesthood is, through these, to be made god and to make god.[^16] For it is to this end that we have by nature reasoning, to seek God; and we received desire, to long for him alone; and we were given anger, to contend for him alone. And the end of the seeking is true knowledge; of the longing, unbroken and fiery love; of the divine contests, the peace that surpasses every mind.[^17] Knowing you, all-holy and all-blessed, to be doers and teachers of these highest goods, she who is truly the servant of God, and daughter and mother, the lady Eudocia the recluse,[^18] has returned to you with her holy flock, showing you, honoured by God, victors over all who were zealous to have her with themselves. For she is truly a servant, as one who fulfils the divine commandments; a daughter, as having through all things in her habit an unvarying likeness to God; a mother, as one who also begets in others the habit of virtue. Rejoice, then, henceforth and exult, having taken back the more honourable part of your holy flock; and especially having received too my sanctified lord George the presbyter, truly God's tilled field, and the most approved husbandman of the divine and great George,[^19] competent in deed and word before God and all the people[^20] in the science of the husbandry of souls, and for this reason lovely to all who love the Lord. But since meanwhile I have become half-dead and cut in half, torn off from their profitable companionship, either receive me too as a sickened member and heal me, or console me through prayers and persuade me to bear the separation dispassionately. For this too is proper to the divine and true priesthood.

LETTER 32

*Of the same, to abba Polychronius.*[^21]

Since we are about to keep the feast of the burial and resurrection of Christ, who was put to death and buried for us according to the flesh, let us examine whether we too have been put to death and buried according to the old man, who is corrupted according to the desires of deceit, and have risen according to the new man, who is [628] renewed according to the image of him who created him.[^22] The sign of the putting to death of the first is complete freedom from the passions; of the resurrection of the second, the spotless and true boldness of conscience, and the illumination of knowledge in the mind. And if these things have come to be in us, let us keep the feast with Christ God; but if not, let us strive at least not to betray the Word to the passions like Judas, or not to deny him like Peter; lest perhaps we be unable to repent, and be led away regretting without profit, or to weep bitterly and wash off the brands of the denial.[^23] For denial of the Word is the refusal, through fear, to do the good; and betrayal is the purposed impulse toward sin in operation: of which may it not befall us to have experience.

LETTER 33

*Of the same, to the same.*

If, according to Job, the great victor over temptations, The life of men is a trial;[^24] and the world, as the Lord said, lies in the evil one;[^25] and, Many are the afflictions of the righteous, as the great David declares;[^26] and we must by all means have the temptations and the wrestling, whether against men or against demons, according to the just judgement of God, as the holy Apostle says[^27] — let us beseech God to give patience, that we may be able to be drawn up out of the mire of the passions and to stand upon the unshaken rock[^28] — I mean the dispassion in Christ through grace — there having been put to death in us the life that tempts, and the world that does evil, and men, and demons: that is, the passionate life, and the pleasurable deceit of sense, and the fleshly sufferings, and the passionate movements of the soul, and the involuntary inflictions that come upon these, which have been called afflictions. Let us endure, then, that we may be saved; for he who endures to the end shall be saved.[^29] The peace of Christ be with us. Amen.

LETTER 34

*Of the same, to the same.*

Going away to his passion, the Lord left us peace; and again, having risen from the dead, he gave us peace.[^30] Hinting at what, through these? That we must carry ourselves through contrary seasons dispassionately: neither, falling under grievous circumstances, to be without the better hope, nor, carried round with bright occasions, to thrust away the thought that tutors us; but to be the same, both suffering and faring well. And this does not come about unless we first tread upon the asp and the basilisk — that is, rise above disobedience to the divine words and the acceptance of wicked thoughts — and trample the lion and the dragon,[^31] that is, [629] anger and desire; which being done away, all peace will be ours, nothing being able to move us toward any of the things that abide here. Let us master anger and desire, and we have cast down the whole house of the foreigners at once, together with those in it,[^32] killing together with the passions the passionate life and way of living.

LETTER 35

*Of the same, to the same.*

If, after that great and fearful desert, the Israelite people receives the land of rest; and — the most astounding report of all[^33] — God, who became man for our sake, being also Lord of all, enters into his glory through sufferings and cross and death, there being no other mode appointed for him, as it is written: Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?[^34] — let us not lose heart, growing faint in afflictions; but let us look to the author of our salvation and perfecter, Jesus,[^35] and let us bear nobly what befalls us. For the end of every affliction is joy; and of every toil, rest; and of every dishonour, glory; and, simply, the end of all the pains borne for virtue is to be with God always, and to enjoy the everlasting relief that has no end. But pray, Father, that we may attain it, having crossed the sea of life well, and having kept the intelligible footsteps of the soul unwetted by its brine.

LETTER 36

*Of the same, to the same.*

Blessed be our master to the Lord,[^36] and blessed the garden of his heart, which Jesus, the true husbandman, planted and has filled with immortal plants of righteousness; of whose fruits we too have partaken, filled with his spiritual vegetables — with which the great Apostle too knows how to nourish those too weak for the unmediated beholding of divine things,[^37] feeding them, as with vegetables, with the natural contemplation of visible things, and through the magnificence of the things that appear leading them by the hand to its Creator. Imitating him, our blessed Father and teacher too has affectionately entertained us, setting before us symbolically, through the vegetables, the understanding of this creation. But give a share of solid food too,[^38] and stir our soul, slackened by pleasures and without appetite for divine things, astringing its aspiration with the subtler principle of providence and judgement;[^39] that thou mayest not nourish thy disciples with vegetables only, teaching only the principles of the things that come to be and pass away; but with wheat and wine and oil too — the principles of the things eternal — strengthen them:[^40] giving the principle that holds the virtues together as firm bread, and with the principle of the unforgetting [632] knowledge, as with wine, warming the heart[^41] of thy followers to a god-making ecstasy; and, as oil, implanting in thy servants the peaceful and untroubled habit of meekness, which nourishes, and keeps unquenched, the flame in them.
