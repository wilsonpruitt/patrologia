[641] LETTER 44

*Of the same, to John the Cubicularius, concerning adoption.*[^1]

Through the law and the prophets God, as good, long ago gave men in many ways the law of love toward one another;[^2] at the end of the times he fulfilled it through himself, [644] as the lover of man, having become man: not loving us only as himself, but even above himself, as the power of the mystery, too, clearly teaches those who have piously received the confession of faith in him. For if willingly, as one liable, he by volition offered himself to passion and death for us, who ought to suffer as liable, it is plain to everyone who has perceived the grace even in some small measure that he loved above himself us, for whom he by volition delivered himself to death; and that, as more than good (though the word be bold), he chose, above his own glory according to nature, the outrage by economy, timely undergone for us, as more honourable. For a surpassing honour and an abounding glory of God, and a progress and showing of the natural glory more than ever into the open, has truly been established: the return to him of those who had gone astray. And nothing is more his own, by way of glory, than the salvation of men; for which every word and every mystery exists; and, the most mysterious of all mysteries, God himself, for love's sake, truly became man by the assumption of flesh ensouled intellectually and rationally, and without change received into himself the passions of the nature, that he might save man, and give himself to us men as an outline for virtue, and a living image of goodwill and love toward himself and toward one another, able to put all to shame toward the requital that is owed.

Since, then, it is God's law, both old and new, that we love one another as much as we too have been loved,[^3] and make one another's misfortunes so much our own that, toward one another, by way of requital, through the relation of love, we exchange one another's dispositions, according to the commandment that bids us rejoice with them that rejoice and weep with them that weep:[^4] I beseech my God-guarded and blessed master, who has one and only aim, to follow, through an equal love for man, Jesus who has passed through the heavens,[^5] bound by no bond of kindred at all to this deceiving world and world-ruler. For truly the present world is a deceiver, ever luring through guile to the desire of perishing things, and binding fast in the slavery of material things those who by nature are free and heirs of the heavenly goods; by sporting through pleasure, and stealthily robbing those who endure to be held fast by these things.

Knowing, then, as I said, that my blessed master has one aim, that of being saved, I beseech him, receiving him who with him is my master, the most magnificent *illustris*, the lord, a man God-favoured,[^6] who bears this my modest note, to become all things to him in which he needs his help after God, or rather, with God, just as we wish God and our neighbour to become to ourselves [645] in whatever we may need.[^7] For he is truly a good man, and of every work of righteousness [?],[^8] and after God the genuine protector of my sojourning; and the eager consoler of the many, frequent and grievous illnesses; and, simply, according to the power that is his, he has succoured both me and many others. The Lord grant him to find mercy of him in that day,[^9] because he has been the refreshment and consolation of many. Hence, if I have found grace in the eyes of my blessed master, and he holds me of one vote with those beloved of him according to God, he will by all means take his part with all zeal, permitting nothing unjust, so far as it comes into his power, to be done against him. For this the law of love dictates, and this the mystery of God's most divine sojourn among men through flesh wills: that he may return, with God, recounting the benefits of my blessed master and the unadulterated zeal of his help toward him; and I shall receive an increase of boasting, having obtained from God to have on earth such a master, eager for every good work, and the ready and most sincere protector of all who are in need.

May God, who alone is good and the lover of man, who for the sake of our salvation dispenses all things to all savingly, himself hold thy right hand, my master, and guide thee into the way of his righteousness,[^10] and count thee worthy of the heavenly kingdom, having laid fair claim here to the divine gifts of grace. May the God of all things and our Lord Jesus Christ preserve our most pious and all-gentle emperors,[^11] and strengthen the might of their pious reign, and speak in their hearts concerning us, their lowly servants, things good and peaceable. And, that I may say something with boldness (for pain makes me bold; yet this itself is a prayer dear to God): may he forgive them that they consented to recall, even for a moment, the all-praised prefect of this land, shining as a lamp in a squalid place,[^12] and in a manner setting all free from the pain of not seeing; a man God-loving, faithful, pious, good, devout, temperate, continent, forbearing, meek, compassionate, merciful, a feeder of the poor, a feeder of strangers, a feeder of orphans, a nurse of the sick, a nurse of the old, a protector of widows, a guardian of monks, without possessions [harmless],[^13] a lover of the Church; and, to sum it up, the friend of all, both living and dead: winning the living by every manner of tender affection, and the dead, honouring what pertains to their burial with the beauties of buildings, as Joseph of Arimathea did the Lord who for us consented to be put to death in the flesh;[^14] and, besides these things, a most ardent zealot for the right word according to the apostolic faith, a herald of a life of righteousness. [648] And to say it briefly: others have been zealous on earth for other things, each according to his aspiration; but George's[^15] work and endeavour is the hospitals, and the homes for the aged, and the hostels for strangers, and the poorhouses, and the monasteries, and the burial-grounds for strangers; and to clothe and to nourish all the multitudes of the poor and the needy that are scattered in herds through the cities, family by family; and to pass beyond the bounds of the east and the west, of the northern and the southern region, with the rays of his love for man.

These things being so, who of those who in any way lay claim to piety does not know how great a goodwill of God toward the whole the well-pleasingness of such works summoned, being ever joined to his eagerness and flourishing?[^16] The contrary also, which befalls through the idleness of these works, I shall pass over in silence for its ill omen. And who will be able to measure the tears poured out in streams from every eye by those who inhabit this land at his departure, when all clung about him and dragged him back by force toward themselves, and in a manner would not let him board the ship; and cried out in lamentations, as though seeking, by family and by age: some, the provider of their poverty; others, the steward of their plenty; the widows, the protector; the orphans, the father; the poor, the lover of the poor, and the strangers, the lover of strangers; and brothers, the lover of the brethren; the sick, the physician; those in misfortune, the consolation; grey hairs, the staff; and, simply, all, him who had become all things to all, that he might gain all, and for all be gained to God?[^17]

Wherefore, according to the grace given thee by God, my blessed master, by what modes and persons thou knowest, if thou couldst make him come back again, know that in the appointment thou wilt have given a great sacrifice to God, and to our God-upheld emperors a genuine goodwill and an unbreakable security.[^18] For no one has been a more genuine servant of their pious reign than he. Farewell to me, much beloved, and of all to me most honoured, genuine servant of God.

LETTER 45

*Of the same, to the same.*

In every generation, from time to time, God, providing for the nature of human things and caring for the salvation of us all, has shown forth one who would live in holiness, flashing out like light with the virtues, as one by purpose capable of the divine laws; having given him as much strength as, in all likelihood, the people being led needed for conversion; that the rest of men, attending to him as to a lamp of the light of works of righteousness, may themselves also order their own life according to God, no passion from ignorance tripping them up. And for us, in due time, a lamp and a rule of virtue has been shown forth: George, the Christ-loving prefect; he who is so great and so much in virtue, and who received from above a power so rich in grace, and surpassing [649] those formerly elected and appointed to this office by as much as we, that is, our generation, weighed down by a great burden of vice, needed for recovery, even out of the multitude of those who do us evil. A man, if any other of the most God-loving of old and now, good and loving of man, merciful, meek, forbearing, long-suffering, humble-minded, temperate, free from anger, free from love of money, loving, unmindful of injuries, beneficent, a lover of strangers, a lover of the poor, compassionate, sharing, most earnest, watchful, a lover of the Church; and, what is more precious than all, a most ardent zealot for the pious word according to the right faith; and the rest are contained, in sequence, in the former letter,[^19] according to which I have also in part signified to my blessed master through another note. For to encompass by the power of speech all the man's achievements is among the impossible and the inadmissible things. And I have declared these things only so that my God-guarded master, knowing the man's love of God, as being, through the very deeds, being under divine grace,[^20] may receive and honour him; and commend him to our pious and all-gentle emperors, who image the divine might on earth; and persuade them not to admit the unjust tongues of lawless men, which work deceit like a sharpened razor, and love vice above goodness;[^21] so that place may not be given to falsehood against the truth, nor injustice take boldness against righteousness, and, simply, vice against all virtue, through the exchange and confusion of these into one another. For it is written: To fine a just man is not good; nor is it holy to plot against just rulers.[^22] For this it befits my blessed master to work, being a lover of wisdom and prudence;[^23] which I pray may be guarded ever for my God-guarded master, being the guard of his soul and of his body. And the bearer of this note of my nothingness, who has the prudence of his soul older than the age of his body, I beseech be received fittingly, for the understanding that becomes him, as one who lays claim to the divine study of the words,[^24] and who does not disdain to have a genuine relation toward me, the lowly.

## Notes
[^1]: P/T. John the Cubicularius, chamberlain at the court of Constantinople, Maximus's friend and the addressee of Letters 2–4, 10, 12, 27, 43; Letters 44 and 45 commend their bearers to him and plead for George, prefect of Africa (Letter 1), recalled to the capital. T. Migne prints περὶ θετικῆς (plate-checked 2026-11-06), literally "concerning adoption" (θετός, adopted); Combefis's Latin title reads *commendatitia*, "of commendation", and the letter is indeed one of commendation. Rendered as printed. The numeral prints ΙΑ΄ for ΜΔ΄ (Latin XLIV).
[^2]: S. πολυτρόπως: cf. Heb 1:1; the law of mutual love, Lev 19:18; Matt 22:39. T. νομοθετήσας ("gave … the law") is read on a damaged plate (plate doubt against ὑποθετήσας); the Latin's *sancita lege* agrees.
[^3]: S. John 13:34; 15:12.
[^4]: S. Rom 12:15. The Latin adds *Apostolus*.
[^5]: S. Heb 4:14. "World-ruler" (κοσμοκράτωρ) is the devil: cf. Eph 6:12; John 12:31. πατριᾶς δεσμῷ, "bond of kindred" (Latin *familiae gentisque … amore*).
[^6]: T. κύριον θεοχάριστον: Migne prints θεοχάριστον in lower case (plate-checked 2026-11-06), so it is an epithet, "God-favoured" (a man graced by God), not the name Theocharistus; Combefis's Latin likewise has *hominem Deo gratum*, "a man pleasing to God". τὸν σὺν αὐτῷ μου δεσπότην: the Latin has *cum eo pariter dominum meum*, "equally my master with him".
[^7]: T. Printed ὥστε, which does not govern βουλόμεθα; read ὥσπερ, "just as". The Latin paraphrases (*quaecumque nobis ut fiat Deus ac proximus, ipsi volumus*).
[^8]: T. The Greek prints παντὸς ἔργου δικαιοσύνης νέο, a fragment: the word that governed the genitive is lost or misread. The Latin breaks off at the same point (*omniumque justitiae operum ...*) and goes on *secundum Deum vetus patronus*, "after God an old protector".
[^9]: S. 2 Tim 1:18 (of Onesiphorus, who "often refreshed me", 1:16).
[^10]: S. cf. Ps 72:23–24; 22:3 LXX.
[^11]: T. πανήμερος, "all-gentle", a court epithet (Latin *clementissimos*); it recurs in Letter 45. The plural is the co-emperors of the day.
[^12]: S. 2 Pet 1:19. ὕπαρχος, here = the prefect (ἔπαρχος) of Africa.
[^13]: T. The brackets are printed: ἀκτήμονα [ἀπήμονα], Combefis's alternative reading inside the Greek. The Latin translates the bracketed word, *innocuum*.
[^14]: S. Matt 27:57–60.
[^15]: P. George, prefect of Africa, to whom Letter 1 is addressed; his departure by ship, the people's grief and Maximus's plea for his return are the matter of this letter and the next.
[^16]: T. προσὸν (neuter) does not agree with εὐαρέστησις; rendered as if προσοῦσα, "being joined". The Latin has no word for it (*ipsa semper ejus studio vigens ac alacritate*).
[^17]: S. 1 Cor 9:22, 19.
[^18]: T. δεδωκὼς τὴν προβολήν: George's appointment (his reinstatement in office) is the gift; the Latin renders *quod ejus praeficiendi provinciae auctor exstiteris*.
[^19]: T. The "former letter" is Letter 44, whose list of George's virtues this one abridges; the "other note" is the same letter.
[^20]: T. ὡς θείας δι᾿ αὐτῶν τῶν πραγμάτων ὑπὸ τῆς χάριτος ὄντα: Migne prints exactly this, with no participle (plate-checked 2026-11-06); rendered as printed, "being under grace" (cf. Rom 6:14). Combefis's Latin has *fultum*, but the Greek has no participle: *tanquam divina re-ipsa fultum gratia*, "as upheld in very deed by divine grace".
[^21]: S. Ps 51:4–5 LXX.
[^22]: S. Prov 17:26 (LXX οὐ καλόν; Maximus has οὐκ ἀγαθόν, and the Latin *non bonum* agrees).
[^23]: S. Wis 8:2; for prudence as a guard, cf. Prov 2:11.
[^24]: T. τῆς θείας τῶν λόγων σπουδῆς: the study of the divine words, that is, the Scriptures; the Latin has *divinae doctrinae*.
