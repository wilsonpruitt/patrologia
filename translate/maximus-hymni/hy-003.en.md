title: Hymni, 3
---
[1424] *III. Hymn of supplication to the holy Trinity.*

Remember me, of the root without beginning[^1]
Ineffable shoot,
And strange breath, to both[^2]
Equal, to offspring and to source,
My three-sunned form;[^3]
When thou judgest me, a sinner,
Save me, thy servant, from the fire,
Thine own, and from every dread
Tempest draw me up, Saviour;
And loose at once the bond
Of the swarm of my grievous evils.
And write me in thy book,
Escaped from this stream
Unwounded and safe.
And that I sing and honour,
In times without measure,
Thy might, grant by thy nod.

[^1]: T. The line initials of this hymn spell ΜΑΞΙΜΟΣ ΤΑΔΕ ΓΡΑΨΕΝ, "Maximus wrote these" (V6: noted, not imitated). The address runs through the three Persons: shoot (the Son) of the root without beginning (the Father), and the breath (the Spirit).
[^2]: T. ἀμφοῖν may be dative with ἶσον ("equal to both", taken here, γόνῳ καὶ ἀρχῇ in apposition) or genitive with ἄημα ("breath of both"). The Latin (*Utrisque gratus Spiritus, Filio Patrique compar*) does not settle it. The English keeps the word where the Greek has it and leans to the dative; a crux, and one that bears on the procession of the Spirit.
[^3]: T. μοι, ethical dative: "my". τρισήλιος, "three-sunned", the hymnographic word (V4).
