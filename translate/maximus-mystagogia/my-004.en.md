For the entrance into the church indicates not only the turning of the unbelievers to the true God, but also the correction through repentance of each of us who believe, yet set aside the commandments of the Lord through a licentious conduct and an unseemly life. For every man, whether murderer, or adulterer, or thief, or proud, or boaster, or insolent, or grasping, or avaricious, or slanderer, or rancorous, or easily led to anger and wrath, or reviler, or calumniator, or whisperer, or easily mastered by envy, or drunkard; and in a word, that I may not lengthen my discourse by enumerating all the species of vice, whoever is held fast by any vice whatever, when he ceases to be held by it willingly through practice and to act in it by intent, and changes his life for the better, choosing virtue in place of vice: such a one, let him be understood and said properly and truly to enter in with Christ, God and high priest, into virtue, which is understood figuratively as the church.

CHAPTER 10

*Of what things the divine readings are a symbol.*

The divine readings of the all-holy books, the teacher said, show forth the divine and blessed wishes and counsels of the all-holy God; through which each of us, proportionately, according to the power that is in him, receives the precepts of what is to be done; and we learn the laws of the divine and blessed contests, contending lawfully according to which we are counted worthy of the victory-worthy crowns of the kingdom of Christ.[^2]

CHAPTER 11

*Of what the divine chants are a symbol.*

The spiritual delight of the divine chants, he said, manifests the reflective[^3] pleasure of the divine goods, which stirs souls up to the pure and blessed yearning for God, and rouses them further to hatred of sin.

CHAPTER 12

*What the salutations of peace signify.*

Through the acclamations of peace that are made from within the sanctuary at the command of the high priest at each reading,[^4] the wise man defined, are shown the divine acceptances conveyed through the holy angels; through which God sets an end to the contests of those who lawfully contend for the truth against the opposing powers, [692] dissolving the invisible grapplings, and giving peace in the annulling of the body of sin,[^5] and giving the saints in return, for their labours on behalf of virtue, the grace of passionlessness;[^6] so that, released from warring, they may refit the powers of the soul for spiritual husbandry, that is, the working of the virtues; through which they broke up the ranks of the wicked spirits, the God and Word being their general, and scattering the bitter and hard-to-escape devices of the devil.

CHAPTER 13

*Of what, specifically in each one, the reading of the holy Gospel is a symbol, and the mystical things after it.*

Hence, immediately after these, the sacred ordinance of the holy church has legislated that the divine reading of the holy Gospel take place: which specifically introduces to the zealous the suffering of hardship for the sake of the word; after which the Word, as high priest of the contemplation of knowledge, coming to them from heaven, contracts for them the way of thinking[^7] of the flesh, as a kind of sensible world; thrusting away the thoughts that still bend down toward earth, and having led them thence, through the closing of the doors and the entrance of the holy mysteries, to the beholding of the intelligibles, both principles and things, their senses now shut, and having come to be outside flesh and world, he teaches them the unutterable things; gathered now through the kiss both to one another and to him, and bringing to him in return for his great benefaction toward them, gratefully, only the thankful confession on behalf of their own salvation, which the divine Creed[^8] intimates in riddle. Then, having set them among the number of the angels through the thrice-holy hymn,[^9] and having granted them the same science as theirs of the sanctifying theology, he brings them to God and Father, adopted as sons in the Spirit through the prayer through which they have been counted worthy to call God Father.[^10] And thence again, as having now passed scientifically through all the principles in beings, he leads them unknowably, through the One is Holy and what follows,[^11] to the unknown Monad, united by grace, and by participation likened to it in the identity undivided as far as possible.

CHAPTER 14

*Of what the divine reading of the holy Gospel is a symbol, and what it signifies generally.*

And generally, it intimates the consummation of this world. For after the divine reading of the holy Gospel the high priest comes down from the throne; and there takes place, through the ministers, the dismissal and expulsion of the catechumens[^12] and of the rest [693] who are unworthy of the divine sight of the mysteries about to be shown; signifying and prefiguring through itself the truth of which it is image and type, and as it were crying out in these things that after the Gospel of the kingdom has been preached, as it is written, in the whole inhabited world, for a testimony to all the nations, then shall the end come;[^13] when our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ comes at his second coming, from heaven, namely, with much glory[^14] — *For the Lord himself, with the voice of an archangel and with the trumpet of God, shall descend from heaven*, says the divine Apostle[^15] — and takes vengeance on the adversaries, and through the holy angels marks off the faithful from the unfaithful, and the unjust from the just, and the defiled from the holy;[^16] and simply, to put it briefly, those who have gone after the flesh from those who have walked by the Spirit of God;[^17] and for infinite and endless ages, as the truth of the divine oracles says, renders to each the just recompense according to the worth of what he has lived.[^18]

CHAPTER 15

*Of what the closing of the doors of the holy church, which takes place after the holy Gospel, is a symbol.*

The closing of the doors of the holy church of God, which takes place after the sacred reading of the holy Gospel and the expulsion of the catechumens, shows both the passing away of material things, and the entrance of the worthy, which will take place after that fearful marking-off and the more fearful sentence, into the intelligible world, that is, the bridal chamber of Christ; and the perfect casting off of the activity of deception in the senses.

CHAPTER 16

*What the entrance of the holy mysteries signifies.*

The entrance of the holy and venerable mysteries[^19] is the beginning and prelude (as that great elder said) of the new teaching that will take place in heaven about the economy of God toward us, and the revelation of the mystery of our salvation which is in the inner shrines of the divine hiddenness. *For I will not drink*, says God and Word to his own disciples, *henceforth of the fruit of the vine, until that day when I drink it new with you in the kingdom of my Father*.[^20]

CHAPTER 17

*Of what the divine kiss is a symbol.*

The spiritual kiss proclaimed to all prefigures and sketches beforehand the concord and oneness of inclination and identity in reason that there will be of all with one another, in faith and love, at the time [696] of the revelation of the ineffable goods to come; through which the worthy receive kinship[^21] with the Word and God. For the mouth is a symbol of the word,[^22] according to which above all all who have partaken of reason, as rational, grow together with all, and with the first and only Word, the cause of every word.

CHAPTER 18

*What the divine Creed signifies.*

The confession of the divine Creed made by all signifies beforehand the mystical thanksgiving that will take place in the age to come for the paradoxical principles and modes of the all-wise providence of God toward us by which we have been saved; through which the worthy show themselves grateful for the divine benefaction, having nothing else whatever besides this to bring in return for the infinite divine goods toward them.

CHAPTER 19

*What the doxology of the Trisagion signifies.*

The threefold acclamation of the sanctification of the divine hymnody made by all the faithful people intimates the union and equality of honour with the bodiless and noetic powers that will appear in the future; in which the nature of men will be taught, in concord with the powers above, through the identity of the unchangeable ever-moving around God, to hymn and to sanctify with three sanctifications the one Godhead of three hypostases.

CHAPTER 20

*Of what the holy prayer «Our Father, who art in heaven» is a symbol.*

The all-holy and venerable invocation of the great and blessed God and Father is a symbol of the enhypostatic and enhyparctic adoption that will be given by the gift and grace of the Holy Spirit; according to which, all human property being overcome and covered over by the visitation of grace, all the saints will be called and will be sons of God, as many as, through the virtues, from here and now have already brightened themselves splendidly and gloriously with the divine beauty of goodness.

CHAPTER 21

*What the end of the mystical sacred rite signifies, of the hymns proclaimed: that is, «One is holy, one is Lord», and what follows.*

The confession of One is Holy and what follows, made by all the people at the end of the mystical sacred rite, shows the gathering and union, beyond reason and mind, that will take place toward the one hidden of the divine simplicity, of those who have been perfected mystically and wisely according to God, in the [697] incorruptible age of the intelligibles;[^23] in which, gazing upon the light of the invisible and more-than-ineffable glory, they themselves too become receptive, with the powers above, of the blessed purity; after which, as the end of all, the communication of the mystery takes place, transforming into itself, and showing those who worthily partake to be like the good by cause,[^24] by grace and participation, lacking it in nothing, as far as is attainable and possible for men. So that they too can both be and be called gods by adoption[^25] according to grace, because of the whole God who has wholly filled them and left nothing of them empty of his presence.

CHAPTER 22

*How and in what manner the deifying and perfecting state of the soul too, considered by itself, specifically in each one, is contemplated through what has been said.*

Come then, walking through the same things by the same way and order, let us contemplate the same things again concerning the soul of knowledge too; and the mind that longs and wishes to ascend a little, as far as it can, together with the discourse, with reverence, to a higher contemplation, and to consider and perceive how the divine ordinances of the holy church lead the soul to its own perfection through true and active knowledge, let us not hinder, God leading it by the hand (if it seems good).

CHAPTER 23

*That the first entrance of the holy synaxis is a symbol of the virtues of the soul.*
