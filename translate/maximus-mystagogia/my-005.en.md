Then again passing over from these, and being gathered toward the one and only summit, which comprehends these principles in a unified way: I mean the holy Gospel, in which all the principles both of providence and of beings pre-subsist uniformly according to one power of comprehension. After which it is again possible and lawful for the God-loving to see by divine sense, with fearless eyes of the mind, the Word and God coming to it from heaven, as the descent of the high priest from the priestly throne signifies; and him perfectly separating from it, after the manner of catechumens, the thoughts that still imagine sense and what is partible according to it. And hence, the soul having again come to be outside the sensible things, as the closing of the doors of the holy church of God suggests be understood, him leading it to the immaterial and simple and unalterable and God-formed science of the intelligibles, free from every form and shape, which is signified through the entrance of the ineffable mysteries; according to which, having gathered its own powers on its own side, it brings itself to the Word,[^2] having united through the noetic kiss the ineffable principles and modes of salvation that concern it, him thoroughly teaching it to confess them with thanksgiving through the Creed.

After these things again, the soul as having now at last, according to a simple and undivided power, through discipleship, by knowledge, embraced the principles both of the sensible and of the intelligible things, him leading it to the knowledge of manifest theology, after its passage through all things, and providing it with an intellection equal to the angels, so far as is attainable to it; and teaching it so much, soberly, as to know God as one, one essence, three hypostases; a Monad of essence in three hypostases, and a Trinity of hypostases consubstantial; a Monad in Trinity, and a Trinity in Monad;[^3] not one and another, nor one beside another, nor one through another, nor one in another, nor one from another; but the same in itself, and by itself toward itself, the same with itself, both Monad and Trinity, having the union unconfused and unconfusedly,[^4] and the distinction undivided and unpartitioned; a Monad according to the principle of the essence, that is, [701] of being, but not according to composition or coalescence, or any confusion whatever; a Trinity according to the principle of how it exists and subsists, but not according to division or alienation, or any partition whatever. For the Monad is not partitioned in the hypostases, nor is it in them relationally and contemplated in them; nor are the hypostases compounded into a Monad, or fill it up by coalescence; but it is the same identical with itself, though in one way and in another. For the holy Trinity of the hypostases is a Monad unconfused in the essence and in the simple principle according to it; and the holy Monad is a Trinity in the hypostases and in the mode of existence; the same whole being understood as this and that differently, according to one principle and another, as has been said; one and only Godhead, undivided and unconfused, and simple and undiminished and invariant; a Monad existing whole according to the essence, and whole the same Trinity in the hypostases, and shining forth uniformly one ray of one thrice-lit light; upon which the soul too, having received, equally in honour with the holy angels, the principles concerning Godhead that are manifest and attainable to creation, and having learned to hymn with them harmoniously and without silence, in Trinitarian fashion, the one Godhead, is led to the adoption by grace through a resembling likeness; through which, having after prayers God as Father, both mystically by grace and alone, it will be gathered, by ecstasy from all things, to the One of his hiddenness; and it will suffer the divine things rather than know them,[^5] so much as not to wish to be its own, nor to be able to be known from itself, by itself or by any other, but only by the whole God, who has taken it up whole in a manner befitting goodness, and has given himself whole to it whole in a manner befitting God [whole to it wholly, as befits God][^6] and impassibly, and has deified it whole; so that it is, as the all-holy Areopagite Dionysius[^7] says, an image and manifestation of the unseen light, a pure mirror, most transparent, unblemished, undefiled, spotless, receiving whole, if it is lawful to say, the beauty of the Good that is its type, and in God-formed manner and undiminishedly shining forth in itself, so far as is possible, the goodness of the silence in the inner shrines.[^8]

CHAPTER 24[^9]

*Of what mysteries the grace of the Holy Spirit, which remains present, is active and effective, in those who are faithful and faithfully gathered, through the ordinances performed at the holy synaxis.*

Accordingly the blessed elder thought it needful, and did not cease exhorting every Christian, to give his leisure to the holy Church of God,[^10] and never to absent himself from the holy synaxis performed in it: both because of the holy angels who remain in it, and who [704] record each time those who enter and present them to God, and make supplications on their behalf; and because of the grace of the Holy Spirit, invisibly present always, but in a peculiar way especially at the time of the holy synaxis, which transforms and refashions each of those found there, and remoulds him, as something true,[^11] toward what is more divine, in proportion to himself, and leads him toward what is signified through the mysteries performed, even if he himself does not perceive it, if he is still among the infants in Christ,[^12] and is unable to see into the depth of the things taking place, and the grace signified through each of the divine symbols of salvation performed, working in him and proceeding in sequence and order from the nearest things to the end of all.

At the first entrance: the casting off of unbelief, the increase of faith, the diminution of vice, the advance of virtue, the abolition of ignorance, the addition of knowledge. Through the hearing of the divine oracles: the firm and immovable habits and dispositions of these things that have been named, I mean of faith and virtue and knowledge. Through the divine songs that follow these: the soul's voluntary assent to the virtues, and the noetic pleasure and delight that arises in it over them. Through the sacred reading of the holy Gospel: the consummation of the earthly way of thinking, as of a sensible world. Through the closing of the doors after these: the passage and transfer of the soul, by disposition, from this corruptible world to the intelligible world; through which, having shut the senses like doors, it renders them pure of the idols of sin. Through the entrance of the holy mysteries: the more perfect and more mystical and new teaching and knowledge concerning God's economy toward us. Through the divine kiss: the identity of concord and oneness of inclination and love of all toward all, and of each toward himself first, and toward God. Through the confession of the Creed: the fitting thanksgiving for the paradoxical modes of our salvation. Through the Trisagion:[^13] the union and equality of honour with the holy angels, and the harmonious intensity of the unceasing sanctifying doxology of God. Through the prayer, through which we are deemed worthy to call God Father:[^14] the truest adoption in the grace of the Holy Spirit. Through the One is Holy and what follows:[^15] the grace that makes one with God himself, and the intimacy toward him. Through the holy partaking of the undefiled and life-giving mysteries: the communion and identity with him by participation, possible through likeness; through which man is deemed worthy to become god from man. For of the gifts of the Holy Spirit which here, in the present life, we believe we have partaken through the grace in faith, of these, in the age to come, in truth, enhypostatically,[^16] in the very reality, according to the unfailing hope of our faith and the firm and inviolable promise of him who [705] promised,[^17] having kept the commandments according to our power, we believe we shall receive, passing from the grace in faith to the grace in sight;[^18] our God and Saviour Jesus Christ, that is, transforming us toward himself by the removal of the marks of corruption in us, and graciously granting us the archetypal mysteries shown forth to us through the sensible symbols here.

But for ease of remembering, if it seems good, let us run over the force of what has been said in summary, and so recapitulate it. The holy Church, then, is, as has been said, a type and image of God; because the unconfused union which he works, according to his infinite power and wisdom, among the different essences of beings, holding them together with himself at the summit as creator, [?][^19] and by one faith and grace and calling joining the faithful to one another in a unified way; the practical and virtuous by one identity of inclination;[^20] and the contemplative and knowers, in addition to these, also by an unbroken and undivided concord.

And of the world, both the intelligible and the sensible, it is a type; as having the sanctuary as symbol of the intelligible world, and the nave of the sensible.

Again, it is an image of man, as imitating the soul through the sanctuary, and putting forward the body through the nave. And of the soul itself, understood by itself, it is a type and image, as bearing through the sanctuary the glory of the contemplative part, and having through the nave the orderliness of the practical.

Of the holy synaxis performed in it, the first entrance generally signifies the first coming of our God;[^21] specifically, the conversion, through him and with him, of those who are being brought in from unbelief to faith, and from vice to virtue, and from ignorance to knowledge.

The readings that take place after it generally make known the divine wills and purposes, according to which all must be educated and conduct their lives; specifically, the teaching and progress according to the faith of those who have believed; and of the practical, the firm disposition according to virtue, by which, walking in line with the divine law of the commandments, they stand manfully and unshaken against the wiles of the devil,[^22] and escape the opposing activities; and of the knowers, the habit according to contemplation, by which, gathering, so far as they can, the spiritual principles of the sensible things and of the [708] providence over them, they are borne unerringly toward the truth.

The divine melodies of the songs: the divine pleasure and delight that arises in the souls of all; by which, mystically strengthened, they forget the past toils of virtue, and grow young again toward the vigorous aspiration for the divine and pure goods that remain.

The holy Gospel generally is a symbol of the consummation of this age; specifically, of those who have believed it signifies the complete abolition of the ancient error; of the practical, the mortification and consummation of the law and the way of thinking according to the flesh;[^23] of the knowers, the gathering and referral of the many and different principles to the most comprehensive principle, the more discursive and more varied natural contemplation having been completed and brought to its limit for them.

The descent of the high priest from the throne, and the expulsion of the catechumens, generally signifies the second coming from heaven of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ,[^24] and the separation of the sinners from the saints, and the just requital according to the worth of each; specifically, of those who have believed, the perfect assurance in faith which God and the Word makes, coming invisibly, through which every thought still limping in any way concerning the faith, in the manner of a catechumen, is driven away from them; of the practical, perfect passionlessness,[^25] through which every passionate and unenlightened thought departs from the soul; of the knowers, the comprehensive science of the things known, through which all the images of material things are chased out of the soul.

[^1]: T. The chunk opens inside a sentence begun in my-004 (the Latin: «with the Word, and with the Word himself, our great and true God and high priest, as guide, entering»). The feminine accusative participles that follow (εἰσερχομένην, διδασκομένην, δεξαμένην, μεταβαίνουσαν …) all describe the soul, governed by a verb of saying in the preceding chunk; the masculine ones (διακρίνοντα, ἄγοντα, ἐκδιδάσκοντα) describe the Word. [^2]: T. Migne prints πρὸς μὲν ἑαυτῆς τὰς οἰκείας δυνάμεις· πρὸς δὲ τὸν Λόγον, ἑαυτὴν καταντᾷ (genitive ἑαυτῆς, and the comma after τὸν Λόγον; both confirmed on the plate). Followed: πρὸς with the genitive, "on its own side", answers πρὸς δὲ τὸν Λόγον, and ἑαυτὴν is the object of καταντᾷ, used transitively, "brings itself to".
