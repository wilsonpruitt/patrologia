The unceasing sanctifying doxology of the Trisagion by the holy angels generally signifies the equality of conduct and way of life and harmony of the divine doxology, of the heavenly and earthly powers, which will come to be at once and in the same in the age to come, when the body of men has been made immortal through the resurrection, and no longer weighs down the soul with corruption, nor is weighed down, but through the change into incorruption has received both power and aptitude for the reception of the presence of God; specifically, of the faithful, the theological emulation of angels according to faith; of the practical, the angel-like brilliance in life, so far as is attainable to men, and the intensity of theological hymnody; of the knowers, the angel-like intellections and hymnings and ever-movings concerning Godhead, so far as is possible to men.

The blessed invocation of the great God and Father, and the proclamation of the One is Holy and what follows, and the partaking of the holy and life-giving mysteries, signify the adoption, union and intimacy, and divine likeness and deification, which will be, through the goodness of our God, upon all and over all the worthy; through which God himself will be all in all those who are saved,[^2] likewise; as archetypal beauty, according to cause, shining in those who, through virtue and knowledge, by grace, likewise shine together with him.

He used to call faithful and virtuous and knowers[^3] those who are being introduced, and those who are advancing, and the perfect, that is, slaves and hirelings and sons: the three orders of those who are saved.[^4] For slaves, the faithful, are those who fulfil the master's commandments out of fear of the things threatened, and who work with goodwill on what has been entrusted to them; hirelings, those who out of longing for the promised goods bear with patience the burden of the day and the heat,[^5] that is, the affliction innate and yoked to the present life from the condemnation of our forefathers, and the temptations upon it for the sake of [712] virtue; and who wisely, by self-chosen inclination, exchange life for life, the present for the future. Sons, those who neither by fear of the things threatened, nor by longing for the things promised, but by the mode and habit of the soul's tendency and disposition, by inclination, toward the good, are never separated from God; after the manner of that son to whom it was said, *Child, thou art always with me, and all that is mine is thine*;[^6] being, so far as is possible, by adoption in grace, this which God is and is believed to be by nature and by cause.

Let us not, then, absent ourselves from the holy Church of God, which, in the holy arrangement of the divine symbols performed, contains so great mysteries of our salvation; through which, fashioning each of us who conducts himself well, in proportion to himself, according to Christ, she brings to manifestation the gift of adoption given through holy baptism in the Holy Spirit, as he conducts himself according to Christ. But with all power and zeal let us present ourselves worthy of the divine gifts, pleasing God through good works; not conducting ourselves like the Gentiles who do not know God, in the passion of desire;[^7] but, as the holy Apostle says, *having mortified the members which are upon the earth, fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry, on account of which the wrath comes upon the sons of disobedience*;[^8] and having put off all wrath, and anger, and filthy speech, and lying,[^9] and, to say it briefly, the whole old man, which is corrupted according to the desires of deceit,[^10] with his practices and desires, let us walk worthily of God, who has called us into his kingdom and glory;[^11] having put on bowels of compassion, kindness, humble-mindedness, meekness, long-suffering; forbearing one another in love, and forgiving each other, if any has a complaint against any, even as the Lord forgave us; and above all the bond of perfection, love, and peace, to which we were also called in one body;[^12] and, to sum it up, the new man, which is renewed unto knowledge according to the image of him who created him.[^13] For living thus we might be able to come to the end of the divine promises with good hope, and to be filled with the knowledge of his will, bearing fruit in all spiritual wisdom and understanding, and increasing in the knowledge of the Lord; being strengthened with all power according to the might of his glory, unto all edification[^14] and long-suffering with joy, giving thanks to the Father, who made us fit for the share of the lot of the saints in light.[^15]

[713] A clear demonstration of this grace is the voluntary co-disposition, through goodwill, toward what is kindred; whose work is to make one's own, according to our power, as God,[^16] the man who in any respect needs our help, and not to leave him uncared for and unprovided for, but with fitting zeal to show forth in activity the disposition in us toward God and our neighbour as living. For a work is the demonstration of a disposition. For nothing is so easy for righteousness, nor so apt for deification (if I may so speak) and for nearness to God, as mercy offered from the soul to those in need with pleasure and joy. For if the Word has shown him who needs to be well treated to be God (*For inasmuch as you did it*, he says, *to one of the least of these, you did it to me*;[^17] and he who said it is God), much more will he show him who is able to do good, and does it, to be truly God by grace and participation, as having taken up, by apt imitation, the activity and property of his beneficence. And if the poor man is God, on account of the condescension of God who became poor for us,[^18] and who takes up sympathetically into himself the passions of each, and until the consummation of the age, according to the proportion of the passion in each, always suffers mystically out of goodness;[^19] much more, plainly, by the fitting account, will he be God who, in imitation of God, through love for man, heals by himself, in a manner befitting God, the passions of those who suffer, and is shown to have by disposition the same power as God, according to the proportion of saving providence.

Who then is so slow toward virtue and so hard to move as not to aspire to Godhead, when its acquisition is so cheap and easily procured and easy? And a safe and inviolable guard of these, and an easy road to salvation, without which, I think, none of the goods will in truth be preserved unharmed for him who has it, is the doing of one's own business, that is, the minding of one's own affairs;[^20] through which, learning to look at and examine our own affairs alone, we are freed from having harm in vain from others. For if we should learn to see and examine ourselves alone, we shall never set upon the affairs of others, however they may chance to be; knowing that the one only judge, wise and just, is God, who judges wisely and justly all that comes to pass, according to the principle by which it has come to be, but not according to the mode in which it has been manifested; which perhaps men too could judge, looking dimly at what appears, about which the truth does not altogether lie, nor the principle of the things that come to pass. But God, seeing the unseen motion of the soul, and the invisible impulse, and the very principle by which the soul has been moved, and the aim of the principle, that is, the end pre-conceived of every action, judges [716] justly, as I said, all the things done by men; which if we are zealous to achieve, we shall also bound ourselves within ourselves, not fastening upon the things outside; we shall not let the eye, or the ear, or the tongue see, or hear, or speak the things of others: if it is possible, entirely; but if not, permitting them to act on these sympathetically rather, and not passionately, and to see and hear and speak for our gain; and only so much as seems good to the divine reason that holds the reins of them. For nothing is more slippery toward sin than these organs, when they are not schooled by reason; and again nothing is more ready for salvation than they, when reason orders them, and gives them measure, and leads them to what it must and wills.

Let us not then neglect, according to our power, to obey God, who calls us to life everlasting and a blessed end through the working of his divine and saving commandments; that we may receive mercy, and find grace for timely help.[^21] For *grace*, says the divine Apostle, *be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ in incorruption*;[^22] that is, with those who, with the incorruption according to virtue, and the pure and unfeigned dignity in their life, love the Lord in doing his wills, and not corrupting anything of his divine precepts.

These things I, concerning these matters, for the reward of obedience, have set out according to my power, as I was taught, not daring to touch the more mystical and loftier things; which, if anyone of the lovers of learning longs to know, let him read what was laboured over inspiredly about these things by the holy Dionysius the Areopagite,[^23] and he will find in truth a revelation of ineffable mysteries, bestowed through his divine understanding and tongue on the race of men, for the sake of those who are to inherit salvation.[^24] And if it has not fallen far short of your desire,[^25] thanks be to Christ, the giver of good things, and to you, who compelled these things to be said. But if it falls somewhere far and much short of the hope, what shall I suffer, or what shall I do, having been weak at speaking? For weakness is pardonable, not punishable; and what is possible and admissible is rather acceptable, not blameworthy; and especially to you, who have resolved to love for God's sake. And to God, too, all that is offered genuinely from the soul according to power is dear, even if it appears small by comparison with great things; who did not even reject the widow who brought the two mites;[^26] whatever this widow was, and the two mites: whether a soul widowed of vice, and having cast off the old law as a husband,[^27] but not yet worthy of the utmost conjunction with the Word and God, yet bringing to him by way of pledge, [717] like mites, the reason and life proportioned to her so far; or faith and a good conscience; or the habit and activity concerning good things; or the contemplation and practice fitting to these; or the proportionate knowledge and virtue; or those a little above these, I mean the principles in the natural and in the written law, which the soul, having acquired them, brings, in ecstasy from them and release from them as from her whole livelihood and life,[^28] wishing to be conjoined to the Word and God alone; and accepts to be widowed of the violent modes and ordinances and customs according to nature and law, as of husbands; or whether the word hints at something else more spiritual than these, and to be contemplated only by those pure in understanding, through the letter fulfilled according to history. For all things, compared with the beholding principle of theology,[^29] the things among men that seem to be great in virtue, turn out to be small. Save that, even if small, and of cheap material and not much prized, yet, equally with the gold coins of the more precious among materials, which the wealthier offer, they bear the royal stamp; and perhaps they have over and above [also][^30] the intent of the woman who offers from her whole disposition.

Imitating this widow, I too have offered to God and to you, beloved, these small and cheap notions and words, brought forth from a cheap and poor understanding and tongue, like mites, concerning the things you commanded; beseeching your blessed and holy soul: first, no longer to seek a written record of anything said by me, for two reasons: one, that I have not yet acquired the fear of God that is pure and abiding,[^31] nor a solid habit of virtue and a steady and unshaken fixity of true righteousness, the things that most of all bear witness to the firmness of words; the other, that, still tossed about like a wild sea by much surge of passions, and far from the divine harbour of passionlessness, and having the end of my life uncertain, I do not wish to have, in addition to my works, the word in writing too as my accuser. And then, accepting it, if it is fitting, for the sake of my obedience, commend me through prayers to Christ, the great and only God and Saviour of our souls; to whom be the glory and the might, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, unto the ages. Amen.
