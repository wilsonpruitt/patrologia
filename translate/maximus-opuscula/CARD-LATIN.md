# LATIN-ONLY SUPPLEMENT: Opusculum 12 (chunk mo-015). THIS OVERRIDES THE CARD ABOVE WHERE THEY DIFFER

Migne prints Opusculum 12 **in Latin only** (cols 141–144): the Greek is lost, and the text survives in the Latin of
Anastasius the Librarian (9th c.), who excerpted it into his *Collectanea*; Combefis's note says so (*unaque nobis
Graece desideratur*). Precedent: Wilson, 2026-11-05, on the Sophronius orations printed in Latin only: *"Let's do them
in Latin."* Your source is **Anastasius's Latin**. Translate the Latin. Everything in the card above still binds
(register, *thou/you*, glossary, notes, file shape, the OCR table and its kinds, the report) except what this
supplement changes.

## The piece
- **mo-015: OPUSCULUM 12 — FROM THE LETTER TO PETER THE ILLUSTRIS** (Latin heading *Defloratio ex epistola ejusdem
  sancti Maximi scripta ad Petrum illustrem*). On the abbot Pyrrhus (the deposed patriarch of Constantinople), who
  sought to be received by Rome without anathematizing his own doctrine; the Roman see as the guardian of the faith.
  Peter: singular *thou*. Pyrrhus: P note on first appearance. The excerpt may break off with Anastasius's
  *Et post pauca* / *Et paulo post* (render as an italic line `*And after a little:*`, the editor's link, not Maximus).

## What changes
- **The scaffold.** Each page gives its Latin column(s) as tagged lines `pNNNN.L.LL` (left column) and `pNNNN.R.LL`
  (right column), from the archive.org OCR of Migne's page, cut to the column boxes. Left column first, then right.
  There is **no witness column**: you are reading the only text. Combefis's long Latin note runs full-width at the top
  of p-0087 and is NOT in your scaffold (it is apparatus); if a stretch of it leaked in (talk of Byzacium, the
  sixth synod, Anastasius's *Collectanea*), it is kind `stray`.
- **Column anchors.** Both columns on these pages are Latin and both are numbered: `[141]` where the left column's text
  begins, `[142]` where the right column's begins (numbers in each `### column NNN` header).
- **The OCR table** records **Latin** misreads: `| pNNNN.L.LL | printed | reading | sure | kind | why |`. Expect `u`/`n`,
  `c`/`e`, `ii`/`u`, `l`/`i`/`t`, æ/œ read as `a`/`e`/`z`, `rn`/`m`, a lost capital, hyphen breaks, Greek words garbled.
  Classical/medieval spellings Migne really prints (`quum`, `charitas`, `-cio`) are **not** errors. `print` = Migne's
  misprint; `doubt` = you cannot tell. Expect 10–30 rows per 1,000 Latin words.
- **Band letters** glued to line ends (`… ut ne, A`): ignore, as in the card; not an OCR row.
- **Note calls** and scripture marks are apparatus: do not reproduce; a leaked footnote line is `stray`.
- **Terms.** The Latin stands for a Greek you cannot see. Where its word clearly renders a glossary term, use the
  glossary's English (*natura* = nature, *substantia/essentia* = essence, *persona* = person, *operatio* = operation,
  *voluntas* = will, *sedes apostolica* = the apostolic see). Where you cannot tell, translate the Latin plainly and,
  for a theological term, give a T note with the Latin.
- **Scripture**: translate the Latin as printed, with an S note.
