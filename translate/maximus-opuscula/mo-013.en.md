One of two things, then, is necessary: either to bring in the teacher[^2] fighting against himself, or [to bring him in] rendering the same nature in Christ, just as also the same operation, and not dictating that the natures are altered, since they go into identity with each other by the invariance of the operation. «For things that are of the same operation,» he says, «are confessed to be also of the same essence.» But if each is unfitting, both to be at odds with himself and not to profess as altered in Christ the natures from which he is, it is clear that he does not hand down one operation of both the essences, that is, of Christ composed from them, by saying, «showing one and kindred operation through both»;[^3] but he presents the superessential Word, being God by nature and incarnate, as putting forth his life-giving and divine operation by nature both incorporeally and through the body. For in saying "kindred" he indicated nothing other than the same life-giving operation, shown forth in the miracles through both, through the all-working command and through the touch of his holy flesh; just as fire too, burning both immaterially and through matter, shows the same burning power either way.

So then, the teacher does not say these things for the destruction of the Saviour's human operation; for in having spoken of the touch of flesh, he plainly signified the human [operation], since the tactile is a clear demonstration of the human operation. But neither has he handed down another of his besides the two according to nature; but he has shown that the operation of command and touch, life-giving by nature, as has been said, through both, proceeds from one and the same God the Word.

But if, hemmed in by these pious proclamations of the Fathers, they bring forward the letter of Heraclianus to Achillius,[^4] according to which they suggest saying in Christ, besides the two, also one nature, as of the whole, and [one] movement, let them interpret it themselves. For the writing runs thus: «One is the whole nature and movement of the Lord in itself; but two are those by reference. One is the movement and nature of the parts by appropriation;[^5] but two are those in themselves. And when I say movement, I mean operation and passion to be signified.»

Then those who put forward these things as the bulwark of their own doctrines must make clear in their account what, and how it is said, and by which of the approved Fathers it is attested, the one nature and movement of the Lord, as of the whole; and what are the two by reference. And what again is the one nature, and likewise movement, of the parts by appropriation; and what are the two in themselves; that is, these three pairings of the natures and movements of the same. And who handed down that the same and one movement signifies each, I mean operation and passion; [128] and how, if [it signifies] both according to them, the Son is not severed from the Father in his every movement, as being passible by nature in every [movement]; since the movement of God and Saviour does not, in any manner whatever, signify each, operation and passion together. For the Divine is impassible by nature, and without relation, and simple; or rather, simplified beyond all simplicity. And how again, with the pairings of the three movements, do they not bring in twelve operations and passions,[^6] if movement signifies both according to them? And who of the saints ever professed the natures in Christ God by reference or by appropriation?

Let them first show these things from the Fathers' ratification, and then thus the supposition of their own doctrines will be ratified for them from it. But if they cannot, let them henceforth let these go, and keep in step with us by those things alone that have been piously decreed, both by the God-bearing Fathers of the catholic Church and by the five holy ecumenical synods; confessing the same and one and only Lord and God Jesus Christ to be God by nature, perfect in all things, and man by nature, perfect in all things, sin alone excepted; and let them not, by the destruction of what is perfect by nature in all things in both, destroy along with it the divine or the human volition and operation of the same; knowing that the superessential Word neither diminished, nor altered, nor made to vanish in any respect at all, either what he was without beginning and without cause, or what he became later for a cause, that is, our salvation;[^7] but rather kept them wholly and perfectly, apart from sin alone, in himself and through himself, in all respects and in all things, being himself properly, as I said, those very things by natural existence. Whence the same and only and one Lord and God of ours is by nature volitive and operative of our salvation according to each of his natures, from which and in which he is constituted.

Since, being God by nature, possessed of authority, and the creator of the authority of all things, he did not become a man without mind, or without share in the natural life that is ours, who have obtained being from him by creation; but he who is himself by nature and alone God became in truth an intellectual man, that is, volitive by nature and operative; having assumed the whole of me with what is mine, that he might grant salvation to the whole of me; having loosed the whole condemnation of sin; since «what is unassumed is unhealed, but what is united to [129] God, this is also saved,»[^8] as he who is named for theology says.

But since, as we were clearing away, with these pious teachings of the holy Fathers, the unreasonable objection of those who are unequally yoked,[^9] they set upon us from another quarter, making a bringing-forward of writings of ours for an onslaught upon us, or rather for the snatching away of the more simple, namely of the letter to Marinus[^10] the presbyter, written, as they say, by us, and of the letter to Pyrrhus,[^11] it is necessary to say a little about these too; assuring your holiness in all things that to Marinus the presbyter we have written variously, on various scriptural chapters; but certainly not that it has been said [by us], in any manner whatever, thus concerning the counterpoised and contrary doctrines, I mean the one and the two, that is, the three operations or volitions fabricated by them in the same and one Christ, as though we were establishing these in him in rivalry with them, either [to Marinus] or to anyone else at all. «For if I build again the things that I destroyed, I make myself a transgressor,»[^12] says the vessel of election. Hence the letter thus put forward, or rather fabricated, as though from us to Marinus, being wholly foreign and not ours, we ourselves cast away, and we exhort all who piously confess the Lord likewise with us to cast it away; that we may wall off every occasion from our opponents, who make excuses in sins,[^13] and who make their slander against us a covering for their own contradiction; who, since they are not upheld by their own account, which is destroyed by the assault of the true, contrive for themselves, by the mask of a supposed writing of ours, a deceitful entry against the simpler; which those will no longer suffer, nor commit, who prefer the true to the false, our defence concerning these things.

As for Pyrrhus, again: he had earlier written us a tome of many lines, and in it had honoured us exceedingly, I know not how; and in that very tome he had made his account of the one and the two operations of the same by way of examination and not of pronouncement, [132] and as it were sought out and invited our judgement and supposition on each account; and we too fittingly honoured him in return in the preamble of our letter to him, fulfilling the apostolic exhortation. For, *In honour preferring one another*, says the divine Apostle;[^14] since the divine word bids us love and bless even those who are otherwise disposed, or who write to us [otherwise].[^15] And at the same time it was my concern not to exasperate the man, but rather to smooth him by the praises addressed to him, so that he might go on to assent to what had been piously laid down as doctrine by me, according to the teaching of the holy Fathers; whence I also acknowledged that I had understood what he had written to me in this way, in accordance with the writing I made for him; urging him, as it were, as I said, and inviting him to the assent of the pious account, which would procure for him the greatest salvation, if, having embraced it, he confessed [should confess][^16] with us as we had written; that is, two operations according to nature of the same and one Christ God, just as also natures united without confusion and without division, divine and human, uncreated and created; but not one and the same, in whatever way it is said. For the account of one and the same operation or volition or nature in the same and one Lord and God of ours is not of the Fathers, but rather heretical.

Such, then, was the manner of our praise to him, that is, to Pyrrhus, hastening gently to draw him away from the counterpoised opinion and to lead him back up to the right account of the faith. But if anyone, missing our aim, still chafes at the praise, not accepting the principle of our economy[^17] in this matter above all, let it be conceded to him, and let the praise be cast away as he thinks fit, being no longer lovely to us either, but rather repugnant after the man's complete turning-aside; but let the force of what was laid down as doctrine to him in orthodox fashion be ratified with us.

You have, holy Fathers, through our defence, the account of the assurance. But quit you like men, and be strong in spirit,[^18] not turning aside to right or left[^19] by men and doctrines of the opposite side; but being established on the royal road and guided by him who said, *I am the way, and the life, and the truth*;[^20] of one soul, minding the same thing;[^21] and the God of peace will be with you all,[^22] who is piously proclaimed always and holily worshipped by you; and who, as the end of the blessed way of life and confession here, provides himself to you there as a more blessed inheritance and rest; with whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory, honour, might, worship, now and always, and to the ages of ages. Amen.

## Notes
[^1]: S. The close of a testimony whose opening stands in the preceding chunk; cf. Cyril of Alexandria, *Thesaurus* 32 (on the identity of operation and essence).
[^2]: T. "The teacher" is Cyril of Alexandria, whom the monenergists claimed; Maximus argues that read their way Cyril would contradict his own *Thesaurus*.
[^3]: S. Cyril of Alexandria, *Commentary on John* 4 (on John 6:53–54), on the miracles worked by word and by touch.
[^4]: P. Heraclianus, bishop of Chalcedon (6th c.), anti-Severan writer; Achillius, the addressee, is otherwise unknown.
[^5]: T. κατ᾿ ἀναφοράν "by reference" (Latin *secundum rationem / relationem*); κατ᾿ οἰκείωσιν "by appropriation" (glossary § VII; Latin *secundum arrogationem*), the technical Christological sense.
[^6]: T. Latin agrees: *duodecim operationes passionesque*.
[^7]: S. Cf. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 29.19 (what he was "without cause", what he became later "for a cause").
[^8]: S. Gregory Nazianzen, *Ep.* 101.32 (to Cledonius); "he who is named for theology" is Gregory the Theologian.
[^9]: S. Cf. 2 Cor 6:14 (ἑτεροζυγοῦντες).
[^10]: P. Marinus, presbyter of Cyprus, addressee of Opusc. 1, 7, 10, 20; the letter here disowned is a forgery circulated under Maximus's name.
[^11]: P. Pyrrhus, patriarch of Constantinople 638–641, Sergius's successor and Maximus's opponent in the disputation of 645.
[^12]: S. Gal 2:18; "the vessel of election" is Paul (Acts 9:15).
[^13]: S. Ps 140:4 LXX.
[^14]: S. Rom 12:10.
[^15]: S. Cf. Matt 5:44; Luke 6:28.
[^16]: T. The bracketed optative ὁμολογήσειεν is printed beside the indicative ὡμολόγησεν as an editorial correction; the English gives both.
[^17]: T. οἰκονομία here in its pastoral sense: the accommodation of the praise to win Pyrrhus.
[^18]: S. 1 Cor 16:13; cf. Luke 1:80.
[^19]: S. Cf. Deut 5:32.
[^20]: S. John 14:6 (the Greek order as printed: way, life, truth); the royal road, cf. Num 20:17.
[^21]: S. Phil 2:2.
[^22]: S. Phil 4:9; cf. 2 Cor 13:11.
