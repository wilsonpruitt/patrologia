[133] OPUSCULUM 10 — COPY OF A LETTER TO MARINUS

*Of the holy Maximus. Copy of a letter to the lord Marinus, presbyter of Cyprus.*[^1]

Directed by divine law according to God, God-honoured father, thou hast overlooked human law, exalting by emptying the mediocrity, and magnifying by poverty the cheapness,[^2] of my inexperience in understanding and speaking, or of my rashness, to speak more properly. For I am not ashamed duly to confess my own passions and those of my weakness, and to uncover them with readiness so much the more as thou hast been eager to cover them with love for man. For who would ever have so admitted the word of one without reason, and received the mind of a frenzied understanding, not to say altogether, have extolled it, as thou, God-honoured father, hast honoured mine to the utmost: not only so far as, out of love, passing over its want of learning, which is indeed the greatest thing for the unadorned toward good repute; but also so far as, out of grace, laying upon it the venerable quality with which thou art wont most beautifully to adorn thine own understanding, thou hast made it shine with excess; from which the principle and mode of true nobility are constituted, or of kinship with God, to say it more truly and also more properly; which knows not how to lift up, for it is not its nature, rather than to make modest. For it is somehow wont to settle one unalterably toward lowliness, by the excess of the things not present uncovering the want the more and making known the nakedness; and, by the display of what is beyond desert, instilling a more austere sense of what is according to desert, when, having mastered the depth of the soul, it makes one see without veil the greatness of the incongruity. Having suffered just this above all, by the clarifying of thy prayers[^3] I have laid hold of divine ordinances, which the principle of the economy that is beyond concept knows how providentially to preserve and to set before those who, like me, need and require guidance by the hand.

*And after a little:*

Indeed, then, the people of the queen of cities have found fault with the synodical [letters] of the present most holy Pope[^4] not in so many chapters as thou hast written, [136] but in two only: of which the one concerns theology, that, he says, he said «that the Holy Spirit proceeds also from the Son»; the other concerns the divine incarnation, that he wrote «that the Lord, as man, is without the ancestral sin». And for the first, they[^5] produced concordant testimonies of the Roman Fathers, and moreover also of Cyril of Alexandria, from the sacred treatise laboured by him on the holy evangelist John;[^6] from which they showed that they themselves do not make the Son cause of the Spirit, for they know the Father as the one cause of Son and Spirit, of the one by generation, of the other by procession; but [they said it] in order to show the going-forth through him,[^7] and in this way to present the conjoinedness and invariance of the essence. For the second, they need no defence at all. For what dispute is there in this? even if it is thought one by those who make pretexts, on account of their perverseness. Nevertheless they put them out of countenance, saying «that he had neither the sin in the mind, by which Adam appears somehow to have first suffered, nor the practice and operation of evil proceeding from it through the body».

These, then, [said] these things, concerning matters on which they were not reasonably accused; but those, concerning matters on which [they are accused] very justly indeed, have made no defence up to now, since [they have made] not even the casting-out of the things brought in by them.

And I besought the Romans, according to thy command, to translate their own [writings], for the sake of escaping the stealthy frauds of those who creep in; but since custom has prevailed to do so and to send so, I do not know whether perhaps they will comply. And besides, there is their not being able to express their own mind so precisely in another diction and tongue as in their own and the one that nursed them, just as we too [cannot express] ours [save] in ours. But surely, once they have learned the abuse by experience, the care for this too will arise in them.

The [writing] on essence and nature, and on hypostasis and person, and the chapters that follow, I have read: a draft[^8] of Theodore of Pharan;[^9] and as by way of introduction they are perhaps not without use. But in the account on person and hypostasis he appears somehow to have kept in step not with the canons on these matters but rather with himself, who calls the operation hypostatic; whom those of the contrary [opinion] put forward as the guide and advocate of their own opinion, having shaped and dictated almost the whole Ekthesis[^10] from him and from his words. For in these respects he somehow overshadowed and darkened the account,[^11] giving to the person as person the operation that characterizes the nature, and not [giving it] the mode of how and of what kind its [137] outcome is; according to which the diversity both of those who act and of the things done is made known, as they are according to nature or contrary to nature. For each of us operates primarily as being something, and not as someone, that is, as man; but as someone, such as Paul or Peter, he shapes the mode of the operation, by remission perhaps or by intensification, [the mode] being shaped by him by inclination in this way or that. Hence in the mode the variation of the persons in practice is made known; in the principle, the invariance of the natural operation. For one is not more and another less operative or rational; but we all equally have reason and its operation according to nature. But more and less, and just or unjust in this way or that, [one is] by the one following more the things according to nature, and the other falling away from them.

Those who have swerved in any point must be watched with exactness. Since they somehow force and drag both the definitions of things and the canons toward what seems good to them, shifting them as if by command, so that they may establish their own [views], forsooth, by perversion.

The quires that were sent (although I did not go through them at leisure), I mean those on the soul and the remaining chapters: I make known what I think about them. For the haste of the one setting out did not allow me to go through them; since only after many days from arriving in Byzacium did he come to my nothingness in Carthage.[^12] And besides, I was hindered also by the feebleness of the eyes that is upon me, so to speak; but since I wished to fulfil thy command about these things as quickly as possible, I have sent [them]. The economy[^13] decided upon by thee, most holy one, for the copying of the volume that contains the interpretation of the evangelist John has seemed very agreeable to me also, thy [servant], so that it may not become a cause of attack against the one who set it out,[^14] for those who not rightly try to dig through everything at their pleasure.

OPUSCULUM 11 — FROM THE LETTER WRITTEN IN ROME

*Of the same. From the letter written in Rome.*[^15]

For all the ends of the inhabited world, and those who everywhere on earth confess the Lord sincerely and in orthodox fashion, look directly, as to a sun of eternal light, to the most holy Church of the Romans and to its confession and faith, receiving from it the flashing radiance of the Fathers' and holy doctrines, as the six holy synods,[^16] inspired by God and divine, set them out sincerely and with all piety, declaring most expressly the symbol of the faith. For from the beginning, from the descent to us of the incarnate God the [140] Word, all the Churches of the Christians everywhere have possessed and hold the greatest [Church] there as their only base and foundation; as being in no way overpowered by the gates of Hades, according to the very promise of the Saviour, but having the keys of the orthodox faith and confession in him;[^17] and opening to those who approach piously the piety that alone is truly [piety] by nature; but shutting and stopping every heretical mouth that speaks injustice on high.[^18] For what the very creator and Master of all, our Lord Jesus Christ, and his disciples and apostles, and the holy Fathers and teachers and martyrs after them, consecrated by their own works and words, by contests and sweats, by toils and blood, and finally by extraordinary deaths, founded and built up, the catholic and apostolic Church, for the sake of us who believe in him: this, by two words,[^19] without labour and without death [toil],[^20] (O the long-suffering and forbearance of God!) they are striving to destroy, and to annul the great and all-luminous and all-hymned mystery of the orthodox religion of the Christians.

## Notes
[^1]: P. Marinus, presbyter of Cyprus (see Opusc. 1, 7, 20). T. The Greek addresses him now in the singular (θεοτίμητε πάτερ, σύ), now in the plural of respect (γεγράφατε, ὑμετέρων, ὑμῖν); the English keeps *thou* throughout, the addressee being one man. The heading's Greek word is Ἴσον, "copy" (Latin *Exemplum*).
[^2]: S. Cf. Phil 2:7 (κένωσις) and 2 Cor 8:9 (πτωχεία): Marinus's condescension figured as Christ's.
[^3]: T. ὑμετέρων, plural of respect (note 1).
[^4]: P. Pope Theodore I (642–649); "the queen of cities" is Constantinople (Latin glosses *Constantinopolitani*).
[^5]: T. "They" = the Romans, defending the pope's synodical letter.
[^6]: S. Cyril of Alexandria, *Commentary on John* (cf. on John 16:14–15).
[^7]: T. The Romans' word, as Maximus reports it, is προϊέναι, "go forth", kept apart from ἐκπορεύεσθαι, "proceed", which is the Father's alone: the Spirit proceeds from the Father and goes forth through the Son.
[^8]: T. σχέδει, read as σχεδίῳ, "draft" (OCR table); Latin *libellum*.
[^9]: P. Theodore, bishop of Pharan in Sinai (early 7th c.), the first literary witness to monenergism.
[^10]: P. The *Ekthesis* (638), the monothelite exposition of faith issued under Heraclius, drafted by Sergius.
[^11]: T. κατὰ ταῦτα λόγον, without article; Latin *in hunc modum sermonem*. Translated as the account (λόγον) of the hypostasis.
[^12]: P. Byzacium, the province south of Carthage in Roman Africa; Maximus writes from Carthage (c. 645–646).
[^13]: T. οἰκονομία here is the practical arrangement for having the volume copied; the glossary word is kept.
[^14]: T. ὁ ἐκθέμενος, "the one who set it out", is the author of the commentary on John being copied.
[^15]: T. The heading is damaged in the Kraken text (OCR table); read from the Latin *Ex epistola Romae scripta*. A fragment, Maximus writing at Rome (after 646).
[^16]: T. "Six synods" in Greek and Latin alike, though Maximus elsewhere counts five (Opusc. 9 above); the fragment may postdate 649 or be retouched.
[^17]: S. Matt 16:18–19.
[^18]: S. Ps 72:8 LXX.
[^19]: T. "Two words": presumably the monothelite formula itself, or the two imperial edicts (Ekthesis and Typos).
[^20]: T. The bracketed καμάτου, "toil", is printed as a correction of θανάτου, "death"; the English gives both.
