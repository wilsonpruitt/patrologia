[141] OPUSCULUM 12 — FROM THE LETTER TO PETER THE ILLUSTRIS

*Extract from the letter of the same holy Maximus, written to Peter the* illustris.[^1]

By thy praiseworthy syllables[^2] thou hast deigned to signify to thine unworthy servant concerning the abbot Pyrrhus.[^3]

*And after a little:*

And indeed it was to be that, perhaps for this cause, I should hasten to thy most renowned[^4] footsteps, to be divinely preserved, for the refutation of him and of all who indiscriminately follow the impious novelty after him; so that all who, like thee, are lovers of piety might know their superfluous abundance of impiety, together with utter ignorance. But I feared lest I be thought to be doing anything against the sacred laws, doing this without the will of the most holy see of the apostolic men, who lead well the whole fullness of the catholic Church and consecrate it in order according to the divine law. Since by this divine law, namely, the heavenly and the earthly things stand, and it is right by no means to swerve from it; but that those who have purposed to live holily, like thee, should do the things that are divine according to God; so that they may have God both as the curator of their deeds and a reception without success [?].[^5]

Since, most renowned lord, worse than these, as I have also intimated before, even than the ancient heretics, is the new faith and exposition (Ἔκθεσις).[^6] For this, through the many years in which he lasted in the episcopate, to the manifestation of his reprobate mind (which God is wont often to bear long-sufferingly, also for the bringing to light of wickedness: not that he may make it greater by the respite, but that, when it has become greater, he may punish it more justly), his predecessor[^7] impiously nourished in the womb, while this man more impiously gave birth to it; and those who came after them[^8] cultivate it to the contempt of the Godhead.[^9]

*And after a little:*

And what is still worse than those, and as it were the special mark of all heretics of every kind: that those who are liable to their own impiety [142] they write as guiltless. For they whisper here and there that Sophronius,[^10] who wisely taught thoroughly and proclaimed the divine doctrines of the catholic Church, had moved them into error.

*And after a very little:*

For the divine Sophronius, as thou thyself bearest witness, most praiseworthy one,[^11] was staying in the region of the Africans with me and with all the pilgrim monks when they were fabricating those perversities against all.

*And after a few things:*

And the house of all was indeed, through the divine concord and unanimity into one, by the favouring grace of God, in the whole world that stands under the sun, peaceful and tranquil, the Churches being fortified by faith. But these, who envied their harmony and their confession toward Christ God, like a certain murky cloud and a roaring or heavy surge of the sea and of barbarians, with no one moving them but the demon alone, the friend of disturbance, who stirred up the human race to enmity, suddenly devised the Ekthesis against the true faith of the Christians; then decrees for it, and uncouth compositions; then acts of minutes; then robber synods and gatherings of bishops, not of those who came together, but of those dragged together by violence; not of those hastening at exhortation, but of those travelling abroad in flight from the barbarians; then commands and threats sent hither and thither against the pious. For they were reducing him who then ruled[^12] to servitude, sophistically, by gifts.

*And after a little:*

They persuaded the man to issue the Ekthesis in his own name.

*A little after:*

And he becomes at once their enemy and their defender, or rather, it must be asserted, their favourer and accuser, publishing their plot, and making known that he was in no way moved of himself, but, forced by them, made the most impious Ekthesis; [143] saying these things in writings on his own behalf, and giving account to John, of holy memory, pope of the elder Rome.[^13]

*And after a very little:*

And they convict themselves, that these most excellent men[^14] quite manifestly made it, and in no way he. For he had no zeal whatever about this, being carried about to other things.

*After brief things:*

In all these things the wretches did not even become of the mind of the apostolic see;[^15] and, what is worthy of laughter, or rather, to speak more properly, most worthy of lament, as demonstrating their audacity, they were not slow to lie rashly even against the apostolic see itself; but, as though they had been made [sharers] of its counsel, and as if having received some decree from it, in their acts composed for the impious Ekthesis they had taken the great Honorius[^16] along with them, making to others a display of their presumption, [namely] the man's very great eminence in the cause of piety. Who, then, O most renowned, and what kind of Sophronius persuaded these false-speakers to do these things, and so atrociously, and for so long a time? What Church did not beseech them? What pious and orthodox prelate did not supplicate them, crying out and adjuring, to cease from their own heresy? Indeed, the holy Arcadius[^17] was breathing out his last and giving up his spirit to God; yet not even up to that hour did he cease to beseech them.

*And after a little:*

And what of the divine Honorius? and what, too, of the aged Severinus after him? what, finally, of him who came after this one, the holy John?[^18] Further, has he who now presides, the most blessed pope,[^19] left out anything befitting supplication? Did not the whole East and the West alike offer tears, laments, entreaties, deprecations, both to God through prayers and to these men through letters? But God indeed accepted the faith of these truly blessed men, and for it has kept their recompense for the ages. But they, despising the Lord's respite, and those who kindly exhorted them to conversion, and remaining torpid under the supplication made by these excellent men through so long a time, would by no means bring themselves to prefer the faith to their own heresy.[^20]

*After some things:*

The divine and great Sophronius, then, coming at that time to Alexandria, at once upon the first reading (for Cyrus[^21] had given him too those nine chapters of impiety to review), crying out something mournful and great, poured forth fountains of tears, fervently beseeching him, supplicating, remonstrating, thrown down at his feet on the pavement, that he should proclaim none of these things from the ambo against the catholic Church of God; since these were plainly the doctrines of the impious Apollinarius.[^22]

*And after a little:*

In such a way, O head venerable to me before all, the men were tearing the divine things with insults hateful to God, and with, as it were, theatrical mockeries among themselves; and the most sacred Sophronius, who lamented so piteously, as one weeping, after the manner of a second Jeremiah,[^23] for the breaking of the catholic Church, and deploring very well and most compassionately so great a fall of the divine doctrines, [144] they in no way consoled …

*And after some things:*

For if the Roman see knows full well that Pyrrhus is not only reprobate but also wrong in mind and wrong in belief, it is surely plain that everyone who anathematizes those who have rejected Pyrrhus anathematizes the Roman see, that is, the catholic Church. For I omit to say that he anathematizes himself too, he who is such, if indeed he communicates with the Roman see and the catholic Church of God. I beseech thee, then, my blessed lord, to command all not to call Pyrrhus most holy or hallowed.[^24] For the sacred rule does not permit him to be called any such thing. For he has fallen from all holiness who has, of his own accord, leapt out of the catholic Church. For it is not right that he be surnamed with any praise whatever, who has long since been condemned and cast out by the apostolic see of the city of Rome on account of the sense of a foreign opinion, until he be received by it, converted to it, or rather to the Lord our God, through pious confession and orthodox faith, by which he may receive sanctification and the name of holy.

So if he wishes not to be a heretic, nor to be called one, let him not satisfy this man or that; for this is superfluous and unreasonable; since, just as when one was scandalized against him all were scandalized, so also when one is satisfied, all without doubt are satisfied. Let him hasten before all to satisfy the Roman see. For when it is satisfied, all everywhere in common will proclaim him pious and orthodox. For he speaks only in vain who thinks that men like me are to be persuaded, and does not satisfy and implore the most blessed pope of the most holy Church of the Romans, that is, the apostolic see; which from the incarnate Word of God himself, and also from all the holy synods, according to the sacred canons and definitions,[^25] has received and holds, over all the holy Churches of God that are in the whole world, in all things and through all things, the dominion, authority and power of binding and loosing. For with it the Word, who rules over the heavenly powers, binds and looses also in heaven.[^26] For if he thinks that others are to be satisfied, and by no means implores the most blessed Roman pope, he does something like one who, accused perhaps of homicide or of some other crime, hastens to show himself innocent not to him who has by the laws obtained the right of judging, but, uselessly and without profit, busies himself to show the cleanness of his conduct to me[^27] and to private men, who have no power to loose him from the charge.

Wherefore, my blessed lord, extend still more the order which is known to have seemed good to thee and according to the good pleasure of God, so that this man may have licence to say or utter nothing to anyone for the sake of doctrine. But learn clearly his will, investigating it more abundantly, whether he is willing to assent;[^28] or, when cast out from his see and from the City, in Africa …[^29]

## Notes
[^1]: P. Peter, *illustris* and general in Africa (addressee of Letters 13–14); the excerpt survives only in Anastasius the Librarian's Latin (*Collectanea*), so the source here is Latin. T. *Defloratio*, "extract".
[^2]: T. *Vestris* … *vos* … *vobis* … *discite*: the Latin's plural of respect to one man; the English keeps *thou* throughout (Peter is also addressed in the singular, *dignatus es*, *tuo*, *benedicte domine mi*).
[^3]: P. Pyrrhus, patriarch of Constantinople 638–641, deposed; in Africa and then at Rome (645–646) he sought reception without anathematizing his doctrine; Maximus calls him "abbot", the rank left to him.
[^4]: T. *famosissimus*, probably for Greek ἐνδοξότατος; kept "most renowned" throughout.
[^5]: T. *sine successu receptionem* does not construe as a wish; perhaps *cum successu* or a lost word. Rendered as printed, marked [?].
[^6]: T. The Latin glosses *expositio* with the Greek word, garbled in the OCR (table). The Ekthesis (638) of Heraclius, drafted by Sergius.
[^7]: P. Sergius, patriarch of Constantinople 610–638, author of the Ekthesis; "this man" is Pyrrhus.
[^8]: T. *et rius posteriores horum*: *rius* is corrupt (table); read as "those who came after them".
[^9]: T. *divinitatis*, taken as for θεότης, "Godhead".
[^10]: P. Sophronius, patriarch of Jerusalem 634–638, the first opponent of the Alexandrian Pact; Maximus's teacher.
[^11]: T. *ut ipsi testamini, laudatissimi*: plural verb and vocative; read as the plural of respect to Peter (*laudatissime*?), cf. note 2.
[^12]: P. The emperor Heraclius (610–641).
[^13]: P. Pope John IV (640–642), to whom Heraclius wrote in 641 disowning the Ekthesis as Sergius's work.
[^14]: T. *fortissimi*, ironic; probably for Greek κράτιστοι.
[^15]: T. *nec sensus apostolicae facti sunt sedis*, literally "did not become of the sense of the apostolic see": they took no account of it, or did not share its mind. Wording uncertain.
[^16]: P. Pope Honorius I (625–638), whose letter to Sergius the monothelites cited for one will.
[^17]: P. Arcadius, archbishop of Cyprus (d. c. 641/642).
[^18]: P. Severinus (pope 640) and John IV (640–642).
[^19]: P. Theodore I (642–649).
[^20]: T. *suae … codesiem fidom praeferre* read as *suae haeresi fidem praeferre* (table).
[^21]: P. Cyrus, patriarch of Alexandria (631–642), whose Pact of Union of 633 with the Theodosians, in nine chapters, taught one theandric operation.
[^22]: P. Apollinarius of Laodicea (4th c.), who denied Christ a human mind.
[^23]: S. Cf. Lam 2:11 and Jer 8:21 (*contritio filiae populi mei*).
[^24]: T. *almificum*, a rare honorific; probably for a Greek title of a cleric. Rendered "hallowed".
[^25]: T. *terminos*, rendered as for ὅρους, "definitions".
[^26]: S. Matt 16:19; 18:18.
[^27]: T. *miis* read as *mihi* (table); doubtful.
[^28]: T. *anaucre* read as *annuere*, "assent"; *anathematizare* is possible but does not fit the letters as well.
[^29]: T. The text breaks off here in the scaffold mid-sentence (*vel cum sede ejectus et Urbe in Africa*); whether Anastasius's excerpt ends here or a line is lost cannot be told from this page.
