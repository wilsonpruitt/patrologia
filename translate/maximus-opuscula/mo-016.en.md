[145] OPUSCULUM 13 — ON THE TWO NATURES OF CHRIST

*Of the holy Maximus. On the two natures of Christ.*[^1]

1. Arius confesses the three hypostases, but denies the Monad, and does not call the holy Trinity consubstantial. Sabellius confesses the Monad, but denies the Trinity; for he calls the same one Father and Son and Holy Spirit. But the Church of God both confesses the Monad and proclaims the Trinity. Macedonius holds things like Arius; for he supposes the Holy Spirit a creature; but the Church proclaims the Holy Spirit consubstantial with the Father and the Son, and affirms him [?] God.[^2]

Likewise also in the case of the one of the holy Trinity, Nestorius speaks of the natural difference, but does not confess the union; for he does not say that it has come about according to hypostasis. Eutyches confesses the union, but denies the difference according to essence, and introduces confusion of the natures. But the Church holds both the union according to hypostasis, on account of the undividedness, and the difference according to essence, on account of the unconfusedness.

2. How does the utmost union have both identity and otherness? Either identity of essences and otherness of persons, or the reverse. For example, in the case of the holy Trinity there is identity of essence, but otherness of persons; for we confess one essence, but three hypostases. In the case of man there is identity of person, but otherness of essences; for while man is one, the soul is of one essence and the flesh of another. Likewise also in the case of the Master Christ: there is identity of person, but otherness of essences; for while the person, that is, the hypostasis, is one, [148] the Godhead is of one essence and the manhood of another. For just as it is impossible in the case of the holy Trinity to confess the union but not to utter the difference, so there is every necessity, in the case of the one of the holy Trinity, to proclaim both the union and the difference.

3. For just as the difference and the union are not signified by the same words in the case of the holy Trinity, but by saying three hypostases the difference, and by confessing one essence the union, is confessed; so also in the case of the one of the holy Trinity: by acknowledging the two natures the difference, and by proclaiming one composite hypostasis the union, is confessed.

4. For just as we anathematize Arius, not as proclaiming in the holy Trinity the difference according to hypostasis, but as not saying the natural union; so also we anathematize Nestorius, not as acknowledging the natural difference in Christ, but as not saying the union according to hypostasis.

5. Just as we anathematize Sabellius, not as proclaiming in the holy Trinity the natural union, but as not saying the difference according to hypostasis; so we anathematize Eutyches, not as not saying the union according to hypostasis in Christ, but as not acknowledging the natural difference.

6. The difference according to hypostasis in the holy Trinity, and the natural difference in the one of the holy Trinity, one must not speak of as in sense, but understand with the eyes of the understanding. And how is it that in the case of the holy Trinity you utter the three hypostases on account of the difference according to hypostasis, but in the case of the one of the holy Trinity you do not utter two natures in one hypostasis, on account of the natural difference?

7. Just as on account of the consubstantiality of the holy Trinity thou sayest one essence, and on account of the being of other hypostasis three hypostases; so on account of the being of other essence of the Word and the flesh, say thou two essences, and on account of their not being each of its own hypostasis,[^3] one hypostasis.

8. Just as in the case of the holy Trinity we say the one essence not to the confusion of the three hypostases, nor the three hypostases to the abolition of the one essence; so in the case of the one of the holy Trinity [149] we say the one hypostasis not to the confusion of his two natures, nor the two natures to the division of the one hypostasis.

9. He who, on account of the difference of the natures, does not say the union according to hypostasis in the case of Christ, is a Nestorian; and he who does not say the natural difference in the union according to hypostasis is a Eutychianist; but he who proclaims both the union according to hypostasis and the natural difference in the one of the holy Trinity holds the royal and blameless faith.

10. He, then, who says both difference and union in the case of Christ neither abolishes the difference nor confuses the union. For indeed the divine Cyril anathematizes those who on account of the difference abolish the union according to hypostasis;[^4] and the ecumenical synod anathematizes those who on account of the union according to hypostasis abolish the natural difference in the one of the holy Trinity.[^5]

OPUSCULUM 14 — VARIOUS DEFINITIONS

*Of Maximus the Confessor. Various definitions.*

Essence and nature are the same; for both are common and universal, as being predicated of many things differing in number, and never circumscribed in any way whatever by one person.

The enhypostatic is what is common according to the essence, that is, the species, which really subsists in the individuals under it, and is not contemplated by bare conception.[^6]

Otherwise, or again: the enhypostatic is what is composed and co-subsists with another, different according to the essence, into the constitution of one person and the coming-to-be of one hypostasis, and is in no way known by itself.

The consubstantial is what is of the same essence as another, but is different from it according to the hypostasis; as is the case with man and with the other species. If then anything is the same as another according to the essence, it is not the same as it also according to the hypostasis; and if anything is one and united with another according to the essence, it is neither one nor united with it also according to the hypostasis; and if anything has difference toward another according to the hypostasis, this has union toward it according to the essence.

Essential union, then, is that which gathers the hypostases that are different in number and many into one and the same essence. Essential difference is the principle according to which the essence, that is, the nature, remains undiminished and unchangeable, at once uncommixed and unconfused, and, making concourse into one hypostasis with another different in species, in no way departs from its own nature according to nature.

[152] Hypostasis and person are the same; for both are particular and proper, as having naturally their circumscription upon themselves, but not their predication in many.

The enousial is that which not only has upon itself, contemplated in it, the aggregate of the idioms by which one is known from another, but also really possesses the common [element] of the essence.[^7]

Of one hypostasis is that which is composed with another into one and the same hypostasis, but is different from it according to essence; as is the case with soul and body, and with the other things which, differing by otherness of nature, are united according to hypostasis. If then anything composed with another according to union is the same according to the hypostasis, that is, has become one hypostasis with it, it is other than it according to the essence. And if anything is one and united with another according to the hypostasis, this is neither one nor of the same genus with it also according to the essence. And if anything composed with another has difference according to the essence, this has union toward it according to the hypostasis.

Hypostatic union, then, is that which gathers and binds together the different essences, that is, natures, into one person and one and the same hypostasis. Hypostatic difference is the principle according to which the otherness, according to the aggregate of the idioms contemplated in the common [element] of the essence, cutting one from another in number, makes the multitude of the individuals.

Relational union is that which gathers the different inclinations into one will. Relational difference is the movement that cuts asunder the one will by otherness of inclination.

Relational union is a friendly co-disposition, or a concordant breathing-together and assent of wills. Relational difference is otherness of inclination, or unlikeness of wills, or division of disposition.

Confusing union is the composition that comes about by concourse out of hypostases into some whole, which does not at all have the existence of its own parts preserved, after the union, in form and figure, but abolished at once, commixed and unknown, and possesses in no way any difference of the things that have run together in it, signifying their essential property.

Division is a cut right through, which sets apart and separates, and causes to be contemplated by itself, both according to essence and hypostasis, the existence of each thing.

It is proper to hypostasis to be seen by itself, and to be set apart in number from the things of the same species. It is proper to the enhypostatic either to be known [153] with another different according to the essence in a hypostasis, by an indissoluble union; or to be naturally in individuals according to existence.

It is proper to essence to be predicated naturally in all and of all, and to shine forth. It is proper to the enousial to subsist really, and to bear upon itself the true characters of all the personal idioms.

Power is enmattered operation; operation is immaterial power. Or again, operation is the accomplishment of a natural power.

Natural will is the essential aspiration for the things constitutive according to nature. Will of inclination is the self-chosen impulse and movement of thought toward either side.[^8]

Difference is a principle according to which the otherness of the things signified toward one another is naturally preserved, and which is indicative of how [a thing] is.

Identity is invariance, according to which the principle of the thing signified possesses what is in every respect singular, known by no mode of difference.

## Notes
[^1]: T. The author line is garbled in the Kraken text (τοΥ Αγιον MλΞοΥ-), read ΤΟΥ ΑΓΙΟΥ ΜΑΞΙΜΟΥ; the line under it (SAΣΤΗ ΜΑΧi) is a leaked Latin head, and the title is confirmed by the Latin *De duabus Christi naturis*.
[^2]: T. The printed καὶ Θεὸν πῶς διαβεβαιοῖ does not construe ("and affirms him how God"); the Latin has *ac Deum esse affirmat*. Perhaps ὄντως, "truly"; the English gives the sense the Latin gives and marks the word.
[^3]: T. ἰδιοϋπόστατον, "of its own hypostasis": neither the Word nor the flesh has a hypostasis apart from the other in the union (Latin *quod non propria cujusque hypostasis est*). Note that ch. 6 addresses a plural *you* (ἐκφωνεῖτε), ch. 7 a singular (λέγεις … λέγε): kept.
[^4]: S. Cyril of Alexandria, the Twelve Anathematisms (Ep. 17, to Nestorius), esp. 3 (against those who divide the hypostases after the union); cf.
[^5]: S. The Council of Chalcedon (451), definition of faith: «the difference of the natures being in no way abolished on account of the union»; cf.
[^6]: T. ἐνυπόστατος, "enhypostatic": two definitions follow, the species really subsisting in its individuals, and the nature co-subsisting with another in one hypostasis. ἐπίνοια = conception (kept apart from λογισμός, thought).
[^7]: T. ἐνούσιον, "the enousial", a term formed on ἐνυπόστατον: what really possesses the common of the essence. Kept as a term of art.
[^8]: T. θέλημα γνωμικόν, "will of inclination": the scholars' "gnomic will". ἐφ᾿ ἑκάτερα, "toward either side", that is, either of two alternatives.
