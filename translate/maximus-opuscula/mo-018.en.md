But he has also the not willing to die, because the flesh naturally refuses death.»[^1] And this same [Father], commenting on the great evangelist John, says:[^2] «Christ himself made the discourse on this, teaching that dying for all he has as willed, because the divine nature had wished it; but as unwilled, because of the passions on the cross. And so far as it came to the flesh refusing death, he says such things.» And the inspired Severian, who was bishop of Gabala,[^3] on *Father, let this cup pass from me*, and on *Father, save me from this hour*:[^4] «The Lord, anticipating, stops the mouths of the heretics, to show that he put on this much-suffering body, the body that is in agony at death, that trembles at the violence of death, that grieves and is troubled at the end of life. He says: *Now is my soul troubled. My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death*: not my Godhead; for the Divine is impassible and untroubled and unafraid. *For the spirit*, says the Lord, *is eager, but the flesh weak.* So he shows two wills: the one divine, the other human.»

But I think that the account concerning the duality of the wills of the same [Christ] has been sufficiently shown by the God-bearing [Fathers]: both of one and another will, divine and human, and of death's being both willed and unwilled for the Saviour — the one on account of what he was from the beginning, the other on account of what he afterwards became. We must begin, then, also the account concerning the difference of the operations, and their duality, in order, with the testimonies of the God-speaking Fathers that set their seal on this one too.

At once, then, the great confessor and teacher Ambrose, the archbishop of Milan, in his second book to Gratian, goes through these things:[^5] «Equal, then, in the form of God, but the same less in the assumption of the flesh and in the passion of the man. For in what way can the same nature be less and equal? And how, if it is less, does the same do likewise the things which the Father does? For in what way is the same operation from a different essence? [168] For can the lesser operate as the greater does? Or can there be one operation where the essence is different?»

And indeed the celebrated Cyril, hierarch of the Jerusalemites, in his discourse on the Gospel text where the Lord made the water wine, whose beginning is:[^6] «A wonder, and this too came about as a wonder. He wrought a wonder; he showed the twofold operation, suffering as man, operating as God, the same; for not one and another, even if in one way and another.» So also Leo, the all-strong and all-holy exarch of the great Church of the Romans, in the dogmatic tome to Flavian among the saints, against Nestorius and Eutyches of evil name:[^7] «For he operates in each form, with the communion of the other, what is proper to it; the Word working what is of the Word, the body carrying out what is of the body; and the one of them shines with the wonders, the other has fallen under the insults.»

And John, who possessed the golden mouth, or rather Christ's, in his discourse on the Widow who brought the two mites into the Treasury, whose beginning is:[^8] «Simple is the name of fasting. And in other things the operation of the manhood of the conjoined natures is different, and the strength of the Godhead different. As I mean: below he toils, and above he holds the elements together. Below he hungers, and from above he supplies rains. Below he is afraid, and above he thunders. Below he stands at a tribunal, and from above he beholds himself. But in the matter of almsgiving, what belongs to the twofold operation runs together. For he who from above, from the Cherubim, awards prizes to the merciful, he, sitting before the Treasury, tests the lovers of man.» The same, in his discourse on Thomas the apostle, against the Arians:[^9] «Having heard these things, I cleansed my soul of unbelief; I stripped off the doubtful inclination, I took up conviction, I touched the body rejoicing and trembling. Together with my fingers I spread out the eye of my soul too, and at last I perceived two operations.»

But indeed also the most wise Cyril, president of the Alexandrians, in the thirty-second chapter of the *Thesaurus*:[^10] «For surely we shall not grant that the natural operation of God and of a thing made is one; lest we either bring up what was made into the divine essence, or bring down the pre-eminence of the divine nature into [169] the place befitting originated things. For those whose operation and power are invariantly one must also have the oneness of the genus preserved.»

These things, then, these men proclaim piercingly,[^11] the Fathers of piety, whom, out of many, or rather countless, and out of the whole, so to speak in Scripture's words, cloud of witnesses,[^12] we have brought forward cursorily, restraining the bulk of the letter, for the most evident demonstration of their true knowledge concerning Christ, the Saviour of the universe; impelled by which, they handed down to the holy Churches, moved by the Spirit, to hold that, just as the natures of the same and one are two, so equal in number to the natures are both his wills and his operations, and one and another volition and operation, divine and human, for the manifestation of the essential difference of the things from which, and in which, and which the same is; [a difference] which banishes the phantasm and the confusion together, and with them division altogether, as being made known[^13] in the one and the same God the Word, incarnate for us, [as the difference] of the things from which he really and unclouded is, according to the indissoluble union. For if the essential difference, both of the united things themselves and of what belongs to them naturally, is not made known without confusion in the utmost union according to hypostasis, neither will the principle of the union be made known without confusion.

But those of the contrary and opposing party [say] the reverse. For Apollinarius the impious[^14] speaks thus in the discourse entitled by him *Demonstration concerning the divine incarnation in the likeness of man*:[^15] «And they do not remember that this will is said to be proper, not to a man from the earth, as they themselves suppose, but to God who came down from heaven.» And the same, again, the fool, in [the book] entitled *The incarnate manifestation itself of God*: «For Christ is one, moved by the divine will alone, just as we also know his operation to be one, proceeding in different wonders and sufferings from his one nature. For he is God incarnate, and is believed so.» And after a little: «And the [words] *Father, if it be possible, let this cup depart from me; nevertheless not mine, but thy will be done*, do not carry the reflection of one and another will, not concurring with one another, but of one and the same, operating divinely, but by way of economy refusing death; since he who said this was God bearing flesh, having no difference in willing.»

And the disciple and successor of that man's impiety, and an enemy, true to his name, to the word of truth, Polemon the heretic,[^16] in his *Antirrhetic* against the holy Fathers, says these things perversely: «For the Word, the Christ, [172] was God, and, united to the perfection of flesh and become man, not having mingled with himself the changeable mind, moved by natural will toward contraries, but rather having become mind to himself, he did all things by the unchangeable divine will. But not the one divine, the other deified, as Gregory the Cappadocian has it,[^17] since [that would be] changeable. For what could be deified could also in every way be made earthy, as the first Adam was.» And in the sixth of the letters written by him to Timothy,[^18] his fellow-worker and wicked accomplice in impiety: «He who says two wills of Christ, according to those who have said so of old and now, either brings in the one as two Christs, divided from one another not by nature only, but indeed by enmity also; or teaches that the same one is at war with himself. For where there is duality, there is in every way division.»

But Themistius too, the shield-bearer of the faction of Severus and the leader of the folly of the Agnoetae,[^19] in chapter 45 of the second book of those against the tome presented to the empress Theodora by Theodosius, the heresiarch of the heretics named after him,[^20] [says] these things, hateful to God: «For not because the sacred Athanasius too said that Christ showed two wills at the time of the passion, shall we therefore also attach two volitions to him, and these at war with one another, according to these syllogisms of thine; but we shall know piously that the one volition of Emmanuel, as of one, moves now in a human way, now in a way befitting God.» And the same ill-met heretic again, in chapter 52,[^21] book 3, of the treatise composed by him in conformity with the impiety of his fellow-tribesman: «I say that the knowledge and the operation of Emmanuel are one, as also the volition. Consider, then, thus: he who is unchangeable, as God, he who is higher than every passion, moved in a manner befitting man according to the volition, refuses the passion; but again, in a manner befitting God, takes courage toward the passion; and each of these through flesh.» And these things these men [say] perversely.

But those who are likewise opposed in mind to these and to the truth — I mean the leaders of division — being driven together into a wrong belief equal to theirs and the same, concerning one will and one operation, who also championed the union of inclination by relation, buzz these things about. Theodore, the evil-minded and contentious, who plundered [the Church] of the Mopsuestians,[^22] in the second book of what he misexpounded [173] on the wonders: «*I will; be thou clean*, the Saviour, saying this to the leper, showed that the volition is one, the operation one, brought forth according to one and the same authority — not, I say, of nature, but of good pleasure — according to which the man who, according to foreknowledge, came to be afterwards from the seed of David was united to God the Word, having from the womb itself the inward appropriation toward him.»[^23]

And the follower and successor of his madness, Nestorius the accursed, in what he calls the *Clear Initiation*,[^24] in the second book of the treatise on it, [says] these things maliciously: «We keep the natures without confusion, joined not according to essence but by inclination; on which account we see also their volition, operation and lordship as one, shown forth by equality of worth. For God the Word, having taken up the man whom he foreordained, was preferred by him in the principle of authority, on account of the foreknown disposition.» And the same madman again, in the same [treatise], book four: «God the Word was not one, and the man in whom he came to be another. For the person of both was one in worth and honour, worshipped by the whole creation, divided in no place or time by otherness of counsel and will.» Indeed Paul the Persian too, being a pseudo-deacon of the most foul heresy of Nestorius,[^25] in his discourse *On Judgement*, utters these things together with them, fighting against God: «Since the union of God the Word toward the man whom he took up did not come about according to essence, one nature did not come about. And if one nature did not come about, Christ is not one single-personed hypostasis. The union, then, is according to good pleasure, held fast by identity of counsel and inclination, so that both the difference of the natures may be shown unconfused, and the mystery of the good pleasure may be displayed by a monad of wish.»

With these, as you see, all-excellent ones — examining both the writings and the doctrines before them — the Ekthesis,[^26] which now, I know not how, has crept in on account of our sins against the blameless faith, has proved very much of one voice and one accord in all things, ratifying the one will in Christ, and the one operation, like the senseless. The one will, by saying: «Whence, following the holy Fathers in all things and in this too, we confess one will of our Lord Jesus Christ»;[^27] and the one operation, by asserting: «But indeed to hold two wills also follows on this, that is, on the maintaining of the two operations.» For indeed if it determines that the wills follow the operation, it is in every way somehow necessary, the consequent having been ratified by it — I mean the one will — that the antecedent also be ratified, namely the one operation. For, hastening sophistically to bring this in together with the one volition, it decreed, as it saw fit, that the volition follows the operation. Therefore
