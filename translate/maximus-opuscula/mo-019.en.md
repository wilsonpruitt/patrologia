title: Opusculum 15, against the Ekthesis, 3
---
[176] … and it knows as Fathers, whom it has followed in this and in all things, not those who are truly holy, but those who are not holy at all.

For surely no one would ever say that Athanasius the celebrated spoke of one will, when he cries out expressly, as we have set out before: «He shows here two wills: the one human, the other divine.»[^1] Nor Gregory the Theologian, who says of the Saviour's human will: «For his willing is not even contrary to God, being wholly deified.»[^2] Nor his namesake, the teacher of the Nyssenes, who says: «The soul wills, the body touches»; and: «By the operating body, and by the impulse of the will that comes to be in the soul»; and: «Willing through the soul, touching through the body»; and: «The human will is one thing, and the divine another; and saying, Not mine, he signified the human by the word; but adding, Thine, he showed the conjoinedness of his own Godhead with the Father.»[^3] Nor John Chrysostom, who teaches: «For the words show not agony only, but two wills. And if what is said were about the Godhead, a certain contradiction arises; but if about the flesh, the things said have reason. For it is no ground of condemnation that the flesh does not will to die; for this belongs to nature. But he displays all the things of nature apart from sin, and with great abundance.»[^4] Nor Cyril of Alexandria, who wisely teaches: «For this was the will of the Father, to bring the economy through to its end. But he has also the not willing to die, because the flesh naturally refuses death.» And: «He has dying for all as what is willed, because the divine nature had wished it; but as unwilled, because of the flesh naturally refusing death.»[^5] Nor Severian, the president of Gabala, who testifies: «So that he shows two wills: the one divine, the other human.»[^6] But neither would anyone say that the great confessor Ambrose spoke of one operation,[^7] who prudently proclaims: «For in what manner is the same operation of a different essence? For can there be one operation where the essence is different?»[^8] Nor Cyril of Jerusalem, who charges: «He showed the twofold operation, suffering as man, operating as God, the same.»[^9] Nor the divine Leo, who declares with understanding: «For each form operates, with the communion of the other, what it has had as its own.»[^10] Nor Chrysostom again: «And in the other things the operation of the conjoined natures is different, of the manhood and of the Godhead; but in the matter of mercy, what belongs to the twofold operation runs together; and so I perceived two operations.»[^11] Nor Cyril likewise, the most sacred teacher of Alexandria: «For we shall surely not grant that the natural operation of God and of the thing made is one.»[^12]

[177] These, or some of these, it declares to be neither holy nor approved mystagogues of the Church; and it declares their glorious confession of two wills and operations to be impious, and alien to Christian doctrine.[^13] For it confirms the account for us, saying this: «And likewise the expression of the two operations scandalizes many, as having been spoken by none of the holy and approved mystagogues of the Church; and moreover there follows from it the professing of two wills, standing contrary to one another; and from this, that two who will contrary things are introduced; which is impious and alien to Christian doctrine.»[^14]

Behold: plainly, and bare-headed, as the saying goes, it defines that the professing of two wills, and of the operations that on its account are their antecedents, equal in number to them, belongs not to holy men, nor to approved mystagogues of the Church, but rather to impious men, as impious and alien, as it says, to Christian doctrine; and as introducing two who will contrary things, for those who preach these things. That is: the two wills, and the operations equal in number to them, it charges to the Spirit-moved Fathers, whom it did not choose to follow, or to call holy, or at all to profess these things with them and after them, for the confirmation of the mystery of Christ God according to the flesh.

But to whom, and who are those, concerning whom and by whom it said the expression «one operation» had been spoken, and whom it follows in this, as in all things, so as to confess the one will of Christ God — it has been shown both who they manifestly are, and that they are the champions of confusion and the leaders of division. For instance (for it is not out of place, for confirmation, to run over them again briefly): Apollinarius the impious, fighting against God thus: «For Christ is one, moved by the divine will alone, according as we know his operation also to be one, and having no difference in willing.»[^15] Polemon, the enemy of the truth:[^16] «With an unchangeable divine will he has made all things; but not the one divine, the other deified, according to Gregory the Cappadocian.» And: «He who says two wills of Christ, after those puffed up of old and now, either introduces the one as two Christs, or teaches one at war with himself.» Themistius the lawless:[^17] «But we know the one volition of Emmanuel, as of one, to be moved now humanly, now in a manner befitting God»; and: «I say that the knowledge and the operation of Emmanuel are one, as also the volition.» Theodore the God-stricken:[^18] «I have shown that the volition is one, the operation one, brought forth according to one and the same authority.» Nestorius the all-stricken:[^19] «Wherefore we see their volition, and operation and lordship, to be one.» Paul, a Persian in inclination too:[^20] «The union is by good pleasure, held together by identity of counsel and inclination»; and: «The mystery of good pleasure is displayed as of a singular wish.» These accursed and execrable men, then, it defines as holy Fathers. These it knows as approved mystagogues of the Church; and, following them in all things, and in this too, and defining with them one volition and operation of Christ God, it confuses and cuts asunder the principle of the economy just as they do.

[180] How then, thinking the things of its opponents, has it supposed that it thinks otherwise, setting the votes crosswise, and choosing and preferring non-being to being? For with truth, not injustice, as judge, it is surely plain to everyone that he is in harmony and agreement with them, not who says what they do not say, but who professes what they teach; since it knows to characterize what is of other doctrine by disharmony, and what is of the same doctrine by harmony. And yet it too bears witness in a way, crying that the foul Nestorius would never have dared to say two wills, but to confess identity of will,[^21] that is, one will, of the two persons fashioned by him.[^22] If then, even according to it, Nestorius professes not two wills but one; and Apollinarius too, and all the impious who come from them, and moreover one operation also, as the account has shown: then beyond dispute, by the things it teaches, it has set forth that we, who say two according to the divine Fathers, are entirely of other mind from them, and that itself, which says one, is of one mind with Nestorius and Apollinarius and those about them; and that it strikes out the divine Fathers, as I said, and enrols the accursed as its own Fathers.

By what cause, or what kind of cause compelling him, has he brought it in against the pious confession? If on the ground that we had no faith, the account is false: for all who professed the right account were Christians even before it. But if on the ground that we had, the mode is one to be shunned, which brings in the superfluous as counterfeit; by which, overshadowing their own villainy, and having not one single testimony at all of any synod, or Father, or Scripture to bring forward in witness of their own views, they say these things, feigning the doctrines of piety, which were handed down to us by those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and ministers of the Word,[^23] and by their disciples and successors in order, the God-inspired teachers of the truth; which is the same as to say, the five holy and ecumenical synods of the most blessed and God-bearing Fathers. But if these handed these things down, what is the demonstration, or whence their confirmation of such doctrines? Besides, since we adhere to and confess the holy synods, it is plain that the invention of what has been brought in is counterfeit. But if these things are not of the holy synods, what is the bombast of this drama, which bends the ill-sounding under the name of the all-renowned, so that the appellation of the glorious Fathers and synods may be a bait for the simpler unto apostasy from the truth — an appellation ingeniously contrived from the very beginning by those of other [181] mind, both for the covering of their own views, as I said, and as a kind of crafty snatching-away of those led astray?

Hence, for the complete abolition of such men — I mean of those who at various times search out deceitful searchings against the faith — a synodal law too is most excellently defined by the holy assembly of the blessed Fathers at Chalcedon, enjoining somewhat thus: «The holy and ecumenical synod has defined that it is permitted to no one to bring forward another faith, that is, to write or to compose, or to think or to teach others; and that those who dare either to compose another faith, that is, to bring it forward, or to teach or hand on another symbol to those wishing to turn to the knowledge of the truth from Hellenism or Judaism, or from any heresy whatsoever — these, if they be bishops or clerics, the bishops are to be alien from the episcopate and the clerics from the clergy; and if they be monks or laymen, they are to be anathematized.»[^24]

Who then, of those who have in any way perceived God, and who hang upon his all-radiant glory — from whom, and through whom, and to whom is the salvation of those who piously confess him — would still choose[^25] to abandon the orthodox faith handed down to us from above and from the beginning through the divine oracles and the Fathers, and to prefer the recent innovation; against which, as indeed against every other invented by those of other mind contrary to pious thought, the lawful sentence of the approved and synodal curse of the holy Fathers gathered at Chalcedon has been issued, as the account has shown? Unless perhaps, grown weary of his own life, he has preferred alienation from the glory that is from God.

Wherefore, beholding it in the facts themselves, master,[^26] and perceiving that it is, plainly and openly, a forerunning and a proclamation — toward the overthrow of all godliness and the denial of the true knowledge of the Only-begotten — of the final error and of the last beast, according to the foretelling of the holy men who thought these things, from divine inspiration and most blessed illumination, for the guarding of us who have reached the ends of the ages:[^27] let us each watch this imported novelty with all the strength of his own heart, so that we be not carried away with it heedlessly, as it were, and carelessly, but stand very weightily and manfully against all impiety, casting down thoughts, to speak in Scripture's words, and every height lifted up against the knowledge of God.[^28] Hence too the Saviour of all himself, our Lord and God, securing us in his love for man: Then, he says, if any man say unto you, Lo, here is the Christ, lo, here, believe it not[^29] — sending the counterfeit and differing faiths and suppositions of the crooked concerning him very far from the mystery concerning him, and keeping his own followers from every alien opinion,[^30] by which [184] those contentious and contrary-minded in manner are wont to trouble, and indeed to war against, the sincerity of the confession toward him; so that the pious and God-loving mind among us may never, caught by any of these, fall away from the truth, slipping aside by the persuasiveness of fables, and not made firm by the demonstration of the words of the Fathers. Watch then and pray, the Word again charges us for this reason, that ye enter not into temptation:[^31] wishing by watching to produce in us sobriety in this, and by prayer his succour according to grace. And he speaks here of the temptation that comes from error, not from necessity, into which we are led from within, not from elsewhere, turning aside from the straight and royal path.

Cutting this straight yourselves, guided by God, guide wisely also those like me, your own, who limp on both hams,[^32] bringing them back to the shadowless and unsetting light of the most divine confession and conduct of the Word, looking to the recompense from there. Since a divine law also charges those strong, like you, to bear the burdens of the weak, and so to fulfil the law.[^33] And a heavy burden, beyond any other among hard things, is the indifference of words about the same matters, which arises in the more childish through the shifting of times and persons, by deceit perhaps, or by threat, of those who coax, fawning or being exacting, and who fasten on unrestrainedly, above all in a time of the disorder of the whole. Stretching out a hand in sympathy, then, to your suppliant, may you not cease, now cleansing him by word with knowledge, now strengthening him by prayer with love for man, and making him kin to, and bringing him before, God the Word, rightly worshipped and believed by you, and King of all the ages, both now and at his fearful appearing; to whom with the Father and the Holy Spirit be honour, might, glory, worship, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
