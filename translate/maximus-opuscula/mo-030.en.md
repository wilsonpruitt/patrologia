[280] OPUSCULUM 27 — VARIOUS DEFINITIONS OF THE HOLY FATHERS ON THE TWO OPERATIONS

*Various definitions of our holy and God-bearing Fathers, on the two operations of our Lord and God and Saviour Jesus Christ.*

*Of the holy Justin, philosopher and at the last martyr, from the discourse to Euphrasius the sophist, On Providence and Faith, whose beginning is: «Undefiled is the Word.»*[^1]

«If God has volition concurrent with his nature, as he has operation also (for God is always by nature volitive and operative), none of the volitive beings will be of the same natural volition and operation with him; since it would then wholly be of the same nature too. And in respect of operation and nature God knows no circumscription.»

*Of the same, from the same discourse.*

«The operation of every essence is the quality inherent in it. Natural and constitutive operation is the defining difference of the nature of the thing indicated; without which nothing whatever, in any respect, has the discernment of its how-being.

«Natural operation is the essential and constitutive quality of every essence; that which is deprived of it is deprived also of all essence.

«Natural operation is an essential power which allows no alteration whatever of one thing into another in respect of essence.

«Natural operation is the power which preserves the unmixed essential distinction of all things from all.»

*Of the holy Alexander, from the letter against the Arians to Aeglon, bishop of Cynopolis.*[^2]

«Natural operation is the innate movement of every essence. Natural operation is the essential principle of every nature, and the principle of its knowledge. Natural operation is the power indicative of every essence.»

*Of Gregory of Nyssa.*[^3]

«The operation of a rational soul is a movement of mind and [281] understanding. Operation is an essential movement characteristic of the nature whose own it is, through which that nature is known as differing essentially from the others. Operation is an effective movement; and that is called effective which is moved from itself. For we say that operation is the natural power and movement of each essence, without which a nature neither is nor is known. For of intellectual beings it is intellection; of sensitive beings, sense, according to which they both naturally touch the things outside and fall under the things outside. Of the winged, flight; of swimming things, swimming; of creeping things, creeping; of walking things, walking; of shoots, sprouting. And, to speak comprehensively, the idiom signifying each nature we call natural operation. Of this only what is not is deprived. For what is, partaking of some essence, will wholly partake also of the power that naturally manifests it. For true reason knows the natural operations of essences as their definitions. The operation of a rational soul is the proper understanding and movement of the mind. The operation of a rational soul is its life according to nature.»

*Of Chrysostom.*[^4]

«The essential operation of God is to be nowhere: not by not being, but by being above place and time and nature, in which beginning and end and coming-to-be are naturally co-conceived; he who is sustainer and maker by wishing alone. For without natural operation the nature of nothing is manifest. For as a characteristic mark that makes it known, the Creator essentially deposited in each nature the movement which distinguishes it from the others.»

*Of the holy Cyril.*[^5]

«Operation and power befitting God is to be everywhere, and to fill the heaven ineffably, and again to fill the earth, and to pass through all things, but to be contained by none. For he is not comprehensible, or bounded by extension; nor is he within any circumscription. For he who is without quantity and without magnitude and incorporeal does not admit of suffering any such thing.»

*Of the holy Basil.*[^6]

«Since the definition of every essence is the operation according to its nature, a confirmation leading the mind up to the nature from which it has its procession, of those whose operation is one and the same, of these the essence too is one.»

*Of the same, from the books against Eunomius.*[^7]

«How is there otherness of essence where identity of operation is known? For when the essences are varied, the operations too must be varied. How is the Spirit not of the same essence with the Father and the Son, being also of the same operation? How is there otherness of essence in the Trinity, in which identity of operation is found? For if it holds invariantly in respect of essence, it will hold so in respect of [284] power too. And of things whose power is equal, the operation too is surely wholly equal; and of things whose nature is one, the operations are the same.»

*Of the holy Gregory of Nyssa.*[^8]

«Therefore the identity of operation in Father and Son and Holy Spirit shows clearly the invariance of the nature. And we can come to know the nature of the one operating through the observation of the operations. And the commonness of the nature is shown in act,[^9] being established through the identity of the operations. But God is the Father, God the Son, God the Holy Spirit; yet one God in the same preaching, because no difference either of nature or of operation is considered in the Godhead. If then both operation and power are one, how is it possible to suspect otherness of nature in those in whom we find no difference in respect of power and operation?

«It is wholly necessary to confess that choice too is appropriate and proper to the nature; and that if they were unlike in nature, the wills too would always be unlike. But the power being sufficient in each of the two, neither will fail toward the fulfilment of its own will. Therefore, if we should see the operations performed by the Father and the Holy Spirit[^10] differing from one another, we conjecture from the otherness of the operations that the operating natures too are different. For it is not possible that things which stand apart in the principle of nature should be brought together toward the form of the operations: neither does fire chill, nor ice warm; but by the otherness of the natures the operations from them too are separated together from one another.»

*Of Cyril.*[^11]

«For things which have obtained the same nature with one another will operate alike. And for those whose principle of how-being is altered, for these the principle of their operation in all things would not be the same either. Things which have the same operation and make use of the same natural powers must in every case be of the same essence. For none of the things that are will bear, toward what is of other genus and of other essence, the same powers and operations invariantly. For things which are of the same operation are confessed to be also of the same essence. That things of other nature and of other genus have the same operation, no one of sound mind would grant. For fire would not operate one and the same thing as water; but just as they have the principle of essence and of quality severed, so too they will render, along with it, a different operation. For of those whose operation and power is invariantly one, of these the commonness of the species must also be preserved. For things having the same operation are wont to be also of the same essence, and otherness of nature in them is implausible. For we shall surely not grant one natural operation of God and of a thing made, lest we either bring up the thing made into the divine essence, or bring down the distinctive prerogative of the divine nature to the [285] mode befitting things originated. For things which have the one operation will bear one and the same principle of how-being.»

*Of the holy Ambrose, bishop of Milan, from the second book to Gratian the emperor.*[^12]

«For how is the same operation from a different essence? For the lesser does not operate so, as the greater operates. For where there is a different essence, it is impossible that there be one operation.»

*Of the holy Basil, from the syllogisms against Eunomius.*[^13]

«One thing is he who has operated, another the operation, and another the effect of operation; and these stand very far apart from one another. For the one operating is he who is moved to operate something. And operation is, as it were, the operative movement, and the very impulse, as one might say, of choice, straining and inclining toward operating something, and ceasing and ending at the same time as it has chosen, both moved more swiftly and running past. And effects of operation are the things accomplished and subsisting from it.»
