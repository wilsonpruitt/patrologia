[856] *Of the same.*

EXPOSITION OF PSALM 59

*Unto the end, for those who shall be changed; for an inscription on a pillar, to David; for teaching, when he burned* [857] *Mesopotamia and Syria Soba;[^1] and Joab returned and struck the valley of salt, twelve thousand.*[^2]

*Unto the end, for those who shall be changed* the present psalm is inscribed:[^3] both because of the transformation and change of inclination and of choice which came to pass[^4] for men at the end of the times, through the sojourn of Christ, from unbelief to faith, and from vice to virtue, and from ignorance to knowledge of God; and because of the universal natural change and renewal in grace of the whole race of men, which shall come to pass hereafter at the end of the ages through our Saviour God himself, from death and corruption to immortal life and incorruption, through the resurrection that is awaited. And *for an inscription on a pillar, to David*: that is, to Christ himself; both because of the destruction of vice, after the manner of a pillar, which has already come to pass by his divine incarnation, in him as author and Saviour, and after him in those who live piously according to him; and because of the utter obliteration of both death and corruption that is yet to come to pass through him.

And *for teaching, when he burned Mesopotamia and Syria Soba*.[^5] For teaching us, who are being changed and shall be changed (plainly, with the praiseworthy and divine change), the psalm has been inscribed by the intelligible David: I mean, by Christ God. For he alone, being the true king of Israel, the Israel that sees God, as the destroyer of all vice and lack of knowledge, and the conqueror of all time and nature, through which vice is wont to be constituted in us, *burned Mesopotamia*,[^6] conversing with us through flesh: that is, the habit of vice which is flooded by the passions of the flesh that are contrary to nature. *And Syria Soba*:[^7] that is, the disposition enslaved to time, that is to say, deceived by this age. For Soba is interpreted "seven";[^8] and this plainly shows the property of time. For time is hebdomadic. For the devil is wont to stir up nature[^9] and time along with himself against virtue and knowledge, joining the unseen wars, as Adraazar did the two Syrias against David.[^10] For Adraazar, rendered into the Greek tongue, is interpreted "loosing strength" [860] or "razing power": and this is the devil, who by deceit at the beginning dissolved the spiritual strength of our nature through the transgression of the divine commandment, and enslaved man to time and to nature. For without the things that are under nature and time he cannot fight against men at all.

*And Joab returned and struck the valley of salt, twelve thousand.*[^11] For everyone who serves as general for the intelligible king David, that is, for our Lord Jesus, against the contrary powers, strikes twelve thousand in the valley of salt. And the valley of salt is the flesh, which became through the disobedience a place of *passions of dishonour*:[^12] in which, as in a valley, every pious and God-loving man strikes, through reason and contemplation, nature and time, that is, the soul's irrational prepossession concerning them, in no way abiding, because of the height of virtue and knowledge, in the laws that are under nature and time. For the number twelve signifies nature and time; since nature is pentadic, because of the senses, and time hebdomadic, as is quite evident to all. And having added five to seven, thou wouldst plainly fill up the number twelve.

And perhaps the number of the slain also signifies the triad of the powers of the soul, wickedly entwined through deceit, contrary to nature, with the tetrad[^13] of sensible things by relation, through which every sin is wont to come to be: which number he will slay outright, and well, who in the struggles on behalf of truth ministers to the intelligible David, and engages the invisible battles with knowledge, and draws up spiritually in battle array against the wicked powers. For every power of the soul, when it is moved contrary to nature, is wont to be entwined with sensible things. So when reason and anger and desire, being three in number, the generic powers of the soul, are worsted by sensible things, which are four, it makes up the number twelve. For four upon three, and three upon four put together with the five, fill up this number: which he destroys who goes over faithfully to the Lord, and fights nobly as a champion against every unseen and hostile spirit.

The present psalm, then, has been inscribed *for those who shall be changed*, for us, *for teaching* about the good things that have come to us from the intelligible David; and *for an inscription on a pillar, to David*: that is, for the proclamation of his divine and saving achievements on our behalf. For an inscription on a pillar signifies the proclamation of achievements, according to the interpreters who are expert in such names.

*O God, thou hast cast us off, and brought us down; thou wast angry, and hast had pity on us.*[^14]

[861] God cast us off when, not having kept the commandment at the beginning, we showed ourselves unworthy of converse with him face to face; he brought us down when, because of our vice, he estranged us from the height of the glory in paradise; he was angry when, for the requital and punishment of vice, he subjected us to the penalty of death; he had pity on us when, through his only-begotten Son, God the Word, who was incarnate and accepted the debt owed on behalf of us all, he redeemed us from death and led us back again into his own glory.

*Thou hast shaken the earth and troubled it; heal its fractures, for it has tottered.*[^15]

He shook our earth and troubled it, that is, the heart, giving us *the sword of the Spirit*,[^16] which distinguishes with knowledge the worse from the better, and rouses the law of the Spirit against the law of the flesh,[^17] and makes in us the praiseworthy war against the passions, and shakes the cohesion of the passions in us, and troubles the conscience over the unseemly memories that come by prepossession. And through the spiritual regeneration in grace he heals the fractures of our heart, which it underwent when, through the disobedience at the beginning, from the transgression in paradise, it tottered and slipped.

*Thou hast shown thy people hard things; thou hast made us drink the wine of compunction. Thou hast given to them that fear thee a signal, to flee from the face of the bow; that thy beloved may be delivered.*[^18]

God shows hard things to those who fear him through the commandments, in the practical philosophy,[^19] setting before them the modes of the mortification of the body; or, on the contrary, through his manifold providence and judgement,[^20] with love for man chastening us for our profit,[^21] us who do not of our own volition bear to come under the divine yoke of the law of the commandments. And he gives the wine of compunction to drink, that is, bestowing the knowledge which, in spiritual contemplation, follows on the purification that comes through practice; for all spiritual knowledge pricks the heart with compunction, bringing it to a perception of the greatness of the divine benefits toward us. And he gives to those who fear him *a signal, to flee from the face of the bow*, teaching those who fear him to bear about *the mortification of Jesus in the body*,[^22] by which the complete inactivity of sin is established. For the true signal is the power that through the cross mortifies the way of thinking of the flesh.[^23] *To flee from the face of the bow*: that is, from the deceitful surface that appears in the things that are. For the face of the bow (I mean, of each demonic passion) is the surface of bodies which provokes sense, wounding sense like the point of an arrow, and stirring up the soul unseemly to the passions. Therefore the Word[^24] says that those who fear the Lord will flee from the face [864] of the bow, but not from the bow. For the bow of sin, without its face, would perhaps do nothing at all against those who fear the Lord.

So that the beloved of God may be delivered. And the beloved of God are the thoughts which, by contemplation, hold together the divine knowledge; whom the modes of the virtues that are constituted in practice deliver from all error and from all the memories of sin[^25] which, by prepossession, trouble the soul.

*Save with thy right hand, and hearken to me. God has spoken in his Holy One: I will exult, and divide Sikima, and measure out the valley of tents.*[^26]

God saves with his right hand, giving his only-begotten Son (for the Son is the right hand of the Father) as a ransom for the salvation of the world. And he hearkens, giving to the righteous man, besides the forgiveness of sins, spiritual gifts of grace as well. For God hears the one who supplicates him, first giving forgiveness of sins through repentance; but he hearkens, giving, besides the forgiveness of sins, the workings that come through spiritual grace as well. And he speaks *in his Holy One*, that is, in his incarnate Word, his divine purpose, *hidden from the ages and from the generations*,[^27] according to the economy in the flesh of Christ himself,[^28] through his manner of life among us, befitting a man, as it were crying out expressly, and giving to the world,[^29] for a pattern of everlasting life, an image more resounding than any voice: the display of good works. And he speaks also in each holy one who keeps his commandments, showing, as it were, his words living through the works, and more resounding than any voice.

And the righteous man exults when, perfected by the grace of the Only-begotten, he becomes able *to divide Sikima*:[^30] that is, to set before others, in proportion, from expert experience, the manly modes of the virtues in practice. For Sikima is interpreted "shouldering": which is the habit that holds back all the passions and is productive of all the virtues. For they say that the shoulder is a symbol of action.

[^1]: T. Migne prints [erat Σωβάλ] after Σωβᾶ: the name stood as Σωβάλ before the editor's emendation (the editor's note b: «Sic emendo», after the codices in what follows, the LXX of 2 Kgdms and 1 Paral., and the Hebrew צובה). The English follows the printed Σωβᾶ, as does the facing Latin (*Soba*). The same line prints Μεσυποταμίαν, a misprint for Μεσοποταμίαν.
[^2]: S. Ps 59:1–2 LXX (Ps 60 title, Heb.).
[^3]: T. Migne prints [ἐπιγέγραπται] "has been inscribed" after ἐπιγράφεται "is inscribed"; the sense is the same.
[^4]: T. γενομένην, "which came to pass"; the facing Latin has *futuram*, "which is to come". Translated as printed.
[^5]: T. Migne prints [Fr. Σωβάλ] after Σωβᾶ: here the edition marked Fr. reads Σωβάλ.
[^6]: P. Mesopotamia: in Greek "the land between the rivers"; hence the habit of vice "flooded" by the passions.
[^7]: T. Migne prints [etiam Fr. Σωβᾶ· et deinceps]: from here on the edition marked Fr. also reads Σωβᾶ. The editor's note b remarks that «seven», the sense Maximus gives the name just below, belongs to a different Hebrew word (שבע), not to צובה.
[^8]: P. Soba: Zobah, the Aramean kingdom of Adraazar (Hadadezer), north of Damascus.
[^9]: T. Φύσιν "nature" kept against a digital witness's Φησίν "he says": the facing Latin has *naturam*, and the pair nature and time runs through the passage.
[^10]: S. cf. 2 Kgdms 8:3–8; 10:6–19 LXX (2 Sam 8:3–8; 10:6–19): Adraazar, king of Soba, and the Syrians he brought against David.
[^11]: P. Joab: David's commander (2 Kgdms 8:16); for the victory in the valley of salt cf. 2 Kgdms 8:13; 1 Chr 18:12.
[^12]: S. cf. Rom 1:26.
[^13]: T. Migne prints τετριάδι with [τετράδι] after it; translated "tetrad", with the bracket and the facing Latin (*quaternioni*), since the sensible things are counted as four below.
[^14]: S. Ps 59:3 LXX (Ps 60:1 Heb.).
[^15]: S. Ps 59:4 LXX (Ps 60:2 Heb.).
[^16]: S. Eph 6:17.
[^17]: S. cf. Rom 7:23; 8:2.
[^18]: S. Ps 59:5–7 LXX (Ps 60:3–5 Heb.).
[^19]: T. "Philosophy" here, as in Maximus generally, is the ascetic life; its practical part is the practice of the virtues.
[^20]: T. Migne prints χρίσεως («anointing»), a misprint for κρίσεως: the sense (beside «providence») and the facing Latin (*judicii*) need «judgement».
[^21]: S. cf. Heb 12:10.
[^22]: S. 2 Cor 4:10.
[^23]: S. cf. Rom 8:6–7.
[^24]: T. ὁ Λόγος: the Word speaking in the psalm; the facing Latin renders it *Scriptura*.
[^25]: T. Migne prints F. ἁμαρτιῶν, "perhaps 'of sins'", after τῆς ἁμαρτίας "of sin"; the English follows the main reading.
[^26]: S. Ps 59:7–8 LXX (Ps 60:5–6 Heb.).
[^27]: S. Col 1:26.
[^28]: T. Migne prints a full stop after οἰκονομίαν; the participles that follow (βοῶν, διδούς) depend on «he speaks», so the English runs the sentence on, as the facing Latin does.
[^29]: T. τῷ βίῳ, literally "to life", in the late sense of the world of men; so the facing Latin (*mundo*).
[^30]: P. Sikima: Shechem, the town of Ephraim; Hebrew *šekem* means "shoulder", whence the etymology that follows.
