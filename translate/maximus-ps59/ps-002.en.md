title: Exposition of Psalm 59, 2
---
[865] and temporary after the manner of tents is the present life.

Or again, the tents of the present valley are the modes of the virtues, which he divides with knowledge for those who are taught, giving a training suited to the habit of each, and a guarding[^1] of the precious things, he who knows how to distribute to each, through good teaching, the way that fits him for salvation.

Or the tents of the valley are the Churches of the nations that believed, pitched through Christ our Saviour God throughout the whole inhabited world: which, I think, is also more manifest than the others.

Or again, he measures out the valley of tents who embraces in knowledge the world of bodies as a valley of tents. For in many places of Scripture the body has been called a tent.[^2]

*Galaad is mine, and Manasses is mine, and Ephraim is the strengthening of my head.*[^3]

Galaad,[^4] according to one of the interpretations of this name, is "revelation"; and Manasses is interpreted "forgetfulness". And revelation and forgetfulness fittingly come to the righteous man: to him who receives through contemplation initiation into things ineffable, and forgetfulness of the labours of virtue, because of the gladness of spiritual contemplation that succeeds the labours of the virtues. And Ephraim is interpreted "enlargement": which is the unfalling hope, by faith, in the good things to come; which is the support of the head of the righteous man, that is, of faith. For faith is the head of every righteous man, and of every good work. And its sure strengthening is the unfailing hope of the good things to come, by which, beholding the things to come as present, we are enlarged in tribulations,[^5] abiding unfallen in temptations.

*Judah is my king; Moab is the basin of my hope.*[^6]

Judah is interpreted "confession". For confession made through prayers rules,[^7] in the saints, over every good. And Judah can be understood also of the Lord, who is the true king of the spiritual Israel, the Israel that sees God: he who, when he became man for us, was confessing to the Father and saying, *I confess to thee, holy Father*.[^8] And Moab[^9] is interpreted "bowel of a father", and is understood as our body, through which, in the practical philosophy, after the manner of a bath (which is what the basin plainly signifies), the purification of sins is wont to come about. For the basin of hope is purification: that is, [868] the readiness, through a life according to purification, for the inheritance of the good things laid up for us in hope.

And Moab can again be understood also of the incarnation of the Lord. For the Son of man became for our sake, and was called, the true basin of our hope: that is, the one who washes and purifies our stains and defilements that come from sin.

*Upon Idumea I will stretch out my shoe.*[^10]

Idumea[^11] is interpreted "blood-like clay"; and it can be taken also of the flesh of the Lord, through which, as through a shoe, he set foot among men, since creation could not otherwise contain the Maker, who is by nature infinite and uncontainable.

And it can again be understood also of the flesh of each of us, upon which we stretch out the shoe: that is, the ascesis and self-mastery that guard the soul from the wicked thistles and thorns of the devil; for this is what the shoe must be understood to be. For without ascesis and exact self-mastery it is impossible for the ascetic of piety to wrestle down the passions of the flesh.

And again, the shoe of the soul can be understood also as the sense that is mortified through reason and contemplation; by which the soul, setting foot on sensible things, passes unharmed through this age, gathering up their principles through the medium of sense, as through a shoe (sense forming images of the differences of visible things by form and shape), toward the understanding and full knowledge of the one, sole and sovereign Word.[^12]

*To me the allophyles have been subjected.*[^13]

For when through flesh the Lord dwelt in our regions, there were subjected to *the might of his strength*[^14] not only the spirits of wickedness,[^15] brought to nought, but also all the nations, which, having received the proclamation through the calling of the Gospel, bowed down. And the allophyles[^16] are subjected also to every righteous man when, having put upon the earth of his flesh, as a shoe, the self-mastery that mortifies the law of sin, he shall tame not only the unclean passions that lurk in it, but shall also bring to nought the wicked demons who work them.

*Who will bring me into the fortified city?*[^17]

The fortified city[^18] is the wisdom gathered together from many divine and mystical objects of contemplation, enclosed, that is to say, as another of the translators[^19] has it, fenced about, by the strongholds of the virtues.

Or again, the fortified city is the state in which he who is truly God-fearing and God-loving becomes impregnable and unapproachable to [869] his enemies, because of the encompassing wall of the holy Powers that guards him.

Or again, the fortified city is the soul walled by reason and life, and full of all the graces of the virtues.

Or again, the fortified city is the habit of true and unstumbling knowledge, which is enclosed by all the moral and natural and theological objects of contemplation; into which every pious man is brought, by no other, but by God alone, who draws all to himself through the unspeakable greatness of his goodness.

*Or who will guide me as far as Idumea?*[^20]

Idumea, as our account showed before, is the constitution of all the creatures that are under sense, about which natural contemplation in the Spirit is wont to be constituted; according to which, *from the beauty and greatness of creatures, by analogy, the Author of coming-to-be is beheld*[^21] by him who piously gathers up the knowledge of the things that are.

Or perhaps it is the practical philosophy of virtue through the flesh, by which our body becomes a temple of the Holy Spirit,[^22] built up together with the beauties of the commandments; to which the same Lord again guides, bestowing on those who love him the science of the things that are seen as well, and teaching the mortification of the members of this earthly and perishable flesh.[^23]

Or, to put it in brief, the fortified city is the contemplation, with knowledge, of intelligible things; and Idumea the science of sensible things: into which the sole Wisdom of the God and Father, the only-begotten Son,[^24] both brings and guides. Wherefore the Prophet, continuing, says:

*Is it not thou, O God, who hast cast us off?*[^25]

That is: Thou, O God, who at the beginning didst cast us off because of sin, thou thyself, having had pity through the bowels of thy mercy,[^26] bringest us into the fortified city, and guidest us as far as Idumea, according to the explanation given before.

*And thou wilt not go forth, O God, with our powers.*[^27]

For God will not go forth with him who is confident in himself, and hopes in the bow, and ascribes salvation to his own sword.[^28] But with those who in God himself butt their enemies with the horn, and in his name set at nought those who rise up against them,[^29] God will fittingly go forth to the war against the invisible enemies.

*Give us help out of tribulation, and vain is the salvation of man.*[^30]

In the manner of a prayer the Prophet, in the person of our common nature, begs God and Saviour that help be given us out of the tribulation: plainly, the tribulation which was poured out over nature because of sin and pressed it down as far as [872] the gates of Hades. For tribulation, to speak as I see it, is the tyranny and dominion of death and corruption; and help is the sure hope of the resurrection through grace, looking to which the pious bear the dejection of death without grief.

Or again, tribulation is the labour on behalf of virtue; and help is the passionlessness that succeeds it by the grace of God: compared with which every salvation of men is vanity and utter non-existence. For this is what it is right to understand the vanity to be.

*In God we shall work power, and he will set at nought those who afflict us.*[^31]

They work power in God who do not ascribe to themselves the achievements of the virtues, nor to their own wisdom the comprehension of the divine mysteries: in whom God himself, alone in the highest degree, and entering wholly in,[^32] works every virtue and bestows knowledge, setting at nought all the wicked demons who oppose both virtue and knowledge, and who attempt, through vice and lack of knowledge, to afflict those who fear him. For he is Saviour of all, because he is also Creator of all. To him be the glory unto the ages. Amen.

[^28]: S. cf. Ps 43:7 LXX. T. κατὰ τὴν ῥομφαίαν ἐπιγραφομένῳ ἑαυτοῦ σωτήριον construes awkwardly; rendered with the facing Latin (*gladio suo salutem ascribit*).
