QUESTION 20

Concerning the different wills of God.

*Answer.* One must suppose three wills in the case of God: by good pleasure, by economy, by concession. And the one by good pleasure is shown by what concerns Abraham, when he said to him, *Go forth out of thy land*.[^4] And the one by economy is shown by what was arranged by economy concerning Joseph, toward the outcome of things to come; and the one by concession is shown by what happened concerning Job.

QUESTION 21

What does the Psalmist mean, saying concerning enemies: «*Of those who beset round about*»?[^5]

*Answer.* Round about is the front, behind, the right, the left. The demons, then, set upon us in front when they entice us through the appearances of matter; and behind, when through the preconceptions of the thoughts they stir up the memory of vice;[^6] and on the left, when through fleshly and licentious passions they lever the soul out of place; and on the right, when through vainglory and pride they set upon the soul.

QUESTION 22

What is: «*Take a psalm, and give the timbrel; a pleasant psaltery with the harp*»?[^7]

*Answer.* Take inspired teaching, and give virtuous practice through the mortification of the body. And as for *the pleasant psaltery with the harp*: by psaltery he means the spirit; and by harp, the soul; and by timbrel, the body.

QUESTION 23

What is: «*A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench*»?[^8]

*Answer.* He who in imitation of the Lord [804] makes use of the principle of compassion neither makes the one bruised by sin be broken utterly by despair, nor quenches the one who has his rational power smoking through vainglory over certain virtues, but lets him keep his eagerness, until he come to the perfection of knowledge. For this, as I think, is also the growing up of the tares together with the good seed:[^9] that is, the passion of men-pleasing and vainglory sprouting up together with the virtues. Whence the husbandman of souls orders that these not be plucked out until the virtues take firm hold, lest perhaps someone, wishing to pull up such things, pluck out together with them the eagerness for virtue.

QUESTION 24

What is: «*If anyone strike thee on the right, turn to him the other also*»?[^10]

*Answer.* If, he says, the demons tempt thee through thoughts on the right cheek, lifting thee up through right-hand works, turn the other: that is, bringing before thy sight the works of left-hand action, as many as have been done by us.

QUESTION 25

How ought we to understand piously what is of the Gospel: That «*The Father will judge no one, but has given all judgement to the Son*»? And how does he say in another place, that «*I will judge no one; but the word that I have spoken, that shall judge him*»?[^11]

*Answer.* Inasmuch as he is God, neither the Father nor the Son will judge anyone. For neither does a man become the judge of irrational animals, but of men. But the Father has given judgement to the Son, not inasmuch as the Son is God, but inasmuch as he has become man. And he will judge all, comparing his own way of life as man with ours. And again his word will judge, that is, the teaching which he displayed through his works, according to what is [805] written: «*Which Jesus began both to do and to teach*».[^12]

QUESTION 26

What is the blasphemy of the Spirit; and how is it that «*Every sin shall be forgiven unto men; but to them that have blasphemed against it, it shall not be forgiven, neither in this age, nor in that to come*»?[^13]

*Answer.* The perplexity concerning the blasphemy of the Spirit has its solution from the very passage. For when the Lord was working various healings, the Jews ascribed these activities of the Spirit to the ruler of the demons.[^14] But that there is forgiveness neither here nor in the age to come for those who blaspheme must be sought next. Some of those who have searched out such things with exactness through the Spirit say that there are four modes through which forgiveness of sins comes about: two here, and two in the age to come. For since the memory does not reach to remembering the faults of the whole of time, so that man may repent of them here, the Master of nature, being a lover of man, arranged by economy modes of repentance even while we do not repent: in the age to come, as has been said, two. When someone who has sinned here indiscriminately,[^15] and again has done good indiscriminately, whether moved to pity and compassion toward his neighbour, or whatever else belongs to love for man: these things, weighed in the age to come at the time of judgement, forgiveness comes about for those to which the scale shall have its tilt.[^16] This is the one mode. And the second is this: when someone held in sins, yet hearing the Lord say, *Judge not, that you be not judged*,[^17] judges no one for fear, in the examination of the life he has lived he is not judged, as a keeper of the commandment. For the most truthful is not forgetful of his own commandment. And the other two modes have their forgiveness here: when someone being in sins is dealt with by providence through economy in misfortunes, in necessities, in illnesses. For in a way he does not know, God purifies him through such things; and if he gives thanks when tried, he receives the reward for his thanksgiving. But if he is ungrateful, he is purified of the sins for which he is chastened, but pays the penalty for his ingratitude. Next: the sins which a man commits against men, as the account has shown, have [808] many grounds for forgiveness. For one sinning against a man, but doing good to another man: the nature against which he sinned, this same has also settled the account. But the blasphemy against the Spirit (and it is unbelief) has no other ground of forgiveness than becoming faithful: fittingly, then, for him who has closed his life in unbelief, neither here nor in the age to come will the sin of unbelief and godlessness be forgiven.

QUESTION 27

What does the riddle of the Proverbs show: «*If thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink; for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head*»?[^18]

*Answer.* We have an enemy against the soul: our body, always warring against us through the uprising of the passions. If, then, the way of thinking of the flesh,[^19] pressed by the conscience, hungers, that is, reaches for salvation; or thirsts for divine knowledge, one must feed it through self-control and labours, and give it drink by meditation on the divine oracles. For thus are heaped upon its head, that is, upon the mind, coals of fire: divine and spiritual thoughts.

QUESTION 28

What does what is said in the 101st Psalm signify: «*I am become like a pelican of the wilderness*»?[^20]

*Answer.* This pelican is a bird;[^21] and the serpent is greatly [809] hostile to its young.[^22] And what does it contrive? It fixes its nest on a height, fencing it about on every side because of the serpent. What then does the evil-contriving serpent do? It looks about for where the wind blows from, and from there breathes its venom upon the young, and they die. The pelican, then, comes, and sees that its children have died; and it looks to a cloud, and flies up on high with its wings; and it strikes its sides, and through the cloud drips upon them, and they rise. The pelican, then, is taken for the Lord; and its children, Adam and Eve, our nature; and its nest, paradise; and the serpent, the apostate devil. The serpent, the author of evil, then, breathed upon the first-formed through the disobedience, and they became dead by sin. And so our Lord and God, through his love toward us, is lifted up on the precious cross; and, pierced in the side, through the cloud of the Holy Spirit he has granted us life everlasting.

QUESTION 29

What is: «*There the sparrows shall make their nests*»?[^23]

*Answer.* By sparrows he means either the souls or the different virtues.

QUESTION 30

What does this signify: «*The heron's dwelling leads them*»?[^24]

*Answer.* They say that the heron is a bird, and that it lives in such chastity[^25] [812] that whenever it is about to come together for intercourse, it mourns forty days; and again after that, another forty. And it fixes its nest in the trees, where it is not overshadowed by anything, but has the air clean. And through these things it signifies chastity. For this leads all the virtues; and it stands apart also from sensible things,[^26] overshadowed by none of the things of time. For the number forty contains the perfection of each of the four elements.

QUESTION 31

What is: «*Repaying the sins of parents upon children, unto the third and fourth generation, of them that hate me*»?[^27]

*Answer.* We have supposed the first generation to be the sowing of evil, that is, the assault; and the second, the desire; and the third, the habit of the generation of evil,[^28] that is, the consent; and the fourth, the activity, that is, the action. It is repaid, then, unto the third and fourth generation: that is, for the consent and for the action. For the assault and the desire are not liable, since the evil has not proceeded to its end.

QUESTION 32

How ought the regret of God which lies in Scripture to be understood piously?[^29]

*Answer.* Those who have resolved to think piously of divine things say that it is impossible that God, being by nature Creator, should not also be by nature provider for the things created. And if this is so, it is of all necessity, since God naturally provides for manhood, that the modes of salvation with him concerning the nature provided for are many.
