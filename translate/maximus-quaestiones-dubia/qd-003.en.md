QUESTION 33

What does «*Upon the three or four impieties of Tyre I will not turn away*» signify?[^1]

*Answer.* I think the prophetic word hints at four impieties: of it,[^2] the assault, the desire, the habit of evil, and the activity. In the case of the first and the second, that is, of the assault and the desire, God shows forbearance, since the evil has not advanced to completion. But in the case of the third and the fourth impiety, that is, of habit and activity, or of assent and action, the Word[^3] fittingly displays the threat.

QUESTION 34

Why can «Father of the Spirit» not be said, or «Christ of the Spirit», as, in the case of the Father and of the Son, «Spirit of God» and «Spirit of Christ» are said variously?[^4][^5]

*Answer.* Just as the Mind[^6] is cause of the Word, so also of the Spirit, but through the medium of the Word. And just as we cannot say that the word is of the voice, so neither can we say that the Son is of the Spirit.

QUESTION 35

What is shown by what is said by the Lord: «*If the eye or the hand or the foot cause thee to stumble, cut them off, and cast them from thee*»?[^7]

*Answer.* It is possible, according to the ready sense, to suppose that the parable hints at friends, as at eyes, and at members of our household, as at our hands, they being necessary to us; and at companions,[^8] who serve us after the manner of feet; whom, if they cause stumbling and harm the soul, the word has bidden us cut off. It is possible also to take the precept otherwise, according to the mode of anagogy. If thou hast contemplative knowledge as an eye, and it lifts [816] thee up to arrogance and pride, cut it off. And if thou hast also a refined practice, and it becomes for thee a cause of elation, cut it off. Likewise also, if thou hast competence for service, which is the foot, and art puffed up as from this, stand away from it. For it is profitable for thee to be without these seeming virtues, and not through elation and pride to go to utter perdition.

QUESTION 36

What does «*The sun was lifted up, and the moon stood in her order*» show?[^9]

*Answer.* When in us the sun of righteousness[^10] is exalted through good works and true knowledge, then the moon stands in her order: that is, our nature, which is subject to change and alteration, receives a firm order.

QUESTION 37

What is that written in the catholic epistle of Peter, «*That they might be judged according to men in the flesh, but live according to God in the spirit*»?[^11]

*Answer.* Since these things were said about those in the flood — and these had utter ignorance of God, and lived with wicked practices — such sins as they committed against men he forgave them through the wars and visitations and various mishaps that befell them day by day; but the sins concerning their ignorance of him he did not forgive them, [817] in order that through faith in him, when out of love for man he came to be in the region of Hades,[^12] as many as believed might be forgiven the sin of impiety, and might live according to God in the spirit, having been judged, that is, in the flesh, as has been said, for their offences against men.

QUESTION 38

What is signified by David playing the psaltery and laying to rest the evil spirit of Saul?[^13]

*Answer.* Everyone who, after the manner of the blessed David, shepherds the sheep, that is, the rational power of the soul, and kills anger and desire, as he did the lion and the bear,[^14] this man, using the word of teaching with a certain lofty contemplation, delights the hearer, and lulls to sleep the passions of vice in him.

QUESTION 39

Since Scripture says plainly that Abraham gave tithes to Melchizedek;[^15] and especially, if he gave everything back, as he himself says, that he did not take even so much as the strap of a sandal,[^16] whence did he give tithes? For neither did he himself carry anything of his own to war, setting out in alarm.

*Answer.* Since the divine Scripture says that Melchizedek is a priest of God, Abraham, then, as holding a layman's rank, will fittingly be believed to have given the tithes to the priest. And that he also had whence to provide them is clear from this. For the kings who campaigned against Sodom not only had taken the things of Sodom, but they also had with them spoils of other lands, which they had captured earlier. All these Abraham took, having cut the kings down; and the things of the Sodomites he gave back, but the others he kept by him, from which he also provided the tithes to Melchizedek.

QUESTION 40

For what reason were the priests according to the law not prevented from having wives, while the priests according to Christ are prevented, as far as custom goes?

*Answer.* Since the priesthood of the Gospel is believed to have come about according to the order of Melchizedek, and not according to the order of Aaron;[^17] and Melchizedek is not recorded to have had a wife. Necessarily, then, the bishops who serve as priests according to his order also do not admit wives.

[820] QUESTION 41

For what reason is it the custom of the Church, in the prothesis of the precious body and blood of the Lord, to set out the loaves and the chalices uneven?[^18]

*Answer.* All the things performed in the Church have a principle above nature. And since these symbols are pre-eminently mysteries and images of the divine essence; and this is incomposite, while every creature is composite; and alone, as has been said, the holy Trinity is simple and incomposite: for this reason, then, the Church sets out the loaves and the chalices uneven, characterizing the Divine in them.

QUESTION 42

Since some are perplexed how the becoming man of the Lord is said to have escaped the notice of all the heavenly powers, when we find both that the prophecies concerning the Lord came about through angels, and that Gabriel announces the conception of the Virgin, and that angels initiate the shepherds into the mystery?[^19]

*Answer.* That the angels knew the becoming man of the Lord that was to be for the salvation of men, one must not doubt. But this escaped them: the incomprehensible conception of the Lord, and its mode; how, being whole in the Father, and being whole in all things and filling all things, he was whole in the womb of the Virgin.

QUESTION 43

What do Gerizim and Bal signify?[^20]

*Answer.* Gerizim is interpreted «circumcision»; and Bal, «lump». In the lump, then, of the gentile portion that has been cleansed,[^21] our Lord Jesus Christ fixes an altar.

QUESTION 44

What are Chauones, Cana, Galilee, Doeg and Hermon?[^22]

*Answer.* Chauones are interpreted «windows». Cana is [821] interpreted «possession»; and Galilee, «revelation».[^23] Doeg the Syrian is the proud thought. Hermon is interpreted «turning-away of beasts». And they say that the river Jordan is born from there. And through these it signifies the grace of holy baptism.

QUESTION 45

What is that said in the Psalms, «*Be at leisure, and know that I am God*»?[^24]

*Answer.* There are six leisures, by achieving which we can come to know God. The first, that from the activity of sin; the second, that from an inflaming diet; the third, that from the pattern of mingling with those who live unguardedly; the fourth, that from a pursuit unprofitable for the life according to God; the fifth, that from possessions[^25] which are ill-reputed and distract the mind into many things; the sixth, to have no will of one's own at all. And this is both the renunciation and the submission that is true and according to God.

QUESTION 46

In the person of whom is Jephthah taken, and his daughter?[^26]

*Answer.* Jephthah, in the person of the Lord; and his daughter, in his all-immaculate flesh. For just as Jephthah was born of a harlot, and was driven out by his own, and having gone out made war and conquered the adversaries, having promised God to bring as a sacrifice whoever of his household should meet him; so also the Lord, having come forth sinlessly as to the flesh from our nature which had played the harlot, and having become the sower of his own flesh,[^27] and having been driven out by those who seemed to be Jews, and having won the war on our behalf, offered his own flesh to God and Father. For Jephthah is interpreted «opening of God».

QUESTION 47

How shall we understand that of the Gospel, «*A greater than John has not arisen among those born; but the [824] lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he*»?[^28]

*Answer.* Whoever humbles himself beyond John (for this is the «lesser»), that one is greater than John. Or also otherwise: since John is believed to have grasped the knowledge attainable here through contemplation, the small and last knowledge in the state that is to appear hereafter is greater than that here. Or also: the highest theologian among men is lesser than the last among angels. Or also: he who holds the last rank in the angelic[^29] way of life is greater than the highest in the justice of the law.

QUESTION 48

What is that of Proverbs, «*He who has thrust hands into hand shall not be held guiltless*»?[^30]

*Answer.* He who mingles base action with virtuous action shall not be held guiltless.

QUESTION 49

What is «*Seven times shall the righteous man fall, and shall rise again*»?[^31]

*Answer.* By the righteous man here one must understand the only truly righteous one, our Lord Jesus Christ. Since, then, he himself is said both to fall and to rise in us, as having accepted all our things out of love for man; and our nature fell seven times: by the transgression of the forefather, which came about as the first; the second, the murder dared by Cain, who first innovated murder; the third, in the generation of Noah, upon which the Spirit of God did not remain, because they were flesh;[^32] the fourth, at the building of the tower; the fifth, in the generation of Abraham, out of which he alone was well-pleasing to God; the sixth, under Moses, the godlessness of the generation in his time having run aground to such a point that he was sent from God for help against so great an impiety; the seventh, in the generation of the prophets, which overshot the generations before it in the measure of vice. Since, then, as has been said, our nature fell seven times, the Lord, overcome by ineffable love for man, raised it up, having united that very nature to himself according to hypostasis.
