QUESTION 51

What is «*It were better for him that an ass's millstone were put about his neck, and that he were cast into the sea, than that he should cause one of the little ones to stumble*»?[^1]

*Answer.* «Little ones», I think, he calls those who are simple in understanding, and who through smallness of mind are unable to discern the judgements of providence. Whoever causes such to stumble, it were better for him to be of the Hellenic lot,[^2] who after the manner of an ass in a mill are held fast by the motion of the world alone; and that he be cast into the open sea; that is, into the confusion of life. And this the Apostle Peter also confirms, saying: *It were better not to have come to know the way of righteousness, than, having come to know it, to turn back.*[^3]

QUESTION 52

As what are Cain and Abel taken?

*Answer.* Cain is taken for the way of thinking of the flesh;[^4] and Abel for mourning, that is, repentance.

QUESTION 53

What is «*Call no man blessed before his end*»?[^5]

*Answer.* Both according to the ready sense, for the mind of the many, because of the uncertainty and the slipperiness of human choice, one must not call anyone blessed until, having passed through every virtue, he closes his life with an end beyond gainsaying. But according to the loftier sense, he who has begun, through repentance and ascesis, to humble and make weak the earthly way of thinking that lives in him is not yet to be called blessed, until through the strenuous toil of ascesis it is put to death and receives completion. For such a one is blessed, as having died with Christ through inactivity in evils, and rising again with him through the height of the virtues.[^6] For this the Psalmist also signifies, saying: *Blessed are the blameless*, the pure, that is, from vice; *who walk in the law of the Lord*,[^7] who journey through good actions.

QUESTION 54

What does «*If they persecute you out of this city,*[^8] *flee into the other*» show?[^9]

[828] *Answer.* Cities are for the guarding and safety and keeping of precious things. A city, then, according to the mode of allegory, is the ascesis conceived from various modes: such as self-control as to wine, abstention from foods, intense vigil, and suchlike, which are a guard and safety. If, then, the demons persecute us from one of these, bringing arrogance or vainglory through such a form of ascesis, it is better to withdraw from such seemingly more exact conduct, lest we fall into pride, and to flee into another virtue free of vainglory, until the principle of passionlessness comes. Or also otherwise: he calls the cities human souls. For the apostles, sent to these by the Saviour, being driven away by those who judge themselves unworthy, move into the souls of others, which have become receptive of their teaching; and such cities of Israel they shall not finish, truly sojourning in them, and here being received in, and there being persecuted by them, until the Son of man comes in his glorious coming, filling all things.

QUESTION 55

How also is «*Mercy by weights*»,[^10] according to the holy Isaiah? For nothing good is without judgement, even if it seemed so in the case of those in the vineyard.[^11]

*Answer.* If the mercy of God is weighed, is it then fittingly also circumscribed?[^12] Rather, then, one must suppose this: that just as we have a power of sight and of hearing and of breathing, receiving not the whole air, or the light, or the voice — since then participation in these would not have been left over for another — but each participates according to the power present to him proportionately; so also the mercy of God gives both the remission and the grace according to the quality of the dispositions of each of the subjects. For example, someone repented perfectly: he is also forgiven perfectly; and likewise in the case of the one who loves.

QUESTION 56

What is that said in Esdras, «*Make them afraid in the law of the Lord*»?[^13]

*Answer.* «Make afraid» is said instead of «Strike them with dread»: not from the good things promised, but from the fearful things threatened. For this is servile, and fitting for Jews.

[829] QUESTION 57

What is «*A weapon in nightly fears, a rest from daily toils*»?[^14]

*Answer.* Night he called the hidden, deceitful assaults of the enemy; and days, the acknowledged temptations that are in the open. He, then, who has received the habit of divine knowledge, and is ignorant of none of the enemy's concepts,[^15] fears none of the hidden assaults of the enemy. For fear is nothing other than an expected evil. But indeed he also reckons as rest the toils on behalf of the acknowledged temptations, because of the proving that comes from grappling with them, and the blessed expectation, hoped for from it, of the crown of incorruption.

QUESTION 58[^16]

What is that said in the seventy-fifth psalm, «*The pondering of man shall confess to thee, and the remnant of pondering shall keep feast to thee*»?[^17]

*Answer.* The feast belongs to those who rejoice; the confession, to those under examination. And the one is of grief, the other of joy. This, then, it says: that in the judgement, when the passing and imperfect pondering [832] of the good is counterweighed with the abiding and completed pondering, this has the greater part;[^18] for the one is examined, while the other becomes the procurer of feast and joy.

QUESTION 59

What is the etymology of *gastrimargia*?[^19]

*Answer.* None either of the grammarians or of the orators has mentioned it. But Aristotle, in his *On Animals*,[^20] mentions an animal called *margos*, which is born from putrefaction, between the earth and the water. And from the time it is born it does not cease eating earth, until, having bored through the earth, it comes to the surface; and having come, it dies for three days; and after three days a rain cloud comes and rains upon it, and it comes back to life, no longer an ever-eater. And from this, I think, the ancient philosophers took their start in calling the voracious *gastrimargoi*. But one who knows how to apply himself piously to the things that are can also take what has been said according to spiritual contemplation. For every passion is of a nature to be born from putrefaction; and once it is born, it does not cease eating the heart that gave it subsistence, until through the habit of knowledge it comes to manifestation. And having come, it dies to the three powers of the soul. And thus the grace of the Spirit, appearing as through a cloud of teaching, gives drops of knowledge and makes alive: not according to the former passionate life, but according to the virtuous one, akin to God.

QUESTION 60

What is the parable in the Gospel of the workers hired into the vineyard? And what is the inequality?[^21]

*Answer.* The judgement of God does not come about according to time and body, because the soul too is timeless and bodiless, in that it does not receive its growth and its end according to time, but makes the motions of its disposition timelessly. It is good to make clear by an example what is said. Often there is someone who has seventy years[^22] in the solitary life; and another, one day. And since the aim of the profession is that, having drawn the soul up from passionate attachment and relation to material things, one should transfer it wholly to God: then the one [833] having seventy years, not having taken any care at all about such passionlessness, died; while the one having one day, having drawn up all his passionate understanding toward material things and having enfolded it wholly in God, was perfected. In the distribution, then, that comes about at the judgement, the one receives the wage worthily, as having brought the aim of the profession to its end; the other by grace, and only because he endured the toil of ascesis.

QUESTION 61

For what reason did the Spirit come down ten days after the taking-up of the Lord?[^23]

*Answer.* Some of those instructed in divine things say that, since according to Dionysius the Areopagite[^24] the angelic powers are nine orders, the Lord, ascending according to the human (for according to the divine he fills all things), allotted one day to each rank, from the lowest to the last.[^25] For these too needed the Lord's sojourn among them. For in him, according to the Apostle, were summed up not only the things on the earth but also those in heaven;[^26] and after this he appeared before God and Father;[^27] and so the Spirit comes down. It is possible also to contemplate the matter set forth according to another mode of contemplation. For since the Word of God is hidden in his ten commandments, and is embodied in us, coming down with us through practice, and again leads us up through knowledge, raising us until we ascend to the highest of all the commandments, the one that says, *The Lord thy God, the Lord is one*:[^28] when, then, having been released from all things, or rather having let go of all things, our mind comes to rest in God himself, then it receives the fiery tongues,[^29] becoming god by grace.

QUESTION 62

What does «*Bring thy foot seldom to thy friend, lest, being filled with thee, he hate thee*» show?[^30]

*Answer.* Friend here he calls our body, because of the natural union with it and the affection toward it. The Word,[^31] then, advises us not to make our care[^32] for the body burdensome, but to make our care for it only by the footstep of the soul; and this too seldom, lest, while thou makest much of its rest, it show in thee the ways of enemies and haters.
