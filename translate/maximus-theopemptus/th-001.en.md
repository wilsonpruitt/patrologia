title: To Theopemptus the Scholasticus
---
[1393] *Of Maximus the monk.*

TO THEOPEMPTUS THE SCHOLASTICUS[^1]

*He had asked about the judge of unrighteousness; and about «If anyone strikes thee on the right cheek»; and about «Touch me not, for I have not yet ascended to the Father».*[^2]

To those for whom the Word is what is worshipped, for these also a life that is the revealer of the word is what is accomplished;[^3] so that, having received the experience of practice as the seal of true knowledge, they may learn from what they have undergone, as is right, how much greater than knowing something is becoming it; and, more than this, becoming it ever more and more, until the undoubting kinship toward the Cause be accomplished for them, circumscribing motion by rest and desire by unchangeability of inclination; to whom also the deification that comes after grace[^4] is given in return as the prize of the holy and godlike conduct of this present life.

Toward these, directing thy life most wisely thyself, God-guarded master, thou art pressing on to seek that which through goodness is set before all, and through infinity escapes all: the first, that it may draw them to itself, goading desire by the overwhelmingness of its loveliness; the second, that it may temper them, restraining satiety, which is the father of insolence and the offspring of dissipation, by the astonishment at its more-than-infinite and divine pre-eminence, for those who do not foresee the flaming sword set with the Cherubim and turning,[^5] for none other than those who turn to virtue without fear; whence, extending thy moderation along with the greatness of thy advance in good things, by which thou also increasest thy progress all the more with safety, thou hast thought it worthy to come to know me, who am unknown and lowly, through that mode which nature afforded to those of her kind — I mean, the letter: which is a word that gathers together the absent and makes manifest those who are not seen, delivering to intellection and joining to it one who till now is not known to sense, insofar as the word itself, inscribed through sense, is thickened, being embodied; by which also thou hast led my nothingness into longing for you the more, in which thou hast incorporeally stirred the sight of thyself;[^6] having shown thyself[^7] in both these respects to hold the equal concerning the word, rather than as much as others, concerning thee, hold the grace of the word as advantages over the rest; [1396] adorned with which through life, how thou hast been eager ever to lead my dim-sightedness to contemplation, and that of holy words, and of words whose ascent is beyond what befits man for one who has not yet perfectly surmounted the nature and coming-to-be of unstable and extended things; let alone for me, who am willingly commixed with the refuse of these, and like a worm roll and take pleasure in the rot of unclean passions. Nevertheless, for the sake of obedience, and as honour to your love, venturing upon things beyond my worth, I say those things concerning which you gave command: by way of conjecture, and not of assertion.

The judge of unrighteousness[^8] in the Gospel is, by way of parable, and is called,[^9] our reasoning, which from the beginning, by transgression, has made its judgement unrighteous; as having not received the commandment of the Creator, but chosen the counsel of the corrupter; from which it also far preferred being earthy to being, and to becoming divine. And its city is sense, or the visible world; resting on which irrationally by relation, it exchanged its sojourning for settled dwelling; neither fearing God, nor yet regarding man,[^10] the same who became man for our sake: the one through folly, the other through contempt; the former not foreseeing the greatness of the judgements, and the latter not keeping itself from the torment which kindness, awaiting the conversion, for the present holds back; with which it wilfully pierced itself through,[^11] having misused, contrary to what was due, the self-determining honour given by the Creator for the fulfilment of what is fitting, and having judged the good that appeared more choiceworthy than the good that is understood, which was not lawful.

And the widow who troubles him[^12] is the rational soul, inasmuch as, for the bearing of fruit of holy and pious works, she does not have the reason that sows good things according to nature, reason held only by this whole affection and disposition toward her, but not abiding with her because of her turning-away and her self-chosen leaning toward the things here.[^13]

And her adversary[^14] — of the soul, I mean — is the material movement and way of thinking of the wretched flesh,[^15] through which the unclean demons are eager to defile the beauty, and to darken the brightness, of the divine image in us and of the dignity that accords with it, whenever, as we fall under them through pleasure, they dreadfully insinuate into us the shames and passions of dishonour;[^16] on account of which death too was sentenced against nature, since she did not choose from the beginning to luxuriate in the life that truly is.

And the bruisings[^17] are the exhortatory scourgings from the conscience, with a certain murmuring, by which, as they are put forth from the soul, the harshness of the reasoning, unyielding toward good things through a more long-standing habit, is wont somehow gently to be bent, since it does not [1397] bear the torment that lurks within and, as it were, scourges it continually and troubles it; until it ravage the passions that ravage the soul, through self-mastery and the voluntary mortification of the members of the flesh.[^18] Then, too, until it lead the soul to near-kinship with reason itself,[^19] through piety and the more perfect zeal for higher things; to which, enacted according to nature, comparing what is his own and his Father's, the God above essence and nature, our Lord Jesus Christ, says: *And shall God not make the vindication of you*,[^20] through me who was incarnate and who works the destruction of the destroyer of nature?

Thus, then, I have gone through the things about this in brief, so far as the available measure of my understanding sufficed; even if others, having understood the passage otherwise, have taken it, some of Christ, others contrariwise of the Antichrist. But I fear to think or to say either: the first, as admitting no comparison; for the vindication is not less of the Son and greater of the Father, or through another, and wholly another, besides the Son.[^21] And how, of the same vindication, does it say by way of comparison: *And shall God not*, it says, *make the vindication of you?* For it has no place: or else it will be shown altogether different, and with a nature that cannot do the like, and therefore, after all, not ranked with it, but only imaging, as lying above it, the one that excels. The second, as inadmissible and impossible: that the inventor and teacher of vice should do what is good; for *a rotten tree*, it says, *cannot make good fruits*;[^22] and vindication is a good, and duly to rid oneself of the importunity of the one who draws near; or else that the Father perhaps, which is not lawful, should be compared to this one, the provider of all virtue and life, from a Son, and that an Only-begotten, equal in nature and in goodness. For good things are not of a nature to be signified through evil things, as neither light through darkness, nor life through death, and what is through what in no way is. And I do not say these things to set those men aside; for I hold to, and cling to, more than to life, the teaching of those who said them; but I secure my own measure out of extreme timidity. But to rule the reading of ὑποπιάζειν[^23] is not fitting for me, because of my ignorance in these matters, but rather for those who are trained and have chosen learning with zeal; who say that it ought to be ruled as barytone rather than as perispomenon, because, they say, no verb is circumflexed unless it has arisen from the contraction of a barytone, as νοῶ, and ποιῶ, and χρυσῶ, and as many as are such, which keep the accent on the same syllable even with a preposition.

The right cheek: I call it right action,[^24] and all that the godly life, established in us through the commandments, characterizes, struck by the evil one [1400] with the presumption of the goodness being performed, and with the blow of the conceit that comes from it, somehow inflamed through vainglory and lifted up against those who have none, or perhaps do not possess the equal;[^25] on account of which it is fitting to turn, as another and as it were the left, the life which already before defiled us according to the deceit of this age, and to be struck by it continually through memory; and to draw in and be moderate as sinners, rather than to be lifted high as saints and through exaltation to fall, not having humility as a holy fixity. And let these things also, performed bodily, be honoured along with the things taken according to contemplation — or rather even before these, for holy preparation and pre-purification — for the sake of the Lord who commanded them, who thence bestows a habit of meekness on those who accomplish them, and a circumscription of the faithlessness of the one who strikes, checking the eagerness of the blow by the readiness of the reception, the striker perhaps feeling shame at, if nothing else, the excess of the submission, and that one who is able to do this does not resist at all, but is restrained because of the command.

And the saying *Touch me not*[^26] was spoken by the Lord to Mary Magdalene when she supposed him to be the gardener;[^27] the mystery showing and hinting that every noetic soul which has not yet surmounted the tabernacle grown together with it,[^28] and which therefore supposes the Word to be creator only of the things in coming-to-be and corruption, which differ in no way at all from garden vegetables — for equal in both are the flow and the outflow, and the circumscription and period of time — because of his going forth about these things and as far as these, and his condescension, has not become worthy of touching him according to the spirit, and that while seeking him among the things that appear, and knowing him only as having become flesh for our sake, but not fittingly knowing him also as God begotten of God the Father. For this, in all likelihood, is what *I have not yet ascended to my Father* suggests to be understood: that he has not been exalted, through utmost theology and faith, by which the nature of visible things is circumscribed, by the soul so disposed; but that she still needs, as under a tutor, for a more complete apprehension of him, to run up through contemplation to the revealing habit of divine things as to a kind of Galilee;[^29] and that he be comprehended whole, as is right, together with the principles prefigured and put forth by him; and to become as much that which he was, he who became that which we are, as he was shown to have become this, save sin alone,[^30] as we too to him, apart from nature alone, when we should attain the gift;[^31] which, receiving it now through hope, and duly adorning it with the fitting works of the commandments, mayest thou enjoy as much as thou hast believed, and delight in as much as thou hast laboured; or rather more, and infinitely many times more, insofar as also its Giver superessentially transcends both the motion of nature and the compass of every age.

## Notes
[^1]: P. Theopemptus: a scholasticus (a lawyer), otherwise unknown; the addressee throughout, «God-guarded master».
[^2]: S. Luke 18:6; Matt 5:39; John 20:17.
[^3]: T. The opening plays on Λόγος, the Word worshipped, and λόγος, the word that a life makes manifest; the facing Latin doubles it, *Verbum ac Sermo*.
[^4]: T. μετὰ χάριν (with the accusative, "after grace"); the facing Latin has *quae gratiae comes est*, as if μετὰ χάριτος. The printed form is kept.
[^5]: S. cf. Gen 3:24.
[^6]: T. τὸν ὑμέτερον here, and ὑμετέρας, ἐκελεύσατε below, are plurals of respect to the one addressee; by the number rule they stand as *you/your*, while the singulars stand as *thou/thee*.
[^7]: T. Read αὑτὸν (reflexive, "thyself") for the printed αὐτὸν, with the facing Latin *te*; the construal of the comparison that follows («than as much as others …») is uncertain. Migne marks the place with an asterisk after προτερήματα and the note «Locus obscurus, ac forte mendosus» ("an obscure passage, and perhaps corrupt").
[^8]: S. Luke 18:6 (ὁ κριτὴς τῆς ἀδικίας).
[^9]: T. The three answers are set as accusative and infinitive, depending on «I say those things» above; rendered as plain statements.
[^10]: S. cf. Luke 18:2, 4.
[^11]: S. cf. 1 Tim 6:10.
[^12]: S. cf. Luke 18:3, 5.
[^13]: T. λόγος here "reason" (Latin *ratio*). Migne prints a full stop before Μόνῃ, but the participles that follow (κρατούμενον, προσμένοντα) agree with λόγον; the clause is joined to its sentence.
[^14]: S. cf. Luke 18:3.
[^15]: S. cf. Rom 8:6–7 (τὸ φρόνημα τῆς σαρκός).
[^16]: S. cf. Rom 1:26 (πάθη ἀτιμίας).
[^17]: S. cf. Luke 18:5 (ἵνα μὴ … ὑπωπιάζῃ με).
[^18]: S. cf. Col 3:5.
[^19]: T. αὐτοῦ τοῦ λόγου: "reason itself", as in the widow's «reason that sows», with the facing Latin *rationis ipsius*; the Word cannot be excluded. ἀγχιστεία "near-kinship", kept apart from οἰκείωσις "kinship".
[^20]: S. Luke 18:7, adapted: the Gospel has «of his elect» (τῶν ἐκλεκτῶν αὐτοῦ) where Maximus has ὑμῶν, «of you».
[^21]: T. The facing Latin ends the clause «quam Filii, aut per Filium», with an alternative («or through the Son») the Greek does not print; a phrase may have dropped. Migne prints no such phrase (checked on the plate: the line ends «πλὴν τοῦ Υἱοῦ.»).
[^22]: S. Matt 7:18.
[^23]: T. The scaffold's ὑποπί-ζειν is read ὑποπιάζειν; Luke 18:5 has ὑπωπιάζῃ, and the facing Latin prints ὑπωπιάζειν. The question is whether the verb is accented as a barytone or circumflexed as a contract.
[^24]: S. cf. Matt 5:39.
[^25]: T. Migne prints ἃ μὴ, a misprint for ἢ μὴ («or … not»; checked on the plate; the facing Latin *aut*).
[^26]: S. John 20:17.
[^27]: S. cf. John 20:15.
[^28]: S. cf. 2 Cor 5:1, 4 (σκῆνος, the body).
[^29]: S. cf. Matt 28:7, 10; Mark 16:7.
[^30]: S. cf. Heb 4:15.
[^31]: T. ὡς καὶ ἡμεῖς ἐκείνῳ: the dative is kept («to him»), as the facing Latin *cum illo*; ἐκεῖνο, «that», would square with «become that which he was». In τῆς δωρεᾶς τῆς ἐπιτύχοιμεν the second τῆς does not construe (perhaps ταύτης, a conjecture); Migne prints τῆς (checked on the plate); left out of the English.
