title: The Spiritual Meadow, chapters 51–64
---
[2905] CHAPTER 51

Anazarbus is the metropolis of Second Cilicia. In its territory, about twelve miles off, there is a laura called the laura of the Egyptians.[^1] So the fathers of that place told us that five years before, an elder named Julian had fallen asleep there. They bore witness to this, saying that he spent about seventy years [2908] in one small cave, having acquired nothing of this world except a hair shirt, a cloak, a mantle [?],[^2] and a wooden cup. And they said this too about him: that the whole time of his life he never lit the light of a lamp, but in the nights a light from heaven shone on him, showing him the letters as he read.

CHAPTER 52

A brother visited abba Elias the solitary at the coenobium of the Cave of abba Sabas,[^3] and said to him, "Abba, tell me a word." The elder said to the brother: "In the days of our fathers three virtues were loved: poverty, meekness and self-control. But now among the monks greed holds sway, and gluttony, and insolence. Take hold of whichever thou wilt."

CHAPTER 53

Abba Stephen Trichinas told us about an elder who lived in the laura of our father among the saints Sabas, that one day he went down to Kothylas, and after spending a little while by the Dead Sea he was coming back up again to his cell. The heat was very fierce, and the elder was about to faint; he stretched out his hands to heaven, to God, and prayed, saying, "Lord, thou knowest that I cannot walk for thirst." And at once a cloud came about him, and did not leave him until he was back in his cell. The distance was twelve miles.

The same Stephen told us this too about the same elder: that one day his own kin came, wanting to see him. So when they came to the laura they asked for his cell; and when some people had shown it to them, they went and knocked at the door. The elder knew who they were, and prayed to God that he might not be seen by them; and opening the door he went out of his cell without being seen by them. And he went off into the desert and did not go back into his cell until they had gone away.

CHAPTER 54

We visited abba Theodore the Alexandrian at Terenuthis, and the elder said to us: "Truly, child, the monks have lost Scetis, as the elders foretold. For believe me, children, an old man: there was great love among the men of Scetis, and ascesis, and discernment. And I came in time to find elders there [2909] [b: A] who, if no one visited them, took no food at all. Among them was an elder named Ammonius who lived near me; and knowing that he kept this practice, I used to visit him every Sabbath, so that for my sake he would take food. For they all had this custom: at whatever hour anyone visited them, they had him make a prayer, and when the visitors had made the prayer, they themselves set the table, and at once took food."

CHAPTER 55

Abba Irenaeus told us that an elder living in Scetis saw the devil by night handing out hoes to the brethren. And the elder said to the devil, "What are these things?" The devil answered, "I am giving the brethren distraction, and so making them more careless about glorifying God."

The same abba Irenaeus told us again, saying: "When the barbarians came to Scetis, I withdrew, and came to the region of Gaza, and took a cell for myself in a laura. And from the abba of the laura I got a book of the elders,[^4] and that same day I took it up to read. And as soon as I opened it I found this chapter: that a brother visited an elder, and the brother said, 'Pray for me, Father.' And the elder said, 'When thou wast with us, I prayed for thee; but now thou hast gone off to thine own, and I no longer pray for thee.' So when I read this, I shut the book and said to myself, 'Woe to thee, Irenaeus, for thou hast gone off to thine own, and the fathers no longer pray for thee.' And at once I gave the book back to the abba, withdrew, and came to the cells. This, children, was the reason for my coming to this place [?]."[^5]

CHAPTER 56

Ptolemais is a city of Phoenicia. In its territory there is a village called Parasema. In it a great elder lived. He had a disciple named John, himself great too, and of great obedience. So one day the elder sent his disciple on an errand, giving him a few loaves as well, for his food. The disciple went off and did the errand and came back, bringing the loaves back whole. When the elder saw the loaves he said to him, "Why didst thou not eat of the loaves I gave thee, child?" He made a prostration and said to the elder, "Forgive me, Father: since thou didst not bless me when thou sentest me, nor bid me eat, that is why I did not eat." And the elder marvelled at the brother's discernment and blessed him.

To this brother, after the elder's falling asleep, when he had fasted forty days, a vision came from God, saying, "Whatever ailment thou layest thy hand upon shall be healed." When morning came, by God's dispensation, behold, a man came bringing his own wife, who had a cancer in her breast. So the man begged him to heal his wife. But he said he was a sinner, [2912] [b: A] and unworthy of such an undertaking. The woman's husband kept on beseeching him to consent and have mercy on his wife. So he laid his hand on her and sealed the ailment with the sign of the cross, and at once she was healed. And from then on God worked many signs through him, not only in his life but after his death as well.

CHAPTER 57

Four [?] miles from the city of Aegae there stood a stylite named Symeon.[^6] He was struck down by lightning and died. Now abba Julian the Stylite, the one on the Gulf,[^7] outside the usual time, said to his disciples, "Put on incense." They said to him, "Tell us the reason, Father." [b: B] He said to them, "Because brother Symeon in Aegae has been struck down by lightning and has died, and behold, his soul is departing with rejoicing." They were twenty-four miles apart.

CHAPTER 58

Abba Stephen Trichinas told us this too about abba Julian the Stylite: that a lion appeared around the place, and was killing many strangers and natives. So one day the elder called his disciple, Pancratius by name, and said to him, "Go about two miles towards the south, and thou wilt find a lion lying there; and say to him, 'The lowly Julian says: In the name of Jesus Christ, the Son of the living God, withdraw from this country.'" The brother went off and found the lion lying there. And when the disciple had spoken the elder's message, at once, on the spot, the lion withdrew; and all glorified God.

CHAPTER 59

Abba Peter, the priest of the same laura,[^8] told us about abba Thalelaeus the Cilician that he spent sixty years in the monastic life, never ceasing to weep; and he used always to say, "God has given this time for repentance, and we shall have to seek it sorely."

CHAPTER 60

A Christ-loving man told us, when we were in Alexandria, something like this. "A nun," he said, "was living in her own house, keeping stillness and caring for her own soul, continuing in fastings and prayers and vigils, and giving much alms. But the devil, who always wars on the race of men, could not bear such virtues in the virgin, and stirred up dust against her. For he cast into a young man a satanic desire for her. The young man stayed outside the house. So whenever the nun wanted to go out and leave her house for the house of prayer, [2913] [b: A] to pray, the young man would not let her, pestering her and pressing her, as is the way of lovers; so that at last the nun was forced, by the young man's pestering, not to go out of her house at all. So one day the nun sent her maid to the young man, saying, 'Come; my mistress wants thee.' The young man went off to her rejoicing, as one who had gained his aim. The nun was sitting at her loom. So she said to the young man, 'Sit down.' And when she had seated him, she said to him, 'Truly, sir brother, why dost thou afflict me so, and not let me go out of my house?' The young man answered, saying, 'Truly, lady, I desire thee very much; whenever I see thee, I become all fire.' She said to him, 'Why, what hast thou seen in me that is fair, that thou lovest me so?' [b: B] The young man said, 'Thine eyes; for it is they that have deceived me.' When the nun heard that her eyes had deceived the young man, she took hold of her shuttle, struck, and put out her two eyes. And when he saw that the nun had put out her two eyes because of him, he was pierced with compunction and went off to Scetis, and he too became an approved monk."

CHAPTER 61

Some of the fathers used to say of abba Leontius the Cilician that he was zealous in the service of our Lady the holy Theotokos Mary of the New Church;[^9] and he spent about forty-five years without going out of the church. He was always recollected, attending to himself.

They told us this too about him, [b: C] that when he saw a poor man coming to him, if he was blind, he gave to him from his hand; but if he could see, he would lay the obols before him, either on the base of a column, or on a bench, or on the steps of the sanctuary, and from there the poor man would take them. When an elder asked him, "Why, Father, dost thou not give from thy hand?" he answered, saying, "Forgive me, Father: it is not I that give, but my Lady the Theotokos, who feeds both me and them."

CHAPTER 62

One of the elders told us about abba Stephen, the priest of the laura of the Aeliotes, that as he sat in his cell the demon cast thoughts into him, saying, "Withdraw; [2916] thou art not profited here." But the elder said to the demon, "I do not listen to thee. I know thee, who thou art. Thou dost not want to see anyone saved; but Christ the Son of the living God, he himself will crush thee."

CHAPTER 63

They used to say of the same man that, as he was sitting in his cell and reading, the demon came again, visible to the eye, saying to him, "Withdraw from here, old man; thou art not profited." Then he said to the demon, "If I am to know that thou wantest me to withdraw, make the thing I am sitting on walk." He was sitting on a wicker chair. The demon heard it, and at once made not only the chair walk but the whole cell. But the elder, seeing the demon's craft, said to him, "Truly, since thou art nimble as well, I will not withdraw." And when he had made a prayer, the alien demon vanished.

CHAPTER 64

Three of the elders visited the same abba Stephen the priest; and while they went on talking about the profit of the soul, he kept silent. The elders said to him, "Thou answerest us nothing, Father; we came to thee for profit." Then he said to them, "Forgive me; until now I do not know what you have been saying. But what I have, I tell you: night and day I see nothing else but our Lord Jesus Christ hanging on the tree." And they were profited, and went away.

## Notes
[^1]: T. Chapter number 51, and 52–53 after it, from the sequence: no Latin CAPUT head survives before LV, and the Greek prints only `σΑΡΥΤ i.` here. LI–LIII fit the heads that do survive (LIV, LV, LVI) if the two Stephen Trichinas stories form one chapter, as the two Irenaeus stories do under LV. σημεῖον as a distance is rendered "mile".
[^2]: T. μεγαλείου is not a garment word I can place; the Latin omits it (and adds *et librum*, which the Greek lacks). "Mantle" is a guess.
[^3]: P. The Cave (Spelaion), a coenobium founded by Sabas near the Great Laura.
[^4]: T. βιβλίον γεροντικόν, a book of the sayings of the elders (a Gerontikon); the next sentence quotes one of its chapters.
[^5]: T. ἐν τόπῳ reads short; the Latin *in hunc locum* suggests ἐν τῷ τόπῳ τούτῳ, which the English follows.
[^6]: T. Greek τεσσάρων "four"; the Latin has *quadraginta* "forty". The number could have been misread on either side, so the English keeps the Greek and marks it.
[^7]: P. Julian the Stylite "of the Gulf": the Gulf of Issus, near Aegae in Cilicia.
[^8]: P. "The same laura": the last laura the Greek names is Sabas's (ch. 53); the Latin makes Stephen Trichinas superior of Sabas's monastery.
[^9]: P. The Nea, Justinian's church of the Theotokos in Jerusalem.
