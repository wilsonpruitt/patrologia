title: The Spiritual Meadow, chapters 80–89
---
[2937] CHAPTER 80

We visited the monastery of abba Theodosius at the Scopelos.[^1] It is a mountain between Seleucia on the mainland and Rhossus in Cilicia. And the fathers of that monastery took us up above the monastery, about a bowshot, and showed us, saying, "This spring, which is very fine and large, we have from God." And they say, "It is not by nature: it is given us by God. For our father among the saints, the great Theodosius, fasted much, and shed tears with many genuflexions, that God might grant us the enjoyment of this water; for at first our fathers filled their jars from the torrent. But God, who always does the will of those who fear him,[^2] granted us the blessing of the water through the prayers of our father. So two years ago some of the brethren asked the superior to build a bath-house in the monastery. And our abba was unhappy about it; yet he gave way, condescending to the weakness of the brethren. And so the bath-house is built, and it was bathed in once only; and at once that great God-given spring, such as it was, dried up. And in truth we tell you, we fasted much, and made many supplications with many tears; and the water of the spring did not come out. So it went a year without coming out,

[2940] [b: A] and we were in great affliction. Then, as soon as our father pulled down the bath-house, at once God gave us the water again."

CHAPTER 81

The same fathers told us this too, saying: "In these days a Christ-loving woman in the parts of Apamea dug a well. And she spent a great deal, and went down a great depth, and found no water; and she was in great despondency, both for the toil and for the expense. So one day the woman sees someone saying to her, 'Send and fetch the likeness of abba Theodosius of the Scopelos, and through it God will give thee water.' And at once the woman sent [b: B] two men, and took the image of the saint, and let it down into the well. And at once, on the instant, the water came out, so that the cistern was half filled. And those who brought the image safe out of the water brought some of it to us, and we drank, and we all glorified God."

CHAPTER 82

We saw an elder named John in that same monastery. And the fathers of the place said of him to us, "Truly, Christians, he is great, and fearful to the demons. For whoever [?] comes here troubled by an unclean spirit, he gives him healing."[^3]

CHAPTER 83

The fathers of the place told us this too about him: "About twenty-four miles from the monastery there is a trading port called Lepte Acra.[^4] In this port a shipmaster who had a ship of three and a half myriads,[^5] and wanted to haul it down into the sea, spent two weeks at it with many workmen (he said he had three hundred workmen a day); and he could not get the ship down into the sea, nor even move it from its place. For evil men had been at the ship.[^6] So the master of the ship was in great despondency and affliction, and did not know what to do. By the dispensation of God the elder went off to those parts. So the shipmaster, seeing the elder, and knowing him by experience, says to him, 'Lord abba, pray for the ship, for by magic it will not go down into the sea.' The elder says to the shipmaster, 'Go, see that I get something to eat, and God can help.' The elder said this so that the shipmaster would go off to his own house. So the elder went alone to the ship, and he makes three prostrations to God, and seals the ship three times with the sign of the cross in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. And the elder came to the shipmaster's house and says to him, 'Go, bring thy ship down into the sea.' And the shipmaster believed the elder, and went with very few men. And as they hauled, at once it was found in the sea. And all glorified God."

[2941] CHAPTER 84

The fathers of the same monastery told us, [b: A] saying: "There was an anchorite in these mountains, very great before God, who spent many years as a grazer.[^7] He died in a small cave, and we did not know it: we thought he had gone off to another desert. So one night he comes in sleep, and appears to our present father, the good and kind shepherd abba Julian, saying, 'Take some men, and come and lift me from the place where I lie, on the mountain called the Deer.' So our father took some of us, and went up to the mountain where he had said. And after searching many hours, we did not find the anchorite's remains; for with time the mouth of the cave had been covered over by wood and snow. So when we did not find them, the abba says, 'Come, children, let us go down.' [b: B] And as we were about to go down, look, a deer came and stood a little way from us, and began to dig the earth with its feet. And our father saw it and says to us, 'Believe me, children, the servant of God is buried there.' And digging, we found the remains whole; and we brought them to the monastery and buried him."

CHAPTER 85

They told us this too, saying: "It was the custom on Great and Holy Thursday for the poor and the orphans of this country to come here, and to receive, each of them, half a modius of wheat, and also five blessings,[^8] and five obols, and a pint of wine, and half [b: C] a pint of honey. Now three years ago there was a scarcity of wheat, and wheat was selling in this country at two modii to the gold piece.[^9] So when the fasts came, some of the fathers say to our superior, 'Lord abba, this year do not give the poor the custom of the season, lest the monastery run short for the brethren; for wheat is not to be found.' And the abba began to say to the brethren, 'Let us not cut off, children, the blessing of our father. Look, it is the commandment of the elder; it is not expedient for us ⟨to break it⟩. Truly, he is the one who takes care of us.' But the brethren kept on pressing the abba: 'We cannot manage to give it, nor do we have it to give.' Then the superior, grieved: 'Go: whatever you want, do it.' So they did not give the blessing according to custom on Holy Thursday and on Holy Friday. The man in charge of the granary went off, and opening it he sees that all the wheat had sprouted; and so they were obliged to throw it into the sea. Then the abba began to say to the brethren, 'He who sets at nought the commands of his father suffers these things. Reap the labours of disobedience! Five hundred modii were to have gone out, and we would have served our father Theodosius by obedience, and comforted

[2944] [b: A] our brethren the poor. Look, about five thousand modii of wheat have perished. What have we gained for ourselves, children? Truly, we have done these two evil things: one, that we transgressed the commandment of our father; and the second, that we did not have our hope in God, but in our granary. From now on, brethren, let us learn from this that it is God who governs all mankind, and that holy Theodosius invisibly takes care of us his children.'"

CHAPTER 86

Thomas the Aegiarius[^10] told us: "Coming out from Aegeae after the feast, when a great storm came on, I put in at the monastery of abba Theodosius at the Scopelos. And while I was there, something of this kind happened. There was an anchorite in those parts living as a grazer; and he used to come every holy Sunday and partake of the holy mysteries. Now once the anchorite took offence, and he went about five weeks without coming to the monastery as he used to. And the men of the monastery were grieved. So while I was there in the monastery, he came on a Sunday. And when the fathers of the monastery saw him, they rejoiced, and made a prostration to him. And he made one too, and they made their peace. And the anchorite partook of the holy Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, and laid himself down in the middle of the church, and at once he was perfected, not having been ill at all. So the fathers of the monastery knew that the anchorite had foreknown his end, and that for this he had come, so that he might go to the Lord holding nothing against anyone."

CHAPTER 87

We visited an estate six miles from Rhossus, and two old laymen gave us hospitality in the church of their estate. The estate lay at the foot of the mountain. So in the church they showed us slabs, and they say to us, "In this tomb, Christians, lies a great anchorite." We say to them, "How do you know?" And they said to us, "Seven years ago we of this estate used to see at night, on the top of the mountain, a light as of fire. We thought it was because of the wild beasts. And we saw this for many days. So one day we went up, and saw no sign at all: neither the lights, nor anything burnt in the woodland. Again [?] at night we would see the same lights. [?] So for three months the fire [?]. So one day we take some men [?] by night, with weapons because of the beasts, and we go up the mountain towards the light, and where the light was we stayed till morning. And in the morning we see a small cave, where the lights had shown; and going in, we find the anchorite dead. He wore a tunic

[2945] of hair, [b: A] and a mantle of cord; and he held a [?] that had a silver cross.[^11] And we found with him tablets written thus: 'I, John the lowly, died in the fifteenth indiction.' So we reckoned the time, and found that he had been dead seven years. And he was as though he had been perfected that day. And we carried him and buried him in the church."

CHAPTER 88

When we came to Theoupolis, a priest of the church told us about a certain abba Thomas, that he was apocrisiarius[^12] of a coenobium in the parts of Apamea. So he came to Theoupolis on the monastery's business. And having stayed there some time, he died at Daphne in the shrine of Saint Euphemia. So the clerics of the place buried him as a stranger in the strangers' burying-ground. Now the next day they bury a woman, and lay her on top of him. It was about the second hour; and about the ninth hour[^13] the earth threw her up. And the people of the place saw what had happened, and wondered; then in the evening they buried her again in the same tomb, and the next day they found the body on top of the grave. So they took the body and buried it in another tomb. Then after a few days they again buried a woman, and laid her on top of the monk, not thinking that he would not let a woman be buried on top of him. So when the earth threw this one up too, then they knew that the elder would not suffer a woman to be buried on top of him. So then they reported it to the patriarch Domninus,[^14] and he had the whole city come to Daphne with candles, and bring down the remains of that holy man with psalmody; and they laid them in the cemetery where many remains of holy martyrs lie, and he made a little oratory over him as well.

CHAPTER 89

One of the fathers told us at Theoupolis: "One day we went up the mountain Amanus on some business, and I found a cave, and going in I find an anchorite with his knees bent and his hands stretched out to heaven, and with the hair of his head reaching to the ground. And I, thinking he was alive, made a prostration to him, saying, 'Pray for me, father.' So when he answered me nothing, I got up and went near him to greet him. And taking hold of him, I found him dead. And I left him and went out. And going on a little, I see another cave, and going in, I found an elder. And he says to me, 'Welcome, brother. Didst thou go into the other cave, the elder's?' And I answered and said to him, 'Yes, father.' And he says to me, 'Didst thou take anything from there?' And I said, 'No.' Then he says to me, 'Truly, brother, the elder has been perfected fifteen years.' And yet he was as though he had fallen asleep an hour before. And when the elder had made

[2948] [b: A] a prayer for me, I withdrew, glorifying God."

## Notes
[^1]: P. The Scopelos ("the Crag"), a mountain near Rhossus on the Gulf of Issus. Its monastery was founded by Theodosius of Rhossus (d. c. 412), not by the Palestinian Theodosius.
[^2]: S. Ps 144 (145):19.
[^3]: T. Οἷος, "such as", does not fit; read in the sense of the Latin *Quicunque*. See the OCR table.
[^4]: P. Lepte Acra, "Narrow Point" (Latin *Exile promontorium*), a trading port on that coast.
[^5]: T. The size is in modii of burden; the Latin has thirty thousand.
[^6]: T. ἦν γὰρ ἀπὸ ἀνδρῶν κακούργων τὸ πλοῖον, literally "the ship was from evildoing men"; the Latin has *a pessimis hominibus incantata*, which the shipmaster's "by magic" bears out.
[^7]: T. βοσκόμενος: a "grazer", a monk who lived in the open on wild plants. The Latin has *vitam solitariam ducens*.
[^8]: T. εὐλογίαι: loaves given as blessings. The Latin reads thirteen.
[^9]: T. The Latin has twelve sextarii to the gold piece.
[^10]: P. The Latin has *Abbas Egiarius*. The epithet probably means "of Aegeae" (Ayas in Cilicia), the town named next.
[^11]: T. μεγαλεῖον does not make sense here; the Latin has *tenebat in manibus argenteam crucem*. See the OCR table.
[^12]: P. An apocrisiarius was a monastery's agent in dealings with the authorities.
[^13]: T. The Latin has *sextam*.
[^14]: P. Domninus, patriarch of Antioch 545–559.
