title: The Spiritual Meadow, chapters 109–115
---
[2972] CHAPTER 109

About abba George, superior of the monastery of abba Theodosius, abba Theodosius his disciple told us — the good and gentle and humble-minded man who became bishop of Capitolias — that he spent twelve years watching to see him troubled even once, and did not see him troubled; and this in the present time, when all neglect and [?][^1] and great insubordination reign. "And who," he says, "so curbed[^2] his eye as our father among the saints George? Who set a door to his hearing as the blessed man did? Bound his tongue as our father did? What sunbeam so lit up the earth[^3] as our father lit up the hearts of us all?"

[2973] CHAPTER 110

[b: A] I took my lord Sophronius, and we went off to the laura of the Eighteenth[^4] at Alexandria, to an elder, a very great one, an Egyptian by birth; and I say to the elder, "Tell us a word, my lord abba: how ought we to live together? For my lord the sophist intends to renounce the world." The elder says, "Thou dost well, child, to renounce the world and save thy soul. So sit in a cell, wherever you wish, only soberly, keeping stillness and praying without ceasing; and I have hope in God, child, that he sends you his knowledge to enlighten your mind."

He said again: "If you wish to be saved, children, flee men. For today we do not stop knocking at every gateway and going round every city [b: B] and country, in case we can somewhere buy ourselves the wages of avarice or of vainglory, and fill our own souls with vanity."

The elder said again: "So let us flee, children, for the time has drawn near."

He said again: "Alas,[^5] how much shall we weep and repent for the things we do not repent of now!"

He said again: "When we are praised we do not hold ourselves in check, nor again do we bear a word. For the one works love of glory in us, and the other produces grief in us wretches. And where there is grief and vainglory, nothing good is found."

He said again: "Our Fathers, as great and wonderful men, shepherded many; but I, the lowly one, cannot rule one sheep, but am always caught by the wild beasts."

He said again: "This is the work of the demons: after casting the soul into sin, then to throw us into despair, that they may destroy us completely. For the demons are always saying to the soul, 'When will he die, and his name perish?'[^6] So if the soul is watchful, it too will cry back at them and say, 'I shall not die, but live, and declare the works of the Lord.'[^7] So since the demons have great shamelessness, they in their turn come back again, saying, 'Flee to the mountains like a [2976] sparrow.'[^8] Therefore we too ought to say to them, 'For he is my God and my Saviour, my helper; I shall not flee away.'"[^9]

He said again: "Be the doorkeeper of thy heart, so that no stranger enters: say, 'Art thou ours, or of the adversaries?'"[^10]

CHAPTER 111

My companion Sophronius and I, being in Alexandria, were going one day to the church of Theodosius; and a bald man meets us, wearing sacking down to the knees; and he seemed to be a fool. So abba Sophronius says to me, "Give me some obols, and thou wilt see the virtue of the man who is coming." I give him five obols. And he took them and gives them to the one who seemed a fool. And he took them, saying nothing at all. And we followed behind him unseen. And when he turned the corner of the street, he stretches out his right hand to heaven, holding the obols, up on high, and after this he makes a prostration to God, and putting the obols on the ground, he went away.

CHAPTER 112

In the beginnings of Tiberius, the emperor and most faithful Caesar,[^11] we went off to the Oasis, and while there we saw a monk great according to God, a Cappadocian by birth. His name was Leo. Many people told us many wonderful things about him. And we, coming to converse with him, and having had long experience of the holy man, were greatly profited, and most of all by his lowliness and stillness and poverty, and the love which he had toward all. So this celebrated elder used to say to us, "Believe me, children, I am to reign." So we say to him, "Believe it, abba Leo:[^12] no one from Cappadocia has ever reigned; so this thought of thine is out of place." But again he would say, "Truly, children, I am to reign"; and no one could persuade him to give up this thought.

So when the Mazices[^13] came and plundered all that country, they came to the Oasis, and killed many monks, and took many captive too; among whom they took from the laura abba John (he had been a reader of the Great Church of Constantinople) and abba Eustathius the Roman and abba Theodore; and all three were sick. So after they had been taken captive, abba John says to the barbarians, "Take me to the city, and I will make the bishop give you twenty-four gold pieces." [2977] [b: A] So one of the barbarians took him and brings him near the city. And abba John went in to the bishop. Now abba Leo was found in the city too, and some others of the Fathers; which is why they were not seized either. So abba John went in and began to plead with the bishop to give the gold pieces to the barbarian. But the bishop was found to have only eight gold pieces. So they wanted to give them to the barbarian. And he would not take them, saying, "Either give me twenty-four gold pieces, or the monk." So the people of the fort were forced to give abba John to the barbarian, weeping and wailing; and they led him away to their tents. So after three days abba Leo took eight gold pieces and went out into the desert where the barbarians were, and pleaded, saying, "Take me too, and the eight gold pieces, and let these go; for they are sick, and are of no service to you, and you will have to kill them. But I am healthy, and I serve you." Then the barbarians took him, and the eight gold pieces as he had said, and let the three go. So abba Leo went off with them for some way, and when he gave out they beheaded him; and abba Leo fulfilled the Scripture which says, "Greater love than this has no man, that a man lay down his life for his friends."[^14] Then we too knew that this was what he meant when he said, "I am to reign." For truly he reigned, laying down his life for his friends.

CHAPTER 113

I went to abba John of Petra, [b: C] taking my companion Sophronius too, and we asked him to tell us a word. And the elder said, "Love poverty and self-control. For believe me when I tell you: when I was at Scetis, in my youth, one of the Fathers had a pain in his spleen, and a little vinegar was sought in the four lauras of Scetis, and it was not found; so great was the poverty and self-control among them. And there were Fathers there, about three thousand five hundred."

CHAPTER 114

The same elder told us again about abba Daniel the Egyptian, saying: "The elder went up one day to Terenuthis to sell his handiwork. And a young man pleaded with the elder, saying, 'For God's sake, good elder,[^15] come to my house and make a prayer over my wife, for she is barren.' And the elder, pressed by the young man, went off with him to his house, and made a prayer for his wife. And God willing, the wife conceived. So some men who did not fear God began to slander the elder, and to say, 'Truly the young man is impotent; it is from abba Daniel that the wife has conceived.' So this report came to the elder. And [2980] the elder sends word to the woman's husband: 'When thy wife gives birth, let me know.' So when the wife had given birth, the young man lets him know, saying, 'By God and by thy prayers, Father, she has given birth.' Then abba Daniel went and says to the young man, 'Make a dinner, and call thy kinsmen and thy friends.' So while they were dining, the elder took the child in his hands, and before them all says to the child, 'Who is thy father?' The child says, 'This man,' pointing with the finger of his hand at the young man. Now the child was twenty-two days old.[^16] And all glorified God, who keeps truth for those who seek him with their whole heart."[^17]

CHAPTER 115

[b: B] Abba John the Cilician, superior of Raithou, used to say to the brethren: "Children, as we have fled the world, let us flee the desires of the flesh too."[^18]

He said again: "Let us imitate our Fathers: with what hardship and stillness they lived here."

He said again: "Let us not defile, children, this place which our Fathers cleansed of demons."

He said again: "This place is for ascetics, not for traders."

He said again: "I came upon elders who had spent seventy years eating only herbs and dates."

The elder said again: "I have been seventy-six years in this place, and have suffered many dread and evil things from the demons."

## Notes
[^1]: T. καί….. as printed: a word or more is lost, or the plate marks a lacuna. The Latin lists *desidia, et negligentia, et mollities, et inobedientia*, so a word like "slackness" is wanting.
[^2]: T. Printed ἐχάμωσεν; read ἐκήμωσεν, "muzzled", i.e. curbed. The Latin: *frenavit*.
[^3]: T. Printed τὴν γλῶσσαν, "the tongue", repeating the line above; the Latin has *terram*, and the comparison wants "the earth" (τὴν γῆν). The English follows the Latin.
[^4]: P. The laura of the Eighteenth: the monastery at the eighteenth milestone from Alexandria (Latin *decem et octo passuum millibus*).
[^5]: T. Ἀβάλαι as printed; the Latin has *Væ, væ*. An interjection of grief either way.
[^6]: S. Ps 40:6 LXX (41:5).
[^7]: S. Ps 117:17 LXX (118:17).
[^8]: S. Ps 10:1 LXX (11:1).
[^9]: S. Ps 61:3 LXX (62:2); the LXX ends οὐ μὴ σαλευθῶ, and Moschus has οὐ μὴ μεταναστεύσω, picking up the verse before.
[^10]: S. Josh 5:13 LXX.
[^11]: P. Tiberius II Constantine (Caesar 574, emperor 578–582).
[^12]: T. Printed λέγων, "saying"; read Λέων, the vocative name, with the Latin *abba Leo*.
[^13]: P. The Mazices: Berber raiders of the Egyptian western desert and the Oasis.
[^14]: S. John 15:13.
[^15]: T. καλόγηρε, "good elder", the vernacular address to a monk.
[^16]: T. εἴκοσι δύο, "twenty-two"; the Latin has *viginti quinque*.
[^17]: S. Echoes Ps 145:6 and 118:2 LXX.
[^18]: S. Cf. 1 John 2:16; Gal 5:16.
