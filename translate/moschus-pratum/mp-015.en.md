title: The Spiritual Meadow, chapters 116–126
---
[2980] CHAPTER 116

Abba Andrew of Messene told us: "When I was young, my abba and I withdrew from Raithu,[^1] and we went out to Palestine; and we stayed with an elder. Now the elder who took us in had one gold piece. He forgot where he had put it, and he supposed that I, the younger, had stolen it. So the elder said to the fathers of the place, 'Brother Andrew took the gold piece.' My abba heard it, and called me and said, 'Tell me, Andrew, didst thou take the elder's gold piece?' I say, 'Spare me, abba, I took nothing.' Now I had a cloak, and I sold it for a gold piece; and taking the gold piece I go to the elder and make a prostration to him, and I say, 'Forgive me, my lord abba, for Satan mocked me, and I took thy gold piece.' There happened to be a layman there as well, and the elder says to me, 'Go, child, I have lost nothing.' So again I made a prostration, saying, 'For the Lord's sake take back the gold piece; look, here it is; and make a prayer for me, for Satan put it into me to steal it and to grieve thee.' The elder says, 'Child, I have lost nothing at all.' So since he could not persuade me, then the layman says to me, 'Truly, my lord brother, yesterday I came and found the elder weeping and making prostrations and in great distress. So when I saw the elder in such distress, I say to him, "Do me a kindness: what is it [2981] that ails thee?" And the elder says to me, "I slandered the brother, that he had taken the gold piece; and look, I have found it, where I put it."' Then the elder was edified by me, because I had not taken it and yet had brought it to him, saying, 'Take the gold piece; for I took it.'"

CHAPTER 117

A brother who had a demon came to abba Symeon the Stylite at the Wondrous Mountain,[^2] so that he might make a prayer for him and drive the demon away from him. Abba Symeon says to him, "Where dost thou live?" The brother says, "At Raithu." The elder answered, "I marvel, brother, how much toil thou hast endured, and what a long road thou hast travelled, to come to me, a sinful man, when thou hast such fathers in thy laura. So go to abba Andrew and make a prostration to him, that he may pray for thee, and at once thou art healed." The brother went away to Raithu and made a prostration to abba Andrew, as abba Symeon had told him,[^3] saying, "Pray for me, abba." Abba Andrew says to him, "Abba Symeon has received the grace of healing." And when he had made a prayer, at once the brother was cleansed, and he gave thanks to God.

CHAPTER 118

Abba Sergius of Raithu told us about a brother there who had been made a deacon, named Menas, speaking thus: "This man went out into the world on an errand, and what happened to him we do not know, except that he left the monastic habit and became a layman. So after a long time he went off to Theoupolis;[^4] and as he was going up from Seleucia he saw from far off the monastery of abba Symeon the Stylite, who is among the saints, and he says within himself, 'Let me go up and see the great Symeon'; for he had never set eyes on him. So he went up, and drew near the pillar; and when abba Symeon saw him, knowing that he was a monk and that he had a deacon's ordination, he calls the man who served him, saying, 'Bring the scissors here.' And when he had brought them, he says to him, 'Blessed is the Lord: tonsure that man,' pointing him out to him with his own finger. For there were many people around the pillar. He marvelled at the word, and at the same time was seized with great fear, and he submitted, uttering no word at all, perceiving that God had revealed his affairs to the holy elder. Abba Symeon says to him, 'Make a deacon's prayer.' And when he had prayed, the saint says to him, 'Go to Raithu, where thou camest from.' And when he said that he was ashamed and could not bear the shame before men, again he says to him, 'Believe me, child, in this: thou oughtest not to be ashamed. For the fathers will receive thee with a cheerful face, and there will be joy for them, and gladness at thy return. And know this too, that God is to work a sign in thee, so that thou mayest know that his compassion has forgiven thee this sin.' And indeed, [2984] [b: A] when he came to Raithu the fathers received him with open arms,[^5] and they put him into the sanctuary as well. And one Sunday, as he was carrying the holy and life-giving blood of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ, suddenly his eye came out.[^6] And from this sign they knew that God had forgiven him the sin, according to the word of the righteous Symeon."

CHAPTER 119

Abba Eusebius, the priest of the laura of Raithu, told us when we visited him that a demon went to an elder's cell in the habit of a monk. And when he knocked at the door, the elder opened to him and says to him, "Pray." The demon says, "Now and ever and to the ages of ages. Amen." The elder says to him a third time,[^7] "Pray." The demon says, "Now and ever, and to the ages of ages. Amen." The elder says to him, "Thou art welcome. Pray, and say: Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, always, now and ever and to the ages of ages. Amen." And when the elder said this, the demon vanished, as though driven off by fire.

CHAPTER 120

Some Pharonite fishermen told us: "One day we went off to Bouchri, across the Red Sea; and having caught a great many fish we came back and anchored near Pteleos. And wanting to cross over to Raithu, we were hindered by contrary winds, and were held fast by a storm at sea for ninety days. And going through that vast desert, we found under a rock three anchorites, dead"[^8]

[2985] CHAPTER 123

I visited abba Zosimus the Cilician at Sinai, [b: A] while I was living at Sinai. For the elder had resigned the bishopric and come back again to his cell; for he had acquired great asceticism. And he told me: "When I was young I went out from Mount Sinai and went off to the Ammoniake,[^9] to stay there in a cell. And I found there an elder wearing a colobion of sibinon.[^10] And when the elder saw me, before I greeted him, he says to me, 'What hast thou come here for, Zosimus? Go thou away from here [?];[^11] for thou canst not settle here.' So I thought that he knew me, and I make him a prostration, saying, 'Do me a kindness, good elder: how dost thou know me?' He says to me, 'Two days ago someone appeared to me, saying, "Look, a monk named Zosimus is coming near thee. Do not let him stay here; for I wish to entrust to him the church of Babylon in Egypt."' And he fell silent and let me be, and went away from me about a stone's throw. And having spent about two hours praying, he came to me, and kissing my forehead he says to me, 'Truly, child, thou art welcome. For God has brought thee here to bury my body.' So I say to him, 'How many years hast thou been here, abba?' He says to me, 'I am completing forty-five years.' And his face appeared to me like fire. And he says to me, 'Peace to thee, child, and pray for me.' And having said this he laid himself down, and the servant of God went to his rest. And I dug and buried him, and after two days I withdrew, glorifying God."

CHAPTER 124

The elder told us this too: "Twenty-two years ago I went up to the Porphyrites,[^12] wishing to settle there; and I took my disciple John with me as well. So when we went, we find two anchorites there, and we stayed near them. One was from Melitene, named Theodore; and the other, Paul, was from the country of the Galatians. And Theodore was from the monastery of abba Euthymius. They wore colobia of buffalo hides. I stayed there about two years; and we were about two stades apart from one another. So one day, while John my disciple was still sitting, a snake strikes him, and at once he was dead, bleeding through all his limbs. So, being in distress, I went off to the anchorites. And when they saw me troubled and distressed, before I said anything, they say to me, 'What is it, abba Zosimus? Has the brother died?' I say to them, 'Yes.' So they came with me, and seeing him flung on the ground they say to me, 'Do not grieve, abba Zosimus; God helps.' And calling the brother, they say, 'Brother John, get up; the elder needs thee.' [2988] And at once the brother got up from the ground. And they sought out the beast and caught hold of it, and before us they cut it in two. Then they say to me, 'Abba Zosimus, go to Sinai. For God wishes to entrust to thee the church of Babylon.' So at once we withdrew. And when we came to Sinai, after a few days the abba of Sinai sent me and two others to Alexandria. And the abba [?] of Alexandria,[^13] the most blessed Apollinarius, took hold of us and made the three of us bishops: one for Heliopolis, another for Leontopolis, and me for Babylon."

CHAPTER 125

Some of the fathers of Sinai told us about abba Sergius the anchorite: "When he was staying at Sinai, the steward put him over the mules. So as he was coming along one day, look, a lion was lying in the road. And when the mules and the muleteers saw the lion, they were gripped with fear and stood off. Then abba Sergius took a blessing[^14] from his bag [?][^15] and went off to the lion and says to it, 'Take the blessing of the fathers, and go off the road, so that we may pass.' And the lion took the blessing and withdrew."

CHAPTER 126

The fathers of that same holy place used to tell us about abba Orentius that one Sunday he came into the church with his kamasion[^16] turned inside out, wearing its fleece outside. So as he stood in the choir, those who live in the place said to him, "Good elder, why hast thou come in like this, and art making a show of us before the strangers?" The elder said to them, "You have turned Sinai inside out, and no one has said anything to you; and because I turn my kamasion, do you find fault with me? Go; and I am setting right what I turned."[^17]

## Notes
[^1]: P. Raithu: the monastic settlement on the Red Sea shore of Sinai (near modern el-Tor).
[^2]: P. Symeon Stylite the Younger, on the Wondrous Mountain near Antioch.
[^3]: T. The Greek spells the name Συμεώνιος here and in the next sentence, Συμεώνην in ch. 118; rendered "Symeon" throughout. The Latin has Simeon everywhere.
[^4]: P. Theoupolis, "God's city", the name Antioch bore after the earthquake of 528.
[^5]: T. ὑπτίαις χερσίν, literally "with upturned hands"; Latin supinis manibus.
[^6]: T. As printed: ἐξῆλθεν ὁ ὀφθαλμὸς αὐτοῦ; the Latin agrees (exiit oculus ejus unus).
[^7]: T. ἐκ τρίτου, "a third time", though only two exchanges are printed; the Latin has simply rursus.
[^8]: T. The Greek breaks off here, at τεθνηκότας, with no full stop. Migne prints the rest of the chapter (their clothing, the calmed sea, the burial at Raithu) in Latin only, then two whole chapters (121, Gregory of Byzantium and Gregory the Pharanite; 122, Stephen the Cappadocian and the two naked anchorites) in Latin only, with no Greek. Nothing is supplied here.
[^9]: P. The Ammoniake: the district of Ammon, the Libyan desert west of the Nile.
[^10]: T. ἀπὸ σιβίνου κολόβιον; Latin colobium de sibino. Sibinon is a coarse cloth, apparently of palm fibre or hair.
[^11]: T. ὕπαγε σὺ τῶν ἔνθεν does not construe as printed; the sense "go hence" is clear and the Latin has just vade. See the OCR table.
[^12]: P. The Porphyrites: Mons Porphyrites, the porphyry mountain of Egypt's Eastern Desert.
[^13]: T. The Greek prints ὁ ἀββᾶς Ἀλεξανδρείας; the Latin has papa Alexandrinus. Apollinarius was the Chalcedonian patriarch (pope) of Alexandria, 551–570. ἀββᾶς and πάπας are not letter-confusable, so this may be Migne's own text.
[^14]: T. εὐλογία, a "blessing": blessed bread given out as a gift; Latin eulogiam.
[^15]: T. ἐκ τοῦ μαργονίου αὐτοῦ: the word is unknown to me; the Latin has de pera, "from his bag". See the OCR table.
[^16]: T. καμάσιον, a hair or fleece garment; Latin sagum.
[^17]: T. The Latin is fuller here: "Go, set right what you overturned, and I will set right what I overturned." The Greek as printed lacks the first clause. No sign of loss in the OCR line, so it may be the Greek of Migne's text.
