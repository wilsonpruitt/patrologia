title: The Spiritual Meadow, chapters 127–133
---
[2988] CHAPTER 127

Amma Damiana, the solitary, the mother of abba Athenogenes the bishop of Petra,[^1] told us: "There was a superior on the holy mountain of Sinai named George, a very great man and an ascetic. On Holy Saturday a longing came upon this abba George, as he sat in his cell: 'I wish,' he says, 'to keep the holy Resurrection in the holy city, and to partake of the holy mysteries in the holy Anastasis of Christ our God.'[^2] So through the whole day the elder stayed turning these thoughts over, and praying. So when it was evening his disciple came, saying to him, 'Give the word, father, for the rule.'[^3] The elder answered him, 'Go; and at the time of the [2989] [b: A] holy partaking come here, and I will come.' So the elder stayed on in his cell. Now when the time of holy Communion came in the holy Anastasis, the elder was found close by the blessed bishop Peter,[^4] and he gives him holy Communion with the priests. So the patriarch, seeing him, says to Menas his syncellus, 'When did the abba of Sinai arrive?' The syncellus answered, 'By your prayers, master, I have not seen him, master, until just now.' Then the patriarch says to the syncellus, 'Tell him, "Do not leave. For I wish thee to take food with me."' The syncellus went off and told the elder. And he said, 'The will of God be done.' So when the elder had finished the synaxes and had venerated the holy tomb, the elder was found in his cell. And look, his disciple knocking, and he says, 'Father, give the word, so that thou mayest communicate.' And the elder went off to the church with his disciple and partook of the holy mysteries. Now archbishop Peter, grieved that he had disobeyed him, after the feast sends letters to the elder, and to the bishop of Pharan, abba Photius, and to the fathers of Sinai, that they should bring the abba to him. So when the man carrying the letters had gone and delivered the letters, he sent three priests to the patriarch: abba Stephen the Cappadocian, the great; and abba Zosimus (of him too we have made mention); and abba Dulcitius the Roman. And the elder made his defence in writing, saying, 'God forbid, my most holy master, that I should have despised your holy angel.'[^5] He wrote this too: 'That your blessedness may know that after six months we are to meet together the Master, Christ our God, and there I shall venerate you.' The priests went out and give the letters to the patriarch. And they said, 'The elder has not gone out to Palestine for many years.' They also showed the letters of Pharan, testifying to this, that the elder had not gone out of the holy mountain of Sinai for some seventy years. But the godly and gentle Peter [called] as witnesses the bishops who had been present, and the clergy,[^6] who said, 'We saw the elder, and we all greeted him with the holy kiss.' So when the six months were fulfilled, the elder and the patriarch went to their rest [?],[^7] according to the elder's prediction."

The same amma Damiana told us this too: "On Fridays, before I was shut in, I used to go off to Saint Cosmas and Damian, and there I spent the whole night. Now in the evening an old Phrygo-Galatian woman used to come, and give everyone who was in the church two lepta each. So I knew her, since she had often given to me. So one day a niece of mine and of the most faithful emperor Maurice, who had come to the holy city to pray, spent a year there. So I took her and went off to Saint Cosmas and Damian. So as we were [2992] in the oratory, I say to my kinswoman, 'Look, lady, an old woman is coming who gives two nummia each; take them, do not be proud.' But she was put out, saying, 'Have I a commandment to take them?' Then I said to her, 'Yes, take them, for the woman is great before God; for the whole week she distributes them to those who are in the church; and she is a widow of about eighty years. So take them, and give them, thou too, to someone else; only do not turn away the old woman's sacrifice.' While we were saying this, look, the old woman coming and giving; and she came and gave with all stillness and silence. She gave to my niece too, saying, 'Take these and eat.' So when she had gone away, we knew that God had revealed to her what I had said, 'Take them and give them to a poor man.' So she sent one of her servants, and he got lupins for the two lepta, [b: B] and she ate, and she affirmed before God, saying that they were sweet as honey; so that she marvelled, and glorified God who gives grace to his servants."

CHAPTER 128

We visited abba Athanasius in the laura of our father Sabas, who is among the saints, and he told us that he had heard abba Athenogenes, the bishop of Petra, the son of amma Damiana, telling something like this:

"My grandmother Iannia had a brother named Adelphius, bishop of Arabissus; and she also had a sister, the superior of the women's monastery. So one day the bishop went off to the monastery to visit his sister. And as he was coming into the courtyard of the monastery, he sees one of the sisters troubled by the demon and flung on the floor. So the bishop called his sister and says to her, 'Does it please thee, how this sister is wronged by the demon and disgraced? Or dost thou not know that thou bearest the judgement of all the sisters, as superior?' She says to him, 'And what can I do against the demon?' The bishop says to her again, 'And what art thou doing here all these years?' And the bishop made a prayer and cleansed the sister."

Again the same Athanasius told us about the same bishop Adelphius, saying that he had heard from amma Iannia, his sister, who said: "When the bishop of Constantinople, John Chrysostom, was exiled to Cucusus, he stayed in our house. Through this we had great boldness and love towards God. So my brother Adelphius used to say, 'When the blessed John died in exile, I had unbearable grief, that this man, the teacher of the world, who had gladdened the Church of God with his words, had fallen asleep away from his throne. And I besought God with many tears to show me in what state he is, and whether he has been ranked with the patriarchs. [2993] So when I had been praying this for a long time, [b: A] one day I came into ecstasy, and I see a man very fair to look on, who took me by the right hand; he led me to a bright and glorious place, and showed me the heralds of piety and the teachers of the Church. For I,' he says, 'was looking round to see the one I longed for, the great John, my beloved. So when he had shown me all of them, and told the proper name of each one, he took me by the hand again and put me outside. And I followed him, sorely grieved, not having seen John, who is among the saints, with the fathers. So as I was going out, the one standing at the door says to me, "No one who comes here goes out grieving." Then I say to him, "This is my grief, that I have not seen my dearest John, the bishop of Constantinople, with the other teachers." And he says to me again, "Dost thou mean John, him of repentance? A man in the flesh cannot see that man; for he stands where the Master's throne is."'"

CHAPTER 129

The same abba Athanasius told again that he had heard abba Athenogenes, the bishop of Petra, saying: "In my country there was a stylite, and all who came to him talked to him standing below, because he had no ladder. If ever a brother said to him, 'I want to tell thee a thought,' he himself would say to him in a gentle voice, 'Come to the kouboukleion';[^8] and he would go off to the other side of the pillar. And so the two of them talked with each other, [b: C] the stylite above and the brother below; and of the others standing there no one heard what was said."

Abba Athanasius, he says, told this too about the same stylite: "Two herdsmen,[^9] who had great love for each other, visited him for many years, the two together, never coming without each other. So one day one of them came, without his companion's knowledge, to visit him; and though he knocked at the gate for many hours, the elder did not allow it to be opened to him; so that, falling into acedia, he turned back. And as he was turning back his friend met him, who was going off to the stylite too; and he turned him back again with him, so that the two might go in to the elder together. So when he knocked at the gate, the elder sent word that the later one should come in alone. So the later one came in and began to beg the elder that the other might come in too. But the elder said he could not receive him; and as he stayed on and begged for many hours, the elder says to him, 'God turns him away, child, and I cannot receive him.' And when they had gone off home, after two days he died."

CHAPTER 130

The same abba Athanasius used to say: "Our fathers kept [2996] [b: A] self-control and poverty until death; but we have widened our bellies and our purses."

The elder said again: "In our fathers' time freedom from distraction was a thing much sought after; but now among us the pot and handiwork carry the day."

Abba Athanasius told us this too: "Once a thought came to me, saying, 'What then is there for those who strive, and for those who do not strive?'[^10] And I became as if in ecstasy, and someone came, saying to me, 'Follow me'; and he led me off to a place filled with light, and stood me by a gate whose appearance cannot be told; and we heard from within as it were a countless multitude singing hymns to God. And when we knocked, someone answered from inside, saying, 'What do you want?' My guide says, [b: B] 'We want to come in.' He answered, saying to me, 'No one comes in here who lives in negligence; but if you want to come in, go off and strive, reckoning nothing of this vain world.'"

CHAPTER 131

Procopius the scholasticus, of Porphyreon,[^11] told us about abba Zacchaeus: "My two sons were studying at Caesarea. Now a great plague broke out in Caesarea, and I was in distress for my children, lest they die, and I did not know what to do. For I said within myself, 'If I send and fetch them, there is no escaping the wrath of God. But shall I leave them there? Perhaps they will die, and I shall not see them.' So, not knowing what I ought to do, I say, 'I am going to abba Zacchaeus, and whatever he tells me, that I do.' So I go to holy Sion; for he spent his time there continually; and I did not find him. And I come into the courtyard, and I tell him about my sons.[^12] So when he heard, he turned toward the east, and he gazed at heaven, stretched out, for about two hours, saying nothing. Then turning to me he says to me, 'Take heart, do not be distressed; thy children do not die in the plague; what is more, after two days the plague ceases from Caesarea.'"[^13]

CHAPTER 133

A Saracen, a pagan, told the townsmen at Clysma,[^14] and us: "I went off to the mountain of abba Antony to hunt. So as I went along, I see a monk sitting on the mountain, holding a book and reading. So I went up to him to do him harm, and perhaps even to kill him. And when I came near him, he stretched out [2997] his right hand towards me, [b: A] saying, 'Stand.' And I spent two days and two nights unable to move anywhere from the place. Then I say to him, 'By the God whom thou worshippest, let me go.' He says to me, 'Go in peace.' And so I was able to withdraw from the place where I was."

## Notes
[^1]: P. Petra: the city of Palaestina Tertia (Arabia Petraea), of which Damiana's son was bishop.
[^2]: P. The Anastasis: the church of the Resurrection in Jerusalem; the word is the same as "Resurrection" earlier in the sentence.
[^3]: T. κανών here is the monks' rule of prayer, the evening office; the Latin has ad synaxim.
[^4]: P. Peter, patriarch of Jerusalem 524–552; the Greek calls him bishop, patriarch and archbishop in turn.
[^5]: T. "Angel" in its old sense of messenger: the patriarch's letter-bearer, or the syncellus who carried the invitation.
[^6]: T. The Greek sentence has no main verb; the Latin supplies afferebat ("brought forward"). "Called" is in brackets for that reason.
[^7]: T. ἀνέπαυον, an active imperfect, does not fit; a middle or passive aorist ("went to their rest") is wanted, and the Latin has requieverunt. See the OCR table.
[^8]: T. κουβούκλιον, a chamber or canopied place at the pillar; the Latin has ad gradus columnae, "to the steps of the pillar".
[^9]: T. The Greek reads δύο βοσκοί, "two herdsmen"; the Latin has duo saeculares, "two laymen". The English follows the Greek.
[^10]: T. The Greek opens the thought with Ὅ τι, which does not construe as printed; the sense is clear from the Latin, Quid putas erit. See the OCR table.
[^11]: P. Porphyreon: a coastal town between Tyre and Sidon. A scholasticus is a man trained in the law, an advocate.
[^12]: T. The Latin is fuller: "I came into the court of holy Mary the Mother of God, and found him standing in a corner of the court, and told him about my sons." The Greek as printed passes straight from the courtyard to the telling; the OCR line shows no break, so this is probably Migne's Greek.
[^13]: T. The Latin goes on, "And it happened according to the elder's word," and then gives a whole chapter in Latin only (132: abba Cyprian, called Cuculas, and the voice that said "Abba Zacchaeus has received this grace"). Migne prints no Greek for it, so nothing is supplied here.
[^14]: P. Clysma: the port at the head of the Gulf of Suez. "Pagan" renders Ἕλλην.
