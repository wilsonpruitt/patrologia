title: The Spiritual Meadow, chapters 143–149
---
[3004] CHAPTER 143

We came to the Thebaid, and in the city of Antinoë we met the sophist Phoebammon[^1], for profit's sake, and he told us: "There was a robber about the parts of Hermopolis[^2], named David, who had stripped many, and killed many too, and done a great many evils, so to speak, as no one else had. So one day, while he was still in the mountain robbing, having with him more than thirty men, he came to himself, pierced with compunction for the evil things he had done; and leaving all those who were with him, he went off to a monastery. And when he knocked at the gate of the [3005] [b: A] monastery, the gatekeeper came out and said to him, 'What dost thou want?' The robber chief said to him, 'I want to become a monk.' And the doorkeeper went in and reported to the abba about him. So the abba came out, and seeing that he was an old man, he said to him, 'Thou canst not be here, for the brethren have great toil, and their ascesis is great; and besides, thou art of another habit of life, and thou canst not bear the rule of the monastery.' But he pleaded with him, saying, 'Yes, I can do it; only receive me.' But the abba held firm, saying, 'Thou canst not.' Then the robber chief said to him, 'So that thou mayest know: I am David the robber chief, and this is why I came here, to weep for my sins. But if thou wilt not receive me, I assure thee with oaths, by[?] him who dwells in heaven[^3], I go back again to my former trade, and I bring those I had with me, and I kill all of you, and I overturn your monastery too.'

"Hearing this, the abba received him within the monastery, and tonsuring him, gave him the holy habit. So he began to strive, and in self-control and obedience and humility he surpassed all those in the monastery. There were about seventy monks in the monastery, by name[^4]. So, profiting them all, he became a pattern for all.

"So one day, as he sat in his cell, an angel of the Lord stood over him, saying to him, 'David, David, the Lord God has forgiven thee thy sins, and from now on thou shalt be working signs.' He answered and said to the angel, 'I cannot believe that God has forgiven me, in so short a time, all those sins of mine, which are heavier than the sand of the sea.' The angel said to him, 'If I did not spare the priest Zacharias when he disbelieved me about his son, but bound his tongue, teaching him not to disbelieve the things spoken by me[^5], am I to spare thee? Therefore from now on thou shalt not speak at all.' Abba David made a prostration, saying, 'When I was in the world, doing lawless things and shedding blood, I spoke; and now, when I want to serve God and offer him hymns, dost thou bind my tongue so that I may not speak?' Then the angel answered, saying, 'Thou shalt speak only at the office[^6]; outside the office thou shalt be wholly silent.' And so it came to pass. For God worked many signs through him. He would chant the psalms; but any other word, great or small, he could not speak." The one who told us these things said, "I saw him many times, and I glorified God."

CHAPTER 144

One of the elders said to the brethren at the Cells[^7], "Let us not choose to be slaves to the pleasures of Egypt, which [?] to Pharaoh, the deadly tyrant."[^8]

He said again, "Would that men had as much zeal for good things as they have for bad ones! And would that all their haste for the theatres and the licentious festivals, for love of money and vainglory and injustice, they had transferred to a longing for godliness! We would not have been ignorant [3008] how much we have been honoured by God, and how strong we are against the demons."

The elder said again, "Nothing is greater than God, nor equal to him, nor even falling a little short of him. What then is stronger or more blessed than the one who has God for his helper?"

He said again, "God is everywhere; but he draws near to those who are godly and who strive, those who are not grave only as far as profession, but bright through works. And where God is, who will want to lay plots, or who has strength to harm?"

He said again, "The strength of men is not in nature, for nature is changeable, but in purpose, with God helping. So let us tend our soul, children, as much as the body."

The elder said again, "Let us gather the remedies of the soul, that is, godliness, righteousness, humility, submission. For the greatest physician of souls, Christ our God, is near, and wants to heal us. So let us not despise it."

He said again, "The Lord teaches us to be sober; but we wretches, through luxury, busy ourselves all the more with pleasures."

The elder said again, "Let us present ourselves to God, as Paul says, as alive from the dead[^9]; neither looking to the things behind, nor forgetting the things ahead; but let us press on toward the mark, for the prize of the high calling."[^10]

An elder was asked by a brother, "Why do I continually judge the brethren?" The elder answered, "Because thou hast not yet known thyself. For he who knows himself does not look at the doings of the brethren."

CHAPTER 145

We came to the ninth milestone from Alexandria[^11], to the coenobium of Salama, and there we found two elders. They told us they were of the Church of Constanti[nople] [?][^12]. And they told us about the blessed Gennadius, patriarch of Constantinople, that he was being troubled by many people on account of a certain cleric who lived very wickedly, Charisius by name. And the patriarch sent for him and tried to admonish him. So when he [3009] [b: A] did him no good by admonition, he gave orders, and had him disciplined, in a fatherly way and in the Church's way. Then, when the patriarch knew that the cleric was being helped not at all (for he was given over to murders and sorceries), he sent one of those who served as messengers[^13] (for Charisius was a reader of the church there), and said to him, "Say to Saint Eleutherius: 'Thy soldier, Saint Eleutherius, sins much; either correct him or cut him off.'" So the apocrisiarius went to the [b: B] house of prayer of the holy martyr Eleutherius, and standing before the altar, looking toward the apse[^14], he stretched out his hand and said to the martyr, "The patriarch Gennadius makes known to thee, holy martyr of Christ, through me the sinner: 'Thy soldier sins much; either correct him or cut him off.'" And on the next day the worker of evils was found among the dead; and all were astounded and glorified God.

CHAPTER 146

When we were at the ninth milestone, in the coenobium of Tougara, abba Menas, the head of the coenobium, told us about pope Eulogius, who is among the saints: "One night, while he was saying the office by himself in the oratory of the bishop's house, he saw standing near him Julian the archdeacon. When he saw him, he was troubled, because he had dared to come in without being announced. Still, he kept silent. And when he had finished the psalm, he made a prostration. The one who had appeared to him in the form of the archdeacon made one too. Then, when the pope had risen and done reverence, that one stayed on the floor. So the pope turned and said to him, 'How long wilt thou not get up?' He said to him, 'Unless thou thyself reach out thy hand and raise me, I cannot get up.' Then the abba stretched out his hand, and taking hold of him raised him up. He began the psalm again; and turning, he no longer saw anyone. So after he had finished the morning office, he called his chamberlain and said to him, 'Why didst thou not announce the archdeacon's coming in? Instead he came in to me like this, unannounced, and at night too.' He said he had neither seen anyone nor had anyone come in. The pope did not believe him, and said, 'Call me the doorkeeper here.' And when he came, he said to him, 'Did not the archdeacon Julian come up here?' He affirmed with an oath that he had neither come up nor gone down. And for the time the pope kept quiet. But when morning came, the archdeacon Julian came up to do reverence. And the pope said to him, 'Why didst thou not keep the order, archdeacon Julian, but came up to me unannounced this night?' He said, 'By the prayers of my master, I neither came up here nor went out of my house, except at this hour [3012] only.' Then the great Eulogius understood that he had seen Julian the martyr[^15], urging him to the raising up of his own church. For it was decayed with time and grown old, and threatening to fall. And the martyr-loving and godly Eulogius set his hand to it with great eagerness, and raised up his church, building it from the foundations and making it bright with every kind of adornment, as befitted a holy martyr."

CHAPTER 147

Abba Menas, the head of the same coenobium, also told us this, that he had heard the same abba Eulogius, pope of Alexandria, say: "When I went up to Constantinople, [I met] the archdeacon of Rome, the lord Gregory, a man [?] and virtuous[^16]; and he told me about the most holy and most blessed Leo, pope of Rome, that it is recorded in writing in the Church of Rome that when he had written the letter to Flavian, who is among the saints, the patriarch of Constantinople, against Eutyches and Nestorius, the ill-named, he laid it on the tomb of Peter, the chief of the apostles; and giving himself to prayers and fasts and sleeping on the ground, he besought the foremost of the disciples, saying, 'Whatever I have left out, as a man, do thou, as the one entrusted with the Church and the throne by our Lord and God and Saviour Jesus Christ, correct.' And after forty days the apostle appeared to him as he prayed, and said to him, 'I have read it and corrected it.' And taking the letter from the tomb of Saint Peter, he unrolled it and found it corrected by the apostle's hand."

CHAPTER 148

Theodore, the most holy bishop of the city of Darnis in Libya, told us: "When I was syncellus to pope Eulogius, who is among the saints, I saw in my sleep a man of reverend appearance, and tall, saying to me, 'Announce me to pope Eulogius.' And I said to him, 'Who art thou, master? How dost thou bid me announce thee?' He answered and said to me, 'I am Leo, the pope of Rome.' So I went in and announced him, saying, 'The most holy and most blessed pope Leo, president of the Church of the Romans, wishes to pay you his reverence.' So when pope Eulogius heard it, he rose at a run and went to meet him; and they embraced each other, and having made a prayer, they sat down. Then the truly divine and God-bearing Leo said to pope Eulogius, 'Dost thou know why I have come to you?' He answered and said, 'No.' He said to him, 'I have come to thank you, because you have made a fine and great defence of my letter, which I wrote to our brother Flavian, the patriarch of Constantinople, [3013] [b: A] making plain my mind and stopping the mouths of the heretics. And know, brother, that it is not to me alone that you have given your inspired toil, but also to Peter the chief of the apostles, and before all to the very truth preached by us, which is Christ our God.' Having seen this not once only but three times, and having been made sure by the threefold vision, I told it to pope Eulogius, who is among the saints. And when he heard it he wept; and stretching his hands to heaven he gave thanks to God, saying, 'I thank thee, Master Christ, our God, that thou hast made me, unworthy as I am, to become a herald of thy truth; and through the prayers of thy servants Peter and Leo thy goodness has accepted our small eagerness, like the widow's two mites.'"[^17]

CHAPTER 149

[b: B] When abba Ammos[^18] came down to Jerusalem and was ordained patriarch, all the superiors of the monasteries went up to pay him reverence; among them I went up with my superior. And the patriarch began to say to the Fathers, "Pray for me, Fathers, for I have been entrusted with a great burden, hard to bear, and the business of the patriarchate frightens me not a little. For it belongs to Peter and Paul and Moses, and to men like them, to shepherd rational souls; but I am a wretch. And more than all, the weight of the ordinations frightens me. For I have found it in writing that the blessed Leo (he says), who became president of the Church of the Romans, stayed forty days at the tomb of the apostle Peter, persevering in fasts and prayers, asking the apostle Peter to intercede for him with God, that his offences might be forgiven. And when the forty days were fulfilled, the apostle Peter appeared to him, saying, 'I have prayed for thee, and all thy sins have been forgiven thee, except for the ordinations. For this alone thou shalt be called to account: whether well, or otherwise, thou hast ordained those thou hast ordained.'"

## Notes
[^1]: T. Φοιβάμωνι as printed; the Latin names him *Philamonem*. Greek kept.
[^2]: T. Printed ἐρήμου πόλεως ("the desert city"), which names no place; read Ἑρμοῦ πόλεως with the Latin *apud Hermopolim* (see OCR table).
[^3]: T. Printed ὅρκοις σε πληροφορῶ, καὶ τὸν κατοικοῦντα: the oath wants a preposition (κατὰ / μὰ?) where καὶ stands; Latin *per eum qui in coelo habitat*. Rendered with the Latin.
[^4]: T. ὀνόματα, persons counted by name (cf. Acts 1:15).
[^5]: S. Luke 1:18–20.
[^6]: T. κανών here is the office of psalmody (Latin *psalmodiae canonem*); so throughout this chunk.
[^7]: P. The Kellia, the monastic settlement of the Cells in Lower Egypt.
[^8]: T. The line as printed has stray marks and no verb; Latin *quae Pharaoni pernicioso tyranno obnoxios faciunt*, "which make us subject to Pharaoh".
[^9]: S. Rom 6:13.
[^10]: S. Phil 3:13–14, freely.
[^11]: T. Printed ἐν τῷ ναῷ Ἀλεξανδρείας ("in the temple of Alexandria"); read ἐν τῷ ἐννάτῳ with the Latin *nono ab Alexandria milliario* and with p0119.19 below.
[^12]: T. The word breaks off at the line end (Κωνσταντι-) and the next line begins a new sentence: a line seems lost. The Latin here has "priests of the Church of Constantinople" and adds that Gennadius was very gentle, chaste in body and continent, before the story.
[^13]: T. τῶν τὰς ἀποκρίσεις διακονούντων: an apocrisiarius, a church's envoy; the Greek then calls him that.
[^14]: T. κόγχη, the niche or apse where the martyr's relics lay; Latin *sepulcrum*.
[^15]: T. Ἰουλιανὸν ἑωρακέναι τὸν μάρτυρα is ambiguous in the Greek; taken with the Latin *Julianum se vidisse martyrem*: the visitor was the martyr Julian, whose church it was, in the shape of his namesake the archdeacon.
[^16]: T. Text defective (ἀνδρί... with a gap and no main verb); Latin *fruitus sum contubernio domni Gregorii ... summi et singularis viri*. P: Gregory, later Pope Gregory the Great, then at Constantinople as papal envoy.
[^17]: S. Mark 12:42; Luke 21:2.
[^18]: P. Amos, patriarch of Jerusalem 594–601. The chapter head (CAPUT CXLIX) is printed across the full page and is missing from the Greek scaffold.
