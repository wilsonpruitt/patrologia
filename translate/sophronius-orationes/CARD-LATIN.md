## The three pieces

  when the Saracens held Bethlehem and the people of Jerusalem could not go out to it. Mai's Latin. It ends with
  Mai's own remark, set in the text column (*In codice Graeco, quo usus sum, deest orationis epilogus…*): render it as

- **so-021: Or. VI, ENCOMIUM OF THE HOLY ARCHANGELS AND ANGELS AND THE OTHER HEAVENLY POWERS** (cols 3315–3322,

## What changes
- **The scaffold.** Each page gives its Latin column(s) as tagged lines `pNNNN.L.LL` (left column) and
  `pNNNN.R.LL` (right column), from the archive.org OCR of Migne's page, cut to the column boxes. Left column first,
  then right. There is **no witness column**: you are reading the only text.
- **Column anchors.** Both columns on these pages are Latin and both are numbered: `[3201]` where the left column's
  text begins, `[3202]` where the right column's begins (numbers in each `### column NNNN` header). A piece that
  starts mid-page takes the number of the column it starts in.
- **The OCR table** now records **Latin** misreads of the scan: `| pNNNN.L.LL | printed | reading | sure | kind | why |`.
  This OCR is decent but not clean. Expect: `u`/`n` (cumulautem → cumulantem, ratioue → ratione, omuesque → omnesque),
  `c`/`e` (co → eo, cnim → enim), `ii`/`u`/`n`, `l`/`i`/`t`, æ/œ/ae read as `a`/`e`/`z`/`x` (letitia = lætitia),
  `rn`/`m`, a capital lost at a paragraph's first letter (`Liam` → `Piam`? read by sense, log it), hyphen breaks,
  Greek words in the Latin garbled. ⚠ Classical/medieval spellings Migne really prints (`caelum`/`cælum`, `quum`,
  `charitas`, `-tio`/`-cio`) are **not** errors. `print` = a real misprint of Migne's; `doubt` = you cannot tell.
  Expect roughly 10–30 rows per 1,000 Latin words. Report every one; the image check reads them all.
- **Band letters**: the A/B/C/D gutter letters are glued to Latin line ends too (`…ut ne, A`): `[b: X]` as in the
  card, not an OCR row.
- **Note calls** `(1)`, `(2)` and scripture footnote marks (`**?`, `*`, raised numbers) are Migne's apparatus, read
  separately: do not reproduce them. A footnote line leaked into the column (`3 Joan, xv, 13. 37 II Reg. …`) is kind
  `stray`.
- **Terms.** The Latin stands for a Greek you cannot see. Where its word clearly renders one of the glossary's Greek
  terms, use the glossary's English (*natura* = nature, *substantia/essentia* for οὐσία = essence, *persona* for
  πρόσωπον = person, *operatio* = operation, *Deipara/Dei Genitrix* = Theotokos, *Dei Mater* = Mother of God,
  *Praecursor* = Forerunner). Where you cannot tell what Greek it stands for, translate the Latin plainly and, for a
  theological term, give a T note with the Latin.
- **Scripture**: translate the Latin as printed (it is the editor's rendering of Sophronius's Greek citation), with
  an S note for the reference.
