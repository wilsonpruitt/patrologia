title: Synodical Letter to Sergius of Constantinople, 5
---
[3181] For this is why, as was observed by one of the wise, the whole brilliant and famous economy of the Only-begotten was brilliantly transacted: that he might both save the image and immortalize the flesh.[^1] Nor in this alone do the deranged go wrong and are driven aside from the straight road (for their impiety would even be bearable, as in a comparison of evils), but they also say countless other things against our apostolic and ancestral tradition: casting out the planting of paradise; not willing that Adam was formed in flesh; finding fault with the forming of Eve from him; rejecting the voice of the serpent; forbidding that the captaincies of the heavenly ranks were created thus by God from the beginning, and imagining them as arising from an earlier condemnation and deviation; dreaming, godlessly and mythically at once, that all rational beings were brought forth in a henad of minds;[^2] disparaging the creation of the waters above the heavens; wanting there to be an end of punishment; and introducing, on the one hand, the utter corruption of all sensible things, [3184] and asserting, on the other, the restoration of all rational beings, angels, men, demons, and pouring their differences back again into a mythical henad; when they also belch out, demonically, that Christ — the one they teach as doctrine in their babbling, not the one we piously proclaim — differs from us in nothing, in glory, or honour, or kingdom, or dominion. And bringing forth countless such things from the devilish and impious treasure of their heart, giving their neighbour to drink, not with one turbid overthrow only, but with countless,[^3] and slaying the souls of men, the wretches — souls for whom Christ deigned to die, and poured out his divine blood as their ransom, and laid down his own soul as a gift most divine beyond all worth. But we, having been given to drink the rational and guileless milk of right and blameless and sober faith, [b: B] and having tasted the good word of God,[^4] thrusting away all their dark doctrines, and being free of all these lawless drivellings of theirs, and treading in our fathers' footsteps, both speak of a consummation of the present world, and believe that that life to come, after the present life, lasts for ever, and hold the punishment to be unending: the one gladdening without cease the doers of the best works, the other paining without pause, nay more, punishing, those who here became lovers of baseness and were not willing to repent before their departure and going out from here. «For their worm shall not die,» says Christ the judge, the truth, «and their fire shall not be quenched.»[^5]

Having received from the apostolic and evangelic, from the prophetic and legal, from the paternal and didactic proclamation, most wise ones, to think and to believe these things, and having made them plain to you, the all-wise, and having hidden nothing from you, it is fitting next, and suitable, and in keeping with the ancient tradition, to make manifest in writing the holy synods of our fathers' and all-holy assemblies, which we cherish as light-guides of our souls and pray to have for ever, so that with them we may also share in the blessed life, as their noble children and successors. Four, then, great and holy ecumenical synods we receive upon the inspired doctrines of the Church, made radiant with evangelic brilliance and made splendid by the number of the evangelic characters. Of these we say the first in rank is the session at Nicaea of the three hundred and eighteen God-bearing Fathers, which, assembled at the divine nod, destroys the defilements of the rabies of Arius. After that one in time — not in glory and grace — the second assembly gathers, the one convened in the queen of cities. One hundred and fifty God-wise Fathers there were, [3185] [b: A] who, led by God, made up this assembly as well; which quenches the triple-thunderbolt impiety of Macedonius, Apollinarius and Magnus, and rescues the companies of the pious from so great and grievous a burning. Third after this, in time alone, I glorify the session that sat [?] at Ephesus,[^6] the former, by divine will (for the one called the second, Dioscorus's, has been caught out as consonant with the reprobate judgement of Eutyches); which first session was known as a full gathering of two hundred holy Fathers, and casts down Nestorius the man-worshipper and all his Christ-fighting impiety. And fourth after the three, in time alone, there gathers the God-wise company of the six hundred and thirty all-praised Fathers, torchbearers of the faith, which holds its divine convocation, prompted by God, at Chalcedon, and had Euphemia the martyr as its fellow-contestant,[^7] who to this day champions their definition of faith, and makes much and unceasing account of their famous and very great assembly.

And it slaughters the profane pair, Eutyches I mean and Dioscorus, and stops up their God-fighting malice, which flows down as from an Apollinarian spring and fills all the channels of impiety. And with their most impious heresy it casts out, through its orthodox pronouncements, the all-polluted heresy of Nestorius the God-fighter too; for it was assembled against this one as well, as still writhing in its shamelessness. Whence it also made it utterly dead, and ostracized it from the courts of the Church. And upon these four great and ecumenical, all-venerable and all-holy assemblies, equal in honour, of the holy and blessed Fathers, I receive also a fifth, another holy ecumenical synod, formed beside these and after these, the one that also took place in the queen of cities, when Justinian then wielded the sceptres of the Roman empire, and all its brilliant definitions. It was assembled to ratify the renowned synod at Chalcedon; and it abolishes, and casts out to destruction, first and foremost Origen the senseless, and all his dreamlike subtleties, and his writings full of impiety of many forms; and with him the doctrines of Evagrius and Didymus, and all their Hellenic and monstrous, nay more, mythical drivellings; after whom it plucks out Theodore of Mopsuestia, the teacher of Nestorius the God-fighter, and casts him out as a foul tare, with his blasphemous writings, from the catholic Church;[^8] and the treatises of Theodoret, composed wickedly and impiously against Cyril the champion of piety, and whatever he charged against the twelve chapters of the same inspired Cyril, and against the first holy synod at Ephesus, and against our right faith, gratifying Nestorius the impious; and it makes sharers in this condemnation those things too [3188] which he wrote in defence of Diodore and Theodore; along with which it also uprooted the letter of Ibas said to have been written to Mari the Persian,[^9] as not only adversary to right doctrines but also full of all impiety.

Those four holy synods, then, sacred and great and ecumenical, I embrace, and enfold with one mind; and upon these I honour this fifth as well, and revere it, and venerate it. And all their contents I gladly accept, both in doctrines and in various teachings, and in the anathemas and definitions against the heresy-makers; whence I both welcome and receive those whom they received and welcomed, and anathematize and reject as many as they subjected to anathema and held to be cast out of our catholic and holy Church. Following these holy and blessed five synods, I know one and only definition of faith, and one lesson I know, and creed, which the all-wise and blessed, the inspired multitude of the three hundred and eighteen God-bearing Fathers at Nicaea uttered from the Holy Spirit; which the assembly at Constantinople of the one hundred and fifty God-inspired Fathers ratified; and the first synod at Ephesus of the two hundred inspired Fathers confirmed; and the compact of the six hundred and thirty all-holy Fathers at Chalcedon received and strengthened, and declared piercingly that it be kept unwounded and unbroken and unshaken.

And we receive, too, with the same arms, and welcome, all the divine and God-wise writings of the inspired Cyril, as full of all rightness and destroying all the impiety of heretics; and above all the two synodical letters to Nestorius, the God-hated and God-driven, the second and the third, to which the twelve chapters are also joined; which burned up all the malice of Nestorius with coals equal in number to the holy apostles. With these I receive also the synodical letter written to the most holy presidents of the East, in which he also called their utterances holy and confirmed the peace with them. Numbered with these we count the very letters of the eastern presidents as well, as received by the divine Cyril himself and attested by him, in undisputed words, to be orthodox.[^10] Like these holy inscriptions of the all-wise Cyril I receive as holy and of equal honour, and as begetter of the same orthodoxy, the God-given and God-inspired letter also of the great and brilliant and God-minded Leo, luminary of the most holy Church of the Romans, or rather of all the Church under the sun; which, the divine Spirit plainly operating in him, he wrote against Eutyches the evil-minded and Nestorius the God-hated and deranged to Flavian, the celebrated president of the queen of cities;[^11] which I also call and define a pillar of orthodoxy, following the holy Fathers who so well defined it, as teaching us all orthodoxy, and destroying all heretical misbelief, and driving it out from the [3189] [b: A] God-guarded courts of our holy catholic Church. With this inspired composition and inscription I also accept all his letters and doctrines, as proceeding from the mouth of Peter the chief, and kiss them and embrace them, and enfold them with all my soul. Receiving these, as I said before — the holy five and divine sessions of the blessed Fathers, and all the writings of the all-wise Cyril, and especially those composed against the madness of Nestorius, and the composition of the eastern presidents written to the most divine Cyril himself and attested by him as orthodox, and whatever Leo, the most holy shepherd of the most holy Church of the Romans, has written, and especially what he composed against the Eutychian and Nestorian abomination — [b: B] the latter I know as definitions of Peter, the former as of Mark. Nay more, all the God-wise teachings of all the chosen mystagogues of our catholic Church, whether they are contained in discourses and writings or in any letters. And, to speak summarily, I receive and embrace all that our holy catholic Church accepts; and, contrariwise, I turn away from, and anathematize, and reckon profane, all that she too all-wisely abhors and holds hostile to her own piety: not only booklets, and little speeches, and God-fighting and spurious doctrines, but also heretical and misbelieving persons, the leaders of misbelieving heresies. And for your full assurance,[^12] leaving nothing out, I set out the very persons whom I anathematize and hold condemned, not with tongue and mouth only, but with heart and spirit too, as having been seen in every way to plot against our holy and catholic faith.

Let them, then, be anathema and a curse[^13] for ever from the holy and consubstantial and venerated Trinity, Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit: first Simon the Magus, the first of all to begin, most wickedly, the most wicked heresies; after him Cleobius, Menander, Philetus, Hermogenes, Alexander the coppersmith, Dositheus, Gortheus, Saturninus, Masbotheus, Hadrian, Basilides, Isidore his son and his superior in madness, Ebion, Carpocrates, Epiphanes, Prodicus, Cerinthus and Merinthus, Valentinus, Florinus, Blastus, Artemon, Secundus, Cassian, Theodotus, Heracleon, Ptolemy, Marcus, Colorbasus, Ademis of Carystus, Theodotus the cobbler, another Theodotus, Euphrates the Persian, Monoimus the Arab, Hermogenes, Tatian the Syrian, Severus, Asclepiodotus, Bardesanes, Harmonius his son and his equal in error, Hermophilus, Cerdo, Sacerdon, Marcion of Pontus, Apelles, Apollonides, Potitus, Prepon, Pithon, Syneros, Theodotus the banker, Montanus, Priscilla and Maximilla his frenzied disciples, Nepos, Elchasai, Origen, [3192] [b: A] another Origen, also called Adamantius,[^14] Sabellius the Libyan, Novatus, Paul of Samosata, Epigonus,[^15] Cleomenes, Noetus of Smyrna, Manes, who bears the name of his godless mania,[^16] Sabbatius, Arius, Meletius, Aetius, Eunomius, Asterius, Eudoxius, Donatus, Macedonius, who fought against the Holy Spirit and received the name of Pneumatomachus that he deserved;

## Notes
[^1]: S. Gregory Nazianzen, Or. 38.13 (= Or. 45.9), ἵνα καὶ τὴν εἰκόνα σώσῃ καὶ τὴν σάρκα ἀθανατίσῃ: "one of the wise".
[^2]: T. ἐν ἑνάδι νόων: the Origenist "henad", the original unity of minds before their fall; the Latin has *in unitate mentium*.
[^3]: S. Hab 2:15, ὁ ποτίζων τὸν πλησίον αὐτοῦ ἀνατροπῇ θολερᾷ.
[^4]: S. 1 Pet 2:2, τὸ λογικὸν ἄδολον γάλα; Heb 6:5, καλὸν γευσαμένους Θεοῦ ῥῆμα.
[^5]: S. Isa 66:24 exactly (οὐ τελευτήσει … οὐ σβεσθήσεται), put in Christ's mouth after Mark 9:48.
[^6]: T. ἐνεδρεῦσαν, as printed, means "lying in wait", which cannot be the sense here; the Latin has *consedit*. The plate perhaps reads a form of ἑδρεύω ("sit"). The English gives "sat" and marks it [?].
[^7]: P. Euphemia of Chalcedon, the martyr in whose church the synod of 451 met; by tradition she ratified its definition.
[^8]: T. τῆς καθολικῆς Ἐκκλησίας: the Latin reads *ab apostolica Ecclesia*.
[^9]: P. The "Three Chapters" condemned by the fifth synod (553): Theodore's person and writings, Theodoret's writings against Cyril, and the letter of Ibas of Edessa to Mari the Persian.
[^10]: S. Cyril, Ep. 4 and Ep. 17 to Nestorius (the latter with the twelve chapters); Ep. 39 to John of Antioch and the Easterners (433, *Laetentur caeli*), which accepts their formulary of union.
[^11]: S. Leo, Ep. 28, the Tome to Flavian of Constantinople (449).
[^12]: T. πρὸς πληροφορίαν ἡμῶν, "our assurance", in the scaffold; the English reads ὑμῶν with the Latin *ad vestram satisfactionem* (see the OCR row).
[^13]: T. Ἀνάθεμα … καὶ κατάθεμα: "anathema and a curse"; καταθεματίζω is "curse" throughout. The Latin transliterates both words (*anathema … et catathema*).
[^14]: P. Two Origens, as in Epiphanius (Panarion 63–64): an obscure Origen, and Origen "also called Adamantius".
[^15]: T. Printed Ἐπίγενος (Latin *Epigenus*); given in the standard form, Epigonus (Noetus's teacher, Hippolytus, Ref. 9.7). The two Hermogenes and three Theodoti are as printed.
[^16]: T. Μάνης ὁ τῆς ἀθέου μανίας ἐπώνυμος: the pun on Mani and μανία is the Greek's own.
