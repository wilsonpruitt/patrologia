title: On the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos, 1–10
---
[3217] ON THE ANNUNCIATION OF THE MOST HOLY THEOTOKOS
*An oration of Sophronius, patriarch of Jerusalem, among the saints.*[^1]

Give the blessing, Father.[^2]

1. Good tidings, brethren, good tidings, and again I will say,[^3] good tidings! — not because they are
higher than all good tidings, and more divine and finer, and more excellent by incomparable pre-eminences;
nor because they show me, their messenger, beautiful, and make and work my feet beautiful[^4] (for they are
better than all good things, and kinder than all kind things, and more wonderful than all wonders, and more
compassionate than all compassionate things, and more fearful and more awful than all fearful and awful
things); but because, being tripled, they also declare the supreme and divine numbering of the blessed and
sovereign Trinity. For everything that is tripled in sacred and divine doctrines, [b: B] or indeed in
God-wise proclamations and august announcements, is set down as an honour of the blessed Trinity — of
Father, I mean, and Son and Holy Spirit; which both is a Trinity and is beheld as a Monad, and is a Monad
and is found to be a Trinity: the one in the three hypostases, the other in the one and only Godhead, that
is, essence and nature.

2. For one is the essence of the superessential and life-originating Trinity proclaimed, as being brought
up into one and only Godhead. Wherefore also it holds its consubstantiality, and its kinship and sameness of
stock, unbroken and firm and unscattered, nay more, unmultiplied. For the holy Trinity, which holds sway
over all things that are and rules them, and royally and divinely has lordship over all created things, is
numbered in its own hypostases — for it is a Trinity, and is believed to be — but in essence and nature,
and likewise in Godhead too, it is above all numbering. For it is not carried out to a triadic enumeration
in respect of essence as well, because it makes the numbering of its own personal hypostases threefold.
And for this reason it is both shown partible in its hypostases, and remains in respect of nature
impartible, and is proclaimed divided in its persons. For each of these appears singly,[^5] and [3220]
[b: A] it is in nature undivided. Whence it possesses the division and the union in a manner truly
paradoxical and astounding: because neither, on account of the One, which the nature is, is it poured
together and driven to one and only hypostasis; nor, on account of the Three, which are enhypostatic
persons, is it cut asunder in alien and total fashion; but it is unbroken and impartible, refusing every
partition and every pairing.

3. For this is both the wonder of it and the most awful thing: that, being one and the same according to
nature, and being one God and one Godhead, it is found three in the persons, not cutting the nature or the
Godhead, but widening its own hypostases and persons into a numbering that is not confused. For as the
Father is God, so the Son is God, so the Holy Spirit is God. And in so far as each is called God, it
presents the one essence of the three persons; but in so far as they are Father and Son and all-holy Spirit
(for these are precisely the names of the three persons and of its truly three hypostases, by which this
blessed Trinity is distinguished one from another without partition of nature), they are truly three and
are believed to be, and by all the faithful, and by the heavenly and invisible powers, they are proclaimed
with a loud voice and glorified: hymned in three sanctifications, but coming together into one natural
lordship. For «Holy, holy, holy, Lord Sabaoth,»[^6] the six-winged Seraphim have cried aloud, who know the
words of theology better than we, as drawing nearer to God than we, and partaking of his pure radiance, and
shone upon by undefiled beams. For thrice by them is the thrice-holy Trinity hallowed, and with three
sanctifications and doxologies it is honoured; and into one lordship it is gathered without confusion by
the cry «Lord Sabaoth». For as the «Holy, holy, holy», sung out triply by the Seraphim themselves, shows the
hymned numbering of the three persons of the blessed Trinity, so the «Lord Sabaoth», said as one and only
once,[^7] shows forth their one and only lordship, according to which the three are also one God without
confusion, and are glorified so, and the one Godhead is honoured [3221] in the Trinity. For why did they not
fall silent [b: A] having said it only once? nor, having cried it twice, hold their peace, but take up the
«Holy» three times? Because the hypostasis of the Trinity is not one and only — away with the abomination of
the Sabellian impiety! — nor are its persons two and only — away with the godlessness of the Macedonian
foulness![^8] — but it cries thrice, as I said, and shouts the «Holy», and offers it with lips that never
cease, and announces it with mouths that are never silent, since three are the hypostases of the blessed
Trinity, most sovereign of all things, so defined, and three are its persons, kindred and of one stock,
which have one Godhead in equal honour, and one lordship in equal strength, and one rule over all things
and a kingdom unbroken.

4. Wherefore we too, the least, who are its most genuine worshippers and servants, wishing irrepressibly to
proclaim good tidings to you, speak triple good tidings, saying, Good tidings, good tidings, and again
crying, Good tidings, bringing in here too the honour of the blessed Trinity, even if the discourse is not
triadic but of the economy, and concerns the One of that same all-august Trinity, and announces to us only
the coming of the Word. For it is good, and most dear to all the faithful, to announce both in either, and
to keep the declaration of each unconfused: that is, both in the songs of the economy to proclaim triadic
words, and in the triadic, that is theological, announcements to bring in the words of the economy (since
the Word too is known to be one of the Trinity, he who undertook the human economy and saved the whole lump
of us men) — yet with distinct and properly fashioned thoughts, nay more, appellations too, lest from this
there be born any mixing and pouring-together of the theological and triadic proclamations and of the
economic and other-kind appellations, on account of the condescension of the Word and God to us lowly ones.

5. So let them be driven from this our holy festival — the Sabelliuses, Marcelluses, Ariuses, Eunomiuses,
Eudoxiuses, Asteriuses,[^9] and as many as are like them and of one cult with them in godlessness, who
[3224] mix the words of theology with the doctrines of the economy, and are mixed and confused by their own
selves, and are unable to find the truth; and indeed the Peters too, and the Severuses,[^10] and the whole
myriad-lipped and myriad-tongued heresy of the Acephali,[^11] who, through their most impious and foul
antipathy to us, the faithful and godly-minded, wove a cross into the thrice-holy cry and confused the words
of the unconfused Trinity. For they too, saying three times the «Holy, holy, holy», impiously add a cross to
the three sanctifications. For if the Thrice-holy is by nature the Trinity's own hymn, how do they fit a
cross to it so untaught, who are truly untaught in divine wisdom?

For the cross is not proclaimed of the Trinity; but of the Son incarnate with a man-loving incarnation, the
One of the Trinity, is both the cross itself and the crucifixion, the cross proclaiming his saving death,
which for our sake he underwent in the flesh, being crucified.

6. For the wretches, not speaking of the Word's incarnation, nor naming the union of essences of different
kind, of Godhead, I mean, and manhood, nor yet proclaiming his birth from the Virgin, divine and in the
proper sense Theotokos,[^12] all of which must go before the cross, lawlessly bring in the cross, wishing to
mingle their foul theopaschism with the sacred doctrines.

For having sickened with the whole confusion of Eutyches, the foul and senseless, and having drunk the whole
many-mixed cup, they confuse all things, mingling the things of the economy with the theological, crushing
the theological into the things of the economy, driving both natures [3225] [b: A] into one nature,
conjoining the two essences into one essence, casting each of the forms, of Godhead and of manhood, out of
their own doctrines, and wanting Christ's form and essence and nature to be one; so that neither the words
of theology may remain pure and untouched, nor the doctrines of the economy go free of their Eutychian — or
rather, ill-starred[^13] — change and confusion. But let those men, having tumbled headlong into the depth
of impiety, be mixed up there, and be washed round by the streams of impiety.

7. But we, the nurslings of piety, and the opponents of their headless quackery, let us hold genuinely to our
fathers' doctrines; and becoming sleepless guardians of [b: B] the apostolic traditions, knowing the triadic
words and recognizing the words of the economy, let us neither confuse these with those, nor mix those with
these, but fit to each what is fitting, and mark off for each its own place and manner and time. And so we
shall be outside scandals, and without communion in the error of heretics, and free of the madness of the
heterodox; and we shall stand upon the inspired faith, whirled about by none of the evil spirits, nor
carried off towards evil thoughts, into which our opponents, having thrust away the standing well, and given
up the safe keeping of their fathers' piety, have pitiably fallen, and have perished, and are tossed by the
waves of heretical impiety.

8. Therefore we, having well fled the abyss of their impiety, and set our feet with safety on the unshaken
rock of the faith, and going up into a high mountain, and standing on the summits of doctrine according to
the evangelic and prophetic ordinances, and lifting up our voice, as we have strength — or rather as God may
grant — let us cry, Good tidings, good tidings,[^14] and again we say, Good tidings, both honouring the
reverence due to the Trinity, and singing the condescension to us of the One of the Trinity, God the Word.
For these, brethren, are our good tidings, which we have come forward to bring as good tidings to you, most
faithful, and wish to proclaim today.

And we urge your love, having fittingly cleansed itself, to receive these our good tidings. For him who has
cleansed himself as he ought, these sacred good tidings of ours do not loathe, nor indeed turn away from; but
they make a saving abode with him,[^15] and fill him with light and illumination, and make him a divine
precinct of God, being by nature a man, yet rich in the manifestation of God.

[3228] All of you, gather; all of you, assemble; all of you, make haste; all of you, run together; all of
you, flock together; all of you, outrun one another, putting on yourselves the wings of a dove, so that you
may fly, and rest well upon Christ;[^16] or taking the feet of the swift-running gazelle, that you may hear
heavenly good tidings, that you may believe an angel speaking good tidings, that you may listen with
God-loving ears to one announcing the coming of God to men; that we too, seeing your eager gathering and
your most ready assembly to hear our lofty good tidings, rejoicing and glad together, may cry: Good tidings,
good tidings, good tidings, confirming the assurance by the threefoldness of the proclamation of the good
tidings.

9. For where the truth goes forth, neither does falsehood ever keep its citizenship, nor will unbelief have
room for its most evil entry; for nothing is harder than unbelief towards divine words. Thence the Jews have
fallen; thence the Samaritans have gone astray; thence the Greeks pass their life in darkness; thence the
heretics were cast down, not believing the good tidings of God, not yielding to the good tidings of God,
remaining set against them and opposed, and being put to death in souls and bodies, and shown to have no
communion in eternal life, and no share in the true light. For falsehood never lays hold of the truth, so
long as it is falsehood and is known to be; nor is darkness ever enlightened, so long as it is darkness and
has grown murky, and in every way has no portion of light-bearing illuminations.[^17] For this reason our
luminous good tidings are proclaimed with a loud voice to you who receive the light; for they do not wish
to be laid up in the ears of unbelievers, nor love to make their dwelling in darkened hearts.

10. You then, as children of the light and light-nurtured offspring,[^18] hear our luminous and illuminating
good tidings. For you were both enlightened in their power, and shone upon by their God-wise mystery in
luminous souls, and received it in pure hearts, and became children of God, receiving the Son of God
faithfully and gladly, and recognizing him as your only Saviour. «For as many as received him,» it says,
«to them gave he power to become children of God, even to them that believe on his name: which were born,
not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God.»[^19]

## Notes
[^1]: T. The printed heading: Τοῦ ἐν ἁγίοις πατριάρχου Ἱεροσολύμων Σωφρονίου λόγος εἰς τὸν Εὐαγγελισμὸν τῆς
ὑπεραγίας Θεοτόκου. The fixed English title stands in capitals; the heading's attribution clause follows it
in italics, as printed.
[^2]: T. Εὐλόγησον, Πάτερ: the reader's request for the blessing before the homily, a liturgical formula
rendered by its received English (house rule).
[^3]: S. Phil 4:4, πάλιν ἐρῶ.
[^4]: S. Isa 52:7 / Rom 10:15, the beautiful feet of the bringer of good tidings; ἄγγελος here is the
messenger, and the preacher claims the verse for himself.
[^5]: T. Ἰδίᾳ γὰρ τούτων ἕκαστον φαίνεται, καὶ τὴν φύσιν ἀδιαίρετος: the Latin expands, *quoad hasce enim
singulas, distincta, quoad naturam vero indiscreta apparet*. The scaffold's p0223.43 (`νοταΕ.`) sits between
τὴν and φύσιν; the sense runs on without a lost line (see the OCR row).
[^6]: S. Isa 6:2–3, the six-winged Seraphim and their cry.
[^7]: T. μίαν, καὶ μόνον λεγόμενον: μίαν stands without a noun (understand φωνήν, the one cry); the Latin
read the same (*unicam, semelque pronuntiatum*). Rendered as printed.
[^8]: P. The followers of Macedonius of Constantinople, who denied the Spirit's Godhead and so counted two
persons.
[^9]: P. Marcellus of Ancyra; Eudoxius, Arian bishop of Constantinople; Asterius the Sophist (all
fourth-century). The plurals are the Greek's (Σαβέλλιοι, Μάρκελλοι …), and the Latin keeps them.
[^10]: P. Peter the Fuller of Antioch, who added "who was crucified for us" to the Thrice-holy (the cross
"woven into" it here), and Peter Mongus of Alexandria; Severus of Antioch.
[^11]: P. The Acephali: the Monophysite "headless", those without a patriarch; § 7's "headless quackery"
(ἀκεφάλου τερθρείας) plays on the name.
[^12]: T. Θεοτόκος is kept as Theotokos, because Sophronius also writes Θεομήτωρ (Mother of God); the two
stay distinguishable throughout.
[^13]: T. Εὐτυχιανῆς … μᾶλλον δὲ δυστυχοῦς: a pun on Eutyches' name ("fortunate"); the Latin keeps it,
*eutychiana … seu potius dystychiana*.
[^14]: S. Isa 40:9, the bringer of good tidings who goes up into a high mountain and lifts up his voice with
strength.
[^15]: S. John 14:23, μονὴν παρ᾿ αὐτῷ ποιησόμεθα.
[^16]: S. Ps 54:7 (LXX), the dove's wings, to fly and be at rest; the gazelle's feet, Ps 17:34 (LXX).
[^17]: S. John 1:5, καὶ ἡ σκοτία αὐτὸ οὐ κατέλαβεν.
[^18]: S. Eph 5:8; 1 Thess 5:5, children of light.
[^19]: S. John 1:12–13, as printed.
