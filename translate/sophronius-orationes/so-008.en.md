title: On the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos, 10–19
---
[3228] You then, as most radiant children of God, hear also the most radiant good tidings of God, through
which you have become children of God by grace, and have obtained a heavenly nobility, casting off the
earthy ignobility, and have taken on a divine birth, putting away the human descent. You then, hear the
sacred good tidings, you who have been counted worthy of the divine initiation into their most perfect
faith.

[3229] But what are our good tidings? or what is their achievement? Listen faithfully as we speak. For we
shall speak most fittingly, as he himself gives to us, he who through us now brings good tidings to you.

11. So God, having in the beginning created out of what was not the universe, all that is after him and was
brought by him into being, things visible and invisible, sensible and intelligible, heavenly and earthly,
creates man too after his own image, framing him of a rational soul and a body; whom he also made the image
and likeness of his own goodness,[^1] granting him a self-determining life, and making him a dweller in a
divine paradise; and having fashioned him good, he laid down for him a divine and saving commandment, so
that he might not be puffed up by the greatness of the image, nor, from his likeness to God, fancy for
himself in his folly the nature of God. But he, having received such gifts from God who fashioned him (for
he had been set forth by him as king of the things on earth, while plainly ruled as king and governed by
God himself who created him, as all created things are too), did not bear the greatness of the gifts, but
is eager to take sameness with God, and the created man desired to be God; and wickedly fancying, by the
deceit of the serpent, the divine and lofty and supreme things, he is borne down to death and corruption,
and falls a most terrible fall, he who wished to snatch the dignity of the divine nature. For this [b: C]
was also most just, that he who wished to exalt himself beyond his worth should be humbled with the utmost
humbling. Since also «whosoever exalteth himself shall be humbled»,[^2] as one who wickedly tries to
snatch what God has not given him. «For no man taketh the honour unto himself,» as the great Apostle said,
«but he that is called of God who giveth it.»[^3]

12. So he was held by death, corrupted along with corruption, coming forth from the earth, he who made bold
to snatch the honour of God, and dragged back again into the earth; today born by a fleshly birth, and
tomorrow put to death by a fleshly death; today seen among the living, and tomorrow numbered among the dead.
And this penalty man bore, who wished to wrong God, suffering it of his own counsel and purpose, having used
ill his self-determination, which he had received when he was honoured by the one who created him, for his
most comely likeness to him and the splendour of the image. «For God made not death, neither rejoiceth he
in the destruction of the living.»[^4]

[3232] And truly pitiable and most wretched was he who of old was blessed: when he appeared as the possessor
of paradise, when he had been set as paradise's first dweller, when he had a life without toil and without
labour, when he passed his days naked and without a tunic, when he did not fear the changes of the air,
when he did not know the freezing of the cold, when he was not acquainted with the blaze of the burning
heat, when going about naked he felt no shame either; when he did not receive the assaults of passions,
when he did not feel the pain of sicknesses, when he was released from human slavery, when he had been
freed from barbarian tyranny, when he did not hear the harassments of tax-gatherers, when he did not see the
encounters of creditors, when he did not dread the mangling of wild beasts, when he possessed the utmost
well-being, when he was fed on the fruits of paradise, when he enjoyed all good things ungrudgingly, when he
received the radiance of God, when, though by nature created, he reigned over earthly creatures, when he
bore the name of God's image, and was wonderfully made bright with the likeness of God.

Of all these he is orphaned, having transgressed the saving commandment of God, as one who had stumbled
against God; and he is made naked and a stranger to his former splendour, and from paradise he is at once
banished, and to the earth again, having become corruptible through sin, he is returned; and through the
multitude of his folly he is likened to the beasts,[^5] putting off the former honour with which God who
created him had beautified him. So he is a slave to corruption and death and passions, having thought to be
rich in Godhead by tyranny.

13. But he who created him and brought him forth when before he was not, seeing him in such a state, pitied
him; and having pitied, had mercy; and having had mercy, is moved with compassion; and being moved with
compassion, as loving man — being, as he is, good and loving man — he rises up with a divine compassion for
his salvation and recall; and he fulfils a counsel ancient and loving man, which he had counselled in love
for man from the beginning. For he did not bear, as the moulder, to look on his moulding being corrupted,
and perishing into earth and corruption.

14. And what is the counsel that God counselled for the salvation and redemption of men? Let Isaiah the
wonderful come forward and cry it, he who foretold the seedless conception of God, and recounted beforehand
his virgin birth; who, at the fulfilment of the divine purpose, singing to God a song of thanksgiving, cried
aloud: «O Lord, my God, I will glorify thee; I will hymn thy name, for thou hast done wonderful things,
[3233] [b: A] an ancient counsel, true; so be it. For thou hast made strong cities a heap, that their
foundations should fall; the city of the ungodly shall never be built. Therefore shall the poor people bless
thee; and cities of wronged men shall bless thee. For thou hast been a helper to every lowly city, and a
shelter to them that are disheartened through want. From evil men thou shalt deliver them.»[^6]

These, brethren, are our divine good tidings; these we bring as good tidings to you today, not as things
about to happen afterwards, but as things already divinely done, which have saved man from his condemnation,
and brought him back to his former brightness [b: B] and blessedness, and made him a friend of God as
before.

15. And how is our salvation wrought by God? or how is the recall accomplished? Hear with understanding and
all wisdom (for these things are wise, and more than wise, and beyond all human wisdom), taking up a divine
mind, and one worthy of a divine promise. He who moulded man from the beginning and made him after his own
image judges to save man and redeem his own image by no other means than by becoming man by nature, and
making his own image his own garment. Hence the unmingled are mingled; hence the undiluted are blended
without dilution; hence the uncompounded are compounded; hence the unconnected are bound fast; hence the
divine things become human, that the human may become more divine; hence the Word of God, remaining God and
the Word of God, is made thick;[^7] hence the uncreated, as begotten of the uncreated God, remaining
uncreated, is created; hence the Most High is announced lowly, and the invisible is beheld visible, and the
intangible admits touch; and the bodiless comes forth embodied, and the fleshless is recorded as flesh; and
the impassible is proclaimed passible; and he who has an immortal nature is declared mortal by nature, that
he might lead passible and mortal manhood to a bright immortality and [3236] impassibility, and make kin to
God and the Father the manhood that was hostile to God and at war with him.

16. And how is this fulfilled, and done, and carried through into a deed that does not lie? For it is
fearful and astounding, and most incredible to the ears of men who understand the most divine things by
human reasonings. But where God is proclaimed, the paltriness of human reasonings is idle. For all things
are most easy for God, and possible, and readily provided, whatever he might wish to do. And this is the
most conspicuous and singular mark of the divine and supreme power, and one wholly incommunicable to every
created nature; for hence the things above human nature are wonderful and astounding, and surpass every
created power.

And how is this accomplished by God, and brought to a renowned end? Luke the wondrous teaches us clearly,
who writes our Gospel, the most sacred of all sacred things, so that none may disbelieve the greatness of
the deed, so that none may falsely charge God with weakness, being himself weak and powerless, and unfit for
every more divine action. As you heard but now when he cried aloud: «In the sixth month the angel Gabriel
was sent from God unto a city of Galilee, named Nazareth, to a Virgin espoused to a man whose name was
Joseph, of the house of David; and the Virgin's name was Mary.»[^8] Hearest thou that God sent him, and
wilt thou disbelieve the deed? Disbelieve not the ordinance of God, but rather believe most piously that God
is most mighty to fulfil whatever he may wish to do. And by whom else could an angel be sent, but wholly by
God alone, who made him and gave him essence out of what was not, and fashioned all angelic nature? If then
God is discerned as the sender, and an angel is proclaimed as the one sent, how is what is said not true?
Let all marvel at these things; but let them not disbelieve the proclamation, knowing the strength of God,
that it is all-mighty and truly almighty.

17. And what does the most blessed angel, being sent, say to the Virgin who knew not man? or how does he
announce to her the declaration of such good tidings? and all that such a mighty work of God brings to birth
through her? «Hail, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee.»[^9] With joy the angel of joy
begins to her. For he knew, and understood, that his message becomes the procurer of joy to all men, and
likewise to all created things, and the destroyer of all grief for all; for he knew that from this [3237]
[b: A] the knowledge of God would shine on the world; he knew that the mist of error would vanish; he knew
that the sting of death would be blunted; he knew that the power of corruption would be corrupted; he knew
that the victory of Hades would be pulled down; he knew that perishing man would be saved, who of old had
been a slave to these, since he was driven from the paradise of luxury and driven out of the blessed sojourn
there. For this reason he makes joy the prelude of his good tidings; for this reason he speaks joy first of
his words; for this reason joy went before these good tidings, as they would be joy to all who believe. For
the divine messages of joy had to begin from joy-making words and sayings. For this reason the angel utters
joy before all, knowing the outcome of his own good tidings, and that his discourse, as it was carried out,
would appear a joy to all the world.

18. For what joy and delight does it not surpass, the message that came to the blessed Virgin, the mother of
joy too? Hail, O bearer of the heavenly joy;[^10] hail, O midwife of the supreme joy; hail, O mother-city of
the saving joy; hail, O cause of the immortal joy; hail, O mystic lodging of the unspeakable joy; hail, O
admirable ploughland of the ineffable joy; hail, O all-blessed spring of the unfailing joy; hail, O
God-bearing treasure of the everlasting joy; hail, O most flourishing [b: C] plant of the life-giving joy;
hail, O unwedded Mother of God; hail, O Virgin unrobbed after childbirth; hail, O spectacle most marvellous
of all marvels. Who will be able to tell thy splendour? Who would dare to speak thy wonder? Who will make
bold to proclaim thy greatness? Thou hast adorned the nature of men; thou hast conquered the ranks of
Angels; thou hast hidden the radiances of the Archangels; thou hast shown the presidencies of the Thrones
second to thee; thou hast made small the height of the Dominions; thou hast outrun the leaderships of the
Principalities; thou hast unnerved[^11] the strength of the Authorities; thou hast come forth a power
mightier than the Powers; thou hast surpassed with earthly eyes the many-eyed of the Cherubim; thou hast
overpassed the six-winged of the Seraphim with the God-moved wings of the soul; and thou hast excelled [b: D]
all creation, as shining with purity more than all creation, and receiving the Creator of all creation, and
bearing him in the womb and giving him birth, and being become Mother of God out of all creation.

19. [3240] For this reason I address thee, «Hail, thou that art full of grace», since thou hast been graced
more than all creation; and I recognize and know the cause of such joy and grace in thee; wherefore I add,
crying aloud and uttering it: «The Lord is with thee»: he who, as Creator, is lord of all creation, but is
with thee, as carried in thy womb, and borne in the womb with an unutterable child-bearing. «The Lord is
with thee»: he who was begotten everlastingly of the Father, and is ever beheld with the Father, but now
comes to be with thee in the conception, and has wrought from thee a fearful incarnation. «The Lord is with
thee»: he who has a first, everlasting birth, and receives a second birth from thee. «The Lord is with
thee»: he who with the Father is lord of all creation, but [b: B] from thee puts on the form of a
servant,[^12] and frees mankind from slavery. For this is why the Lord becomes a servant, that he may show
the servant a lord by grace. «The Lord is with thee»: he who formerly was outside all creation, but now is
seen with thee, the created one, and through thee has become numbered with created things; for in thee the
uncreated is created, and from thee the Creator comes forth created. I see the uncreated become a creature
in thee, and I behold the fleshless made perfect as flesh.
