title: On the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos, 29–35
---
[3252] «Behold, thou shalt conceive in the womb»[^1] a conception without seed, a conception unutterable, a conception ineffable, a conception divine, which no coming-together of a man has tilled, which no union of a male has wrought, which there is no one among men able to declare. For how shall those who are born according to nature have strength to tell the things that are above nature? Since then thy conception is supernatural, do not examine the reasonings that are according to nature.

«And thou shalt bear a son,» not only temporal and perishable, but indeed also eternal, and more primal than all things of time; «and thou shalt bear a son,» not only beheld as fleshly, but indeed also incorporeal; «and thou shalt bear a son,» not only appearing as thine, the Virgin's, but indeed also known as Son of God; «and thou shalt bear a son,» not only made known as man, but also believed true God; «and thou shalt bear a son,» not in need of salvation from another, but bestowing [b: D] salvation on all men.[^2] [3253] [b: A] «And» for this reason «thou shalt call his name Jesus,»[^3] which name is interpreted Saviour. For it was needful, O Virgin, that the Saviour of all, being born, should himself be allotted the name of the Saviour; that from the very name the power of him who was born might be shown, and the saving delivery of thy child be signified to all men.

30. «He shall be great,» as being called «Angel of great counsel,» and hymned «mighty God,» and celebrated «wonderful counsellor» «of the Father,» and appearing «Prince of peace,» and having authority, as Maker, over all creation, and making peace for the heavenly things with the earthly. For the achievements of his own rule he bears upon his own shoulder and arm; for not by another's power, but rather by his own, does he accomplish what is done, making a peaceful settlement well up for all. But he is also «father of the age to come,» the only one begotten of the only God, the begetter, and being father of the age to come, and glorified before all ages with the Father himself. Concerning whom Isaiah also, anticipating, prophesied: «A child, he says, is born to us, and a son is given to us» — thy child, O Virgin, whose birth I now announce to thee — «whose rule is upon his shoulder; and his name is called Angel of great counsel, Wonderful, Counsellor, mighty God, Potentate, Prince of peace, Father of the age to come.»[^4]

«He shall be great»: not as one who is not great, and afterwards will become so, but as one who is always great, and after these things also will become so. For being made known as Son by nature of the great God and Father, he too is defined as great, and will be proclaimed God by nature; for being connatural with God the begetter, he will surely also have identity in essence with thee.[^5] For God, in begetting, would never beget a Son unequal to himself and unlike, but surely connatural and wholly like, and in all natural things without variation.

31. «He shall be great, and shall be called Son of the Most High.»[^6] For great he is always, and is named Son of the most high God, as being proclaimed from the great God himself, and his only-begotten Son. But "he shall be," I say, and "shall be called," not as though he were not these things — do not so suspect; but on account of the much-wondered birth from thee I cry these things, and the fleshly becoming man from thee. For "shall be" and "shall be called," being by nature temporal appellations, are said of him as having become man after the manner of you men, since in truth he also goes forth after your manner. [3256] [b: A] Likewise also the saying, «The Lord God shall give him the throne of David his father; and he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.»[^7] For all these things are uttered for the sake of the fleshly birth from thee. For having once endured to become man for your sakes, after your manner, he also accepts to hear and to suffer all human things for your sakes, that he may not be thought a phantom by those who hear, that it may not seem a seeming to those who see, that he may show by deeds the greatness of his true condescension towards you, that he may present clear proofs of his own becoming man for your sakes. Since how shall a man who has not been allotted an earthy delivery, and borne an earthy lowliness (for what is lowlier than dust?), either become great, [b: B] or be called true Son of the Most High? Or how should he inherit the throne of David without successor? Or how should he reign over the house of Jacob always, and his kingdom have no end? For David and Jacob are interpreted as Christ himself, taken for mystical contemplation. For he himself has been established mighty of hand, that is, in power, and he supplanted your adversaries, and granted you the victory over them.[^8] Through whom also those of men who believe in him will fittingly supplant the people of the Jews of old, and will carry off its birthright in time, as having believed Christ well.

But being these things according to the eternal nature and the incomprehensible birth from the Father, on account of the incarnation from thee, the godly-minded, he is said also to receive them; that what he himself is according to the immortal nature, these he may grant to you also according to the divine grace, to whom he has also, in his love for man, been born consubstantial according to the flesh — that from thee, the passible and mortal.

32. But the God-receiving Virgin, bearing in the womb, and all-pure, and excelling all creation together, [3257] [b: A] hearing of a divine conception, and learning of a wonderful child-bearing, and receiving the good tidings of a paradoxical childbirth, wondering at the things said, and knowing the nature of women, and examining naturally their conceptions, and judging wisely their childbirths, that these never come about apart from a man's coming-together, and seeing herself untouched, and untasting of such intercourse even so far as a thought, says to Gabriel the archangel, who was declaring to her these magnificent things, the great matters of God, and speaking the good tidings of joy: «And how shall this be to me, since I know not a man?»[^9] How shall I believe thy words, which are beyond human nature? Very many women who have given birth I have seen, and without union with males I have seen none giving birth. And how shall these things come about in me, who have not run the natural courses? And how do I become mother of a male son, I who wholly know not conjunction with a male? Perhaps thou thinkest of me, O angel, since I have been betrothed to a man according to the law of Moses, that I have also seen a human bed, from which conceptions and child-bearings and childbirths spring? Not yet have I made the marriage coming-together with my betrothed and guardian. And how dost thou declare to me conception and birth, and surely the suckling of a child to be born?

33. Rather, art thou ignorant, O minister of good tidings beyond nature, that Mary was not reared with men, nor kept company with male children? Know the brightness of my virginity, and thou wilt not tell me of a conception that knows no man. Know the greatness of my purity, and thou wilt not speak to me of an unwedded child-bearing. Know the blamelessness of my chastity, and thou wouldst not tell me of an unnatural childbirth. Know my untouched cleanness, and thou wilt not cry to me of an incredible suckling.

Rather, hast thou not learned that from my very first birth I was reared in the Holy of Holies,[^10] where a man is not found, where none of men enters, where no passage of males is espied? Know the reverence of my way of life, and do not speak to me of fleshly thoughts. Know the all-pureness of my manner of living, and do not suspect in me the customs of mothers.

Virginity and marriage are not the same, O angel; [3260] celibacy and union are not the same; all-holy purity, and the bed of female and male, are not the same. For though «marriage» is established «honourable, and the bed» is, according to the law of Moses, «undefiled,»[^11] yet the grace of virginity lies far above these. Far is the difference of these from that; and these things admit no comparison at all with one another. How then dost thou thyself, leaving the tokens of virginity, announce the propositions of marriage and the bed?

For these reasons I dread thy message; for these reasons I fear thy address; for these reasons I do not admit thy greeting, without prudent searching, without wise scrutiny, without true examination, lest, believing faithless words, I too be deceived in mind, and walk in the footsteps of my mother Eve, and become for wretched men the matchmaker[^12] of greater evils and worse misfortunes. [3261] Hence I answer thee again, according to nature: «And how shall this be to me, since I know not a man,» and shall bear a man, as thou saidst? An incredible message: the word is foreign to the sequence of words, and alien to common custom. Wherefore I would never receive it, unless thou tell a consistent account of the outcome of the things said by thee; for apart from the rendering of an account, nothing of the kind could ever come about at all. Give then an account of thy message, and then I will believe thy words. Eve's transgression makes me more sober and more prudent; into which that deceitful hissing of the serpent, the author of evil, brought her down, being guileless and unfeigned and having no part in the searching of reasonings. A second Eve, then, I would never become, believing unaccustomed words.

34. Having heard these things from the virginal and blameless mouth speaking to him, [b: B] the archangel answers her gently again; and of the things puzzled over according to nature he furnishes the solution supernaturally, and releases the all-pure one from the searching of reasonings.

I know, O Virgin, and am persuaded, that thou hast not beheld the bed of a man; I see the stainlessness of thy virginity; I behold the divineness of thy mind; I see the untouchedness of thy purity; I perceive thy unalloyed cleanness. I understand that thou wast reared and didst grow in the Holy of Holies, and thou hast from there holiness both in body and in soul. I know that Joseph has been allotted only a name empty of the thing; for he has not come forward to thee as the tiller of a childbirth, but as the faithful and God-loving servant of the childbirth.

Why dost thou now speak to me of thy prerogatives?[^13] I will speak to thee of things higher than these. Thou tellest me things natural and human; but I cry to thee things lofty and most divine. If thou hadst known the power of my words, thou wouldst not have brought before me the customs of womenfolk; thou wouldst not have offered me lowly words; thou wouldst not have cried aloud to me the laws of nature, outside which females never bear. I too know human custom, even if I am not by nature a man. I too know that the intercourse of man and woman is wont to beget the man who is born. I am not unknowing of these things in knowledge, even if I am without experience of them in operation; for the incorporeal nature is one, and the nature of bodies again is established as another; and the law and the manner of life of the incorporeals is one, [3264] [b: A] and the custom of bodies is another. Put away, I pray thee, the things that creep on the ground and are earthly, and look, I pray thee, at the things lofty and heavenly; put away, I pray thee, the things cheap and least, and see, I pray thee, the things wonderful and greatest. Not a natural childbirth do I announce to thee today, that which proceeds from the will of a man and of flesh;[^14] but I speak to thee of a childbirth beyond nature and custom.

35. I know, O Virgin, that never has a virgin given birth; but thou shalt give birth, seen a virgin and remaining one. I know that never without union with a man has a woman borne; but thou indeed shalt bear, God decreeing the childbirth. Human nature did not make itself, that it should also define its own laws. God, who fashioned it and defined the laws that befit it, is able also, when he wills, to change the laws of nature which he himself laid down in making it. God is the one who wills; and who would set himself against God's ordinances? For all women, becoming mothers, first come together with men; but thou, kept a virgin and remaining one, shalt become a mother by divine will. Another conception God will newly work in thee; another [?] fashioning[^15] God would show in thee. For nothing at all of what he may wish to do is impossible to him; but the will at once becomes deed, none of the creatures resisting him, of which he himself is the most mighty worker. How then could God, who can do all things, not be able to make thee too, O Virgin, a mother? Unless it be by laws and bounds and customs which he himself laid down earlier, as though he too were subject to them, and serving the laws which he decreed that men should serve. The saying is not good; the answer is not pious; the verdict is not God-loving — to make God the slave of his own laws, God to will to serve the customs of slaves. I rejoice beholding thy sobriety; I rejoice perceiving thy caution; I rejoice seeing thy prudence, even if thou speakest against my addresses. For I do not judge thy words to be of unbelief, nor do I know thy utterances to be of contradiction, but of wise and prudent searching, and of a searching mind and of prudence.
