[3301] ON THE EXALTATION OF THE PRECIOUS CROSS AND THE HOLY RESURRECTION
*An oration of Sophronius, archbishop of Jerusalem.*[^1]

A festival of the cross, and who would not leap for joy? A proclaiming of the Resurrection, and who would not laugh? For the one, fixed in the place of the Skull and having the Master of creation nailed to it, [b: C] «tore up the handwriting that was against us,»[^2] [3304] and set us free from the bonds of sin — that which Adam our forefather signed when he transgressed the commandments of God — and made us ready to leap with gladness, like calves let loose from their bonds.[^3] «For where sin abounded, the grace of God superabounded.»[^4] And the other banished the corruption of death, and drove away the gloom of Hades, and raised the dead from their tombs, and, to speak prophetically, wiped away the tear from every face,[^5] and has bestowed on every man the grace that truly passes to no successor. For the gift of the Resurrection is not partial, nor was the achieving of it shown for some few. For he was God of all creation who in human flesh made his burial in it, or rather his resurrection; who does not know how to grant partial graces, nor does any respect of persons hold citizenship with him. For, being known as true God of all, he stretches out the gracious gift of salvation to all men, sparing his own image and renewing it most perfectly, since every earthly man too has been fashioned in the image of God.

The memorials of the cross have come forth, and who among men would not crucify himself? For it knows this one as its most genuine worshipper: him who has crucified himself to the world,[^6] and shows himself so by his deeds, who has been found out a genuine friend of the cross. The dedication-feast of the Resurrection, and what believer will not be renewed, thrusting away all the deadness of the passions and putting on incorruption of soul? For one death of the soul is defined, and another is known, of the body that is about it. That death sin conceives, as the founder and leader of this see, James the brother of God, has written;[^7] this the dissolution of the elements is wont to beget, out of which the body also essentially consists. Yes, and the withdrawal of the immortal soul as well, even if it does not seem so to the sons of physicians, who profess to heal bodies alone; since to man, when he disobeyed the divine commandment, this very thing had been given by the Creator as a clear penalty. The cross is raised on high, and who will not be lifted mystically from the earth? For where the Redeemer is lifted up, there the redeemed too goes and leaps across, longing ever to be with the one who saved him, and to harvest from him the succour that does not perish. Today the Resurrection goes forth, and brightens all things by its procession; tomorrow the cross appears, and holds out its gifts to its worshippers.[^8] Today the Resurrection is spread abroad, and tomorrow the cross flies overhead: the one pillorying corruption, the other the phalanxes of the demons; the one proclaiming by itself that death has truly been put to death, the other announcing to all that all the evil-doing of the demons has been abolished, and that all their foul and soul-destroying operation has been deadened. And O the wonder! and why am I at a loss to tell the [3305] [b: A] mystery? For of old the cross went before the Resurrection, but now the cross has the Resurrection as its leader and forerunner.

O the wonderful exchange! For I see here too the saying of the Saviour openly fulfilled in them: «For behold, the last have become the very first, and conversely the first have appeared last.»[^9] And who then will be able to tell the cause of these exchanges and alterations? For it is not that these were running some race, and the one sprang ahead while the other, being slow, came in behind. For why did not, as before, the divine cross rise first and flash forth, and the light-bearing Resurrection shine after it on the third day? And what our fathers had in mind when they made this exchange we cannot say without doubt. But we suppose and conjecture that those who come from the ends of the earth to the life-bearing veneration of them are the cause of the postponing and the advancing; so that these, having first celebrated the joyful and brilliant feast of the Resurrection, and straight after it beholding the blessed Exaltation of the Cross, might have its all-powerful company as a fine and saving provision for the road: running with them on their journeys, sailing with them on the sea, and awarding salvation everywhere, and guarding them from all adversities, and showing you yourselves by the very facts that the all-mighty power of the cross has encompassed all the ends of the world, and that it fills all things, and is present everywhere without toil, bringing the faithful safe through hardships, and kindling salvation like a beacon for them as they live piously, and abolishing the counsels of all enemies.

But perhaps there is also some other, hidden reason, which the teachers of this church who lived of old knew and understood,[^10] and which we, the least, are not ashamed to confess that we do not clearly know at present. May God grant the knowledge of this too, as we firmly believe, for the sole benefit of you, the most faithful. What then is loftier for us than these blessed feasts? What among the things that are is holier for us than these sacred festivals? Or how shall we rejoice and leap, celebrating the feasts of these? The shining of the Resurrection, and the torch-bearing veneration of the cross! These are for us the trophies of all salvation. These, having redeemed us from death and passions, and from the most evil ravaging of the demons, have led us back to our Master, having undone what is downcast and sullen, and having spread out for us the radiance of the things that make glad. Or does not the life-begetting Resurrection give us the entry into immortal life? Or does not the cross too, being lifted up, beget in us redemption from the passions? For truly these have shown us once more partakers of close kinship with God, for whose sake they have both come to be and risen upon all of us who are on earth.

[3308] Knowing, therefore, the mystical power of these, and how greatly they have benefited us, and of what things they have become the procurers for us, let us celebrate them so, nobly and piously, as they wish to be honoured: «not in chamberings and wantonness, not in strife and envying, not in rapines and injustices»;[^11] and the catalogue of the rest I pass over in silence. For I dare to say, most genuine brethren, joint heirs with us of the same faith, and enriched together with the same spiritual births, that the one who wishes to celebrate them so they not only do not look upon, do not admit, but even turn away from and abhor, as one who conducts himself unfittingly towards them, and works what is altogether hateful to them. Therefore I exhort and urge you to hate and shun those things which our hallowed things hate, and to love and do those things which we know please them. And those things know how to please and delight them which lead the doer up to salvation, and which guide him to eternal life. Or have not these, shining out from Christ upon men, flashed upon us a life that does not end, and a light that does not set? So then let us too change our own ways of life, and, stripping off our former conduct as harmful and destructive, let us run well in newness of life. Or does not the Resurrection give us the allotment of life? Or has not the cross crucified our old man?[^12]

If then we revere the Resurrection, and celebrate its feast, let us also love a newer life, through which we shall be its friends not with the mouth alone, but also its most intimate initiates. And if we also kiss the cross, why do we not also crucify the passions together with the members, that we too may cry out with Paul: «I am crucified with Christ; and I live no longer, but Christ lives in me»?[^13] If then Christ promises to live in these — in those who have crucified themselves to the world, and «have put to death their earthly members,» as Paul cries and testifies[^14] — why do we not also work such things, and put to death every earthly member: passion, evil desires, and the rest of their enumeration; that Christ may live in us also, and grant the life that does not age? So then let us pursue peace with all, and procure sanctification with it; for without these «no one will ever see the Lord,» as Paul again has testified to us. And for this reason Christ is also proclaimed peace — «For he himself, it says, is our peace» — and he has been named «sanctification».[^15] Peace, as having brought peaceful concord, and having united the heavenly things with those on earth, and having made both one Church; and sanctification and redemption (for besides the former this too is proclaimed), as having become the redeemer of us captives, and not only having redeemed us from demons and passions, but also having implanted in us the sanctification that is of God.

[3309] [b: A] The things which you have heard from my voice,[^16] let us pursue with all zeal and purpose, and acquire, and seize, and through them be joined to Christ — the fair and blessed conjunction. For him who comes to him thus he will by no means cast out of his goodness and blessedness — away with the thought![^17] Let us hasten then to acquire this concord with him, than which there is nothing more precious, and let us run to purchase Christ's living in us, than which nothing is mightier, that, having acquired this wealth, we may also enjoy the kingdom of heaven, and find eternal life in Christ himself, our God and Saviour, with whom to the Father be glory, with the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

ON THE FEAST OF THE CROSS
[b: B] *A homily of Sophronius, most holy among monks and afterwards patriarch of Jerusalem, delivered at the veneration of the precious and life-giving cross in the middle week of holy Lent; and that through sincere fasting it is possible, with the mind cleansed, to approach without condemnation and to partake of the divine mysteries.*[^18]

All physicians, often recognizing the onsets of the diseases that come upon us, mix certain drugs on purpose and avert the infirmities that are about to come in through the body's want of appetite, so that it may not take some spreading hold and produce in the sufferer a pain hard to bear. And often, from their great experience, they are wont easily to beat off even the ailments that linger long in bodies, and especially if the sufferer happens to have given himself into the physician's hands and, becoming obedient, follows his measures. In the same way our good God too has done: he sets before us, as a drug that soothes pain, this sacred and all-excellent fast, not bounded by times and seasons but kept continually, as bridling the appetites of the flesh, and checking besides the spreading that comes upon the body from soft living, so that the body, campaigning against the spirit out of the love of pleasure that is in it, may not sink the spirit under water, and bring about no small fall. For truly, according to the voice of the great herald Paul, they are drawn up in battle facing one another: «For the flesh, it says, lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; and these are opposed to one another.»[^19]

[3312] [b: A] For this reason then, beloved, the God of all has set the forty-day fast plainly before us all,[^20] with a view, surely, to more contests, and to the overthrow of the spirits of the air; that, having cleansed them wholly away, we may arrive through a pure way of living at the Lord's day of the three-day and life-bearing Resurrection, and, pure, may be seen by our pure God, and rise with him, and be glorified with him, and be received by him as friends. For a traveller too, as regards the place he must reach day by day: if only he come within sight of the resting-place, he makes his eagerness for the journey the greater, even if he be wearied by the length of the most difficult road, being greatly nourished by the hope of refreshment. For we too are wayfarers, running up through our brief life and making our passage through it: those who have lived well arriving at the palaces above, where our citizenship is;[^21] but those who have lived impiously, like me, will appear from there buried in some lowest pit of their own passions, by which they were held fast, enslaved while they lived, buried in them.

And how, they say, is it possible for anyone to run up to the things above while still living in this life? How? I say: being given wings by the purifying mind, and rejoicing with the powers above, celebrating the overthrow of the all-destroying [?] enemy;[^22] so as, in soul and body, through the gentle fast and the touching of the life-giving and undefiled wood, as cleansed on both sides, to leap together, as we said, with the heavenly powers, whose fellow-citizens we too shall become when the Judge comes down from heaven. And how, he says, would this come about? When, with pure lips, through both the illumination of our ruling part and the repulsion and resistance of the opposing spirits, we shall appear worthy of the pure touching of the life-giving and undefiled cross; since our good God too, through the tradition of the holy Fathers, has shown us this road — of the profitable fast, I mean — that, having offered up a little of our hardship, and being cleansed of the filth, and having checked the plots of the passions, we may receive the perfect remission that comes of the touching of the life-giving wood from him who was fixed upon it, as it now remains to be seen: otherwise [?] how is the one helping the other, and leading by the hand to the day of the Resurrection?[^23] The fast effecting the lightness of the body, and the most pure veneration of the divine cross leading our mind away from the confusion of the most shameful works, and leading it up to the palaces above. These things «the mediator of God and men» has caused to come to pass,[^24] he who wrought our salvation in the midst of the earth by the saving nailing of his cross, and by the mid-point of this divine fast has shown us good hopes of the Resurrection.

[3313] [b: A] But O fast, God-given tool, which like an axe cuts down the passionate appetites that have sprung up in us; which Adam having lost was shown an exile from the common life of paradise, but Moses having kept became a beholder of God! O fast stronger than fire, which reduced the Chaldaean fire to ashes, and kept the three children unwounded! O fast, which grieved the prophet Jonah, and saved the city of the Ninevites! And if anyone should wish to track out the ways of fasting, perhaps he may be able to grasp the boundless judgements of God.[^25]

But that we may not praise the fast alone and its life-giving gifts, come, let us set in the midst the life-giving wood of the precious cross, set before us in the midst of the fast and fixed in the midst of the earth for our salvation. What, and how, as we hymn it with mouth and heart? Thus let us speak, crying and saying to it: Hail, precious cross, on which the Son and Word of God, spreading out his hands beneath, embraced us, and brought us to the heavenly Father. Hail, cross, through which our senses are cleansed of every stain through our whole-hearted embracing of thee. Hail, cross, who didst unfailingly procure for our fathers joy unspeakable[^26] through him who was nailed upon thee. Hail, cross, astonishment of the flaming sword, since through thee it turned its back to the grateful thief.[^27] Hail, cross, holding which we now, the faithful people, the holy nation, bring thee [?] as our supplication [b: C] to Christ and God who was fixed upon thee;[^28] wherefore we also greatly glory in thee, since on thee our Master stretched out his undefiled hands, and on thee was poured out the God-flowing blood of the Son and Word of the invisible Father; and those who piously partake of it today are reconciled to our life-giving God for the evils already committed by them, and we easily obtain their release, both through his supreme condescension that came to be towards us, and through the remembrance of his life-giving passion.

Hail, cross, victory-making weapon of the great King, who at his second and dread coming art to journey along with the heavenly powers and with the assemblies of all the righteous, who also shall shine out with thee as luminaries, declaring and preparing the way for the coming of the King of all creation; whose operation of grace, and of the inconceivable beauty, of the glory laid up in thee, which thou didst receive from Jesus who was nailed upon thee, shall flash forth more manifestly with the righteous, to the shame of the Jews, but to the boast of those who have beheld [3316] thy own and divine operations in this thick and most heavy flesh. But, O precious cross, most sure guardian of Christians and most mighty destroyer of all the assaults of the adversaries, shine upon us with the radiance laid up in thee; enlighten the eye of our heart,[^29] that we too may choose to suffer some such thing as the Son and Word of the invisible Father suffered on thee, and, for him, to lay down our lives unto death, as he did for us. For if we are willing to choose to suffer this, and to prefer this good and kindly road, we shall be able with ease to be conformed also to his sufferings; to which make us, uncondemned, through the power of thy operations, attain, and become worthy beholders and worshippers,[^30] [b: B] by the invincible authority of him who through thee put death to death; and deem us worthy at the same time to look upon the bright and light-bearing day of the Pascha with cheerful gladness, as a pledge of the more express and truer Pascha, and [?] for the enjoyment of those eternal good things;[^31] of which may it be granted all of us to partake, by the grace and love for man of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be glory, together with his Holy Spirit, now and ever, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
