title: Encomium of Saint John the Forerunner, 10–15
---
[3337] 10. So then the priest is great: he both disbelieves mystically, [b: B] and more mystically still is deprived of the going-forth of speech, on account of the irrationality of Jewish unbelief; and from this John, the Forerunner of the Word, comes forth, bearing in himself the virtue of his begetter; if indeed the tree is known from its fruit, and a rotten tree cannot make good fruit, according to that divine and God-wrought voice.[^1] For if anyone should not be willing to understand the old man's unbelief in this way, the old man will be much inferior in virtues to Elisabeth. For she, although she had in no way heard these things (for the old man was speechless towards her), [?] on seeing the blessed Virgin come to her,[^2] and on learning of the virginal greeting, at once proclaimed her Theotokos: «Blessed art thou among women,» crying out with a loud voice, «and blessed is the fruit of thy womb. And whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me? For behold, as the voice of thy greeting came into my ears, the babe leapt in exultation in my womb»;[^3] and she worthily called her blessed, saying also: «Blessed is she that believed, for there is a fulfilment to the things spoken to her from the Lord.»[^4] For not only did she recognise by the Spirit that she was with child, but she was also rich in the knowledge of the things spoken to her from God by an angel's voice; which would be altogether incredible and implausible. For it shows the perfection of Zacharias towards God, not only in the angel's having been sent to him, the angel who set such things in order on earth by a promise, and in his becoming [?] of John the Baptist,[^5] and in his being dignified with kinship to Christ — «For behold,» says the very angel who brought the good tidings to the holy Virgin, «Elisabeth thy kinswoman»[^6] — but also from the things he sang prophetically after John's birth, when he foretold what concerned Christ, crying: «Blessed is the Lord God of Israel, for he hath visited and wrought redemption for his people, and hath raised up a horn of salvation for us in the house of his servant David, as he spake by the mouth of his holy prophets which have been since the world began»;[^7] and the other things that he added to these concerning him, which he also prayed every hour to behold before death.

[3340] 11. In like manner too from what he prophesied at the end of the song concerning his own child: «And thou, child,» he says, «shalt be called the prophet of the Most High: for thou shalt go before the face of the Lord to prepare his ways; to give knowledge of salvation to his people in the remission of our sins, through the bowels of mercy of our God.»[^8] These things he spoke not from the heart, but being made full of the Holy Spirit: for it is written thus of him in the Gospels: «And Zacharias,» it says, «his father was filled with the Holy Spirit, and prophesied, saying»[^9] — saying what? These things, plainly, which we have spoken of before. And it is quite plain that this Zacharias would not clearly have been filled with the Holy Spirit unless he had been someone faithful to the Spirit and worthy of his longed-for filling; and that he would not have been styled the father of John either, unless he had been a match for this dignity too.[^10] Let all these things, then, lead the way to John's conception, showing him, before his conception, greater than all among those born of women; even as Christ, the truth, truly bore him this witness too;[^11] lest some should suppose him, on this ground — that he was not born of any great persons — less than any one of men. And this the evangelic writing too, taking care for it, as I think, by this Scripture concerning him set forth beforehand, proscribed in advance, the suspicion of those who would suspect this, saying plainly: «And they were [?] both walking before God in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless.»[^12] What then? That Zacharias and Elisabeth, John's begetters, should have won at that time such an evangelic vote, bearing witness to them of the citadel of the virtues — this is the surpassing thing to behold among men. For the unbribed vote not only granted them the just, but added also the «before God»: for they studied to appear just to God alone by evangelic laws, foreseeing these with a prophetic eye as already laid down; and to attempt the way of life of «the left hand hiding the right»,[^13] anticipating in work and deed the precept of such legislation. And to the vote this too is clearly added: the blameless fulfilment of all the Mosaic commandments and ordinances together, to show those who have this lawful verdict as both of them with both — crowned with the boasts of the old legislation, and made brilliant with the heights of the evangelic way of life.

12. For such it was needful and fitting that John's progenitors too should be seen; since he who was to come from them was to be shown to us as mediator of each Testament, and truly to hold together in himself what is choice in both. For it is confessed that he was marked out as the end of the Elder (for he himself completed the number of the prophetic choir, since all the prophets prophesied until John the Baptist),[^14] and as the beginning and prelude of the Newer: for he led the way before all the apostles; since he was sent to go before and to run before Christ, with a holy [?] angelic dignity.[^15] For he had been bidden to walk before God as an angel:[^16] «And from the days of John the Baptist [3341] [b: A] the kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent take it by force.»[^17] For taking from him the beginning of its being forced, through the evangelic manner of life it is willingly taken by force by all. From a father, then, who was priest and prophet, and in other respects brilliant and godly-minded, in the womb of a mother holy and a prophetess, this John, who has the pre-eminence among all the prophets, is conceived; who also alone prophesied in the womb, since he recognised the Master present in the virginal womb[^18]; and alone in the womb became pregnant with the Holy Spirit, bearing in the womb within himself and being borne in the womb: borne in the womb by a barren mother, but bearing in the womb the grace of the Spirit, and making a prophetic travail before he was travailed of, and before he was born giving birth to the Spirit's gift; if indeed, according to the divine Isaiah, they conceive in the womb and travail, and then afterwards bring forth, who serve the Spirit prophetically, and announce his hidden counsel openly.[^19] But these things John did later, after his paradoxical conception: for he had been conceived in a barren womb, and laid down in an aged mother by an aged father. Before this, meanwhile, he is quiet, under constraint, yet obeying his mother's bidding: for he did not wish to carry the grace of the Spirit inoperative, with which, according to the angelic voice, he was filled while still dwelling in the bosom.[^20] And when that five-month time had gone by, which shows forth to us the law of sense through the pentad, akin to it and dear, of the bodily and swifter senses — during which the mother, since she was a mother, hid herself, and hid with herself this prophet who could not be silenced.[^21]

13. And now the sixth month was come; and there was come the [b: C] Virgin, bearing in her womb the Uncreated being created: for in the sixth month she conceives him who in six days wrought the six-day world, and on the sixth day created man, and again, when man had fared ill, created him anew through the cross. John no longer endured to keep quiet, nor did the voice consent to be still while the Word was present; but he became a herald before his time, his heralding in no way hindered by the bonds of the tongue: for by his leapings he cried that he was present who redeems us men from bonds, and grants us to leap as men loosed from bonds; he exulted, crying out in the Spirit that he had come who takes away every tear from every face,[^22] and bestows a steadfast exultation on the whole race. He stretched out his finger and showed the Lamb of God, slain on our behalf for sin in the stead of kids,[^23] and taking away the sin of the world entirely. He stretched out both his hands, and proclaimed beforehand the trophy of the cross, which he who was present in the Virgin's womb had come to set up against the demons. He stood upright, and mystically sang by his posture the resurrection of all from Hades, which he who was then hidden in the unsown womb showed forth when hidden in the tomb. And perhaps he would have fought with his mother, because she was hindering [3344] [b: A] with natural bonds him who longed to cry out such things, and was holding him a prisoner against his will, had she not quickly lent him her voice, and allowed him to speak out plainly through her to the blessed Virgin: «Blessed is the fruit of thy womb; and whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?»[^24] For perhaps this proclamation is John's, even if it was proclaimed by the mouth of Elisabeth: since he cried out the kindred and same-worded voice to Christ the Saviour when again, as he was baptizing the crowd in the Jordan, Christ himself too came to him to be baptized by him. For then too he cried out, as he saw him drawing near: «I have need of thee [?] to be baptized, and comest thou to me!»[^25] Hast thou recognised the kinship of the voice? Recognise that he it is who uttered both that one and this; and the one he uttered through himself, the other through his mother's mouth.

14. And I think that he who was thus hot for the proclamation of Christ pleaded wordlessly with the one who sent him, against her who bore him in her womb and barred his going: Master, thou didst send me to proclaim thy indescribable coming on earth, and my mother holds me fettered; thou biddest me cry out, and she has bound up my tongue; thou hast sent me to run before, and she has shut up my course. Thou art Master of nature: command, and nature performs what is commanded; only give the order, and my mother cannot hinder; only speak, and what is spoken will come to be at once. For thou hast power running together with thy will, and all things serve thy almighty decree. But if thou dost not bid me accomplish this, do not condemn me for sloth in the course, thou who didst legislate the hindrance of the course: for thou thyself didst set the bounds of nature, which those who serve the laws of nature cannot overpass. But the Master, for John's heat for proclaiming, set nothing ahead of the nature well decreed for him from the beginning, whose laws — he too having become man like us for us men — he deigned to serve with us. But perhaps here too he answered him those words which afterwards, too, he answered when coming to baptism: «Suffer it now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.»[^26] Hearing him, as is likely, John was quiet, and dared speak no more; although he was pressing hard to go forth from the belly that held him before the time appointed for all that are conceived, and to begin the proclamation given him from God. And so, while he saw for three months more the Creator dwelling with him and enduring the same ordinances of nature, the time of the birth comes on: for the three months the Virgin who bore him in her womb remained with Elisabeth,[^27] that he who was coming forth from her, and was being sent to the world as a true herald, might be fully initiated, in his mother's womb as in a Mosaic cloud, into the mysteries of the supreme Trinity.[^28] And he is born by the ordinance of the God hidden in the virginal womb.

15. And when born, he at once ended the reproach of the mother who bore him, which she had from the charge of childlessness; but he did not loose the bridle of the tongue of the father who begot him, which he had from the stumbling of unbelief. And the child was, to all who saw, beyond dispute [?]:[^29] [3345] [b: A] for she that bore him, her soul illumined by the Spirit, wished him to be called John; which is uttered in the Hebrew tongue, but passing into the Greek signifies grace and help of God.[^30] But the kinsfolk, the friends and nurslings of the synagogue, hearing the prophetic name, and thinking that the grace of God and the Father was present to the world, denied it, and fled from it with its own significance, and indeed tried and wanted to change it, and said rather boldly to her who had borne him: «There is none in thy kindred that is called by this name»;[^31] for so they were bound to do, who filled up the Jewish kindred. But he, taking a tablet by a sign, since he could not speak, engraved: «John is the child's name.» And not only did he write this in the wax, but also, [b: B] writing it in letters, he cried it out in words, showing by the motion of the hand the movement of the tongue running with it, and by the running stylus showing the word running along with it. For wishing to signify this the blessed evangelist too said more expressively: «And he asked for a writing-tablet, and wrote, saying: John shall be his name.»[^32] For at once he both wrote and spoke, and granted to see as simultaneous the operation of each part, of tongue and of hand, each of them.[^33] For he did not write and not speak, or speak and not write; but he spoke equally and wrote; and the hand ran below, and the word flowed above; and neither of the two had the victory over the other, but both, as friends, greeted the turning-post of the course; even as runners who have run out from the starting-lines, and both have received crowns of victory, neither being willing to confess defeat. At which all wondered;[^34] for what was done was truly worthy of wonder. And if to those who then wondered at the matter it was wondered at only bodily, since too it was manifested to them only bodily, yet for the initiates of the Spirit the things of the Spirit will not stop at what is seen; but the word indicates that it holds something hidden, which, searching it out in the Spirit, let us track spiritually; and, having hunted it down — the lamp, that is, sending down its ray to us, the children of the Spirit — let us set it before you as spiritual food.
