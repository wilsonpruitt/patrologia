title: On Saints Peter and Paul, 1–6
---
[3356] [b: A] ON SAINTS PETER AND PAUL
*Of our father among the saints, Sophronius, archbishop of Jerusalem: on the holy Peter and Paul, the blessed apostles, on the fourth day of the Nativity.*[^1]

1. Again an indissoluble dyad has shone upon us; again a pairing that cannot be unyoked has blossomed for us; again a conjunction not to be cut asunder has burgeoned for us: apostolic festivals running together into one feast and festival, and making us brilliant with double brightnesses, appearing in one brilliance of the sun's light. For today is Peter, the very chief of the apostles, and Paul, Peter's match in grace and near him in the dignity of the apostleship, even if he was not of that holy twelve of the light-shining apostles, whose choosing and calling no mere man could have contrived — no prophet, no patriarch, no just man, no heavenly and bodiless angel: for these are all set as servants, and fellow-servants with us men, even if they differ from us by a great measure in virtues and spiritual dignities and graces; but Christ himself, the Master of all, both God and Lord, he who is twofold in nature and single in hypostasis, he who is composed of divine and human nature, and is plainly known in essence divine and human, and is in no way divided into them, nor admits cutting of any kind, because of the unchangeable and impartible union. For neither did he confuse the things from which he is, though he united them to one another according to hypostasis; nor did he set apart from one another the things in which he is, even if in them he is known perfectly as God and man, the same one, and beheld in himself; he who knows the hearts and reins,[^2] and beholds the counsels of the invisible soul, which each man counsels secretly in his heart, even if he thinks to escape, most thievishly, those who are of the same nature.

2. Thus caring for our salvation, for the sake of which he also came down to us from heaven, he made choice of fishermen, and apportioned to them the grace of apostleship, numbered together with their quantity, but not dividing their rank, nor confusing their order; but, in the unity of the apostolic dignity and grace, keeping the difference of the order. For the wisdom of God and the power,[^3] and the Word, most knowing of all that is known, has judged it good both that the dignity of the apostleship be kept single, and that the difference of the order be known without confusion; so that neither should the order pass into disorder [3357] [b: A] (for the disorderly is hateful to God), nor the grace of the dignity be cut asunder into unequal graces. For the gifts are without repentance,[^4] and the bound which he himself should fix shall in no wise, as the psalmody says, be passed over.[^5] He who orders all things by balance and weight,[^6] and gives to each his gifts by a counsel that accepts no persons, after the first choosing, which he made upon twelve apostles, and the second, which he wrought upon two and seventy disciples[^7] — after the ascent from earthly affairs into the heavens, which came to pass by reason of the flesh which he assumed from us (for before, fleshless and bodiless, he had come down to us) — shows forth a third choosing also, finely increasing the numbering of the apostles, not working a difference of apostleship. For God, the potter of us all, has power over the clay and the moulded thing to make vessels, and to bring forth as he wills,[^8] none working against his counsels. He it was who, plainly choosing Paul also, said to Ananias, Paul's baptist and teacher — for he both enlightened him when he had been blinded, and baptized him when enlightened, and when baptized taught him the God-inspired teaching —: «Go», to his divine catechizing, nay more, to his recovery of sight too, «for he is a vessel of election unto me, to bear my name before the nations, and kings, and the sons of Israel: for I will show him how great things he must suffer for my name's sake.»[^9] Nay, Paul himself too, the wonderful, showing the appointment of his own dignity, wrote: «He that operated in Peter for the circumcision, operated in me also for the nations.»[^10]

3. Since, then, the Saviour's appointment by choice fitted the inspired Paul to Peter, the first of the apostles and herald, and bestowed one rank on both — even if he put forward the one for the apostleship of those in the circumcision, and allotted the other to the gentile flocks; and gave one grace and God-given rank — that of adoption, I mean, to God and Begetter — to those who through him turn to Christ himself, and graciously granted one name to both, to be named Christians from Christ himself: fittingly we too, their disciples and followers, cherishing their faith and embracing their teaching, have joined to one another those whom the grace of the Spirit joined; and we establish a common festival for them, as for men who received an appointment common and of equal glory; and we keep one feast to Christ, since one spiritual [?] flashes upon us through the brightness of both.[^11] For those whom Christ himself fitted together, and adorned with one grace, it is not without danger to divide from one another: so decided those who begot us into Christ.[^12] For this reason they appointed one festival for them, lest they should be seen fighting against Christ's vote. And we too, the least, hastening to follow them in all things, and coming together, gather a common concourse for them, and by this most brightly honour [3360] their conjunction. For where Peter the chief is proclaimed, there Paul too is named at once; and wherever Paul, the inspired man, appears, Peter too is shown in company with him.

4. Thus the wise and wondrous men make their companionships with one another unspeakable: not willing separation, turning from partition, shunning divisions, fleeing cuttings, not loving severances, as offspring of hatred and the outworks of enmity; wishing through these to teach us the profit of spiritual loving, and the usefulness of friendship according to Christ. For what is not done according to Christ our God knows how to provide not one benefit to those who do it. For this reason both of them, teaching us the advantage of love, and figuring in themselves its operation, also keep the festival in common. Whence the one, knowing the unfailingness of the bond of love for us towards one another, defined it as greater than all the gifts,[^13] as one that knows not how to work ill to the neighbour: «For love,» he says, «worketh no ill to the neighbour»;[^14] and he commands those who have put it on to receive [?] one another;[^15] and not this only, but he enjoins them not to seek their own things, but to long for the neighbour through loving;[^16] which makes those who partake of it one body and one Spirit, and sets them in the one hope of their calling,[^17] and shows them sharers of eternal life. And the other: «Having purified your souls,» he says, «in the obedience of the truth, unto unfeigned love of the brethren, love one another from the heart fervently; and as servants of God, honour all of us; love the brotherhood,»[^18] knowing that loving makes us brothers to one another. Whence also before all things he bids us to have fervently the love towards ourselves, because he declared definitively that love covers a multitude of sins.[^19]

5. But we, labouring at the contrary of their teachings, not only do not wish to love one another, but wish even to hate our neighbours as enemies; not knowing, as it seems, that enmity and hatred are offspring of the evil spirit; whence we both wrong these, and afflict them, and shoot them through with countless slanders, and work every evil there is against them, once we have been widowed of love; which, according to Paul the all-wise, worketh no ill to the neighbour: for the finest work of love has truly been found out — not to work ill to the neighbour. But neither the divine teachings of these men, full of divine grace, and providing salvation to the doers themselves, not hearers only,[^20] nor again their wise examples, have been able to bring us over to love: so grievously are we sick with hatred of brothers, and so terribly ailing with enmity towards one another, which is properly both the spring and the mother of all evils. Wherefore I exhort you, most dear ones, that, keeping the festival — apostolic and friendly at once — of Peter and Paul, the wondrous men, we emulate their loving, and long for the conjunction into one body, even as we received it in the chrism of the one [3361] [b: A] faith; and that we cease from the evils against one another, and share with them the heavenly rewards of love, being styled true pupils, and being their children, and known as sons, as holding fast their loving, and honouring exactly their precepts, and corrupting nothing at all of their holy teachings.

6. But why do the wise and all-greatest men now do this — coming forward for us, after Stephen the all-excellent, into plain view, and willingly choosing the fourth act?[^21] And yet in the dignity of the chief apostleship and appointment they go before not Stephen only, the brilliant protomartyr, but all God-loving men together, and take the first place in its greatness. For it is not unclear that a martyr's brilliance and illumination [b: B] is far worsted by the apostolic radiance and station. For having given to Stephen the third festival after Christ's saving birth, they kept the fourth for themselves,[^22] remembering the commandments of the Master, and eager to fulfil them here too. For to those who wish to take the first places, and pursue the choosing of the greater dignities, he decreed that they should choose the last rank, speaking plainly to them somewhat thus: «He that would be first among you, let him become last of all»; and, «Whosoever will be first among you, let him be your servant»; and again, «He that is greater among you, let him become as the younger, and he that leadeth as he that serveth. For he that exalteth himself shall be humbled, and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.»[^23] Nay, those too who were last called to the work of the vineyard first [b: C] received the wage.[^24] Whence, knowing this clearly, they both take the second rank, and grant the first place to the divine Stephen; that they might most justly become first, and teach us by their own examples the best road of exaltation — littleness, I mean, and humbling — which Christ, first operating it, cut as a road: Learn of us, they say, uttering it by their works, that we are meek and lowly in heart,[^25] according to the decree of Christ the Master and teacher. And for this reason the one said that we should esteem one another better than ourselves;[^26] and the other wishes us to gird ourselves with humility towards one another: «For God,» he says, «resisteth the proud, but giveth grace to the humble»; wherefore he also adds to such a discourse: «Humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the season of visitation.»[^27] Of these, most longed-for brethren, being styled hearers and disciples, let us join imitation to learning, and deeds to the hearing of the word, taking hence love towards one another; that, hastening both in word and in deed to the imitation of them, and bearing their likeness, and so keeping their feast in their likeness, we may gladden our souls and spirits, and brighten with our senses [3364] [b: A] their most bright festival. For seeing this they are gladdened the more, and exult the more, and, rejoicing at once and delighting, offer to God their supplications for us, interceding for a peaceful life and asking for the kingdom of heaven; whose enjoyments and shining-forth may it be granted all of us to gather — having ordered ourselves as is due, and made ourselves brilliant with divine repentance, and bound ourselves tight with the bond of love — and to receive the ageless and unending life, in Christ Jesus our Lord; with whom to God and the Father and to the all-holy Spirit be glory, honour, might, majesty and hymning, always, now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

*Of Sophronius, the most holy archbishop of Jerusalem: from the encomium on Saint John the Theologian and Evangelist, whose beginning is: «John, the son of thunder, thunders the "In the beginning was the Word."»*[^28]

*And after other matter,*

John, the child of Zebedee and Salome, shone forth. For this too was found to be the mother's name, even as the holy Fathers who spoke by the Spirit of God detected it. Whence also another good lot, far better and greater than the lot of every good fortune.[^29] And this was the glory of the sisterhood of the mother who bore him. For Salome, who bore the great John, was the daughter of Joseph, who was supposed to be the father of Christ, the Saviour of us all, as the all-wise Luke recorded.[^30] For he was not father in work and fact: since he neither knew at all her who is truly Theotokos and ever-virgin Mother of the Lord, nor stripped her of the immaculate and virginal grace. And she was the true sister and co-heir of James and Joses and Judas and Simeon,[^31] and moreover of Mary and Anna; whom the inspired Fathers too rightly name brothers of God,[^32] adorning them with such a rank as far as the name, and the name only; since they were in every way born of him who was reckoned among men to be Christ's father, and was so called in the Gospels; even as this all-pure and all-immaculate Virgin, the Mother of God, is seen to address Joseph, she who in no way at all knew a man: «Child,» she says, «both I and thy father have sought thee sorrowing.»[^33]

*And a little after.*

When Zebedee called Salome, the new Sarah, into the midst, that as a sister she might entreat Christ.[^34]
